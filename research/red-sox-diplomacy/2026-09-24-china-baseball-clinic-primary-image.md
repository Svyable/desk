# China baseball clinic: primary-image evidence and attribution boundary

## Why this matters

A surviving U.S. government image gives the China chapter a strong, scene-level public-diplomacy artifact without requiring reconstructed dialogue or motive. The image was published from Ambassador Nicholas Burns's official account on October 22, 2024 and is preserved on Wikimedia Commons with its original social-media source and U.S. Department of State public-domain provenance.

## Verified evidence

### October 22, 2024 — Beijing baseball clinic

Wikimedia Commons preserves an 875 × 657 PNG titled **“Ambassador Burns with Chinese baseball fans.png.”** Its description says Burns is standing with Chinese baseball fans, wearing a jersey and a Boston Red Sox cap and holding a baseball. The preserved official-account caption reads: “Great to see so many young Chinese friends attend our Baseball Clinic in Beijing. Future members of @Redsox Nation.”

## Manuscript value

These artifacts establish something stronger than generic “Red Sox fan” biography. During Burns's China ambassadorship, baseball and Red Sox identity were visibly present in official, public-facing activity. The October 2024 artifact is especially valuable because it joins four things in one authenticated object: Burns, young Chinese participants, an explicitly identified baseball clinic in Beijing, and Burns's public invocation of “Red Sox Nation.”

A safe manuscript formulation is therefore:

> By October 2024, baseball had become visible in Burns's public-facing work in Beijing. In an official post from a baseball clinic, he appeared with young Chinese participants wearing a Red Sox cap and holding a baseball, calling them “future members” of Red Sox Nation.

The quotation should remain attached to the authenticated official post; do not turn it into dialogue spoken at the clinic unless audio/video proves he said it aloud.

## Attribution and inference boundaries

This evidence **does not establish**:

- who conceived, funded, organized, or staffed the clinic;
- whether the Boston Red Sox organization, MLB, MLB China, a school, a Chinese baseball body, or another partner provided institutional assistance;
- what Burns said orally at the event beyond any separately authenticated recording/transcript;
- the names, ages, schools, or affiliations of the young participants;
- any participant's reaction, motive, political view, or later relationship with baseball;
- that the clinic changed attitudes toward the United States or produced a diplomatic outcome;
- that Burns used baseball pursuant to a formal public-diplomacy strategy unless documentary evidence ties the event to such a strategy.

Do not attribute any first-person account of this event to **Libby Baylies** absent authenticated material from her.
