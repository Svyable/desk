## 2004 contemporary-reporting deepening

The strongest manuscript-grade formulation comes from the August 18, 2004 *Christian Science Monitor* report, published during the Athens Olympics. It records the federation calling the U.S. ambassador for help and quotes Burns, by then U.S. ambassador to NATO, describing himself as a “confirmed, unconditional, rabid fan of the Boston Red Sox.” More importantly, it attributes to Burns a retrospective judgment about the Greece baseball project itself: he called introducing baseball to Greece “a joint venture between America and Greece” and “one of the best things I ever did as ambassador.”

That quotation can be promoted with explicit attribution because it is a named, contemporaneous press interview rather than reconstructed first-person material. It strengthens the book’s argument that baseball was not merely private fandom for Burns: in Greece he later publicly characterized the baseball-building effort as part of his ambassadorial work.

The preserved *Athens News* account adds institutional scale, but should remain one tier lower until the original issue is retrieved. It reports that the Baltimore Orioles had worked with the Hellenic Amateur Baseball Federation since 1999; that Orioles scout Rob Derksen was assigned to the effort; and that Major League Baseball International was expected to contribute roughly $500,000 to the federation, alongside equipment and development support. Those details reinforce the institutional-assistance story while also making the negative boundary sharper: the documented club-level partner is Baltimore, not Boston.

### Safe manuscript promotion

A defensible sentence is:

> Burns’s Red Sox allegiance was personal, but his most consequential baseball work in Greece ran through other institutions. During the 2004 Athens Olympics he told the *Christian Science Monitor* that introducing baseball to Greece had been “a joint venture between America and Greece” and “one of the best things I ever did as ambassador”; contemporary reporting traces the practical assistance through Peter Angelos, the Baltimore Orioles, and Major League Baseball International.

Keep the two short quotations attached to the *Monitor* interview. Do not convert Burns’s retrospective assessment into evidence that baseball measurably improved bilateral relations, and do not imply Boston Red Sox organizational participation.

### Retrieval targets

Priority archival retrieval is now the original *Athens News* issue carrying Robert Bruce’s “Greek-American Backs National Baseball Team,” including page number, publication date, photographs, captions, and any continuation pages. Secondary targets are HABF records from 1999–2004, MLB International program files, Orioles/Angelos correspondence, Rob Derksen papers or interviews, and U.S. Embassy Athens public-affairs files. These could establish dates, money, equipment, personnel, and image provenance without relying on retrospective web copies.
