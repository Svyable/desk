# Nicholas Burns — Red Sox speech/source pack

Updated: 2026-09-12

Purpose: primary-source and well-attributed material for writing or revising remarks involving Ambassador R. Nicholas Burns, his long identification with the Boston Red Sox, and his wife Elizabeth “Libby” Baylies. This note keeps Nicholas Burns’s own words separate from material about Libby so that a Red Sox line is never accidentally attributed to her without evidence.

## Nicholas Burns: primary-source Red Sox material

### 1. Worcester Polytechnic Institute commencement — May 24, 1997

**Source:** U.S. Department of State archive, “Preparing for the International Age,” commencement address at Worcester Polytechnic Institute.  
https://1997-2001.state.gov/policy_remarks/970524.burns.html

Burns establishes the identity early: **“A long suffering fan of the Boston Red Sox, especially this year!”** He then turns baseball into a joke about globalization, noting that Japanese players were reaching nearly every major-league park while Fenway and the Red Sox bullpen still needed help.

**Speech use:** This is an unusually early, clean example of Burns using Red Sox suffering as self-deprecating autobiography. It works well for an arc that later lands on the 2004 championship: the public persona predates the victory.

### 2. State Department daily press briefing — June 4, 1997

**Source:** U.S. Department of State archive, Daily Press Briefing, Nicholas Burns as briefer.  
https://1997-2001.state.gov/briefings/9706/970604db.html

Asked about former Red Sox pitcher Roger Clemens, Burns riffs: **“We’re still bitter in Boston. Bitter in Boston is the headline. We’re poor losers in Boston.”** He keeps going, joking that the Sox need resuscitation and that Boston is praying for them.

**Speech use:** Strongest source for Burns’s comic rhythm around the team. It is spontaneous rather than ceremonial and gives a usable contrast with his much more triumphant post-2004 language.

### 3. Boston College commencement — May 20, 2002

**Source:** Boston College, R. Nicholas Burns ’78 commencement address.  
https://www.bc.edu/content/bc-web/bcnews/campus-community/alumni/r--nicholas-burns-2002-commencement-address.html

Burns calls himself **“a loyal member of the Red Sox nation”** and predicts Boston will finally be home to World Series champions after an 84-year pause.

**Speech use:** Valuable because the prediction is wrong in 2002 but only two years early. It captures hope before vindication and is much better material than retrospectively claiming inevitability.

### 4. U.S. Institute of Peace remarks — March 23, 2005

**Source:** U.S. Department of State archive, remarks at the U.S. Institute of Peace.  
https://2001-2009.state.gov/p/us/rm/2005/45781.htm

Only months after the 2004 title, Burns says: **“I’m a native-born member of Red Sox Nation.”** He describes the championship as “redemption” after defeating the Yankees and Cardinals.

**Speech use:** This is the clean before/after hinge with the 1997 “long suffering” material. Burns himself changes the vocabulary from suffering and prayer to redemption.

### 5. Balkans trip briefing — October 7, 2005

**Source:** U.S. Department of State archive, briefing on Burns’s October 10–14 trip to Brussels and the Balkans.  
https://2001-2009.state.gov/p/us/rm/2005/54610.htm

Before discussing diplomacy, Burns jokes that the State Department is **“shutting down … at 4 p.m.”** so employees can watch the Red Sox play the White Sox, then deadpans that the game can go on the department’s internal network.

**Speech use:** Good evidence that the Red Sox bit was part of his working diplomatic persona, not only commencement-stage material.

### 6. Marshall Plan 60th anniversary speech in Paris — June 12, 2007

**Source:** U.S. Department of State archive, “Commemoration of the 60th Anniversary of the Marshall Plan.”  
https://2001-2009.state.gov/p/us/rm/2007/87176.htm

Burns says he and Ambassador Craig Stapleton share a bond as fans of **“the greatest baseball team in the States, the Boston Red Sox.”** He immediately casts the Yankees as the “evil empire.”

**Speech use:** The sports rivalry becomes a diplomatic icebreaker in Paris. Useful for showing how local identity travels with him into formal international settings.

### 7. Massachusetts Bay Community College commencement — May 23, 2013

**Source:** Harvard Kennedy School Belfer Center, “Nicholas Burns Addresses Massachusetts Graduates.”  
https://www.belfercenter.org/publication/nicholas-burns-addresses-massachusetts-graduates

Burns uses Boston sports as civic glue, saying the Red Sox, Patriots, Bruins, and Celtics **“bind us together as a ‘Community’”** and that the Sox create a “Nation” across New England.

**Speech use:** This is the strongest source for moving beyond fandom into a larger point: sports as common language, regional memory, and community identity.

### 8. John Courtney Murray, S.J. Lecture — November 13, 2019

**Source:** Harvard Kennedy School Belfer Center, “Our Future: The Pursuit of a ‘Newer World.’”  
https://www.belfercenter.org/publication/our-future-pursuit-newer-world

Opening in New York, Burns says he and Father Matt Malone share **“an abiding faith in the superiority of the Boston Red Sox”** and jokes that Malone lives behind enemy lines as a representative of Red Sox Nation.

**Speech use:** A polished late-career version of the same device: establish New England kinship, tease New York, get the room, then turn to serious material.

### 9. Official biographical language

**Sources:** George W. Bush White House archive and current Harvard Kennedy School biography.  
https://georgewbush-whitehouse.archives.gov/government/nburns-bio.html  
https://www.hks.harvard.edu/faculty/nicholas-burns

The Bush White House biography explicitly calls Burns a life-long member of Red Sox Nation; Harvard’s current faculty biography retains the same description. A 2024 Kennedy School announcement jokes that his lifelong Red Sox devotion is his “only lapse from non-partisan diplomacy.”

**Speech use:** These are useful for an introducer’s voice. The Red Sox identity is not merely anecdotal; institutions have repeatedly treated it as part of his public biography.

## Libby / Elizabeth Baylies: verified source material

### Identity and shared life

The State Department’s archived biography identifies Burns’s wife as Elizabeth Baylies; later sources and Burns’s own speeches call her Libby.  
https://1997-2001.state.gov/about_state/biography/burns.html

In his 2017 American University of Paris commencement address, Burns recalls meeting and falling in love with Libby while both were American students in Paris, then returning there together four decades later.  
https://www.belfercenter.org/publication/nicholas-burns-gives-commencement-address-and-receives-honorary-degree-american

In his 2021 Senate confirmation statement, Burns thanks Libby and says they served the U.S. government overseas together in Mauritania, Egypt, Israel, Greece, and Belgium.  
https://www.foreign.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/102021_Burns_Testimony.pdf

**Speech use:** If a speech needs to widen from “Nick the Red Sox fan” to the actual life around the diplomat, Libby is not a decorative spouse reference. Burns repeatedly frames the overseas career as shared service.

### Libby’s own public-service record in Greece

Contemporaneous and later reporting identifies Elizabeth Baylies-Burns as a founder/instigator and honorary president of MDA Hellas, the Greek Muscular Dystrophy Association. The group opened a specialized neuromuscular-disease unit at Athens’s Aghia Sophia Children’s Hospital in 2002 and later expanded its work.  
https://www.ekathimerini.com/culture/9992/president-inaugurates-a-project-of-mda-hellas/  
https://musculardystrophynews.com/news/mda-hellas-gives-greek-sma-duchenne-patients-reason-to-hope/

A State Department feature from the Athens years also places Elizabeth Allen Baylies alongside Ambassador Burns in the embassy’s public cultural work.  
https://1997-2001.state.gov/publications/statemag/statemag_jul2000/feature5.html

**Speech use:** This supplies a substantive, independently checkable way to acknowledge Libby: civic and humanitarian work during the family’s diplomatic postings, not just attendance beside him.

### Libby in Burns’s speeches

Burns explicitly brings Libby into several public addresses. In his 2017 Remembrance Day speech he opens by thanking the hosts on behalf of **“my wife, Libby, and I.”** In his 2019 Versailles remarks, he uses Libby’s great-uncle Hervey Allen and the couple’s trip tracing Allen’s First World War route as the personal doorway into the history he is about to discuss.  
https://www.belfercenter.org/publication/remembrance-day-speech-requiem-great-war  
https://www.belfercenter.org/publication/remarks-ambassador-ret-nicholas-burns-conference-versailles-1919-2019

**Speech use:** These passages show Burns’s normal pattern when family belongs in a speech: concrete shared history first, then the larger public subject.

## Attribution boundary: do not manufacture a Libby Red Sox quote

For this research pass, I found strong primary-source Red Sox language from Nicholas Burns and strong independent material on Libby Burns, but **no reliable transcript or primary source in which Libby herself makes a Red Sox statement**. A 2021 Senate hearing, for example, places Libby in the room while Senator Ed Markey introduces Burns as a proud member of Red Sox Nation; that remains Markey’s statement, not hers.  
https://www.congress.gov/117/chrg/CHRG-117shrg47863/CHRG-117shrg47863.pdf

Until a direct recording, transcript, letter, or interview is located, do not put Red Sox words in Libby’s mouth. It is safe to say that she shared the long diplomatic life Burns describes, that she had her own public-service record, and that Burns’s Red Sox identity was a recurring public motif throughout that shared life.

## Best narrative sequence for a speech

A compact, evidence-backed arc is:

1. **1997 — suffering:** Burns is the “long suffering” fan and jokes that the Sox need resuscitation.
2. **2002 — hope:** he predicts the championship too early, which makes the later payoff more human.
3. **2004/2005 — redemption:** after the title, Burns explicitly uses the language of redemption.
4. **2013 — community:** the Sox become more than a team; they are one of the institutions that bind New England together.
5. **2019 — identity:** decades into diplomacy, Red Sox Nation is still his instant shorthand for home.
6. **Libby — the life around the joke:** pair the baseball thread with her shared Foreign Service life and her MDA Hellas work, while keeping every attribution exact.

That sequence gives the material an actual story: suffering → hope → redemption → community → durable identity, with Libby grounding the public persona in the longer shared life behind it.
