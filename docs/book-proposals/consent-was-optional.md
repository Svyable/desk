# Consent Was Optional

Working subtitle: Edgewood Arsenal and the Soldiers Who Became the Experiment

## Thesis

Edgewood Arsenal is a documented history of how a military institution can convert service, secrecy, scientific ambition, and incomplete disclosure into a system where consent exists on paper while voluntariness becomes difficult to locate. The book should reconstruct the program from records rather than folklore: what agents were tested, who authorized the work, what volunteers were told, what researchers believed they were learning, what harms were alleged or documented, and how later courts and investigations judged the bargain.

## Evidence posture

Keep four lanes visible: documented/declassified records; credible testimony and litigation records; disputed retrospective claims; synthesis. Do not collapse every Edgewood experiment into one protocol or imply every participant experienced the same exposure. A signature is evidence of a form, not automatically proof of informed consent.

## Narrative engine

Begin with the volunteer pipeline and the room in which military research turned a soldier into a subject. Move backward into Cold War chemical-warfare anxiety, then through psychochemicals, incapacitating agents, medical monitoring, secrecy, veterans' later attempts to learn what happened, and the changing law of human-subject research.

## Research targets

Prioritize National Academies/NRC reviews, Army records, GAO and congressional material, federal litigation, DoD human-research rules, and contemporaneous medical literature. Secondary reporting may guide discovery but should not carry disputed factual claims by itself.

## Defeat conditions

If the archival record cannot support a clean distinction between specific protocols, exposures, and participant populations, narrow the book rather than generalize. If a dramatic claim appears only in retrospective testimony, label it accordingly.
