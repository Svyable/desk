# Plutonium Patients

Working subtitle: Human Injection Experiments and the Birth of the Radiation-Consent Reckoning

## Thesis

The wartime and postwar plutonium injection experiments reveal a collision between urgent atomic medicine, secrecy, uncertain toxicology, and patients whose clinical vulnerability made them scientifically useful. The story should resist easy hindsight: reconstruct what researchers knew about plutonium at the time, why human metabolism data seemed necessary, and exactly where clinical care blurred into nontherapeutic experimentation.

## Evidence posture

Primary backbone: Department of Energy human-radiation records, Advisory Committee on Human Radiation Experiments, declassified Manhattan Project/Atomic Energy Commission material, medical publications, and surviving case documentation. Separate documented injections from broader radiation-experiment allegations.

## Narrative engine

Use individual cases only where records permit responsible reconstruction. The recurring object is the sample: injected material enters a body, excreta and tissues become measurements, coded identities enter papers, and decades later those codes are translated back into people.

## Research targets

ACHRE final report and oral histories; DOE Openness records; National Archives; Manhattan Project medical records; peer-reviewed plutonium biokinetics literature; federal hearings and settlements where relevant.

## Defeat conditions

Never treat a coded case as identified unless the archival linkage is solid. Avoid converting later ethical condemnation into claims about a researcher's private intent unless documented.
