# Guatemala, 1946

Working subtitle: Infection, Empire, and the Experiment America Tried to Forget

## Thesis

From 1946 to 1948, U.S.-supported researchers in Guatemala deliberately exposed vulnerable people to sexually transmitted infections in experiments that later became an official American scandal. The deeper book is about how a project that would have been ethically explosive at home crossed borders, institutions, languages, and hierarchies until human beings became experimental material.

## Evidence posture

Anchor the narrative in the Presidential Commission for the Study of Bioethical Issues, CDC archival review, original records where available, and subsequent litigation and scholarship. Distinguish deliberate exposure, inoculation, diagnostic work, and treatment studies. Avoid implying every subject was infected or that every researcher had identical knowledge.

## Narrative engine

Follow the paperwork outward: investigators, U.S. agencies, Guatemalan institutions, prisons, psychiatric facilities, soldiers, sex workers, and patients. Then follow it forward to rediscovery, apology, archival reconstruction, and the problem of accountability after perpetrators and many subjects are dead.

## Research targets

Presidential Commission report and appendices; CDC report; National Archives holdings; contemporaneous PHS material; Guatemalan archival work; court records; peer-reviewed historical scholarship.

## Defeat conditions

Do not infer consent status, infection status, or outcome for individuals unless the surviving record supports it. Where counts differ across official reconstructions, show the discrepancy.
