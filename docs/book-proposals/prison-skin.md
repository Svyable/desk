# Prison Skin

Working subtitle: Holmesburg, Human Testing, and the Market Price of Captive Bodies

## Thesis

At Holmesburg Prison, incarcerated men became a research population for dermatology, pharmaceuticals, chemicals, and other studies. The book's central problem is economic and institutional: what does consent mean when the research payment available to a prisoner can exceed ordinary prison earnings, while liberty itself is controlled by the institution hosting the experiment?

## Evidence posture

Differentiate protocols, sponsors, eras, and levels of risk. Use prison records, published studies, court material, congressional records, FDA/DoD records where applicable, and Allen Hornblum's historical work as a lead rather than sole authority for contested details.

## Narrative engine

The skin is both organ and marketplace. Follow how a prison became attractive to researchers, how subjects were recruited and paid, what was placed on or in their bodies, how academic and commercial incentives interacted, and why later ethical standards came to treat prisoners as a specially protected population.

## Research targets

University archives; PubMed-era publications and earlier journals; federal prisoner-research regulations; National Commission materials; court records; Philadelphia archives; sponsor records where obtainable.

## Defeat conditions

Avoid attributing a protocol to a sponsor without documentation. Do not flatten compensation into coercion by definition; examine the actual prison economy and recruitment conditions.
