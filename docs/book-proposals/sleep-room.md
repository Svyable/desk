# Sleep Room

Working subtitle: Ewen Cameron, Psychic Driving, and the CIA's Search for a Rewritable Mind

## Thesis

At Montreal's Allan Memorial Institute, psychiatrist Ewen Cameron pursued depatterning and psychic driving while a CIA front organization covertly funded part of his work under the MKULTRA umbrella. The book's job is to separate three stories usually fused into one: Cameron's own psychiatric theory, what patients actually experienced, and what the CIA knew, funded, or hoped the research might yield.

## Evidence posture

Primary anchors: declassified CIA material, Canadian government records, court files, institutional archives, Cameron's publications, and documented patient testimony. Never imply CIA direction of a specific treatment unless the record supports it. Funding is not identical to operational control.

## Narrative engine

Start inside the Sleep Room, but only with details supported by records. Move outward to postwar psychiatry, Cameron's reputation, depatterning, repetition, drugs and sleep, the Society for the Investigation of Human Ecology, MKULTRA funding channels, patients' later discovery, lawsuits, and compensation.

## Research targets

CIA CREST/declassified MKULTRA records; U.S. Senate Church-era and 1977 MKULTRA hearings; Canadian archival inquiries; court records; McGill/Allan Memorial material; Cameron's peer-reviewed publications.

## Defeat conditions

Reject claims of operational CIA control when only grant linkage exists. Do not manufacture patient scenes from generalized descriptions of the program.
