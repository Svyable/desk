# Project Sunshine

Working subtitle: Fallout, Human Tissue, and the Secret Measurement of the Atomic Age

## Thesis

Project Sunshine tried to measure global fallout by obtaining human tissue from around the world, especially bone, and testing it for radioactive contamination. Its unsettling power comes from a collision between legitimate scientific uncertainty and acquisition practices that could treat the dead, including children, as specimens whose families were outside the informational loop.

## Evidence posture

Anchor in declassified AEC records, ACHRE, congressional material, laboratory reports, and documented tissue-collection correspondence. Avoid repeating sensational claims about body-part acquisition unless traceable to records.

## Narrative engine

Follow strontium-90 from bomb cloud to soil, milk, bone, laboratory counter, and policy argument. The human body becomes a global dosimeter, while the logistics of obtaining specimens reveal what secrecy did to ordinary norms of permission.

## Research targets

DOE Openness collections; ACHRE; AEC meeting minutes and correspondence; UNSCEAR-era fallout science; St. Louis Baby Tooth Survey records; congressional investigations; peer-reviewed radiological-health literature.

## Defeat conditions

Do not conflate Project Sunshine with every tissue-collection program of the era. Track provenance specimen by specimen where the narrative depends on lack of consent.
