# Green Run

Working subtitle: Hanford, Iodine-131, and the Secret Weather Experiment Over Washington

## Thesis

In December 1949, Hanford deliberately processed unusually short-cooled fuel and released a large amount of iodine-131 and other radionuclides in an operation later known as the Green Run. The book asks why the release was conducted, what was actually measured, what communities were told, and how decades of secrecy changed the meaning of an experiment whose plume crossed inhabited land.

## Evidence posture

Use declassified Air Force/AEC/DOE documents, Hanford environmental records, CDC dose-reconstruction work, National Academies reviews where relevant, and contemporaneous meteorology. Distinguish confirmed purpose from competing interpretations of intelligence objectives.

## Narrative engine

Treat the plume as the protagonist: fuel leaves the reactor, iodine enters the stack, weather carries it across farms and towns, monitoring stations try to see it, secrecy buries the event, and later dose reconstruction tries to reverse the wind.

## Research targets

DOE Hanford collections; CDC Hanford Environmental Dose Reconstruction Project; National Archives; declassified intelligence/monitoring records; Washington State health material; peer-reviewed thyroid-dose and epidemiology literature.

## Defeat conditions

Do not claim the public was deliberately used as experimental subjects unless documentary evidence establishes that purpose. Keep release quantity and dose estimates tied to specific official reconstructions.
