# Prose Authoring Standard

This is the Desk's authoring-time quality bar for books, essays, narrative nonfiction, introductions, conclusions, and other voice-sensitive prose. It complements `.agents/skills/human-prose/SKILL.md` and applies before prose is considered ready for review.

The goal is not merely to remove obvious AI tells after drafting. The first authored version should already have a point of view, narrative movement, purposeful paragraphing, evidence proportionate to the claim, and the specificity and judgment of Sven Hardy Benson. Do not manufacture irregularity to simulate a human voice.

## Establish what this book can do

Before a new book or substantial expansion, state its central question, the
reader it addresses, and what that reader should understand differently by the
end. Identify the strongest plausible answer that competes with the book's
answer. A subject is not yet an argument: “a book about artificial intelligence”
does not explain why these chapters belong together or why this book is needed.

Compare the proposal with nearby Desk books and the relevant published work.
Name the difference in mechanism, evidence, perspective, period, or form. A new
title and a new set of examples do not establish a distinct book if the argument
and progression repeat an existing manuscript. Do not claim novelty from an
incomplete search. Narrow the claim to the comparison actually made.

Keep a short working account of these choices in the book's existing research
or planning notes when the task warrants it. Reuse that record across drafting
sessions; do not build a second outline bureaucracy. Update it when research
changes the book's direction. For a small edit, use the existing manuscript
rather than producing a new proposal.

## Design the movement of the whole book

Give each chapter a necessary job. Know what the reader brings into it, what
changes during it, and what becomes possible afterward. A chapter can establish
a mechanism, follow a consequence, overturn a comfortable explanation, or make
a previously abstract cost visible. It needs more than another setting in which
the same thesis can be announced.

Read the sequence for dependencies, escalation, relief, and return. If several
chapters can exchange places without changing anything, investigate whether the
book is accumulating entries rather than developing. This is a diagnostic, not
a ban on essay collections, reference works, or deliberately modular books.
Each form must earn its own arrangement.

Vary chapter scale and shape when the material calls for it. A concentrated
close reading, a historical sequence, a technical explanation, and a sustained
case can coexist. Do not force every chapter through anecdote, explanation,
three examples, objections, and slogan. Place difficult explanation where the
reader has a reason to want it. Let an unresolved question carry forward without
turning every ending into a trailer.

Plan the ending against the opening. It should fulfill, complicate, or honestly
revise the book's original promise. Returning to an earlier object or question
works when its meaning has changed; merely repeating the opening image does not
supply an ending.

## Protect the particular voice

Before a substantial writing pass, identify a few passages in the existing book
that work and note why: the precision of a distinction, a patient explanation,
a dry observation, a sudden change of scale, or a sentence that holds genuine
doubt. Use those passages to calibrate judgment and distance from the reader.
Do not reproduce their syntax as a house template.

Sven's voice need not sound identical across a mathematical argument, a history,
and a speculative essay. Preserve the stance appropriate to this book. Do not
invent first-person experience, interviews, access, convictions, or biography to
make the narrator more intimate or authoritative. Distinguish the narrator's
interpretation from what a source or participant said.

Allow wit, warmth, anger, hesitation, and surprise where the material earns
them. People should have constraints and purposes beyond illustrating the
thesis. After suffering or catastrophe, resist turning their experience into a
convenient inspirational lesson. Precision and restraint can carry emotion.

## Write the chapter, not the outline

Do not leave the scaffolding visible.

A chapter should not read like a sequence of bullets rewritten as prose. Avoid repeated one-sentence paragraphs that merely enumerate considerations, consecutive mini-headings disguised as topic sentences, and sections that move mechanically through `first`, `second`, `third`, or equivalent unnamed list logic.

Combine adjacent sentences when they belong to the same thought. Let a paragraph carry an argument far enough to develop pressure, qualification, causality, or consequence. Use a short paragraph when the break changes emphasis or pace, not because every sentence has been given its own stage.

A useful default is that paragraphs should contain movement: from fact to implication, scene to pressure, mechanism to consequence, claim to qualification, or question to evidence. A paragraph that only restates its opening sentence more elegantly is probably unfinished.

## Build narrative movement

Even analytical nonfiction needs a storyline. A chapter should change the reader's understanding as it proceeds.

Whenever the material permits, begin with something observable: an event, decision, object, institution, transaction, person, document, number, conflict, or constraint. Establish what is happening before announcing what it means. Preserve what participants knew at the time, what remained uncertain, and what changed.

Do not use anecdotes as decorative cold opens that are abandoned once the lecture begins. If a chapter opens on an auction, meeting, court case, experiment, filing, machine, policy decision, or person, carry that material far enough for it to do argumentative work.

Do not manufacture scenes, dialogue, motives, sensory detail, or biography. Story comes from sequence, stakes, causality, and uncertainty, not invented atmosphere.

## Earn abstraction

Move from concrete material toward principle rather than starting with the principle and filling in examples underneath it.

Do not announce a lesson before the evidence has created a need for it. Avoid generic declarations such as `this changes everything`, `the real lesson is`, `the key insight is`, or `what this teaches us` unless the sentence names something specific that the preceding material could not already say for itself.

Aphorisms should compress earned understanding. They should not function as connective tissue. If a memorable sentence could be moved into another business, technology, or self-help book unchanged, it is probably too generic.

## Prefer depth to catalogs

A list can clarify true categories. It should not substitute for thinking.

When several examples make the same point, keep the ones that deepen the argument and cut the rest. When several risks, causes, or policy options genuinely differ, explain the consequential differences rather than giving each item an identical sentence and moral.

Avoid long runs of rhetorical inventory: `X matters. Y matters. Z matters.` Avoid question inventories that merely restate the chapter's scope. Avoid presenting eight possible mechanisms when three developed ones establish the point and the others add no new consequence.

Do not force every chapter into a framework, taxonomy, scorecard, set of pillars, numbered model, or named protocol. Name only structures that genuinely compress recurring material.

## Control repetition across the book

A book may have recurring themes; it should not repeatedly rediscover them.

Before drafting a chapter, know what earlier chapters have already established. Do not re-explain the same distinction, caveat, historical analogy, or definition unless the new chapter changes its meaning or applies it in a materially different setting.

Watch especially for repeated book-level conclusions: `the system matters`, `there is no magic threshold`, `the answer is not X but Y`, `the future is uncertain`, `institutions matter`, `the wall is...`, or equivalent thematic refrains. Repetition should create resonance, not evidence that chapters were generated independently.

When a concept must recur, advance it. Add a new mechanism, exception, cost, actor, historical case, or consequence.

## Use symmetry sparingly

Parallelism is a tool, not a default rhythm.

Avoid pages built from repeated constructions such as `not X but Y`, `X does this; Y does that`, `one side... the other side...`, `the first... the second...`, or a sequence of perfectly balanced oppositions. Keep the strongest contrast and let surrounding prose use ordinary syntax.

Do not use em dashes as automatic drama, qualification, or sentence glue. Use commas, periods, parentheses, colons, or a rewritten sentence when they express the relationship more naturally. An em dash should earn the interruption.

## Make formatting serve prose

For manuscript chapters, follow the repository rule: one `# Title` heading followed by paragraphs. Do not introduce subheads, YAML, callout boxes, faux slides, checklist formatting, or decorative emphasis unless the surrounding form explicitly requires it.

Do not create a visual list by putting each short sentence in its own paragraph. Paragraph breaks are part of meaning and rhythm.

Italics, bold, equations, block quotes, and inline labels should be rare enough to retain force. Do not use typography to manufacture importance the prose has not earned.

## Preserve uncertainty and causal honesty

Distinguish what is known from what is inferred, disputed, projected, alleged, or uncertain. Do not turn a plausible mechanism into a universal law because the cleaner sentence sounds better.

Keep competing explanations alive when the evidence requires them. In historical writing, preserve the difference between what participants knew then and what hindsight reveals now. In science, technology, finance, policy, and intelligence-adjacent material, do not let narrative momentum erase uncertainty.

Do not use false authority. Never invent a quotation, attribution, consensus, statistic, causal claim, or source to make prose feel finished.

## Let research put the argument at risk

Research should be able to change the outline, not merely furnish it. Develop
the strongest relevant counterexample far enough to test the proposed
explanation. Show what it forces the book to narrow, distinguish, abandon, or
leave unresolved. Do not select only objections that are easy to dismiss or
quarantine important counterevidence in a ritual caveat paragraph.

For consequential claims, preserve usable links and concrete source locations
in the research trail, and give readers a citation where they need one. Match
the authority of the source to the claim. Distinguish a verified or declassified
record, firsthand testimony, secondary reporting, a disputed allegation, and
speculation. An official document can establish that someone made a claim
without establishing that the claim is true.

A promising scene with insufficient evidence is a research task, not permission
to reconstruct dialogue or atmosphere. Keep open factual questions in working
notes, linked to the affected passage. Do not quietly convert a placeholder into
confident prose during a smoothing pass.

## Make explanations rewarding to follow

Introduce a term when the reader needs it; show the mechanism before relying on
the label. Track who acts, what changes, who bears the cost, and which constraint
makes the outcome possible. In quantitative material, explain the denominator,
baseline, units, and scale that give a number meaning. A correct statistic can
still mislead when its comparison is missing.

Use analogies to illuminate a specific relationship and identify their limit
when it matters. Do not let an analogy become evidence for the claim it was
introduced to explain. For mathematics and technical subjects, a worked example,
counterexample, or derivation may provide the movement a scene supplies in
history. An abstract opening can be right when the problem itself creates
curiosity; do not bolt on a human anecdote solely to obey a default.

## Make specificity do the work

Prefer concrete nouns, actual institutions, named mechanisms, dates, amounts, documents, constraints, and decisions when they are supported by the source material.

Specificity should change understanding, not merely decorate the page. A named filing matters if its language or timing changes the argument. A number matters if scale matters. A person's role matters if it explains why a decision could or could not be made.

Do not add sensory detail or personal texture that is not in the evidence.

## Vary cadence for meaning

Sentence and paragraph length should follow the thought.

Let a longer sentence carry history, qualification, causal sequence, or accumulation when those elements belong together. Use a short sentence when it genuinely lands something the preceding prose has earned. Avoid strings of short declarative sentences used as synthetic gravitas.

Fragments can work when they belong to the voice. They should not become a formatting habit.

Read the page for repeated setup-pivot-ending shapes. If every paragraph introduces a point, reverses it, then closes with an aphorism, vary the architecture.

## End chapters without stamping them

A chapter does not need to restate its thesis three times before stopping.

Avoid summary ladders, policy checklists, motivational instructions, teaser language, and a final polished slogan that repeats what the reader already understands. If the chapter's evidence has done its work, the ending may narrow to one consequence, unresolved tension, concrete return, or forward pressure into the next chapter.

Do not confuse closure with neatness. Some subjects should end with uncertainty still alive.

## Revise in the order that changes the outcome

First read for the book's promise, structure, and missing evidence. Decide what
to cut, combine, move, or investigate before polishing sentences. A beautifully
edited chapter can still be unnecessary. Scope those changes to the authorized
task; record an out-of-scope structural problem rather than rewriting adjacent
chapters without permission.

Then revise the chapter's argument and movement. Reverse-outline the actual
pages briefly: what does each passage contribute? Look for a missing causal
step, an example that never develops, a counterargument answered too cheaply,
and stretches that repeat a conclusion. Preserve a useful complication even
when removing it would make the chapter smoother.

Only then perform the paragraph and sentence passes. Cut duplication before
compressing distinct ideas. Prefer an exact verb to decorative intensity. Check
pronoun references, transitions, time shifts, overloaded sentences, mixed
metaphors, and unintended changes in narrator distance. Read selected passages
aloud, or attend explicitly to their sound and syntax, to find monotony and
awkwardness. Do not mechanically alternate sentence lengths.

Finish by reading the changed material in sequence with its neighbors. Check
that a moved explanation still arrives before it is needed, a recurring example
has consistent facts, and the ending follows from the chapter now on the page.
A vocabulary swap is not a developmental edit; removing every unusual sentence
is not an improvement in voice.

## Match the review to the stage

An exploratory fragment may be unresolved. A working chapter should have a
purpose and make its evidence gaps visible. A complete draft should sustain its
argument across the actual manuscript, though revision may remain. A release
candidate requires a whole-book editorial read of that edition, including the
middle and ending. Do not infer release readiness from a strong opening or a
sample of individually polished chapters.

For a substantial revision or release review, record a concise editorial note
in the existing research/review location. Identify the manuscript scope or
commit reviewed, the main weakness addressed, specific passages that support
the judgment, and unresolved problems with their next action. Explain why a
suggested cut or rewrite improves the reader's experience. Generic praise such
as “compelling and well researched” is not evidence of a completed review.
For a small edit, a concrete PR description is sufficient; no extra artifact is
required.

Separate release-blocking defects from optional taste preferences. Examples of
blockers include an unsupported central claim, a broken argument, substantial
repeated chapters, unfinished passages, or failure to deliver the book's stated
promise. A preference for a different metaphor or rhythm is not automatically a
blocker. Review can be performed by an agent; this standard adds no mandatory
human-review or external-approval step.

Word counts, readability scores, phrase searches, and AI detectors cannot
certify literary quality. Mechanical checks establish only what they measure.
Keep applicable length requirements, but never pad to pass them. If the evidence
cannot sustain the commissioned scope, report the shortfall and the substantive
work needed. Do not label a manuscript publication-ready because its files,
length, and metadata pass validation.

## Minimum authoring check

Before treating voice-sensitive prose as ready, verify all of the following:

- The chapter has a discernible progression rather than an expanded outline.
- Paragraph breaks reflect meaning, not a habit of placing every sentence on its own line.
- Adjacent choppy lines have been combined where one fuller paragraph carries the thought better.
- Examples are few enough to receive useful specificity.
- Claims are no more certain than the evidence.
- No quote, attribution, statistic, or biographical detail was invented.
- The chapter does not repeatedly use canned symmetry, em dashes, triads, question inventories, or aphorisms as scaffolding.
- Formatting remains prose-first and consistent with the manuscript rules.
- The chapter advances rather than restates earlier chapters.
- The ending trusts the reader and does not stamp a slogan onto material that already landed.
- The result sounds specifically like Sven Hardy Benson working through this subject, not like a generic editor or model demonstrating good prose.

Use these questions to locate work, not to award a numerical quality score.
For a completed drafting or revision task, address material defects within scope
before handing it off. For an explicitly exploratory task, preserve useful
unfinished work and state what remains unresolved. Release readiness also
requires the whole-book review described above and the existing release gates.
