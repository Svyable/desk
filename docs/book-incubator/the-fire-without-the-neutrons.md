# The Fire Without the Neutrons

Working subtitle: **Aneutronic Fusion, Charged-Particle Exhaust, and Why the Cleaner Reaction Is Usually the Harder One**

Fusion is already a field where public discussion compresses half a century of plasma physics into one word. Aneutronic fusion makes the compression worse. The phrase suggests a reaction that gives up fusion energy without bathing the machine in neutrons. That promise is real enough to be scientifically attractive and misleading enough to be dangerous.

DIA's AAWSAP technical-review material lists *Aneutronic Fusion Propulsion I* and *II* among the commissioned reports. The subject sits on firmer physics than warp metrics and on harsher engineering than many fusion headlines admit. Reactions such as proton-boron-11 can place much of their primary energy into charged particles rather than neutrons, potentially allowing magnetic manipulation or direct energy conversion. The catch is that the required plasma conditions are much more demanding than for deuterium-tritium fusion, and secondary reactions and impurities can still produce neutrons and radiation.

This book is about reaction choice as system design. D-T is “easy” only by fusion standards, but it has the largest practical reaction rate at comparatively accessible temperatures and pays for that convenience with 14.1 MeV neutrons. D-He3 and p-B11 move the neutron problem while raising temperature, confinement, fuel-supply, bremsstrahlung and reactivity problems. The clean exhaust fantasy becomes a plasma-distribution problem.

A possible spine: Coulomb barriers; fusion cross sections; Lawson criterion and its limits; D-T as baseline; neutron damage; D-He3; helium-3 scarcity; p-B11; resonance structure; non-Maxwellian distributions; bremsstrahlung losses; alpha heating; direct conversion; magnetic mirrors; field-reversed configurations; dense-plasma-focus claims; inertial approaches; beam-target schemes; propulsion-specific metrics; the DIA studies; why military/space analysts care about charged exhaust; and what experimental milestone would actually distinguish a propulsion-relevant aneutronic concept from another impressive plasma shot.

Evidence boundary: aneutronic reactions are established nuclear physics, and multiple fusion programs study advanced fuels. No aneutronic fusion propulsion system has been demonstrated. “Aneutronic” usually means neutron-poor relative to D-T, not literally zero neutrons under all realistic operating conditions. A high ion temperature or fusion-product detection is not equivalent to net power or a usable rocket.
