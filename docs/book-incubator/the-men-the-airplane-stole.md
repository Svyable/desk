# The Men the Airplane Stole

Working subtitle: **Operation COLDFEET, the Fulton Skyhook, and the Arctic Intelligence Mission That Looked Like Cinema**

In 1962 two men parachuted onto an abandoned Soviet drifting ice station in the Arctic, collected documents and equipment, and then left without a runway. Their extraction method was a cable suspended from a balloon. A modified aircraft flew in, caught the line on a yoke mounted to its nose, and hauled the men bodily into the air before winching them aboard.

The Fulton surface-to-air recovery system is the rare spy gadget that looks less plausible after you see a diagram. Yet CIA records document its use during Operation COLDFEET. The mission exploited a simple accident of polar life: Soviet ice stations sometimes had to be abandoned when their runways fractured or drift conditions changed. What was inaccessible by conventional landing became an intelligence opportunity if the United States could put people down and remove them another way.

The book's deeper subject is the temporary window between impossible and routine. Helicopters, satellites, long-range aircraft, and modern rescue methods eventually make some technologies unnecessary. For a brief period, though, a balloon, a cable, a harness, and a carefully flown airplane solved a unique extraction problem.

A possible spine: drifting ice as territory; Soviet polar stations; how a station becomes abandoned; the intelligence value of trash and instruments; Robert Fulton; earlier airborne pickup systems; testing on cargo; testing on people; locating the target; the shift from NP-9 to NP-8; parachuting onto a moving ice floe; what the team collected; weather and extraction timing; the physics of being snagged by an airplane; the successful recovery; what the material revealed; later military use; Hollywood borrowing the image; and why cinematic technologies sometimes begin as ugly logistics.

Evidence boundary: Operation COLDFEET, the two-man insertion, abandoned Soviet station, recovered material, and Skyhook extraction are documented. Specific intelligence gains should be tied to the declassified mission history rather than repeated from secondary legend.
