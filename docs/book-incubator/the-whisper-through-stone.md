# The Whisper Through Stone

Working subtitle: **High-Frequency Gravitational Waves and the Attempt to Build a Signal the Earth Cannot Block**

Gravitational waves are no longer hypothetical. LIGO turned kilometer-scale spacetime strain into data. But the waves LIGO observes are astrophysical, low in frequency by radio standards, and generated by absurdly energetic cosmic systems. A different literature asks whether much higher-frequency gravitational waves might be generated and detected in the laboratory and, eventually, used for communication or sensing.

DIA commissioned a report titled *High-Frequency Gravitational Wave Communications*. Its summary reviews proposed generators and detectors and even explores communication through the Earth. The existence of the report is extraordinary. The engineering claims inside it require much more skepticism than the title.

The book's center is the coupling problem. Gravity is weak. An electromagnetic transmitter works because charges couple strongly to electromagnetic fields. A gravitational-wave transmitter must move stress-energy in a way that radiates appreciable curvature, and ordinary laboratory masses are pathetic compared with binary neutron stars. Raising frequency changes some scaling relationships, but it does not repeal the tiny gravitational coupling constant.

A possible spine: linearized general relativity; quadrupole radiation; why there is no gravitational dipole antenna analogous to radio; strain; astrophysical detections; frequency bands from pulsar timing to LIGO to proposed high-frequency searches; laboratory generator concepts; piezoelectric arrays; electromagnetic conversion proposals; inverse Gertsenshtein effects; detector noise; quantum limits; claimed Li-Baker architectures; null searches; indirect cosmological constraints; “through-Earth” communication geometry; link budgets; the DIA report; what a serious technology-forecast document should do with a mechanism whose principle is allowed but coupling is ruinous; and a final engineering audit in watts, strain, aperture and integration time.

Evidence boundary: gravitational waves are established, and high-frequency gravitational-wave theory/detection is a legitimate research topic. No practical laboratory HFGW communication system has been demonstrated. Specific detector sensitivities or megabit-through-Earth claims in forecasting literature should be independently derived, not inherited as facts because DIA printed them.

Starting points:

- DIA, *High-Frequency Gravitational Wave Communications*: https://www.dia.mil/FOIA/FOIA-Electronic-Reading-Room/FileId/170033/
- LIGO/Virgo/KAGRA literature for experimentally established gravitational-wave detection and detector noise budgets.
- Peer-reviewed high-frequency gravitational-wave reviews and experimental searches, with particular attention to whether proposed conversion mechanisms satisfy complete energy and noise accounting.

If the book works, the reader should finish respecting gravitational-wave physics more and the proposed telephone less.
