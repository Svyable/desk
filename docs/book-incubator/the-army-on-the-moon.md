# The Army on the Moon

Working subtitle: **Project HORIZON and the 1959 Plan for a Military Outpost Beyond Earth**

In 1959 the United States Army completed a multi-volume study for Project HORIZON, a proposal to establish a manned lunar outpost. The Army's own historical center still points researchers to the original study. The plan imagined an initial twelve-man base, a heavy launch cadence, underground or protected habitation, nuclear power, logistics chains, communications, medical capability, and a military rationale for getting there before a rival could.

The dates are what make it uncanny. The study was produced before anyone had orbited the Earth, before Apollo existed, and before the United States had accumulated much practical human spaceflight experience. Yet the documents move quickly from “can we reach the Moon?” to supply schedules, construction, command relationships, and the possibility of treating lunar terrain as strategic ground.

This is not a book about a secret Moon base. It is the opposite: a book about how bureaucracies make detailed plans for futures that never arrive. HORIZON was real as planning, not as construction. That distinction opens a richer subject—the moment when military institutions had to decide whether space was merely another operating domain or an entirely different political object.

A possible spine: Sputnik and institutional panic; Redstone Arsenal; the Army Ballistic Missile Agency; von Braun's team; why the Army wanted a lunar role; the HORIZON study; the proposed schedule; cargo mass and launch cadence; construction concepts; power; communications; defense thinking; the logistics arithmetic; cost; the creation of NASA; transfer of Army space capabilities; civilian versus military space policy; the Outer Space Treaty that came later; what Apollo borrowed from the same industrial base; the moon-base plans that followed; and the odd persistence of lunar military thinking in every new era of strategic competition.

Evidence boundary: Project HORIZON and the Army study are official and documented. No HORIZON base was constructed. The manuscript should separate what the study proposed from what political leadership authorized, funded, or expected to happen.
