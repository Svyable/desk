# The Glacier Fortress

Working subtitle: **Camp Century, Project Iceworm, and the Cold War Plan to Hide a Missile Network Inside Greenland**

The United States Army built a working base under the Greenland ice sheet. It cut tunnels, installed laboratories and living spaces, supplied the settlement across the ice, and powered Camp Century with a portable nuclear reactor. Behind the public engineering and science story sat Project Iceworm: a much larger Army concept for dispersing strategic missiles through an enormous under-ice tunnel network.

The deep joke was on the planners. Ice looks solid on a map. A glacier is moving architecture. It creeps, deforms, closes tunnels, shifts loads, and quietly turns geometric certainty into maintenance. Camp Century demonstrated that people could build and live beneath the ice. It also produced the kind of engineering evidence that made the larger fantasy much harder to sustain.

The book should avoid the lazy reveal that Camp Century was “fake science.” Real science came out of the place, including ice-core work that became scientifically important long after the military logic evaporated. The stronger story is dual use in its most literal form: an installation can be scientific, strategic, diplomatic, and experimental all at once without any one label exhausting the truth.

A possible spine: why Greenland mattered; sovereignty and the 1951 defense agreement; snow roads and overland logistics; trench construction; Camp Century as a lived place; the PM-2A reactor; ice mechanics; the Army's strategic problem; Project Iceworm and mobile basing; interservice rivalry; Denmark and the consent problem; what planners knew about ice motion; tunnel deformation; why Polaris and Minuteman changed the comparison; closure; what was left beneath the ice; climate change and remobilized waste; and the strange afterlife of a military base as a climate archive.

Evidence boundary: Camp Century and Project Iceworm are well documented. The missile network was proposed, not deployed. The Danish government was not simply a co-author of the secret missile scheme; sovereignty and disclosure are central to the story.
