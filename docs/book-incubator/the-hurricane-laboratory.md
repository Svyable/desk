# The Hurricane Laboratory

Working subtitle: **Project STORMFURY and the Years the United States Tried to Weaken Hurricanes on Purpose**

From 1962 to 1983 the United States ran Project STORMFURY, an organized research program aimed at hurricane modification. Aircraft seeded selected storms with silver iodide under a specific physical hypothesis: stimulate convection outside the existing eyewall, encourage a broader replacement eyewall, and reduce the strongest winds.

The early results were seductive. On some seeded days, measured winds decreased. If the story stopped there, STORMFURY would sound like lost weather-control technology. It did not stop there. Better observations of unmodified hurricanes later showed that storms naturally undergo concentric eyewall cycles capable of producing the same kind of changes. Researchers also learned that hurricane convection generally lacked enough supercooled water for the seeding mechanism to work as originally imagined.

That reversal makes this a much better book than a triumphal weather-control story. STORMFURY is a case study in causal inference performed on systems too large to rerun. You cannot make two identical hurricanes, seed one, leave the other alone, and compare the result. Nature keeps changing the control group.

A possible spine: weather modification before hurricanes; cloud-seeding physics; Project Cirrus as prehistory; the birth of STORMFURY; aircraft and instrumentation; Esther; Beulah; Debbie; what observers thought they saw; the ethics of intervening in a storm that might make landfall; international politics; the scarcity of suitable experimental hurricanes; improved radar and aircraft observations; concentric eyewall cycles; the collapse of the original physical premise; the project's end; later geoengineering debates; causal inference in one-off natural systems; and why failed intervention can still produce excellent science.

Evidence boundary: STORMFURY, the seeding flights, the original hypothesis, apparent wind reductions, and later scientific reassessment are all documented by NOAA. The book should explicitly reject claims that the project demonstrated reliable hurricane control.
