## Archival priorities

1. Original Whitecoat protocol files, consent forms, volunteer briefing materials, rejection/refusal records, adverse-event reports, treatment schedules, and study rosters.
2. Army Medical Unit / USAMRIID annual reports and command histories spanning 1954–1973.
3. Fort Detrick records identifying the administrative boundary between defensive medical studies and offensive biological-warfare programs.
4. Seventh-day Adventist General Conference, church periodicals, chaplain correspondence, and participant-recruitment materials.
5. Study-level publications for tularemia, Q fever, vector research, and candidate vaccines.
6. Participant oral histories with protocol identification wherever possible.

## Claim boundary

The current draft does not claim that Whitecoat was wholly coercive, wholly exemplary, or free of injury. It treats more than two thousand conscientious-objector volunteers, documented deliberate infectious-disease research, later reuse of Whitecoat data, and the 2005 follow-up as established starting points. Program-wide claims about refusal without penalty, exact consent quality, church pressure, individual injuries, and protocol-specific risk require further archival proof.
