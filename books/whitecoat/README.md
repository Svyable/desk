## Thesis

Operation Whitecoat is usually flattened into one of two stories: proof that military human experimentation can be ethical, or another Cold War program in which the state used people as instruments. The surviving record is harder. More than two thousand conscientious objectors, most of them Seventh-day Adventists, entered an Army medical-research system in which some men deliberately inhaled or received infectious agents, while investigators developed vaccines, treatment schedules, and challenge models for diseases with military significance. The central question is not whether the volunteers signed forms. It is whether consent remains voluntary when every available choice is nested inside military service, religious community, institutional expectation, and a state biodefense mission.
