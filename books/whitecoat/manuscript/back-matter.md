# Back Matter

Whitecoat should remain open to revision as more protocol records, consent forms, correspondence, participant files, and church materials are recovered. The manuscript's working distinction is simple: the program's documented voluntariness is evidence, not a verdict. Its military setting is a source of ethical pressure, not proof of coercion. Its useful science is benefit, not retroactive justification.

The decisive future work is archival. A mature edition should reconstruct study-level recruitment, consent language, challenge method, expected risk, actual adverse events, treatment timing, refusal behavior, and later reuse of results. It should also compare Whitecoat directly with contemporary civilian challenge research rather than relying on notorious abuses as the only benchmark.

No claim in this book requires that every Whitecoat subject understood or experienced the program identically. Where participant testimony conflicts, the conflict belongs in the record.
