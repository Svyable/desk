# 17 — Theories That Survive Contact

Fast radio burst theory has never suffered from a shortage of imagination.

Compact-object mergers, neutron stars, magnetars, black-hole environments, cosmic strings, exotic compact objects, asteroid interactions, plasma structures, and artificial transmitters have all appeared somewhere in the conceptual ecology. Most hypotheses can be made to produce a short burst on paper if enough free parameters are available.

The question is what survives contact with the whole record.

Repetition penalized universal cataclysm models. Host localizations penalized Galactic explanations for cosmological events. SGR 1935+2154 rewarded magnetar models. Old stellar environments penalized the narrow claim that all burst-capable magnetars must be newborn products of massive-star collapse. Polarization and rotation measures constrain magneto-ionic environments. Burst substructure constrains coherence and source-region timescales. Multiwavelength non-detections limit some energetic counterparts.

Within magnetar theories, the remaining fight is partly about where coherent radio emission is produced. Magnetospheric models place the action close to the neutron star. Shock models place it farther out, when relativistic ejecta interact with surrounding material and can drive maser-like coherent emission. Both families can be elaborated. Both must eventually answer the same observational ledger.

The decisive data may not be a single spectacular burst. They may be correlations: radio energy versus X-ray energy, delay distributions between high-energy and radio components, polarization-angle behavior, repetition statistics following magnetar flares, environment dependence, or source-age trends.

Theories become valuable when they tell observers what to catch next.

A model that explains every burst after it occurs is a vocabulary. A model that predicts which burst should not occur is physics.
