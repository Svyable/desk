# 13 — The Telescope Makes the Population

A telescope survey is not a census taker walking door to door. It is a net with a particular mesh dragged through a particular part of parameter space.

CHIME listens at lower frequencies than many earlier FRB surveys. Parkes had a different beam, sensitivity, sky exposure, and frequency range. ASKAP, DSA, FAST, Arecibo, MeerKAT, and interferometric arrays each carve the transient sky differently. Pulse width matters because finite time resolution dilutes narrow events and scattering broadens others. Spectral structure matters because an intrinsically bright burst can land mostly outside the band. Dispersion-search limits matter. RFI excision can remove astrophysical events that look too much like interference. Trigger thresholds matter.

The observed FRB population is therefore the convolution of the sky with machinery.

This is not unique to FRBs. What is unusual is how violently a millisecond transient exposes the issue. There is no leisurely source to return to unless it repeats. Voltage buffers overwrite themselves. Real-time pipelines have to decide which moments deserve preservation. A bad threshold can erase history.

CHIME’s catalog work explicitly used simulated injections to estimate detection efficiency across burst parameters. That methodological fact is as important as the headline number of events. A raw catalog tells us what the telescope found. An injection-recovery framework begins telling us what it missed.

Selection effects are sometimes discussed as nuisances after the interesting physics. Here they are part of the physics because population claims feed directly into source rates, energetics, and evolutionary channels.

If narrowband repeaters are easier or harder to see in a given instrument, the apparent repeater fraction changes. If high-DM bursts are scattered into widths that fall below sensitivity, cosmological rate inferences shift. If one survey dwells on the same sky daily while another covers vast area shallowly, repetition becomes instrument-dependent.

The universe did not produce “536 CHIME bursts.” It produced an unknown number of radio events, of which a particular system, under a particular configuration, preserved 536 for a catalog.

That sentence should sit above every population plot.
