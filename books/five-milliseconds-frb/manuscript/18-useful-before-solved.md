# 18 — Useful Before Solved

The first fast radio burst was a question.

The mature field is a set of tools built around a question that has not completely gone away.

We still do not possess a universally accepted microscopic emission mechanism for every FRB. We do not know whether all apparent subclasses share one engine. We do not know the full distribution of formation channels, active lifetimes, repetition rates, or environmental conditions.

And yet the phenomenon is no longer scientifically fragile.

Perytons were identified and reproduced. A population appeared. A source repeated. A repeater was localized to a galaxy. Magnetars demonstrated FRB-like behavior in our own Galaxy. Large surveys measured population structure. Old stellar environments widened the progenitor problem. Localized bursts became probes of the intergalactic medium.

The mystery became productive.

That may be the most useful correction FRBs offer to the culture surrounding anomalies. People often imagine only two stable states: debunked or explained. If an observation is not false, then eventually it should collapse into one satisfying mechanism. Until then, proponents treat uncertainty as permission and skeptics treat uncertainty as weakness.

Real science has more rooms than that.

A phenomenon can be real but heterogeneous. A source class can be identified while its radiation physics remains disputed. A contaminant can mimic one signature but fail others. A mechanism can work for some members of a class and not yet all. A transient can be useful as a probe before its engine is fully understood.

This is why I do not want to end with the usual sentence that “the mystery remains.” It is true and almost content-free.

The better ending is this:

The pulse lasted five milliseconds.

We have been learning how to deserve it ever since.
