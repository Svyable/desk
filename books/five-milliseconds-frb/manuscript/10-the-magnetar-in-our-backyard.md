# 10 — The Magnetar in Our Backyard

On April 28, 2020, the argument moved into the Milky Way.

SGR 1935+2154, a known Galactic magnetar, produced a millisecond radio burst so bright that it occupied the same conceptual territory as the extragalactic FRB population. STARE2 measured an enormous fluence. CHIME/FRB saw the event in its band. High-energy activity accompanied the radio burst. For the first time, the field had an FRB-like event tied directly to an object class long suspected of being capable of the necessary violence.

This was not the end of the mystery. It was the end of one kind of mystery.

Before 2020, magnetars were plausible engines because they combine compactness, colossal magnetic energy, crustal and magnetospheric activity, and observed high-energy bursts. After SGR 1935+2154, the statement became stronger: at least one magnetar can produce radio emission in the FRB phenomenological neighborhood.

The distinction between “can” and “does all the time” is where the science lives.

FRB 200428 was less energetic than many cosmological bursts. It arrived with X-ray activity, whereas many extragalactic FRBs lack detected high-energy counterparts. Different magnetars do not produce identical radio behavior. The same source can emit across a wide energy range. Selection effects are ferocious because a burst easy to detect inside our Galaxy could disappear below threshold at hundreds of megaparsecs.

A magnetar label also leaves the radiation mechanism open. One family of models places coherent emission in the magnetosphere, where magnetic reconnection, pair plasmas, and curvature-like processes can operate. Another family invokes relativistic ejecta slamming into surrounding material and generating synchrotron-maser emission in a shock. SGR 1935+2154 improved the prior for magnetar-powered models without making those models equivalent.

Popular accounts love a solved mystery because the phrase closes cleanly. The April 2020 event deserved a different sentence: **the source class problem narrowed dramatically, while the emission-mechanism and population problems remained alive.**

That is more interesting anyway.

A field does not mature by replacing a question mark with a noun. It matures by making the remaining question marks smaller, more local, and more expensive for theories to evade.

The magnetar in our backyard did exactly that.
