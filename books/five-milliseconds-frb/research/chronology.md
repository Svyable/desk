# Chronology

- **24 July 2001** — Parkes Multibeam Pulsar Survey records the event later called FRB 010724 / the Lorimer burst.
- **2007** — Lorimer et al. publish the bright, dispersed, millisecond event and infer an extragalactic interpretation as the leading explanation.
- **2010–2011** — perytons emerge as a troublesome class of terrestrial, swept-frequency radio transients with superficial FRB resemblance.
- **2013** — Thornton et al. report four additional bursts, turning a singular curiosity into a population problem and strengthening the cosmological interpretation.
- **2015** — Petroff et al. reproduce Parkes perytons through microwave-oven door opening during magnetron shutdown and show important distinctions from FRB 010724.
- **2016** — Spitler et al. report repetition from FRB 121102, ruling out any universal model in which every FRB destroys its source.
- **2017** — Chatterjee et al. localize FRB 121102 directly to an extragalactic host environment.
- **2018–2019** — CHIME/FRB begins transforming the field through high event rates and a relatively uniform survey population.
- **28 April 2020** — SGR 1935+2154 produces an exceptionally bright Galactic radio burst detected by STARE2 and CHIME/FRB, accompanied by high-energy activity and demonstrating that magnetars can generate FRB-like emission.
- **May 2020** — Macquart et al. use localized FRBs to constrain the cosmic baryon inventory.
- **2021** — first CHIME/FRB catalog publishes 536 bursts detected in its initial survey interval, including repeated bursts from known repeating sources.
- **2022** — FRB 20200120E is localized to a globular cluster in M81, challenging a simple picture in which all relevant magnetars must be very young core-collapse products.
- **2025** — FRB 20240209A is associated with the outskirts of a quiescent old galaxy, further expanding plausible formation environments.
- **2025** — CHIME Outrigger-era localizations demonstrate a new precision regime, allowing individual galactic environments to become stronger discriminants.
