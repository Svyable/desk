# Contradictions and Incentive Audit

## Contradiction 1: “The sweep proves space” versus perytons
A cold-plasma-like frequency sweep is strong evidence only when the full form, beam behavior, repetition pattern, instrument context, and interference environment agree. Perytons matter because morphology alone was not enough.

## Contradiction 2: singular catastrophe versus repetition
The Lorimer burst invited one-off catastrophic models partly because no further bursts were found in substantial follow-up. FRB 121102 later showed that at least some sources survive repeated bursts. The mistake would be retroactively treating the original inference as foolish; it was a reasonable conditional inference from limited data.

## Contradiction 3: magnetars explain FRBs versus old environments
SGR 1935+2154 supplied direct evidence that magnetars can produce FRB-like emission. Globular-cluster and quiescent-host events complicate only the narrowest young-magnetar formation story, not the entire magnetar family of explanations.

## Contradiction 4: repeaters versus nonrepeaters
Catalogs show statistical differences, but “nonrepeater” often means “not observed to repeat under a finite cadence, threshold, frequency coverage, and exposure.” Taxonomic language can smuggle observing limits into ontology.

## Contradiction 5: mystery versus utility
The source mechanism remains debated, yet FRBs already probe ionized matter along cosmological sight lines. A phenomenon can be mechanistically open and instrumentally mature.

## Incentives

- **Discovery incentives:** a new astronomical class is career-making; researchers have incentives to establish novelty.
- **Skepticism incentives:** extraordinary transients are vulnerable to RFI embarrassment; observatories have strong incentives to rule out terrestrial contamination.
- **Theory incentives:** dozens of source models can flourish when observations are sparse; publication rewards novelty more readily than quiet elimination.
- **Instrument incentives:** new arrays are often justified partly by high-impact transient science; population forecasts can influence funding narratives.
- **Media incentives:** “alien signal” and “mystery solved” framings both compress uncertainty and overstate closure.

The book should not imply bad faith where normal scientific incentives and sparse data suffice.
