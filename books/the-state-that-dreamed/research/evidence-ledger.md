# Evidence Ledger

## Class 1 — verified / declassified / documented

- U.S. intelligence and defense organizations funded and conducted remote-viewing/anomalous-cognition research and operational tasking across the 1970s–1995 period.
- SRI and later SAIC performed government-sponsored controlled experiments.
- Targ and Puthoff published SRI results in *Nature* in 1974.
- Marks and Kammann published methodological criticism in *Nature* in 1978 identifying ordinary informational cues in judging material from early experiments.
- The National Research Council's 1988 *Enhancing Human Performance* review found little or no support for remote viewing's usefulness.
- AIR's 1995 evaluation separated laboratory research from operational utility.
- Jessica Utts concluded the reviewed experimental record supported anomalous cognition; Ray Hyman disputed that inference.
- AIR's operational assessment found reports vague/inconsistent on specifics and concluded remote viewing failed to produce actionable intelligence warranting continuation.
- The Transparent Psi Project (2023) preregistered an adversarial replication of a Bem precognition paradigm and did not reproduce the claimed effect.
- A 2025 PLOS ONE meta-experimental replication project likewise did not establish the claimed precognitive effect.

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete

- Some later SRI/SAIC experimental series showed statistical departures from chance under improved controls.
- Certain operational sessions, including famous Soviet-site cases, contained correspondences contemporaneous participants regarded as striking.
- U.S. intelligence had genuine reasons to believe Soviet/Eastern Bloc institutions were interested in parapsychology; the scale, success and military relevance of those foreign programs remain incompletely documented in open sources.
- Program persistence plausibly reflected both perceived empirical signal and strategic expected-value calculations.

## Class 3 — disputed / unconfirmed

- Remote viewing constitutes a demonstrated non-sensory information channel.
- Pat Price or other viewers obtained specific classified target information that could not have been available through any ordinary pathway.
- Soviet/Russian programs achieved operational psychic weapons or reliable psychotronic capabilities.
- STAR GATE produced a hidden record of repeated actionable successes inconsistent with the 1995 public evaluation.
- Any post-1995 U.S. successor program exists merely because classified successors are possible.

## Class 4 — speculative synthesis / open conjecture

- Reciprocal U.S.–Soviet threat perception may have sustained psi research through a feedback loop even if neither side possessed a useful capability.
- Secrecy, subjective scoring, selective operational memory and occasional genuine statistical anomalies may jointly explain why the historical record remains resistant to simple debunking or vindication.
- A weak anomalous cognition effect, if real, could be scientifically revolutionary while remaining operationally useless.
- Modern adversarial preregistration is capable of resolving more of the remote-viewing dispute than retrospective archive argument.

## Guardrail

No classified access is claimed. No document's classification marking is treated as evidence that its substantive claim is true. Testimony is not converted into proof. Patents, memoirs and retrospective anecdotes are not treated as demonstrations of efficacy.
