# Contradictions and Incentives Audit

## Central contradictions

**Funding vs proof.** Government sponsorship is documented; underlying paranormal efficacy is not thereby established.

**Laboratory anomaly vs operational failure.** Utts read later controlled results as evidence for anomalous cognition; AIR nevertheless found the operational product unsuitable for intelligence use. These can both be true if an effect is small, unstable or dependent on laboratory conditions.

**Early flaws vs later controls.** Transcript cueing and scoring problems damage early evidence but cannot automatically explain every later experiment. Later evidence must be evaluated on its own controls.

**Impressive anecdotes vs denominator.** Famous cases may contain genuinely striking correspondences, but the probability of such cases cannot be assessed without the full distribution of sessions, misses, target pools and scoring rules.

**Foreign interest vs foreign capability.** U.S. reports document concern over Soviet research. They do not independently verify every Soviet claim or establish military efficacy.

**Transparency vs perfection.** The Transparent Psi Project itself required a correction after audit. That is evidence that open methods expose deviations, not evidence that transparent research is error-free.

## Incentive map

- **Program managers:** continuation, staff protection, demonstration of relevance.
- **Contractors:** follow-on funding, scientific recognition, access to unusual research opportunities.
- **Intelligence consumers:** actionable specificity; may be impatient with weak statistical effects.
- **Congressional sponsors:** technological surprise avoidance, constituent/ideological interest, budget ownership.
- **Skeptical reviewers:** methodological rigor, reputational cost if seen as credulous.
- **Proponent researchers:** reputational cost if effect disappears; incentive to emphasize successful paradigms.
- **Subjects/viewers:** status, identity, feedback effects, selective memory.
- **Journalists/publishers:** vivid cases outperform denominator-heavy null histories.
- **Foreign intelligence services:** possible incentive to exaggerate, conceal, or seed perceptions of exotic capability.

None of these incentives is evidence of fraud. They are alternative causal pathways that must be modeled before attributing persistence to the phenomenon itself.

## Disinformation boundary

The possibility that Soviet or U.S. actors exaggerated psychic research for strategic reasons is plausible in the abstract but not established as a general explanation here. Specific deception claims require specific documentary support.
