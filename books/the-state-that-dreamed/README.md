# The State That Dreamed

## Remote Viewing, Cold War Intelligence, and the Experiment Nobody Finished

### Thesis

The United States did not spend two decades on remote viewing because it had proved psychic spying. It spent because the expected-value calculation was lopsided: a cheap capability with even a tiny chance of penetrating denied targets was worth testing, especially while American analysts believed Soviet counterparts were testing similar claims. The resulting program produced a strange split record—documented government sponsorship, some statistically anomalous laboratory results, operational anecdotes that impressed insiders, persistent methodological criticism, and a 1995 review that found no actionable intelligence value. Modern preregistered adversarial psi experiments then add a neglected control: when believers and skeptics agree in advance on protocol and analysis, prominent precognition claims have failed to replicate.

The book is about what this record does and does not allow us to say. It does not prove remote viewing. It does not prove that every positive result was fraud or error. It does show that intelligence bureaucracies can rationally sustain research into low-probability, high-upside claims; that operational usefulness and scientific existence are different questions; that secrecy degrades retrospective audit; and that anomalous research improves when disagreement is converted into experimental architecture rather than rhetoric.

## Evidence posture

1. **Verified / documented:** CIA, Army, DIA and contractor involvement; SRI/SAIC research; Fort Meade operational tasking; the 1988 NRC review; the 1995 AIR evaluation; archival tasking and memoranda; published methodological criticism; modern preregistered replication efforts.
2. **Credible but incomplete:** some SRI/SAIC statistical anomalies; selected operational sessions described by program participants and retrospective reviewers as impressive; foreign-program assessments whose underlying source base is only partly public.
3. **Disputed / unconfirmed:** that remote viewing produced reliable target-specific information beyond chance; that particular famous operational sessions could not have arisen through cueing, selection, subjective matching, or undocumented information pathways; broad claims of Soviet psychic-weapon capability.
4. **Speculative synthesis:** low-cost/high-upside intelligence logic and reciprocal threat perception may explain program persistence better than either “the government proved psi” or “everyone involved was irrational”; a modern adversarial protocol could still test remote viewing itself rather than only Bem-style precognition.
