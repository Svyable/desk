# 17. What Would Actually Change Our Minds

A field becomes scientific when its enemies are allowed to specify the trapdoor and its friends agree to stand on it.

For remote viewing, the decisive experiment should not be designed to maximize beautiful anecdotes. It should maximize discrimination among explanations.

Hypothesis A: no anomalous information transfer; apparent hits arise from chance, cueing, target structure, judging flexibility and selection. Hypothesis B: a small genuine effect exists in some people but is weak and unreliable. Hypothesis C: a trainable capability produces operationally meaningful information. These hypotheses predict different things.

Under A, preregistered forced-choice performance should converge to chance, free-response rankings should show no stable advantage, gifted-viewer selection should fail out of sample, and effects should not reproduce across independent laboratories once leakage channels are closed.

Under B, a small above-chance effect may persist, but it should generalize prospectively and survive changes of personnel. Its magnitude should be estimable. We should learn which task variables modulate it. The effect should not depend indefinitely on one experimenter's presence.

Under C, viewers should produce low-base-rate target features at rates high enough to improve blinded analyst decisions. Operational utility must be measured directly.

The experiment I would run has three layers.

**Layer one: detection.** Thousands of sessions across multiple laboratories, with preregistered targets, automated randomization, frozen scoring, complete data release and both free-response and forced-choice endpoints.

**Layer two: portability.** The protocol moves to laboratories chosen by skeptics using software and target systems designed jointly. No original experimenter is required for the effect.

**Layer three: utility.** Analysts receive mixtures of real viewer output and matched synthetic/control output without knowing which is which. Their task is to identify diagnostic target facts or choose follow-up collection actions. The question is not whether prose resembles the target after revelation; it is whether the information improves a decision before revelation.

A positive result would not immediately prove telepathy, clairvoyance or precognition. Those are mechanistic labels. It would establish an anomalous information channel that ordinary models had failed to explain. Then physics and neuroscience would have a legitimate emergency.

The next experiments would vary shielding, time separation, target entropy, feedback, distance and causal ordering. If distance does not matter but feedback timing does, that points one way. If effects vanish when future target revelation is prevented, another. If no physical variable matters except experimenter identity, sociology becomes more competitive.

A null result under a jointly signed, high-powered protocol would not prove human experience contains nothing unexplained. It would kill a much more specific and useful claim: that remote viewing, as operationally defined, produces a reproducible information advantage under agreed conditions.

That would be progress.

The strange privilege of extraordinary claims is that a decisive null can be almost as valuable as a positive. Either outcome closes expensive epistemic loops.

What cannot continue forever is the current arrangement, where believers own the hits, skeptics own the flaws, and nobody owns the experiment.
