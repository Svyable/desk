# Evidence Ledger — The Frozen Navy

The manuscript uses four evidence classes. These are not labels for whether a source is respectable; they describe what the present public evidence can establish about a claim.

## Class 1 — Verified / declassified / documented

- Project Habbakuk was a real secret Allied wartime research and engineering effort centered on giant floating airfield / aircraft-carrier concepts using ice and reinforced ice.
- Canada’s National Research Council coordinated substantial Habbakuk research and engineering work.
- A large refrigerated experimental structure was built at Patricia Lake in Jasper National Park, Alberta.
- Canadian government support included significant authorized expenditure; the official wartime history records an initial one-million-dollar authorization when Canada was asked to undertake further work.
- The project involved insulation, refrigeration, structural material, construction, and engineering questions beyond the bulk ice itself.
- Reinforced frozen wood pulp was mechanically different from ordinary ice and was investigated seriously.
- Max Perutz published Habbakuk-related frozen-wood-pulp work in the Journal of Glaciology after the war and connected it to glacier-flow questions.
- The British Chiefs of Staff and Combined Chiefs of Staff discussed Habbakuk at high level in 1943.
- Mountbatten described Habbakuks as floating seadromes / giant aircraft carriers and discussed possible staging and heavy-aircraft uses.
- U.S. planners formally compared floating-airfield alternatives and conventional escort carriers.
- By September 1943, improved carrier prospects were explicitly reducing the perceived need for special floating landing fields for at least one contemplated operation.
- Canadian officials concluded that no fundamental scientific reason then known made construction impossible in principle, while engineering, schedule, cost, steel, manpower and interference with other war programs weighed strongly against proceeding.
- Parks Canada conducted an underwater archaeology survey of Patricia Lake in October 1992 focused on Habbakuk remains.

## Class 2 — Credible but incomplete reconstruction

- Exact full-scale Habbakuk II specifications. Several designs/variants circulated; later dimensions and displacement figures must be tied to a specific design state.
- Exact refrigeration architecture, pipe layout, redundancy plan and thermal design margin of the final proposed configuration.
- Exact residual steel tonnage and the detailed basis for Canadian comparisons with steel construction.
- Detailed Patricia Lake construction sequence, staffing, conscientious-objector labor arrangements and day-to-day operations.
- Exact chain of individual contributions to reinforced-ice formulations and the pykrete name.
- Precise extent to which Habbakuk material research fed later glaciological models beyond the connection explicitly made by Perutz.
- Detailed internal national disagreements within Britain, Canada and the United States beyond the policy records currently used.

## Class 3 — Disputed allegations / folklore / claims without adequate independent confirmation

- The exact popular version of the pistol demonstration in which Mountbatten fires at pykrete at Quebec and the ricochet nearly hits or grazes Admiral Ernest King.
- Exact Churchill bathtub demonstrations as commonly retold.
- Claims that a full operational frozen carrier was nearly complete.
- Claims that Habbakuk was literally unsinkable or invulnerable rather than potentially unusually damage tolerant.
- Claims that cancellation occurred because timid officials failed to understand a proven superior technology.
- Claims that pykrete alone would have removed the need for strategic steel and other scarce industrial inputs.

## Class 4 — Speculative synthesis / open conjecture

- Habbakuk as a general control case for the expected institutional and material footprint of other genuinely bizarre classified programs.
- The claim that the most valuable output of the Habbakuk program may have been a procurement “no” purchased before full-scale commitment.
- The argument that a material can succeed at its own layer while the system built around it fails at architecture, timing or strategic substitution.
- The claim that the pykrete demonstration became culturally dominant because its benefit was visually compressible while refrigeration, opportunity cost and schedule were not.
- The broader inference that physical weirdness and social weirdness should be treated as separate variables when evaluating extraordinary technology claims.

## Claim boundaries

The manuscript does **not** claim an operational Habbakuk carrier existed. It does not claim that senior Allied interest proved the design would succeed. It does not claim that a 1943 technical-feasibility judgment proves modern economic feasibility. It does not settle the famous projectile-demonstration anecdote without primary source reconstruction. It does not treat later pykrete experiments as retroactive validation of fleet-scale performance.
