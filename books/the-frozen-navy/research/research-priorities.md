# Research Priorities and Defeat Conditions

## Publication-grade retrieval priorities

1. Read and annotate the complete Lorne W. Gold NRC monograph, not only institutional summaries.
2. Retrieve British National Archives / Admiralty / Combined Operations Habbakuk files and establish a design-version chronology.
3. Retrieve Canadian NRC laboratory records, C. J. Mackenzie correspondence, and Montreal Engineering Company reports.
4. Reconstruct the Patricia Lake prototype from original drawings, construction records, staffing, power/refrigeration data and test logs.
5. Read Perutz’s full 1948 paper alongside his later autobiographical accounts.
6. Compare Mountbatten papers, Alan Brooke diaries and any contemporary security/conference records to reconstruct the projectile demonstrations.
7. Retrieve the full U.S. ad hoc floating-airfield committee report and technical enclosures referenced in FRUS.
8. Build a quantitative thermal model covering construction freezing load, service heat leak, machinery/crew heat, seawater temperature, refrigeration coefficient of performance and failure buffer from thermal mass.
9. Build a structural model covering pykrete creep, allowable deck deformation, reinforcement, openings, concentrated machinery loads and battle damage.
10. Reconstruct propulsion assumptions, resistance, maneuvering, station keeping, and logistics requirements.
11. Compare full-system resource demand with contemporary escort-carrier, concrete floating-field and conventional-base alternatives.
12. Obtain the full Parks Canada Patricia Lake underwater archaeology report and current management status.

## Falsification / thesis defeat conditions

The book’s core thesis is that Habbakuk’s reinforced-ice work was technically serious and partly successful at the material/prototype level, while full-system economics, engineering burden, schedule and strategic substitution undermined the case for operational construction.

Revise or abandon that thesis if primary records establish any of the following:

- Canadian technical leadership actually concluded the material or basic physical concept was unworkable and the later official summary has materially distorted that judgment.
- The full-scale design required little refrigeration, little scarce material, and manageable construction effort, making the “architecture lost” interpretation substantially false.
- The Patricia Lake structure was merely a trivial display with no meaningful thermal/construction test function.
- Perutz’s published work is shown not to derive materially from Habbakuk research despite its title and stated connection.
- The 1943 Allied policy record shows Habbakuk received only ceremonial or cursory attention rather than real programmatic consideration.
- Conventional alternatives did not materially erode the project’s relevant requirement during 1943, contrary to the published official record.

## Conditions that would strengthen but not prove the thesis

- Original cost sheets showing escalating refrigeration/steel/manpower estimates.
- Test records showing reinforced ice met relevant mechanical targets while system studies worsened.
- Minutes explicitly tying cancellation to opportunity cost rather than disbelief in the material.
- Engineering drawings that show the support-system mass/complexity dominating the nominally cheap structural concept.
- Contemporary comparisons with escort-carrier output and VLR aircraft availability.

## What cannot be recovered experimentally

No modern pykrete block can establish how an operational Habbakuk would have performed in 1944. The historical system depended on wartime materials, industrial capacity, aircraft, enemy weapons, doctrine, logistics and strategic timing. Modern experiments can test physics; they cannot recreate the procurement decision without modeling the vanished environment.
