# Source Log — The Frozen Navy

This log records sources actually used or identified in the first-pass manuscript. A URL in this file is a retrieval trail, not an endorsement of every claim on the linked page.

## Primary / official / institutional

### National Research Council Canada — 1916–1945 history

URL: https://nrc.canada.ca/en/corporate/history/1916-1945

Use: institutional confirmation of Project Habbakuk, secret Alberta prototype, NRC coordination of universities and companies, pykrete as reinforced ice, need for insulation/refrigeration/steel/investment, and strategic obsolescence as radar, escort ships, remote bases and longer-range aircraft improved.

Caution: retrospective institutional history; convenient summary language can compress credit and technical nuance. Use Gold and wartime records for publication-grade detail.

### Gold, Lorne W. — The Canadian Habbakuk Project

NRC-IRC-4948, 1992, 323 pages.

URL: https://nrc-publications.canada.ca/eng/view/object/?id=d98d3b9e-2cf4-4d2e-b33c-4731a3fe7779

Use: principal Canadian project history and archival roadmap.

Caution: full monograph still requires systematic annotation in the next pass; do not attribute page-specific details until checked directly.

### Perutz, M. F. — 1948 Journal of Glaciology paper

Title: “A Description of the Iceberg Aircraft Carrier and the Bearing of the Mechanical Properties of Frozen Wood Pulp Upon Some Problems of Glacier Flow.”

DOI: https://doi.org/10.3189/S0022143000007796

Cambridge record: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-glaciology/article/description-of-the-iceberg-aircraft-carrier-and-the-bearing-of-the-mechanical-properties-of-frozen-wood-pulp-upon-some-problems-of-glacier-flow/BE12BCCE68FE5D9C307299A2F1F2DFC6

Use: direct scientific bridge from wartime reinforced-ice work to glacier mechanics; confirms the work was technically legible after the military program ended.

### FRUS — British Chiefs memorandum, August 18, 1943

URL: https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1943/d433

Use: multiple Habbakuk variants; British Chiefs’ view that floating-airfield research/design had advanced enough to consider production of types; strategic-material and construction context.

### FRUS — Combined Chiefs minutes, August 19, 1943

URL: https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1943/d388

Use: Mountbatten’s description of Habbakuks as floating seadromes/giant aircraft carriers, possible staging/advance-landing use, discussion at senior Allied level.

Boundary: establishes briefing and consideration, not technical validation.

### FRUS — King to Roosevelt / ad hoc committee, September 7, 1943

URL: https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1943/d587

Use: formal U.S. comparative examination of floating seadromes, recommendation context, escort-carrier competition.

### FRUS — Secretariat Minutes, September 10, 1943

URL: https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1943/d579

Use: Churchill’s recorded observation that improved carrier prospects appeared to make other artificial floating fields unnecessary for the immediate operation; evidence of strategic-requirement decay.

### FRUS — floating-airfield alternatives

URL: https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1943/d568

Use: Admiral King’s discussion of Armstrong Seadrome, naval pontoons, dry-dock sections, timing and resource tradeoffs. Demonstrates Habbakuk was compared with a portfolio of architectures rather than judged in isolation.

### Government of Canada — Royal Canadian Navy Battle of the Atlantic

URL: https://www.canada.ca/en/navy/corporate/history-heritage/battle-atlantic/1939-1945.html

Use: strategic context for the mid-Atlantic air gap/Black Pit and its closure through very-long-range aircraft and escort carriers.

### Naval History and Heritage Command

URL: https://www.history.navy.mil/

Use: U.S. official context on Battle of the Atlantic, escort carriers, radar, longer-range aircraft, and anti-submarine adaptation.

### Parks Canada — Archaeological Activities 1992

URL: https://parkscanadahistory.com/publications/archaeological-activities-1992.pdf

Use: direct institutional record that Parks Canada’s underwater archaeology team surveyed Patricia Lake in October 1992 to inventory and assess the remains of the Second World War refrigerated Habbakuk aircraft-carrier prototype; useful physical-evidence control.

## Secondary / synthesis leads requiring deeper primary confirmation

- Max Perutz autobiographical accounts of Habbakuk and firing demonstrations.
- Alan Brooke diaries / papers for the projectile-demonstration story.
- Lord Louis Mountbatten papers for demonstrations and Combined Operations sponsorship.
- Geoffrey Pyke papers for concept chronology and authorship/credit questions.
- British Admiralty and Combined Operations Habbakuk technical files.
- Montreal Engineering Company design/cost records.
- Canadian National Research Council raw test data, construction correspondence and project staffing.
- Contemporary U.S. military retrospective descriptions giving the roughly two-million-ton scale and representative dimensions.

## Explicitly not treated as proof

- Repeated popular accounts of a bullet nearly striking Admiral King.
- Churchill bathtub anecdotes without primary provenance.
- Modern classroom pykrete demonstrations as validation of wartime full-scale design.
- Later reinforced-ice research as proof that Habbakuk would have been operationally successful.
- Senior political interest as proof of technical efficacy.
