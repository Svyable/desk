# Contradictions, Incentives, and Open Questions

## Contradictions / unresolved details

### The projectile demonstration

The popular Quebec version, Perutz-associated London versions, and later retellings do not presently form a clean single narrative. Treat projectile resistance as supported; treat exact location, shooter, witnesses and near-miss as unresolved until primary diaries/papers are compared.

### Who “invented” pykrete

NRC’s public retrospective uses a simplified attribution to Geoffrey Pyke. Technical credit may be distributed among Pyke, researchers who explored reinforced ice, and NRC/Canadian work. Do not force a single inventor until project files are reviewed.

### The definitive ship

“Habbakuk” covered several designs. Period British records explicitly discuss multiple types. Popular dimensions—especially the enormous two-million-ton, roughly two-thousand-foot vessel—must be attached to a design version rather than presented as timeless specifications.

### Why the project ended

No single cause should be given exclusive credit. Surviving records support a combination: engineering/construction burden, refrigeration and material complexity, rising cost, residual strategic-material demand, manpower, schedule, improving escort-carrier availability, longer-range aircraft, radar, remote bases, and changing operational requirements.

## Incentive audit

### Wartime promoters

Incentive: simplify the system around its strongest property, emphasize survivability and low bulk-material demand, and accelerate decisions under crisis conditions.

Risk: early cost and schedule estimates may be optimistic; disadvantages distributed across support systems can remain offstage.

### Canadian engineering institutions

Incentive: give useful advice while protecting national war priorities and limited manpower.

Risk: could become institutionally conservative about a British-sponsored megaproject. Counterweight: the record does not dismiss the science; it explicitly preserves technical feasibility while criticizing system burden.

### U.S. military planners

Incentive: prefer architectures that fit expanding American production and existing doctrine.

Risk: industrial abundance can make radical material substitution look less attractive than it would from a resource-constrained British perspective.

### Postwar memoirists and popular historians

Incentive: tell memorable stories. Pistol ricochets, bathtubs and eccentric personalities travel better than refrigeration calculations.

Risk: fusion of multiple events, relocation to more famous settings, and assignment of colorful lines or reactions that were never recorded contemporaneously.

### NRC retrospective history

Incentive: highlight national scientific ingenuity.

Risk: simplify credit and program complexity. Counterweight: NRC’s own summary openly describes insulation, refrigeration, steel and investment burdens and does not portray the ship as suppressed triumph.

### Skeptical popular accounts

Incentive: use Habbakuk as evidence of wartime absurdity or government incompetence.

Risk: reverse the distortion by ignoring that reinforced-ice work was scientifically serious and that Canadian engineers did not reject the project on a basic-physics ground.

## Open questions for archival work

- What is the earliest traceable Pyke proposal and how did the design change before Canadian involvement?
- Which reinforced-ice formulations were tested, at what temperatures, and with what mechanical protocols?
- What were the measured creep rates used in design calculations?
- What exactly did the Patricia Lake model test, and what raw data survive?
- Who built and maintained the Patricia Lake installation?
- What was the complete refrigeration and insulation architecture of the leading full-scale design?
- What exact strategic-material quantities did the Canadian and British estimates require?
- Can the steel-versus-pykrete cost comparison be reconstructed from original worksheets?
- What were the complete findings of the U.S. ad hoc committee whose enclosure is only partly exposed in published diplomatic records?
- How many distinct projectile demonstrations can be documented from contemporaneous or first-person sources?
- Which accounts first introduced the Admiral King near-miss and Churchill bathtub details?
- Did the project materially influence later reinforced-ice engineering beyond Perutz’s scientific publication?
- What precisely remains at Patricia Lake, and what did the 1992 underwater survey map?
