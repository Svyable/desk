# 2025 AHI assessment audit — the consensus changed at the edge

## Scope

This is one bounded current-source correction for **THE SOUND INSIDE YOUR HEAD**, stacked on the active manuscript branch rather than revising Chapter 1 concurrently. Chapter 1 currently ends its intelligence chronology with the 1 March 2023 Intelligence Community Assessment. That is no longer the latest public U.S. intelligence position.

The January 2025 update matters because it does something more interesting than reverse the 2023 conclusion: **the majority judgment held while two components moved at the margin**.

## What changed in January 2025

Public reporting on the updated Intelligence Community assessment says five of seven participating agencies continued to judge it **very unlikely** that a foreign adversary was responsible for reported anomalous health incidents. Years of collection and targeting still had not produced compelling intelligence tying a foreign actor to a specific reported event.

At the same time, two components changed their judgments with **low confidence** after new reporting about foreign directed-energy research and capability:

- one judged there was a **roughly even chance** that a foreign actor had used a novel weapon or prototype device against a **small, undetermined subset** of U.S. personnel or dependents reporting possible AHIs;
- another judged there was a **roughly even chance** that a foreign actor had developed such a capability, while judging actual deployment unlikely.

Authoritative baseline for the prior assessment: ODNI, *Updated Assessment of Anomalous Health Incidents*, 1 March 2023: https://www.dni.gov/files/ODNI/documents/assessments/NIC-Unclassified-ICA-Updated-Assessment-AHI-March2023.pdf

Contemporaneous reporting on the January 2025 update:

- Reuters, 10 January 2025: https://www.reuters.com/world/us/most-us-spy-agencies-think-havana-syndrome-was-likely-not-caused-by-foreign-foe-2025-01-10/
- Associated Press, 10 January 2025 (PBS republication): https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/u-s-intelligence-finds-no-link-between-havana-syndrome-and-foreign-power-but-two-spy-agencies-say-possibility-of-foreign-weapon-could-be-responsible-for-injuries

Until an official declassified 2025 assessment text is recovered and archived in the research trail, do not pretend the press briefing is equivalent to the primary document. The existence and direction of the update are well corroborated; exact wording should remain attributed to the public briefing/reporting where necessary.

## Why this changes the book

The current chapter says the 2023 ICA was a reversal of attribution rather than a reversal of microwave physics. That distinction survives. What no longer survives is treating the 2023 judgment as the endpoint.

The 2025 update supplies a much better stress test for the manuscript's causal ladder:

**capability evidence can move without event attribution moving with it.**

New intelligence about foreign weapons research was enough for two components to increase the probability they assigned to a capability or possible use in a small subset. It was not enough to identify a device at a scene, identify a specific victim subset prospectively, name an operator, or overturn the majority view that a foreign adversary was very unlikely to explain the reported population.

That is almost a laboratory demonstration of the book's central epistemic problem. Evidence can legitimately update one rung while leaving neighboring rungs largely untouched.

## The subset problem gets harder

The 2025 minority judgment does not rescue a defined `core` population. Public reporting says the potentially affected subset remained **small and undetermined**. That phrase should create pressure rather than relief.

If a component cannot specify which incidents belong to the subset, the hypothesis remains difficult to test clinically or forensically. A future chapter should ask what prospective features would identify the cases *before* attribution evidence is examined: sensory phenomenology, onset profile, location geometry, contemporaneous environmental measurement, objective clinical findings, clustered timing, or another criterion.

Otherwise `small subset` can become a container into which unresolved cases are placed after the fact.

The opposite mistake is equally serious. Heterogeneity in the overall population can hide a genuine rare mechanism. A majority conclusion about the full AHI population does not logically exclude a different cause in a small number of events. The book should keep both failure modes alive.

## Strongest counterargument

The 2025 shift may ultimately be less dramatic than it sounds. Both changed judgments were low-confidence and, according to the public briefing, were driven substantially by new intelligence about **foreign capability development**, not direct evidence tying a foreign actor or device to a particular AHI. The update can therefore be read as evidence that the engineering prior changed while the event-attribution evidence remained weak.

If later primary records show that the minority components had event-specific intelligence rather than capability reporting, this interpretation must change. Conversely, if subsequent review returns those components toward the majority or fails to identify a prospectively defined subset, the manuscript should not preserve the 2025 split merely because disagreement is narratively attractive.

## Current-source complication for later research

By 2026, public reporting described further classified/foreign technical work involving pulsed-energy devices and continuing U.S. review. Those reports are potentially important but are not yet a clean primary-source basis for manuscript factual claims. They should enter the research queue as leads, not as proof that an AHI weapon caused any reported incident.

The publication standard should remain severe: **a device capable of producing some relevant biological effect is capability evidence; it is not event attribution.**

## Editorial consequence

When the active manuscript is ready for a prose pass, Chapter 1 should not simply append a paragraph saying `then two agencies changed their minds.` The stronger move is to let the chronology itself demonstrate Bayesian updating at different causal rungs:

- 2020 National Academies: mechanism plausibility under clinical/evidentiary constraints;
- 2022 IC Experts Panel: pulsed EM plausible for a subset;
- 2023 ICA: intelligence collection points strongly away from a foreign campaign;
- 2025 update: majority attribution judgment holds, while two components raise low-confidence estimates about foreign capability and possible use in a small undetermined subset.

That sequence is messier than a verdict and therefore more faithful to the evidence.

## Publication holds

- Do not call the January 2025 update an Intelligence Community reversal; five of seven components retained the core 2023 attribution judgment.
- Keep **low confidence** attached to the two changed component judgments.
- Do not turn `roughly even chance` into `likely` or `probable`.
- Do not turn foreign directed-energy research progress into evidence that a particular AHI was caused by such a device.
- Do not claim the 2025 assessment identified the affected subset; public reporting describes it as small and undetermined.
- Do not name the two components from later anonymous-source reporting as settled official fact unless an authoritative public record identifies them.
- Do not let later reports of experimental pulsed-energy devices retroactively prove an operational adversary campaign.

## Research queue

2. Separate the evidence that moved the two components into `capability`, `event`, and `attribution` bins.
3. Recover any public technical basis for the reported government-funded biological-effects experiments rather than relying on briefing summaries.
4. Track subsequent 2025–2026 official reviews and congressional oversight, preserving political/institutional claims as claims until underlying evidence is public.
5. Ask whether any later record prospectively defines the small subset rather than selecting it after unresolved cases are known.
