# Evidence ledger — THE SOUND INSIDE YOUR HEAD

## Governing causal ladder

**reported event → exposure → biological coupling → acute effect → persistent injury → device → operator → adversary → campaign**

No rung inherits certainty from the rung below it.

## Class 1 — verified / documented

- Allan H. Frey published *Auditory System Response to Radio Frequency Energy* in *Aerospace Medicine* in 1961. PubMed record: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/13895080/
- Frey and Messenger published a 1973 psychophysical study in *Science* reporting perception induced by pulse-modulated ultrahigh-frequency electromagnetic energy, with perception primarily dependent on peak power and secondarily on pulse width: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/4719908/
- U.S. personnel in Havana reported acute and persistent symptoms beginning in 2016; State publicly documented medically evaluated personnel and later a similar Guangzhou case. Archived State record: https://2017-2021.state.gov/establishment-of-the-health-incidents-response-task-force/
- The 2020 National Academies report judged directed pulsed RF energy the most plausible mechanism among those it considered for the distinctive acute presentation, while identifying substantial uncertainty and possible reinforcing/additive mechanisms: https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/25889
- The 2022 IC Experts Panel judged that a subset of AHIs could not be easily explained by known environmental or medical conditions and that pulsed electromagnetic energy could plausibly explain core characteristics, with information gaps: https://www.dni.gov/index.php/newsroom/reports-publications/reports-publications-2022/item/2273-complementary-efforts-on-anomalous-health-incidents
- The 1 March 2023 Intelligence Community Assessment reported that most IC agencies judged it very unlikely that a foreign adversary was responsible for reported AHIs and that evidence pointed away from a global adversary campaign: https://www.dni.gov/files/ODNI/documents/assessments/NIC-Unclassified-ICA-Updated-Assessment-AHI-March2023.pdf

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete

- Patient and witness accounts of sudden directional sound/pressure, vestibular disturbance and subsequent symptoms. These establish reported phenomenology, not exposure mechanism.
- The National Academies mechanism judgment. Serious expert synthesis, but constrained by available records and not a device recovery or exposure measurement.
- The 2022 IC Experts Panel subset judgment. Important because it narrows rather than universalizes the anomaly; public evidence remains incomplete.
- Investigative reporting alleging intelligence leads involving foreign services. Treat as reporting until documentary or independently reproducible evidence closes attribution.

## Class 3 — disputed / unconfirmed

- A microwave or other directed-energy weapon caused the original Havana cases.
- Russia, another state, or a specific intelligence service operated such a device.
- One mechanism caused the entire AHI population.
- The microwave auditory effect, by itself, explains persistent neurological injury.
- A covert operational weapon with the necessary range, geometry, power, concealment and targeting performance has been publicly demonstrated in connection with AHIs.

## Class 4 — speculative synthesis

### Mechanism-attribution gap
A real bioeffect can increase plausibility for one causal rung while contributing almost nothing to operator or adversary attribution.

### Subset trap
If a heterogeneous case population contains a genuinely unusual subset, analyzing the whole population may wash out the signal; selecting the subset after seeing the data may manufacture one. The solution is prospective case criteria, not narrative preference.

### Causal promotion
Public argument repeatedly jumps from `possible mechanism` to `weapon`, then from `weapon` to `attack`, then `attack` to `adversary`. Each arrow needs independent evidence.

## Contradictions that must remain visible

1. **National Academies 2020 vs IC 2023 are not direct opposites.** One primarily evaluated plausible medical/physical mechanisms under its evidentiary constraints; the later IC assessment focused heavily on attribution and intelligence collection. A plausible mechanism does not entail an adversary campaign.
2. **2022 Experts Panel vs 2023 ICA.** The panel kept pulsed EM energy plausible for a subset; the ICA found intelligence evidence pointing away from adversary responsibility for the reported population. Preserve both.
3. **Recorded sounds vs RF hypothesis.** Audible recordings from Cuba cannot simply be equated with an internally perceived microwave auditory effect. Acoustic explanations for a recording do not necessarily explain every patient's symptoms; likewise a bioeffect does not explain an externally recordable sound without an additional mechanism.
4. **Clinical reality vs causal unity.** Reject the false choice between `patients were genuinely ill` and `one exotic attack caused all cases`.

## Publication holds

- Do not call AHI a microwave attack as fact.
- Do not call the Frey effect proof of injury.
- Do not infer a foreign operator from mechanism plausibility.
- Do not use Soviet/Russian RF research as evidence of a specific operation without operational evidence.
- Do not collapse all AHI reports into the original Havana presentation.
- Do not treat classification or redaction as positive evidence for a hidden weapon.
- Patents, if used, establish filing and disclosed concepts only, not efficacy or deployment.

## Strongest conventional countermodel

The reported AHI population is heterogeneous. Some cases have identifiable medical, environmental or psychosocial explanations; others remain unresolved because evidence is sparse, delayed or noisy. The microwave auditory effect is physically real but may be irrelevant to the incidents. Early unusual cases, institutional concern, media attention and broadened reporting criteria can produce a case ecology in which multiple causes accumulate under one label. Intelligence collection then fails to identify a foreign campaign because there was no unitary campaign.

This model must be allowed to win.

## Defeat conditions for the book's stronger thesis

The book should narrow sharply if primary evidence shows that the distinctive subset cannot be defined prospectively; if RF exposure parameters required to reproduce the reported acute phenomenology are operationally implausible at relevant distances/geometries; if better clinical reanalysis removes the claimed distinctive pattern; or if a mundane causal model explains the high-information cases with fewer unsupported assumptions.

Conversely, a recovered device, contemporaneous calibrated exposure measurement, independently replicated biomarker tied to a known exposure regime, or documentary operational evidence would materially promote the directed-energy hypothesis.
