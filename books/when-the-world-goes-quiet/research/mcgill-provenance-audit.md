# McGill provenance audit — WHEN THE WORLD GOES QUIET

Purpose: keep Chapter 2's useful `capability gap` while preventing the historical sequence from becoming neater than the surviving record.

## 1. What the McGill experiments directly establish

The manuscript is strongest when it stays with the experimental result: reduced patterned stimulation can alter cognition and perception without implying an empty nervous system or a programmable mind. The primary McGill anchors already in the chapter — Heron, Doane & Scott (1956) and Heron (1957) — should carry claims about the apparatus, difficulty tolerating prolonged isolation, perceptual disturbance and appetite for stimulation.

Primary anchors:
- Heron, Doane & Scott (1956), *Visual Disturbances after Prolonged Perceptual Isolation*: https://doi.org/10.1037/h0083661
- Heron (1957), *The Pathology of Boredom*: https://doi.org/10.1038/scientificamerican0157-52

Do not let later intelligence history retroactively change what these experiments measured. They measured subjects under controlled perceptual isolation; they did not demonstrate interrogation efficacy, durable belief implantation, reliable truth extraction or an operational `mind-control` capability.

## 2. The provenance problem: influence is not a single arrow

Chapter 2 currently says that particular pathways from academic deprivation research into intelligence techniques must be established document by document. Keep that standard. McGill's own archival description of the Donald O. Hebb papers is useful because it explicitly warns against collapsing the archive into the familiar CIA-origin story: the papers contain correspondence showing broad academic interest in the deprivation work and Cold War concern extending beyond one sponsor or program.

Authoritative archival map:
- McGill University Library, discussion of the Donald O. Hebb papers and sensory-deprivation correspondence: https://www.mcgill.ca/libraries/sites/libraries/files/2025-06/oln120_final.pdf

This is a map to archival material, not a substitute for the underlying correspondence. Before the manuscript attributes motive, origin or causal influence to a particular defense/intelligence sponsor, recover the relevant contract, letter, grant record or testimony.

### Publication hold

Do not write the historical chain as `Hebb discovered deprivation → CIA noticed → MKULTRA operationalized it` unless primary records support every arrow. The experiments, Canadian defense interest, CIA behavioral-research programs, KUBARK doctrine and later coercive practice are related historical objects, not interchangeable labels.

## 3. A useful human complication: Hebb's intellectual project was larger than deprivation

McGill's institutional biography describes Hebb's central project as reconnecting psychology with neural mechanism; his 1949 *Organization of Behavior* and work across psychology and neurophysiology predate the sensory-isolation story that later became culturally dominant.

Authoritative source:
- The Neuro / McGill University, Donald Hebb biography: https://www.mcgill.ca/neuro/about/donald-hebb-phd

That gives the eventual prose a better proportion. Hebb should not enter the book as a proto–mind-control scientist whose importance begins when intelligence institutions become interested. The deprivation work belongs inside a much larger attempt to understand how experience, environment and neural organization constrain behavior.

## 4. Counterevidence against the clean sponsor story

The strongest counterargument to this caution is that sponsor interest can still matter historically even if it did not originate the underlying science. Defense or intelligence funding can redirect questions, select applications, create classified follow-on work and alter which findings receive operational attention. A primary-source chain showing those effects would be consequential.

The correct standard is therefore not `academic first, so intelligence influence is irrelevant`. It is: **separate origin, funding, influence, application and demonstrated capability.** Each is a different historical proposition.

## 5. Manuscript consequence

Chapter 2's best line of inquiry is not who `invented mind control`. It is how a reproducible low-level effect acquired progressively larger institutional meanings.

Use five rungs when later chapters reconstruct that escalation:

1. **experimental effect** — what changed in the subject;
2. **scientific interpretation** — what investigators thought the effect meant;
3. **sponsor interest** — why an institution paid attention;
4. **operational adaptation** — what procedure was actually used outside the laboratory;
5. **capability evidence** — whether the adaptation reliably improved the intended outcome.

The ladder is intentionally lossy. Evidence for rung 1 does not automatically climb to rung 5.

## Strongest counterargument / defeat condition

If primary contracts, correspondence, program records and outcome reports establish a direct sponsor-to-laboratory-to-operational chain with measured gains in the intended intelligence outcome, the manuscript should say so plainly and retire the weaker `interest without capability` framing for that case. The point of the provenance audit is not to preserve ambiguity. It is to make each historical arrow earn its own evidence.
