# Research brief — The Frontier Effect

## Thesis

Solving a sufficiently important hard problem redistributes finite collective attention. The solution changes not only the stock of knowledge but the map of prestige, tractability, technique, funding, training and neighboring questions. AI increases the potential velocity of this redistribution by making some forms of generation, search and verification cheaper, creating larger and faster swings of effort between frontiers.

## Distinction from nearby Desk work

*Search Frontier* studies how constrained systems search possibility spaces and why verification becomes scarce when generation gets cheap. *The Frontier Effect* begins one step later: a frontier has moved. It follows the redistribution of effort after the move and asks whether AI accelerates that reallocation enough to change the social structure of discovery.

## Strong competing explanation

Research attention may be much stickier than the thesis implies. Careers, grants, departments, journals and tacit expertise create path dependence. Major solutions can deepen investment in a field rather than release it. The book must measure both migration and persistence rather than assuming attention behaves like frictionless capital.

## Evidence classes

1. **Documented:** dates and status of named problems; publication and acceptance rules; bibliometric/career/funding changes where measured; documented AI mathematical results.
2. **Credible but incomplete:** interviews, institutional accounts and retrospective claims about why researchers changed problems or fields.
3. **Disputed/unconfirmed:** claims that a current major problem is solved before field-wide validation; claims that AI systems possess general mathematical creativity based on benchmark performance.
4. **Speculative synthesis:** “frontier velocity,” “attention release,” and the claim that AI could create repeated civilization-scale reallocations of cognitive effort. These are book hypotheses, not established scientific laws.

## Research queue

Quantify the post-solution attention shift around Fermat, Poincaré and bounded prime gaps using bibliometrics and conference/program changes. Reconstruct the Polymath projects as deliberate attention-pooling infrastructure. Find primary accounts from mathematicians who changed research direction after a major proof. Separate “solution creates a field” from “solution empties a field.” Track formal proof assistants and AI systems by what they actually verified or discovered, not promotional category labels.
