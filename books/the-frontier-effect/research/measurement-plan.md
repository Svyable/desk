# Measurement plan — The Frontier Effect

The book's core claim should be testable. “Attention moves” cannot remain a literary impression.

## Unit of analysis

A **frontier event** is a dated change in the status of a recognized mathematical problem or bottleneck: a proof, disproof, independence result, new finite bound, major reduction, new equivalence, or method that changes expert estimates of tractability.

Candidate events:

- Wiles/Taylor–Wiles and Fermat's Last Theorem, 1993–1995
- Perelman and Poincaré/geometrization, 2002–2010 acceptance arc
- Zhang bounded prime gaps, 2013
- Maynard–Tao multidimensional sieve, 2013–2014
- selected Polymath projects
- later formalization or AI-assisted events where dates and artifacts are unambiguous

## Quantities to measure

### 1. Attention inflow and outflow

For a five- or ten-year window around a frontier event:

- number of papers using the problem name or central method
- number of distinct authors entering the topic
- number of authors leaving or reducing publication in the topic
- conference programs, workshops and special sessions
- PhD theses and postdoctoral projects where data are obtainable
- grant or fellowship language only where records are comparable

The book should not assume a solution causes attention to leave. A frontier event may produce an **attention surge** before later decay.

### 2. Method diffusion

Track whether techniques developed around the frontier appear in other problems or fields.

Candidate measures:

- citation paths from the key proof/method papers into other MSC categories
- appearance of named techniques in unrelated problem families
- expository and graduate-course adoption
- formal-library reuse where dependency graphs are available

This is the strongest version of the thesis: the durable effect of a solved problem may be the migration of its machinery.

### 3. Frontier velocity

Working definition, not established terminology:

The rate at which a recognized problem's best-known state changes over time.

For a quantitative problem this can sometimes be literal: best bound by date. Bounded prime gaps is the clean test case.

For qualitative problems, use dated milestones rather than fake a scalar.

### 4. Verification lag

Measure the gap between:

- announcement / preprint
- serious expert checking
- corrected or stabilized proof
- journal publication
- broad field acceptance
- formal verification where applicable
- incorporation into later work

Fermat is the historical control. The September 2026 Navier–Stokes announcement is a live case and should not be prejudged.

### 5. Assimilation lag

Harder and more interesting than verification lag.

Possible proxies:

- first graduate texts or lecture courses built around the method
- first major results treating the solved theorem as a routine dependency
- citation half-life
- formal library availability
- surveys that shift from “open problem” framing to “tool/method” framing

## Falsifiers and competing explanations

The thesis weakens if:

- major solved problems show no systematic redistribution of authors, citations or methods relative to matched unsolved controls;
- attention is explained mostly by funding, hiring or publication fashions unrelated to frontier events;
- famous problems attract attention but their solutions do not measurably affect neighboring work;
- method diffusion is rare and most important proofs remain local achievements;
- AI increases candidate generation without increasing accepted mathematical output or reducing any meaningful frontier lag.

## Controls

Avoid comparing famous solved problems only with each other.

Potential controls:

- comparably prestigious unsolved problems over the same period
- technical breakthroughs without a famous problem attached
- fields with stable publication volume and no major frontier event
- multiple dates for the same problem: announcement, correction, publication, prize/acceptance

## Confounders

- overall growth of mathematics and arXiv
- changes in indexing and database coverage
- retirement/death/hiring cycles of major researchers
- new journals or conferences
- exogenous computing improvements
- prize announcements and media coverage
- terminology changes that break keyword counts
- citation inflation and field-specific citation norms

## Standard for manuscript claims

Do not write “the proof released thousands of mathematicians” unless the data support it.

Prefer:

- “publication volume rose/fell by X in the defined corpus”
- “N authors entered the topic within Y years under this authorship rule”
- “the method spread into these named areas”
- “participant accounts describe a shift, but bibliometric evidence is mixed”

The book is allowed to have a thesis. It is not allowed to manufacture its evidence.
