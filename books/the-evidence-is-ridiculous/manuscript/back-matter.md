# Back Matter

## What 'True' Means Here

This book makes a narrower promise than that every detail of a popular legend has been confirmed. Its central historical events and scientific observations are documented, with source pointers in the relevant chapters and the companion research ledger. Where an episode concerns a proposal, prototype, test, established observation, unrepeated finding or later reconstruction, the distinction should be clear on the page. The narrator's questions and judgments are interpretation, not reports of having witnessed the events.

The chapters do not contain invented dialogue, fictionalized eyewitness scenes, supposed secret interviews, or claims of special access. The original institutions may have misunderstood the implications of their own work; a present-day author can misunderstand them too. Claims that depend on official history should be read alongside contemporary records and contrary sources where available.

Historical engineering programs sometimes appear here because the fact that money was spent and machinery was constructed can be proved. The proposals were not necessarily practical, ethical, or successful. Scientific experiments can reveal something real under controlled conditions without establishing a universal biological mechanism. Environmental disasters demand different care from military oddities or postal bureaucracy.

## Sources and Revisions

The Desk edition includes `research/source-ledger.md`, recording the principal source, what it supports, what it does not support and the next questions for verification. Source links have been supplied for reader checking, not to imply endorsement by the source institution. Before a Shelf edition, a full citation and fact review must confirm that links remain useful and that all dates, populations, sample sizes, legal statements and contested interpretations are accurate.
