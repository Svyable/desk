## Fact-check issues actually encountered

1. **First parcel-post child:** Nancy Pope's 2013 Postal Museum account gives family Beauge and carrier Vernon Little. A later USPS history hosted on the same museum site gives the names Beagle and Vernon Lytle, and identifies the boy as James. The manuscript discloses the difference. Confirm against contemporary postal documents and newspaper accounts; do not choose a spelling merely for fluency.
2. **Ghost Army:** The U.S. Army describes operations classified until 1993; the Ghost Army Legacy Project identifies 1996 as declassification of the original 1945 official history. The chapter now states the distinction rather than inventing a single universal date.
3. **Oregon's balloon-bomb victims:** The Oregon Encyclopedia and May 31, 1945 *Capital Journal* identify Elsie Mitchell; one Oregon Historical Society memorial summary calls her Elyse, and HistoryLink acknowledges both variants. Use contemporaneous spelling where possible and preserve names rather than treat the fatalities as comic local color.
4. **CIA Acoustikitty:** The agency's account disputes the famous fatal taxi incident. The primary declassified memo supports the existence and operational limits of the program but does not prove the story's attractive ending.
5. **Blue Peacock:** The chicken heating method was a suggestion in a real weapons design program, not a documented field deployment or a functional chicken-equipped nuclear land mine. Final edition should seek the original UK National Archives file.
6. **Oregon whale:** The Oregon Encyclopedia and extant television coverage support the central disaster and specific preparations. The moving footage is evidence of physical consequences, not a warrant for invented officials' dialogue.
7. **Camp Century / NB-36H / Oklo:** Keep the evidence states distinct. The Greenland base and its reactor operated; the vast missile network was proposed. The NB-36H carried a reactor that did not propel the plane. Oklo's fission was naturally occurring and reconstructed from isotope evidence, not ancient human technology.

## Literary revision still needed

The initial book is deliberately made of twenty independent incidents. That gives range and commercial appeal but risks reading like twenty extended encyclopedia entries or twenty applications of one debunking formula. Before release, read consecutively for repetition of “the record shows,” “the distinction matters,” and endings that announce the evidentiary lesson. Replace duplicated lectures with consequential people, actions and primary documents. Give more sustained narrative movement to the strongest chapters (Guano/Navassa, Ghost Army/Viersen, Lake Nyos/Monoun, Camp Century/ice core, the mail missile) and allow quieter cases to remain quieter.

Preserve the humane handling of actual harm: Navassa labor exploitation, the six Oregon balloon-bomb fatalities, the victims of the Cameroonian gas releases, and the history of nuclear tests and contamination must not become comedy by proximity. Avoid ungrounded private motives or invented quotations.

**Remaining publication blockers:** full source-by-source verification, factual corrections, full-book literary edit, legal/rights and metadata review, and production-specific checks. The book is saved in Desk with all-rights-reserved metadata and remains **Drafting**. Do not release to Shelf or claim a finished bestseller.
