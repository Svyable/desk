# Source ledger — The Evidence Is Ridiculous

**Working research trail, 16 November 2026.** The book's twenty central incidents are real, but many popular versions contain invented dialogue, false outcomes, exaggerated scope, or undocumented motives. This ledger records the claim the evidence can support. Sources are cited in the narrative close to the relevant passages. An institutional or primary source is not automatically impartial, complete, or current; official records, memoirs, technical studies, archival footage and later interpretations should be distinguished.

The entry's **status** applies to its narrow central event; it does not certify every sentence of the manuscript. Before releasing a trade edition, perform a claim-by-claim check, review source corrections, fill the gaps below, and seek documented firsthand accounts where they improve the work. Keep rights and copyright restrictions on source material in force.

## Cross-book fact review and falsification tasks

1. **Verify links.** Perform a fresh source-by-source availability and content audit before publication. Several archives have changed URLs; a link's presence is not proof that the exact statistic, date or quotation appears there. Wherever possible, use original reports rather than later institutional retrospectives.
2. **Respect event stage.** Verify each assertion separately: idea considered, memo authenticated, apparatus built, tests performed, deployment, outcome, and later interpretation. In Chapters 5, 6, 8, 9 and 18, **proposal vs deployment** is the central truth boundary.
3. **Investigate contested details.** Acoustikitty's taxi tale; actual source/chain of command for Blue Peacock's poultry idea; bat-bomb test accident chronology; causes of ant behavior; historical extent of Navassa worker mistreatment; precise mechanisms and triggers at Nyos and Monoun.
4. **Deep human context.** Chapter 3's Navassa laborers, Chapter 10's six balloon-bomb victims, Chapter 15's Cameroonian communities, Chapter 18's Greenlandic stakeholders must not serve as interchangeable illustrations for the book's theme.
5. **Editorial repetition.** The first-pass draft repeats the distinction between evidence and folklore; retain it where the reader needs it but cut repeated declarative lectures. Chapters must be linked by genuine changes in scale and consequence, not identical endings. Several chapters are just above 3,000 words; deepen only where a documented person, decision or causal mechanism warrants it.
6. **Review risks.** Do not turn historical weapons into practical instructions; descriptions should remain historical and high-level. Do not invent direct eyewitness access, scenes, dialogue or motives. Respect source copyright and permissions, and inspect any planned images or archival reproductions separately.
7. **Release state.** This ledger supports an expanded *working manuscript*, not a publication-ready label. Full literary read, line edit, final source verification, metadata checks and rights review remain open. No Shelf release authorized.
