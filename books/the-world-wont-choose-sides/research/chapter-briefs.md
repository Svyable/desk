# Chapter Briefs

## Prologue — The Refusal
Establish the failure of the two-color map and define selective alignment as a portfolio strategy rather than neutrality.

## 1 — The World Won’t Choose Sides
Define the mechanism: assets, alternatives, and divisible issues. Introduce the possibility of a crisis “margin call.”

## 2 — Strategic Autonomy Is Not Neutrality
Trace the intellectual and historical line from nonalignment to contemporary multi-alignment without pretending they are identical.

## 3 — Geography as a Portfolio
Show how straits, ports, borders, bases, sea lanes, and regional access turn location into bargaining leverage.

## 4 — India’s Many Alignments
Test strategic autonomy against the Quad, Russia ties, Gulf energy, defense procurement, BRICS, and technology policy.

## 5 — The Gulf’s New Optionality
Explain how Asian energy demand, sovereign wealth, regional security, and technology ambitions widened Gulf choices.

## 6 — Saudi Arabia’s Two Markets
Follow the asymmetry between U.S.-centered security and Asia-centered energy demand.

## 7 — The UAE as Switchboard
Use logistics, finance, ports, technology, and defense ties to test whether connectivity itself becomes power.

## 8 — Turkey Inside and Outside
Examine how an ally bargains with the alliance and what treaty membership does and does not require.

## 9 — Indonesia’s Quiet Scale
Test non-alignment in a state whose population, minerals, sea lanes, and industrial ambitions make refusal economically consequential.

## 10 — Brazil and the Power of Distance
Study how continental scale and geographic distance from Eurasian flashpoints create room for diplomatic independence.

## 11 — South Africa and the Politics of Memory
Examine how anti-apartheid history, Global South politics, trade, and institutions shape responses to Western pressure.

## 12 — Weapons Without Allegiance
Compare diversified procurement, indigenous production, interoperability costs, and political conditions attached to arms.

## 13 — Energy Without Camps
Trace oil, gas, uranium, renewables, grids, and critical minerals as both dependencies and leverage.

## 14 — Chips, Clouds, and Standards
Show where digital infrastructure can create alignment even when political language rejects blocs.

## 15 — The Sanctions Test
Study moments when financial networks and secondary sanctions force a concrete choice.

## 16 — Votes That Do Not Add Up to a Bloc
Use BRICS, G20, UN voting, and issue coalitions to distinguish institutional dissatisfaction from strategic unity.

## 17 — What Great Powers Keep Getting Wrong
Compare U.S. and Chinese attempts to convert one-domain cooperation into broader loyalty.

## 18 — The Leverage of Not Choosing
State the conditions under which optionality survives and the moments when a state really does have to choose.
