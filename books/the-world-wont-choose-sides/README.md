*Middle Powers and the New Balance of Power*

The working thesis is that the defining geopolitical fact of a more competitive world is not the formation of two clean camps. It is the growing leverage of states that refuse to make one relationship answer every strategic need. India can work with the United States in the Quad while buying Russian energy. Saudi Arabia can depend on American security ties while deepening economic links with China. Indonesia can defend a non-aligned tradition while courting capital and technology from every direction. Turkey can remain inside NATO while bargaining with actors NATO regards as adversaries.

This is not neutrality. It is portfolio statecraft: selective alignment across security, energy, technology, trade, finance, and institutions. The book asks when that strategy creates real autonomy, when it becomes dependence with better branding, and what great powers misunderstand when they demand loyalty from countries whose interests do not fit neatly inside a bloc.
