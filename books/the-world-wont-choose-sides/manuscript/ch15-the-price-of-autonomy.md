# The Price of Autonomy

Strategic autonomy sounds like freedom.

In practice, it is often redundancy.

Multiple weapons suppliers.

Multiple energy sources.

Multiple payment routes.

Domestic industrial capacity.

Larger foreign-exchange reserves.

Sovereign cloud infrastructure.

More diplomatic missions.

More expensive logistics.

Optionality costs money.

The cheapest system usually concentrates.

One supplier. One standard. One alliance. One market. One logistics chain.

Concentration can maximize efficiency in stable conditions.

Autonomy purchases insurance against political change.

This is why middle-power strategies should be evaluated economically, not only rhetorically.

A government that wants multiple suppliers must pay for integration. A country that wants domestic industry may accept higher costs. A state that refuses an alliance guarantee may need to spend more on its own defense.

The price can become too high.

A strategy of never choosing may produce inferior capabilities across several domains. Partners may withhold their best technology. Investors may dislike regulatory fragmentation. Military forces can become difficult to sustain.

Autonomy therefore has an efficient frontier.

The objective is not maximum independence.

It is enough independence to preserve bargaining freedom in the decisions that matter most.

This requires prioritization.

Which dependencies could become coercive?

Which can be tolerated because switching remains easy?

Which sovereign capabilities are worth subsidizing?

Which alliance commitments actually increase freedom by reducing vulnerability?

The paradox is important.

Sometimes the most autonomous choice is deeper alignment.

A reliable security guarantee can free a state from spending resources on threats it cannot handle alone. A common technology standard can give access to a larger ecosystem. A trade agreement can reduce dependence on a single market.

Autonomy is not distance from partners.

It is the ability to choose partnerships without being trapped by them.
