# Back Matter

## Acknowledgments

This Desk draft draws on official diplomatic and economic records, trade and investment data, arms and infrastructure reporting, academic and policy research on strategic autonomy, and current reporting on the behavior of pivotal states. Errors of interpretation remain the author's.

## Note on sources and method

The manuscript treats selective alignment as observable behavior rather than a label assigned by preference. A state may cooperate deeply with the United States in one domain, trade heavily with China in another, maintain legacy defense ties with Russia, participate in BRICS or other non-Western institutions, and resist permanent dependence on any one partner.

Three categories are kept separate: documented behavior, the manuscript's interpretation of that behavior, and projections about how the pattern might hold or break in a future crisis. Terms such as middle power, hedging, strategic autonomy, and multi-alignment are analytic shorthand; they do not substitute for the underlying record.

Current facts are especially perishable in this book. Trade shares, sovereign-wealth allocations, technology partnerships, arms purchases, sanctions exposure, infrastructure deals, institutional memberships, and leadership positions should be date-stamped and rechecked near publication.

## Source notes by part

**Framework and leverage.** Economic scale, geography, capital, industrial capacity, military relationships, and institutional access are treated as sources of option value rather than guarantees of bargaining success.

**Country chapters.** India, the Gulf states, Indonesia, Turkey, Brazil, South Africa, and other pivotal states are analyzed through concrete behavior across security, trade, technology, finance, and diplomacy. No chapter assumes that one vote, purchase, summit, or institutional membership reveals a permanent geopolitical identity.

**Infrastructure and technology.** Ports, cloud systems, payment rails, arms relationships, and technology supply chains are treated as forms of dependence that can create leverage without automatically producing political loyalty.

**Crisis and order.** The final chapters test the thesis under pressure. Selective alignment may be easier in peacetime than during war, sanctions escalation, or acute security crises. Evidence of harder bloc consolidation is treated as genuine counterevidence rather than an exception to be explained away.

## Revision note

This is a complete first Desk draft, not a frozen current-affairs snapshot. Shelf publication should follow a near-release fact sweep and final falsification pass. The book's thesis remains contingent: if pivotal states begin accepting durable bloc discipline across most major domains, the manuscript should say so rather than preserve its title by force.
