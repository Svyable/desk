# Contradictions and Incentives

## Core contradictions

**Observation versus history:** Sweetman et al. report positive oxygen slopes; multiple earlier nodule-area chamber studies cited by critics did not.

**Voltage versus power:** measurable surface potentials do not by themselves establish sufficient sustained current or free-energy source for water splitting.

**Nodule association versus controls:** the original paper emphasizes nodule-only ex situ production; critics argue negative-control behavior points to apparatus artefact.

**Biological suppression versus chemical disturbance:** poison-treated cores argue against known biology but the poison itself changes chemistry.

**Urgency versus uncertainty:** mining decisions are advancing while the phenomenon remains under editorial and experimental dispute.

## Incentives

The original research received support from entities with seabed-mining interests and disclosed that support. The Metals Company subsequently criticized the result, creating incentives on both sides rather than a simple sponsor-aligned narrative. Environmental organizations have incentives to foreground precautionary interpretations. Mining firms have incentives to foreground artefact interpretations. Journals and media reward novelty. Regulators face critical-mineral and environmental mandates simultaneously.

None of these incentives decides the measurement. They increase the value of independent cruises, preregistration, blinded analysis and raw-data release.
