# 4. The Optode Is Part of the Ocean

An oxygen optode does not contain a tiny person counting molecules. It infers oxygen from the behavior of a luminophore whose signal changes with quenching.

That distinction becomes important when the claim is a sign reversal in a flux that is normally negative. Pressure, temperature, drift, calibration history, response time and fouling are not housekeeping. They are candidate physics.

The original team reported calibration work and complementary Winkler titrations in ex situ experiments. Critics nonetheless focused on inconsistencies in starting oxygen concentrations and chamber behavior. The argument is useful because it points toward an experiment that does not require anyone to trust anyone else's sensor.

Run orthogonal chemistry. Put two independent oxygen-sensing technologies in the same chamber. Take discrete samples for Winkler titration. Add an internal standard. Blind the chamber identity from the analysts. Archive raw voltage or phase-lifetime traces rather than only processed oxygen curves.

A genuine oxygen source should survive a change of detector principle.

This sounds almost insultingly elementary. Many important anomalies live precisely in the distance between `elementary` and `actually done`.
