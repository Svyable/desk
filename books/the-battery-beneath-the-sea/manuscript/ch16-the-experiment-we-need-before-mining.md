# 16. The Experiment We Need Before Mining

The next experiment should be designed so that both camps can lose.

Deploy matched benthic landers across nodule-rich and naturally nodule-poor control sites. Randomize chamber assignments. Include inert nodule-shaped ceramics, washed natural nodules, undisturbed nodule patches and sediment-only chambers. Use two independent oxygen-sensing methods plus discrete Winkler samples. Measure dissolved hydrogen, peroxide, pH, redox potential, temperature and relevant metal species.

Instrument the electrochemistry separately. Record open-circuit potential, current under known loads, impedance and integrated charge. Characterize nodule mineral oxidation states before and after incubation. If oxygen is electrochemical, require a closed electron and energy balance.

Most important, preregister the analysis. Define what slope counts as production, how drift is corrected, how failed sensors are handled, and what statistical comparison constitutes replication before the chamber identities are unblinded.

Run the hardware through full-pressure blank descents. Archive every raw trace publicly.

Then repeat the campaign with a team that did not build the first lander.

This protocol is deliberately boring. That is a compliment. Boring experiments are how extraordinary claims stop being sociological objects and become measurements.
