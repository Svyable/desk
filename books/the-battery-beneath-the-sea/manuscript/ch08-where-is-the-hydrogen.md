# 8. Where Is the Hydrogen?

The cleanest objection to the water-splitting story is a missing product.

If the net process is conventional electrolysis of water, oxygen evolution at one half-reaction is paired with reduction chemistry at the other. In the simplest account, hydrogen appears. Critics emphasized that hydrogen was not demonstrated in the original field result.

Absence of a hydrogen measurement is not the same as measurement of absence. Hydrogen can dissolve, diffuse, react, feed microbes or be consumed by mineral surfaces. But once hydrogen is allowed to vanish through secondary pathways, those pathways become testable obligations.

A serious replication should use dissolved-hydrogen sensors, headspace-free sampling, isotope tracers and measurements of reduced species. It should track electrons, not just oxygen molecules.

The reason is epistemic. A single anomalous product invites stories. Paired products constrain stories.

Physics gets much harder to fool when both sides of the equation are instrumented.
