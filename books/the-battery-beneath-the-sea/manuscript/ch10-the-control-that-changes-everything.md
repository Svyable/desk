# 10. The Control That Changes Everything

The dark-oxygen debate now turns on a brutally simple object: a chamber without nodules.

The 2025 Frontiers critique argues that omitted negative-control data showed oxygen increases even when nodules were absent, pointing toward lander or chamber artefact rather than nodule production. The original paper, by contrast, emphasized nodule-only ex situ incubations as support for association with nodules.

Those claims cannot be resolved by rhetoric about extraordinary evidence. They require complete release of the control series with deployment history and raw traces.

A negative control is not a ceremonial box to tick. It measures the experiment's tendency to manufacture the phenomenon.

This is especially important at abyssal pressure because a chamber can trap gas, disturb water, or change sensor conditions in ways that are invisible in a shipboard blank. Controls must travel through the same descent, bottom contact, closure and recovery sequence as experimental chambers.

The decisive comparison is not nodule chamber versus imagination. It is nodule chamber versus a nearly identical piece of hardware that experienced the same ocean but not the hypothesized cause.
