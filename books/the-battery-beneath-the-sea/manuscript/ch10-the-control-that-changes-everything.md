# 10. The Control That Changes Everything

A chamber came back with oxygen rising and no nodules inside it.

That is the awkward piece of the dark-oxygen story. The 2025 critique by Patrick Downes and colleagues says control deployments containing bottom water but no nodules also showed oxygen increases. It points to a second problem as well: western Clarion-Clipperton Zone chamber data used in the 2024 paper had previously been described in a 2023 publication as coming from chambers without nodules. If those descriptions are correct, the experiment accidentally supplied the comparison its central interpretation most needed.

The distinction matters. Oxygen rising without nodules does not prove that the optode invented the signal, and it does not prove that no light-independent oxygen chemistry exists on the abyssal seafloor. Sediment, dissolved species, chamber materials, trapped air, pressure history or some disturbance-triggered reaction could still be involved. What it damages directly is the narrower inference that nodules were necessary for the in-situ rise.

Downes and colleagues go further. They argue that the chambers began some deployments at oxygen concentrations inconsistent with ambient bottom water and interpret that mismatch, together with the nodule-free increases, as evidence of incomplete ventilation and residual air. That is a plausible artefact model because it makes two observations with one cause. It is not yet the same thing as reproducing the full signal by deliberately trapping a known amount of air in an otherwise identical descent.

There is also evidence pulling the other way. Sweetman and colleagues reported ex-situ incubations in which nodules alone were associated with oxygen increases, and in January 2026 Sweetman publicly disputed the trapped-bubble explanation, saying his group had used the landers for years without encountering bubbles and had run tests intended to exclude them. Those claims keep the argument alive, but they do not make the missing controls disappear. A shipboard nodule incubation and a four-kilometre lander deployment are different experimental systems.

The useful control therefore has to be boring in an unusually expensive way. Two chambers should descend together, vent together, strike bottom together, close on the same schedule and carry duplicate oxygen sensors. One gets the hypothesized cause; the other does not. Better still, the series should separate nodules, nodule-free sediment, bottom water alone, inert nodule-shaped material and deliberately introduced microbubbles, with chamber identities blinded during analysis and every raw sensor trace released.

Then the possible explanations stop sharing the same evidence. A nodule-specific source should track nodules. A sediment process should survive their removal. A pressure or ventilation artefact should follow the hardware. Deliberately seeded bubbles should reproduce the characteristic curve if residual air is the culprit.

The controversy is more interesting than a missing checkbox. Somewhere in the existing record may already be the observation that tells us what the famous oxygen rise was not. The problem is that a control only acquires that power when its history is as visible as the result it contradicts.
