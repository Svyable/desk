# 6. Electrolysis Must Pay the Bill

Water splitting is not impressed by metaphor.

At standard conditions, the reversible potential for overall electrolysis is about 1.23 V, and real electrodes usually require additional overpotential. The 2024 paper knew this and discussed catalytic pathways, manganese oxides, transition metals, defect sites and a lattice-oxygen-mediated mechanism that might lower effective barriers.

The critics' objection is not that catalysis is imaginary. It is that catalysis does not create free energy. A catalyst can lower kinetic barriers. It cannot make an energetically uphill net reaction run forever without a coupled downhill process.

That distinction is the heart of the thermodynamic critique.

If nodules produce oxygen electrochemically, something else must be consumed or transformed. Name it. Measure it. The mineral should change oxidation state, a reductant should disappear, hydrogen or another reduced product should appear, or an external energy source should enter.

The phrase `internal redistribution of electrons` is not yet a fuel.

This chapter does not declare the mechanism impossible. Natural electrochemical gradients exist everywhere from corrosion cells to hydrothermal systems. It demands stoichiometry. For every mole of oxygen, where did the four electrons come from? What accepted them? What chemical potential paid for the separation?

An extraordinary claim becomes ordinary science the moment its ledger closes.
