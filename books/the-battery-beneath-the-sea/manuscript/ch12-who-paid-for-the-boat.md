# 12. Who Paid for the Boat

The original work was partly funded by The Metals Company through Nauru Ocean Resources, a company with exploration rights in the same mineral province. The company later became one of the most forceful critics of the dark-oxygen interpretation.

That sequence ruins an easy conspiracy story.

Industry funding does not make the original result true or false. Industry criticism does not make the critique corrupt. What funding changes is the incentive map: site access, cruise logistics, reputational stakes, commercial timelines and the value of particular interpretations.

The scientists disclosed support. Critics with mining affiliations should be read with the same attention to incentives. Environmental organizations amplifying the finding also have policy incentives. Journalists have novelty incentives. Journals have attention incentives. Governments have critical-mineral incentives.

None of this substitutes for an oxygen measurement.

The mature use of conflict-of-interest information is not to decide whom to believe. It is to decide which measurements must be made independent of belief.

Blind analysis and independent cruises are institutional technologies for doing exactly that.
