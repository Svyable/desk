# 9. The Poisoned Core

One of the original team's strongest arguments against ordinary biological production was ugly but useful: poison the biology.

Mercury chloride was added to ex situ core incubations, yet oxygen increases were still reported. The authors noted caveats—some microbes can detoxify mercury and microhabitats may escape complete exposure—but used the result to argue that known oxygen-producing microbes were unlikely to explain the bulk effect.

That is credible but incomplete evidence, not closure.

A poison control changes more than biology. Mercury can alter surface chemistry. Killing cells releases substrates. Sterilization can change redox states. The proper question is whether the effect survives multiple independent ways of suppressing biological metabolism without creating the same geochemical perturbation.

Gamma sterilization, filtration, autoclaving and metabolic inhibitors each have different artefacts. None is perfect. Their disagreement is information.

If oxygen appears only under one sterilization protocol, the chemistry of the control becomes suspect. If it persists across orthogonal methods and nodule-only systems, a nonbiological mechanism becomes much harder to dismiss.

The poisoned core was not the end of the biological question. It was the beginning of a better experimental design.
