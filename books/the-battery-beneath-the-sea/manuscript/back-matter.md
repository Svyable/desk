# Back Matter

## Chronology

- **1989** — DISCOL disturbance experiment ploughs a Peru Basin nodule field.
- **2015–2019** — revisits document persistent biological effects decades later.
- **22 July 2024** — Sweetman et al. publish *Evidence of dark oxygen production at the abyssal seafloor* in Nature Geoscience.
- **2025** — technical criticism intensifies; Downes et al. publish a detailed Frontiers critique arguing the claim conflicts with prior chamber data and thermodynamic constraints.
- **2025** — a Nature study reports persistent biological impacts around four decades after historical test-mining disturbance.
- **21 January 2026** — NOAA issues a final rule revising U.S. deep-seabed mining application procedures.
- **28 January 2026** — Nature reports plans for new robotic field work to test dark oxygen.
- **8 April 2026** — Nature Geoscience adds an Editor's Note to the 2024 paper saying aspects are under concern and editorial review.
- **2026** — U.S. exploration/commercial-recovery applications advance under DSHMRA while ISA exploitation rules remain under development.

## Defeat conditions

The book's central scientific concern weakens sharply if independent pressure-clean experiments with matched controls show no oxygen production and identify a reproducible chamber/sensor artefact sufficient to explain the original signal.

The electrolysis hypothesis is defeated if no stoichiometrically adequate redox partner, current capacity or energy source can be demonstrated under realistic conditions even when oxygen production is independently reproduced.

The policy argument remains partly intact because long-lived mining disturbance is supported independently of dark oxygen.

## Research priorities

Recover complete raw lander traces and negative controls; reproduce optode calibration under pressure; perform full electrochemical charge balance; measure hydrogen and reactive intermediates; compare nodule-rich and nodule-poor sites prospectively; build a study-by-study prior chamber database; quantify ecological significance of any replicated flux; follow the Nature Geoscience editorial process and 2026 robotic campaigns.
