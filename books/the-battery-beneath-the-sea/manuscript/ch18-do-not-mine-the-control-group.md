# 18. Do Not Mine the Control Group

There is a phrase experimentalists use without ceremony: preserve the control.

Humanity is now learning to apply that idea at landscape scale.

The Clarion-Clipperton Zone contains places likely to be disturbed, places intended as reference areas, contract blocks, protected areas and sites scientists have barely sampled. If industrial collection begins broadly before the dark-oxygen controversy and other baseline questions are resolved, future investigators may inherit an experiment whose pre-intervention state exists mainly in spreadsheets.

This is not an argument that one contested paper should veto all seabed mining. It is an argument about sequencing.

First determine whether oxygen production is reproducible. Determine whether it scales with nodule abundance and surface state. Determine whether it is continuous, episodic or purely disturbance-induced. Determine whether the energy and electron balance closes. Determine whether the phenomenon matters to local ecology rather than merely occurring at chemically tiny rates.

If the answers are no, policy loses a weak argument and gains knowledge. If yes, mining models gain a missing process before equipment removes the substrate.

The strange privilege of this moment is that the decisive experiments are still possible.

A civilization can be forgiven for not knowing what the abyss does in darkness. It is harder to forgive destroying the comparison before asking.
