# 11. Five Experiments That Saw No Miracle

The strongest case against a new phenomenon is rarely `I dislike the mechanism`. It is prior apparatus that should have seen it and did not.

Downes and colleagues pointed to five independent studies using comparable benthic-chamber approaches in nodule-rich regions without reporting oxygen production resembling the 2024 claim. That historical record creates a real replication problem.

But nulls must be compared at the level of conditions. Were nodule surface areas similar? Did chambers disturb sediment in the same way? Were oxygen sensors equally sensitive? Were deployments long enough? Were the old studies optimized to measure consumption and therefore prone to discard positive slopes as artefacts?

A phenomenon can hide in protocol differences. An artefact can also be born from them.

The correct next step is a harmonized table of every relevant deployment: coordinates, nodule abundance, chamber geometry, pressure history, mixing, sensor model, calibration, duration, oxygen trajectory, sediment disturbance and data exclusions. The literature should be turned into a dataset.

If dark oxygen is real but conditional, the conditions should emerge. If it is apparatus-specific, that should emerge too.
