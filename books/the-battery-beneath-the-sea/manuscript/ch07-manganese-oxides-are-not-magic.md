# 7. Manganese Oxides Are Not Magic

Manganese oxides are chemically rich enough to encourage bad sentences.

They have multiple oxidation states, high surface areas, catalytic behavior, defects, tunnels and strong interactions with trace metals. Nodules are not electrically uniform pebbles. That complexity makes them plausible sites for redox chemistry.

It does not make every redox pathway plausible.

Sweetman and colleagues explicitly modeled one possible oxygen-liberating manganese reaction and concluded that under seafloor conditions it could explain only a negligible fraction of the observed signal. That negative result deserves more attention than the geobattery metaphor because it demonstrates the correct instinct: calculate the maximum inventory and ask whether it is big enough.

The same discipline should now be extended across all proposed mineral pathways. Measure Mn(II), Mn(III), Mn(IV), iron states, dissolved metals, peroxide, reactive oxygen species and surface mineralogy through time. Determine whether oxygen production scales with a chemically finite reservoir or persists after repeated cycles.

If the effect exhausts, it may be a disturbance-triggered chemical transient rather than an ecological oxygen source. If it persists, the energy input must be found.

Those are radically different worlds.
