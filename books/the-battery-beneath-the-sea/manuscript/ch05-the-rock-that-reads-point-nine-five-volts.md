# 5. The Rock That Reads 0.95 Volts

The most seductive number in the dark-oxygen paper is 0.95 volts.

The team measured potential differences between platinum probes placed at different points on nodule surfaces after immersion in artificial seawater. Some measurements approached 0.95 V. A control rock did not behave the same way. It is easy to see how `geobattery` followed.

But a voltage is not an energy source.

An electrochemical cell requires coupled redox reactions, charge transport, reactants, products and a free-energy budget. Measuring an open-circuit potential between heterogeneous mineral surfaces can reveal electrochemical disequilibrium without establishing sustained current capable of water splitting.

This is where the controversy becomes a physics problem. The relevant question is not whether a multimeter needle moves. It is how many coulombs can move through a defined circuit before the potential collapses, which species are oxidized and reduced, and whether the integrated charge is commensurate with the oxygen claimed.

A battery can have voltage and almost no capacity. A mineral surface can hold local potentials that disappear when asked to do work.

The next experiment therefore needs polarization curves, impedance spectroscopy, current integration, product accounting, mineral-state changes before and after discharge, and a closed charge balance. The oxygen curve alone is downstream evidence. The electrochemical pathway should be caught in the act.
