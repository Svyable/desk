# Woods replication boundary — Conditions Not Met

## Editorial purpose

The opening currently has the right instinct—separate a null result from a decisive reproduction—but it risks making apparatus mismatch itself too narratively exculpatory. This note reconstructs the strongest version of the Woods/Sheffield result available from the primary conference paper and NASA records, then states what the manuscript may and may not infer.

## Primary record recovered

R. C. Woods, S. G. Cooke, J. Helme, and C. H. Caldwell, “Gravity Modification by High-Temperature Superconductors,” AIAA 2001-3363, 37th Joint Propulsion Conference, July 2001. DOI: https://doi.org/10.2514/6.2001-3363

An accessible copy of the conference paper is indexed at:
https://hiroko.or.jp/wp-content/file/gravity-control/Gravitational%20shielding/ShieldingBySuperconductivity/AIAA/GravityModificationByHigh-TemperatureSuperconductors.pdf

Use the AIAA DOI as the reader-facing bibliographic anchor. Treat the third-party PDF only as an access copy to be checked against the publisher record.

The paper reports no measurable gravity modification within approximately ±0.03% for the tested subset of conditions. More importantly for this book, the authors themselves say their tests did not fulfil all conditions specified for the claimed Podkletnov effect. Their experiment therefore supplies a quantitative null for the configuration actually tested; it does not by itself establish that the complete claimed apparatus was reproduced.

## NASA engineering record

NASA NTRS record 20020068834, Larry Smalley, “Consequences of Coupled Electromagnetic-Gravitational Fields,” NASA/CR-2002-211840 (July 2002):
https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20020068834

This NASA record says the experiments had not been successfully repeated because of difficulty producing stable fired superconducting disks, reports that the firing problem had been solved by Li, and says installation of the disk in the motor under superconducting temperatures and tailored magnetic fields still remained. This is useful evidence about the state of NASA-associated engineering work in 2002. It is not evidence for a gravity effect.

## Claim ladder the manuscript should preserve

1. Podkletnov and Nieminen published an anomalous weight-change claim in 1992.
2. Woods et al. tested a subset of the claimed operating conditions and obtained a null result at roughly ±0.03% sensitivity.
3. Woods et al. acknowledged that important specified conditions were not fulfilled.
4. NASA-associated records likewise document fabrication and apparatus-completion problems.
5. Therefore those experiments constrain the configurations they actually tested more strongly than they constrain a fully specified Podkletnov apparatus.
6. None of this supplies positive evidence that the omitted conditions would produce gravity modification.
7. More than three decades without a robust, independent positive reproduction is itself powerful negative evidence against the phenomenon, especially because a real percent-scale effect should eventually become reproducible if the apparatus can be specified and built.

The book becomes weaker if it stops at proposition 5. The intellectually serious tension is between 5 and 7.

## Strongest counterargument

The claimant bears a replication burden too. A scientific claim cannot indefinitely protect itself by adding fabrication details, tacit knowledge, unavailable materials, or operating conditions after null results. If a percent-scale effect exists only in a device that independent laboratories cannot build from the public specification, the epistemic failure is not merely the replicators'. The specification itself has failed to become transferable scientific knowledge.

This is stronger than saying “the null experiments were imperfect.” It creates two independent ways for the claim to lose:

- a faithful, preregistered reproduction meets the claimed conditions and finds no effect; or
- after serious, transparent engineering effort, the claimed apparatus remains insufficiently specified or reproducible for independent laboratories to instantiate it.

The second outcome does not prove the underlying physical effect impossible. It does justify withholding scientific belief and prevents “conditions not met” from becoming an immortal escape hatch.

## Manuscript consequence

Chapter 1 should eventually replace any implication that the historical problem is simply that skeptics ran the wrong experiment with the harder problem: **how much parameter mismatch weakens a null result, and at what point does an untransferable recipe become evidence against the claim itself?**

That gives Chapters 7–9 a genuine argumentative progression rather than three versions of the same caveat:

- Chapter 7: what Woods actually tested and bounded;
- Chapter 8: parameter closure across all serious attempts;
- Chapter 9: the point at which tacit knowledge and bespoke apparatus cease to excuse nonreplication.

## Factual holds

Before manuscript revision, recover and page-index the publisher copy of AIAA 2001-3363 if accessible, including exact sensitivity, disk dimensions, temperature, field/levitation configuration, rotation state, sensor geometry, and the authors’ wording about unmet conditions. Do not rely on a later review for those numbers when the primary paper is available.

Also reconstruct NASA NTRS 19990019627 and 19990046249 experiment-by-experiment. “NASA tested Podkletnov” is too coarse a sentence unless each configuration, material, measurement precision, and claimed condition is explicit.

Do not state that no independent positive replication exists until a citation-level replication audit has checked the published record, including claimed unpublished/Toronto/Sheffield reproductions and later Hathaway work. The absence of an obvious publication in a search is not enough.
