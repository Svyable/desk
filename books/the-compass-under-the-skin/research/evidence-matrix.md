# Evidence matrix — The Compass Under the Skin

| Claim | Class | Current support | What would strengthen it | What would defeat or sharply narrow it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Birds can use Earth’s magnetic field as a compass | 1 — verified/documented | Decades of orientation experiments in which changing field direction changes preferred heading; replicated across species | Larger preregistered multi-lab datasets with raw trajectories | Robust failures under correctly reproduced field/light conditions across labs and species |
| The avian compass is an inclination compass rather than a polarity compass | 1 | Classic behavioral reversal experiments and later syntheses | Modern automated replication across taxa | Consistent polarity-dependent orientation under conditions excluding alternative cues |
| Light-dependent radical-pair chemistry contributes to the avian compass | 2 — credible/incomplete | Wavelength dependence, RF disruption, visual-pathway evidence, cryptochrome biophysics | Genetic or molecular perturbation that selectively removes compass behavior and rescues it | Intact compass after specific elimination of the required radical-pair chemistry under appropriate light |
| CRY4 is the in-vivo avian magnetoreceptor | 2 | Retinal expression/localization, seasonal/context evidence, 2021 in-vitro magnetic sensitivity | Necessity/sufficiency experiments in an intact bird; causal neural pathway | Normal compass behavior with selective CRY4 loss plus validated loss of CRY4 function |
| Urban RF noise can disrupt some bird magnetic-compass behavior | 1 for tested conditions; 2 for ecological scale | Double-blind Oldenburg shielding/noise experiments; later RF-frequency work | Independent field-scale replication tied to migration outcomes | Repeated high-powered failures to reproduce behavioral disruption under matched spectra/intensities |
| Iron-rich pigeon-beak dendrites are the receptor | 3 — disputed/not established | The historically claimed cells were reidentified as macrophages in 2012 | A different, innervated, reproducible receptor structure would constitute a new claim, not rescue the old one | Already substantially defeated for the original anatomical claim |
| A particle-based receptor contributes magnetic map/intensity information | 2 | Pulse experiments, trigeminal evidence, map behavior across taxa | Reproducible receptor anatomy plus perturbation and neural recording | Consistent null perturbations and identification of a non-particle mechanism for map information |
| Sea turtles use geomagnetic information in natal homing | 2, with strong behavioral/ecological support | Magnetic-map experiments and geomagnetic-imprinting correlations | Longitudinal tracking with controlled magnetic manipulation | Population/nesting patterns and experimental orientation explained by nonmagnetic cues under stronger controls |
| Humans transduce Earth-strength magnetic rotations neurally | 2 | 2019 alpha-ERD result with directional/polarity specificity and public data | Independent preregistered replication with artifact-resistant EEG/MEG and within-subject effects | High-powered independent failures under matched protocol or discovery of a field-correlated artifact |
| Humans possess a useful conscious magnetic sense | 3 | No convincing behavioral demonstration follows from EEG alone | Reproducible above-chance behavioral discrimination linked to neural response | Continued behavioral nulls despite replicated neural transduction |
| Vertebrate magnetoreception uses one universal receptor | 4 — speculative and currently disfavored | Narrative simplicity only; diverse taxa/behaviors suggest otherwise | Homologous receptor/pathway explaining compass and map functions across taxa | Clear demonstration of distinct physical mechanisms for distinct magnetic tasks |

## Book-level defeat condition

The manuscript’s central synthesis—that behavior is more secure than receptor identity and that magnetic navigation may be mechanistically plural—would need major revision if a single receptor/pathway were causally demonstrated to be necessary and sufficient for both compass and map functions across the key vertebrate systems discussed here. Conversely, failure of CRY4 specifically would not defeat magnetoreception; it would defeat one mechanism claim while leaving the behavioral phenomenon intact.
