# Publication gate — The Compass Under the Skin

This run establishes an 18-chapter working manuscript and research architecture. It does **not** claim to meet Desk’s automated full-book minimum of 3,000 words per chapter / 54,000+ words. The GitHub connector does not execute the repository’s local length and editorial scripts, and the first-pass chapters are intentionally not padded to simulate completion.

Before publication or catalog/Reader promotion, the next pass must:

1. Recover and page-index the foundational Wiltschko orientation experiments rather than relying on later reviews for historical details.
2. Build a protocol-level ledger of inclination-compass reversals, light-wavelength results and magnetic-field intensity “functional window” experiments.
3. Reconstruct the radical-pair mechanism quantitatively enough to show Earth-field energy scales, spin-state mixing, reaction yields, anisotropy and why RF perturbation is mechanistically informative without pretending the bird computes quantum mechanics.
4. Expand CRY1/CRY4 candidate history and include genetic/molecular evidence from other taxa that complicates a CRY4-exclusive story.
5. Reconstruct the 2014 Oldenburg noise experiments and later replications/constraints by spectrum, intensity, shielding, blinding, species and assay.
6. Rebuild the 2020 local-eye RF null experiment and the 2022 75–85 MHz positive result on common physical units and explicit model predictions.
7. Build the pigeon-beak anatomical history from original histology through the 2012 macrophage correction, identifying exactly which prior claims were defeated and which trigeminal findings survived.
8. Construct a magnetic-pulse ledger including cage, free-flight, age and migration-stage effects, emphasizing nulls and heterogeneous outcomes.
9. Deepen sea-turtle magnetic-map and geomagnetic-imprinting work with primary experimental papers and alternative environmental explanations.
10. Add salmon, lobster/insect and mammalian cases only where they materially test whether magnetic sensing is mechanistically plural; avoid a species catalog.
11. Reproduce the 2019 human EEG analysis logic from the authors’ public data/analysis description and search systematically for independent replications, failures or artifact critiques through the publication date.
12. Separate “transduction,” “neural representation,” “conscious perception” and “navigational utility” throughout the human chapters.
13. Quantify the ecological RF question using measured urban spectra and realistic free-ranging exposure rather than laboratory threshold rhetoric.
14. Expand military/research-funding history only from public grant records; do not infer classified capability from AFOSR/DARPA support.
15. Run `scripts/check-book-length.py` (or the repository’s current equivalent), cover/metadata checks and `scripts/check-desk.py` locally.
16. Perform a whole-book anti-slop pass: remove repeated compass-versus-map explanations, canned “mystery” language, quantum hype, symmetrical contrasts and chapter endings that merely stamp the thesis.
17. Read the manuscript sequentially for chapter dependency and merge any chapters that remain interchangeable after expansion.
18. Leave shared Desk catalog and Reader surfaces untouched until the objective length gate and whole-book editorial gate pass.

## Release-blocking questions

- Is CRY4 framed as candidate rather than solved receptor everywhere?
- Are positive and null RF/pulse results represented on matched experimental terms?
- Does the book accidentally imply human health risk from avian sensory disruption?
- Are human EEG findings described as neural transduction rather than conscious magnetoreception?
- Does every “quantum” passage state the biological inference separately from the physical mechanism?
- Has the old beak-receptor claim been corrected without falsely implying that all particle-based magnetoreception is dead?
