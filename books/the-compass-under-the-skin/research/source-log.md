# Source log — The Compass Under the Skin

Checked 2026-09-13 unless noted. This log favors primary papers and authoritative reviews. Links are evidence trails, not endorsements of every interpretation in the cited paper.

## Core behavioral and mechanism sources

- Henrik Mouritsen, “Long-distance navigation and magnetoreception in migratory animals,” *Nature* 558 (2018), 50–59. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-018-0176-1 — broad review of navigation, multisensory integration, compass versus map problems, and open mechanistic questions.
- Roswitha Wiltschko and Wolfgang Wiltschko, “The Magnetic Compass of Birds: The Role of Cryptochrome,” *Frontiers in Physiology* 12 (2021). https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2021.667000 — review of avian compass behavior, inclination compass, functional window, light dependence and cryptochrome evidence.
- P. J. Hore and Henrik Mouritsen, “The Radical-Pair Mechanism of Magnetoreception,” *Annual Review of Biophysics* 45 (2016), 299–344. https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-biophys-032116-094545 — mechanism review; use for spin chemistry and explicit unresolved assumptions.
- Jingjing Xu et al., “Magnetic sensitivity of cryptochrome 4 from a migratory songbird,” *Nature* 594 (2021), 535–540. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-03618-9 — in-vitro CRY4 magnetic sensitivity; stronger response in European robin CRY4 than chicken or pigeon CRY4; does not by itself establish in-vivo receptor necessity.
- Haijia Wu et al., “Protein-protein interaction of the putative magnetoreceptor cryptochrome 4 expressed in the avian retina,” *Scientific Reports* 10 (2020), 7364. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-020-64429-y — retinal localization/context and interaction evidence.

## Radiofrequency / electromagnetic-noise evidence

- Svenja Engels et al., “Anthropogenic electromagnetic noise disrupts magnetic compass orientation in a migratory bird,” *Nature* 509 (2014), 353–356. https://doi.org/10.1038/nature13290 — double-blinded Oldenburg experiments; orientation recovered with grounded shielding and was lost again with introduced broadband noise. Important boundary: this is a specialized sensory effect in birds, not evidence of generalized human harm.
- Bo Leberecht et al., “Broadband 75–85 MHz radiofrequency fields disrupt magnetic compass orientation in night-migratory songbirds consistent with a flavin-based radical pair magnetoreceptor,” *Journal of Comparative Physiology A* 208 (2022), 97–106. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00359-021-01537-8 — frequency-targeted behavioral disruption relevant to radical-pair models.
- A. N. Pakhomov et al., “Magnetic compass of garden warblers is not affected by oscillating magnetic fields applied to their eyes,” *Scientific Reports* 10 (2020). https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-020-60383-x — important counterevidence against the most ambitious simple retinal-image/RF-disruption formulation; does not eliminate all radical-pair models.

## Magnetite / anatomical corrections

- Christoph Daniel Treiber et al., “Clusters of iron-rich cells in the upper beak of pigeons are macrophages not magnetosensitive neurons,” *Nature* 484 (2012), 367–370. https://doi.org/10.1038/nature11046 — decisive correction to a previously influential anatomical interpretation.
- Roswitha Wiltschko and Wolfgang Wiltschko, “The magnetite-based receptors in the beak of birds and their role in avian navigation,” *Journal of Comparative Physiology A* 199 (2013), 89–98. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00359-012-0769-3 — review of trigeminal and magnetic-intensity evidence after the beak-cell correction; useful for separating pathway from receptor identity.
- Wolfgang Wiltschko and Roswitha Wiltschko, “Migratory orientation of European Robins is affected by the wavelength of light as well as by a magnetic pulse,” *Journal of Comparative Physiology A* 177 (1995), 363–369. https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00192425 — early evidence motivating a two-component compass/map interpretation.
- “A magnetic pulse does not affect free-flight navigation behaviour of a medium-distance songbird migrant in spring,” *Journal of Experimental Biology* (2022). https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.244473 — null free-flight result that constrains confident particle-based map claims.

## Magnetic maps and geomagnetic imprinting

- J. Roger Brothers and Kenneth J. Lohmann, “Evidence for geomagnetic imprinting and magnetic navigation in the natal homing of sea turtles,” *Current Biology* 25 (2015), 392–396. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2014.12.035 — population-level evidence linking magnetic field signatures and nesting geography; inferential rather than direct receptor identification.
- Kenneth J. Lohmann and colleagues’ magnetic-map experiments in sea turtles are treated as a separate evidence stream from avian compass mechanism. Primary papers should be page-indexed in the next pass before quantitative claims are tightened.

## Human magnetoreception

- Connie X. Wang et al., “Transduction of the Geomagnetic Field as Evidenced from alpha-Band Activity in the Human Brain,” *eNeuro* 6 (2019), ENEURO.0483-18.2019. https://doi.org/10.1523/ENEURO.0483-18.2019 — reports repeatable alpha-band desynchronization to selected Earth-strength field rotations and polarity dependence. The result is neural transduction evidence, not proof of conscious perception, navigation, or ecological function. Data and analysis scripts were made public by the authors.

## Mechanism development after CRY4

- Matt C. J. Denton et al., “Magnetosensitivity of tightly bound radical pairs in cryptochrome is enabled by the quantum Zeno effect,” *Nature Communications* 15 (2024), 10823. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-55124-x — theoretical/mechanistic work extending plausible radical-pair configurations; not in-vivo proof.

## Claim-handling rules

- “Animals orient magnetically” is not equivalent to “cryptochrome is proven to be the receptor.”
- “A purified protein is magnetically sensitive” is not equivalent to “that protein is necessary and sufficient in the intact animal.”
- “A neural response exists” is not equivalent to “a conscious sense exists.”
- “RF noise disrupts a bird compass” is not evidence that ordinary RF exposures produce generalized pathology in humans.
- “Iron-containing tissue exists” is not evidence that it is a receptor. Chain anatomical identity to innervation and perturbation evidence.
- Competing magnetic channels are allowed. Do not force compass and map evidence into a single receptor model merely for narrative neatness.
