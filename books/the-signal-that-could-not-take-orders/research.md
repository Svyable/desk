# Research ledger — The Signal That Could Not Take Orders

## Evidence posture

**program-existence-declassified / operational-tasking-documented / Iran-hostage-tasking-documented / laboratory-statistical-anomaly-contested-but-serious / mechanism-unestablished / operational-utility-poor-in-1995-review / celebrated-cases-denominator-incomplete / Soviet-threat-perception-documented-but-capability-claims-need-audit / paranormal-certainty-not-warranted / institutional-option-value-thesis-interpretive.**

## Four-class working ledger

### 1 — Verified / declassified / documented

- CIA publicly acknowledges that it investigated remote viewing, transferred the effort to DIA, later received the program back for review, commissioned an independent evaluation, and did not restore it after the 1995 assessment.
- Declassified GRILL FLAME session reports document actual government tasking. Iran-hostage sessions are especially valuable because the files themselves label viewer impressions as **raw intelligence data** and place responsibility for interpretation on the requester. That language is evidence of use, not evidence that the impressions were correct.
- The CIA STARGATE collection contains training sessions with explicitly poor target correlation as well as operational material. Failure records matter because any serious accuracy claim requires the denominator.
- A March 1995 declassification planning memorandum documents CIA/DIA efforts to declassify sponsorship, technical reports, targets, customers and resources where possible.
- The 1995 review process included both technical and operational-utility components. CIA's later public summary says the review found enough accurate experiences to raise a question about randomness but judged performance too unreliable, inconsistent and sporadic for intelligence use.

### 2 — Credible but incomplete

- Jessica Utts argued that controlled experimental evidence supported anomalous cognition. This is a serious statistical claim by a qualified statistician; it is not a mechanism and does not establish operational utility.
- Ray Hyman accepted that the experimental record deserved analysis while disputing whether flaws, replication structure and interpretation justified a paranormal conclusion. His objections must be reconstructed from the actual AIR material rather than reduced to “skeptic says no.”
- Program participants and customers sometimes reported striking correspondences. Those reports become evidentially useful only when target definition, cueing, transcript time, feedback, scoring rule, alternatives, misses and post-hoc matching are recoverable.

### 3 — Disputed / unconfirmed

- Claims that viewers reliably described Soviet installations, located hostages, found missing aircraft, identified submarines, penetrated sealed targets, viewed Mars, or supplied decisive intelligence cannot be promoted from anecdote by repetition. Each is a separate provenance problem.
- “The CIA proved psychic powers” is not what the released institutional record says.
- “The CIA proved remote viewing was fake” is also too clean. The 1995 dispute partly concerned what could legitimately be inferred from the experimental statistics; the operational-utility judgment is stronger and different.
- Soviet/Russian psychotronic capability claims require source-by-source auditing. U.S. threat memoranda can prove that U.S. officials worried about Soviet work without proving Soviet efficacy.

### 4 — Speculative synthesis / open conjecture

- A small, uncertain effect can rationally attract intelligence funding when the hypothetical upside is enormous and adversary activity is poorly known. That does not mean the effect exists.
- Occasional vivid correspondences may create a “lottery-ticket memory” inside institutions: the memorable hit remains available to champions while routine low-specificity sessions disappear into files. This must be tested against the operational ledger, not asserted psychologically.
- Remote viewing may be an unusually pure example of the difference between detecting an effect and engineering a capability. Even a genuine anomaly would still face reliability, calibration, bandwidth, latency, adversarial deception, attribution and decision-theory problems.

## Contradiction and incentive audit

| Claim | Counterpressure | What would close it |
|---|---|---|
| Controlled RV exceeded chance | Optional stopping, sensory leakage, judging flexibility, non-independent replications | protocol-by-protocol preregistration/history and independent reanalysis |
| Operations produced useful intelligence | selective case memory; vague descriptors; missing misses | task-level denominator with contemporaneous customer scoring |
| Government longevity implies efficacy | bureaucratic inertia, adversary fear, cheap option value, congressional interest | budget/tasking history tied to validated outcomes |
| Termination proves failure | termination can reflect utility threshold rather than metaphysical verdict | AIR technical + utility reports and decision memoranda |
| Soviet interest validates U.S. concern | mirror imaging and threat inflation | primary Soviet/Russian program records and independent results |
| Famous sessions are evidentially decisive | post-hoc matching and cue contamination | timestamped transcript, target pool, cueing, feedback and blind judges |

## Chronology skeleton

- **1970s:** CIA-supported work at SRI investigates remote viewing and related anomalous cognition claims.
- **Late 1970s:** CIA leaves direct sponsorship; defense organizations continue related work.
- **1978–1980:** Army/DIA GRILL FLAME activity develops; declassified files document both training and operational sessions.
- **1979–1981:** Iran hostage crisis generates operational remote-viewing tasking. Released reports repeatedly warn that impressions are raw data requiring requester interpretation.
- **1980s:** program names, sponsors and organizational homes change; operational and research work continue under defense sponsorship.
- **Early 1990s:** activity continues under STAR GATE nomenclature.
- **1995:** DIA transfers program responsibility/material to CIA for review; CIA commissions American Institutes for Research evaluation; technical reviewers disagree on interpretation of experimental evidence while operational evaluation finds inadequate intelligence utility.
- **1995:** CIA declines to continue the program.
- **Late 1990s onward:** large collections of records become public, creating the unusual possibility of studying both celebrated sessions and mundane failures.

## Research rules for expansion

1. Never count program existence as evidence of paranormal efficacy.
2. Never count a requester’s tasking as confirmation of the viewer’s answer.
3. Preserve misses. A case ledger without misses is unusable.
4. Separate laboratory scoring from intelligence usefulness.
5. Record whether target pools and transcripts were available to judges.
6. Distinguish contemporaneous customer evaluations from participant memoirs.
7. Treat classification as missing information, not positive evidence.
8. Treat Soviet-interest claims as threat-perception evidence until Soviet-side primary material is recovered.
9. Patents, if encountered, prove filing only.
10. Do not infer a physical mechanism from statistical performance.

## Frozen publication gate

Before publication, complete: (a) the AIR 1995 technical report page-by-page with Utts/Hyman points represented in their strongest forms; (b) the operational-utility appendix and customer interviews; (c) a denominator-complete sample of operational projects across years, not merely famous sessions; (d) SRI protocol evolution and sensory-leak controls; (e) Pat Price / coordinate-viewing celebrated-case reconstruction; (f) Iran-hostage project scoring against known outcomes; (g) Soviet/Russian source audit; (h) budget and organizational chronology; (i) statistical reanalysis with target-pool and judge independence; (j) current peer-reviewed replication/meta-analysis literature through 2026.

The manuscript may expand before these are complete, but claims gated by them must remain explicitly provisional.
