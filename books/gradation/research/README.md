# Gradation — research brief

## Research question

When should a changing condition be represented continuously, and when does a change in degree cross a threshold that makes the system behave differently in kind?

The book tests a dual claim:

1. Institutions often impose hard categories on underlying continua and then mistake the administrative boundary for a natural discontinuity.
2. Observers often assume smooth change where feedback, capacity, saturation, path dependence or propagation can produce genuine nonlinear transitions.

The useful object is therefore not “nuance” in the abstract. It is the shape of the response.

## Research method

Each chapter should identify three separate layers whenever possible:

- **Underlying state or exposure.** What actually varies?
- **Response function.** How does consequence change as the state changes?
- **Decision rule.** Where has an institution, organism or engineered system attached an action threshold?

Claims are strongest when those layers can be kept distinct. A physical phase transition can support a claim about real state change. A policy eligibility line can support a claim about imposed discontinuity. A predictive score plus an operational cutoff can support a claim about where estimation becomes governance. One kind of evidence should not be substituted for another merely because the metaphor sounds similar.

The evidence mix includes peer-reviewed research, government technical material, major institutional assessments, NBER working papers, primary historical scholarship and established engineering or physiological references. Descriptive examples that do not carry the argumentative burden may remain uncited in prose, but the factual hinges should be recoverable through the source ledger.

## What would weaken the thesis?

The book should remain vulnerable to evidence. Important falsifiers or limiting cases include:

- A purportedly continuous domain turns out to contain a stable, mechanistically grounded discontinuity at the exact point the manuscript criticizes as arbitrary.
- Dichotomization or categorization loses little decision-relevant information in the focal setting, or improves decisions enough that the lost information is immaterial.
- A claimed threshold effect disappears under better measurement, larger samples or a model that allows smooth nonlinear response.
- A claimed cascade is better explained by independent actors responding to the same outside shock rather than by propagation from one actor to another.
- A path-dependence or hysteresis story does not survive tests for persistent differences once current conditions are controlled adequately.
- A neighborhood or status gradient attributed to causal exposure is mostly selection or composition in the specific case being used.
- A benefits cliff appears severe in one program viewed alone but becomes small after the full tax-and-transfer schedule, take-up behavior and household costs are modeled.
- A policy cutoff creates local unfairness but substantially improves transparency, administrability, due process or safety relative to smoother alternatives.
- A finer continuous model performs worse because its apparent precision exceeds the reliability of the underlying data.
- A dramatic “tipping point” story is not supported by a mechanism, a discontinuity, feedback or a change in propagation.

These are not problems to hide. They are the boundary conditions of the argument.

## Research cautions

### Category does not equal error

Categories reduce complexity. They are indispensable for language, law, coordination and machine action. The manuscript should criticize the mismatch between category and underlying structure, not categorization itself.

### Smooth does not equal fair

A continuous or personalized score can make discrimination more granular, obscure appeal points or project false precision. Replacing a visible cutoff with a model is not automatically an improvement.

### Threshold does not equal tipping point

The manuscript should reserve strong threshold language for cases with a defensible change in mechanism, state, capacity, feedback or action rule. Rhetorical milestones are not enough.

### Model does not equal mechanism

Schelling-type models, threshold models and stylized dose-response curves reveal possible structures. Their empirical relevance has to be established separately.

### Correlation does not equal exposure effect

Status, geography, neighborhood, education and health are entangled with selection. Randomized, quasi-experimental and longitudinal evidence should carry more weight than cross-sectional pattern matching when causal language is used.

### Precision is not certainty

Scores, probabilities and thresholds should be described with enough uncertainty that the manuscript does not reproduce the false authority it criticizes.

## Core empirical anchors

The manuscript is built around several research anchors rather than dozens of decorative citations:

- Weber/Fechner/Stevens traditions in psychophysics: perception varies by degree and equal physical increments need not produce equal perceived increments.
- Modern color and speech-perception research: continuous sensory spaces can coexist with useful lexical or task categories.
- Altman and Royston on dichotomizing continuous variables: hard cutoffs can discard information and reduce statistical power.
- Neurophysiology of the action potential: graded membrane behavior can trigger regenerative all-or-none events.
- EPA/FDA dose-response practice: effects depend on amount, response type, uncertainty and population heterogeneity rather than substance labels alone.
- NIST phase-transition data and the broader thermodynamic literature: continuous control variables can be associated with genuine state changes.
- Blanchard and Summers on hysteresis: current outcomes can depend on history rather than current measured conditions alone.
- Whitehall and related social-gradient research: many health outcomes vary across status hierarchies rather than only between poverty and affluence.
- Wright and Arrow on learning by doing: cumulative experience can change future production costs and capability.
- Chetty, Hendren, Katz and collaborators on childhood exposure to neighborhoods and the geography of opportunity.
- CBO work on effective marginal tax rates: taxes and transfer phaseouts can create steep local incentives even when no single program tells the whole story.
- Granovetter threshold models and Schelling's segregation models: aggregate discontinuity can emerge from heterogeneous local thresholds and interactions.
- Traffic-flow experiments demonstrating spontaneous stop-and-go waves on a circular road without an external bottleneck.
- Regression-discontinuity evidence around low-birth-weight treatment thresholds: an administrative classification line can alter treatment intensity and outcomes for otherwise very similar cases.
- IPCC definitions and assessments of tipping points: “critical threshold” language is useful only when linked to system reorganization and uncertainty.

## Editorial standard

A chapter is not strengthened by adding five frameworks where one mechanism will do. The preferred pattern is:

Observation or scene → mechanism → evidence → counterpressure → consequence.

The prose should avoid converting every finding into a slogan. Where evidence is mixed, the disagreement belongs in the book. Where a metaphor crosses domains, the chapter should name what transfers structurally and what does not.

The aim is not to make *Gradation* sound certain.

It is to make its uncertainty better shaped.
