# Back Matter

## Evidence note

This manuscript treats Project Plowshare's gas-stimulation work as a documented engineering history rather than as evidence for hidden nuclear activity. Gasbuggy, Rulison, and Rio Blanco were publicly acknowledged U.S. nuclear experiments whose technical and stewardship records are available through the Department of Energy and OSTI. Where later summaries compress the original record, the next research pass is explicitly tasked with moving claims upstream into contemporary experiment reports, production tables, environmental statements, correspondence, and economic studies.

Four evidence classes govern the manuscript. **Verified/documented** claims are supported by official records, primary technical reports, treaty texts, or comparably direct evidence. **Credible but incomplete** claims depend on model reconstruction, incomplete archives, uncertain counterfactuals, or later synthesis. **Disputed/not established** claims are kept as allegations or rejected when the public record does not support them. **Speculative synthesis** is Sven Hardy Benson's interpretation and is labeled as such rather than laundered into historical fact.

The book deliberately distinguishes radioactive constituents in experimentally produced gas from claims of regional environmental contamination. DOE's long-term stewardship records report no Gasbuggy-related contamination in monitored surface and groundwater locations; that finding does not make the nuclear event reversible, and the continuing stewardship obligation does not itself prove off-site contamination.

## Source orientation

The primary public research spine is maintained in `research/source-log.md`. Highest-value source families are DOE Office of Legacy Management site records; declassified and open technical reports in OSTI/OpenNet; contemporary AEC, Lawrence Radiation Laboratory, and industry reports; federal energy forecasts; treaty texts and State Department diplomatic records; environmental reviews; and records concerning massive hydraulic fracturing as a contemporary alternative.

## Research boundary

The current manuscript is a coherent complete argument, not a publication-ready edition. Chapter expansion must come from experiment-specific production curves, gas-composition tables, fracture models, economic assumptions, local records, and contemporaneous decision documents rather than from repeating the thesis. The publication gate and explicit defeat conditions are preserved in the research directory.
