# Contradictions and Incentives

## Contradictions to preserve

- Luminous reports recur historically, yet many individual modern flashes have ordinary electrical explanations.
- Laboratory rock electricity is real enough to study, yet scale-up to crust/atmosphere remains unresolved.
- Geological clustering in retrospective cases is suggestive, yet selection and observation biases are difficult to reconstruct.
- A physical precursor can exist without producing useful forecast skill.

## Incentive map

Researchers proposing a novel mechanism benefit from attention to unexplained cases and may underweight mundane confounds. Skeptics benefit from protecting prediction science from low-specificity claims and may underinvestigate rare residual cases. Media outlets reward spectacular footage. Witnesses receive social reinforcement after major events. Utilities may possess decisive timing data but have little research incentive to curate it for geophysics. Emergency managers bear the cost of false alarms, making operational standards properly stricter than existence claims.

No incentive demonstrates that a claim is false or true. Incentives identify where independent records matter most.
