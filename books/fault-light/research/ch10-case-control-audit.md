# Chapter 10 case-control audit

## Primary paper

Robert Thériault, France St-Laurent, Friedemann T. Freund & John S. Derr, `Prevalence of Earthquake Lights Associated with Rift Environments`, *Seismological Research Letters* 85(1), 2014, 149–178.

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1785/0220130059
- NASA publication record: https://airbornescience.nasa.gov/content/Prevalence_of_Earthquake_Lights_Associated_with_Rift_Environments

## Frozen case-series facts

The paper assembled **65 American and European earthquakes associated with reliable earthquake-light reports**.

Key reported pattern:

- ~**85%** of the cases spatially associated with rift environments;
- **63/65 = 97%** occurred at or adjacent to regional subvertical faults, including rifts, grabens, strike-slip or transform faults;
- only two cases were associated with shallow-dipping thrust faults in subduction settings;
- ~**80%** of cataloged EQL earthquakes were larger than magnitude 5.

These are **percentages within selected EQL cases**. They are not estimates of:

- `P(EQL | rift earthquake)`;
- `P(EQL | subvertical fault)`;
- predictive value of a luminous anomaly;
- false-positive rate during ordinary non-earthquake time.

## Missing denominator

A useful control set should be matched on observation opportunity as well as seismology. Candidate matching variables:

- magnitude;
- hypocentral depth;
- region / tectonic province;
- event year / observation era;
- local time;
- population density and distance to settlements;
- electrification / utility-grid density;
- camera / newspaper / scientific-observer availability;
- weather and cloud state where recoverable;
- archival coverage and language availability;
- independent tectonic/fault geometry.

The strongest retrospective design would sample **earthquakes without qualifying EQL reports** from the same source population and classify geology blinded to case/control status.

## Why `all earthquakes globally` is not an adequate control by itself

Historical EQL ascertainment is strongly opportunity-dependent. Oceanic, remote, daytime or poorly documented earthquakes have less chance to enter an eyewitness/photo catalog. Modern electrified urban events have both more observers and more infrastructure-based luminous confounds.

A crude global denominator could therefore create a different bias rather than fixing selection bias.

## Prospective promotion test

The rift/subvertical-fault result should be promoted only if continuous monitoring shows a predeclared optical/electrical anomaly class is enriched around future target earthquakes relative to matched controls and ordinary time.

Recommended observatory bundle:

- calibrated all-sky camera;
- photometer / spectrometer where practical;
- electric-field mill;
- magnetometer;
- air-ion counter;
- weather;
- seismic timing;
- synchronized utility-grid telemetry.

Predeclare:

- target magnitude/depth;
- spatial radius;
- pre-, co- and post-seismic windows;
- optical/electrical event classifier;
- cloud/weather exclusions;
- infrastructure-fault exclusion rules;
- primary score and false-alarm denominator.

## Defeat condition

If a sufficiently powered prospective network finds no excess of the preregistered anomaly class around matched rift/subvertical-fault earthquakes, the geological-enrichment claim should be demoted even if individual historical cases remain unexplained.

If enrichment survives independent sites and event sets, the 2014 catalog becomes substantially stronger as an early pattern-detection study rather than merely an archival curiosity.
