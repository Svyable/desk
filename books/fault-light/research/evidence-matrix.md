# Evidence Matrix

## Class 1 — verified / documented

- USGS recognizes earthquake lights as a reported phenomenon and records scientific disagreement over the evidentiary status of individual cases.
- Some supposed earthquake-light reports have ordinary electrical-infrastructure explanations, including power-line arcing.
- Peer-reviewed literature documents electrical effects in stressed rocks and proposes positive-hole charge carriers as one mechanism.
- Thériault et al. published a retrospective analysis of 65 EQL cases and reported an association with rift environments/subvertical faults.
- Fidani published a post-event catalog of luminous and other reports around the 2009 L'Aquila earthquake.

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete

- Some historical/photo cases may represent genuine geophysical luminous phenomena rather than infrastructure or weather.
- Tectonic setting may modulate the probability of observable crust-atmosphere electrical coupling.

## Class 3 — disputed / not established

- Earthquake lights as a unified phenomenon with one mechanism.
- Reliable short-term earthquake prediction from luminous observations.
- Any specific viral video as geophysical without source reconstruction.
- The positive-hole model as the uniquely correct mechanism.

## Class 4 — speculative synthesis

- EQL may resolve into several classes: infrastructure-induced, atmospheric, rupture-coincident geophysical and possibly pre-rupture electrical phenomena.
- Dense multimodal observatories could settle much of the controversy cheaply relative to earthquake-loss stakes.
- The history of EQL is a case study in the distinction between existence, mechanism and forecast utility.
