# Seismo-Electromagnetics

Earthquake lights sit inside a larger and more contentious field: seismo-electromagnetics. Researchers have investigated ultra-low-frequency magnetic changes, electric potentials, ionospheric disturbances, radio propagation anomalies and other electromagnetic effects associated with earthquakes.

Some mechanisms are mundane and real. Ground motion can disturb conductors. Fluid flow can generate electrokinetic potentials. Cracking can produce electrical effects. The hard part is separating signals tied to stress evolution from environmental and anthropogenic noise.

The field's recurring mistake is category collapse. Evidence that rocks produce charge under stress does not establish an ionospheric precursor. Evidence of an ionospheric anomaly does not establish crustal causation. A correlation around one earthquake does not establish forecast skill.

Fault Light therefore treats each coupling step separately: source, transport, surface expression, atmospheric/ionospheric response, detection and prediction. A claim can be strong at one layer and weak at the next.

That layered discipline is less satisfying than a grand electromagnetic earthquake theory. It is also how one avoids converting interesting physics into an oracle.
