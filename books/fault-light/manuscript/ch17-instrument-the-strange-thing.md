# Instrument the Strange Thing

There is a peculiar institutional gap around anomalous phenomena. The cost of arguing about them can exceed the cost of measuring them.

Earthquake lights are almost designed for this problem. They are rare, brief, embarrassing, heterogeneous and entangled with catastrophic events. Conventional grants prefer phenomena with cleaner prior probability. Enthusiast communities collect observations but often lack calibration and controls.

The escape is not a special institute for mysteries. It is to add modest anomaly instrumentation to serious geophysical observatories: all-sky cameras, calibrated photometry, electric-field sensors and utility-data agreements alongside seismometers and GNSS.

Most nights the system will record aircraft, lightning, grid faults, meteors and sensor errors. Good. That builds the negative library required to recognize anything unusual.

If nothing geologically distinctive appears, the result is valuable. If something does, there is finally a clock attached to it.

Strangeness is not a research method. Instrumentation is.
