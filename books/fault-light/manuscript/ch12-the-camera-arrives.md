# The Camera Arrives

Billions of cameras should have killed weak anomaly claims. Sometimes they do. Sometimes they reveal a new problem: billions of cameras also manufacture billions of ambiguous clips.

Modern earthquakes now arrive with phones, dashcams, CCTV, satellites and utility sensors. Luminous flashes captured during shaking frequently have obvious electrical explanations. Videos can show multiple blue-green flashes synchronized with grid failure. That is useful negative evidence against treating every earthquake-associated flash as geology.

Yet ubiquitous video also creates an opportunity the historical literature never had. Multi-angle reconstruction can locate a source. Frame timing can be compared with seismic-wave arrival. Utility telemetry can confirm faults. Spectral response, rolling shutter and exposure artifacts can be modeled.

The field should stop asking the internet whether a clip “looks like” an earthquake light. It should build event packets: original files, metadata, exact observer position, orientation, seismic trace, weather radar, grid-fault logs and independent cameras.

A camera is not a witness with perfect memory. It is an instrument with failure modes. Treat it like one.
