# Light Without Oracle

The most defensible ending is smaller than the folklore and stranger than dismissal.

There is a historical literature of luminous phenomena associated with earthquakes. The USGS acknowledges disagreement rather than declaring the category imaginary. Peer-reviewed work has proposed physical mechanisms by which stressed rocks could activate charge carriers and, under some conditions, produce surface and atmospheric electrical effects. A 2014 analysis reported a geological association with rift environments and steep faults.

None of that establishes a reliable earthquake-warning method.

The gap matters because science often treats existence and usefulness as one decision. A thing is either bunk or a tool. Earthquake lights may occupy the middle: a real but heterogeneous family of effects, some anthropogenic, some atmospheric, perhaps some geophysical, with weak or nonexistent operational predictive value.

That middle is worth defending. Nature contains mechanisms that do not organize themselves for human convenience.

The right response is neither prophecy nor ridicule. Put clocks on the cameras. Put electric-field sensors beside the seismometers. Keep the false alarms. Log the grid failures. Make the mechanism predict numbers. Then wait long enough for the Earth to be unfair to everybody's favorite theory.
