# Notes on Evidence

This draft uses four evidence classes throughout the research apparatus.

**Class 1 — verified/documented:** official agency statements, peer-reviewed papers, original instrument records and traceable historical documents.

**Class 2 — credible but incomplete:** serious testimony or reporting that is plausible but lacks enough independent documentation to freeze as fact.

**Class 3 — disputed/unconfirmed:** claims that remain contested, lack independent confirmation, or are frequently repeated beyond what the source establishes.

**Class 4 — speculative synthesis:** the author's proposed connections, mechanisms or research designs. These are arguments, not findings.

The central claim is deliberately defeasible. If prospective, well-controlled observation shows that luminous events around earthquakes are fully explained by infrastructure, weather and ordinary atmospheric sources, the geological portion of the thesis should be abandoned. If a geological luminous effect survives but shows no useful prospective relationship to impending earthquakes, the phenomenon may remain scientifically interesting while the prediction claim fails.
