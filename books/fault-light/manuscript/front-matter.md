# Fault Light

*Earthquake Luminosity, Rock Electricity, and the Prediction Trap*

Sven Hardy Benson

There are subjects science rejects because they are false, subjects it neglects because they are inconvenient, and subjects that linger in a more awkward state: reported often enough to resist burial, measured too poorly to settle, explained by mechanisms that are suggestive without being complete.

Earthquake lights belong to the third category. They are a bad subject for anyone who needs an easy verdict. The United States Geological Survey does not say they are imaginary. It says geophysicists disagree about how many reports correspond to a real earthquake-associated luminous phenomenon. That is a much more interesting sentence.

This book does not argue that lights predict earthquakes. It asks what survives after ordinary electrical failures, weather, memory, selection effects and mythology have been given every reasonable chance to win.
