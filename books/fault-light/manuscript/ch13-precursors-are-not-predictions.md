# Precursors Are Not Predictions

Suppose tomorrow we prove that a particular electrical or luminous anomaly occurs more often before some earthquakes. We still have not proved earthquake prediction.

Prediction requires prospective performance. A precursor must occur early enough to matter, often enough before target earthquakes to be sensitive, rarely enough without them to avoid intolerable false alarms, and within a spatial window useful for action. It must beat whatever baseline forecast already exists from seismicity and tectonics.

This distinction is where many extraordinary literatures fail. Researchers report a signal before a known event, then implicitly reverse the arrow. But emergency managers live forward in time. They see signals without knowing which ones will acquire an earthquake afterward.

The denominator is all precursor-like signals, not only the ones retrospectively paired with earthquakes.

Earthquake lights can be physically real and operationally useless. That possibility is not a defeat. It may be the correct scientific result.
