# The False-Alarm Denominator

The most important dataset in earthquake prediction is usually the one nobody photographs: nothing happened.

Imagine a valley camera system records ten unusual glows in a decade. One occurs three hours before a magnitude 6 earthquake. That case will travel. The other nine will not. Unless the nine remain in the denominator, the apparent predictive value becomes fiction.

The same applies to electromagnetic transients, ionospheric anomalies, radon excursions, animal behavior and every other proposed precursor. Post-event selection rewards coincidence.

A credible earthquake-light program therefore needs continuous logging, automated classification and preregistered thresholds established before earthquakes occur. Every alarm-like event must stay in the database whether or not the Earth cooperates.

The odd thing is that the scientific infrastructure for doing this has become cheap. Cameras, electric-field sensors, GNSS timing and local storage cost a fraction of a seismic station from earlier eras. The expensive part is patience: maintaining instruments through years of uneventful nights.

Anomaly science improves when boredom is funded.
