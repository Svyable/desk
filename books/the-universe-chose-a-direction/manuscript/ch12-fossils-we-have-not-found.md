# Fossils We Have Not Found

There is a particular kind of scientific object that exists first as a blank space in a parameter plot.

The axes are labeled. The forbidden region is shaded. The remaining white territory is not evidence that something lives there. It is permission to keep looking.

Frontier cosmology contains many such territories.

Magnetic monopoles. Cosmic strings. Axions. primordial black holes. sterile neutrinos. stochastic gravitational-wave backgrounds from phase transitions. relic particles from hidden sectors. Each has a different theoretical pedigree and evidentiary status. Some are solutions to specific problems. Some arise generically in classes of models. Some are dark-matter candidates. Some are already strongly constrained. None should be spoken of as if it had been found merely because a serious paper gives it a name.

This chapter is about the discipline required to study fossils that may not exist.

The phrase sounds contradictory. Archaeology begins after the artifact. Frontier physics often has to build the metal detector before it knows whether the civilization was there.

Magnetic monopoles are a classic case.

Maxwell’s equations treat electric and magnetic fields with an asymmetry familiar to every student: electric charges exist as isolated sources; no isolated magnetic charge has been observed. Bar magnets have north and south poles, but cut one in half and you get two smaller dipoles, not a free north pole and a free south pole.

Dirac showed in 1931 that the existence of even one magnetic monopole could explain why electric charge is quantized in units. Later grand unified theories produced monopoles naturally as topological defects when a high-energy gauge symmetry breaks to a lower-energy group. In some models, monopole production in the early universe is so efficient that standard cosmology would contain far too many.

This became the monopole problem.

Inflation offers a solution by diluting any preexisting monopoles to negligible density if enough accelerated expansion occurs after they form. The same mechanism that addresses horizon and flatness problems can erase a predicted relic.

No confirmed fundamental magnetic monopole has been detected.

This absence has not made monopoles irrelevant. It has made them a constraint on cosmic history and a target for experiments. Searches use collider detectors, cosmic-ray observatories, ancient materials, superconducting induction devices, and other methods. Each null result closes part of parameter space under stated assumptions.

The fossil remains hypothetical; the search is empirical.

Cosmic strings occupy a related position.

They can arise in symmetry-breaking transitions when the vacuum manifold admits stable line defects. Their gravitational effects could include lensing, bursts, and a stochastic gravitational-wave background produced by an evolving network of loops. Pulsar timing arrays, ground-based interferometers, cosmic microwave background measurements, and other observations constrain string tension and network scenarios.

Interest increased after pulsar timing arrays reported evidence for a common-spectrum low-frequency gravitational-wave background in the 2020s. The leading astrophysical interpretation involves a population of supermassive black-hole binaries. Cosmic strings and other early-universe sources have been studied as possible contributions or alternatives. The data did not become proof of strings because an exotic model could fit part of the spectrum.

A background is not a provenance label.

This sentence is worth writing above every frontier signal.

The universe can generate similar observables through different mechanisms. A stochastic gravitational-wave background is especially vulnerable to this because it is, by definition, an unresolved superposition. Astrophysical binaries, primordial processes, defects, and phase transitions can contribute across overlapping bands.

Detection is therefore only the first symmetry break in the inference. The second is source separation.

Humans have become very good at turning “something is there” into “which history put it there?” through spectral shape, anisotropy, polarization, cross-correlation, population statistics, and multi-band observations.

The same discipline applies to phase-transition backgrounds.

A strong first-order transition can produce a broad stochastic spectrum. The peak frequency observed today depends on the transition temperature, redshift, and characteristic timescale. The amplitude depends on the released vacuum energy, duration, bubble-wall behavior, plasma efficiency, and other model details. Sound waves in the plasma can dominate under many conditions; turbulence contributes; direct bubble-collision terms matter differently depending on the microphysics.

A spectrum can therefore be translated into a transition only through a model.

The model can be wrong.

This is not pessimism. It is what makes multiple observables valuable. Suppose a future space interferometer detects a millihertz stochastic background with a shape consistent with a strong first-order electroweak-scale transition. Collider measurements also reveal deviations in Higgs self-coupling, and a new scalar particle is found. Electric dipole moment experiments constrain CP violation. A model tying these together becomes much more compelling than a fit to one spectrum.

Independent constraints convert a story into a web.

The ideal frontier discovery is overdetermined.

Dark matter has taught physicists this lesson painfully.

The gravitational evidence for nonbaryonic dark matter is overwhelming in the standard cosmological interpretation: galaxy dynamics, clusters, gravitational lensing, the CMB, and large-scale structure all demand more gravitating matter than baryons supply. Yet decades of searches have not identified the particle or field responsible. WIMPs were once the dominant candidate because thermal freeze-out could naturally produce an abundance near what is observed and supersymmetric theories offered candidates. Direct-detection experiments, colliders, and indirect searches have carved away large regions of simple WIMP parameter space without finding an accepted signal.

The phenomenon is established. The relic identity is not.

This distinction should be tattooed on cosmology.

A missing mechanism does not make the observed phenomenon speculative.

A theoretically elegant candidate does not make the candidate observed.

The same holds for baryogenesis. Matter dominance is observed. The generating mechanism is unknown. For inflation, a period of accelerated early expansion is strongly supported as a framework by several successes but the underlying field or mechanism is not identified, and alternative early-universe scenarios remain actively investigated. For dark energy, accelerated expansion is observed; a cosmological constant fits remarkably well, but its microscopic origin is unknown.

The universe contains facts with missing causes.

Frontier physics is the craft of keeping the fact solid while the cause remains fluid.

This is harder than it sounds because language leaks certainty uphill.

A paper says “in this model, a Peccei-Quinn phase transition can produce a LIGO-band gravitational-wave signal.” A popular summary becomes “LIGO could hear the axion.” A social post becomes “gravitational waves may have found axions.” By the end of the chain, conditional theory has become quasi-observation.

The same compression happens inside science if we are not careful. A benchmark scenario becomes “the expected signal.” A prior becomes a fact. A null result is described as “ruling out” a whole class when it only excludes specific assumptions.

The human brain prefers nouns to conditional clauses.

A research trail must keep the clauses.

This is why the source notes for this book distinguish four levels.

Established physical framework: symmetry breaking, Higgs physics, parity violation, baryon asymmetry, laboratory defects.

Active but credible theoretical development: specific cosmological phase-transition models, cosmic-string network predictions, electroweak baryogenesis extensions, time-crystal phase classifications.

Observational constraints without discovery: searches for monopoles, strings, primordial backgrounds, new scalars, electric dipole moments.

Authorial synthesis: the claim that these cases collectively support thinking of the low-energy world as frozen history and of intelligence as a mechanism for recovering alternatives.

Only the last belongs to me.

The others belong to communities of evidence.

This separation is especially important in esoteric writing because obscurity can imitate authority. If the reader has never heard of a sphaleron or a vacuum manifold, the author can make almost anything sound established by putting enough Greek letters nearby. Technical vocabulary should increase accountability, not reduce it.

A useful rule is that the more exotic the object, the more ordinary the sourcing should be.

Link the paper.

State the date.

Say whether the experiment observed the phenomenon or constrained it.

Say what model assumptions connect the data to the claim.

Say what would falsify the interpretation.

This is not stylistic modesty. It is how frontier physics stays connected to physics.

Monopole searches are instructive because they span wildly different environments.

Collider experiments can search for highly ionizing signatures of monopoles produced in high-energy collisions. Cosmic searches look for monopoles arriving from astrophysical or primordial sources. Geological materials can act as long-duration detectors if a monopole would leave a track. Superconducting loops could register persistent currents induced by magnetic charge passage.

Each method has a different exposure and sensitivity.

A null result in one does not mean “no monopoles.” It means no monopoles within the flux, mass, charge, velocity, production, and acceptance regime probed.

This is what honest ignorance looks like when quantified.

The same pattern will define gravitational-wave archaeology. LIGO and Virgo probe tens to thousands of hertz. Pulsar timing arrays probe nanohertz. LISA will probe millihertz. Proposed future detectors target other bands. A primordial background can be loud in one and invisible in another depending on its source scale and spectral shape.

The universe does not owe us a signal in the band we funded first.

This is one reason multi-band gravitational-wave astronomy is so important. A broad spectrum across bands can constrain source classes. Non-detections at one frequency can eliminate models that fit another. An astrophysical binary population has different frequency evolution and anisotropy from many primordial sources.

The absence of a fossil in one layer may tell you where not to dig.

This chapter also needs to acknowledge a more uncomfortable possibility.

Some transitions may leave no recoverable fossil at all.

Thermalization is ruthless. Inflation may erase pre-inflationary relics. Later entropy production can dilute abundances. Defects can annihilate. Fields can relax. Signals can redshift below accessible amplitudes. A high-energy event can matter enormously for the state of the universe and leave no independent observable beyond the phase it established.

History can become underdetermined in principle for practical observers.

This is where cosmology brushes against epistemology. We may be able to construct multiple high-energy theories that produce the same low-energy observables within all accessible precision. If no experiment can distinguish them, physics faces a limit not of imagination but of evidence.

The correct response is not to declare all stories equal.

Theories differ in simplicity, consistency, quantum gravity embedding, naturalness, explanatory scope, and predictions at other scales. But empirical underdetermination can be real over a domain.

The universe may have forgotten more than we can recover.

The previous autonomous book, *The Last Witnesses*, explored a different form of cosmic forgetting: evidence that becomes inaccessible to future observers as accelerated expansion carries galaxies beyond horizons and redshifts cosmological signals. This book is not repeating that thesis. Here the concern is internal to phase history: transitions can erase their own microhistory while preserving the resulting state.

The distinction matters.

One is loss of access over cosmic future time.

The other is loss of path information during equilibration and phase formation.

Both remind us that truth and retrievability are separate.

A transition can have happened in one specific way even if no later observer can determine which.

Science does not guarantee omniscience.

That limit should make us more careful with the fossils we do have.

A confirmed cosmic string would be priceless not because strings are exotic, but because it would expose symmetry-breaking topology at an energy scale likely inaccessible to direct experiments. A primordial gravitational-wave background from a phase transition could reveal the dynamics of a vacuum change. A fundamental monopole would transform gauge theory and cosmology. An axion detection could solve the strong CP problem and perhaps identify dark matter depending on the axion population.

The highest-value relics are often valuable because they open otherwise sealed epochs.

This is why scientists tolerate long searches with null results.

The detector is a question kept alive in hardware.

A detector can outlast a fashion in theory. It can discover the thing it was not built to find. It can turn a hypothetical fossil into an accidental one.

The history of astronomy is full of this. Pulsars were not the expected output of radio surveys. The cosmic microwave background was detected accidentally by Penzias and Wilson while they were dealing with excess antenna noise, though theoretical work had predicted relic radiation earlier. Fast radio bursts appeared in archival pulsar-survey data. Gravitational waves opened a new sky and immediately delivered black-hole populations that changed astrophysical expectations.

A broad instrument gives reality room to improvise.

This is particularly important for an autonomous author tempted by a fresh thesis.

Novelty is not evidence.

An underwritten idea should be underwritten because the synthesis has been neglected, not because facts have to be invented to support it.

The thesis of this book does not require a cosmic string detection.

It is stronger if it survives without one.

Broken symmetry is established in laboratory and particle physics. The electroweak broken phase is established. Defect formation in condensed matter is established. Parity violation is established. Matter-antimatter asymmetry is established. Time-crystalline phases are experimentally realized. These are enough to show that low-energy reality can contain durable asymmetries and phase-dependent structure not obvious from the most symmetric statement of the laws.

The hypothetical cosmic fossils add a frontier question: how much of early phase history remains observable?

If none are found, the philosophical thesis narrows but survives.

If some are found, the universe becomes more historically legible.

That is a falsifiable hierarchy, not a pile of marvels.

Humans have a tendency to fill missing fossils with myth. We always have. A strange bone becomes a dragon. A gap in a genealogy becomes an ancestor king. A light in the sky becomes an omen.

Modern science has not removed the tendency. It has built procedures around it.

Blind analyses. control regions. preregistration in some fields. independent collaborations. significance thresholds. trial-factor corrections. replication. open data. instrument calibration. source catalogs. Bayesian model comparison. simulation campaigns.

None are perfect. Together they make imagination pay rent.

A frontier detector is a machine for charging the rent.

Most theories cannot afford it.

That is good.

The fossil we have not found is allowed to be beautiful.

It is not allowed to be present tense.
