## Why it is distinct from other secrecy/intelligence books

The manuscript is not primarily about whether secrecy can exist, intelligence services can deceive, or UAP observations are physically anomalous. Its novel burden is comparative and material: **what institutional residue do real secret hardware-exploitation programs leave, and can that residue be used as a falsifiable baseline for evaluating alleged non-human exploitation?**

The central collision is between documented Cold War foreign-technology exploitation and modern extraordinary retrieval testimony. The book allows each evidence stream to discipline the other: documented secrecy defeats naive impossibility arguments; documented program residue defeats naive claims that secrecy explains every missing trace.
