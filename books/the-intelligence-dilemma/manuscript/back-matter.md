# Back Matter

This book makes a claim about learning, not about the moral status of a tool.

The claim is that generative AI can increase a student's visible performance without increasing the underlying capability in the same proportion. It can also improve learning. The difference depends on what the student is trying to learn, what work the system carries, what knowledge the student already has, how assistance is structured, and what evidence the school uses to judge mastery.

That is why the research trail does not reduce the subject to a single effect size.

The strongest positive evidence matters. Structured AI tutoring can improve learning in particular settings. AI can help live tutors give stronger support. Students can receive explanations, translation, rehearsal, practice, accessibility support, and feedback that would otherwise be scarce or expensive.

The strongest cautionary evidence matters too. Immediate task performance is not the same thing as durable learning. Assistance can conceal gaps that reappear when support is removed. Fluent output can be mistaken for understanding. Students with stronger prior knowledge may be better positioned to detect errors and use the same tool productively. Assessment artifacts become weaker evidence when the process that produced them is opaque.

The manuscript therefore keeps several distinctions separate.

Performance is not identical to learning.

Learning is not identical to certification.

Assistance is not automatically substitution.

Independent work is not automatically more authentic than tool-assisted work.

Human teaching is not automatically better than machine assistance.

And the existence of a capable tool does not establish that the capability it can perform is no longer worth learning.

The evidence base is still young in places. Model capabilities change quickly. Studies are often specific to one age group, subject, interface, prompt structure, instructor setting, or version of a system. Survey responses about usefulness are not learning outcomes. Short-term gains are not automatically durable transfer. A classroom policy that works in one institution may fail elsewhere because the incentives, support, curriculum, or student population differ.

For that reason, current claims are recorded with their conditions wherever practical. The research notes distinguish experiments from observational work, guidance from measured outcomes, and institutional examples from general evidence. They also record counterevidence and circumstances that should weaken the book's argument.

The book would need substantial revision if abundant AI assistance consistently produced durable, transferable capability in the same proportion that it improved visible output, while leaving assessment validity, independent judgment, source evaluation, and educational relationships largely unchanged. It would also need revision if the distinction between assisted and independent capability proved irrelevant to the professions and civic settings schools prepare students to enter.

The opposite extreme would require revision too. If schools treated unaided performance as the only legitimate form of competence, they would misrepresent modern work and discard real educational gains. Professionals use tools. Students should learn to use them well. The practical question is not whether intelligence came only from inside one skull. It is whether the learner remains capable of understanding, judging, directing, and taking responsibility for the work.

That is also the boundary on the book's constructive recommendations.

More oral assessment is useful only where live reasoning is relevant evidence.

More process visibility is useful only where the process reveals something the final artifact cannot.

Independent checkpoints are useful only where independent capability matters.

AI disclosure is useful only when it clarifies intellectual responsibility rather than creating ritual paperwork.

Human contact is worth protecting not because humans are always more accurate or kind, but because reciprocal obligation, belonging, conflict, accountability, and shared consequences are part of education itself.

The research files accompanying the manuscript are an audit trail for the concrete claims used in the argument, not an exhaustive bibliography of education, learning science, assessment, or artificial intelligence. Where newer evidence changes the balance, the manuscript should change with it.

The durable test is simple enough to survive changes in model names and interfaces.

Did the student become more capable?

Capable with tools when tools are appropriate.

Capable without them where independent judgment matters.

Capable of knowing the difference.

That is the standard the book asks schools to keep.
