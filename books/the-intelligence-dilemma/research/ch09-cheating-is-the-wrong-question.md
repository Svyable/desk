# Research Trail — Chapter 9: Cheating Is the Wrong Question

## Purpose

Reframe academic integrity around assignment purpose, permitted assistance, disclosure, and demonstrated capability rather than treating every use of generative AI as the same act.

## Sources read

### Harvard Graduate School of Education AI Policy

- URL: https://registrar.gse.harvard.edu/learning/policies-forms/ai-policy
- What it supports: HGSE encourages responsible experimentation but distinguishes permissible learning-oriented uses such as clarification, brainstorming, and contextual examples from using generative AI to create all or part of an assignment and submit it as one’s own unless the instructor specifies otherwise.
- Use in chapter: concrete example of a policy that distinguishes forms of assistance rather than treating all AI interaction identically.
- Limitation: one school’s policy; not evidence that this is the dominant institutional approach.

### Harvard Faculty of Arts and Sciences, Generative AI Guidance

- URL: https://oue.fas.harvard.edu/faculty-resources/generative-ai-guidance/
- What it supports: Harvard provides sample maximally restrictive, fully encouraging, and mixed course policies, and requires faculty to communicate course-specific expectations. The encouraging and mixed examples place responsibility on students to evaluate AI output and acknowledge use.
- Use in chapter: supports the claim that rational AI policies can vary by course and assignment purpose.
- Limitation: sample policies are guidance, not proof of classroom effectiveness.

### Harvard Extension School, Academic Integrity / Student Responsibilities

- URLs: https://extension.harvard.edu/enrolled-students/academic-integrity/ and https://summer.harvard.edu/academic-opportunities-support/policies-and-regulations/student-responsibilities/
- What it supports: submission of computer-generated work without attribution is prohibited under the school’s academic-integrity rules, while instructors may set assignment-specific policies; permitted outside assistance is expected to be acknowledged.
- Use in chapter: example of disclosure and instructor-specific permission operating together.

### University of Cambridge SHSS, “Generative artificial intelligence (AI) and scholarship”

- URL: https://www.cshss.cam.ac.uk/education/generative-artificial-intelligence-ai-and-scholarship
- What it supports: Cambridge resources include principles, student guidance, a sample student declaration for permitted GenAI use, and guidance for investigating suspected misconduct.
- Use in chapter: supports the idea that institutions are developing disclosure and local-policy mechanisms rather than relying solely on categorical bans.
- Limitation: resource framework, not a measured outcome study.

### HEPI Student Generative AI Survey 2026

- URL: https://www.hepi.ac.uk/reports/student-generative-ai-survey-2026/
- What it supports: widespread use in assessed work, substantial reported changes to assessment, and student anxiety about false accusations of misconduct.
- Use in chapter: context for why integrity systems based only on suspicion and detection can create trust problems.
- Limitation: UK undergraduate self-report; does not measure detector accuracy.

## Claims intentionally bounded

- The chapter does not argue that AI use cannot constitute cheating; clear violations of clear rules remain academic misconduct.
- It does not claim that authorship has no meaning. It argues that authorship and responsibility need more explicit definitions when assistance becomes general-purpose.
- It does not make numerical claims about AI-text detector false-positive rates; that evidence belongs primarily in Chapter 13, “The Assessment Arms Race.”
- It treats oral defense, supervised work, and process evidence as ways to establish capability, not as universal replacements for take-home work.

## Follow-up reporting

- Collect AI-use declaration templates from several universities and examination bodies.
- Interview academic-integrity officers about how cases are adjudicated when AI use is partly permitted.
- Research disability and language-access cases where editing or translation assistance intersects with integrity rules.
- Study whether assignment-level AI policies reduce disputes or merely shift them to boundary cases.
