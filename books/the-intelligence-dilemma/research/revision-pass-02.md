# Revision pass 02 — Part II: The Student Changes

## Purpose

Revise Chapters 6–10 so Part II moves through five different consequences for the learner rather than repeating the opening book thesis under new labels.

## Part II movement

- **Ch 6 — Productive Struggle:** Which effort is educational, and how should help be timed?
- **Ch 7 — The Prompted Mind:** What happens when novices can delegate high-level cognition before apprenticeship is complete?
- **Ch 8 — The Invisible Curriculum:** What norms do students learn from peers, teachers, households, and product interfaces before formal policy catches up?
- **Ch 9 — Cheating Is the Wrong Question:** What is the educational contract, what assistance does it permit, and what remains the student’s responsibility?
- **Ch 10 — The New Achievement Gap:** When access becomes cheap, where does advantage migrate?

## Revision priorities

### Chapter 6

Protect the distinction between useful cognitive effort and accidental friction. Productive failure, retrieval practice, expertise reversal, and guarded AI tutoring are evidence for timing and scaffolding—not a moral defense of hardship.

Reduce overlap with Chapters 1–5 about generic practice-versus-proof distinctions. Keep the focus on **when to intervene**.

### Chapter 7

Make cognitive delegation the center. Prompt wording matters, but the deeper skill is problem framing, evaluation, and knowing what to keep internal.

Protect the novice-before-manager paradox: AI gives beginners something like a cognitive subordinate before they have completed the apprenticeship that normally makes supervision meaningful.

### Chapter 8

Focus on social transmission and product defaults. The invisible curriculum is not “students use AI a lot”; it is **students learn what AI is for from local norms and interfaces even when the institution has not taught them deliberately**.

Keep survey claims bounded to their populations and use them as evidence of adoption/guidance gaps, not causal evidence about learning.

### Chapter 9

Make the chapter about the contract rather than detection. Clear violations of clear rules remain cheating. The prior problem is specifying which kinds of assistance are compatible with the assignment’s purpose.

Keep detector mechanics largely out of this chapter; Chapter 13 owns the assessment arms race.

### Chapter 10

Preserve both sides of the equity case. AI can expand access to tutoring-like help, translation, practice, and explanation. It can also shift advantage toward prior knowledge, implementation quality, AI proficiency, household support, and paid capability.

The central claim is not that AI widens inequality. It is that **equal access is not equal capacity to benefit**.

## Voice risks

- repeating “the same tool, different role” after Part I has already established it;
- long rhetorical inventories where a smaller number of concrete contrasts would carry more weight;
- treating every chapter ending as a slogan;
- repeating the pilot / musician / programmer analogies;
- using “not X, but Y” as the default pivot.

## Evidence boundaries

- Kapur and retrieval-practice research support specific sequencing mechanisms, not “struggle is good.”
- expertise-reversal evidence is broader instructional-design literature, not direct chatbot evidence.
- student-use surveys in Chapter 8 are largely higher-education and self-report; do not generalize exact prevalence to K–12.
- university AI policies in Chapter 9 are examples of policy design, not proof that those policies improve outcomes.
- equity evidence in Chapter 10 is heterogeneous; preserve the possibility that well-designed assistance disproportionately benefits less-prepared learners.

## Success test

At the end of Part II, the reader should have moved from the mechanics of learning to the formation of habits and unequal advantage:

1. help changes what effort remains;
2. delegation changes what thinking the student owns;
3. repeated use becomes a social curriculum;
4. rules need an explicit contract of responsibility;
5. the ability to convert abundant intelligence into durable learning is itself unevenly distributed.
