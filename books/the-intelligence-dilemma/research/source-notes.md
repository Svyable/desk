# Source Notes — The Intelligence Dilemma

Working research queue. Do not promote an item into manuscript fact until the original source has been read and the claim has been recorded accurately.

## Learning science

Research questions:

- What does the evidence say about retrieval practice, generation, worked examples, feedback timing, desirable difficulty, and durable retention?
- Which findings generalize across age groups and subjects, and which do not?
- When does immediate help improve learning, and when can it create an illusion of competence?
- What evidence exists on novice versus expert use of external aids?

Priority source families:

- peer-reviewed cognitive-science and educational-psychology literature
- major syntheses or meta-analyses on retrieval practice, feedback, worked examples, and cognitive load
- original studies rather than popular summaries when a chapter turns on a specific effect

## AI tutoring and learning outcomes

Research questions:

- Under what conditions do AI tutors improve measured learning rather than satisfaction or task completion?
- How do outcomes compare with human tutoring, conventional digital tutoring, and no tutoring?
- What role do hints, Socratic prompting, delayed answers, mastery checks, and teacher supervision play?
- How sensitive are findings to model version and subject matter?

Priority source families:

- randomized or controlled evaluations of AI-supported tutoring
- university and school pilots with published methodology
- independent replications where available
- vendor studies only when methods and conflicts are clear

## Student use

Research questions:

- How are students actually using generative AI for homework, writing, coding, studying, language learning, and test preparation?
- How much use is disclosed to teachers?
- Do students distinguish between brainstorming, editing, tutoring, and generation?
- What do students report about dependence, confidence, time savings, and learning?

Priority source families:

- representative student surveys where possible
- longitudinal surveys that show change over time
- qualitative interviews used as texture rather than prevalence estimates

## Teacher use

Research questions:

- Where does AI reduce teacher workload without degrading instructional quality?
- How are teachers using AI for differentiation, feedback, lesson planning, communication, and administrative work?
- What kinds of errors create the greatest burden when teachers must verify machine-produced material?
- Does AI create more time for student interaction or simply increase expected output?

Priority source families:

- teacher surveys with transparent sampling
- district pilots
- teacher-union and professional-association reports
- time-use studies where available

## Assessment and academic integrity

Research questions:

- Which assessment formats remain useful evidence of independent capability?
- What are the documented error rates and limitations of AI-text detection?
- How are institutions changing policies around disclosure, permitted assistance, oral defense, in-class work, and process evidence?
- What harms arise from false accusations or overreliance on detectors?

Priority source families:

- university academic-integrity policies and revisions
- testing organizations
- peer-reviewed detector evaluations
- original documentation from vendors when describing product claims, clearly labeled as vendor claims

## Equity and access

Research questions:

- Does low-cost AI tutoring narrow gaps in access to academic support?
- Which students have the strongest devices, subscriptions, prompting knowledge, adult guidance, and institutional permission?
- Does prior knowledge create compounding returns to AI use?
- How do language, disability, broadband, and school resources alter outcomes?

Priority source families:

- national and regional access data
- school-system studies
- disability and accessibility research
- studies that report subgroup outcomes rather than only averages

## Privacy, safety, and child data

Research questions:

- What student data is collected by AI education products?
- What rules govern minors, school records, consent, retention, model training, and third-party sharing in major jurisdictions?
- What risks are created by storing a learner's mistakes, personal disclosures, learning disabilities, behavioral signals, or inferred traits?
- How do schools evaluate vendors and contracts?

Priority source families:

- official education-department guidance
- privacy regulators
- statutory and regulatory text
- district procurement documents and data-processing agreements
- vendor privacy policies used only to establish what vendors state publicly

## Policy and institutional response

Research questions:

- Which early bans were later revised, and why?
- Which school systems moved toward guided use instead of prohibition?
- What distinctions are emerging between classroom practice, homework, and high-stakes assessment?
- How do policies differ by student age?

Priority source families:

- school district and university policy pages
- education ministries and departments
- examination boards
- teacher and administrator interviews about implementation, not just written policy

## Credentials and the meaning of grades

Research questions:

- How much do employers and universities rely on grades, degrees, portfolios, standardized tests, interviews, or demonstrated work?
- Which signals become less trustworthy when take-home production can be automated?
- Are institutions shifting toward live assessment, oral defense, practical demonstrations, or verified process?

Priority source families:

- admissions and hiring research
- professional licensure bodies
- testing organizations
- employer surveys with careful attention to sampling and incentives

## Historical parallels

Potential comparisons to research carefully:

- calculators in mathematics education
- spell-check and grammar tools in writing
- search engines and open-web research
- Wikipedia and encyclopedic authority
- laptops and one-to-one device programs
- graphing calculators and computer algebra systems
- coding autocomplete and integrated development environments

The historical chapter material should not use "people panicked before and everything was fine" as a shortcut. Each technology altered different capabilities, and generative AI participates in a wider range of cognitive tasks than most earlier classroom tools.

## Interviews to pursue

Build a reporting mix rather than a parade of famous names:

- a primary-school teacher
- a high-school English teacher
- a high-school mathematics or science teacher
- a community-college instructor
- a university writing instructor
- a computer-science instructor
- a school principal or district technology leader
- a student who uses AI heavily and believes it improves learning
- a student who deliberately limits AI use
- a parent with concerns about dependence
- a learning scientist
- an AI-tutoring researcher
- a privacy expert focused on minors
- an employer or professional educator responsible for assessing competence

## Source ledger convention

For each source that enters the manuscript, record:

- source title
- author or institution
- publication date
- URL or DOI
- source type
- chapter(s) supported
- exact claim supported
- relevant quotation or data location
- limitations / competing interpretation
- date accessed

No quotation should enter the manuscript without a traceable original source.
