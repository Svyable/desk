# Revision pass 04 — Part IV, conclusion, and back matter

## Purpose

Revise the book’s constructive final movement so it does not read as five increasingly broad summaries of the same thesis. Each chapter should answer a different question, and Chapter 20 should synthesize rather than rehearse the entire manuscript.

## Part IV movement

- **Ch 16 — Curriculum After Answers:** What should students know and practice when retrieval and generation are cheap?
- **Ch 17 — The Apprenticeship Classroom:** How should school make expert practice, consequential decisions, and gradual independence visible?
- **Ch 18 — What Must Stay Human:** What educational goods depend on reciprocal human community and accountable relationships rather than information delivery?
- **Ch 19 — The School That Knows Why:** What institutional discipline lets different AI rules remain coherent instead of arbitrary?
- **Ch 20 — The Intelligence Dilemma:** What settlement follows from the whole book, and what single standard should survive changing tools?
- **Back matter:** What are the research boundaries and falsification conditions, without restating the conclusion?

## Revision priorities

### Chapter 16

Protect the case for domain knowledge. Cheap retrieval does not imply that internal knowledge is worthless because judgment needs structure. Make verification disciplinary rather than generic “critical thinking.”

Reduce repeated material about practice/certification already handled in Parts I and III. The chapter should own **curricular content and intellectual habits**, not assessment architecture.

### Chapter 17

Keep cognitive apprenticeship as the organizing framework. AI can cheaply produce endpoints; apprenticeship makes the path and expert judgment visible. Preserve explicit instruction and worked examples as counterweights to romantic project-based learning.

Avoid repeating Chapter 11’s teacher-attention argument or Chapter 9’s integrity contract except where needed to explain visible practice.

### Chapter 18

Do not build a list of tasks machines can never do. The durable case is reciprocal obligation: students need experience with adults and peers who have independent needs, can withhold consent, remain present tomorrow, and share consequences.

Keep the countercase strong: human environments can be biased, unsafe, humiliating, or inaccessible; AI can sometimes provide better support.

### Chapter 19

Make “why is this here?” the institutional discipline. Constructive alignment, validity, and TEQSA support the narrower claim that learning goals, allowed assistance, and evidence should align.

Guard against compliance theater. The chapter should recommend less documentation when documentation has no educational purpose.

### Chapter 20

Do not re-summarize each previous chapter. The conclusion should resolve the book around one standard: **did the student become more capable?**

Preserve the final line/idea: the graduate should be able to use intelligence without disappearing inside it.

The conclusion should hold both truths at once—AI can genuinely increase learning and genuinely preserve the appearance of learning without enough underlying change—then offer a concise institutional settlement.

### Back matter

Convert from a second conclusion into an evidence note:
- distinguish measured outcomes from guidance and analogy;
- state where evidence is young or context-specific;
- name findings that would materially weaken the book;
- point readers to chapter research trails;
- avoid rhetorical restatement of the manuscript.

## Voice risks

- repeated lists of “what schools should do” across Chapters 16, 19, and 20;
- repeated “tools change but principles endure” formulations;
- too many professional analogies near the ending;
- a conclusion that feels like a table of contents in prose;
- a back matter section that competes emotionally with Chapter 20.

## Evidence boundaries

- *How People Learn II* and cognitive-apprenticeship literature predate widespread generative AI and support mechanisms, not AI-specific effect sizes.
- lateral-reading and civic-online-reasoning evidence supports teachable verification practices in online information evaluation, not automatic transfer to every discipline or AI interaction.
- project-based-learning evidence is heterogeneous; apprenticeship is not a defense of minimal guidance.
- relationship/belonging literature contains substantial correlational evidence.
- constructive alignment and professional testing standards are design/validity frameworks, not experiments proving a specific AI policy.
- TEQSA is Australian higher-education guidance and should remain an example, not universal prescription.

## Success test

The ending should feel like forward motion:

1. define what knowledge and verification still belong in the curriculum;
2. make consequential practice visible while tools are present;
3. preserve reciprocal human community where it is itself educational;
4. align assignments, assistance, and evidence with explicit purpose;
5. judge the system by whether learners become more capable, with and without assistance as each context requires.
