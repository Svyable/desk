# Revision pass 03 — Part III: The Institution Reacts

## Purpose

Revise Chapters 11–15 so the institutional section separates five questions that the complete draft sometimes allowed to overlap: scarce attention, human accountability, assessment security, oral evidence, and data governance.

## Part III movement

- **Ch 11 — Thirty Teachers for Thirty Students:** AI can expand responsive instructional capacity; the institutional question is where the saved attention goes.
- **Ch 12 — The Teacher Is Not an Answer Key:** explanation may become abundant while accountable judgment, norm-setting, and responsibility remain distinct institutional functions.
- **Ch 13 — The Assessment Arms Race:** detector-led policing is a weak security architecture; credible assessment needs layered evidence tied to the capability claim.
- **Ch 14 — The Oral Return:** conversation is valuable because it is responsive evidence, not because speaking is inherently more authentic than writing.
- **Ch 15 — The Data Shadow:** personalization creates pressure to retain developmental traces; educational governance should protect purpose limitation, selective forgetting, and the student’s right to remain unfinished.

## Revision priorities

### Chapter 11

Avoid re-arguing Chapter 5’s student-facing tutoring case. Focus on the **institutional allocation of scarce adult attention** and the difference between productivity gains that increase human contact versus productivity gains consumed by more output or larger ratios.

Preserve Gallup/Walton as self-reported teacher-use/time-savings evidence and Tutor CoPilot as bounded evidence for spreading pedagogical expertise through human tutors.

### Chapter 12

Do not claim permanent human uniqueness. The chapter’s strongest argument is accountability: schools need identifiable people who can interpret context, set norms, make consequential judgments, and answer for them.

Keep relationship evidence bounded; association is not a universal causal treatment.

### Chapter 13

Keep the OpenAI 2023 classifier as a historical warning, not proof that all future detection must fail. Center layered assurance rather than a catalog of evasion tricks.

Chapter 9 owns the moral/contract question; Chapter 13 owns institutional evidence architecture and proportional enforcement.

### Chapter 14

Protect the strongest distinction: oral evidence is useful because an examiner can adapt the next question to the previous answer. Avoid selling the viva as AI-proof or universally fair.

Keep accessibility, anxiety, language, examiner consistency, staff time, and scale visible. Hybrid evidence is the preferred settlement.

### Chapter 15

Keep legal claims narrow: FERPA and COPPA are partial U.S. legal baselines, not universal prohibitions on educational AI. The deeper argument is normative: schools collect information about people while they are deliberately incomplete.

Protect “memory must earn its place” and distinguish assistance from profiling. Avoid speculative claims that every system infers emotion, risk, or personality accurately.

## Voice risks

- two consecutive chapters saying “teachers are still important” without different mechanisms;
- repeating the crisis-of-inference thesis instead of applying it to assessment architecture;
- detector/evasion inventories that date quickly;
- romanticizing oral exams;
- privacy language that becomes generic technology-policy prose rather than staying rooted in the learner’s developmental condition.

## Evidence boundaries

- Gallup/Walton teacher time savings are self-reported estimates.
- Tutor CoPilot measures near-term lesson mastery in a specific tutoring program.
- teacher–student relationship evidence includes substantial correlational literature.
- OpenAI’s retired classifier is one historical system, not a theorem about detection.
- oral-assessment implementations are promising but context-specific and resource-sensitive.
- FERPA/COPPA claims should remain tied to their actual scope; selective amnesia is a proposed design principle, not a statutory mandate.

## Success test

At the end of Part III, the reader should see a coherent institutional response:

1. use AI to increase responsive capacity;
2. preserve accountable human judgment on consequential decisions;
3. build layered evidence rather than a detector arms race;
4. restore adaptive forms of verification where they improve inference;
5. collect and retain only the student data that can justify its educational purpose.
