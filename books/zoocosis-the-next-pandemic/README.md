# Zoocosis: The Next Pandemic

**Sven Hardy Benson**

## Working thesis

The next pandemic is unlikely to be best understood as a bolt from nature. Pandemic risk is increasingly manufactured at the interfaces humans build among wildlife, livestock, ecosystems, trade, cities, industrial food systems, and global mobility. **Zoocosis** is the condition in which civilization repeatedly creates the ecological and economic circumstances for animal pathogens to encounter, adapt to, amplify within, and sometimes spread efficiently among humans.

This is not a claim that any particular future pathogen can be predicted, nor that wildlife itself is the enemy. The book follows the opposite argument: spillover is probabilistic, many animal-to-human infections terminate immediately, and the dangerous variable is often the architecture of contact and amplification that humans construct.

WHO's One Health framing reports that more than 60% of reported emerging infectious diseases globally originate in animals, wild or domestic, and identifies animal trade, agriculture, livestock farming, urbanization, extractive industries, climate change, habitat fragmentation, and encroachment as relevant stressors. The One Health High-Level Expert Panel argues that preventing spillover at source deserves a place alongside preparedness and response rather than assuming detection after spillover will always be fast enough.

## Reader promise

A pandemic book that begins before Patient Zero.

Rather than another chronology of lockdowns, vaccines, laboratories, or emergency rooms, *Zoocosis* follows the pathogen's opportunity structure: forest edge, farm, cage, barn, slaughter line, market, transport network, wastewater stream, respiratory tract, hospital, aircraft cabin. Each step asks what must happen for an animal pathogen to become a human outbreak and which parts of that chain are actually governable.

The commercial hook is deliberately uncomfortable: we spend enormous sums preparing to fight pandemics after they become visible while continuously rebuilding the interfaces that make spillover possible.

## Evidence discipline

1. **Established/documented:** zoonotic origins of many emerging infectious diseases; documented spillover pathways; known reservoir and intermediate-host systems; land-use, husbandry, wildlife-contact, trade, and mobility mechanisms where supported by evidence; One Health surveillance and prevention programs.
2. **Credible but incomplete:** risk attribution for particular interfaces or ecological changes where causal pathways are plausible but effect sizes remain uncertain; estimates of undiscovered zoonotic viral diversity; incomplete outbreak-origin reconstructions.
3. **Disputed/not established:** definitive origin claims where evidence remains contested; claims that a particular industry, species, country, market, laboratory, or land-use event will cause "the next pandemic"; precise dates or identities of future pandemic pathogens.
4. **Speculative synthesis:** Zoocosis as a systems-level description of civilization's repeated production of spillover opportunity; proposals for measuring a society's pandemic-generating interface burden before outbreaks occur.

## Proposed 20-chapter architecture

1. **Before Patient Zero** — why outbreak narratives begin too late.
2. **A Door Between Species** — what spillover actually requires.
3. **Reservoir Is Not Villain** — bats, rodents, birds, primates, and ecological function.
4. **Forest Edge** — fragmentation, land conversion, extraction, and new contact surfaces.
5. **Bridge Animal** — livestock and other intermediate hosts as biological amplifiers.
6. **Barn Air** — density, respiratory transmission, husbandry, and industrial scale.
7. **Market Geometry** — mixing species, people, surfaces, transport, and time.
8. **Blood, Saliva, Breath** — routes of exposure and why most encounters go nowhere.
9. **Adaptation Has No Plan** — mutation, selection, recombination, reassortment, and the danger of teleology.
10. **Amplifier** — when an infection gets enough replication opportunities to matter.
11. **One Flight Away** — mobility converts local biology into global timing.
12. **Cities Without Species Boundaries** — peri-urban wildlife, livestock, pets, vectors, waste, and water.
13. **Hotter Maps** — climate-driven range shifts without reducing every disease trend to climate change.
14. **Medicine for Animals** — antimicrobial use, resistance, and a second One Health pandemic problem.
15. **Watching Everything** — pathogen discovery, genomic surveillance, wastewater, sentinel animals, and false security.
16. **Prediction Problem** — why cataloguing viruses is not the same as forecasting pandemics.
17. **Prevention Before Preparedness** — source reduction versus stockpiles, vaccines, and emergency response.
18. **Price of the Interface** — economics: who profits from risk and who pays for prevention.
19. **Zoocosis Index** — a proposed measurable framework for contact, amplification, adaptation opportunity, and mobility.
20. **Next Pandemic** — not prophecy: a tour of the intervention points available before the first unexplained cluster.

## Case spine to develop

The manuscript should reconstruct several pathogen/interface systems rather than treating zoonosis as one generic phenomenon: Nipah and livestock/land-use interfaces; influenza and avian/swine mixing; MERS and camel-human transmission; Ebola ecology and outbreak uncertainty; HIV's historical cross-species emergence; mpox and changing human transmission networks; henipaviruses; coronaviruses with careful origin-language discipline; mosquito/tick systems where climate and land use alter vector contact; and antimicrobial resistance at the animal-human-environment boundary.

No case should be forced into the thesis. Counterexamples matter: interfaces that look dangerous but do not generate sustained transmission, successful biosecurity interventions, and diseases whose emergence is poorly explained by the favored drivers.

## Strongest competing explanation

Pandemics may be so dominated by stochastic pathogen properties, rare evolutionary events, human susceptibility, and post-spillover transmission dynamics that broad ecological-interface interventions have limited predictive value. If this is true, preparedness, rapid detection, vaccines, therapeutics, ventilation, and health-system resilience may dominate source prevention.

The book must take this seriously rather than making every forest clearing or poultry barn a pandemic countdown clock.

## Defeat conditions

The central Zoocosis framing weakens if comparative evidence shows that the proposed human-created interface variables fail to predict or explain spillover opportunity better than simpler baselines; if source-prevention interventions consistently prove infeasible or ineffective relative to downstream controls; or if major modern zoonotic emergence is better explained by mechanisms substantially independent of the ecological/economic interfaces emphasized here.
