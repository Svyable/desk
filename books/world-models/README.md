# World Models

*The Race for 4D Supremacy*

Language models learned to predict the next token. The next race is over systems that try to predict what happens next in a world.

*World Models* follows the attempt to give machines something closer to rehearsal: persistent space, physical consequence, time, action, and counterfactual futures. The commercial version is already visible in spatial intelligence, robotics, autonomous vehicles, interactive generated environments, and world foundation models. The strategic version appears under a wider set of names—synthetic training environments, digital twins, autonomous mission models, AI-native simulation, wargaming, and decision-support systems.

The book’s working question is narrower than “who will win AI?” It asks who can build the most useful models of reality, update them fastest from observation, and close the loop from simulation to action. Silicon Valley has frontier models, compute, venture capital, maps, video, robotics startups, and hyperscale infrastructure. China has manufacturing density, robot deployment, industrial policy, enormous physical-world data opportunities, and a fast-growing embodied-intelligence ecosystem. Militaries worldwide have spent decades building simulators, instrumented ranges, digital representations of weapons and terrain, and institutions whose job is to rehearse futures before committing lives and hardware.

“4D” is used here deliberately but not mystically: three-dimensional space plus change through time, especially change caused by an agent’s actions. “Supremacy” is a hypothesis to test, not a conclusion to smuggle in. A photorealistic video generator is not automatically a reliable simulator. A military digital twin is not automatically a learned world model. A benchmark win is not battlefield advantage. The book will keep those distinctions alive.

The central wager is that the next strategic layer of AI may be less about answering questions than cheaply generating experience: millions of plausible futures in which a robot, vehicle, company, city, or military can learn before reality charges for the mistake. The strongest competing explanation is that “world model” becomes another elastic AI label while the decisive advantages remain elsewhere—in sensors, proprietary data, chips, actuators, manufacturing, maps, logistics, software integration, and institutions capable of turning models into decisions.
