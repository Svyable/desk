## A note on claims that move faster than books

World models are developing quickly enough that model names, benchmark positions, access policies, deployment claims, and even the meaning of the term can change between research and publication. The manuscript will therefore prefer dated primary sources, technical reports, program documents, and reproducible evaluations over undated summaries. Where a company, government, or military organization is the source of a capability claim, the text should say so.

The research trail for this working edition lives in `../research/`. It is intended to preserve the evidence behind consequential claims, including the source date, what the source actually establishes, and what remains inference or dispute.

## Working definitions

**World model.** A model that represents enough of an environment’s state or dynamics to predict, generate, or evaluate possible future states, often conditioned on actions. The book does not assume that every system marketed as a world model satisfies the same standard.

**World foundation model.** A general-purpose learned model intended to be adapted across physical-AI or simulation tasks rather than trained for one narrow environment.

**4D.** Three-dimensional spatial structure plus evolution through time and action. The term is descriptive shorthand in this book, not a claim that a system has a complete model of physical reality.

**Digital twin.** A digital representation of a physical asset, process, environment, or system that is connected to its real counterpart by some combination of data, models, and updates. A digital twin may use learned world models, but the terms are not interchangeable.

**Synthetic training environment.** A simulated environment used to train or evaluate people, algorithms, or systems. It can be built from conventional simulation, learned generative models, or both.

**Reality gap.** The difference between behavior learned or evaluated in simulation and behavior that survives contact with the physical world.
