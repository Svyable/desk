*Allocating Intelligence* is about the economic and institutional problem that arrives after capable machine intelligence becomes cheap enough to be ordinary. The important question stops being whether a machine can answer and becomes who gets the answer, when, with what authority, under what verification burden, and at whose expense.

The book treats intelligence as an allocatable resource rather than a magical property. It follows the practical consequences of that view through emergency triage, mission control, aviation, manufacturing, markets, education, public infrastructure, and AI governance. Its central claim is that abundance does not abolish scarcity. It moves scarcity into attention, verification, memory, authority, latency, and trust.
