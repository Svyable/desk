# The Current Is a Confession

A high-voltage propulsion claim should show you its current trace.

Not the average current printed on the power supply. The current trace.

Brown-style devices live near corona, leakage and breakdown regimes where microamps and milliamps carry enormous explanatory weight. If thrust rises when charge transport rises, the apparatus is confessing that mobile charge is involved. That does not automatically reduce the force to ion wind, especially inside solids or unusual dielectrics, but it tells the experimenter where to look before inventing a gravitational coupling.

Current is also where dramatic demonstrations often become disappointingly ordinary. A lifter may seem to be driven by voltage because thrust grows strongly as the voltage knob turns. But voltage controls field intensity, and field intensity controls ionization. Once corona begins, current can change steeply. A force that appears to scale like voltage squared over a narrow range may be consistent with several mechanisms unless current and charge density are measured simultaneously.

This is why scaling-law arguments from old electrogravitics literature deserve reanalysis with full electrical diagnostics.

A claimed V² dependence is not a fingerprint of gravity.

Electrostatic energy itself scales with voltage squared. Maxwell stress does too. Dielectrophoretic forces can inherit similar dependencies. Electrohydrodynamic thrust can produce approximate voltage-power laws depending on geometry and discharge regime. The exponent is evidence only when competing models predict different exponents under controlled conditions.

Pulsed systems complicate the problem further. Fast voltage edges capacitively couple into supports. Discharge impulses kick structures. Electromagnetic forces act on wiring loops. A balance that is quiet under DC can become a seismograph for its own power electronics.

The obvious response is bandwidth.

Measure force and current on the same clock. If a thrust impulse follows a discharge spike, do not average the spike away and publish the remaining force. If a slow force persists after charging current decays, that is more interesting. If reversing polarity reverses a signal but not the heating profile, useful. If the effect follows stored field energy while leakage is held constant across devices, more useful still.

Brown’s old apparatus lived before cheap synchronized acquisition made this easy.

We do not have that excuse.

A frontier experiment in 2026 can digitize every channel, publish raw traces and allow strangers to search for the coupling the original team missed. The hardest part is not instrumentation. It is agreeing in advance that an ugly conventional correlation is allowed to win.

The current is a confession only if somebody is willing to listen.
