# A Force Too Small for YouTube

The experiment most likely to discover new propulsion physics will probably make terrible video.

The force will be small. The apparatus will be enclosed. Nothing will visibly rise. The interesting signal may be a few standard deviations of displacement on a calibrated torsion balance after days of pressure sweeps and polarity reversals.

That is not a defect.

Large Brown-style lifter thrust in air is already easy to obtain conventionally. If we are searching for a non-electrohydrodynamic residue, the experiment should move away from the regime where the ordinary force is huge.

This changes the engineering priorities.

Do not optimize thrust first. Optimize interpretability.

Use a geometry whose field can be modeled. Keep stored energy measurable. Make the electrical leads symmetric. Place the power source off the moving balance if possible and characterize every mechanical coupling it introduces. Use optical readout so the sensor does not push back. Put matched control articles beside the claimed article. Rotate the assembly. Change pressure by orders of magnitude. Run with gas species that alter ion mobility. Repeat at two laboratories with different balances.

Most important: decide what residual matters before opening the data.

Suppose a force of one micronewton persists at 10^-6 torr. Is that extraordinary? It depends on the apparatus. Outgassing can produce microthrust. A warm wire can. Photon pressure from a watt-scale asymmetry is nanonewton-level but not automatically irrelevant. Electrostatic interaction with grounded chamber walls can be larger. Magnetic forces from supply currents can survive vacuum perfectly because magnetism does not need air.

Vacuum eliminates ion wind. It does not eliminate physics.

A convincing anomaly should have a signature that conventional artifacts struggle to imitate simultaneously. It should reverse with an internal configuration change while total power and temperature stay nearly fixed. It should reproduce after the apparatus is rebuilt. It should survive chamber rotation. It should appear on a second kind of thrust balance. Its magnitude should follow a predeclared scaling law across devices.

That kind of signal will look boring until it becomes impossible to dismiss.

This is the opposite of the historical Brown culture, where the most persuasive object was often the one that moved most visibly. Visibility is useful for invention. It is dangerous for inference.

A levitating frame asks the audience to explain a spectacle.

A micronewton residual asks physics to explain a number.

I trust the number more.
