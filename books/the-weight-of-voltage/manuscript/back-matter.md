# Back Matter

## A short chronology

- **1920s–1930s:** Brown develops high-voltage capacitor force ideas associated later with the Biefeld–Brown name.
- **1952:** Brown’s Project Winterhaven proposal outlines a coordinated program including force measurement, dielectric studies and vacuum tests.
- **1956:** Aviation Studies (International) publishes *Electrogravitics Systems*, surveying gravity-control and electrostatic-propulsion claims.
- **1957–1958:** Brown files patent applications that mature into major U.S. electrokinetic patents.
- **1960:** U.S. Patent 2,949,550 is issued.
- **1965:** U.S. Patent 3,187,206 is issued.
- **late 1980s–1990:** R. L. Talley’s vacuum testing is reported in the advanced-propulsion literature; steady anomalous thrust is not established in high vacuum.
- **1996–2002:** NASA operates the Breakthrough Propulsion Physics Project.
- **2004:** Marc Millis’s NASA technical memorandum concludes rigorous Biefeld–Brown tests point to ion wind and that vacuum testing does not indicate new propulsion physics. NASA contractor work on asymmetric capacitors likewise develops conventional momentum-transfer explanations.
- **2011:** A *Journal of Electrostatics* analysis quantitatively models Brown–Biefeld thrust through unipolar ion drift.

## What would change the conclusion

A repeatable force in high vacuum that survives quantified electrostatic, magnetic, thermal, mechanical, radiative and outgassing controls; reproduces independently; and follows a preregistered scaling law incompatible with known momentum-transfer mechanisms would materially change the book’s assessment.

No such public result is claimed here.
