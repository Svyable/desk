# Winterhaven II

If I had to spend one million dollars on Townsend Brown, I would not build a flying disc.

I would build three boring rooms.

Room one would hold a high-vacuum chamber with two independent thrust balances: a torsion pendulum and a flexure balance. Room two would hold an atmospheric pressure rig capable of controlled gas composition, humidity and pressure. Room three would be metrology: high-voltage calibration, current probes, optical interferometry, thermal imaging, vibration characterization and the electronics needed to synchronize everything.

Then I would build families of devices instead of a hero prototype.

Asymmetric wire-to-foil geometry. Symmetric controls. Brown-style solid dielectric capacitors. Matched thermal dummies. Swappable dielectric inserts spanning large permittivity ranges. DC and pulsed variants, but only after the DC artifacts are understood.

Every device would receive an identity before testing. The analyst would not know which configuration was in the chamber during the first pass.

Pressure would be swept, not sampled at “air” and “vacuum.” That curve matters. If thrust follows gas-mediated expectations through corona and transitional regimes, we learn something. If a residual turns on below a particular pressure, we learn something else. Gas species would change ion mobility. Mechanical orientation would change. The chamber would rotate relative to gravity. Wiring would be rerouted between runs to expose cable coupling. Grounded-wall geometry would change to expose electrostatic reaction forces.

The raw data would be public.

Not because crowds are wiser than laboratories, but because fringe claims need adversaries. Somebody should be rewarded for finding the forgotten cable force.

The pre-registration would name three outcomes.

Outcome A: force is fully accounted for by electrohydrodynamic, electrostatic, magnetic, thermal and mechanical mechanisms within uncertainty. Brown’s strong electrogravity claim loses again.

Outcome B: a small residual survives but lacks stable scaling or cross-lab replication. Interesting instrument problem. No propulsion claim.

Outcome C: a reproducible residual survives controls, appears in independent apparatus and follows a preregistered scaling law inconsistent with modeled conventional forces. Publish immediately, invite hostile replication and stop calling it antigravity until the coupling is characterized.

This would be expensive by tabletop standards and cheap by aerospace standards.

It would also be historically clean.

Winterhaven’s original purpose was to perform critical experiments that could confirm or deny an electrodynamic-gravitational relationship. Seventy-four years later, the best tribute is not to assume the confirmation happened in secret.

Run the denial hard enough that confirmation would mean something.
