# Evidence Matrix

## D1 — verified / documented / directly reported

| Claim | Evidence | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| European robins use an inclination compass rather than a simple polarity compass | Wiltschko & Wiltschko 1972 and later behavioral work | Species/task-specific; not every animal magnetic sense is an inclination compass |
| Weak RF fields can disrupt magnetic-compass orientation under controlled conditions in migratory birds | Ritz 2004; Engels 2014; Leberecht 2022; later RF studies | Does not by itself identify receptor or imply wild population harm |
| Grounded aluminum screening restored robin orientation in the Oldenburg campus experiments while unscreened/noise conditions disrupted it | Engels et al. 2014 | Environmental spectrum was broad; no single urban source established |
| Cluster N lesions impair robin magnetic-compass orientation while ophthalmic trigeminal section did not in that task | Zapka et al. 2009 | Does not establish subjective visual experience or exclude trigeminal roles in other magnetic tasks |
| Previously proposed iron-rich pigeon upper-beak cells are macrophages | Treiber et al. 2012 | Refutes that cellular identification, not all particle-based magnetoreception |
| Trigeminal input is required for compensation in at least one reed-warbler magnetic-map displacement paradigm | Chernetsov/Kishkinev program | Does not identify peripheral receptor |
| European robin CRY4 is magnetically sensitive in vitro under published conditions; specific electron-transfer residues matter | Xu et al. 2021 | In vitro protein evidence, not complete in vivo receptor proof |
| Juvenile loggerheads can learn food-associated magnetic signatures | Goforth et al. 2025 | Learned discrimination is not proof of a continuous global coordinate map |
| RF treatment disrupted turtle compass behavior while preserving learned map-conditioned response | Goforth et al. 2025 | Strong functional dissociation; mechanism identity still inferred |
| The 2025 turtle paper acknowledges AFOSR grant FA9550-20-1-0399 | Published paper funding statement | Evidence of public basic-research funding only |
| A 2019 human EEG study reported alpha-band responses to selected Earth-strength field rotations | Wang et al. 2019 | Neural response report; no established conscious compass or health implication |

## D2 — credible but incomplete / model-dependent

| Claim | Why credible | Missing link |
|---|---|---|
| CRY4 is a primary receptor for the avian magnetic compass | Retinal localization, expression, spin chemistry, in vitro magnetic sensitivity, behavioral fit | Selective in vivo causal manipulation + cellular transduction + rescue |
| A radical-pair mechanism underlies the avian/turtle magnetic compass | Inclination behavior, light dependence, RF disruption, cryptochrome chemistry | Exact in vivo radical pair, receptor organization and signaling chain |
| Magnetic map sensing uses a particle-based cephalic receptor | RF-resistant turtle map, trigeminal map evidence, head-localized perturbation | Receptor cell/material/mechanical coupling not established |
| Animals use intensity/inclination combinations as regional position cues | Virtual-location behavior and map studies | Exact psychophysical resolution and neural representation vary by taxon |
| Human magnetic transduction may persist unconsciously | 2019 EEG geometry-dependent result | Independent replication, peripheral receptor and behavior |
| Urban electromagnetic noise could affect migration under some conditions | Controlled compass disruption + real anthropogenic spectra | Route-level dose-response and population consequence |

## D3 — disputed / unsupported or insufficiently confirmed

| Claim | Problem |
|---|---|
| The old pigeon upper-beak iron clusters are magnetic receptors | Corrective histology identifies them as macrophages |
| CRY4 has been proved to be the complete avian magnetoreceptor | Molecular and in vivo transduction chain remains incomplete; cryptochrome identity debates remain |
| RF exposure generally harms humans because birds are RF-sensitive | Cross-species/mechanism leap; no such inference follows |
| Urban RF explains bird population declines | No adequate causal field evidence; major confounding hazards exist |
| AFOSR funding proves a classified operational magnetoreception program | Funding acknowledgment does not establish hidden capability |
| Human magnetite proves a latent magnetic sense | Material presence is not receptor/transduction evidence |
| Birds literally see colored magnetic field lines | Phenomenological metaphor, not direct evidence |

## D4 — speculative synthesis / open conjecture

- Evolution may have repeatedly recruited separate physical mechanisms for map and compass sensing within the same animals.
- Stable anthropogenic electromagnetic distortions may be calibratable while rapidly varying noise is more disruptive.
- Electromagnetic habitat could eventually become a conservation variable analogous to acoustic or light pollution if field consequences are demonstrated.
- Sensor-fusion and reliability-estimation principles from animal navigation may be more useful for GPS-denied engineering than copying cryptochrome chemistry itself.
- Some humans may retain vestigial magnetic transduction without conscious navigational use.
- Selection in urban environments could favor altered cue weighting or magnetic-compass robustness; no direct evidence presently establishes this.
