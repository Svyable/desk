# Human geomagnetic EEG replication audit

Checked September 12, 2026.

## Primary result

Wang et al. 2019 remains the central controlled human geomagnetic EEG experiment in this manuscript:

- Connie X. Wang et al., “Transduction of the Geomagnetic Field as Evidenced from alpha-Band Activity in the Human Brain,” *eNeuro* 6 (2019): https://doi.org/10.1523/ENEURO.0483-18.2019
- Full open text / methods: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6494972/
- Raw data and analysis code were released through Caltech data repositories linked from the paper.

The final paper reports **36 adult participants**. The strongest group-level result was alpha-band event-related desynchronization after selected Earth-strength magnetic-field rotations, with marked stimulus asymmetry: counterclockwise horizontal rotations under a downward-directed vertical field produced the clearest response, while matched upward-field rotations did not. The authors also reported inclination responses.

A separate **sham declination experiment used 18 participants** and did not show the magnetosensory response under sham conditions.

The paper identified **four strongly responding participants** and retested them weeks or months later. Those individuals reproduced similar response tuning, timing, and topography within the same research program.

### Replication class

That four-person retest is **within-program repeatability**, not independent replication. It is valuable because it tests whether individual response profiles persist across sessions. It does not answer whether a new laboratory, coil system, EEG setup, participant pool, and analysis implementation will recover the effect.

The study's open data/code and detailed coil description make independent reproduction unusually tractable for an anomalous-sensory claim.

## Current field-level status

Lam & Malkemper's 2026 *Physiological Reviews* synthesis remains the most useful current status check:

- Billy Y. K. Lam & E. Pascal Malkemper, “Magnetosensation: the unsolved mystery,” *Physiological Reviews* 106 (2026), 2383–2439: https://doi.org/10.1152/physrev.00032.2025
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41902539/

The review calls the Kirschvink-group EEG work the most rigorous human geomagnetic study to date and treats the reported alpha-ERD as substantial evidence for a possible subconscious human magnetic response. It also states the key boundary explicitly: **independent replication is essential before firm conclusions can be established**.

That review therefore supports a stronger manuscript posture than either common extreme:

- too strong: “human magnetoreception has been proved”;
- too weak: “there is no serious human evidence.”

The defensible status is: one unusually controlled, open-data experimental program reported a selective, internally repeatable cortical response to geomagnetic field rotations; the effect's portability across independent laboratories remains unresolved.

## Important asymmetries that a replication should freeze

A convincing replication should not merely test for any change in alpha power after any magnetic manipulation. It should preregister the specific response topology reported in 2019:

1. downward versus upward vertical field component;
2. counterclockwise versus clockwise horizontal rotation;
3. active rotation versus antiparallel-current sham;
4. inclination-sweep conditions;
5. Earth-strength field amplitude and rotation speed;
6. participant orientation relative to the coil system;
7. alpha-ERD time window and scalp/topographic analysis;
8. handling of individual differences and pre-stimulus alpha power.

The awkward clockwise/counterclockwise asymmetry is scientifically useful. If a new laboratory reproduces a complicated response surface rather than merely a generic EEG change, artifact and researcher-degree-of-freedom explanations become harder to maintain.

## Mechanism boundary

Polarity sensitivity was used by Wang et al. to argue against a standard radical-pair inclination-compass mechanism and in favor of a ferromagnetic transducer. This is a **constraint on candidate mechanisms**, not identification of a magnetite receptor.

No receptor organ, receptor cell, magnetite structure, peripheral nerve, or causal lesion/perturbation has been shown to generate the human EEG response.

The pigeon-beak lesson therefore applies to humans too: finding magnetic material in tissue is not equivalent to identifying a sensory receptor.

## Behavioral boundary

The 2019 participants did not consciously report the field rotations. A cortical alpha response does not establish useful directional behavior or navigation.

A future behavioral bridge should therefore be prospective:

- independently replicate the EEG effect first;
- then test whether individual neural response strength predicts performance on a double-blind directional task under controlled field manipulation;
- only after that should language such as “human magnetic sense” be used in a functional navigation sense.

Until then the cleanest phrase is **candidate unconscious geomagnetic transduction**.
