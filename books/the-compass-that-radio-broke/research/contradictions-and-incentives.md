# Contradictions, Incentives, and Known Failure Modes

## Contradictions the manuscript preserves

**CRY4 versus other cryptochrome candidates.** CRY4 has strong modern molecular evidence, but some avian literature favors Cry1a or emphasizes reaction pathways not captured by a CRY4-only narrative. The book treats CRY4 as a leading candidate, not a settled monopoly.

**Whole-animal RF effects versus local-eye RF nulls.** Some whole-animal RF experiments strongly support a radical-pair compass, while a 2020 garden-warbler experiment applying oscillating fields locally to the eyes did not disrupt orientation. This can constrain anatomy/exposure models; it cannot simply be omitted.

**Beak iron versus macrophage correction.** Earlier work treated iron-rich beak structures as receptor candidates. Treiber et al. 2012 identifies the prominent structures as macrophages. Later trigeminal map evidence remains real but anatomically homeless.

**Compass trigeminal independence versus map trigeminal dependence.** This is preserved as a functional dissociation, not averaged into “mixed evidence.” It is one of the main reasons the book adopts a multi-system model.

**RF-sensitive compass versus RF-resistant turtle map.** This is the strongest current behavioral evidence for separate mechanism classes, but replication and receptor identification remain necessary.

**Laboratory disruption versus unknown wild consequence.** Controlled disorientation is documented. Population-scale effects of anthropogenic RF are not.

**Human EEG response versus absent conscious compass.** A reported neural response does not establish conscious sensation or navigational utility.

## Incentives and interpretive pressures

**Mechanism ownership.** Laboratories may become associated with radical-pair, magnetite, trigeminal or cryptochrome models. Replication and preregistered cross-lab prediction matrices reduce theory-protection incentives.

**Quantum novelty.** Journals and media have incentives to frame magnetoreception as “quantum birds.” The technical claim is narrower: spin-dependent reaction chemistry may be functionally exploited.

**Environmental-health crossover.** Bird RF results can be recruited into generalized human-EMF arguments. The book blocks cross-species extrapolation absent receptor/function evidence.

**Defense relevance.** Public AFOSR support can be inflated into classified-program narratives. Conversely, researchers may understate obvious GPS-denied-navigation relevance. The proper evidentiary claim is simply public defense interest unless records establish more.

**Sensational phenotype language.** “Turtle dance,” “sixth sense,” “built-in GPS,” and “birds see magnetic fields” are memorable but can outrun assay meaning. Manuscript prose translates them back into conditioned response, map cue, compass task and candidate phenomenology.

**Negative-result erasure.** Local RF nulls, failed receptor identifications and nonreplications are easy to omit from a compelling mechanism narrative. They are retained because they define the mechanism’s boundaries.

## Disinformation assessment

There is no identified state disinformation program materially shaping the core magnetoreception evidence reviewed here. Military funding creates institutional-interest questions but not evidence of deception. If classified-adjacent claims are later added, each should be traced to a public document, named testimony, or credible reporting and kept out of D1 unless independently documented.
