# 18. What Would Change My Mind

A book about an unresolved mechanism should end with terms of surrender.

Here are mine.

I would sharply downgrade the radical-pair account of the avian compass if well-powered, blinded, independent studies repeatedly showed that targeted disruption of the leading cryptochrome spin-chemical pathway leaves magnetic compass behavior and its neural correlates intact under conditions where the classic behavioral effect is robust. I would downgrade it further if the field’s radiofrequency and light-dependence constraints could be reproduced but were shown to arise from a non-radical-pair transducer.

I would upgrade CRY4 from leading candidate to identified receptor component if a mutation that specifically removes its Earth-strength magnetic sensitivity in vitro also abolishes the corresponding in-vivo compass signal and behavior, followed by rescue.

I would upgrade an iron-based avian receptor if a specific peripheral cell type were shown to contain organized magnetic material, connect to the appropriate neural pathway, respond to field manipulations at physiological strength, and lose that response when the magnetic structure is selectively disrupted.

I would substantially upgrade human magnetoreception if independent laboratories reproduce the 2019 EEG geometry dependence with preregistered protocols, stable within-subject responders, apparatus-artifact controls, and a mechanistic perturbation that predicts who responds. I would still not call that a conscious magnetic sense without behavioral evidence.

I would downgrade the human claim if the effect disappears across rigorous independent replications or tracks coil acoustics, induced electric fields, analysis flexibility, or another mundane artifact.

I would reject the central synthesis of this book—that magnetoreception is a real sensory phenomenon whose receptor story remains plural and incompletely resolved—if the cross-species behavioral evidence collapsed under modern blinded replication. I do not expect that. The behavioral base is too broad. But a thesis that cannot name its own defeat condition is advertising.

There is a broader lesson.

Science is often imagined as a ladder from mystery to mechanism. Magnetoreception looks more like a ship rebuilt at sea. The behavior remained while candidate hardware was replaced. A famous beak receptor died. A photochemical mechanism gained strength. Neural pathways split. A human signal appeared at the edge. The field became more constrained and less narratively simple.

That is what progress can look like when nature is not obligated to provide a clean reveal.

The compass no one can find may turn out to be several compasses.

Or one molecular trick used in several ways.

Or a photochemical compass plus an iron-based map system whose peripheral receptor is still hiding in tissue everyone has already stained.

The correct ending is not mystery.

It is a list of experiments.
