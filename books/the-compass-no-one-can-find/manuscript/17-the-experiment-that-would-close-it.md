# 17. The Experiment That Would Close It

A frontier field needs an experiment that can embarrass everyone.

For avian radical-pair magnetoreception, the closest thing to a kill experiment would combine genetics, behavior, chemistry, and neurophysiology in one causal chain.

Choose a species with robust laboratory magnetic orientation and tractable breeding or gene manipulation. Establish a preregistered baseline protocol under tightly characterized light and electromagnetic conditions. Confirm that field rotations produce the expected orientation shift and that sham rotations do not.

Then alter the candidate cryptochrome in a way chosen from its spin chemistry, not merely delete the gene wholesale. A complete knockout can introduce developmental and circadian confounds. Better is a mutation predicted to impair the relevant radical-pair pathway while preserving expression and other major functions.

Before testing the animal, purify the mutant protein. Show that the intended magnetic sensitivity is lost or sharply changed in vitro while ordinary folding and relevant photochemistry remain acceptably intact.

Then return to the bird.

If the radical-pair model is right, the magnetic compass should degrade in the predicted way. Solar or stellar orientation should remain where possible. General vision should remain. Locomotion should remain. The behavioral deficit should follow the magnetic task.

Now record the brain. Cluster N or other implicated visual pathways should lose the field-dependent signature associated with the compass condition while retaining ordinary visual responsiveness.

Finally rescue the phenotype by restoring the wild-type sequence or receptor function.

That is a chain:

physical property → molecule → cell → circuit → behavior.

Each arrow is where frontier claims usually hide.

A parallel experiment should attack the iron/trigeminal branch. Identify field-responsive peripheral cells through unbiased functional screening rather than historical location. Trace them into the trigeminal system. Perturb the candidate magnetic structure physically or genetically. Test map-like magnetic responses separately from the light-dependent compass.

The experiments should be multi-lab by design. Apparatus duplicated independently. Analysis preregistered. Raw field spectra published. Positive and negative animals retained in the denominator. Failed replications treated as data, not betrayal.

There is a reason to be this severe. Magnetoreception is mature enough that another decade of suggestive localization will add less than one decisive causal chain.

The field does not need a more persuasive diagram.

It needs a receptor that can be broken and repaired.
