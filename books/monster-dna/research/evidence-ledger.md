# MONSTER DNA — evidence ledger

## Working epistemic rule

Do not turn a sequence into an organism, a nondetection into proof of absence, or a famous witness category into a biological taxon.

## Class 1 — verified / documented

- Environmental DNA consists of genetic material shed or otherwise released into environmental media. USFWS explicitly uses eDNA for invasive, native and imperiled-species monitoring: https://www.fws.gov/project/environmental-dna-edna
- USGS uses eDNA operationally for hard-to-detect invasive organisms including Burmese pythons and invasive carp: https://www.usgs.gov/programs/biological-threats-and-invasive-species-research-program/science/battling-invaders
- USGS warns that an eDNA detection establishes genetic material, not necessarily organism presence at the sampling point: https://nas.er.usgs.gov/eDNA/
- Goldberg et al. 2016 reviews transport, degradation, false inference and QA/QC burdens in aquatic eDNA detection: https://doi.org/10.1111/2041-210X.12595
- Rees et al. 2014 reviews aquatic-animal eDNA detection and the importance of DNA release, diffusion and degradation: https://doi.org/10.1111/1365-2664.12306
- Tsuji et al. 2019 synthesized protocols across 438 aquatic-macroorganism eDNA studies, useful evidence that method heterogeneity is itself part of the inference problem: https://doi.org/10.1002/edn3.21
- Itakura et al. 2019 found eDNA useful for Japanese-eel distribution and examined relationships with abundance/biomass; this is a control against naïvely converting eel-DNA quantity into individual eel size: https://doi.org/10.1002/aqc.3058
- USFWS reports a 2024 U.S. National Strategy for Aquatic eDNA developed by an interagency task team: https://www.fws.gov/story/2024-07/skin-scales-and-scat-national-edna-strategy

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete

- The 2018 Loch Ness eDNA campaign and 2019 public reporting described extensive sampling and widespread eel DNA. Exact methods, sequence-accession trail, sampling map, controls and peer-reviewed publication status must be reconstructed before manuscript-level quantitative claims.
- Historical witness reports may be useful for defining candidate morphology, habitat and sampling strata, but testimony does not establish taxonomic identity.
- Unassigned metabarcoding reads can be scientifically interesting but may reflect database incompleteness, low-quality fragments, conserved barcode regions or ordinary undescribed diversity.

## Class 3 — disputed / not established

- A surviving plesiosaur population in Loch Ness.
- A resident breeding population of any large unknown vertebrate in Loch Ness.
- A giant eel large enough to explain classic Nessie descriptions. Widespread eel DNA establishes eel genetic material, not exceptional individual body size.
- Any claim that the Loch Ness survey alone proved universal absence of an unknown organism.
- Any claim that an unclassified environmental sequence is evidence of a cryptid without replication, phylogenetic placement and organism-level linkage.

## Class 4 — speculative synthesis / open conjecture

### Biological residue burden
A resident biological hypothesis incurs expected molecular and ecological residue. Expected detectability should generally increase with population size, residency, biomass, reproductive activity and sampling coverage, but the relationship must be calibrated by taxon and environment.

### Absence has a calibration curve
Repeated nondetection should update belief only through estimated detection probability under the actual protocol. A negative bottle is not empty evidence; it is weak or strong depending on assay sensitivity, coverage, transport, degradation and target ecology.

### Narrative refuge
Measurement imperfections can become permanent refuge for anomaly claims when no prospective defeat condition exists. This is an epistemic pattern to test, not a claim about believer motives.

## Contradictions to preserve

- eDNA can outperform visual/capture surveys for rare or secretive species, yet it can also produce transported positives that do not locate an organism.
- Rapid degradation can make water-column eDNA temporally informative while simultaneously creating false negatives after a target has moved or shedding has fallen.
- Sediment can preserve DNA longer, increasing historical reach while weakening claims about current presence.
- More sensitive assays improve rare-species detection while raising contamination and false-positive burdens.
- Broad metabarcoding can discover unexpected taxa while depending heavily on primer choice and reference-database completeness.

## Publication holds

Do not publish exact Loch Ness sample counts, species counts, percentages, read totals or spatial coverage until recovered from the originating scientific record rather than press summaries.

Do not say `no plesiosaur DNA` unless the assay/reference design actually had a validated path to identifying the hypothesized lineage.

Do not infer eel size from eel-DNA abundance.

Do not describe an eDNA positive as a captured or visually confirmed organism.

Do not describe nondetection as proof of absence without an explicit detection-probability model.

## Primary-source queue

1. Recover the Loch Ness project’s originating methods/data/publication record and sampling coordinates.
2. Reconstruct positive controls: which known resident vertebrates were detected and which known taxa were missed?
3. Build a quantitative occupancy/detection model for hypothetical resident populations at 1, 5, 20 and 100 individuals under plausible shedding ranges.
4. Review species-specific shedding experiments across fish, amphibians and reptiles.
5. Reconstruct invasive-carp cases where eDNA detection preceded physical capture, including transported-DNA disputes.
6. Reconstruct Burmese-python eDNA sensitivity against visual survey/capture denominators.
7. Audit reference-database completeness for candidate large-vertebrate lineages.
8. Review sedimentary DNA persistence and its implications for historical-versus-current presence.
9. Design a preregistered multi-lab cryptid survey with blinded controls, archived filters and explicit defeat thresholds.

## Strongest defeat condition

The book’s distinctive thesis weakens if quantitative review shows that plausible large resident vertebrate populations can routinely remain eDNA-invisible even under dense, replicated, validated aquatic sampling, or if cryptid-scale inference adds nothing beyond ordinary rare-species occupancy methodology. In that case `biological residue burden` is rhetoric, not a useful framework, and should be cut.
