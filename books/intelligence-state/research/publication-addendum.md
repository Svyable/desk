## Chapter 11 — The Eighty Percent Problem

### AI adoption versus realized economic benefit

Inter-American Development Bank, **Will AI Boost Productivity—or Widen Inequality?** (July 27, 2026):

- Around 80% of firms in Latin America and the Caribbean use AI tools.
- 23% report economic benefits.
- 6% report significant impact.
- The IDB emphasizes investment, skills, digital infrastructure, governance, and workflow redesign as necessary complements to adoption.

Source: https://www.iadb.org/en/blog/economic-analysis/will-ai-boost-productivity-or-widen-inequality

### Jobs, augmentation potential, and digital bottlenecks

World Bank / International Labour Organization work on generative AI and jobs in Latin America and the Caribbean:

- Research estimates roughly 26–38% of jobs in the region are exposed to generative AI, depending on methodology and country structure.
- Roughly 8–14% of jobs could experience productivity-enhancing transformation under the studied scenarios.
- About 2–5% are estimated to face full-automation risk under current capabilities.
- Up to roughly half of jobs with augmentation potential—around 17 million jobs in the cited work—may be unable to realize that potential because of gaps in digital access and infrastructure.

Sources:
- https://www.worldbank.org/en/topic/poverty/publication/generative-ai-and-jobs-in-lac
- https://www.worldbank.org/en/results/2025/04/15/quantifying-the-jobs-potential-of-ai-in-latin-america-and-the-caribbean

Treat these figures as model-based estimates, not forecasts of realized employment outcomes.

## Chapter 13 — Government Gets an Agent

### AI in core government functions

OECD, **Governing with Artificial Intelligence: The State of Play and Way Forward in Core Government Functions** (September 18, 2025):

- The report analyzes 200 AI use cases across 11 core government functions.
- It identifies benefits including automation and tailoring of public services, decision support, forecasting, fraud detection, and support for public servants.
- It also identifies risks and implementation barriers including biased data, opacity, overreliance, digital divides, skills gaps, legacy IT, financial constraints, and weak guidance.
- Its seven recurring enabling layers are governance, data, digital infrastructure, skills, investment, procurement, and partnerships.
- OECD reporting indicates that a majority of OECD countries were already using AI in public-service design and delivery by the underlying survey period.

Sources:
- https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/governing-with-artificial-intelligence_795de142-en.html
- https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/2025/06/governing-with-artificial-intelligence_398fa287/full-report/ai-in-public-service-design-and-delivery_09704c1a.html
- https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/2025/06/governing-with-artificial-intelligence_398fa287/full-report/ai-in-public-procurement_2e095543.html

## Chapter 16 — Democracy at Machine Speed

### Synthetic-content transparency and provenance

NIST, **Reducing Risks Posed by Synthetic Content: An Overview of Technical Approaches to Digital Content Transparency** (November 20, 2024):

- Reviews technical approaches for content authentication and provenance, watermarking, synthetic-content detection, testing, auditing, and related transparency mechanisms.
- Treat provenance, watermarking, and detection as distinct tools with different failure modes; none should be presented as a universal truth detector.

Source: https://www.nist.gov/publications/reducing-risks-posed-synthetic-content-overview-technical-approaches-digital-content

### C2PA Content Credentials

Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity, **C2PA Technical Specification 2.4** (April 2026):

- Defines a technical architecture for cryptographically verifiable provenance information about digital assets.
- Content Credentials can preserve assertions about creation and modification history, signatures, ingredients, and other provenance data.
- The design goals include privacy, interoperability, scalability, security, accessibility, global applicability, and whole-workflow provenance.
- Version 2.4 added new format and assertion capabilities; the existence of provenance does not establish that the underlying claim or content is substantively true.

Sources:
- https://spec.c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/2.4/specs/C2PA_Specification.html
- https://spec.c2pa.org/post/contentcredentials/

## Chapter 17 — The Hemisphere Bargain

The proposed **Hemisphere Intelligence Compact** is an analytical proposal of this book, not an existing treaty or announced intergovernmental program. Its components—shared evaluations, emergency compute access, talent mobility, trusted data spaces, infrastructure coordination, procurement standards, and capability-building finance—should remain clearly labeled as recommendations.
