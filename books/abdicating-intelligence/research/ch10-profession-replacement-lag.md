# Chapter 10 — profession replacement lag and publication-safe media

Rechecked 2026-09-14 for the media/research enhancement pass on “A Profession Without Beginners.”

## Why this evidence belongs here

Chapter 10 is strongest when it treats professional succession as a stock-and-flow problem rather than asserting that AI necessarily deskills workers. The missing empirical anchor is lead time: in some professions, changes to entry and training capacity can take years to appear in the visible stock of experienced practitioners.

Medicine provides a useful concrete example because U.S. federal agencies explicitly model physician supply as a long-horizon workforce problem. This is an analogy about replacement lag, not evidence that AI causes physician shortages.

## Federal evidence

### HRSA — physician workforce projections

The Health Resources and Services Administration’s Bureau of Health Workforce states that preparing a physician can take more than a decade of education and residency and therefore argues that future workforce shortages require action well in advance. Its current National Center for Health Workforce Analysis projection estimates an overall shortage of 141,160 physicians in 2038, including shortages across 30 of 35 modeled physician specialties.

Source: https://bhw.hrsa.gov/data-research/projecting-health-workforce-supply-demand

Publication boundary: these projections are scenario/model outputs, not a prediction with certainty. They should not be used to imply that shortages are caused by AI, reduced junior work, or any single training mechanism.

### CMS — additional graduate medical education positions

CMS has been implementing congressionally authorized expansions of Medicare-supported graduate medical education. Its FY 2025 financial report describes allocation of 1,200 GME slots phased in over multiple years, with emphasis on hospitals serving underserved communities. Separately, CMS finalized implementation of 200 additional Medicare-funded GME positions beginning in 2026 under section 4122 of the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2023, with at least half directed to psychiatry or psychiatry subspecialties.

Sources:
- FY 2025 CMS Financial Report: https://www.cms.gov/files/document/cms-financial-report-fiscal-year-2025.pdf
- CMS FY 2025 IPPS final-rule release describing the additional 200 positions beginning in 2026: https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/press-releases/hhs-finalizes-new-policies-support-underserved-communities-mitigate-drug-shortages-promote-safety

What this supports: training capacity is an infrastructure variable that can be expanded deliberately, but expansion operates with long lags. The useful analogy for Chapter 10 is that professions cannot instantly replenish experienced practitioners after a pipeline shock.

What it does not support: the 1,200 and 200 slot policies do not establish an optimal physician supply, and they are not evidence about AI-driven automation.

## Broader apprenticeship context

The U.S. Department of Labor maintains Registered Apprenticeship data through Apprenticeship.gov. The system spans construction, healthcare, technology, energy, education, transportation, financial services, and other sectors. Its live state dashboard currently includes data through September 3, 2026, while DOL continues federal and state investments to expand apprenticeship capacity.

Sources:
- https://www.apprenticeship.gov/data-and-statistics
- https://www.apprenticeship.gov/data-and-statistics/apprentices-by-state-dashboard
- https://www.dol.gov/newsroom/releases/eta/eta20250630

This is useful context because it shows that structured replacement pipelines are not peculiar to medicine. Do not treat Registered Apprenticeship counts as a proxy for all professional training or compare them directly with physician residency counts.

- Place the figure shortly after Chapter 10 introduces the stock-and-flow distinction and before the manuscript moves into medicine, software, and other examples.
- Keep the entire pipeline on one page or one EPUB viewport where possible; splitting the visible stock from the weakened replacement flow defeats the comparison.
- Prefer full text width in print.
- Preserve grayscale contrast; the figure does not rely on color.
- Keep the caption attached to the figure and retain the explicit no-causal-AI qualifier.
- Use the supplied alt text in EPUB/HTML rather than a filename-derived description.
- Do not place CMS or HRSA agency logos on the figure. Authority should come from citations, not borrowed institutional visual identity.

## Editorial implication

The most useful addition to Chapter 10 is not another warning that juniors need practice. It is a time-delay mechanism. A profession can report strong current output, strong senior headcount, and even rising productivity while its future replacement rate has already changed. That lag is why succession risk can be economically rational to ignore in the short term and expensive to discover late.
