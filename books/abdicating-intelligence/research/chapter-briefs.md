# Chapter Briefs and Falsification Notes

These briefs track what each chapter has to establish and what evidence would weaken the argument. They are not prose outlines.

## Chapter 1 — The Answer You Cannot Check

**Job:** Open with *Mata v. Avianca* and distinguish machine error from institutional failure to verify.

**Evidence to seek:** Sanctions order; automation-bias research; professional responsibility and inspectable-source practices.

**Counterevidence:** AI-supported verification can outperform human-only review and should not be rejected merely because machines generated the first draft.

**Falsification note:** The chapter fails if it implies the court prohibited AI use or if “human review” is treated as sufficient without competence, time, evidence, and authority.

## Chapter 2 — The Old Fear

**Job:** Use the *Phaedrus* writing story to reject both nostalgia and automatic dismissal of cognitive-change concerns.

**Evidence to seek:** Primary text; cognitive-offloading and transactive-memory literature.

**Counterevidence:** Writing and other external memory technologies massively increased collective cognition despite changing individual memory practice.

**Falsification note:** Do not claim writing caused a measurable historical decline in memory for which the chapter has no evidence.

## Chapter 3 — The Things We Put Down

**Job:** Define cognitive offloading as an adaptive strategy and separate storage, calculation, framing, and search.

**Evidence to seek:** Risko and Gilbert; Sparrow, Liu, and Wegner; reminder-choice research.

**Counterevidence:** Offloading often improves performance and can be the rational allocation of scarce memory and attention.

**Falsification note:** The chapter fails if offloading is equated with laziness or if all external cognition is treated as the same transfer.

## Chapter 4 — The Map in Your Head

**Job:** Show why internal models can matter even when external navigation is better at route execution.

**Evidence to seek:** London taxi hippocampal work; neural activity during navigation from long-term knowledge.

**Counterevidence:** GPS and turn-by-turn navigation provide enormous benefits; memorizing a city is not a universal cognitive ideal.

**Falsification note:** Do not infer simple causation from cross-sectional taxi-driver anatomy or imply that a larger hippocampal measure straightforwardly means “more intelligence.”

## Chapter 5 — Out of the Loop

**Job:** Establish the automation paradox: ordinary performance can improve while takeover awareness or practice weakens.

**Evidence to seek:** Bainbridge; Endsley and Kiris; FAA manual-flight guidance.

**Counterevidence:** Automation is central to aviation safety and can reduce workload and human error.

**Falsification note:** Do not blame complex aviation accidents on “too much automation” without accident-specific evidence.

## Chapter 6 — The Vanishing Repetition

**Job:** Explain how generation, retrieval, feedback, and structured practice can produce learning hidden inside routine work.

**Evidence to seek:** Generation effect; retrieval practice; deliberate-practice literature; productive failure and worked-example counterliterature.

**Counterevidence:** Unguided struggle can waste time; examples and scaffolding can improve novice learning.

**Falsification note:** Do not turn difficulty into virtue or repeat a simplistic ten-thousand-hours claim.

## Chapter 7 — The Verification Trap

**Job:** Show that verification has its own cognitive and economic burden and that independence matters more than repeated agreement.

**Evidence to seek:** Automation-bias reviews; AI risk-management frameworks; engineering assurance concepts.

**Counterevidence:** Model-based review and automated checks can be stronger and more scalable than human checking.

**Falsification note:** Do not prescribe a human checkpoint where it adds only delay or ceremonial approval.

## Chapter 8 — The Student Who Gets It Right

**Job:** Separate assisted task performance from later learning and show that interface design can change the relationship.

**Evidence to seek:** Bastani et al. high-school mathematics field experiment; educational AI guidance.

**Counterevidence:** Guardrailed tutoring in the same research mitigated the negative learning effect; AI can expand access to individualized support.

**Falsification note:** One study is not evidence that generative AI universally harms education.

## Chapter 9 — The Junior Problem

**Job:** Treat entry-level work as both production and a historical learning pipeline.

**Evidence to seek:** Generative AI at Work; situated cognition and apprenticeship research.

**Counterevidence:** AI can diffuse expert patterns to novices and may accelerate development rather than interrupt it.

**Falsification note:** Do not defend junior drudgery merely because it once coincided with learning.

## Chapter 10 — A Profession Without Beginners

**Job:** Extend the pipeline problem across law, medicine, and software while keeping it a testable hypothesis.

**Evidence to seek:** Professional training structures; cognitive apprenticeship; evidence on AI-assisted skill acquisition as it emerges.

**Counterevidence:** If machines eventually assume both routine and exceptional responsibility, some human skill pipelines may rationally shrink.

**Falsification note:** Do not claim a demonstrated profession-wide collapse of expertise without longitudinal evidence.

## Chapter 11 — The Memory Outside Us

**Job:** Give the strongest affirmative case for extended and distributed cognition.

**Evidence to seek:** Clark and Chalmers; Hutchins; Hardwig; transactive-memory literature.

**Counterevidence:** External systems can create opaque or concentrated dependencies even while increasing total capability.

**Falsification note:** The book fails conceptually if it treats cognition as valuable only when located inside one human skull.

## Chapter 12 — The Machine That Teaches

**Job:** Show that superior AI performance can become educational material for humans.

**Evidence to seek:** Human Go performance after superhuman AI; call-center productivity gains for less experienced workers.

**Counterevidence:** Suggestion-level assistance and end-to-end autonomous action create different exposure and learning conditions.

**Falsification note:** Do not infer durable learning from short-run productivity alone.

## Chapter 13 — Institutional Amnesia by Design

**Job:** Separate stored records from operational institutional memory and examine vendor-mediated cognition.

**Evidence to seek:** Therac-25 system-safety analysis; knowledge-management and continuity practice.

**Counterevidence:** AI retrieval can make institutional records more usable and preserve retiring expertise.

**Falsification note:** Do not reduce Therac-25 to an “AI-like” accident; it is an analogy about system safety and overconfidence in software.

## Chapter 14 — The Default Answer

**Job:** Examine the shift from ranked sources to generated synthesis as the default interface to knowledge.

**Evidence to seek:** Pew browsing research on AI summaries and source clicking; source-economy evidence as it develops.

**Counterevidence:** Generated summaries can improve access, comprehension, and search efficiency.

**Falsification note:** Do not imply that traditional search was neutral or that every reduction in clicking indicates worse knowledge.

## Chapter 15 — The Private Reality Machine

**Job:** Explore personalized explanation as both accessibility technology and a risk to shared reference points.

**Evidence to seek:** Research on personalization, common knowledge, persuasion, and AI-mediated explanation in later revisions.

**Counterevidence:** Personalization can translate across expertise levels and worldviews while preserving shared source records.

**Falsification note:** Do not claim personalized systems necessarily create political polarization without evidence.

## Chapter 16 — Cognitive Debt

**Job:** Define cognitive debt as a future capability obligation, not as a moral judgment against convenience.

**Evidence to seek:** Skill-decay research, automation studies, succession examples, portability and resilience practice.

**Counterevidence:** Lost capabilities create no debt when they are genuinely obsolete or cheap to rebuild.

**Falsification note:** The metaphor fails if it labels every automated task a liability.

## Chapter 17 — The Reserve Mind

**Job:** Develop selective cognitive redundancy based on consequence, recovery time, reversibility, and correlated failure.

**Evidence to seek:** Recurrent training, safety engineering, continuity planning, vendor concentration evidence.

**Counterevidence:** Human fallbacks can be slower and less reliable than automated alternatives; machine redundancy may be better.

**Falsification note:** Do not equate reserve with full manual duplication.

## Chapter 18 — Useful Friction

**Job:** Distinguish safety friction, learning friction, and agency friction from arbitrary inconvenience.

**Evidence to seek:** Retrieval and generation effects; safety check practices; human-factors research on alerts and habituation.

**Counterevidence:** Excessive friction creates bypass behavior, fatigue, delay, and paternalism.

**Falsification note:** Every proposed friction should have a named purpose tied to consequence, learning, or agency.

## Chapter 19 — Tools for Thought

**Job:** Translate the argument into product behavior that can preserve provenance, uncertainty, agency, learning, and exit.

**Evidence to seek:** Human-centered AI design; Microsoft knowledge-work research; tutoring evidence; provenance practices.

**Counterevidence:** Completion-first tools are appropriate for many tasks and should not be forced into teaching behavior.

**Falsification note:** “Tool for thought” cannot be branding alone; the system should measurably improve a user's reasoning environment.

## Chapter 20 — The Right to a Human Reason

**Job:** Separate meaningful human reason and contestability from ceremonial human clicking in consequential decisions.

**Evidence to seek:** OECD principles; NIST risk-management guidance; legal and administrative due-process scholarship for later revision.

**Counterevidence:** Fully automated routine decisions can be faster, fairer, and more consistent than reperforming them manually.

**Falsification note:** The chapter's title is normative; do not present it as a universally existing legal right.

## Chapter 21 — The New Literacy

**Job:** Define durable AI literacy beyond prompt technique: source, uncertainty, calibration, objective, independence, learning, agency, and social meaning.

**Evidence to seek:** AI literacy research and domain-specific professional standards as they mature.

**Counterevidence:** Users cannot and should not inspect every system interaction; mature literacy must permit rational trust.

**Falsification note:** Do not turn literacy into permanent skepticism or a generic checklist detached from domain knowledge.

## Chapter 22 — The Unplugged Test

**Job:** Make reserve capability testable without pretending unaided performance is the only real performance.

**Evidence to seek:** Simulation, tabletop exercises, recurrent proficiency testing, transfer-of-learning research.

**Counterevidence:** Some old skills deserve retirement, and machine or procedural fallbacks may be stronger than human ones.

**Falsification note:** Tests should diagnose the human-machine system, not punish workers for capabilities the workflow stopped training.

## Chapter 23 — Intelligence Without Abdication

**Job:** Synthesize an institutional design philosophy based on consequence, succession, dependence, legitimacy, and periodic redesign.

**Evidence to seek:** Cross-domain examples accumulated through the book; new empirical evidence should alter boundaries rather than be fit to the thesis.

**Counterevidence:** Strong automation may justify moving more authority away from humans where evidence supports it.

**Falsification note:** The chapter fails if it freezes a permanent “human versus machine” boundary.

## Chapter 24 — The Performance of Knowing

**Job:** Examine what happens when polished expert-like output becomes cheap and traditional artifacts become weaker signals of the producer's underlying understanding.

**Evidence to seek:** Processing-fluency research; truth-effect literature; human-AI trust and explanation studies; changing assessment practices.

**Counterevidence:** AI can reduce exclusionary jargon, improve expert communication, and make sophisticated participation available to capable outsiders.

**Falsification note:** Do not claim that fluent prose is inherently deceptive or that older credentials and artifacts were reliable measures of understanding.

## Chapter 25 — The Council of Machines

**Job:** Distinguish nominal multiplicity from functional independence in machine ensembles and agent debates.

**Evidence to seek:** Multi-agent debate research; heterogeneous versus homogeneous ensemble performance; cases of incorrect model consensus; system-resilience literature.

**Counterevidence:** Ensembles, self-consistency, debate, and majority voting can materially improve performance even when independence is imperfect.

**Falsification note:** Do not imply that agreement among models is useless; the relevant variable is how much new information or distinct error structure each member contributes.

## Chapter 26 — The Childhood Question

**Job:** Treat growing up with conversational AI as a developmental design problem while preserving the current lack of long-run causal evidence.

**Evidence to seek:** Bastani et al.; UNESCO education guidance; UNICEF child-centered AI guidance and emerging research on child-chatbot interaction.

**Counterevidence:** AI tutoring can expand access, personalize practice, support disability access, and create abundant feedback and rehearsal.

**Falsification note:** Do not claim that conversational AI has already been shown to damage children's social, emotional, or cognitive development over the long run.

## Chapter 27 — The Question Before the Answer

**Job:** Move upstream from answer quality to problem framing, objective choice, anomaly recognition, and hypothesis generation.

**Evidence to seek:** AI-assisted scientific-discovery literature; research on hypothesis generation; examples where categories or objectives determine what a system can see.

**Counterevidence:** AI can widen the hypothesis space, cross disciplinary boundaries, surface alternatives, and help humans ask questions they would not generate alone.

**Falsification note:** Do not romanticize human intuition or assert that machines cannot generate important questions; the claim is about governance of framing and objective choice.

## Chapter 28 — The Human Who Signs

**Job:** Separate real accountability from ceremonial human sign-off in AI-mediated work.

**Evidence to seek:** Automation-bias and complacency literature; AI risk-management guidance; scientific authorship and professional-responsibility policies; audit and provenance practices.

**Counterevidence:** Fully automated processes with strong monitoring, appeal, and accountable system ownership can be safer than exhausted humans rubber-stamping every case.

**Falsification note:** “Human in the loop” is not treated as sufficient; responsibility must be paired with authority, evidence access, competence, time, and the capacity to change the system.

## Chapter 29 — Cognitive Sovereignty

**Job:** Extend the dependency argument to organizations, states, and language communities without equating sovereignty with technological autarky.

**Evidence to seek:** OECD competition work on cloud, compute, and model concentration; switching and interoperability evidence; UNESCO multilingual AI work; public-sector and national AI infrastructure strategies.

**Counterevidence:** Global model and cloud providers can give small organizations and countries better capabilities than local self-sufficiency, and concentration can coexist with strong competition and portability.

**Falsification note:** Do not use “sovereignty” as a synonym for domestic ownership. The relevant test is agency under interdependence: ability to switch, adapt, verify, preserve records, represent local law or language, and continue essential functions.

## Chapter 30 — Still Thinking

**Job:** End on responsibility rather than superiority: what people and institutions remain responsible for understanding and choosing.

**Evidence to seek:** No new evidence burden; conclusion should accurately compress earlier findings, including the expanded arguments about signals of expertise, machine consensus, children, question formation, accountability, and cognitive sovereignty.

**Counterevidence:** Preserve the possibility that internal human skills can shrink while collective intelligence grows.

**Falsification note:** The ending should not turn into anti-AI nostalgia or claim human uniqueness it has not established.
