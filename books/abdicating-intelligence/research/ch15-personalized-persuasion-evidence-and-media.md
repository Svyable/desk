# Chapter 15 — Personalized persuasion evidence and media

## Editorial purpose

Support the chapter's distinction between helpful personalization and individualized persuasion without implying that every personalized AI system is manipulative or that experimental effects establish population-scale political harm.

## Source 1 — Matz et al., personalized persuasion with generative AI

**Source:** Sandra C. Matz, Jacob D. Teeny, Sumer S. Vaid, Heinrich Peters, Gabriella M. Harari, and Moran Cerf, “The potential of generative AI for personalized persuasion at scale,” *Scientific Reports* 14, 4692 (2024).

**URL:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-53755-0

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-53755-0

**Publication date:** February 26, 2024.

**Authority:** Peer-reviewed research article in *Scientific Reports*.

**What it establishes:** Across four studies comprising seven sub-studies (total N = 1,788), the authors compared personalized and non-personalized messages generated with ChatGPT across consumer and political contexts and several targeting dimensions, including personality, ideology, and moral foundations. The paper reports that personalized messages were significantly more influential than non-personalized messages across the tested settings. The authors describe 30 of 33 message instantiations as directionally effective and 20 as statistically significant.

**What it does not establish:** The experiments do not show that all personalization increases persuasion, that the measured effects persist indefinitely, that ordinary deployed assistants currently infer or use these traits in the same way, or that personalized AI has already altered elections or public opinion at population scale.

## Source 2 — Federal Trade Commission, dark patterns

**Source:** Federal Trade Commission, “Bringing Dark Patterns to Light” and accompanying release.

**URLs:**

- https://www.ftc.gov/reports/bringing-dark-patterns-light
- https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2022/09/ftc-report-shows-rise-sophisticated-dark-patterns-designed-trick-trap-consumers

**Publication date:** September 2022.

**Authority:** U.S. Federal Trade Commission staff report and agency release.

**What it establishes:** The FTC documents interface practices that can obscure, subvert, or impair consumer choice, including disguised advertising, buried material terms, difficult cancellation flows, and privacy interfaces designed to steer users toward greater data sharing. The report is useful here because it shows that manipulation need not take the form of a plainly false sentence; choice architecture and presentation can do consequential steering.

**What it does not establish:** The FTC report predates widespread generative-AI deployment and is not evidence that conversational AI systems currently engage in the specific conduct described. Its examples concern digital design and commerce, not generative personalized explanation.

**Rights:** U.S. federal government source. Do not reuse FTC seals or page artwork. The book can safely cite and paraphrase the report; any direct visual reuse requires item-level verification.

**Publication suitability:** High for factual support and short properly attributed excerpts. No FTC artwork is needed for this chapter.

**Title:** Shared source, personalized paths

**Concept:** Two architectures for personalization.

- In the first, a durable common source feeds multiple tailored explanations. The wording and examples can differ, but each path remains visibly traceable to the same record.
- In the second, a user profile influences not only explanation but also which evidence is surfaced, which objections are omitted, and which frame is emphasized. Different users can therefore leave with different apparent objects of discussion.

## Manuscript integration boundary

A defensible sentence is: “In a 2024 series of experiments totaling 1,788 participants, Matz and colleagues found that ChatGPT-generated messages matched to recipient traits were more influential than non-personalized messages across the tested consumer and political contexts.”

Do not turn this into “AI knows how to manipulate everyone,” “personalization always works,” or a claim about election outcomes.

The FTC material can support the narrower proposition that digital systems can steer choices through presentation and interface structure without relying on a straightforward factual lie. It should not be cited as evidence about conversational AI behavior.

## Next research opportunity

The highest-value follow-up is longitudinal evidence about whether repeated individualized conversational framing changes users' ability to recognize counterarguments, identify shared source material, or accurately describe opposing positions. Current persuasion studies are much stronger on immediate attitude effects than on durable shared-reality consequences.
