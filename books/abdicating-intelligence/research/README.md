# Research brief — Abdicating Intelligence

The book's research question is not whether AI makes people smarter or dumber in the abstract. It is which forms of cognitive delegation change assisted performance, later independent performance, expertise formation, verification, institutional memory, and recovery capacity — and under what conditions.

The manuscript deliberately carries counterevidence. Cognitive offloading can be adaptive. Transactive and distributed cognition are foundational to civilization. AI assistance has produced large gains for less experienced workers in at least some field settings, and superhuman AI has been associated with improved and more novel human play in Go. Those cases constrain any deskilling claim.

The strongest negative claims are similarly scoped. Automation research supports risks around out-of-the-loop performance, complacency, and automation bias, but these are properties of human-automation systems rather than proof that automation is intrinsically harmful. Education evidence distinguishes immediate assisted performance from later unassisted learning and shows that design can change the outcome.

## Research rules

- Prefer primary sources, peer-reviewed research, court orders, government guidance, and first-party institutional material.
- Do not turn one experiment into a universal law.
- Distinguish cross-sectional evidence from causal evidence.
- Preserve the difference between individual cognition and distributed system capability.
- Treat professional deskilling as a pipeline hypothesis unless longitudinal evidence supports a stronger claim.
- Treat the legal phrase “right to a human reason” as the author's normative formulation, not a statement of universal current law.
- Update the source ledger when new factual examples enter the prose.
- If a later source materially contradicts the thesis, preserve the contradiction and revise the argument rather than explaining it away.

The chapter briefs are argument tests, not prose outlines. Their falsification notes identify claims that would make the chapter misleading if ignored.
