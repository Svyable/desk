# Chapter 13 — Institutional memory evidence and media

## Purpose

Support the chapter's institutional-memory argument with legally clean primary sources and an original figure. The evidentiary target is narrow: organizations can possess records yet lose reliable retrieval, interpretation, continuity, and independent operational capability.

## Source 1 — Columbia Accident Investigation Board, Volume I (2003)

- **Institution:** Columbia Accident Investigation Board / NASA Technical Reports Server.
- **Publication date:** August 1, 2003.
- **Primary URL:** https://ntrs.nasa.gov/archive/nasa/casi.ntrs.nasa.gov/20030093634.pdf
- **NTRS record:** https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20030093634
- **Rights status:** NTRS marks the report **“Work of the US Gov. Public Use Permitted.”** Distribution limit: Public.
- **Publication suitability:** Suitable for commercial-book factual use and, if needed later, carefully selected facsimile reproduction from the government report. Avoid NASA insignia/logos as decorative branding; use document pages only when substantively necessary.
- **Authority:** Independent accident investigation board report hosted by NASA.
- **Relevant finding:** The report does not reduce the Columbia loss to foam damage. It identifies organizational causes and calls for an independent technical authority, independent safety assurance, and characteristics of a learning organization. The report says the investigation examined more than 30,000 documents, conducted more than 200 formal interviews, and considered the Shuttle program's history, culture, management practices, and safety architecture.
- **Use boundary:** Columbia is evidence that safety knowledge and failure imagination are organizational, not evidence that AI systems produce Columbia-like failures. Do not imply an AI causal analogy.

### Manuscript use

Use as a compact historical anchor immediately after the Therac-25 discussion: Columbia shows a mature technical organization can possess enormous amounts of documentation and expertise while organizational routines, incentives, communication, and assumptions still determine whether danger becomes actionable knowledge.

## Source 2 — NASA Columbia lessons-learned collection

- **Institution:** NASA Safety and Mission Assurance.
- **URL:** https://sma.nasa.gov/sma-disciplines/accllp/columbia
- **Rights/provenance:** U.S. federal government source; individual embedded third-party media should still be checked item by item before reuse.
- **Relevant context:** NASA's lessons-learned page explicitly identifies cultural lessons including organizational silence, communication breakdown, and normalization of deviance, and links the CAIB report and later NASA case studies.
- **Reuse decision:** **Do not copy page artwork, mission patches, embedded photographs, or logos in this pass.** The source is used for factual orientation and source discovery only.

## Source 3 — NIST SP 800-34 Rev. 1, Contingency Planning Guide for Federal Information Systems

- **Institution:** National Institute of Standards and Technology.
- **Publication date:** 2010; NIST page updated February 19, 2025.
- **URL:** https://www.nist.gov/publications/contingency-planning-guide-federal-information-systems
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.SP.800-34r1
- **Authority:** U.S. federal technical guidance.
- **Relevant point:** Contingency planning treats alternate processing, recovery priorities, testing/training/exercises, and interim operational measures as part of continuity—not merely possession of backups. Earlier NIST guidance also explicitly described manual methods as one possible interim recovery mechanism.
- **Rights posture:** Use factual concepts and citations. No NIST graphics are reproduced in this pass. If a future facsimile is desired, verify the specific page and any incorporated third-party material before reuse.
- **Use boundary:** NIST guidance concerns federal information-system contingency planning, not “cognitive sovereignty” as such. The chapter can borrow the continuity logic without presenting NIST as endorsing the book's terminology.

## New original asset

### `media/ch13-memory-needs-a-route.svg`

**Title:** *Memory needs a route back to the record*

**Purpose:** Show three layers—durable records, retrieval/interpretation, and operational action—plus an independent fallback route. The figure clarifies the chapter's core architecture: AI may be the preferred interface without becoming the sole practical path to sources or degraded operation.

**Rights:** Original project artwork created for this manuscript. No third-party artwork, logos, icons, screenshots, or distinctive source graphics reproduced or adapted.

**Caption:**

> **Memory needs a route back to the record.** An AI interface can make an archive dramatically more useful, but institutional memory becomes brittle when retrieval, interpretation, and operation depend on a single convenience layer. The durable record layer should remain independently accessible, and critical functions should have a tested degraded mode. Conceptual figure; not a quantitative reliability model.

**Credit line:** Original diagram by Sven Hardy Benson / project artwork.

**Alt text:** Grayscale layered diagram. At the bottom is a durable record layer containing primary records, versions, provenance, portable formats, and independently accessible sources. Above it is a retrieval and interpretation layer containing search, AI summaries, indexes, taxonomies, explanations, and decision support. At the top is operational action: decisions, controls, services, and explanations. A dashed independent route bypasses the middle layer and connects records to action through source access, tested fallback, and human know-how. A side box asks whether the institution can find the source, explain the rule, operate in degraded mode, migrate providers, and reproduce history if the main interface disappears.

## Rejected / deferred media

### NASA Columbia photography and mission imagery

Rights are often permissive for NASA-produced material, but images can include mission patches, identifiable people, contractor material, or other elements requiring item-level checking. No photograph adds enough explanatory value here to justify those complications. The chapter needs an architecture diagram, not tragedy as decoration.

### CAIB report cover/facsimile

The report itself is rights-clean on NTRS and a facsimile could be used later. Deferred because a cover image would be mostly documentary decoration. A specific interior page should be selected only if its actual content advances the argument and survives grayscale reproduction.

## Evidence distinctions to preserve

1. **Verified historical finding:** CAIB identified organizational as well as physical causes in the Columbia accident and recommended changes in technical authority, safety assurance, and organizational learning.
2. **Verified continuity practice:** NIST contingency guidance treats testing, alternate capability, recovery priorities, and interim procedures as part of operational resilience.
3. **Book argument:** AI-mediated archives should preserve provenance, independent source access, provider portability, and degraded operating capability.
4. **Speculation / forward risk:** A future institution may become unable to interpret its own rules without an AI vendor. Treat this as a scenario, not an observed general law.
