# Light Without Object

## Working subtitle
Ball Lightning, Hessdalen, and the Mistake of Giving Every Luminous Anomaly a Vehicle

## One-sentence thesis
Some of the most stubborn aerial anomalies may be neither misidentified ordinary objects nor extraordinary craft but transient physical systems—plasma, combustion, dusty aerosols, electrical structures, or other self-organized luminous matter—and the existence of several experimentally demonstrated routes to object-like light makes “what vehicle was it?” the wrong first question.

## Governing discipline
This book does **not** claim that Hessdalen is ball lightning, that plasma explains UAP generally, or that any laboratory fireball reproduces a specific field observation. It asks what happens to anomaly classification when nature can manufacture bounded, moving, luminous phenomena without manufacturing vehicles.

## Evidence classes
1. Verified/documented: instrumented Hessdalen observing campaigns; peer-reviewed spectroscopic observation of a lightning-associated luminous ball; laboratory production of stable atmospheric-pressure luminous fireballs; laboratory silicon-arc luminous bodies.
2. Credible but incomplete: particular Hessdalen optical/radar/spectral correlations and proposed geophysical mechanisms that require source-by-source reconstruction.
3. Disputed/unconfirmed: claims that a single plasma model explains Hessdalen, ball lightning, or named UAP cases; extraordinary interpretations of object-like lights without independent physical evidence.
4. Speculative synthesis: a “light without object” category should sit explicitly inside anomaly taxonomies between conventional misidentification and engineered craft.

## Publication gate
Do not publish until the 1984 Hessdalen report and later instrument papers are reconstructed at observation level; known conventional identifications are separated from residual events; every mechanism is tied to an energy budget; and no laboratory analogue is promoted into identity by resemblance alone.
