# Angles At Breakfast, Demons in the Briefing Room

**Sven Hardy Benson**

---

There is an easy version of this story.

It has a secret Christian network at breakfast, an astrologer on the telephone, UFOs over California, intelligence officers carrying folders into rooms without windows, and a president talking at the United Nations about an alien threat from outside this world. Put those facts close enough together and the reader's mind will do the rest.

That is exactly why they have to be pulled apart.

Ronald Reagan did speak warmly in 1985 about a private fellowship that reached across all three branches of the federal government and into foreign capitals. Nancy Reagan did consult astrologer Joan Quigley, though the extent of Quigley's influence became a fight among participants and memoirists. Reagan did publicly imagine an extraterrestrial threat as a device for thinking about human unity. Later stories also attribute UFO sightings, private conversations, extraordinary briefings, and theological interpretations of anomalous phenomena to people around the Reagan-era national-security world.

The archive does not give all of those claims the same weight.

This book is about the distance between them.

A presidency is a machine for receiving advice. Some advice arrives as an intelligence estimate. Some comes from a spouse. Some comes from a friend who never appears on an organization chart. Some comes from clergy, donors, political allies, diplomats, physicians, pollsters, military officers, or people whose authority rests on a private conviction that cannot be audited in the usual way. The presidency does not make those inputs equivalent merely by receiving them. But it does make their routes consequential.

That is the route I want to follow.

Not because an astrologer proves an occult presidency.

Not because a prayer group proves a theocracy.

Not because a speech about aliens proves a briefing about aliens.

And not because a classified file proves that the exciting version of the story is hidden inside it.

The more interesting possibility is that power is always more porous than its organizational chart. Belief and bureaucracy do not occupy separate buildings. They share elevators.

That is where we begin.
