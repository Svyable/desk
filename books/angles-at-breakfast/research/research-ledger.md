# Research ledger — Angles At Breakfast, Demons in the Briefing Room

## Governing question

How did public religion, private spiritual networks, astrological counsel, anomalous-experience claims, intelligence secrecy, and extraterrestrial or demonic interpretations actually enter the Reagan-era presidential environment, and what evidence exists for influence rather than mere proximity?

## Four evidence classes

### Class 1 — documented / contemporaneous / archival

1. Ronald Reagan's January 31, 1985 National Prayer Breakfast remarks explicitly described an informal fellowship, said fellowships existed in all three branches of the federal government and abroad, and said the work was effective precisely because it was private.
2. Reagan publicly used an extraterrestrial-threat hypothetical in his September 21, 1987 address to the United Nations General Assembly and then tied the image back to war and the threat of war.
3. The Hoover Institution holds the papers of Joan Quigley and identifies her as Nancy Reagan's White House astrologer. Donald Regan and Nancy Reagan offered sharply different retrospective accounts of the extent of her influence.
4. Wheaton College archives hold extensive Fellowship Foundation collections, including National Prayer Breakfast material, core-meeting files, correspondence, and international-work records.
5. Douglas Coe was a longtime leader associated with the Fellowship Foundation and the National Prayer Breakfast; the precise Reagan-era relationship network must be reconstructed from archival records rather than inferred from Coe's later reputation.

### Class 2 — credible but incomplete

1. Astrology affected at least some presidential scheduling, but the frequency, mechanism, and policy consequence require event-level reconstruction rather than reliance on memoir generalizations.
2. Reagan's repeated alien-threat rhetoric may have had private conversational antecedents, but public rhetoric alone cannot establish the content of classified briefings.
3. Later witness accounts of Reagan UFO sightings may preserve genuine recollection, but each claim needs earliest-source dating, witness provenance, and mundane astronomical/aviation controls.
4. Fellowship relationships may have supplied informal channels among officials and foreign leaders. Informal access is not automatically policy influence.

### Class 3 — disputed / unconfirmed

1. That Reagan received a verified briefing establishing extraterrestrial visitation.
2. That Douglas Coe or the Fellowship directed Reagan administration policy through a hidden command structure.
3. That Joan Quigley determined substantive national-security or diplomatic policy rather than influencing timing or scheduling.
4. That Reagan's public alien-threat remarks encoded classified knowledge.
5. That a Reagan-era group concluded UFO/UAP phenomena were demonic and materially changed national policy on that basis.
6. Claims associated with a so-called Collins Elite unless and until meeting, membership, document, and provenance chains can be independently reconstructed.

### Class 4 — speculative synthesis

1. The Reagan presidency is a useful case for studying competing authorities of knowledge because faith, astrology, intelligence analysis, witness testimony, and secrecy occupied adjacent social space without sharing an evidentiary standard.
2. Private relationship networks can influence access and framing even when they leave little conventional bureaucratic paper.
3. Secrecy amplifies retrospective myth by making absence compatible with mutually contradictory stories.

## Contradiction ledger

### Private fellowship vs secret command structure

Reagan's own speech establishes that he valued privacy in the fellowship and described its spread through government. It does not establish a chain of command, membership list, or policy-control mechanism. The manuscript must reconstruct ties individually.

### Astrological scheduling vs astrological policymaking

Memoirs disagree about Quigley's reach. Build a dated table of claimed recommendations and compare against presidential schedules, travel changes, decision records, and independent explanations. Do not convert one verified scheduling intervention into universal control.

### Alien metaphor vs alien intelligence

Reagan's UN wording is public and contextualized as an argument about human unity and war. Any claim that it reflects classified extraterrestrial information needs an independent document or attributable witness chain.

### Theology vs anomaly classification

A Christian official may interpret an anomalous claim through a theological worldview. That does not establish either the anomaly or a government-wide theological policy. Track who used which language, in what document, and when.

## Strongest conventional account

Nothing hidden needs to unify the material. Reagan's faith and Fellowship contacts belong to ordinary elite religious networking; Nancy Reagan's astrology was a private coping and scheduling practice whose influence was later sensationalized; UFO stories are retrospective folklore around a president comfortable with science-fiction imagery; alien-threat rhetoric was a Cold War metaphor. Secrecy merely made unrelated facts easier to braid together later.

This account gains strength if event-level records show no causal bridge among the religious, astrological, intelligence, and anomalous strands.

## Strongest network-influence account

Informal spiritual relationships, spouse-mediated astrological advice, unconventional intelligence interests, and private conversations may have shaped access, timing, framing, and interpretation in ways poorly captured by formal presidential records. Influence could therefore be real without constituting a unified conspiracy or a single occult doctrine.

This account gains strength only through specific causal episodes: identifiable adviser, identifiable communication, identifiable decision or framing change, and evidence close in time to the event.

## Publication holds

- Do not write that Coe “controlled,” “ran,” or secretly directed Reagan without primary evidence of a specific intervention.
- Do not call the Fellowship a cult as authorial fact.
- Do not use Reagan's alien-threat rhetoric as proof of a UFO briefing.
- Do not say Quigley dictated policy unless a policy-specific causal chain survives cross-checking.
- Do not present a later UFO witness account as contemporaneous.
- Do not present Collins Elite literature as a verified government program.
- Do not use Christian belief as a proxy for belief in demons, or belief in demons as evidence of a particular anomaly.
- Separate Ronald Reagan from Donald Regan carefully in every note and citation.
