# Chronology

- **1956–1957** — U.S. officials debate faster ways to obtain broad reconnaissance coverage; early satellite-reconnaissance concepts develop inside wider Air Force programs.
- **7 February 1958** — President Eisenhower approves the CORONA effort as a crash program.
- **1958–1960** — Repeated launch, camera, spacecraft, and recovery failures.
- **11 August 1960** — Discoverer 13 recovery vehicle is recovered after returning from space; no reconnaissance film aboard.
- **18 August 1960** — Discoverer 14 launches with operational camera and film.
- **19 August 1960** — Discoverer 14 film capsule is recovered in midair; first fully successful CORONA reconnaissance mission.
- **1960–1972** — CORONA evolves through multiple camera generations and repeated missions, producing extensive photographic coverage.
- **1960s–1970s** — Frames later used by archaeologists capture Near Eastern and other landscapes before many subsequent land-use changes.
- **25 May 1972** — Final CORONA mission launches.
- **1971–1984** — KH-9 HEXAGON operates, creating another major historical imagery archive later useful for archaeology and environmental research.
- **1972** — Landsat begins satellite operations, providing a civilian Earth-observation record but generally at coarser spatial resolution than CORONA for many archaeological features.
- **1990–1992** — U.S. reviews consider scientific/environmental value and declassification of early reconnaissance imagery.
- **23–24 February 1995** — Executive action declassifies early CORONA/ARGON/LANYARD imagery and directs archival/public-access arrangements.
- **Late 1990s–2000s** — Archaeologists increasingly use CORONA for ancient landscapes, road systems, settlement prospection, and pre-development baselines.
- **2002** — Additional declassified KH imagery becomes publicly available under a second major release.
- **2003** — Jason Ur publishes CORONA-based work on ancient road networks in northern Mesopotamia.
- **2004** — Published work demonstrates CORONA applications at al-Raqqa.
- **2006** — Satellite-image prospection work in central Syria further develops archaeological use cases.
- **2012** — Casana, Cothren, and Kalayci publish a major methodological synthesis, including improved orthorectification and the CORONA Atlas effort.
- **2011–2019** — Large portions of KH-9 HEXAGON imagery are declassified, transferred, processed, and progressively made easier to access digitally.
- **2020** — Archaeological literature addresses global-scale CORONA prospection and automated/crowdsourced/expert approaches.
- **2022** — Hammer, FitzPatrick, and Ur publish a practical archaeological treatment of declassified HEXAGON imagery.
- **2020s** — Declassified satellite archives are increasingly treated as long-term historical Earth-observation datasets rather than merely intelligence-history artifacts.

## Chronology cautions

Dates above refer to program milestones, declassification, and publication. Public availability often lagged formal declassification because film had to be transferred, catalogued, scanned, indexed, and served. The expansion pass should distinguish formal release date from practical digital accessibility.
