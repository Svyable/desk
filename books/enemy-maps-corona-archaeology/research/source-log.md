# Source Log

## Program history and public archive

- National Reconnaissance Office, **“CORONA: America’s Eyes in Space.”** Official program history. Covers approval in February 1958, early failures, Discoverer 14, film return, mission counts, resolution progression, and the program’s role in constraining strategic estimates. https://www.nro.gov/About-NRO/history/history-corona/
- National Reconnaissance Office, **A History of Satellite Reconnaissance, Volume I — CORONA.** Declassified official history, available through the NRO declassified-programs portal. https://www.nro.gov/foia-home/foia-declassified-nro-programs-and-projects/
- National Reconnaissance Office, **CORONA Star Catchers.** Oral histories with recovery crews; useful for film-return logistics and the human recovery system. https://www.nro.gov/Portals/135/documents/history/csnr/corona/StarCatchersWeb.pdf
- U.S. Geological Survey, **Declassified Intelligence Satellite Photographs**, Fact Sheet 2008-3054. Public archive scale, declassification dates, NARA/USGS custody, and scientific value. https://doi.org/10.3133/fs20083054
- U.S. Geological Survey EROS, **Declass 1: CORONA, LANYARD & ARGON Missions.** Current public dataset documentation and metadata caveats. https://doi.org/10.5066/F78P5XZM
- U.S. Geological Survey, **U.S. Department of Defense and Intelligence Community—100 years of mapping and remote sensing collaboration, 1879–1979**, Circular 1552. Especially useful for declassification history and later scientific applications. https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/cir1552/full
- U.S. Geological Survey EROS, **Declassified Satellite Imagery 2** (KH-7 / KH-9) and **Declassified Satellite Imagery 3** (KH-9 HEXAGON). Public archive documentation and later declassification. https://www.usgs.gov/centers/eros/science/usgs-eros-archive-declassified-data-declassified-satellite-imagery-2 and https://www.usgs.gov/centers/eros/science/usgs-eros-archive-declassified-data-declassified-satellite-imagery-3

## Archaeological applications

- Jesse Casana, Jackson Cothren & Tuna Kalayci (2012), **“Swords into Ploughshares: Archaeological Applications of CORONA Satellite Imagery in the Near East,” Internet Archaeology 32.** DOI: https://doi.org/10.11141/ia.32.2. Core methodological and historiographic source: declassification, panoramic distortion, orthorectification, development-driven loss, and Near Eastern case studies.
- Jason A. Ur (2003), **“CORONA Satellite Photography and Ancient Road Networks: A Northern Mesopotamian Case Study,” Antiquity 77(295).** Core source for hollow ways / regional movement networks.
- Jason A. Ur, Harvard project pages on **CORONA Photography**, **Hollow Ways**, and **Assyrian Canals**. Useful public summaries tied to published research and mapped examples. https://jasonur.scholars.harvard.edu/pages/corona-photography-1 ; https://jasonur.scholars.harvard.edu/pages/hollow-ways-1 ; https://jasonur.scholars.harvard.edu/pages/hollow-ways-0
- Keith Challis et al., **“Corona Remotely-Sensed Imagery in Dryland Archaeology: The Islamic City of al-Raqqa, Syria,” Journal of Field Archaeology 29.** DOI: https://doi.org/10.1179/jfa.2004.29.1-2.139
- Keith N. Wilkinson et al. (2006), **“Satellite imagery as a resource in the prospection for archaeological sites in central Syria,” Geoarchaeology 21(7).** DOI: https://doi.org/10.1002/gea.20136
- Jesse Casana & Jackson Cothren, **“Stereo analysis, DEM extraction and orthorectification of CORONA satellite imagery: archaeological applications from the Near East,” Antiquity.** Technical source on stereo pairs, DEM extraction, and geometric correction.
- Jesse Casana (2020), **“Global-Scale Archaeological Prospection using CORONA Satellite Imagery: Automated, Crowd-Sourced, and Expert-led Approaches,” Journal of Field Archaeology.** DOI: https://doi.org/10.1080/00934690.2020.1713285. Important for global-scale opportunities and false-positive / workflow limits.
- Emily Hammer, Mackinley FitzPatrick & Jason Ur (2022), **“Succeeding CORONA: declassified HEXAGON intelligence imagery for archaeological and historical research,” Antiquity 96(387), 679–695.** Technical and case-study bridge into KH-9. https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/succeeding-corona-declassified-hexagon-intelligence-imagery-for-archaeological-and-historical-research/A4FD28FA08B98A32D91AEDAB6D878B8E
- National Endowment for the Humanities, **“Preserving the Cultural Heritage of the Ancient Near East and the Mediterranean World.”** Documents the CORONA Atlas project and its use of approximately 4,200 declassified images. https://www.neh.gov/divisions/preservation/featured-project/preserving-the-cultural-heritage-the-ancient-near-east-and-t

## Claim boundaries to preserve

- Declassification proves public release, not completeness of surviving coverage.
- CORONA’s military effectiveness does not certify archaeological interpretations.
- A visible feature may be archaeological, natural, modern, or an image artifact until tested.
- “Nearly global” imagery is not an unbiased archaeological sample; strategic priorities, weather, mission success, season, and film limits affected observation.
- Old imagery preserves a twentieth-century landscape condition, not an untouched ancient landscape.
- Patents are not needed for the current thesis and should not be used as evidence of system performance.
- No claim in this project requires non-public classified information.
