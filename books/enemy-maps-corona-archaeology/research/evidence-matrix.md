## Four-class rule

1. **Verified/declassified/documented:** can be stated directly with source.
2. **Credible but incomplete/model-dependent:** state with qualifier and identify dependency.
3. **Disputed/unconfirmed:** attribute; do not convert to narrative fact.
4. **Speculative synthesis/open conjecture:** explicitly mark as the book’s argument or a research question.

## Defeat conditions

The book’s stronger thesis should be narrowed if any of the following is established during expansion:

- archaeological applications turn out to depend overwhelmingly on already-public aerial photography rather than declassified satellite archives;
- Cold War collection priorities have negligible effect on the geographic/temporal availability used by archaeological studies;
- paired case studies fail to show meaningful archaeological information preserved in older declassified imagery after later landscape alteration;
- the public-access/declassification chronology is materially different from the one summarized here;
- the strongest case studies do not survive independent field or multi-source validation.
