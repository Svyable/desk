# THE HIT RATE — evidence ledger and research trail

Working research notes. These are not manuscript claims merely because they appear here.

## Thesis

The neglected collision is between two propositions that are often treated as mutually exclusive: controlled remote-viewing research may contain a small above-chance statistical residue, while the same phenomenon may still be too poorly calibrated to function as intelligence. The book tests the conversion chain from experimental effect → session-level information → analyst interpretation → decision improvement.

## Evidence class 1 — verified / declassified / documented

1. CIA publicly acknowledges U.S. government research into remote viewing and states that the work moved between CIA and DIA before an independent evaluation in the mid-1990s. CIA says the evaluation found accurate experiences sufficient to challenge randomness but judged the phenomenon too unreliable, inconsistent and sporadic for intelligence use. Source: CIA, “Ask Molly: Did CIA Really Study Psychic Powers?” https://www.cia.gov/stories/story/ask-molly-did-cia-really-study-psychic-powers/

2. CIA FOIA STARGATE records contain operationally framed remote-viewing sessions tied to the Tehran hostage crisis. Example: “SUMMARY REMOTE VIEWING SESSION CCC12,” dated 5 March 1980, CIA-RDP96-00788R000800760001-1. The report explicitly describes the impressions as raw intelligence data and assigns interpretation/use to the requester. https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp96-00788r000800760001-1

3. A separate Tehran-related report, session CC70, contains the same raw-intelligence warning and identifies the request as coming from SOD/J3/OJCS at the Pentagon. CIA-RDP96-00788R000800580001-1. https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp96-00788r000800580001-1

4. The archive preserves explicit failures. Session LIII states that the target images appeared to show “absolutely no target correlation” and records protocol differences. CIA-RDP96-00788R000900390001-1. https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp96-00788r000900390001-1

5. Tressoldi & Katz (2023), Journal of Scientific Exploration 37(3), report a systematic review/meta-analysis of remote-viewing studies through 2022: 36 studies, 40 effect sizes, with an average effect they interpret as above chance. This is evidence of a published meta-analytic claim, not independent validation of psi. DOI: https://doi.org/10.31275/20232931

6. The wider free-response/ganzfeld literature contains both positive meta-analytic claims and significant replication/methodological dispute. Storm, Tressoldi & Di Risio (2010) reported positive mean effects for selected free-response datasets. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1037/a0019457. Milton & Wiseman (1999) is a necessary counter-source on failure to replicate an earlier ganzfeld effect: DOI https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.125.4.387

## Evidence class 2 — credible but incomplete

- Participant and evaluator recollections of operational successes may identify cases worth reconstructing, but must be checked against contemporaneous tasking, complete transcripts, all sessions/viewers, analyst notes and ground truth.
- Official retrospective summaries compress changing project names, sponsors, protocols and contractors. Administrative continuity must be reconstructed from primary records rather than assumed from the label STARGATE.
- Claims that particular sessions influenced searches or collection decisions require downstream decision records; a striking transcript alone does not establish operational utility.

## Evidence class 3 — disputed / unconfirmed

- Claims that remote viewers reliably located hostages, submarines, aircraft, people or facilities at operationally useful rates.
- Claims that Soviet/Russian programs demonstrated an operational psychotronic capability beyond U.S. work.
- Claims that the public termination of STARGATE was a cover for a continuing classified psychic-intelligence program.
- Claims that remote viewing has a demonstrated quantum, electromagnetic, consciousness-field or nonlocal physical mechanism.
- Claims that classification or redaction itself validates spectacular outcomes.

These may be investigated as claims and histories of belief. They are not manuscript facts without independent support.

## Evidence class 4 — speculative synthesis / open conjecture

### Statistical-operational gap
A measurable aggregate anomaly may fail to produce calibrated session-level information. This is the core thesis and must survive comparison with ordinary weak-signal intelligence systems.

### Semantic amplification
Free-form language may amplify subjective confidence because many descriptors permit multiple plausible mappings after target revelation. Test by comparing fixed pre-reveal scoring with retrospective narrative scoring.

### Analyst-transfer problem
If interpretation is delegated downstream, analyst knowledge can become part of the effective sensor. Apparent performance may therefore belong to a human-plus-context system rather than the viewer alone.

### Hit-rate ambiguity
Different denominators—descriptors, sessions, viewers, targets, requests, operational actions—can produce incompatible “success rates.” The book should refuse any rate that does not name its denominator.

## Contradiction and incentive audit

- Intelligence sponsors had incentives both to explore adversary surprise and to terminate low-yield programs.
- Contractors and researchers could have career, funding and reputational incentives to interpret ambiguous results favorably; skeptics and agencies could have reputational incentives to minimize embarrassing work. Incentives are not proof of distortion.
- Classification can remove context needed to audit success, but it also encourages outsiders to fill gaps with dramatic assumptions.
- Retrospective celebrity cases face survivorship bias: memorable successes are easier to transmit than routine failures.
- Operational users face asymmetric costs. A false lead can consume scarce collection resources even if the source is above chance in aggregate.

## Strongest countermodel

There is no anomalous information transfer. Positive results emerge from sensory leakage, cueing, target-set artifacts, flexible scoring, optional stopping, selective publication, participant selection, weak blinding, post hoc correspondence and ordinary sampling variation. Government continuation reflects Cold War uncertainty, bureaucratic momentum and fear of adversary advantage rather than demonstrated efficacy.

The book must give this model enough detail to win if the evidence supports it.

## Competing countermodel

A weak anomalous effect is real but behaves like a low-bandwidth, poorly calibrated sensor. Scientific detection and operational rejection are therefore both rational. Under this model, mechanism is unresolved and spectacular anecdotes are unnecessary.

## Defeat conditions for the book’s central synthesis

Demote the statistical-operational-gap thesis if:

- strong reanalysis shows the claimed laboratory effect disappears under adequately blinded, preregistered, independently replicated protocols; the simpler artifact model then dominates;
- operational archives establish stable prospective accuracy with clear denominators and documented decision value, making “operationally unusable” too broad;
- the 1995 evaluation is found to have been materially mischaracterized in the public CIA summary;
- later high-quality work supplies calibrated session-level probabilities and robust prospective performance that bridge the conversion gap.

## Quantitative publication burden

Before later chapters state rates, extract or calculate where possible:

- total trials and exclusions;
- expected chance rate under the actual target-selection/scoring design;
- effect size and uncertainty, not p-value alone;
- between-viewer and within-viewer variance;
- target-set size and target similarity;
- judge count, blinding and inter-rater agreement;
- complete operational request denominator;
- number of sessions/viewers per request;
- fraction producing any analyst-rated useful information;
- fraction causing action;
- false-lead burden and resource cost;
- prospective versus retrospective scoring;
- conventional intelligence already available at scoring time.

## Source-use rule

A patent proves filing, not efficacy. A classified marking proves security handling, not truth. A declassified report proves the report existed, not that its subject succeeded. Testimony proves that a person made a claim. A meta-analysis proves that a specified body of published results aggregates in a specified way under its model. Each evidentiary object stays inside its lane.
