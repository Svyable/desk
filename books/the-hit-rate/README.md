## Reader promise

This book does not ask the reader to believe in psychic spying. It asks a narrower and more uncomfortable question: what should we conclude when an experimental literature can produce above-chance statistics while an intelligence service still cannot turn the effect into dependable intelligence?

The central distinction is between **statistical anomaly** and **operational information**. A weak, unstable effect can be scientifically interesting and operationally useless at the same time. Conversely, operational failure does not by itself prove that every laboratory effect was imaginary. THE HIT RATE follows the gap between those statements through the declassified U.S. remote-viewing record, the methodological fights around it, and later meta-analytic claims.

The book keeps four evidence classes visible throughout: documented/declassified facts; credible but incomplete reporting or testimony; disputed allegations without independent confirmation; and speculative synthesis/open conjecture. Testimony is never converted into proof, and declassification is never treated as validation of the claims inside a document.
