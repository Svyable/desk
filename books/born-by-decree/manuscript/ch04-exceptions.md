# Chapter 4 — Exceptions

The exceptions in Decree 770 reveal the state's model of legitimate pregnancy avoidance. A termination could be authorized when pregnancy threatened life, when grave hereditary disease was present, when the woman had severe disability, when age crossed a threshold, when she already cared for enough children, or when pregnancy followed rape or incest.

These categories made reproductive permission conditional on facts legible to an institution. Age could be checked. Prior children could be counted. Diagnoses could be certified. A commission could decide whether danger was sufficient. The woman's desire not to continue a pregnancy was not itself an admissible category.

This is more than a moral distinction. It creates paperwork incentives. When access depends on diagnosis, diagnosis becomes a route around prohibition. When access depends on number of children, fertility history becomes an administrative credential. When physicians can be punished for unauthorized intervention, they face pressure to document defensively.

The thresholds also changed. In 1972, Decree 53 lowered the age exception from over forty-five to over forty. In 1985, Decree 411 reversed course, returning the age threshold to over forty-five and tightening the parity exception from four children to five. That movement is evidence against treating the system as static. Officials adjusted the gate in response to demographic and political priorities.

The 1972 relaxation is especially revealing. A coercive system can produce costs severe enough that it must reopen a narrow channel without abandoning the larger policy. The 1985 tightening shows the opposite movement: when birth targets disappoint again, the state can constrict the channel.

Exceptions are often presented as evidence that a restrictive law is humane. They can be. They are also sensors. Every exception requires someone to decide whether a person qualifies. In Romania, that decision became one of the places where demographics entered medicine as administration.
