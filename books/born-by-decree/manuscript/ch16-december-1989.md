# Chapter 16 — December 1989

Ceaușescu's regime collapsed in December 1989. On December 26, Decree-Law No. 1 removed the abortion restrictions.

The speed of repeal is itself evidence. A rule that had shaped reproductive medicine for twenty-three years disappeared almost immediately after the political system that sustained it.

Legal abortion then surged. In the early post-communist years Romania recorded extraordinarily high abortion rates as pent-up demand met newly lawful access and contraception remained underdeveloped. This was not proof that Romanian women had suddenly become indifferent to pregnancy. It was evidence that abortion had been serving as a primary fertility-control method before and after the ban, with legality changing its safety and visibility.

The maternal mortality response is even more important. Abortion-related deaths fell dramatically after legalization and improved contraceptive access. Because the change in law was abrupt, Romania offers unusually strong historical evidence about the health consequences of forcing abortion underground.

No natural experiment is perfect. The health system, reporting practices, economy and political environment also changed after 1989. Yet the size and specificity of the abortion-mortality decline make it difficult to sustain an account in which legal restriction played only a minor role.

The regime had spent decades trying to make births visible and abortions disappear. After repeal, abortions became statistically visible again—and deaths began to disappear instead.
