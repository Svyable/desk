# Chapter 8 — Adaptation

A fertility restriction does not operate on a passive population. People search.

Romanian women and families used whatever tools remained: abstinence, timing, withdrawal, scarce or illicit contraception, sympathetic doctors, bribery, false indications, traditional methods, improvised abortion and networks of people who knew who could help. The distribution of these options was unequal. Money, urban location, professional connections and medical knowledge could change the level of risk.

That inequality is one reason aggregate statistics can conceal the cruelty of a restriction. Two women facing the same law may face very different probabilities of surviving an unwanted pregnancy. One finds a physician willing to create a legal route. Another attempts a dangerous procedure alone.

As adaptation spread, fertility fell from its post-1966 peak even though the legal regime remained. The state therefore confronted evidence that prohibition had diminishing returns. Instead of treating that as information about reproductive preference, the regime repeatedly treated it as an enforcement problem.

The 1985 tightening is the clearest example. The age exception was raised again and the parity threshold moved from four children to five. The message was unmistakable: when birth outcomes disappointed, legal eligibility contracted.

Adaptation is sometimes described as resistance, but that word can romanticize what was often desperate pragmatism. Many people were not trying to make a political statement. They were trying to control the size and timing of their families under conditions where the lawful tools had been removed.

This matters beyond Romania. Policies that assume people will not adapt tend to mistake compliance costs for preference change. When the adaptation is hidden, officials can also underestimate how much coercion is required to maintain the policy.

Decree 770 did not fail because nobody obeyed it. It failed as a sustainable demographic technology because people kept wanting something the state had decided not to count as a legitimate reason.
