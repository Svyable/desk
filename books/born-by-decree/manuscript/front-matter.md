# Born by Decree

## Romania's Reproductive Police State

**Sven Hardy Benson**

Romania did not discover in 1966 that governments care about births. What changed was the administrative ambition. A demographic disappointment became a legal prohibition; a legal prohibition became a medical gate; the medical gate required records, committees, exceptions, suspicion and enforcement. The body was not merely regulated. It became a reporting surface for the state.

This book follows that machinery from the text of Decree 770 through the clinics and mortality tables it helped produce. It does not claim that every Romanian woman experienced the policy in the same way, that every doctor enforced it with equal zeal, or that every child abandoned to an institution was there because of one decree. It does make a harder, narrower argument: when a government tries to compel fertility while suppressing legal means of avoiding pregnancy, it must eventually police information as well as behavior. The more people adapt, the more intrusive the enforcement has to become.

The evidence is divided throughout into four classes: verified/documented; credible but incomplete; disputed or not established; and speculative synthesis. Romanian statutes, demographic and medical research, Romanian historical institutions and Romanian-language testimony carry the factual spine. Later recollection is used as witness, not as a substitute for records.
