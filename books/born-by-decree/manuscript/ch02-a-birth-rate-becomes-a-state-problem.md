# Chapter 2 — A Birth Rate Becomes a State Problem

The demographic premise behind Decree 770 was legible in the language of the state: abortion was described as harmful not only to women but to natality and natural population increase. That pairing converted a medical act into an economic and national variable. A private reproductive decision now had a measurable public cost.

Pronatalist governments rarely describe themselves as coercive at the beginning. They describe a shortage. Too few births. Too few future workers. Too much demographic aging. Too much individual choice relative to collective need. Once the problem is expressed as a target, the administrative question becomes how far the state may go to change the number.

Romania had legalized abortion broadly in 1957. By the mid-1960s abortion had become a major means of fertility regulation in a setting where modern contraception was limited. The 1966 reversal therefore did more than prohibit a procedure. It removed an established mechanism families were already using to keep births near desired levels.

That is why the immediate fertility response was so large. A behavior that had been available at scale was abruptly constrained. But the spike should not be confused with stable preference. Later Romanian and international analyses show fertility falling again after the initial shock even while restrictions remained. The population did not simply accept a permanently higher family size. People searched for substitutes.

This is the first general lesson of the Romanian record: policies that remove a widely used option can create dramatic short-run discontinuities without changing the underlying preferences that produced the behavior. If the preferences persist, adaptation follows.

For a state committed to the target, adaptation looks like sabotage. The policy therefore tends to deepen. Medical discretion becomes suspicious. Contraceptive access becomes a loophole. Miscarriage becomes potentially investigable. A woman arriving hemorrhaging at a hospital may be both a patient and a possible offender or witness.

The demographic target did not merely sit above medicine. It changed what medicine was for.
