# Contradictions and Incentives

1. **Birth counts vs preference.** The state could observe births but not directly observe desired family size. A short-run increase could therefore be mistaken for changed preference.
2. **Illegal abortion vs statistics.** Successful clandestine procedures evade official counts; complications are overrepresented in records.
3. **Medicine vs prosecution.** Clinicians needed truthful histories while patients and providers could face criminal exposure from the same information.
4. **Official safety rhetoric vs mortality.** The decree justified restriction partly in the name of women's health while unsafe-abortion mortality rose.
5. **Pronatalism vs austerity.** The regime demanded more births while household material conditions worsened in the 1980s.
6. **Institutional-care narrative.** Post-1989 orphanage horrors create incentives for monocausal retrospective explanation; reproductive policy, disability governance, poverty and institutional neglect must be separated.
7. **Memory incentives.** Survivors and clinicians may compress repeated experiences; official sources had strong incentives to minimize policy failure. Neither source class is self-validating.
