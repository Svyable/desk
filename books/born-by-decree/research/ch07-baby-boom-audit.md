# Chapter 7 — Baby-boom quantitative audit

## What the annual series actually shows

World Bank World Development Indicators, surfaced through FRED, records Romania's crude birth rate at 14.2 births per 1,000 people in 1966 and 27.0 in 1967. It remained 26.6 in 1968, then fell to 23.3 in 1969, 21.1 in 1970, 19.6 in 1971, 18.8 in 1972, and 18.2 in 1973.

The same source records total fertility at 1.90 births per woman in 1966, 3.66 in 1967, 3.63 in 1968, 3.19 in 1969, 2.89 in 1970, 2.67 in 1971, 2.55 in 1972, and 2.44 in 1973.

Sources:
- FRED / World Bank WDI, crude birth rate for Romania: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/SPDYNCBRTINROU
- FRED / World Bank WDI, total fertility rate for Romania: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/SPDYNTFRTINROU

## Timing boundary

Decree 770 was dated October 1, 1966. Article 8 states that it entered into force thirty days after publication. The 1967 annual figures are therefore the first full-calendar-year demographic series after the restriction took effect; they should not be described as though every 1967 birth was caused by the decree.

Primary law:
- Romanian Legislative Portal, Decretul nr. 770/1966: https://legislatie.just.ro/public/detaliidocument/177

## What the spike does and does not prove

The 1966–1968 discontinuity is large enough to support a strong immediate policy-effect claim. It does not, by itself, identify the composition of that response. A period total fertility rate is constructed from age-specific fertility rates in a given year; it is not completed family size for a real cohort. The spike cannot by itself partition births into prevented abortions, changed conception timing, marriage timing, births brought forward, or durable increases in lifetime family size.

The later decline under continuing restriction supports an adaptation argument, but not the claim that policy effects vanished. By 1973 total fertility had fallen to 2.44, still above the 1966 value of 1.90. Keil and Andreescu's 1967–1989 time-series analysis reports a significant positive effect of Ceaușescu-era pronatalist policy on overall fertility while also describing a continuing struggle between state policy and reproductive behavior.

Counterevidence / long-run source:
- Thomas J. Keil and Viviana Andreescu, `Fertility policy in Ceausescu's Romania`, *Journal of Family History* 24(4), 1999, DOI 10.1177/036319909902400405: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11623953/

## Claim boundary for the chapter

Safe: Decree 770 produced an extraordinary immediate rise in Romanian fertility and birth rates; those measures declined substantially within a few years despite continued restriction; the longer-run policy still affected fertility.

Too strong without further reconstruction: the 1967 spike consisted entirely of previously intended abortions; the later decline measures illegal abortion directly; adaptation fully neutralized the policy; the 1967 cohort's later institutional or labor-market experiences can be attributed to cohort size without separate evidence.
