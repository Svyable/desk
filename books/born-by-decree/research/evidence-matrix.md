# Four-Class Evidence Matrix

## Class 1 — Verified / documented
- Decree 770 issued October 1, 1966; prohibition with enumerated exceptions.
- Medical-commission authorization structure and prosecutor notification for emergency terminations.
- Decree 53 (1972) lowered the age exception from >45 to >40.
- Decree 411 (1985) restored >45 and raised parity exception from four to five children.
- Fertility/births rose sharply immediately after the decree, then declined while restrictions remained.
- Abortion-related maternal mortality became the dominant component of Romanian maternal mortality by the late 1980s and fell sharply after legalization.
- Decree 770 was repealed immediately after the December 1989 revolution.

## Class 2 — Credible but incomplete
- Detailed bedside/police questioning practices described by clinicians and patients.
- Informal provider networks, bribery and diagnostic workarounds whose prevalence is difficult to quantify.
- Household-level reasons for individual births or abortions.
- Contribution of pronatalist policy to individual child abandonment decisions.

## Class 3 — Disputed / not established
- Exact cumulative number of women killed by the policy when presented without a reproducible mortality series.
- Claims that every Romanian woman underwent monthly gynecological examination in every period and locality.
- Claims that doctors universally withheld emergency treatment until police arrived.
- Claims that Decree 770 alone caused the post-1989 orphanage catastrophe.

## Class 4 — Speculative synthesis
- Coercive pronatalism as an information state: adaptation creates blind spots that induce surveillance escalation.
- The 1967 birth spike as a policy-success trap that encouraged officials to confuse short-run shock with durable preference change.
- Medical distrust as an endogenous cost of turning clinical facts into potential criminal evidence.
