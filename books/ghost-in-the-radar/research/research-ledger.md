# Research ledger — Ghost in the Radar

## Governing thesis

Sensor evidence is not raw reality. Radar, infrared, electro-optical and fused tactical systems transform physical inputs into object-like propositions. Cold War radar-deception programs demonstrate that this transformation can be attacked deliberately. The book uses that documented capability as a control for modern anomaly epistemology: before extraordinary performance is assigned to an object, the complete measurement → processing → geometry → association → display → operator → fusion chain must earn the object and its kinematics.

The thesis is **not** that modern UAP cases are PALLADIUM or electronic warfare. That attribution requires case-specific evidence.

## Evidence classes

### Class 1 — verified / declassified / documented

- CIA OXCART/stealth history and public institutional records establishing the Cold War intelligence problem of Soviet radar detection.
- Official AARO case imagery and case-resolution reports demonstrating several distinct endpoints: physical object established but identity unresolved; insufficient data to distinguish physical signature from artifact; mundane resolution after geometric reconstruction; bird/balloon resolutions.
- Standard radar/electronic-warfare technical literature on false targets, jamming, deception, track formation and sensor fusion, to be reconstructed from primary manuals/papers.

### Class 2 — credible but incomplete

- Gene Poteat's attributed public accounts of Project PALLADIUM, including false-target injection, adversary-response monitoring and Cuban operations. He is a technically relevant firsthand source, but the project needs document-level reconstruction before every operational detail is promoted to Class 1.
- Other named CIA/NSA/OXCART participant recollections where provenance and role can be established.

### Class 3 — disputed / unconfirmed

- Claims that PALLADIUM or a descendant system explains the 2004 Nimitz encounters or later Navy UAP cases.
- Claims that specific modern cases were U.S. or foreign electronic-warfare tests without direct documentary evidence.
- Claims of secret sensor-deception capabilities substantially beyond publicly demonstrated EW principles.
- Claims that unresolved UAP necessarily represent non-human technology.

### Class 4 — synthesis / open conjecture

- **Adversarial phenomenology:** anomaly analysis should model an intelligent opponent capable of manipulating the observation chain, not merely passive noise and natural confounders.
- **Corroboration debt:** multiple sensors add evidentiary weight only to the extent that their error/deception channels are independent or explicitly modeled.
- **Object ladder:** detection → track → physical object → reconstructed kinematics → conventional-category exclusion → extraordinary performance → origin. No rung inherits the confidence of the previous rung automatically.

## Contradictions and incentives to track

- Intelligence services benefit from concealing EW capabilities and may also benefit when outsiders misattribute tests to exotic phenomena.
- Witnesses can be accurate about what a display showed while being unable to establish what physical process produced the display.
- Official resolution offices have institutional incentives toward closure and may lack original high-quality data; official conclusions therefore require methods, not deference.
- UAP advocacy has incentives to treat `unresolved` as positive evidence of extraordinariness.
- Skeptical commentary has incentives to elevate a merely possible mundane explanation into a solved case.
- Classification creates asymmetric evidence: absence of public EW detail is not evidence of exotic technology, but neither can hypothetical classified EW be used as an unfalsifiable explanation.

## Quantitative burden

For every major sensor case, extract where available:

- sensor type/model and operating band;
- platform position, attitude and motion;
- target line of sight and angular rate;
- range source: measured, inferred, assumed or unavailable;
- frame rate / scan rate;
- track initiation and association logic;
- whether raw returns or only processed display/video survive;
- cross-cueing between sensors;
- independent observers and communication paths;
- environmental propagation conditions;
- claimed speed/acceleration and uncertainty interval;
- which variables must be assumed to obtain extraordinary kinematics;
- known spoof/artifact channels for that sensor;
- what an adversary would need to know/control to reproduce the observation.

## Strongest conventional countermodel

Most apparently anomalous military observations need no coordinated deception. Sparse range information, parallax, glare, track-file interpretation, balloons, birds, drones, atmospheric propagation, ordinary aircraft and retrospective narrative compression may explain the bulk of cases. PALLADIUM could be historically fascinating yet contribute little to modern UAP resolution.

This model must be allowed to win.

## Strongest extraordinary countermodel

Some cases may contain genuinely independent multimodal measurements with calibrated range and kinematics that survive artifact, geometry, platform-motion, mundane-object and plausible deception analyses. If so, sensor skepticism should increase confidence in the residual rather than function as permanent doubt.

This model must also be allowed to win.

## Defeat conditions

The book's distinctive `adversarial phenomenology` claim weakens substantially if:

1. document-level PALLADIUM reconstruction fails to establish meaningful false-target capability;
2. modern operational sensors demonstrably authenticate physical targets and kinematics in ways that make correlated deception/artifact channels negligible for the cases discussed;
3. deception adds no explanatory or methodological value beyond ordinary sensor-error analysis;
4. the manuscript cannot identify a concrete evidentiary test that distinguishes deliberate manipulation from passive error.

## Publication holds

Do not state until sourced at primary-record level:

- exact PALLADIUM dates, locations, hardware or target parameters;
- that Cuban fighters chased a PALLADIUM ghost;
- exact CIA/NSA organizational roles in individual operations;
- that PALLADIUM directly influenced any named modern EW system;
- that any named UAP incident was spoofed;
- that a sensor was `corroborated` without mapping independence/cross-cueing;
- that an unresolved AARO case demonstrated anomalous performance unless the official technical record actually says so.
