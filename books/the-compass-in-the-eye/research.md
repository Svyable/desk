# Research ledger — The Compass in the Eye

## Working rule

Do not use “quantum compass” as a conclusion. Radical-pair spin chemistry is quantum mechanics; demonstrating magnetic sensitivity in a purified protein is not the same experiment as demonstrating that an intact bird reads that protein as direction. Every chapter must keep molecule, cell, circuit, behavior and navigation separate until a source actually connects them.

## Class 1 — verified / documented

- Xu et al., *Nature* 594 (2021), “Magnetic sensitivity of cryptochrome 4 from a migratory songbird.” European-robin CRY4 produced magnetically sensitive photochemistry in vitro; mutations implicated a flavin–tryptophan electron-transfer chain. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-03618-9
- Treiber et al., *Nature* 484 (2012), “Clusters of iron-rich cells in the upper beak of pigeons are macrophages not magnetosensitive neurons.” A major anatomical candidate was overturned by systematic histology and mapping. https://www.nature.com/articles/nature11046
- Wiltschko, Nießner & Wiltschko, *Frontiers in Physiology* 12 (2021), review of the avian magnetic compass, including inclination rather than polarity behavior, functional-window behavior and wavelength dependence. https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2021.667000
- Hore & Mouritsen, *Annual Review of Biophysics* 45 (2016), radical-pair magnetoreception review and mechanistic constraints. https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-biophys-032116-094545

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete

- Kobylkov et al., *Journal of Comparative Physiology A* (2022), broadband 75–85 MHz fields disrupting magnetic compass orientation in night-migratory songbirds, interpreted as consistent with a flavin radical-pair receptor. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00359-021-01537-8
- The RF literature is unusually diagnostic because weak oscillating fields can, under some radical-pair models, perturb spin dynamics. But experimental geometry, shielding, local electromagnetic noise, field amplitude and replication history matter. Treat each frequency claim as protocol-specific.
- CRY4 is a serious molecular candidate, not a solved receptor. The 2021 Nature result is in vitro. The paper itself establishes magnetic sensitivity of the molecule, not an intact receptor-to-brain pathway.
- Avian trigeminal pathways carry magnetic information in some experiments. That does not automatically establish magnetite as the transducer, nor does it mean the retinal compass is false. Multiple magnetic channels remain plausible.

## Class 3 — disputed / unconfirmed

- “CRY4 is the bird compass.” Too strong. Competing cryptochrome candidates and photochemical objections remain.
- “Iron particles in the pigeon beak are the magnetic receptor.” The famous six-locus receptor anatomy was badly damaged by Treiber et al.; do not resurrect it through secondary summaries.
- “Radio towers scramble migration.” Behavioral RF effects in controlled settings do not establish population-scale ecological harm without exposure and field evidence.
- “Quantum entanglement lets birds see magnetic fields.” Popular shorthand outruns the demonstrated biology. Spin-correlated radical pairs and field-sensitive reaction yields are enough; subjective visual phenomenology is not established.

## Class 4 — synthesis / open conjecture

The field may have spent decades asking for *the* magnetic sense when animals could be combining at least two jobs: a directional compass and a positional/map channel. A retina-based radical-pair mechanism could plausibly supply direction while another receptor supplies intensity or gradients. This is a useful synthesis, not a finding.

A second conjecture is methodological: magnetoreception is a model case for a scientific object that is real at the behavioral level, plausible at the physical level, increasingly specific at the molecular level, and still elusive at the anatomical/transduction level. That inverted evidentiary stack explains both the field's durability and its susceptibility to premature “mystery solved” headlines.

## Contradiction and incentive audit

**CRY4 tension.** Xu et al. make CRY4 physically compelling. Roswitha and Wolfgang Wiltschko's published comment on the Nature page points to behavioral orientation under wavelengths they argue do not fit the proposed CRY4 photoreduction route and to retinal localization issues. Preserve this dispute rather than choosing a winner by prestige.

**Magnetite tension.** Failure of the upper-beak receptor anatomy does not falsify every magnetite mechanism everywhere. It falsifies a particular influential anatomical identification. The manuscript must not inflate a correction into a universal negative.

**RF tension.** RF disruption is attractive because it appears mechanism-specific. That attractiveness creates an incentive to treat every disorientation result as a radical-pair assay. Require frequency, amplitude, shielding, field geometry, behavioral endpoint, blinding and replication details.

**Quantum incentive.** “Quantum biology” attracts attention and can turn ordinary but beautiful spin chemistry into metaphysics. The book uses quantum mechanics exactly where the mechanism requires it and nowhere else.

## Chronology spine

- 19th–20th centuries: behavioral and homing observations accumulate before a receptor theory is mature.
- 1960s–1970s: controlled avian orientation experiments establish magnetic compass behavior and unusual properties such as inclination sensitivity.
- 1978: Schulten and colleagues develop radical-pair ideas relevant to magnetic biological effects.
- 2000s: cryptochrome and RF-disruption experiments make a photochemical compass increasingly testable.
- 2012: Treiber et al. overturn the influential iron-rich upper-beak-cell identification by showing the cells are macrophages.
- 2016: Hore and Mouritsen synthesize radical-pair constraints and the experimental program.
- 2021: Xu et al. demonstrate magnetic sensitivity in European-robin CRY4 in vitro; the same year Wiltschko et al. argue behavioral evidence points strongly to cryptochrome but favor a different candidate/photocycle interpretation.
- 2022 onward: frequency-resolved RF work continues to test radical-pair predictions while receptor identity remains open.
