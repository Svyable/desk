# The Compass in the Eye

**Subtitle:** Magnetoreception, Radical Pairs, and the Sense Biology Still Cannot Point To

**Author:** Sven Hardy Benson

**Status:** Drafting

**Chapters:** 18

## Thesis

Animals unquestionably extract useful information from Earth's magnetic field, but the most compelling candidate mechanism in birds—a light-dependent radical-pair reaction in retinal cryptochrome—has reached an unusual evidentiary state: physics can make the effect, proteins can show magnetic sensitivity, behavior follows several predicted constraints, and yet biology still cannot point cleanly to the receptor and say *there*. The book is about what it means for a sensory system to be strongly evidenced before its organ is securely identified.

## Four evidence classes

1. **Verified/documented:** behavioral magnetic orientation in multiple taxa; inclination-compass behavior in birds; light dependence; in-vitro magnetic sensitivity of European-robin CRY4; iron-rich upper-beak cells previously proposed as receptors being identified as macrophages.
2. **Credible but incomplete:** radiofrequency disruption consistent with radical-pair predictions; cryptochrome localization and molecular candidates; trigeminal magnetic information; candidate magnetic map mechanisms.
3. **Disputed/unconfirmed:** any single cryptochrome as *the* avian receptor; a unique receptor organ; claims that one behavioral protocol closes the mechanism; broad claims that anthropogenic RF noise is a population-level migration threat.
4. **Speculative synthesis:** birds may possess multiple magnetic senses with different jobs, and the field's long search for one anatomical compass may itself be the category error.

## Defeat condition

The central thesis weakens if a receptor cell, molecule, transduction chain, neural pathway and behavior are connected end-to-end under geomagnetic-strength fields with independent replication, leaving no serious multi-receptor ambiguity. It also weakens if the strongest radical-pair behavioral signatures fail robust preregistered replication while a competing mechanism explains them better.
