## Contradiction and incentive audit

**Institutional retrospective bias.** CIA and Navy public histories have incentives to present strange historical programs as ingenious, bounded and responsible. Their acknowledgment establishes program existence better than it establishes complete operational performance.

**Myth-market bias.** Animal espionage produces unusually memorable stories. Retellings select cats, dolphins and spectacular failures while discarding routine training, negative trials and denominator data.

**Classification inversion.** A missing operational record is not evidence for a sensational mission. Conversely, later declassification does not prove that every earlier rumor was false. Claims remain event-specific.

**Animal-rights advocacy incentives.** Advocacy reporting can surface welfare facts institutions underemphasize, but allegations of weaponization require independent documentary support.

**Military success bias.** Demonstrations, exercises and deployments are not interchangeable. A capability shown under training conditions does not establish comparative effectiveness against alternative sensors in combat.

## Chronology

- 1959 — Navy dates the beginning of its marine-mammal work to this year.
- 1960s — Marine Mammal Program establishes its long-running Point Loma presence; multiple species are investigated.
- Cold War — CIA explores animal intelligence platforms including pigeon photography and acoustic-animal concepts.
- 11 June 1969 — Navy photograph records trained porpoise Tuffy performing a task at Point Mugu.
- Early 1990s — Navy says classification surrounding marine-mammal missions is lifted sufficiently for broader public explanation.
- March 2013 — Navy dolphins detect buried Howell torpedo sections off San Diego.
- 2018 — Navy publicly documents dolphin mine-countermeasure training during RIMPAC SOCAL.
- 2024 — CIA publishes a retrospective survey of animal espionage projects while noting that portions of the pigeon story remain classified.

## Central epistemic rule

A documented program establishes that an institution investigated or used a capability. It does not establish every alleged mission, claimed performance level, or later legend attached to the program.
