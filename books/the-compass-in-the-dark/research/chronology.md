# Chronology

- 20th century: behavioral experiments establish magnetic orientation across birds and other taxa.
- 1970s–1990s: radical-pair and magnetite mechanisms develop into major competing families.
- 2004: Mora et al. report trigeminal mediation of magnetic anomaly discrimination in homing pigeons.
- 2012: Treiber et al. show canonical iron-rich pigeon-beak cells are macrophages, not magnetosensitive neurons.
- 2012: pigeon brainstem recordings report neurons encoding magnetic direction, intensity and polarity.
- 2014: Engels et al. report broadband anthropogenic electromagnetic noise disrupting European robin magnetic compass behavior under double-blind conditions.
- 2017–2018: Cry4 localization and seasonal-expression work strengthens interest in avian retinal CRY4.
- 2021: Xu et al. report magnetic sensitivity of European robin cryptochrome 4 in vitro.
- 2021–2024: additional RF-disruption and spin-chemistry work narrows radical-pair predictions.
- 2024: Luo et al. propose an RF-only behavioral test in the absence of Earth's static field.
- 2024: Nature Communications work identifies a possible quantum-Zeno route for Earth-strength sensitivity in a tightly bound cryptochrome radical pair.
- 2026: localized magnetic-interference experiments in juvenile loggerhead turtles provide evidence for cephalic magnetic map receptors without identifying the receptor itself.
