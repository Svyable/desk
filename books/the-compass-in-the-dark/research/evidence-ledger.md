# Evidence Ledger

## 1. Verified / declassified / documented

- Controlled magnetic-field manipulations alter orientation in multiple animal taxa.
- European robin CRY4 exhibits magnetic sensitivity in vitro (Xu et al., Nature, 2021).
- Anthropogenic broadband electromagnetic noise disrupted magnetic compass orientation in double-blinded European robin experiments (Engels et al., Nature, 2014).
- The famous iron-rich pigeon-beak cell clusters were identified as macrophages rather than magnetosensitive neurons (Treiber et al., Nature, 2012).
- Juvenile loggerhead turtles can be conditioned to magnetic signatures; 2026 localized-interference work implicates a cephalic receptor region.

## 2. Credible but incomplete

- Cryptochrome radical-pair chemistry is a serious candidate mechanism for the avian inclination compass.
- Trigeminal pathways likely carry at least some magnetic information in birds.
- Multiple magnetic senses may coexist within the same animal.

## 3. Disputed / unconfirmed

- The identity and anatomical location of the avian magnetic receptor.
- Claims that CRY4 is necessary and sufficient for avian compass orientation.
- Claims that laboratory RF disruption establishes broad ecological harm.
- Strong claims of conscious human magnetoreception.

## 4. Speculative synthesis

- Map and compass functions may use distinct receptor physics integrated centrally.
- Anthropogenic RF noise may constitute a sensory-habitat variable under specific ecological conditions.
- A cross-level perturbation program linking cryptochrome molecular parameters to behavioral RF response could provide a decisive causal test.

## Incentives and error modes

The field contains incentives in both directions. Quantum language attracts publicity and can encourage mechanistic overstatement. Skepticism based on field-strength intuition can underrate spin chemistry. Anatomical searches are vulnerable to iron contamination and selection bias. Behavioral orientation studies are sensitive to subtle environmental variables, sample size, circular statistics, season and laboratory conditions. Mechanistic claims should therefore survive laboratories with different disciplinary priors.
