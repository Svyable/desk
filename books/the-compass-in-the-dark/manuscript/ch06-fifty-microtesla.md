# Fifty Microtesla

Earth's field is weak enough that magnetoreception has always attracted a credibility tax. Depending on location, the geomagnetic field is on the order of tens of microtesla. A refrigerator magnet is many orders stronger.

This comparison is rhetorically powerful and scientifically incomplete.

A sensory system does not need to perform mechanical work against thermal agitation. It needs a repeatable physical dependency that can be amplified. Photoreceptors detect individual photons because molecular events are embedded in biochemical gain and neural processing. Hearing begins with microscopic mechanical displacements. The nervous system is an empire built on amplification.

The real question is therefore not whether fifty microtesla looks small on an engineer's bench. It is whether a candidate molecular system has reaction dynamics whose field dependence survives the decoherence, rotational disorder, biochemical noise and timescales of a living cell.

Radical-pair theory tries to answer that quantitatively. Hyperfine interactions within the radicals compete with the external field. Spin relaxation can erase directional information. Inter-radical interactions can suppress sensitivity. Molecular ordering matters. Reaction lifetimes matter. The entire mechanism lives or dies in details that cannot be replaced by saying quantum.

This is healthy. Frontier science becomes pseudoscience when its vocabulary immunizes it from scale analysis. Magnetoreception remains interesting precisely because the weak-field problem has not been waved away; it has been attacked experimentally and theoretically.

The field has still not closed the budget. A magnetically sensitive purified protein may display a measurable effect larger than what persists in vivo. Neural averaging might amplify tiny biases, but amplification schemes must be biologically plausible. The animal has only so much retinal tissue, only so many molecules, only so much integration time before movement changes the geometry.

Fifty microtesla is not an argument against magnetoreception. The animals have already won that argument behaviorally. It is a demand placed on every proposed receptor.
