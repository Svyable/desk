# What the Eye Might Be Doing

The retina is appealing for a magnetic compass because it already solves a similar engineering problem: convert weak, patterned physical signals into spatial neural information.

A radical-pair compass could exploit that existing architecture. Photochemistry changes with molecular orientation relative to the geomagnetic field. Ordered receptor molecules distributed across the retina produce a directional modulation. Downstream circuitry compares the pattern against visual or vestibular information. The result need not be a literal glowing band across the sky. It could be a bias in contrast, a neural channel never entering the animal's conscious visual scene, or something we do not have vocabulary for.

The eye hypothesis accumulated several kinds of support. Bird magnetic compass orientation depends on light conditions. Cryptochromes occur in retinal cells. European robin Cry4 has been localized to photoreceptor populations including double cones, and its expression shows biologically suggestive patterns. In vitro work later established that robin CRY4 photochemistry is magnetically sensitive.

But the retina is not a solved location. The field has spent decades learning that plausible localization is not causal identification. A receptor must be necessary, or at least manipulable in a way that changes magnetic behavior while preserving the rest of the animal's sensory function.

That is difficult in a migratory bird. Knock out a retinal protein and you may disturb circadian rhythms, vision, development or other functions. Illuminate one spectral band and you change both magnetochemistry and ordinary vision. Place a coil near the head and you affect more than one tissue.

A 2025 Physiological Reviews synthesis emphasized these unresolved issues. Cryptochrome remains a leading hypothesis, but spectral mismatches, cellular localization questions and conflicting genetic results in other species leave alternatives alive.

The right posture is not frustration. This is what a real mechanistic frontier looks like. The easy experiments have been done. The remaining ones require interventions that separate overlapping biological functions cleanly enough that the inference survives.
