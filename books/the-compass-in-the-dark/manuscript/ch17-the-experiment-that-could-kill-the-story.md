# The Experiment That Could Kill the Story

A frontier field should design the experiment it fears.

For avian radical-pair magnetoreception, I would build a multicenter study around genetically and biochemically characterized cryptochrome variants with preregistered quantitative predictions. The goal would not be to show that birds orient. We already know they often do. The goal would be to couple a specific molecular perturbation to a specific change in field sensitivity.

The behavioral facilities would be electromagnetically characterized before animals arrived. Coil systems would be independently audited for harmonics, vibration, acoustic noise and thermal effects. Experimenters scoring orientation would not know condition. Analysis would be locked before unblinding. Multiple laboratories would run the same perturbations.

The molecular arm would measure the same cryptochrome variants under controlled photochemical conditions, deriving predicted changes in magnetic sensitivity. The behavioral arm would then test whether the direction and magnitude of those changes appear in intact animals.

A strong null would hurt. If variants with radically different in vitro magnetic responses produced indistinguishable compass behavior, the case for that molecular pathway would weaken. If radiofrequency predictions repeatedly failed across audited laboratories, some of the radical-pair story would have to be revised.

That is the point.

A theory becomes scientific not when it explains an old mystery beautifully but when it gives an adversary a clean way to injure it.
