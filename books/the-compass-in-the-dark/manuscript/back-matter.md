# Back Matter

## Evidence classes

**Class 1 — verified / documented.** Numerous animals alter behavior in experimentally manipulated geomagnetic fields. European robin CRY4 is magnetically sensitive in vitro. Weak radiofrequency noise has disrupted magnetic compass orientation in controlled bird experiments. The iron-rich pigeon-beak cells once widely treated as candidate magnetoreceptors were shown to be macrophages. Juvenile loggerhead turtles can be conditioned to magnetic signatures, and localized magnetic interference near the head disrupts that learned recognition more than comparable interference elsewhere.

**Class 2 — credible but incomplete.** Cryptochrome radical-pair chemistry is a leading mechanistic account of the light-dependent avian compass. Trigeminal pathways appear involved in some magnetic responses, plausibly including map/intensity information. Cephalic localization in turtles narrows but does not identify the receptor.

**Class 3 — disputed / unconfirmed.** A specific anatomical avian magnetite receptor remains unconfirmed. Human magnetoreception claims remain preliminary and heterogeneous. Population-level ecological effects from anthropogenic electromagnetic noise are not established merely by laboratory orientation disruption.

**Class 4 — synthesis / conjecture.** Birds may carry distinct magnetic map and compass systems with different receptor physics. Modern electromagnetic infrastructure may alter sensory habitat for some species. A molecular-to-behavior perturbation program could decisively separate cryptochrome and mineral mechanisms.

## Contradiction ledger

The strongest contradiction in the field is productive: evidence for magnetic behavior has strengthened while receptor certainty has repeatedly weakened. The pigeon-beak magnetite model lost its canonical cell type in 2012. CRY4 gained direct in-vitro magnetic sensitivity in 2021 but still lacks decisive organism-level causal proof. Radiofrequency disruption fits radical-pair expectations yet does not uniquely identify cryptochrome. Light dependence is strong in avian compass work but not every magnetic behavior across taxa is light-dependent.
