# When Quantum Biology Is Not a Metaphor

Quantum biology attracts nonsense because quantum mechanics already sounds like permission to be vague. Consciousness, healing, intention and information are routinely attached to the word quantum without a Hamiltonian, a timescale or a measurement.

Magnetoreception is different in a narrow, important way. The proposed mechanism depends on electron-spin dynamics that are unambiguously quantum mechanical. Radical-pair reactions are a real branch of spin chemistry. Magnetic-field effects on reaction yields are experimentally established in chemical systems.

What remains uncertain is whether evolution built an animal compass out of that machinery.

That distinction should discipline the language. It is fair to call radical-pair magnetoreception a quantum-biological hypothesis. It is not fair to say avian navigation proves long-lived macroscopic quantum coherence in the mystical sense. The relevant coherence is molecular, transient and chemically specific.

The achievement, if the mechanism is correct, would still be extraordinary. A warm organism would be using quantum spin evolution not despite biological noise but within a biochemical architecture adapted to exploit it. Evolution would not need to preserve fragile laboratory purity. It would need only enough signal, often enough, for selection to improve orientation.

The best way to protect the wonder is to make it smaller. Name the radicals. Measure their lifetimes. Change amino acids. Vary magnetic field orientation. Calculate the response at fifty microtesla. Follow the signal into a cell.

Mysticism expands with ambiguity. Science earns strangeness by shrinking it.
