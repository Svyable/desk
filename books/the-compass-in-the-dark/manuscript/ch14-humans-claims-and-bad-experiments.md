# Humans, Claims, and Bad Experiments

Humans are magnets for magnetic claims.

People report sensing north. Devices promise to entrain bodies with fields. Geomagnetic storms are blamed for mood, sleep, disease and collective behavior. Some effects may eventually prove real. Most claims arrive with a methodological problem before they acquire a biophysical one.

Animal magnetoreception does not grant humans a magnetic sense by analogy. Evolution can preserve receptors in migratory lineages and lose or repurpose them elsewhere. Humans retain cryptochromes, but possession of a protein family is not evidence of a conscious compass.

There have been serious experiments probing human neural responses to rotated Earth-strength magnetic fields, including reported EEG changes under controlled conditions. Those results deserve replication and mechanistic work. They do not justify stories about intuitive geomagnetic navigation or magnetic healing.

The standard should be the same one imposed on birds. Define the stimulus physically. Blind participants and experimenters. Pre-register the direction and timing of effects. Control acoustic, vibrational, thermal and electrical cues from coils. Replicate across laboratories. Then identify the transduction pathway.

Magnetoreception is a useful antidote to both credulity and reflexive dismissal. It proves that Earth's field can be biologically meaningful. It does not prove that every organism or every reported symptom is magnetically mediated.

A real sixth sense is not a permission slip for the rest.
