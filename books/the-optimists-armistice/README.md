Ralph Bunche’s life joins American race, empire, decolonization, the creation of the United Nations, the Arab-Israeli conflict, peacekeeping, civil rights, and the practical craft of mediation.

The book’s dramatic center is method rather than sainthood: what a mediator actually does when parties mistrust one another, violence changes the negotiating structure, and the institution he represents possesses little coercive power of its own.
