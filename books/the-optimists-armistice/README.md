Ralph Bunche’s life joins American race, empire, decolonization, the creation of the United Nations, the Arab-Israeli conflict, peacekeeping, civil rights, and the practical craft of mediation.

The book’s dramatic center is method rather than sainthood: what a mediator actually does when parties mistrust one another, violence changes the negotiating structure, and the institution he represents possesses little coercive power of its own.

The architecture is intentionally elastic. Twenty chapters is the current working shape, not a ceiling; additional chapters should be added wherever the documentary record earns a distinct dramatic center rather than compressing consequential episodes for symmetry.

## Evidentiary rule

No invented dialogue or interiority. Scenes must be supported by archives, Bunche papers, UN records, oral histories, contemporaneous reporting, or reliable biographies. The book will distinguish the armistice achievement from a comprehensive peace settlement and will treat the displacement and refugee crisis of 1948 as part of the historical record rather than background scenery.
