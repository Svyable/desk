# EXPLAIN YOURSELF, EARTH — editorial brief

**Title:** EXPLAIN YOURSELF, EARTH
**Subtitle:** Twenty True Stories From a Planet That Will Not Behave
**Author:** Sven Hardy Benson
**Form:** linked narrative nonfiction; twenty reported episodes and mechanisms, not a listicle or speculative future book.

## Reader promise and central question

**Question:** What happens when the physical and biological world repeatedly violates everyday intuition but remains entirely within natural law?

**Promise:** The reader will leave with a more exact sense of what can happen in glaciers, deserts, storms, forests, animal bodies and the deep Earth—and with practical habits for separating what scientists observed from the interpretation still being tested. The pleasure lies in the real, stubborn particulars: a lake of observations rather than a metaphysical theory of 'glitches.'

**Ideal reader:** someone who likes *A Short History of Nearly Everything*, field science, strange natural history, beautiful mechanisms and evidence that is not domesticated for a motivational conclusion.

## Distinction from neighboring books

*Ideas From The Future & Other Odd Things In My Head* begins with imagined institutions and engineering futures. *The Evidence Is Ridiculous* begins with actual but bizarre human actions, wars, agencies, postal rules and experiments. **This book puts human contrivance in the background. The main actors are matter, organisms and physical systems.** It avoids reuse of the previous twenty cases: no Sky-lab spiders, slime-mold Tokyo, lake Nyos, brine pools beneath the Gulf, natural Oklo reactors or wartime animal weapons.

The strongest competing view is that the natural world contains no surprises once the mechanism is understood, and this is merely a collection of misleading headlines. The book must make the mechanism interesting enough to disprove that objection; where a new study is tentative, explain what has not been established. The title gives permission to be amused, not credulous.

## Sequence and change

1–6: material and physical systems refuse ordinary intuition, from visible blood-colored ice and moving rocks to sound, light and vibrations beyond easy perception. 7–14: life manages a series of biological engineering problems that would sound irrational if proposed by a product designer—ice, circulation, regeneration, counting, heat and atmospheric electricity. 15–20: the unit of life and the location of life become unstable—one forest as one clone, an entire species in one place, animals that live on dead bone, fish without hemoglobin, viruses exceeding familiar size categories, and deep microbial ecosystems. End not with cosmic significance but with the literal depth of inhabited Earth.

## Source and ethics rule

The central incident for each chapter must be verified in an original paper or authoritative field record. A reader link inside prose and an evidence ledger entry identify the specific claim. Do not create scenes of expeditions nobody recorded, invent conversations, assign animal mental states, or use measurements from different species interchangeably. In chapters with conservation consequences, discuss the material conditions, not only the striking physiology. Bring competing results when needed (notably Pando age, causes of booming dunes, extreme claims about photosynthesis and regeneration, and dark-biosphere population estimates).
