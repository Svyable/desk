## Reader promise

Twenty verified observations from the natural world that defy everyday intuition: iron-rich brine emerging from an Antarctic glacier; rocks moving across desert mud; dunes booming like machinery; luminous seas detected from orbit; lightning above storms; the Earth's continuous seismic hum; ice worms; fungal ice hair; frogs that survive freezing or hide their blood; a sea slug's regenerating head; carnivorous plants that respond to sequences of signals; flowers that generate heat; spiders sensing atmospheric electricity; a vast aspen clone; a fish endemic to one tiny hot-water cave; bone-eating worms; fish without hemoglobin; enormous viruses; and microbes deep in rock.

The natural phenomenon is the protagonist. A chapter may feature a particular paper, expedition, specimen, conservation dispute, or changed model, but it must not suggest nature intended to entertain or confuse people. **True** means a named, documented observation or controlled result—not proof of every hypothesis explaining it. The book must distinguish the observed effect from the favored mechanism, the measurement from the estimate, and the ordinary scientific uncertainty from exaggerated folklore.

**Evidence discipline:** cite real research and authoritative field records; record contrary findings, alternate mechanisms, sample sizes and known limitations. No invented interviews, first-person expedition claims, fake dialogue, or made-up discovery scenes. The title is playful; the science is not.
