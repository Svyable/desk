*A Guide to Surviving the Agent Economy*

The working thesis is that the scarce skill in the agent economy is not prompting but turning broad machine capability into a qualified role. Managers may call the process onboarding, coders may call its pieces staging and evals, and trades have an older word for the larger pattern: apprenticeship. The agent gets a training ground before it gets the keys.

The book's core distinction is **capability versus qualification**. A model may be broadly capable while a specific role remains unqualified for consequential work. Examples, corrections, tests, tools, source hierarchies, permissions, escalation rules, logs, and operating history can turn repeated human judgment into **accumulated operating judgment** — an asset that may matter even as base models improve and become widely available.

The first 21-chapter manuscript arc is now present, together with front matter, back matter, a chapter-mapped research trail, and rights metadata. The book remains **Drafting** while chapters are expanded and mechanically checked against the Desk full-book standard, evidence is tightened, and the whole manuscript receives a publication edit.

## Working vocabulary

**Training ground** is the bounded environment where an agent can be wrong cheaply. **Apprenticeship** is the process of moving from observation and shadow work toward more complete responsibility. **Qualification** is the evidence that a role has earned a defined level of authority. **Accumulated operating judgment** is the reusable layer of examples, tests, corrections, tools, permissions, and operating history that may compound around the role.

These are authorial synthesis terms, not external technical standards or claims that an AI system develops like a human apprentice.
