# Research trail and evidentiary matrix

## Evidence classes

**Class 1 — verified / declassified / documented**

- National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine, *An Assessment of Illness in U.S. Government Employees and Their Families at Overseas Embassies* (2020). The committee judged directed pulsed RF energy the most plausible mechanism among those considered for distinctive acute features, while emphasizing limitations. https://doi.org/10.17226/25889
- ODNI, *IC Experts Panel on Anomalous Health Incidents Executive Summary* (declassified February 1, 2022). The panel states that pulsed electromagnetic energy, particularly RF, plausibly explains core characteristics of a subset of AHIs; attribution was outside its remit. https://www.dni.gov/files/ODNI/documents/assessments/2022_02_01_AHI_Executive_Summary_FINAL_Redacted.pdf
- ODNI archive, *Complementary Efforts on Anomalous Health Incidents* (February 2, 2022), describing panel composition and access to more than 1,000 classified documents. https://archive.dni.gov/index.php/newsroom/reports-publications/reports-publications-2022/3591-complementary-efforts-on-anomalous-health-incidents
- National Intelligence Council, *Updated Assessment of Anomalous Health Incidents* (March 1, 2023). https://www.dni.gov/files/ODNI/documents/assessments/NIC-Unclassified-ICA-Updated-Assessment-AHI-March2023.pdf
- NIH/JAMA AHI studies (March-April 2024), finding no significant group-level MRI pattern or broad biomarker signature explaining symptoms. NIH overview: https://www.nih.gov/news-events/news-releases/nih-studies-find-severe-symptoms-havana-syndrome-no-evidence-mri-detectable-brain-injury-or-biological-abnormalities ; imaging DOI: https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2024.2424 ; clinical DOI: https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2024.2413
- GAO-24-106593, *Havana Syndrome: Better Patient Communication and Monitoring of Key DOD Tasks Needed to Better Ensure Timely Treatment* (July 29, 2024). https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-24-106593
- House Intelligence Committee statement, June 11, 2026, reporting DNI recall of the earlier AHI ICAs. This documents the committee’s statement and the recall; Chairman Crawford’s characterization of the assessments as fraudulent is attributed opinion, not established mechanism or attribution. https://intelligence.house.gov/2026/06/11/chairman-crawford-statement-on-director-gabbards-recall-of-the-flawed-intelligence-community-assessments-on-ahi/
- House Homeland Security Committee release, January 15, 2026, opening an inquiry into reports that HSI acquired a device allegedly relevant to AHI. The inquiry itself is documented; the device’s relevance is not established. https://homeland.house.gov/2026/01/15/homeland-republicans-investigate-reports-of-havana-syndrome-device-acquired-by-dhs-under-the-biden-administration/

**Class 2 — credible but incomplete reporting / testimony**

- Reuters, January 10, 2025, reporting the updated intelligence split in which most agencies retained a very-unlikely foreign-adversary judgment while two agencies shifted because of new reporting. https://www.reuters.com/world/us/most-us-spy-agencies-think-havana-syndrome-was-likely-not-caused-by-foreign-foe-2025-01-10/
- AP, January 10, 2025, parallel account of the seven-agency split and low-confidence dissent. https://apnews.com/article/9432754ff9889e24a356a77321631c59
- Washington Post, February 14, 2026, reporting a Norwegian government researcher’s pulsed-microwave self-experiment, subsequent neurological symptoms, and U.S. official interest. Strong reporting, but the experiment is not publicly documented as a controlled or peer-reviewed study. https://www.washingtonpost.com/national-security/2026/02/14/havana-syndrome-cia-norway-experiment/
- Investigative journalism linking Russian intelligence personnel or research to AHI-relevant locations/capabilities. Treat event-level attribution as incomplete unless independent technical or documentary evidence closes the chain.

**Class 3 — disputed allegations / unconfirmed claims**

- A specific foreign state, unit, or officer caused any named AHI event.
- A device reportedly acquired by U.S. authorities is the same technology used in historical incidents.
- Russian-origin components in any recovered or purchased device establish Russian state operation.
- Every case grouped under the AHI label shares one external cause.
- Persistent symptoms imply persistent structural brain injury.

**Class 4 — speculative synthesis / open conjecture**

- Some AHI events may represent unintended biological side effects of surveillance or electronic-intelligence systems rather than deliberate anti-personnel attack.
- The principal intelligence failure may be category construction: broad case definitions could obscure a smaller, technically distinctive subset.
- The optimal response is prospective multimodal instrumentation at high-risk posts, separating care eligibility from research classification and mechanism from attribution.
- If a practical pulsed-energy device can reproduce a narrow acute phenotype under controlled conditions, historical attribution would still require event-specific matching of waveform, geometry, access, and adversary presence.

## Contradictions to preserve

- 2020 National Academies mechanism ranking versus 2024 NIH lack of persistent MRI/biomarker signature.
- 2022 IC expert-panel mechanism plausibility versus 2023 majority foreign-attribution skepticism.
- 2025 two-agency shift versus continued five-agency skepticism.
- 2026 recall of earlier assessments versus the absence, as of this draft, of a public replacement assessment proving foreign attack.
- Patient testimony and severe symptoms versus heterogeneous case definitions and common background symptoms.
- Historical Soviet microwave research and Moscow embassy illumination versus the absence of a public forensic chain tying those precedents to Havana.
