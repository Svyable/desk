# Front Matter

*The Invisible Blow: Anomalous Health Incidents, Directed Energy, and the Intelligence Problem of an Unseen Weapon*

Sven Hardy Benson

A note on evidence: this book treats “Havana Syndrome” as a historical label, not a settled diagnosis. The U.S. government’s preferred umbrella term is anomalous health incidents (AHIs). The manuscript distinguishes four evidence classes throughout: documented facts and declassified records; credible but incomplete reporting or testimony; disputed allegations without independent confirmation; and speculative synthesis.

The thesis is narrower than the headlines. Pulsed radio-frequency energy has documented sensory and biological effects, and U.S. scientific/intelligence panels have considered it a plausible mechanism for a subset of AHIs. That does not establish that a directed-energy weapon caused any specific incident, that one foreign state conducted a campaign, or that all reported AHIs share a cause. The most revealing feature of the case may be institutional: mechanism, diagnosis, attribution, and care were repeatedly collapsed into one argument even though they require different evidence.

This manuscript uses only public, lawfully available sources. It does not claim access to classified material.
