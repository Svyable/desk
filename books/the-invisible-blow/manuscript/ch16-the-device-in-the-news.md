# The Device in the News

In 2026, the AHI story acquired hardware.

Or at least reports of hardware.

Media accounts said U.S. investigators had obtained a portable device some officials suspected might be relevant to anomalous health incidents. House Homeland Security Republicans responded by asking the Department of Homeland Security for details of an alleged acquisition by Homeland Security Investigations, including cost, testing, provenance, and operational purpose. The committee release carefully described these as reports, not settled facts.

This is how a disputed technology should enter a serious book: one rung at a time.

Class one: a congressional committee publicly sent an inquiry about reports of a device. Documented.

Class two: credible journalists reported that U.S. authorities acquired and tested a device with relevant characteristics. Credible but incomplete.

Class three: the device is the same kind of system used in historical AHIs. Unconfirmed.

Class four: the acquisition reveals the identity of the attacker or explains the global incident pattern. Speculation.

The ladder prevents narrative acceleration.

The Norway self-experiment reported by the Washington Post has the same structure. The reported existence of a powerful pulsed-microwave apparatus and an adverse self-exposure is important. But without published waveform, dosimetry, medical records, controls, and replication, it cannot bear the causal weight people want to place on it.

The danger is that hardware feels like closure.

A photograph of a box can overwhelm a decade of ambiguous epidemiology. Intelligence history is full of captured systems whose operational significance took years to understand. A device may be prototype, decoy, research instrument, surveillance tool, counter-drone system, electronic-warfare component, or something genuinely novel. Russian-origin components do not establish Russian state operation. A frequency generator does not reveal doctrine.

Foreign materiel exploitation has a useful rule: characterize before narrating.

Disassemble the system.

Measure emissions across operating modes.

Map beam pattern.

Determine peak and average power.

Identify storage and switching components.

Test range under realistic architecture.

Use phantoms and instrumented surrogates to map energy deposition.

Compare predicted sensory effects with blinded experiments where ethical.

Search historical incident sites for signatures the device should have produced.

Only then ask whether the object fits the crime.

This chapter is deliberately unsatisfying because the public evidence remains incomplete. The device reports could become the most important development in the case or a spectacular dead end.

The correct posture is readiness to be surprised by either.
