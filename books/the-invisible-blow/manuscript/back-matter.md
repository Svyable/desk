# Back Matter

The public record on anomalous health incidents is unusually unstable. Readers should treat dates as part of the evidence.

1961 — Allan H. Frey publishes early work on human auditory perception of pulsed radio-frequency energy.

Cold War — The Soviet Union directs microwave radiation toward the U.S. Embassy in Moscow; U.S. agencies study potential biological implications through work including Project PANDORA.

Late 2016 — U.S. government personnel in Havana report unusual acute sensory and health events.

2020 — National Academies publishes *An Assessment of Illness in U.S. Government Employees and Their Families at Overseas Embassies*, judging directed pulsed RF energy the most plausible mechanism among those considered for distinctive acute features, while emphasizing major data limitations. https://doi.org/10.17226/25889

February 2, 2022 — ODNI releases the AHI Experts Panel executive summary. The panel, working from more than 1,000 classified documents plus scientific and medical material, concludes pulsed electromagnetic energy, particularly RF, plausibly explains core characteristics of a subset of cases. The panel does not assess attribution. https://www.dni.gov/files/ODNI/documents/assessments/2022_02_01_AHI_Executive_Summary_FINAL_Redacted.pdf

March 1, 2023 — A coordinated Intelligence Community Assessment says most agencies judge foreign-adversary responsibility very unlikely. https://www.dni.gov/files/ODNI/documents/assessments/NIC-Unclassified-ICA-Updated-Assessment-AHI-March2023.pdf

March 18, 2024 — NIH announces two JAMA studies finding no significant MRI-detectable brain injury pattern or broad biological abnormality distinguishing the studied AHI group from matched controls, while documenting substantial symptoms and some balance-related findings. https://www.nih.gov/news-events/news-releases/nih-studies-find-severe-symptoms-havana-syndrome-no-evidence-mri-detectable-brain-injury-or-biological-abnormalities

July 29, 2024 — GAO reports problems in DOD care pathways for affected personnel. https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-24-106593

January 10, 2025 — Updated intelligence reporting reveals a split: five of seven agencies retain a very-unlikely foreign-adversary judgment while two shift toward greater possibility of foreign capability, at low confidence.

January-February 2026 — Congressional inquiries and credible press reporting describe alleged U.S. acquisition/testing of devices considered potentially relevant to AHI and a Norwegian pulsed-energy self-experiment. These reports do not establish historical attribution.

June 11, 2026 — House Intelligence Committee chairman Rick Crawford announces DNI recall of the earlier AHI Intelligence Community Assessments for reassessment. https://intelligence.house.gov/2026/06/11/chairman-crawford-statement-on-director-gabbards-recall-of-the-flawed-intelligence-community-assessments-on-ahi/

The book’s unresolved question is intentionally smaller than “what is Havana Syndrome?” It is: what evidence would distinguish a transient directed-energy exposure from the combined background of ordinary illness, vestibular disorders, environmental effects, acoustic events, reporting contagion, and intelligence uncertainty?

The answer is not another retrospective argument. It is instrumented prospective capture.
