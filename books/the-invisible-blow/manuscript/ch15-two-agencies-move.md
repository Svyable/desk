# Two Agencies Move

January 2025 did not produce consensus.

It produced a crack.

Most U.S. intelligence agencies continued to judge it very unlikely that a foreign adversary had caused the anomalous health incidents. Two components shifted their views after new reporting related to foreign capability. Public accounts said one moved to roughly even odds that a foreign actor may have developed or used a novel capability in some cases; another considered development possible but deployment less likely. Both judgments carried low confidence.

Reuters reported the split on January 10, 2025: https://www.reuters.com/world/us/most-us-spy-agencies-think-havana-syndrome-was-likely-not-caused-by-foreign-foe-2025-01-10/. AP reported similarly: https://apnews.com/article/9432754ff9889e24a356a77321631c59.

The interesting word is not “possible.”

It is “capability.”

Mechanism and attribution had been converging from opposite directions. The 2022 experts panel said pulsed electromagnetic energy could plausibly explain core features. The majority 2023 assessment said foreign adversary involvement was very unlikely. A technical mechanism can be plausible while foreign possession or use remains unlikely. New evidence that an adversary may possess a relevant device changes the prior probability of attribution without proving a single event.

This is basic Bayesian logic and terrible television.

Imagine three bins. In the first, physics permits the effect but no actor is known to possess a practical system. In the second, one or more actors possess systems capable of producing similar effects, but no system is linked to a case. In the third, a specific system is forensically linked to a specific event.

Public debate jumps from bin one to bin three.

The 2025 shift, as publicly described, moved some analysts toward bin two.

That is significant. It is not the same as solving Havana.

The shift also illustrates why intelligence agencies can disagree without one side being corrupt. Different agencies possess different collection. The National Security Agency, if public reporting identifying it as one of the dissenters is correct, may see communications and technical signals unavailable to a medical or regional analytic component. Army technical intelligence may evaluate foreign hardware differently from CIA counterintelligence. Institutional perspective can create bias, but it can also reveal real evidence asymmetry.

A mature system wants dissent where evidence is asymmetric.

The worse outcome is forced consensus.

The 2026 recall of earlier assessments will likely cause many readers to treat the 2025 dissent as vindicated. That is premature. Recall means reassessment. It makes the prior majority less authoritative. It does not make the minority proven.

The proper status as of this writing is unstable.

Technical plausibility remains open.

Foreign capability appears more plausible to at least some agencies than it did in 2023.

Public attribution remains unresolved.

That is not a satisfying ending.

It is a useful middle.
