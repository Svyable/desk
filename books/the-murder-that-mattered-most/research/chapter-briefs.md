## Cross-chapter checks

A decade verdict should not be retained merely because its chapter is already polished. Before release, each winner should survive three comparisons: the strongest rival on direct observed consequence, the strongest rival on counterfactual consequence, and the strongest rival on moral or cultural memory.

Where those produce different winners, the chapter should say why the book’s institutional-afterlife standard chooses one rather than imply consensus that does not exist.

The most important source-checking targets are the claims that connect the killing to later change. Event chronology is usually easier to establish than causation. A reliable chapter therefore distinguishes:

- **documented response** — a law, investigation, organization, election, migration, court case, or administrative change explicitly connected to the event;
- **reasonable contribution** — an event that formed part of a broader political sequence without being the sole cause;
- **counterfactual possibility** — a claim about what might have happened if the victim survived, which should never be written as observed fact.

The book is strongest when those categories remain visible in the prose.
