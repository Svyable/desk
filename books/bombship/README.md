Working promise: reconstruct Project Orion closely enough to separate a machine that sounds impossible but uses ordinary physics from propulsion claims that require new physics, missing energy, or hidden causal steps. The book will not treat government sponsorship as proof of feasibility, nor cancellation as proof that Orion was technically complete. It will track the distance from concept to subsystem test to integrated nuclear vehicle, including the parts never crossed.

The competing explanation is deliberately strong: Orion may have been less a suppressed near-ready starship than a brilliant paper-and-subscale program whose hardest integrated problems were never forced into one flight article. Treaty politics mattered, but so did mass, launch architecture, radiation, fallout, nuclear-device logistics, safety, cost, and the absence of a nuclear pulse flight test.
