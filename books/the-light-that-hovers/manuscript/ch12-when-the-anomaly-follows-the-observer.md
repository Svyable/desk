# When the Anomaly Follows the Observer

A strange place becomes stranger when the lights know the conference schedule.

Reports often increase when organized observers are present. That could mean investigators choose periods of historically high activity. It could mean more eyes detect more events. It could mean publicity brings more reports. It could mean observers introduce new light sources. The worst interpretation would be to treat increased observations during field campaigns as evidence that the phenomenon responds to attention.

Observation effort is a denominator.

If five residents casually watch the valley for an hour a night, their event rate cannot be compared with a campaign of twenty people using image intensifiers until dawn. Automated cameras change the denominator again. A proper occurrence rate is events per usable sensor-hour under defined detection criteria.

This matters beyond Hessdalen. UAP datasets are shot through with observer selection. Pilots report more where pilots fly. military sensors see more where military sensors operate. smartphone videos explode after smartphones become common. A rise in reports can reflect a rise in surveillance rather than a rise in objects.

Persistent monitoring offers a solution if the system records its own uptime.

Every camera should log when it is functioning, where it is pointed, exposure settings, weather obstruction and software state. Event detection should operate continuously, not only when humans expect activity. A randomly chosen sample of non-event nights should be reviewed with the same care as event nights.

The Blue Box lineage in Hessdalen matters partly because automation can weaken observer expectancy. It does not eliminate selection. Motion-detection thresholds, camera upgrades and data-retention practices create their own biases.

The apparatus has a history too.

A scientifically mature Hessdalen archive should therefore contain not only sightings but the changing sensitivity of the observatory. A year with fewer events after a camera upgrade might mean the phenomenon declined, the detection threshold changed, or false positives were filtered better.

The anomaly does not merely live in the valley.

It lives in the relationship between valley and detector.
