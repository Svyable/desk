# The Valley as a Battery

The phrase `natural battery` is dangerous because it sounds explanatory before the circuit is drawn.

Electrochemical differences in geology are real. Put dissimilar materials in an electrolyte and potentials arise. Ore bodies, groundwater and mineral interfaces can participate in natural electrical systems. The open question is whether any such system in Hessdalen can deliver the field strength, current and spatial geometry required by a luminous atmospheric event.

Voltage is cheap. Power is expensive.

A handheld electrostatic generator can produce tens of thousands of volts and almost no useful continuous power. A car battery produces only twelve volts but can melt metal. Any geological-light theory that quotes electric potential without current, source resistance and duration is half a theory.

The same discipline applies to atmospheric fields. A large potential difference can exist over kilometers while local current density remains tiny. To sustain visible emission, charge must move through a region in a way that continually excites molecules or powers another luminous process.

This suggests a direct experiment. Build an electrical map of the valley over seasons. Measure ground potential differences with stable electrodes. monitor telluric currents. add field mills above ground. pair the measurements with atmospheric conductivity and weather. Then freeze an event-detection algorithm before looking for correlations.

If unusual lights consistently occur when a particular electrical configuration appears, the mechanism earns attention.

If not, the battery metaphor should be retired.

What I like about this experiment is that it does not require believing the lights are extraordinary. A null result would improve the environmental characterization of the valley. A positive result would still need causal testing. Correlation between electric conditions and lights could arise because both respond to weather.

A recurrent anomaly site should be designed so that every hypothesis leaves useful infrastructure behind when it fails.

That is the opposite of paranormal research as tourism. It is how a weird place becomes a field station.
