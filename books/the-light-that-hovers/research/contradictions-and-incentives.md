# Contradictions, Incentives, and Failure Modes

## Mystery inflation

A site known for anomalous lights attracts observers who arrive expecting anomalous lights. Ordinary objects enter the archive with a higher prior probability of being noticed and a lower social threshold for being called unusual.

## Skeptical compression

Demonstrating that some lights are car headlights does not demonstrate that every event is a headlight. The correct unit of analysis is the event plus its measurement chain, not the site's reputation.

## Researcher attachment

Long-running anomaly projects create personal and institutional incentives to preserve the phenomenon as interesting. Interpretive claims from project participants deserve the same calibration and independent replication demanded elsewhere.

## Instrument seduction

Radar, spectroscopy and photometry sound objective, but each can generate misleading precision when calibration, geometry, saturation, signal processing or source association is uncertain. An instrument does not abolish interpretation.

## UFO narrative capture

Hessdalen appears in UFO literature and scientific-anomaly literature. That attention supplies volunteers and funding but can encourage premature technological interpretation.

## Plasma as closure word

Plasma is a state of matter, not a full mechanism. Any plasma account must specify ionization source, lifetime, energy input, confinement or replenishment, spectrum, motion and interaction with the environment.

## Negative-result invisibility

Thousands of hours without extraordinary events matter. Event rates, sensor uptime, weather windows and observation opportunity must be reported, not only successful captures.
