# Evidence Ledger

## Class 1 — Verified / documented

- Project Hessdalen organized an instrumented field campaign in 1984 and published a technical report.
- The report records 53 observations classified by the project as Hessdalen phenomena during the January–February campaign.
- The campaign used cameras, radar, magnetometry, radio-spectrum equipment, seismography, radiation detection and infrared viewing.
- Automated monitoring has operated in Hessdalen since the late 1990s.
- Norwegian–Italian EMBLA field campaigns occurred in the early 2000s and produced optical, radio and radar records.
- Ball lightning has a long scientific literature, including peer-reviewed physical models and laboratory analogues.
- Ordinary sources including vehicles, aircraft, astronomical objects and instrumental artifacts contaminate luminous-anomaly datasets.

## Class 2 — Credible but incomplete

- A subset of Hessdalen observations may represent unusual atmospheric electrical or plasma-like phenomena.
- Some optical observations appear persistent or structured enough to deserve calibrated multisensor follow-up.
- Coincident visual/radar observations are potentially important but require raw-data reconstruction and error analysis.
- Geology, atmospheric ionization, dust/aerosol chemistry and local electrical conditions may contribute to some events.

## Class 3 — Disputed / unconfirmed

- Extreme radar-derived speeds interpreted as actual translational velocity of a compact object.
- Claims that Hessdalen lights demonstrate solid craft.
- Claims of responsive or intelligent behavior based on laser interactions or observer reports.
- Metallic-particle deposition attributed to the phenomenon without secure chain of custody.
- Specific VLF anomalies attributed to an unknown source where anthropogenic explanations remain viable.
- Extraterrestrial or non-human technological origin.

## Class 4 — Speculative synthesis

- Hessdalen may be a mixed-source anomaly ecology rather than one phenomenon.
- Recurrent luminous hotspots may offer better UAP control sites than opportunistic military encounters because observation can be prospective.
- A subset of UAP reports elsewhere may involve poorly classified atmospheric electrical phenomena, without implying that all or most do.
- The best scientific value of Hessdalen may be the development of instrumentation and classification methods rather than discovery of one exotic mechanism.
