# Source Log

Checked or refreshed 2026-09-10 unless otherwise noted. Links are direct to primary, official, peer-reviewed, or serious institutional sources where available.

## Framing / technomania

## Nuclear abundance

- U.S. Nuclear Regulatory Commission historian Thomas Wellock, “Too Cheap to Meter: A History of the Phrase.” Important because it reconstructs Strauss’s 1954 speech, the fusion-vs-fission dispute, the later *Meet the Press* exchange, and contemporary utility skepticism: https://www.nrc.gov/reading-rm/basic-ref/students/history-101/too-cheap-to-meter
- NRC History 101 index, refreshed August 2026: https://www.nrc.gov/reading-rm/basic-ref/students/history101
- Lewis L. Strauss, remarks to the National Association of Science Writers, 16 September 1954, linked through the NRC historical record above. Use quotation sparingly and prefer NRC contextualization.

## Artificial intelligence / labor / productivity / investment

- International Labour Organization, “Generative AI and jobs: A 2025 update,” 20 May 2025. Key boundary: one in four workers globally are in occupations with some degree of GenAI exposure; the study argues transformation is more likely than redundancy for most exposed jobs: https://www.ilo.org/publications/generative-ai-and-jobs-2025-update
- ILO Working Paper 140, *Generative AI and Jobs: A Refined Global Index of Occupational Exposure*, 20 May 2025: https://www.ilo.org/publications/generative-ai-and-jobs-refined-global-index-occupational-exposure
- IMF Staff Discussion Note, “Gen-AI: Artificial Intelligence and the Future of Work,” 2024. Key boundary: roughly 40% of global employment estimated exposed, with higher exposure in advanced economies and both substitution and complementarity channels: https://www.elibrary.imf.org/view/journals/006/2024/001/article-A001-en.xml
- IMF, “AI Will Transform the Global Economy. Let’s Make Sure It Benefits Humanity,” 14 January 2024: https://www.imf.org/en/Blogs/Articles/2024/01/14/ai-will-transform-the-global-economy-lets-make-sure-it-benefits-humanity
- BIS Annual Economic Report 2026. Documents AI-related capital expenditure as a macroeconomically significant investment force in 2025–26 while emphasizing uncertainty and financial fragility: https://www.bis.org/publications/aer-2026
- Iñaki Aldasoro et al., BIS Bulletin 130, “AI and the global economy: implications for central banks,” 28 July 2026. Key boundary: productivity upside potentially large but uncertain and uneven: https://www.bis.org/publications/bulletin-130-ai-and-global-economy-implications-central-banks
- Pablo Hernández de Cos, BIS, “Artificial intelligence, growth and financial stability: challenges for central banks,” speech delivered 10 September 2026. Summarizes task-level studies showing 10–65% productivity gains and roughly 20–50% time savings in studied domains; cites >$1tn expected AI-related capex by the five largest big-tech firms across 2025–26 and industry estimates of $3–4tn global AI investment by 2030. Treat as BIS synthesis, not a substitute for each underlying study: https://www.bis.org/speeches/20260910-artificial-intelligence-growth-and-financial-stability-challenges-central-banks
- BIS Working Paper 1367, “The AI investment race,” 14 July 2026. Models contest externalities and possible overinvestment in a winner-take-most race; comparison with canal, railway and dot-com booms is analytical, not a prediction that an AI crash must occur: https://www.bis.org/publications/working-paper-1367-ai-investment-race
- BIS Bulletin 120, “Financing the AI boom: from cash flows to debt,” 7 January 2026: https://www.bis.org/publications/bulletin-120-financing-ai-boom-cash-flows-debt

## Sources intentionally not treated as proof

- Founder predictions about AGI dates, post-scarcity, job elimination, or specific market sizes: useful as primary evidence of belief/incentive, not as factual evidence of outcome.
- Patents: evidence of filing and disclosed claims, not demonstrated efficacy.
- Vendor benchmark claims without independent replication: Class 2 or 3 depending on disclosure and reproducibility.
- Viral social-media claims about technology capabilities: discovery leads only until independently sourced.
