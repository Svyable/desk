# Project Seal — evidence ledger

## Thesis under test

Project Seal is useful not because it proves that an operational “tsunami bomb” existed, but because it documents a serious military attempt to scale an observed physical effect into a weapon and exposes the distance between experimental possibility and deployable capability.

## Class 1 — verified / documented

- A research work titled *Project SEAL: The Generation of Waves by Means of Explosives*, attributed to Thomas David James Leech and the New Zealand Department of Scientific and Industrial Research, is catalogued as a 1950 publication. WorldCat: https://search.worldcat.org/title/31071831
- Released New Zealand government records concerning Project Seal were publicly reported in 1999. NZ Herald: https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/tsunami-bomb-nzs-devastating-war-secret/AEHW23IAWH3JRLUTZR4PRAPS64/
- The project concerned artificial inundation/wave generation using underwater explosives and was associated with wartime work at Whangaparaoa.
- No public record located in this pass establishes a full-scale operational coastal weapon test.

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete reporting / testimony

- 1999 reporting based on released government files says U.S. and British defense officials remained interested after the war and reports a July 1946 communication concerning Karl Compton, Bikini-test information and Leech's work. This must be upgraded by locating and indexing the underlying correspondence.
- Secondary historical accounts repeatedly report roughly 3,700 experimental explosions. Treat as high-confidence but page-index before using as a precision claim throughout the manuscript.
- Accounts report Admiral Halsey's interest after preliminary Pacific observations. Locate original memorandum.

## Class 3 — disputed / insufficiently confirmed

- Claims that Seal had produced a ready or near-ready ten-metre-tsunami weapon.
- Exact claims that a line of 2,000,000 kg of explosive eight kilometres offshore would reliably inundate a city, absent the original calculation, assumptions and coastal geometry.
- Claims assigning a specific Japanese city or invasion beach as an operational target without primary planning records.
- Any claim that later natural tsunamis or earthquakes demonstrate continuation or deployment of Seal-like weapons.

## Class 4 — synthesis / conjecture

- Project Seal may be a particularly clean historical example of the gap between energetic possibility and militarily useful coupling.
- Its later mythology may illustrate a recurring epistemic error in classified-technology discourse: discovery that a strange program existed is incorrectly treated as evidence that its strongest proposed capability worked.
- Nuclear weapons may have made the concept appear newly attractive after 1945 while simultaneously failing to solve the central coupling/propagation problem. This requires later explosion-wave literature.

## Contradictions to track

1. Reports variously describe the program as abandoned in early 1945, continuing analytically after the war, and producing a 1950 report. Separate field experimentation, administrative project status and later analysis.
2. “Successful tests” can mean successful measurement/model validation, not successful demonstration of an operational weapon.
3. Accounts differ on whether the project concluded feasibility or merely conditional theoretical feasibility.
4. Popular descriptions use “tidal wave” and “tsunami” loosely. Preserve the historical terminology while explaining the physics.

## Incentive audit

- Wartime sponsors had incentive to explore asymmetric offensive possibilities rapidly.
- Researchers had normal institutional incentives to characterize continued investigation as worthwhile.
- Post-declassification journalists had incentive to foreground the spectacular “tsunami bomb” frame.
- Modern conspiracy ecosystems benefit from collapsing program existence into demonstrated capability.
- Skeptical retrospectives can make the inverse mistake: treating eventual non-deployment as proof that the underlying experiments were unserious.

## Defeat condition

The book's central interpretation should be revised if primary technical records show that Project Seal progressed to a reproducible, militarily practical full-scale weapon configuration with demonstrated coastal effects and a credible deployment method. Conversely, if the primary record shows that even the researchers regarded destructive scaling as physically implausible rather than merely impractical, the manuscript must stop presenting the concept as balanced between plausibility and absurdity.
