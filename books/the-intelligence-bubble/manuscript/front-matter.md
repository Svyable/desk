# The Intelligence Bubble

*How AI Will Transform the Economy—and Why So Many Investors Will Lose Money*

Sven Hardy Benson

August 2026

**Author's Note**

This is a book about a technology I expect to succeed.

That sentence matters because the easiest way to misunderstand what follows is to mistake skepticism about prices for skepticism about artificial intelligence. The two are not the same. A bridge can be useful and overpriced. A railroad can transform a nation and bankrupt its financiers. A network can become indispensable after the securities issued to build it have been crushed. A product can improve at astonishing speed while the companies making it discover that improvement has destroyed their pricing power.

Artificial intelligence is already useful enough that the important debate is no longer whether it will matter. The better debate is economic: who pays, who earns, who finances, who bears the fixed costs, who controls distribution, who can switch suppliers, and what happens when a scarce capability becomes abundant.

This manuscript is written in that spirit. It is not a forecast of a single crash date, nor a claim that every AI company is overvalued, nor an argument that the buildout is unnecessary. It is an attempt to separate three questions that markets repeatedly merge into one:

1. Is the technology real?
2. Will society use a great deal of it?
3. Will the owners of today's favored assets earn attractive returns from the prices they are paying today?

The first two can be true while the third is false.

That is the intelligence bubble.
