# Chapter 12 — The Day the Growth Rate Slows

Research snapshot: September 1, 2026.

## CoreWeave debt calendar

- CoreWeave, Form 10-Q for quarter ended June 30, 2026: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1769628/000176962826000366/crwv-20260630.htm
- The filing reports $35.551 billion of future principal payments on total debt at June 30, 2026, including $4.413 billion in the remainder of 2026 and $6.184 billion in 2027.
- It also reports several 2026 financing transactions, including a $2.75 billion 9.75% senior-note issue, $1.25 billion of 9.625% senior notes, €2 billion of 8.50% senior notes, a $4 billion convertible issue, and new delayed-draw term facilities.
- Use: demonstrates that demand growth and financing calendars coexist. It does not by itself establish distress or predict default.

## Microsoft asset-life mix

- Microsoft FY2026 Q1 earnings call: https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor/events/fy-2026/earnings-fy-2026-q1
- Microsoft reported $34.9 billion of quarterly capital expenditure, with roughly half directed to short-lived assets, primarily GPUs and CPUs, and the remainder to long-lived assets expected to support monetization for 15 years and beyond.
- Microsoft FY2026 Q4 earnings call: https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor/events/fy-2026/earnings-fy-2026-q4
- Microsoft reported $41 billion of quarterly capital expenditure, with roughly two-thirds directed to short-lived assets, primarily CPUs and GPUs.
- Use: supports the distinction between accelerator replacement cycles and the longer lives of sites and other infrastructure. Do not treat “AI infrastructure” as one homogeneous asset class.

## Meta capex and depreciation countercase

- Meta FY2025 results, January 28, 2026: https://investor.atmeta.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2026/Meta-Reports-Fourth-Quarter-and-Full-Year-2025-Results/
- Meta initially guided to $115–135 billion of 2026 capital expenditures and said infrastructure costs, including depreciation, would be the largest driver of expense growth.
- Meta Q1 2026 results, April 29, 2026: https://investor.atmeta.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2026/Meta-Reports-First-Quarter-2026-Results/
- Meta raised its 2026 capex range to $125–145 billion and reported $32.23 billion of operating cash flow for the quarter.
- Meta Q2 2026 results: https://investor.atmeta.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2026/Meta-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx
- Meta reported $31.08 billion of second-quarter capex, $31.86 billion of operating cash flow, and $60.80 billion of quarterly revenue.
- Meta FY2024 results, January 29, 2025: https://investor.atmeta.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2025/Meta-Reports-Fourth-Quarter-and-Full-Year-2024-Results/
- Meta disclosed that it extended the estimated useful life of certain servers and network assets to 5.5 years beginning in 2025 and expected the change to reduce 2025 depreciation expense by approximately $2.9 billion.
- Use: counterevidence against a simplistic financing-crisis narrative. A highly profitable buyer can absorb low utilization or experimentation much more easily than a leveraged pure-play supplier. The useful-life disclosure also shows why depreciation assumptions become economically material as infrastructure fleets grow.

## Falsification / uncertainty notes

- Large capex alone does not prove overbuilding. It can be rational when demand is supply-constrained or when infrastructure protects a larger profitable franchise.
- High debt alone does not prove insolvency. Maturity timing, contracted cash flows, collateral, refinancing access, and sponsor/customer support matter.
- Longer server useful lives are not automatically aggressive accounting; workload reuse and software improvements can genuinely extend economic usefulness.
- A slowing percentage growth rate can coexist with rising absolute additions. Deceleration should be interpreted alongside margins, cash generation, utilization, unit pricing, concentration, and financing obligations.
