# Chapter 16 — abundance economics

Research note for the September 2026 revision of **The Abundance Dividend**.

## Generative AI at Work

Primary research source: Erik Brynjolfsson, Danielle Li, and Lindsey R. Raymond, *Generative AI at Work*, NBER Working Paper 31161; published version in *The Quarterly Journal of Economics* (2025).

- The study examined staggered access to a generative-AI conversational assistant among 5,179 customer-support agents.
- The working-paper abstract reports an average productivity increase of about 14%, measured as issues resolved per hour.
- Gains were highly heterogeneous: the largest improvements accrued to novice and lower-skilled workers, while effects for the most experienced and highest-skilled workers were small.
- The authors offer suggestive evidence that the system helped diffuse practices associated with stronger workers and accelerated movement down the experience curve.

Canonical source:

- https://www.nber.org/papers/w31161

The chapter uses this result as evidence of heterogeneous productivity effects and possible diffusion of practice, not as a universal estimate of AI productivity.

## Shifting Work Patterns with Generative AI

Primary research source: Eleanor W. Dillon, Sonia Jaffe, Nicole Immorlica, and Christopher T. Stanton, *Shifting Work Patterns with Generative AI*, NBER Working Paper 33795, issued May 2025 and revised November 2025.

- The field experiment covered 66 firms and 7,137 knowledge workers.
- In the latter half of the six-month experiment, the 80% of treated workers who used the integrated generative-AI tool spent about two fewer hours per week on email and reduced work outside regular hours.
- The researchers did **not** detect broad shifts in the quantity or composition of workers' tasks from individual-level AI provision during the experiment.

Canonical source:

- https://www.nber.org/papers/w33795

This is retained as counterevidence against the stronger claim that individual access to generative AI automatically produces organization-level transformation.

## Stanford AI Index 2026

Source: Stanford Institute for Human-Centered AI, *2026 AI Index Report*, Economy chapter.

- The report says 88% of surveyed organizations reported using AI in at least one business function in 2025, up from 78% in 2024; the underlying adoption figures are survey-based and should be read directionally.
- The report estimates U.S. consumer surplus from generative-AI tools at roughly $172 billion annually by early 2026, up from about $112 billion a year earlier.
- The same report documents rapidly rising AI investment and infrastructure spending, illustrating that user surplus and supplier capital intensity can increase at the same time.

Canonical resources:

- https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index/2026-ai-index-report
- https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index/2026-ai-index-report/economy

## Falsification / counterevidence note

The chapter's abundance thesis can fail at several levels. Cheaper inference may not produce proportionate productivity if verification, integration, physical execution, regulation, or organizational redesign remain binding constraints. Savings can also be competed away downstream rather than retained as higher margins. The revision therefore distinguishes social surplus from investor return and user productivity from profit capture.
