# Chapter 11 — IPO supply and public-float mechanics

Checked 2026-09-01 for the Chapter 11 revision.

## CoreWeave IPO mechanics

Primary source: CoreWeave, Inc. final prospectus, Form 424B4, dated March 27, 2025 and filed March 31, 2025.

- SEC filing: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1769628/000119312525067651/d899798d424b4.htm
- Registration No. 333-285512.
- Final offering: 37,500,000 Class A shares at $40 per share.
- CoreWeave offered 36,590,000 shares; selling stockholders offered 910,000 shares.
- The prospectus describes underwriter and market-standoff restrictions applying to most outstanding Class A shares and related securities, generally until an earnings-based release point or 180 days after the prospectus, subject to exceptions and additional founder restrictions.
- The prospectus explicitly warns that substantial sales after restrictions expire, or the perception that such sales may occur, could pressure the market price.

CoreWeave's March 31, 2025 Form 10-Q later described aggregate IPO proceeds of about $1.4 billion after underwriting discounts and commissions and offering expenses payable by the company.

Falsification note: a lock-up expiration is not evidence that holders actually sold. The chapter therefore treats expiration as removal of a restriction and potential supply, not as a scheduled liquidation event.

## Anthropic IPO timing

Contemporaneous source: Reuters, August 27, 2026, reporting on The Information.

- https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/anthropic-plans-publicly-unveil-ipo-prospectus-after-labor-day-information-2026-08-27/
- Reuters reported that Anthropic was preparing to make its IPO prospectus public shortly after Labor Day, with a possible listing in late September or early October.
- The report followed earlier reporting that Anthropic had confidentially filed to go public.

Falsification note: these are reported preparations and possible dates, not a company guarantee. The chapter explicitly says the calendar can move and distinguishes confidential filing, public prospectus, and completed listing.

## Interpretive boundaries used in the chapter

- Primary issuance raises capital for the company and dilutes existing ownership percentages.
- Secondary sales transfer existing shares and increase tradable supply without themselves raising capital for the company or diluting other holders.
- Stock compensation can create dilution over time independently of whether employees sell immediately.
- Lockups constrain sale for a period; their expiration changes possible float but does not prove selling.
- The "IPO supply shock" is presented as a relative market-clearing argument: new investable claims matter when they grow faster than capital willing to own them at prevailing prices. The chapter does not claim that every IPO mechanically depresses a sector.
