# Chapter 3 research — depreciation and server useful lives

Working note for Chapter 3, **The Story Stock and the Spreadsheet**. This note records the sources behind the chapter's discussion of accounting life, physical life, and competitive economic life. It is not a claim that any disclosed useful-life estimate is improper; the point is that reasonable companies can reach different estimates because fleets, workloads, reuse paths, and expected technology transitions differ.

## Meta Platforms — server and network useful lives

**Primary source:** Meta Platforms, Inc., 2025 Form 10-K, filed with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission.

https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000162828026003942/meta-20251231.htm

Relevant disclosures:

- Meta extended the estimated useful lives of most servers and network assets to **5.5 years**, effective January 1, 2025.
- Meta states that useful lives are management estimates based on current facts and circumstances and may differ from actual useful lives because of changes in business operations, planned asset use, and technological advancement.
- Meta reported that the useful-life change reduced 2025 depreciation expense by **$2.92 billion** and increased net income by **$2.59 billion**, or **$1.00 per diluted share**.
- Meta reported 2025 property-and-equipment depreciation of $18.00 billion, including $13.36 billion for servers and network assets.

Corroborating SEC filing from the transition period:

https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000132680125000054/meta-20250331.htm

This filing states that most server and network asset lives were extended to 5.5 years effective January 1, 2025.

## Amazon — shorter lives for a subset of servers

**Primary source:** Amazon.com, Inc., 2025 Form 10-K, filed with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission.

https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000004/amzn-20251231.htm

Relevant disclosures:

- Amazon had increased the estimated useful life of servers from **five years to six years** effective January 1, 2024.
- Effective January 1, 2025, it changed the useful lives of a **subset of servers and networking equipment from six years to five years**.
- Amazon explicitly attributed the shorter lives to the **increased pace of technology development, particularly in artificial intelligence and machine learning**.
- The company's 2025 10-K lists server and networking equipment useful lives of five to six years because the fleet is not treated uniformly.

**Primary source for quantified 2024/2025 accounting effects:** Amazon.com, Inc., 2024 Form 10-K note on equipment useful lives.

https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872425000004/R10.htm

Relevant disclosures:

- The 2024 move from five-year to six-year server lives reduced 2024 depreciation and amortization expense by **$3.2 billion** and benefited net income by **$2.5 billion**.
- Amazon expected the 2025 shortening of useful lives for a subset of assets to reduce 2025 operating income by approximately **$0.7 billion** based on assets already on the balance sheet at December 31, 2024.
- Amazon recorded approximately **$920 million** of accelerated depreciation and related charges in Q4 2024 after deciding to retire certain servers and networking equipment early.
- The filing again attributes the shorter lives and retirement decisions to faster technology development, particularly AI and machine learning.

A later 2025 quarterly filing quantified the continuing effect:

https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872425000123/R8.htm

For the nine months ended September 30, 2025, Amazon said the useful-life change increased depreciation and amortization expense by $889 million and reduced net income by $677 million, primarily affecting AWS.

## Interpretation discipline

These disclosures support several narrower claims used in the chapter:

1. **Useful life is an estimate, not a physical constant.** The same broad category of computing infrastructure can carry different estimated lives across firms and within a firm's own fleet.
2. **Accounting life can move in either direction.** Meta lengthened most server/network lives in 2025 while Amazon shortened a subset during the same broad AI investment cycle.
3. **The effect can be material.** The disclosed changes moved annual depreciation, operating income, or net income by billions of dollars at hyperscaler scale.
4. **Opposite estimates are not proof of manipulation or error.** Fleet composition, workload placement, software efficiency, custom silicon, power economics, reuse opportunities, and planned retirement policies differ.
5. **Investors should distinguish physical, accounting, and competitive economic lives.** None of the SEC filings directly supplies a universal competitive-life estimate; that is an analytical layer that must be inferred from operating evidence.

## Falsification / counterevidence to preserve

- Longer useful lives can be economically justified if software improvement, workload migration, internal reuse, or better fleet management keeps older equipment productive.
- Shorter useful lives can be justified even when hardware remains physically functional if newer systems change cost-per-workload economics enough to make replacement rational.
- Depreciation is non-cash in the period recognized, but ignoring it entirely can overstate sustainable free cash flow when the underlying asset base requires recurring replacement.
- Conversely, treating depreciation as a perfect proxy for replacement capex can also be wrong when assets remain productive beyond accounting life or when capacity is still expanding rapidly.

The chapter therefore avoids calling either Meta's or Amazon's accounting treatment aggressive or conservative in isolation. The evidence is used to show why the assumption deserves explicit operating scrutiny in AI-era valuation models.
