# Chapter 2 — Bubble ingredients source notes

Research refresh for `manuscript/ch02-the-first-bubble-with-every-ingredient.md`.

## Scarcity and accelerated-computing demand

- NVIDIA, **CFO Commentary on Fourth Quarter and Fiscal 2025 Results**. Fiscal 2025 Data Center revenue was $115.2 billion, up 142% year over year. Fourth-quarter Blackwell revenue was $11.0 billion, and large cloud service providers represented approximately 50% of Data Center revenue. Used to support the chapter's claim that the early accelerator shortage coincided with extraordinary real demand rather than being merely promotional narrative.
  - https://investor.nvidia.com/files/doc_financials/2025/Q425/Q4FY25-CFO-Commentary.pdf

## Hyperscaler capital spending and strategic capacity

- Microsoft, **Fiscal Year 2026 First Quarter Earnings Conference Call**. Microsoft reported $34.9 billion of quarterly capital expenditures; roughly half was for short-lived assets, primarily GPUs and CPUs, with the remainder including long-lived data-center assets. Management tied the spending to cloud and AI demand. Used to distinguish genuine demand from the separate question of whether every layer earns an attractive return.
  - https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor/events/fy-2026/earnings-fy-2026-q1

- Microsoft, **Fiscal Year 2026 Fourth Quarter Earnings Conference Call**. Microsoft reported $41 billion of quarterly capital expenditures, with roughly two-thirds for short-lived assets, primarily CPUs and GPUs. Used to show the scale and persistence of strategic capacity investment by a cash-rich hyperscaler.
  - https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor/events/fy-2026/earnings-fy-2026-q4

## Geopolitical and resilience-driven capacity

- U.S. Department of Commerce, **CHIPS Incentives Award with Intel**, November 26, 2024. Commerce announced up to $7.865 billion in direct funding supporting Intel's U.S. leading-edge manufacturing expansion and framed the program in terms of supply-chain and national-security resilience.
  - https://www.commerce.gov/news/press-releases/2024/11/biden-harris-administration-announces-chips-incentives-award-intel

- U.S. Department of Commerce, **CHIPS Incentives Award with SK hynix**, December 19, 2024. Commerce announced up to $458 million in direct funding for high-bandwidth-memory packaging and R&D capacity in Indiana, explicitly describing HBM as a core AI supply-chain constraint.
  - https://www.commerce.gov/news/press-releases/2024/12/biden-harris-administration-announces-chips-incentives-award-sk-hynix

- U.S. Department of Commerce, **CHIPS Incentives Awards with Corning, Edwards Vacuum, and Infinera**, January 17, 2025. The awards targeted lithography materials, semiconductor-grade vacuum pumps, photonic fabrication, and advanced packaging, and Commerce described them as resilience and technological-leadership investments. Used to support the chapter's argument that strategic policy can rationally support redundant or domestic capacity even when the private return threshold alone would not.
  - https://www.commerce.gov/news/press-releases/2025/01/us-department-commerce-announces-chips-incentives-awards-corning

## Interpretation and counterevidence

These sources do **not** establish that AI infrastructure is in a bubble. They establish several of the chapter's narrower ingredients: extraordinary observed demand, unusually large capital commitments, short-lived hardware investment beside long-lived site investment, and explicit public-policy support for strategically redundant semiconductor capacity.

The countercase remains live: if demand is sufficiently elastic and AI becomes a durable platform layer, capacity that looks aggressive against current usage may later prove economical. The chapter therefore treats these facts as conditions that widen the range of possible capital-cycle outcomes rather than as proof of an imminent crash.
