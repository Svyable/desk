# Evidence ledger — THE OCEAN HEARD EVERYTHING

## Governing thesis

A sensor network built for one secret military ontology can become a high-value instrument for phenomena its designers did not care about. SOSUS and modern hydroacoustic treaty monitoring provide a documented positive control: submarine-detection and nuclear-test-monitoring infrastructure also records whales, volcanoes, earthquakes and ice. The book tests a broader proposition: **the more capable the sensor, the more dangerous it is to confuse classification with identification.** An unexplained contact is first a measurement problem, not permission to promote an ontology.

## Class 1 — verified / declassified / documented

- NOAA Ocean Exploration states that the U.S. Navy has operated SOSUS cabled hydrophone arrays since the 1960s for military purposes and offered civilian scientists dual-use access after the Cold War. NOAA says it has used those arrays since 1991 to detect northeast-Pacific submarine volcanic eruptions and blue-whale movements. It also notes restricted secure-facility access and an estimated SOSUS investment above $16 billion. Source: https://oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/technology/acoustics/
- Stafford, Fox & Clark, *JASA* 104 (1998), “Long-range acoustic detection and localization of blue whale calls in the northeast Pacific Ocean,” DOI 10.1121/1.423944. This is a core case for military infrastructure becoming a biological observatory.
- CTBTO states that low-frequency sound can propagate thousands of kilometres through the ocean, especially through the SOFAR channel; its hydroacoustic network uses eleven stations to monitor the world's oceans for nuclear explosions. Source: https://www.ctbto.org/our-work/monitoring-technologies/hydroacoustic-monitoring
- CTBTO completed the eleven-station hydroacoustic portion of the International Monitoring System in June 2017. Source: https://www.ctbto.org/resources/for-the-media/press-releases/ctbto-completes-hydroacoustic-part-international-monitoring
- CTBTO explicitly documents scientific reuse of IMS data for marine-mammal migration, climate, tsunami warning, volcanoes, earthquakes and ice. Sources: https://www.ctbto.org/resources/for-the-media/press-releases/nothing-escapes-global-ear-nuclear-tests-volcanoes and https://www.ctbto.org/news-and-events/news/whale-signals-detected-international-monitoring-system-ims-facility
- The U.S. Navy's SURTASS LFA environmental record documents a dedicated low-frequency sound research program in 1997–98 involving blue, fin, gray and humpback whales. It reports 33 playback experiments, more than 950 humpback sightings, 500 hours of passive acoustic data, exposure levels of 115–152 dB, and mixed behavioral responses. Source: https://www.nepa.navy.mil/Completed-Projects/SURTASS-LFA-Sonar/scientific-research/
- The Navy states that more than $16 million was spent on scientific research supporting the SURTASS LFA environmental review. Source: https://www.nepa.navy.mil/Completed-Projects/SURTASS-LFA-Sonar/environmental-impact/

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete

- Exact historical SOSUS detection ranges, array geometries, processing thresholds, false-alarm distributions and adversary-specific signatures remain partly classified or scattered across retrospective histories. Do not infer them from later civilian use.
- The degree to which particular biological discoveries were impossible without SOSUS, versus merely accelerated by access to it, needs case-by-case reconstruction.
- Claims about how often biological/geophysical sources appeared as initially unidentified military contacts require denominators from logs or operator histories, not anecdotes.
- The scientific value of classified archives may be large, but archive-retention policy, mission filtering and data destruction can make the surviving record systematically nonrepresentative.

## Class 3 — disputed / unconfirmed

- No unidentified hydroacoustic contact should be described as non-human technology, an unknown craft, or a novel biological organism without independent discriminating evidence.
- Claims that naval acoustic networks possess decisive hidden evidence for USOs/UAP are not established by the public evidence gathered here.
- Claims that low-frequency naval sonar caused any specific historical mass stranding require event-level causal evidence; association, exposure plausibility and mechanism are separate rungs.
- Acoustic anomalies circulated without raw waveform, array geometry, clock calibration, propagation model and alternative-source analysis remain anecdotes, not solved events.

## Class 4 — speculative synthesis / open conjecture

### Ontology lag
A sensor may acquire a signal before the institution possesses the category needed to identify it. Prediction: historical reclassification should cluster after cross-domain expertise or reference catalogues become available.

### Mission-shaped ignorance
The same sensitivity that makes a military array scientifically valuable can make its archive scientifically biased. Prediction: apparent source populations inferred from a military network should shift when corrected for array geography, passband, threshold, duty cycle, mission filtering and retention.

### Classification inversion
Secrecy can protect receiver characteristics while making outside interpretation of an anomalous signal worse. Declassification may therefore reduce mystery without adding new measurements simply by releasing geometry, calibration or processing context.

### Planetary-ear test
For extraordinary acoustic claims, require an end-to-end burden: source energy and spectrum → coupling into water → propagation path → receiver geometry/sensitivity → timing coherence → localization → repeatability → independent sensor agreement → alternative-source rejection. If a claim cannot survive this chain, “classified sensors saw it” adds prestige but not identification.

## Quantitative extraction burden

For every major event/case record: date/time standard; sensor coordinates where public; hydrophone depth; array aperture; sample rate/passband; source bearing/range estimate; sound-speed profile; SOFAR-axis depth; propagation-loss model; received level and reference units; signal duration; bandwidth; coherence across elements; localization uncertainty; false-alarm/background rate; candidate source classes; independent seismic/infrasound/radionuclide/satellite/visual corroboration; analyst access; raw-data availability; classification status; later reclassification.

For biological work add species, call type, source-level assumptions, seasonal distribution, number of detections, effort denominator and localization validation. For sonar bioeffects add transmitted source level, received exposure, distance, behavioral state, control periods, sample size, response definition, recovery time and confounds.

## Contradiction / incentive audit

- Navy incentive: preserve anti-submarine capability and receiver details; also demonstrate environmental compliance for active sonar.
- Scientific incentive: gain access to uniquely capable arrays; risk overclaiming novelty or ecological reach from mission-shaped samples.
- Treaty-monitoring incentive: demonstrate global verification sensitivity; must still discriminate natural and anthropogenic non-nuclear sources.
- Advocacy incentive: emphasize plausible biological harm from military sound; risk collapsing exposure, behavioral response, injury and population effect.
- Military/UAP narrative incentive: treat classified sensor provenance as a substitute for raw data. Explicitly prohibited in manuscript.

## Publication holds

1. Do not state exact SOSUS performance against Soviet submarines unless sourced to declassified/official records.
2. Do not convert “detected at long range” into a basin-wide universal range without source-level and propagation conditions.
3. Do not imply whale calls were routinely mistaken for submarines absent operator/log evidence.
4. Do not imply classified hydrophone archives contain UAP/USO evidence merely because they contain unidentified signals.
5. Do not turn Navy LFA exposure studies into proof of no harm or proof of population-level harm; preserve endpoints and denominators.
6. Distinguish passive SOSUS listening from active low-frequency sonar throughout.

## Defeat condition

The book's stronger thesis fails if dual-use scientific discoveries do not materially depend on military/treaty sensor capabilities after ordinary scientific instrumentation and sampling bias are accounted for, or if purported “ontology lag” cases reduce to ordinary analyst error with no measurable role for missing cross-domain categories. The narrower historical story remains true even if the general theory fails.
