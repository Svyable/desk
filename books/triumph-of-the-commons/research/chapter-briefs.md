# Chapter Briefs and Falsification Notes

## Chapter 1 — The Pasture in Our Heads

**Claim under test:** Cheap cognition changes the governance problem only if the institution around the commons is well designed.

**Evidence lane:** Hardin's tragedy framing; Ostrom's empirical work on durable commons and polycentric governance.

**What would make this false?** If digital/global commons consistently require either strict central ownership or fail under anonymous scale, the institutional analogy weakens.

## Chapter 2 — Altruism Had a Bandwidth Problem

**Claim under test:** A major altruistic dividend comes from shifting avoidable learning and compliance burden off individuals.

**Evidence lane:** Administrative-burden research; public service design practice.

**What would make this false?** If AI reduces agency cost but does not improve take-up, error rates, user time, or experienced burden, the claimed dividend is overstated.

## Chapter 3 — A Billion Invisible Volunteers

**Claim under test:** Agents can fill recurring coordination and support roles that are uneconomic to staff everywhere.

**Evidence lane:** NIST human-role governance as a control; evidence from shared digital-service infrastructure.

**What would make this false?** If oversight and integration costs rise roughly in proportion to agent scale, the 'invisible volunteers' metaphor fails economically.

## Chapter 4 — The Public Option for Intelligence

**Claim under test:** A public capability floor for useful intelligence can coexist with private competition.

**Evidence lane:** DPI and public-service component reuse; interoperability standards.

**What would make this false?** If a public option crowds out innovation, becomes chronically inferior, or creates a single coercive dependency, plural provision may be preferable.

## Chapter 5 — Where the Surplus Goes

**Claim under test:** The social result of automation depends on where productivity surplus is routed.

**Evidence lane:** Public-service reuse and cost-saving precedents; distribution is treated as a proposal rather than a measured fact.

**What would make this false?** If competitive markets reliably diffuse gains through prices and wages without deliberate surplus-routing institutions, the proposed mechanisms may be unnecessary.

## Chapter 6 — The Same Machine Can Accelerate Cruelty

**Claim under test:** The same scaling properties that make AI useful make abuse cheap, requiring defensive capacity and reversible authority.

**Evidence lane:** NIST AI RMF and playbook; provenance and risk-management practices.

**What would make this false?** If defensive systems create more surveillance, exclusion, or false positives than harm avoided, a broad defensive commons can backfire.

## Chapter 7 — The Commons Has Enemies

**Claim under test:** Commons fail through capture, abuse, maintenance debt, dependency, and bad governance more often than through lack of intelligence.

**Evidence lane:** Ostrom design principles; service lock-in and total-cost guidance.

**What would make this false?** If high-performing AI commons do not need participation, monitoring, conflict resolution, or exit, the governance thesis is too strong.

## Chapter 8 — The Commons That Already Won

**Claim under test:** Large knowledge commons already prove that distributed contribution can produce durable shared assets, but verification becomes the new scarcity.

**Evidence lane:** Wikimedia Foundation annual report and governance history.

**What would make this false?** If machine contribution increases correction workload faster than useful coverage, AI should be constrained to scouting, maintenance, or narrow assistance.

## Chapter 9 — The Million-Laboratory Planet

**Claim under test:** Open AI-generated scientific infrastructure can broaden the cognitive layer of research while leaving experiments and scarce facilities rival.

**Evidence lane:** AlphaFold Protein Structure Database and open-access model.

**What would make this false?** If access to instruments, samples, cohorts, or expert validation dominates outcomes, cheap cognitive assistance may have little effect on neglected research.

## Chapter 10 — Digital Rails Before Intelligent Apps

**Claim under test:** Intelligent public services work best on secure, reusable digital rails rather than as isolated apps.

**Evidence lane:** World Bank and UNDP DPI programs; GOV.UK Service Standard.

**What would make this false?** If shared rails amplify exclusion, privacy loss, or cascading errors more than they reduce duplication and burden, modular local systems may be safer.

## Chapter 11 — Disaster Week

**Claim under test:** Disasters reveal the value of common situational awareness and interoperable contribution.

**Evidence lane:** Humanitarian OpenStreetMap Team's Haiti response record.

**What would make this false?** If machine assistance produces proprietary or unverified crisis intelligence that local responders cannot correct or use, it may reduce rather than improve coordination.

## Chapter 12 — The Bureaucracy That Learns

**Claim under test:** AI can help bureaucracies learn by preserving institutional memory, surfacing repeated failure, and reducing process burden.

**Evidence lane:** GOV.UK whole-service and continuous-improvement principles; administrative-burden literature.

**What would make this false?** If automation mainly accelerates enforcement and throughput while appeals, exceptions, and policy correction stagnate, the 'learning bureaucracy' claim fails.

## Chapter 13 — A Civilization That Can Practice

**Claim under test:** Civilizations can rehearse failure and use AI to expand scenario coverage before real crises.

**Evidence lane:** FEMA HSEEP exercise/evaluation doctrine.

**What would make this false?** If synthetic scenarios systematically distract from observed hazards or overwhelm institutions with low-quality edge cases, simulation may create false preparedness.

## Chapter 14 — The Capability Treasury

**Claim under test:** A Capability Treasury could fund and steward reusable public cognitive infrastructure as a portfolio.

**Evidence lane:** DPI shared-foundation model and public procurement analogies; proposal is explicitly normative.

**What would make this false?** If pooled capability funds become politically captured, technologically stale, or unable to allocate better than decentralized buyers, the institution should be rejected.

## Chapter 15 — The Altruism Market

**Claim under test:** Matching, verification, and maintenance can make altruistic capacity discoverable without reducing help to one metric.

**Evidence lane:** Knowledge-commons and public-service component registries as partial analogies.

**What would make this false?** If contribution markets invite gaming, distort local priorities, or spend more on verification than they create in value, direct provision is better.

## Chapter 16 — The One-Percent Civilization

**Claim under test:** A small share of rising AI capability could finance large public benefits only if full physical and maintenance costs are counted.

**Evidence lane:** IEA Energy and AI; total-cost-of-ownership service guidance.

**What would make this false?** If energy, hardware, staffing, and replacement costs erase the expected savings, the one-percent framing is rhetoric rather than economics.

## Chapter 17 — The Commons Stack

**Claim under test:** A reusable stack of identity, provenance, permission, knowledge, tools, evaluation, human escalation, and contribution can make public intelligence portable.

**Evidence lane:** GOV.UK open standards/common components; DPI interoperability principles.

**What would make this false?** If standardization creates a monoculture or central gatekeeper whose failures propagate globally, federation should take priority over a shared stack.

## Chapter 18 — Rights in a World of Cheap Intelligence

**Claim under test:** Cheap intelligence requires cognitive civil rights that are exercisable through actual procedures.

**Evidence lane:** NIST trustworthiness properties; UN/UNDP DPI safeguards.

**What would make this false?** If contestability, human review, privacy, and non-digital access cannot be delivered at workable cost, high-stakes AI deployment should remain narrower.

## Chapter 19 — What Humans Are For

**Claim under test:** As routine cognition becomes cheaper, the comparative value of human judgment, care, presence, accountability, and refusal rises.

**Evidence lane:** Administrative-burden evidence and NIST human-AI role guidance provide constraints; much of the chapter is normative.

**What would make this false?** If institutions use AI primarily to intensify monitoring and workload rather than release human time, the predicted prestige and work shift will not occur.

## Chapter 20 — The Great Reinvestment

**Claim under test:** Abundant intelligence can create positive-sum shared capital when outputs are reusable and institutions reinvest the gains.

**Evidence lane:** Ostrom governance, Wikimedia-scale knowledge production, public digital infrastructure, and open-standard reuse.

**What would make this false?** If reusable cognitive output fails to generate durable shared capability or the gains remain concentrated, the book's central reinvestment thesis is false.
