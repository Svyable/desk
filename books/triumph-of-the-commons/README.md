**When AI Begins Contributing at Scale to the Global Well-Being of the World—and Altruism Accelerates Like Never Before.**

The central thesis is that artificial intelligence changes the economics of helping. Translation, tutoring, navigation, scientific search, administrative support, planning, evaluation, and institutional memory can become cheap enough to operate as shared capability rather than scarce professional attention. But abundance does not distribute itself. The book asks what institutions would be required to turn cheap cognition into durable global well-being without building a new machinery of surveillance, capture, dependency, or automated cruelty.

The argument begins with the tragedy of the commons and Elinor Ostrom's correction to it, then moves through public intelligence, digital public infrastructure, scientific and humanitarian commons, financing, maintenance, rights, adversarial defense, and the human role in a civilization where useful cognition is no longer the binding constraint it once was.
