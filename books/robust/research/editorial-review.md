# Robust — Editorial Review

**Opened:** 2026-09-03  
**Completed:** 2026-09-03  
**Standard:** `.agents/skills/human-prose/SKILL.md`  
**Reference voice:** existing Sven Hardy Benson narrative nonfiction in Desk, including *Predictable*.

## Diagnosis before intervention

The manuscript’s argument, evidence discipline, and chapter differentiation were strong enough to preserve. The main voice risk after rapid expansion to thirty-one chapters was **cadence density** rather than factual or structural weakness.

The pre-pass manuscript audit found:

- 66,937 words across 35 manuscript files;
- zero exact repeated prose paragraphs across files;
- one exact repeated sentence across chapters (`Exit is not the only form of power`, in Chapters 20 and 24);
- many chapters with long runs of paragraphs six words or shorter, including a nineteen-block run in Chapter 12 and a fourteen-block run in Chapter 31;
- no markdown blockquotes and no obvious imitation-attribution patterns;
- quotation marks used primarily for hypothetical dialogue, example language, product/policy terms, and short phrases rather than long reproduced source prose;
- no pseudo-equations or arbitrary mathematical frameworks detected in the manuscript scan.

Compared with the calmer paragraph cadence of existing Sven Hardy Benson narrative nonfiction in Desk, the draft overused the visual rhythm of one-line fragments. The problem was cumulative: many individual fragments worked, but long repeated runs made the book sound more generated and keynote-like than authored.

## Intervention rule

The editorial pass was deliberately conservative.

1. Preserve claims, examples, chapter order, headings, evidence, and the six-property robustness thesis.
2. Preserve deliberate short punches and the closing cadence of Chapter 20.
3. Consolidate only **runs of four or more consecutive prose blocks of seven words or fewer**, grouping them into ordinary paragraphs with a modest upper size bound.
4. Leave markdown lists, tables, code, and headings structurally untouched.
5. Make only explicit copy/continuity corrections discovered in the full-manuscript review:
   - change Chapter 20’s premature `final argument` wording to `core argument`;
   - remove the one exact duplicated `Exit is not the only form of power` sentence by consolidating the Chapter 20 version;
   - replace Chapter 25’s premature `because this book ends` line;
   - correct several lowercase sentence starts created during fast drafting;
   - correct `glamourous` to `glamorous` in Chapter 14.
6. Re-run the manuscript, repetition, catalog, and Reader audits after the pass.

## Post-pass result

The deterministic cadence pass landed as a manuscript-only edit and was then checked against the same publication audit used before the intervention.

- Final manuscript count after the pass: **66,938 words across 35 manuscript files**.
- Exact repeated prose paragraphs across files: **0**.
- Exact repeated cross-chapter sentences: **0**.
- The longest non-deliberate run of very short prose blocks fell materially; the remaining conspicuous short-line run is the intentional closing cadence of Chapter 20.
- Continuity wording in Chapters 20 and 25 no longer presents either chapter as the literal end of the full manuscript.
- A final spot-check of Chapters 1, 5, 12, 20, 31, and the back matter found the consolidated paragraphs readable and consistent with the calmer paragraph cadence of the reference voice.
- The post-pass catalog/Reader audit remained healthy.

## What this pass was not

This was not a rewrite into generic smooth prose. It did not erase Sven Hardy Benson’s short-sentence texture, argument-first voice, or recurring interest in mechanisms and tradeoffs. It did not add decoration, invented scenes, new authority, new frameworks, or unsupported certainty.

The standard was not maximum polish. The standard was a manuscript that reads as one writer making deliberate rhythmic choices rather than a draft defaulting to the same short-paragraph machinery everywhere.
