# THE LOUDEST THING

## What an alien astronomer could actually know about us

Sven Hardy Benson

Drafting.

This book uses one civilization we know exists—ours—as the control experiment for technosignatures. It does not ask what advanced civilizations might build until it has priced what present-day Earth actually leaks, reflects, emits, pollutes, illuminates, launches, and deliberately transmits.

The epistemic rule is simple: detectability is not existence. A nondetection excludes only the signal class, power, geometry, duty cycle, distance, wavelength, and observing time the search could have seen.

Evidence classes used throughout:

1. **Verified/documented:** measured terrestrial emissions; published instrument sensitivities; actual SETI observations; observed interstellar objects; laboratory spectroscopy and atmospheric chemistry.
2. **Credible but incomplete:** modeled detectability of terrestrial technologies and proposed technosignatures under stated assumptions.
3. **Disputed/unconfirmed:** any claim that an observed astronomical source is technological without independent confirmation.
4. **Speculative synthesis:** the book's arguments about visibility bias, civilization silhouettes, and how search programs may accidentally select for particular technological histories.
