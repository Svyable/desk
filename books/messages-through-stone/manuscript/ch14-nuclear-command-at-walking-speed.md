# Nuclear Command at Walking Speed

The communications system belonged to a force built around speed of consequence and slowness of detection. Ballistic missiles could cross oceans in minutes. The submarines carrying them survived by moving quietly through enormous spaces. The ELF link connecting national command to those boats operated at a data rate that feels prehistoric beside the weapons it served.

This mismatch is the book's strangest image. Advanced systems do not necessarily advance together. A nuclear deterrent can depend on a channel whose useful payload is tiny because reliability under a peculiar physical constraint matters more than throughput.

That should make us cautious when judging old military technology by consumer metrics. Faster is not always better. More bandwidth is not always the objective. A strategically crucial message may consist of very little information if it arrives where other signals cannot.

The Cold War built machines at radically different tempos and then joined them into one command system. Project ELF was the slow nerve attached to a very fast weapon.
