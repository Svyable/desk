# Granite

There is a temptation to describe Project ELF as a radio built on granite. More accurately, it was a radio whose design had to care about what lay under the trees.

The conductivity structure of the ground influences how current injected through the electrodes spreads. The Navy evaluated sites not only as parcels but as electrical environments. This is one of those details that makes old classified-adjacent infrastructure feel alien after the fact. The map of suitable places is partly a geologic map.

That produced a collision of scales. Nuclear command-and-control was being decided in places where local people thought in terms of timber, wells, hunting land, taxes, property, and county jurisdiction. The Navy thought in terms of attenuation, current paths, survivability, antenna geometry, and global reach.

Neither map canceled the other. The technical map explained why engineers kept coming back to the upper Midwest. The civic map explained why those returns became conflict. Granite did not merely support the antenna. It selected the communities that would have to live with the argument.
