# What the Monitoring Found

By the 1990s the ELF ecological monitoring program had accumulated years of work. The National Research Council reviewed eleven studies. Its broad conclusion was that the monitoring program had not demonstrated obvious adverse ecological effects attributable to operation of the system.

That sentence has to be kept intact. A book about secrecy loses credibility the moment it treats an inconvenient negative finding as merely another layer of concealment.

Some investigators believed particular biological changes might have occurred. The committee did not treat those observations as a coherent demonstration of ecological harm. It examined design, exposure assessment, statistics, and interpretation. That distinction between an isolated effect and a replicated adverse ecological pattern is the hinge.

The strongest version of the Project ELF story therefore cannot be that the Navy built a machine that science later proved was poisoning the forest. The record we have does not support that claim. The stronger story is about what happens when a strategically necessary system produces enough uncertainty and distrust that ecological monitoring becomes part of its operating legitimacy.
