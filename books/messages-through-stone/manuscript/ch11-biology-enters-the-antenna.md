# Biology Enters the Antenna

Once Project ELF became an environmental controversy, biology was pulled into a strategic communications program.

The Navy sponsored studies of plants, animals, ecological communities, and field exposure near the facilities. This research acquired an impossible social burden. Supporters wanted reassurance. Opponents wanted independent proof that an unusual military electromagnetic environment was harmless. Scientists had to answer a narrower question: what effects could actually be detected under the study designs available?

This is where the evidence classes matter. A reported difference in one ecological variable is not automatically evidence of injury. A null result is not proof that no possible effect exists. Multiple comparisons create false positives. Poorly characterized exposure can blur real effects. Statistical significance can arrive without biological importance.

The National Research Council's later review is useful precisely because it resists both propaganda shapes. It found no obvious adverse ecological effects such as unusual population changes or large-scale mortality during monitoring, while also identifying weaknesses and recommending stronger independent review. That is less emotionally satisfying than a verdict. It is better science.
