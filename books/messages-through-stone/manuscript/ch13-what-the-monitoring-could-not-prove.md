# What the Monitoring Could Not Prove

Negative evidence has structure. A study can fail to find an effect because no effect exists, because the effect is too small, because exposure was mismeasured, because the outcome was badly chosen, because statistical power was inadequate, or because noise buried the signal.

The National Research Council review repeatedly returns to design. It recommended independent evaluation of reanalyses and laboratory work and noted opportunities to strengthen integration across studies. That does not reverse the overall finding. It defines its boundaries.

Public controversies often destroy this middle ground. One side says no demonstrated harm means proven safety. The other says methodological limitation means the negative findings are worthless. Both positions are easier to chant than defend.

Project ELF deserves a stricter sentence: the monitoring record did not show obvious adverse ecological effects during the period studied, and some aspects of the evidence were not strong enough to close every narrower question. That is not indecision. It is a description of what the evidence can carry.
