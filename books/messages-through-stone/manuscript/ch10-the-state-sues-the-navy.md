# The State Sues the Navy

In 1984 the dispute reached federal court. Wisconsin and Marquette County, Michigan challenged the Navy's environmental review. The resulting opinions are unusually valuable because they preserve both administrative history and the collision between national defense claims and environmental procedure.

The litigation should not be flattened into heroes versus secrecy. The Navy had a genuine strategic mission. State and local challengers had legitimate questions about whether the environmental record was adequate for a project that imposed fields, currents, construction, and ecological uncertainty across large areas.

NEPA did not require a judge to design submarine communications. It required the federal government to examine environmental consequences through a legally recognizable process. That is a profound change in how a classified-adjacent military technology meets the public. The system could not be justified solely inside the chain of command.

The case also shows why infrastructure is different from a secret aircraft. You can hide performance specifications. You cannot make every pole, easement, current path, and environmental study disappear. Physical scale creates procedural visibility.
