# The Infrastructure of Invisibility

Stealth is usually pictured on the platform: coatings, quiet machinery, shape, depth, tactics. Project ELF relocates part of invisibility onto land.

A submarine could remain harder to detect because people hundreds or thousands of miles away accepted the cost of a communications architecture designed around that concealment. Forest corridors, transmitters, electrodes, power, environmental studies, court fights, local opposition, and federal budgets all sat upstream of the boat's quiet patrol.

This changes how strategic systems should be drawn. The submarine is not the whole weapon system. Neither is the missile. A credible deterrent depends on command, authentication, communications, maintenance, training, navigation, intelligence, political authority, and infrastructure whose connection to the final weapon may be invisible to the public.

Project ELF made one hidden dependency unusually physical. To keep a submarine hidden, the Navy built something that could not be.
