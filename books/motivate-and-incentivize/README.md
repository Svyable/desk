# Motivate & Incentivize

Most books about motivation begin inside the person: purpose, discipline, desire, grit, meaning.

Most systems begin outside the person: quotas, bonuses, fines, rankings, commissions, deadlines, badges, promotions, penalties, defaults, prices.

*Motivate & Incentivize* is about what happens when those two worlds meet.

The working thesis is that incentives do more than change the payoff attached to an action. They redirect attention, define what counts, reveal what an institution actually values, and sometimes change the meaning of the behavior itself. A well-designed incentive can unlock effort, attract the right people, and make progress visible. A badly designed one can produce astonishing performance against the wrong target.

This is not an argument against money, metrics, goals, or rewards. Incentives often work. The harder problem is that they work through humans, organizations, and increasingly machines that learn the game they have actually been given rather than the purpose somebody intended.

The book follows that problem from bank sales floors and piece-rate factories to schools, health behavior, compensation systems, online platforms, status games, and AI reward functions. Its practical question is simple to state and difficult to answer: how do you create a system that makes the behavior you genuinely want easier to choose without teaching people to game the proxy?
