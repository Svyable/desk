# Motivate & Incentivize

**Sven Hardy Benson**

---

A company can tell people to care about customers and pay them to care about accounts opened.

A school can say it values learning and reward the score.

A hospital can say it values health and reimburse the procedure.

A platform can say it wants meaningful connection and optimize the click.

None of these systems needs a villain to drift. The drift can begin with a reasonable question: how will we know whether the work is happening?

Then somebody chooses a number.

The number makes performance visible. Visibility makes comparison possible. Comparison makes reward possible. Before long, the number is no longer describing the work. It is organizing the work.

That is the territory of *Motivate & Incentivize*.

Motivation is usually treated as a property of the person. Incentives are treated as levers applied from outside. In practice, each changes the other. A bonus can increase effort or make a task feel purchased. A fine can deter behavior or turn a social obligation into a price. A quota can focus a team or narrow its vision until everything outside the quota becomes somebody else's problem. A target can reveal excellence. It can also teach people exactly where to cheat.

The interesting question is therefore not whether incentives work.

They do.

The interesting question is what, exactly, they teach a person to optimize—and what disappears from view when the optimization begins.
