# Evidence Matrix — The Ocean Was Listening

## Class 1 — verified / documented

- **SOSUS / Project Caesar existed as a U.S. Navy undersea-surveillance architecture.** Official Navy IUSS history publicly attributes implementation to the Project Caesar Navy/industry team. Source: https://www.csp.navy.mil/cus/About-IUSS/Father-of-SOSUS/
- **The system's purpose was anti-submarine surveillance.** Navy/IUSS and later institutional histories document this mission.
- **Scientific access expanded around 1990–91 as the Cold War ended.** WHOI's AHA history says the Navy gave NOAA access to North Pacific SOSUS arrays in October 1990 and NOAA data systems began operation in August 1991. WHOI's narrative history describes mission declassification in 1991. These are compatible milestones, not necessarily identical events. Sources: https://www.whoi.edu/ocean-learning-hub/ocean-topics/ocean-tech/acoustics/autonomous-hydrophone-array-aha/ and https://www.whoi.edu/oceanus/feature/ears-in-the-ocean/
- **Hydroacoustic monitoring was used for seafloor seismicity.** WHOI describes PMEL use of Northeast Pacific SOSUS to detect low-level seismic waves at the Juan de Fuca Ridge.
- **The 1997 Bloop was a real hydroacoustic event.** NOAA PMEL preserves the signal and comparison material.
- **NOAA's current interpretation is cryogenic.** PMEL says the broad-spectrum Bloop sounds are consistent with icequakes from large icebergs and notes later Antarctic icequakes with very similar spectrograms. Source: https://www.pmel.noaa.gov/acoustics/sounds/bloop.html
- **Ice events can propagate over ocean-basin distances.** NOAA states icequakes can be detected on multiple sensors at ranges over 5,000 km.

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete

- Exact historical boundaries between scientific access, mission declassification and release of technical specifications require a primary-document chronology.
- The 52-Hz call record supports an unusual whale-like acoustic source tracked over time, but source identity and biological interpretation remain incomplete without direct identification.
- Operator recollections about biological/geological 'clutter' may be historically useful but must be separated from contemporaneous logs and released operational records.

## Class 3 — disputed / unconfirmed

- The Bloop as a gigantic unknown animal. NOAA's comparison evidence now supports icequake attribution; monster claims lack equivalent positive evidence.
- The 52-Hz source as literally unable to communicate with other whales or demonstrably 'lonely.' Frequency alone cannot establish social isolation or subjective state.
- The proposition that a signal detected on a military-origin network is itself military, artificial or classified in origin.
- Any claim that unresolved public hydroacoustic signals establish unknown technology or unknown megafauna without a denominator-complete comparison against known source classes.

## Class 4 — speculative synthesis / open conjecture

- **Secret-sensor inversion:** public anomalous-event narratives may over-ascribe mystery to the source when the missing information is concentrated in sensor geometry, processing, sensitivity and comparison libraries.
- **Accidental-observatory thesis:** large security infrastructures can become scientifically valuable because persistent sensors accumulate environmental baselines no purpose-built civilian program could initially afford.
- **Anomaly half-life hypothesis:** as labeled comparison libraries grow, many apparently exotic acoustic anomalies should become classifiable faster; events that resist this process become more—not less—interesting.

## Contradictions and incentives

- Military institutions have incentives to conceal sensor performance even when concealing the environmental source is unnecessary.
- Scientific institutions have incentives to publish novel signals and mechanisms, which can bias attention toward unusual events.
- Media have incentives to anthropomorphize individual animals and maximize unresolved mystery.
- Cryptozoological communities have incentives to preserve biological interpretations of high-amplitude unknown sounds.
- Debunking communities can overcorrect by treating a plausible class attribution as event-level proof. For Bloop, NOAA's later comparison evidence is strong, but manuscript language should preserve the distinction between consistency/classification and eyewitness-level source capture.

## Defeat condition

The book's core argument should be revised if primary records show that scientific reuse of SOSUS was marginal rather than consequential, or if a denominator-complete audit finds that mature hydroacoustic networks retain a large, stable class of high-quality unexplained signals despite geometry, raw data and relevant comparison libraries.
