*How Civilization Finds Its Next Move*

Civilization is a search process operating under constraint. Minds, companies, markets, laboratories, governments, investors, artists, and machines all confront possibility spaces far larger than the time, money, attention, and risk budget available to inspect them.

The book asks a different question from the usual stories about intelligence and strategy: not only *what does a system know?* but *what does it search?* It follows the economics and psychology of exploration, evaluation, stopping, local maxima, institutional memory, consumer choice, scientific experimentation, venture portfolios, markets, culture, geopolitics, and machine-generated possibility.

The central argument is that as generating options becomes cheaper, advantage migrates toward the systems that can decide which possibilities deserve attention, test them against reality, preserve useful failure, and commit without becoming trapped by their own prior searches.
