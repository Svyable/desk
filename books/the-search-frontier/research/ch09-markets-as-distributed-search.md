# Chapter 9 research note — Markets as Distributed Search

## Verified anchors

- Friedrich A. Hayek, “The Use of Knowledge in Society,” *American Economic Review* 35(4), September 1945, pp. 519–530. Used for the dispersed-knowledge problem and the price system as a compressed mechanism for coordinating adjustments without requiring every participant to know the underlying cause. Verified against the 1945 bibliographic record and reproduced paper text from Liberty Fund/Econlib.
- Vernon L. Smith, “Experimental Auction Markets and the Walrasian Hypothesis,” *Journal of Political Economy* 73(4), 1965, pp. 387–393. Used for controlled experimental evidence that market institutions can exhibit equilibrating behavior under specified auction rules. Verified against Chapman University’s institutional record and abstract.
- Vernon L. Smith, “Markets as Economizers of Information: Experimental Examination of the ‘Hayek Hypothesis’,” *Economic Inquiry* 20(2), April 1982, pp. 165–179, DOI 10.1111/j.1465-7295.1982.tb01149.x. Used for the explicit experimental-information framing and the importance of institutional rules. Verified against Wiley’s publisher record and Cambridge’s later reprint summary.

## Falsification / counterevidence note

The chapter does not treat market prices as complete information or profit as a social-welfare score. Its strongest counterclaim is that decentralized actors can still make correlated errors, omit external costs and needs without purchasing power, suppress entry through market power, or converge on a distorted metric. The argument therefore narrows to a conditional one: markets can be powerful distributed search institutions when participation, entry, feedback quality, liability, and institutional rules keep rival experiments possible and consequential.
