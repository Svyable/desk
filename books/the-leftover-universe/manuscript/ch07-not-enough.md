# Not Enough

There is a category of disappointment peculiar to physics: finding exactly the phenomenon you needed and discovering it is quantitatively useless.

CP violation exists.

The electroweak theory can violate baryon plus lepton number at high temperature.

The early universe was out of equilibrium at various moments.

Sakharov's checklist appears, at first glance, to be lying on the Standard Model's kitchen counter.

And yet the Standard Model does not make our universe.

That sentence needs qualifiers. The Standard Model plus general relativity and a cosmological initial state describes an astonishing amount of what happened after the earliest moments. It predicts particle interactions with extraordinary precision. Combined with the standard cosmological framework, it supports successful accounts of nucleosynthesis, recombination, structure formation and much else. “Does not make our universe” here means something narrow: with the measured Higgs mass and known CP violation, it does not provide a viable mechanism that generates the observed baryon asymmetry from a symmetric initial state.

The failure has two famous pieces.

The electroweak phase transition is not violent enough.

The available CP violation is not large enough.

Both statements became firmer through experiments that were not designed as simple baryogenesis tests.

The Higgs boson is central. Before its discovery, electroweak baryogenesis inside or near the Standard Model remained more open because the nature of the electroweak phase transition depends on the Higgs mass. For a sufficiently light Higgs, finite-temperature effects could in principle produce a first-order transition in which bubbles of broken phase nucleate and expand. Such a transition supplies the nonequilibrium surfaces baryogenesis can exploit.

The Higgs discovered at CERN in 2012 has a mass near 125 GeV.

At that mass, calculations and lattice studies indicate the Standard Model electroweak transition is a crossover, not the strong first-order transition required by the simplest electroweak baryogenesis picture. There is no dramatic bubble wall doing the necessary work in the minimal theory.

The universe changed phase too politely.

That is a deliberately anthropomorphic sentence, so let me repair it: the equilibrium free-energy structure of the Standard Model at the measured parameters does not yield the needed discontinuous transition.

New fields can change the thermal potential. Add a scalar singlet. Extend the Higgs sector to multiple doublets. Introduce supersymmetric partners. The electroweak transition may become first order. But every addition has consequences. Higgs couplings can shift. New particles can appear at colliders. Vacuum stability changes. EDMs can grow. Flavor observables move.

A baryogenesis model is never allowed to alter only the chapter where it solves the problem.

The second failure is CP violation.

The CKM matrix contains a complex phase and produces experimentally observed CP asymmetries. But the effective amount of CP violation available in the high-temperature electroweak plasma is drastically suppressed. One measure is the Jarlskog invariant multiplied by products of quark mass-squared differences. Normalized to the electroweak temperature scale, the resulting dimensionless quantity is tiny compared with the baryon asymmetry we need.

The exact comparison depends on the calculation, so slogans like “ten orders of magnitude too small” should be treated as pedagogical scale statements rather than universal constants. The robust conclusion is that CKM CP violation cannot drive successful electroweak baryogenesis in the Standard Model.

This is an important distinction between existence proofs and magnitude proofs.

Kaons proved CP violation exists.

B factories proved the CKM mechanism accounts for large classes of it.

Cosmology demands enough integrated asymmetry under specific thermal conditions.

These are different achievements.

The inability of known CP violation to account for baryogenesis is one of the strongest reasons physicists expect additional sources of CP violation somewhere beyond the Standard Model, if the asymmetry was generated dynamically through Sakharov-type mechanisms.

“Expect” is not “have observed.”

The history of particle physics is littered with expected new physics that failed to appear on schedule. Supersymmetry was expected by many to show up at the LHC. So were various compositeness scenarios, extra dimensions and dark-matter particles. Null results are not embarrassing footnotes. They are the shape of the frontier.

Baryogenesis therefore lives in an uncomfortable position. It is an observational fact demanding explanation in a theory that otherwise works spectacularly well. The obvious extensions are constrained. The remaining possibilities range from elegant to baroque, accessible to effectively inaccessible.

One can respond by multiplying models.

One can also ask what kind of evidence would deserve belief.

Suppose the LHC or a future collider discovers a second Higgs-like scalar. That would be electrifying. It would not establish electroweak baryogenesis. The new scalar's couplings would need to be measured. The finite-temperature potential would need reconstruction or reliable inference. New CP violation would need identification. EDM limits would enter. Cosmological consistency would matter.

Suppose LISA detects a stochastic gravitational-wave background with a spectrum compatible with a first-order electroweak-scale phase transition. Again, extraordinary. Still not enough. Many early-universe transitions at hidden scales could produce gravitational waves. One would need cross-evidence tying the transition to the electroweak sector and CP physics.

Suppose an electron EDM is discovered.

That would demonstrate new CP violation beyond the tiny Standard Model expectation. It would be one of the most important particle-physics discoveries in decades. Yet translating the EDM into the baryon asymmetry would still require a model. The same operator responsible for the EDM may or may not have been active in the needed thermal process.

Suppose DUNE measures a large leptonic CP phase.

This would show neutrino and antineutrino oscillation probabilities differ in vacuum terms governed by the PMNS phase. It would strengthen the case that the lepton sector contains substantial CP violation. It would not prove high-scale leptogenesis, because the phases controlling heavy-neutrino decays can be independent of the low-energy phase measured in oscillations.

The universe does not owe us one observable per mystery.

This is why “not enough” is more than a calculation. It is an epistemic discipline.

We need enough asymmetry physically and enough evidence scientifically.

The two thresholds can be confused. A model can generate the correct baryon abundance with adjustable parameters and still have little evidentiary support. Conversely, an experiment can discover a new CP-violating effect and still leave the cosmic mechanism underdetermined.

The correct abundance is not a magic validation code.

There is a deep problem of degeneracy. Many mechanisms can be tuned or naturally arranged to produce a baryon-to-entropy ratio near the observed value. The asymmetry is one number. A theory can contain many parameters. Unless the mechanism predicts additional correlated observables, fitting the number is cheap.

A serious baryogenesis program therefore tries to overconstrain itself.

Electroweak baryogenesis is attractive partly because it can sit near experimentally accessible energies. New scalars might appear at colliders. Higgs couplings might deviate. EDMs might be seen. A first-order transition might generate gravitational waves. The parameter space can be attacked from multiple directions.

High-scale thermal leptogenesis is elegant in a different way. Heavy Majorana neutrinos can simultaneously help explain small neutrino masses through the seesaw mechanism and generate a lepton asymmetry. But the simplest versions may put the heavy states far above direct reach. Evidence then becomes more circumstantial: neutrino mass patterns, Majorana nature, lepton-number violation, cosmology, perhaps flavor structure.

Affleck-Dine baryogenesis exploits scalar fields carrying baryon or lepton number in supersymmetric or related frameworks. It can generate large asymmetries through coherent field dynamics. Its signatures depend on the broader model and can involve Q-balls, dark matter or gravitational waves.

GUT baryogenesis uses decays of ultraheavy particles associated with grand unification. Such theories often predict proton decay, making underground detectors part of the test.

Asymmetric dark matter models relate the visible baryon asymmetry to a corresponding asymmetry in the dark sector. The rough coincidence that dark matter carries about five times the cosmological mass density of baryonic matter becomes potentially meaningful: perhaps both abundances arise from linked asymmetries rather than independent accidents. Again, a family of possibilities, not a result.

There are mechanisms involving sterile-neutrino oscillations at GeV scales, sometimes called ARS leptogenesis after Akhmedov, Rubakov and Smirnov. Unlike ultraheavy thermal leptogenesis, these can place new neutral leptons within reach of intensity-frontier experiments or collider searches. Experiments such as SHiP proposals, LHC detectors, beam dumps and precision flavor studies can probe relevant parameter space.

There are models where primordial black holes participate in baryogenesis through evaporation or out-of-equilibrium environments. Models where axion-like fields create chemical potentials for baryon or lepton number. Models tied to inflation or reheating. Models using dark-sector phase transitions. Models exploiting neutrino mass generation in different ways.

The abundance of mechanisms is a warning against narrative certainty.

Physics has not selected a winner.

That does not mean all mechanisms are equally plausible. Some are ruled out in broad forms. Some require less tuning. Some solve multiple problems with one structure. Some are experimentally accessible. Some fit naturally within theories motivated independently. Reviews weigh these virtues constantly. But there is no accepted baryogenesis mechanism analogous to stellar nucleosynthesis, where the principal processes are established and observed consequences line up across domains.

The asymmetry is measured. The genesis is not.

This gap has an interesting relation to the hierarchy of confidence in cosmology.

We can be highly confident that ordinary matter exists.

We can be highly confident that the observable universe contains vastly more matter than antimatter on large scales.

We can infer the baryon density precisely from the CMB and nucleosynthesis.

We know CP violation exists in weak interactions.

We know neutrinos oscillate and therefore have mass.

We know the Higgs mass and much about electroweak symmetry breaking.

Then the confidence gradient steepens. Which baryogenesis mechanism operated? Unknown. At what temperature? Unknown. Which new particles participated? Unknown. Which CP phases mattered? Unknown.

A good book should let the gradient remain visible.

There is a bad habit in explanatory writing: because uncertainty makes prose feel less authoritative, the writer rounds it away. “Scientists believe X caused Y.” A field with six live mechanisms becomes one smooth sentence. The reader is protected from the interesting part.

The interesting part is that our existence rests downstream of a gap in the account.

Not a mystical gap where anything may be inserted. A bounded gap with numbers on both sides.

On one side: the Standard Model and measured cosmological abundance.

On the other: Sakharov's requirements and a zoo of candidate mechanisms.

Between them: not enough.

There is another “not enough” problem hiding in the strong interaction. QCD allows CP violation through the theta term, yet the neutron EDM tells us the effective theta parameter is astonishingly small. Theoretical room for CP violation exists where nature appears not to use it.

This contrast is almost rude.

The weak sector violates CP but cannot make enough baryons.

The strong sector could violate CP much more visibly but apparently does not.

The lepton sector may violate CP substantially, but the amount is not yet settled.

Beyond the Standard Model may contain exactly what baryogenesis needs, but precision experiments keep finding nothing.

A universe built from asymmetry is remarkably coy about its asymmetries.

This coyness gives human measurement an odd role. We are searching for excesses and absences at once.

An excess of matter over antimatter.

An excess of one decay rate over its conjugate.

An absence of proton decay.

An absence of EDMs.

An absence of antimatter domains.

An absence of strong CP violation.

An uncertain excess of neutrino over antineutrino oscillation probability.

The story is not “physics found asymmetry.” It is a map of where asymmetry is allowed, observed, suppressed and missing.

Maps of absence require patience.

The neutron EDM experiment does not become meaningless after a decade of null results. It becomes a tighter contour. Proton decay detectors do not “fail” each year the water remains quiet. They erase ranges of model space. Collider searches for new scalars do the same. Cosmological data rule out thermal histories.

A great deal of frontier physics is the art of making nothing informative.

That statement applies to the baryon asymmetry in a second sense. The counterfactual is nearly nothing: if symmetric matter and antimatter annihilated without a residue, ordinary material structure would be absent. We are trying to explain a small deviation from that null outcome by collecting null results now.

There is some dry humor in this.

A leftover civilization constructs billion-dollar machines to understand why it was not zero.

The temptation is to answer with anthropic reasoning. Obviously we find ourselves in a universe with enough matter to support observers because otherwise no observers would ask. This is true as a selection statement and empty as a mechanism. It cannot tell us whether leptogenesis or electroweak baryogenesis occurred. It cannot predict an EDM. It cannot choose a neutrino mass ordering.

The anthropic fact is downstream of the physical problem.

The physical problem remains quantitative.

How large is the asymmetry?

What interactions produce it?

At what rate?

At what temperature?

What washes it out?

What survives to recombination?

Which observables today remember the process?

Numbers are the cure for inspirational cosmology.

They are also what makes the insufficiency undeniable. If Standard Model CP violation were merely aesthetically unsatisfying, theorists could argue taste. If a crossover electroweak transition were merely less dramatic than bubbles, writers could prefer drama. But calculations say the ingredients do not yield the observed baryon abundance.

The cosmos has graded the model.

This is one of the reasons baryogenesis is such a clean frontier despite its complexity. There is a target value. Whatever happened has to account for a net baryon asymmetry compatible with observations. It cannot produce a universe with equal bulk matter and antimatter. It cannot overproduce baryons wildly without other adjustments. It cannot destroy nucleosynthesis. It cannot contradict the CMB.

The residue is small enough to be demanding.

The rest of the book will follow the candidate places where enough might be hiding.

The baryon sector itself, where new CP violation has finally been observed directly in a decay but not at cosmological strength.

The neutrino sector, where mass already proves the minimal Standard Model incomplete and where leptogenesis offers a route from invisible particles to visible matter.

The Higgs sector, where a hidden first-order transition could have written baryon number on bubble walls.

The strong sector, where missing CP violation points toward axions.

The sky, where antimatter searches test whether the asymmetry is truly global.

The laboratory, where antihydrogen is trapped and dropped.

None is enough yet.

That is not a conclusion to apologize for.

It is the frontier's address.
