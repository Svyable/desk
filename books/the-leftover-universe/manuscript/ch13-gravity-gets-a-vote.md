# Gravity Gets a Vote

For decades antimatter had fallen only in arguments.

The arguments were mostly one-sided. General relativity says freely falling test bodies follow spacetime geometry without a sign attached to their baryon number. Quantum field theory joined to gravity gives no casual permission for an antiproton to accelerate upward while a proton accelerates downward. CPT symmetry and energy conservation make simple “antigravity” stories difficult. Indirect constraints were already strong.

Still, there is a difference between a theory making a prediction and an antiatom falling in an apparatus.

In 2023 ALPHA-g made the latter happen.

The experiment was a direct test of the gravitational behavior of antihydrogen. Trapped antiatoms were released in a vertically oriented magnetic apparatus. The collaboration recorded where they annihilated as magnetic confinement was weakened. If antihydrogen experienced ordinary attractive gravity, more atoms should escape downward under appropriate release conditions. If it experienced an equal and opposite acceleration—if antimatter “fell up”—the distribution would reverse.

It did not reverse.

Within the precision of the first measurement, antihydrogen behaved consistently with ordinary downward gravitational acceleration and excluded the simplest repulsive antigravity scenario.

This result was simultaneously unsurprising and historic.

Unsurprising because almost no mainstream framework expected a kilogram of antimatter, if one could make such a thing, to shoot toward the ceiling in Earth's field.

Historic because the gravitational force on neutral antimatter had finally been observed directly rather than inferred from consistency.

Physics needs both kinds of knowledge.

The gap between them can last a long time when the experiment is vicious.

Gravity is weak. On a single hydrogen atom near Earth's surface, the gravitational force is around \(10^{-26}\) newtons. Electromagnetic forces on a charged particle can exceed that by absurd factors. Antihydrogen must therefore be neutral enough that stray fields do not impersonate gravity. Magnetic fields used to trap it also exert forces because the antiatom has a magnetic moment. Thermal motion dominates unless the atoms are extremely cold.

To watch gravity move an antiatom, first remove almost everything stronger than gravity.

That is harder than it sounds because almost everything is stronger than gravity.

A tiny electric potential can overwhelm the effect. A magnetic-field gradient can steer the atom. A residual gas collision can end it. Vibrations, detector geometry and the initial velocity distribution matter. The ALPHA-g apparatus was built vertically because gravity needs a direction along which the trap can be opened symmetrically enough to compare escape upward and downward.

The experiment is a lesson in subtraction again.

Remove charge effects.

Reduce kinetic energy.

Model magnetism.

Suppress collisions.

Then gravity, the weakest familiar interaction, gets a vote.

The first result did not measure the gravitational acceleration of antihydrogen to the precision of ordinary falling-object experiments. Its uncertainty was broad. That is normal for a first direct measurement. The important achievement was entering the regime where the sign and rough magnitude could be tested.

Future measurements can narrow it.

Other CERN experiments pursue the same question differently. AEgIS aims to measure the gravitational acceleration of antihydrogen using pulsed production and interferometric or deflection techniques. GBAR plans to create antihydrogen ions, sympathetically cool them to ultralow temperatures with ordinary ions, remove the extra positron and let the resulting neutral antihydrogen free-fall.

Different engineering, same absurd objective: drop the undroppable.

Why should a book about baryogenesis care?

Because exotic gravitational behavior has often been offered as a shortcut around the matter-antimatter asymmetry. Perhaps matter and antimatter repel gravitationally and segregate. Perhaps antimatter hides in regions gravity pushed away. Perhaps antigravity prevents annihilation domains.

Direct antihydrogen gravity measurements close the simplest versions of that escape.

They do not prove every imaginable modified-gravity model false. Sophisticated theories can include equivalence-principle violations, new long-range forces or composition-dependent couplings while satisfying existing constraints. But the burden grows. Any viable deviation has to fit ALPHA-g, precision tests of the equivalence principle with matter, astrophysical observations, CPT-related constraints and cosmology.

The easy ceiling is gone.

This is how a mature mystery behaves. The more we learn, the less room remains for flamboyant explanations.

The antiatom falls down.

Its spectrum matches hydrogen.

Its antiproton has the expected mass and charge.

Known CP violation is real but insufficient.

The problem concentrates.

Gravity also reminds us that the asymmetry problem occurs inside a universe where matter is not the dominant gravitating substance.

Baryons contribute only about five percent of the present cosmic energy budget in the standard cosmological model. Dark matter contributes roughly a quarter. Dark energy accounts for most of the rest. The exact percentages depend on cosmological fits, but the hierarchy is robust.

The visible residue is a minority component.

Yet baryons dominate the luminous structures we recognize directly because they interact electromagnetically. Gas shocks, cools and radiates. It forms disks. Atoms make spectral lines. Stars ignite. Dark matter shapes gravitational wells but does not, as far as we know, form tables.

This distinction between gravitational importance and chemical importance matters.

A baryon asymmetry solves only one piece of the inventory. A complete origin story for the universe also needs dark matter or a replacement account of the phenomena attributed to it, dark energy or modified cosmic dynamics, inflation or another account of initial perturbations, and whatever high-energy completion quantum gravity requires.

Baryogenesis is not “the reason anything exists.”

It is a reason ordinary material structures exist.

The title of this book is deliberately provocative because human language calls those structures “things.” A photon is a thing too. A black hole is a thing. A quantum field is not nothing. If baryons had annihilated completely, the universe would still contain physical reality.

What would vanish is the kind of persistent, complex, electromagnetically structured matter from which known observers are built.

That is enough of a problem without stealing all of ontology.

Gravity's vote therefore has two roles. It tests whether antimatter is anomalous under the interaction that organizes cosmic structure. And it keeps baryons in perspective as one gravitating component among several.

The large-scale universe formed hierarchically. Dark matter overdensities grew under gravity, providing wells into which baryonic gas fell. The gas could dissipate energy and collapse more deeply. Stars formed. Feedback from stars and black holes reheated and expelled some gas. Galaxies emerged through a negotiation between collisionless dark matter and dissipative baryons.

The baryon asymmetry supplied the gas.

Gravity supplied the architecture.

Dark matter supplied much of the scaffolding.

No single ingredient gets to claim the city.

This is another cure for human-centered cosmology. If we ask only what conditions were necessary for us, every necessary ingredient can be made to sound like destiny. The better habit is causal decomposition.

What did the baryon asymmetry enable?

A net inventory of ordinary matter.

What did gravity do?

Amplified density differences and bound structures.

What did electromagnetism do?

Allowed cooling, chemistry and radiation.

What did nuclear physics do?

Made stars and elements.

What did biological evolution do?

Something incomparably later and messier.

The chain contains necessity without intention.

Antimatter gravity tests also illuminate the equivalence principle. Einstein's equivalence principle says, roughly, that locally the effects of a uniform gravitational field are indistinguishable from acceleration, and that freely falling bodies follow trajectories independent of internal composition under general relativity. Precision experiments with ordinary matter have tested universality of free fall to extraordinary levels. The MICROSCOPE satellite, for example, compared accelerations of test masses of different composition and found no violation at roughly parts in \(10^{15}\).

Antimatter is composition difference taken to an extreme.

Does an antiatom with opposite internal charges but the same inertial mass follow the same gravitational acceleration?

ALPHA-g says yes within its much coarser present precision.

The difference in precision is not embarrassing. Ordinary matter can be machined into macroscopic test masses and dropped, suspended or orbited. Antimatter arrives atom by atom after a chain of accelerators and traps. Experimental difficulty sets the frontier.

There is a long-term possibility that antihydrogen gravity becomes a precision equivalence-principle test rather than a sign check. Laser cooling, improved magnetic control and larger samples can reduce uncertainty. GBAR's ultracold strategy aims at very low initial velocities. Every improvement asks whether matter-antimatter equality survives another digit.

If a deviation appeared, the consequences would spread wildly.

One would need to ask whether the effect violates CPT, the equivalence principle, Lorentz invariance or introduces a new force. Cosmology would have to be recalculated. Tests with neutral mesons, spectroscopy and gravitational redshifts would become relevant. The baryogenesis story might change because particle and antiparticle trajectories in the early universe could differ under new fields or curvature.

This is why precision null tests are not boring.

They guard foundational assumptions that entire explanatory chains use silently.

A baryogenesis calculation usually assumes antimatter gravitates normally because the early universe's expansion responds to stress-energy, not to a sign convention that sends antiparticles away. If that premise failed, many standard thermal histories would need revision.

ALPHA-g placed a direct experimental brick under the premise.

There is also a lovely experimental asymmetry in how the result is observed. The antihydrogen does not bounce onto a scale. It annihilates when it reaches the trap wall. Gravity is inferred from the spatial distribution of destruction.

The antiatom's final event tells us how it fell.

A detector surrounding the trap reconstructs charged pions from antiproton annihilation, locating the vertex. The sample's death becomes its trajectory measurement.

Antimatter experiments are full of this inversion. To know where the antiatom was, wait until it ceases to be an antiatom.

That would be unbearable as a metaphor, so we will leave it as instrumentation.

The result also corrects a public misconception fed by science fiction. Antimatter is not negative mass. The prefix “anti” refers to quantum numbers, not gravitational sign. A positron has positive electric charge where an electron has negative charge, but both have positive inertial mass. An antiproton has negative electric charge where a proton is positive, but again positive inertial mass. General relativity couples to energy-momentum.

No standard equation says opposite electric charge implies upward fall.

The persistence of the antigravity idea reveals how strongly language shapes intuition. Anti-money is debt. Antidote opposes poison. Antimatter therefore sounds like matter with all signs reversed.

Physics is less linguistically obedient.

Some signs reverse. Mass does not, as measured. Magnetic moments reverse appropriately with charges and spin conventions. Spectra remain equal under CPT. Gravity appears attractive.

The mirror is selective.

This selectivity is exactly why baryogenesis requires careful symmetry language. C is not P. CP is not T. CPT is not “everything reversed.” Baryon number is not electric charge. Lepton number is not mass. A process can violate one and preserve another.

The early universe did not choose “matter” as a package of all positive properties.

It generated a net sign in particular quantum numbers.

Humans later named the surviving sign positive in some cases because we lived inside it.

The proton's charge is \(+e\) because Benjamin Franklin's older convention and later particle discoveries fixed a sign choice, not because baryons are metaphysically positive. Had Franklin reversed his convention, electromagnetism would work identically with all charge signs swapped.

Likewise the statement “positive baryon asymmetry” contains convention. The invariant physical fact is unequal populations.

This should make us suspicious whenever a cosmic asymmetry gets narrated as victory.

Matter did not deserve to win.

Antimatter did not lose because it was unstable.

Both are locally stable under the same conditions when isolated from opposites, subject to the same decay structure for corresponding states except CP-violating probability differences in appropriate unstable systems.

The difference is historical inventory.

Gravity helps preserve that neutrality of interpretation. It pulls antihydrogen down with the same indifferent geometry.

If an anti-Earth existed in an otherwise empty region, general relativity says it would curve spacetime like Earth. An anti-apple would fall onto anti-ground. Anti-astronomers could develop Newtonian gravity from anti-cannonballs. Nothing about their mechanics would announce that our sign is “normal.”

The normality comes from neighborhood.

Cosmology is the study of how neighborhoods became global.

Could the universe be globally baryon symmetric while our observable patch is matter dominated? Inflationary models can in principle generate domains or separate regions with different baryon signs. The observational issue is whether antimatter domains lie beyond our horizon or sufficiently far away that annihilation boundaries are hidden. If so, local measurements cannot establish the global sign.

This is a legitimate logical possibility and a reminder that “the universe is matter dominated” usually means the observable universe and nearby large-scale structure.

A region beyond the cosmological horizon is, by definition, not currently observable. Statements about its baryon sign are model-dependent.

Our ignorance has a horizon.

That does not make nearby antimatter galaxies likely. Gamma-ray constraints and cosmic-ray searches make large domains within the observable universe difficult. But the global topology of baryon number could remain unknowable in principle if inflation separated domains farther than light can reach.

The leftover may be local on a scale larger than everything we see.

This possibility will return when we search the sky for antihelium.

For now, the important result is smaller and harder.

An antiatom made in Europe fell toward Earth.

The universe did not distinguish it with gravity strongly enough for us to see.

One more flamboyant answer disappeared.

The residue problem became quieter.
