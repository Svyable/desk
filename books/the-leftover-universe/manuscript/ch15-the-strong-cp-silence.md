# The Strong CP Silence

The strong interaction is allowed to violate CP and, as far as we can tell, almost refuses.

This is one of the universe's more perverse bookkeeping choices.

Quantum chromodynamics, QCD, is the theory of quarks and gluons. It explains the force that confines quarks inside protons and neutrons and binds nuclei indirectly through residual interactions. Its gauge structure permits a term in the Lagrangian that violates P and CP, multiplied by an angular parameter usually called \(\theta\).

Nothing obvious in bare QCD requires this parameter to be tiny.

Experiment says the physically relevant combination is tiny beyond reason.

The strongest familiar constraint comes from the neutron electric dipole moment. If the QCD theta angle were moderately large, the neutron would possess an EDM far above current limits. Decades of experiments have found no such EDM. The implied effective theta angle is less than roughly \(10^{-10}\).

A dimensionless parameter could naturally be order one.

Nature writes ten zeros.

Why?

That is the strong CP problem.

Put it next to baryogenesis and the mismatch becomes almost comic. The early universe seems to require additional CP violation beyond what the Standard Model's weak quark sector provides. Meanwhile the strong sector contains an allowed source of CP violation that must be suppressed to nearly nothing.

Physics has too little asymmetry in one drawer and an unopened bottle in another.

You cannot simply pour the second into the first. A large QCD theta angle today is excluded. The mechanisms and thermal histories differ. Still, the juxtaposition suggests that CP violation is not a single global dial. It is structured by fields, symmetries and dynamics.

One sector can break CP.

Another can dynamically erase it.

A third may hide phases in particles too heavy to see.

The Peccei-Quinn mechanism is the most famous proposed explanation for QCD's silence.

Roberto Peccei and Helen Quinn introduced a new global symmetry, now called \(U(1)_{PQ}\), that is spontaneously broken. The effective theta parameter becomes a dynamical field rather than a fixed arbitrary number. Nonperturbative QCD effects generate a potential whose minimum lies at the CP-conserving value. The field relaxes toward that minimum in the early universe.

The strong interaction solves its own embarrassment dynamically.

The mechanism predicts a new pseudo-Nambu-Goldstone boson: the axion.

Frank Wilczek and Steven Weinberg independently recognized the particle consequence in 1978. The original “visible” axion models were quickly constrained. Later invisible axion models, especially KSVZ and DFSZ constructions, allowed a much higher symmetry-breaking scale and much weaker couplings.

Then cosmology made the axion more interesting.

The axion field in the early universe can begin displaced from the minimum of its potential. As the universe cools and the axion mass becomes relevant, the field starts oscillating. Those coherent oscillations behave like cold dark matter. Axions can also be produced through topological defects such as strings and domain walls depending on when Peccei-Quinn symmetry breaks relative to inflation.

A proposed solution to a missing CP violation became a candidate for missing mass.

The connection is not decorative. It ties the allowed axion mass and abundance to cosmic history. Different assumptions about inflation, reheating and defect contributions produce different preferred ranges.

Experiments search accordingly.

ADMX uses a resonant microwave cavity in a strong magnetic field. Galactic axions passing through can convert into photons via the axion-photon coupling when the cavity frequency matches the axion mass. The signal would be a tiny excess of microwave power at a narrow frequency set by the local dark-matter velocity distribution.

The detector tunes through frequencies like a radio waiting for a station whose broadcast is the halo.

HAYSTAC uses related haloscope principles at different frequencies and quantum-noise techniques. MADMAX proposes dielectric disks to enhance conversion at higher masses. ORGAN, CAPP experiments and many others cover additional ranges. ABRACADABRA and DMRadio target ultralight axions through oscillating electromagnetic effects. CAST and the planned IAXO helioscope search for axions produced in the Sun by converting them in laboratory magnetic fields.

A theoretical angle in QCD has become an industry of magnets.

No axion has been confirmed.

The parameter space is enormous because axion-like particles more general than the QCD axion can span many masses and couplings. Experimental exclusions advance in wedges rather than a single closing net.

Why does this belong in a book about leftover baryons rather than dark matter?

Because the strong CP problem is the negative image of baryogenesis.

Both ask why a symmetry violation has the magnitude it does.

For baryogenesis: why enough CP violation in the early universe to create \(10^{-10}\)-scale net baryon abundance?

For strong CP: why less than \(10^{-10}\) in a parameter that could have been large?

The same order-of-magnitude coincidence should not be overread; the numbers refer to different quantities and do not imply a direct relation. But their coexistence is conceptually instructive. Nature is not “slightly asymmetric.” It is selectively asymmetric.

Selection demands mechanism.

There are models that connect axion dynamics directly to baryogenesis. An axion or axion-like field changing in time can couple derivatively to baryon or lepton currents, acting like an effective chemical potential that biases particle over antiparticle production. This family includes spontaneous baryogenesis and axiogenesis scenarios. In some constructions, axion rotation in field space supplies both dark matter-related dynamics and the baryon asymmetry.

These ideas are active research, not established history.

They are valuable because they show what becomes possible once a symmetry-solving field is dynamical in the early universe. The axion is not only a particle to detect; it is a clock-like background that can carry initial conditions and angular motion.

A field can remember a direction.

That direction can become charge.

Again, the poetry is dangerous. We need the actual couplings.

Spontaneous baryogenesis typically uses a time-dependent scalar field coupled to a baryon current. The changing field shifts the free energy of states carrying positive and negative baryon number, creating an effective chemical potential. If baryon-number-violating interactions are active, the plasma develops an asymmetry. When those interactions freeze out, the asymmetry remains.

The mechanism can satisfy Sakharov's spirit in a generalized way, sometimes avoiding the conventional requirement for explicit CP violation because the time-dependent background itself selects a direction and can break CPT spontaneously or effectively.

This is why textbook lists of three conditions have footnotes.

The universe can be more inventive than the first theorem statement.

Axion dynamics may also generate helical gauge fields or interact with electroweak sphalerons. Theoretical variants are numerous. None has yet earned the status of the QCD axion mechanism itself, which remains unconfirmed but tightly motivated by the strong CP problem.

The strong CP silence also motivates another famous proposal: perhaps the up quark is exactly massless, allowing theta to be rotated away. Precision lattice QCD and hadron phenomenology rule this out; the up quark has nonzero mass.

A clean escape closed.

This is typical of good problems. Simple solutions die first.

The remaining explanations include Peccei-Quinn symmetry, spontaneous CP structures, Nelson-Barr models and other mechanisms that arrange theta to be naturally small. Each creates its own new particles, symmetries or flavor signatures.

The silence has architecture.

Neutron EDM experiments are the microphone.

A neutron EDM can receive contributions not only from QCD theta but from many CP-violating operators beyond the Standard Model. Improving the limit therefore attacks both strong CP and baryogenesis candidates. A future nonzero EDM would not immediately identify its source; measurements across neutrons, protons, deuterons, atoms and molecules could help disentangle operator structures.

There are proposals for storage-ring EDM experiments using charged particles. Instead of trapping neutral neutrons, one circulates protons or light nuclei in carefully configured electric and magnetic fields and looks for a tiny spin precession out of the orbital plane. Different systems have different sensitivity to underlying CP-violating sources.

A ring can become a spectrometer for symmetry.

The experimental challenge is severe because ordinary magnetic fields mimic EDM signals. A false field millions of times smaller than Earth's can matter. Counter-rotating beams, field reversals, comagnetometers and geometric controls are built into designs.

Fundamental asymmetry must be distinguished from a crooked laboratory floor.

This is a recurring pattern across the book. We search for a cosmic preference while surrounded by local preferences we created accidentally.

Detector material favors one interaction cross section over another.

Earth's magnetic field bends charges.

Proton colliders begin with matter rather than antimatter.

Neutrino beams have different fluxes in neutrino and antineutrino mode.

Gravity points down.

The scientific method is partly a machine for removing our asymmetries from nature's.

That is a better human-role thesis than “observers create reality.”

Observers create bias constantly.

Experimentalists spend careers subtracting it.

The strong CP problem itself may someday connect directly to the baryon asymmetry through a discovered axion. Imagine a sequence of results:

A haloscope detects a narrow axion-like dark-matter signal whose mass and coupling fit a QCD axion relation.

Independent experiments confirm it.

Astrophysical observations are consistent.

A theory of Peccei-Quinn breaking predicts a particular early-universe field history.

Gravitational-wave or cosmological signatures constrain topological defects.

A baryogenesis mechanism using axion dynamics predicts the observed baryon abundance and some correlated EDM or collider signature.

At that point two silences—missing strong CP and missing antimatter—could share a mechanism.

We are nowhere near that chain today.

The correct response is neither dismissal nor announcement. It is to notice that frontier physics advances by building possible bridges and then loading them with measurements until most collapse.

Strong CP is a load-bearing test because it is so cleanly unnatural in the naive theory.

Technical naturalness is a specialized concept. A small parameter is called technically natural if setting it to zero increases the symmetry of the theory, making quantum corrections proportional to the small parameter rather than driving it large. The QCD theta problem is not simply that \(10^{-10}\) looks aesthetically tiny. It is that the Standard Model gives no obvious symmetry reason for the required value after accounting for quark mass phases.

Peccei-Quinn makes the smallness dynamical rather than accidental.

This distinction matters whenever cosmology invokes tiny numbers. Small numbers are not inherently suspicious. The electron Yukawa coupling is small compared with the top quark's. Neutrino masses are tiny. The cosmological constant is catastrophically small in Planck units. What requires explanation depends on radiative stability, symmetry and theoretical structure, not visual dislike of decimals.

The baryon asymmetry's smallness is different again. The number is not a Lagrangian parameter but a historical outcome. A tiny final abundance can arise from ratios of masses, loop factors, out-of-equilibrium efficiencies and exponential washout.

One ten-to-the-minus-ten can be natural where another is not.

Numbers do not carry philosophical labels.

The human brain wants to link them because coincidence feels expensive. Physics requires an interaction term.

This chapter's title, then, is not suggesting the strong interaction has been silent about baryogenesis. It is silent in a specific experimental sense: a CP-violating effect allowed by QCD has not appeared.

That silence may be telling us a new field exists.

If the field is the axion, the invisible dark matter halo may be the dynamical residue of QCD cleaning up its CP phase.

Our baryonic bodies would then live inside another kind of leftover: coherent field oscillations from a symmetry-breaking event.

Two residues occupying the same galaxy.

One visible, one dark.

One created by an asymmetry we need to explain.

One perhaps created by a mechanism that explains an asymmetry we do not observe.

That is sufficiently strange without embellishment.

The universe may be composed less of substances than of unresolved bookkeeping.
