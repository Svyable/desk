# Sakharov's Three Doors

In 1967 Andrei Dmitrievich Sakharov published a short paper whose title, in translation, is usually rendered along the lines of “Violation of CP Invariance, C Asymmetry, and Baryon Asymmetry of the Universe.” It is hard to improve on the efficiency of that title. The paper connected three things that were only beginning to look like parts of one problem: the observed dominance of matter, symmetry violations in particle physics, and the possibility that particle reactions in an expanding universe could create the imbalance dynamically.

Sakharov is better known outside physics for what happened to him later. He helped develop the Soviet hydrogen bomb, became an increasingly public critic of nuclear testing and political repression, won the Nobel Peace Prize, was exiled internally to Gorky, and returned to Moscow under Gorbachev. That biography can overwhelm a technical paper. It should not be turned into foreshadowing, as though a man who worried about political asymmetry was destined to write equations about baryons. History does not need that kind of tidiness.

The physics can stand without the allegory.

Sakharov asked what would be required if the early universe began with no net baryon number and nevertheless evolved into the baryon-dominated world we observe. His answer is conventionally summarized in three conditions:

Baryon number must be violated.

C and CP symmetries must be violated.

The relevant processes must occur out of thermal equilibrium.

A modern expert can immediately add qualifications. There are generalized scenarios, subtleties involving CPT, departures from the simplest equilibrium assumptions, and quantum kinetic effects. But the three conditions remain an extraordinarily durable map. Every proposed mechanism is forced to explain where it gets its asymmetry, how it changes the relevant charges, and why the asymmetry does not wash out.

Three doors. None opens cheaply.

Start with baryon number.

Baryons include protons and neutrons and the wider family of three-quark hadrons. In ordinary low-energy reactions, baryon number appears conserved. Assign baryons \(B=1\), antibaryons \(B=-1\), and mesons \(B=0\). Chemical reactions do nothing to it. Nuclear reactions rearrange baryons but do not casually erase net baryon number. A proton can sit inside hydrogen for times so long that human history is irrelevant.

If baryon number were an exact unbreakable conservation law and the universe began at \(B=0\), no sequence of ordinary reactions could end at \(B>0\). The ledger would balance forever.

So a dynamical origin from zero requires some way around that conservation.

This sounds like a request for lawlessness. It is not. Conservation laws in modern physics are not all equal in status. Electric charge appears exactly conserved in the Standard Model. Baryon number and lepton number emerge as accidental global symmetries of the renormalizable Standard Model Lagrangian rather than being imposed as gauge symmetries. Nonperturbative electroweak effects violate \(B+L\), while conserving \(B-L\). At ordinary temperatures those processes are fantastically suppressed. At temperatures above the electroweak scale in the early universe, sphaleron transitions can be active.

The word sphaleron deserves a better reputation. It sounds like a villain from a children's space opera. It is instead a type of unstable field configuration associated with transitions between different electroweak vacuum sectors. The important cosmological consequence is that these transitions can change baryon and lepton numbers.

The Standard Model, then, already contains a route through Sakharov's first door in the hot early universe.

This does not solve baryogenesis. It complicates the bookkeeping helpfully.

Because electroweak sphalerons violate \(B+L\) but preserve \(B-L\), an asymmetry produced in lepton number can be partially converted into baryon number. This is the bridge that makes leptogenesis possible. A heavy particle decay can first bias leptons over antileptons; sphalerons redistribute that asymmetry into the baryon sector before electroweak processes freeze out.

Matter can inherit its imbalance.

This is one of the more elegant inversions in the subject. The question “why are there more protons than antiprotons?” may have an answer whose crucial actors are neutrinos or other leptonic states. The visible residue could be secondhand.

Now the second door: C and CP violation.

C symmetry asks whether physics is unchanged when particles are replaced by antiparticles. Weak interactions famously violate C. A universe in which baryon-number-violating reactions treat particles and antiparticles exactly the same would still struggle to generate a net preference. If a process produces baryons slightly more often than antibaryons, its charge-conjugate process cannot occur at exactly the same rate and erase the advantage.

But C violation alone is insufficient in the standard argument. CP must be violated as well. Otherwise symmetry-related processes involving opposite helicities or spatial configurations can compensate, leaving no net baryon asymmetry after summing over states.

The experimental fact of CP violation was only three years old when Sakharov wrote.

James Cronin and Val Fitch's 1964 neutral-kaon result had shown that CP was not exact in weak interactions. This was an astonishment. The combined operation had been hoped to rescue a deeper symmetry after parity and charge conjugation individually failed. Nature declined the rescue.

Sakharov could therefore point to a real crack in the mirror.

The crack was not large enough.

We now understand quark-sector CP violation through the CKM matrix with impressive precision. A complex phase in the mixing of quark flavors allows decay amplitudes to interfere differently for matter and antimatter. Kaons, B mesons, D mesons and, now experimentally, certain baryons display CP-violating differences. But the amount and form of CP violation in the Standard Model are insufficient by many orders of magnitude for successful baryogenesis under the known electroweak history.

This is a subtle kind of scientific frustration: the required phenomenon exists, but in the wrong dosage.

Imagine discovering a leak in a dam after a valley floods, then calculating that the leak could have filled a bathtub. You have found the right category of failure and not the cause of the landscape.

New physics baryogenesis models therefore often introduce additional CP-violating phases. Two-Higgs-doublet models, supersymmetric models, singlet extensions and other constructions can supply new sources. Leptogenesis can obtain CP violation from phases in neutrino-related couplings. Grand unified theories create other channels. Each proposal has to survive the accumulating precision bounds from colliders, flavor physics and electric dipole moment searches.

The asymmetry has too much evidence for the Standard Model and too little direct evidence for what comes next.

The third door is departure from thermal equilibrium.

This one can seem like a technical afterthought. It is not. If reactions and their inverses occur in perfect equilibrium with all the relevant distributions adjusted, CPT and unitarity impose relationships that prevent the system from simply accumulating a net baryon number under the usual assumptions. The bias needs a temporal ratchet.

Heavy-particle decay is the clean textbook example. Suppose an unstable species \(X\) can decay through baryon-number-violating channels and its antiparticle ̅\(X\) has slightly different branching fractions because of CP violation. If \(X\) decays while abundant and out of equilibrium—perhaps because the universe expands faster than inverse reactions can restore the population—a net baryon number can remain.

The mechanism is conceptually simple enough to fit on a blackboard. Making it consistent with a real particle theory and every cosmological constraint is another matter.

Electroweak baryogenesis uses a different source of nonequilibrium: a first-order electroweak phase transition. Bubbles of the broken-symmetry phase nucleate and expand. Particles interacting with the bubble walls experience CP-violating effects that bias densities. Sphalerons in the unbroken phase convert those biases into baryon number. Inside the broken phase, sphaleron transitions must be sufficiently suppressed so the new asymmetry is not erased.

A successful bubble wall has two jobs: write and protect.

The measured Higgs boson mass makes the Standard Model electroweak transition a crossover rather than a sufficiently strong first-order transition. Again the known theory almost supplies the ingredients but not in the required form. New scalar fields or modified Higgs sectors can produce a first-order transition, which is why precision Higgs measurements can have cosmological stakes.

Leptogenesis often uses out-of-equilibrium decays of heavy right-handed Majorana neutrinos. At temperatures high enough to produce them, these states decay into leptons and Higgs particles with CP-violating rate differences. If the decays occur sufficiently out of equilibrium and washout is controlled, a net lepton asymmetry survives. Electroweak sphalerons then convert part of it to baryon number.

The heavy neutrinos in the simplest thermal leptogenesis scenarios may live at energies far beyond direct accelerator reach. This is scientifically awkward and not fatal. A theory can be tested through lower-energy consequences, consistency relations and cosmology without manufacturing every actor. But the farther the decisive states recede from direct access, the more important it becomes not to mistake elegance for confirmation.

Sakharov's conditions are sometimes presented as if they were a recipe: add one cup baryon violation, two tablespoons CP violation, shake out of equilibrium, bake until galaxies form. They are closer to constraints on recipes. They tell you what any certain class of successful mechanism must accomplish. They do not tell you which ingredients nature used.

There is another reason the conditions endure. They turn a metaphysical-sounding question into a research program.

Why is there something rather than nothing?

Philosophers can spend centuries negotiating what “nothing” means.

Why is the baryon-to-photon ratio positive and of order \(10^{-10}\)?

Now you can build apparatus.

The narrowing is not an evasion. It is one of science's characteristic powers. Replace the unbounded question with a measurable residue, then ask what dynamics can produce it.

Proton decay searches attack the first door. If a proton can decay, baryon number is not absolute in the way ordinary experience suggests. Grand unified theories often predict decay channels such as \(p \rightarrow e^+ \pi^0\) or \(p \rightarrow \bar{\nu} K^+\), depending on the model. Super-Kamiokande's enormous water Cherenkov detector has pushed lifetime limits beyond \(10^{34}\) years for important channels. Hyper-Kamiokande will increase exposure dramatically.

A lifetime limit that dwarfs the age of the universe sounds absurd until you remember statistical ensembles. You do not wait beside one proton for \(10^{34}\) years. You watch an enormous number of protons for a human-scale time. If the process exists with a given probability, a sufficiently large target gives you a chance.

Ten kilotons of patience can interrogate a symmetry.

Neutron-antineutron oscillation searches approach baryon violation differently. A free neutron transforming into an antineutron would violate baryon number by two units. In a nucleus, analogous processes can lead to annihilation signatures. New proposed experiments seek improved free-neutron sensitivity using intense beams, long flight paths, magnetic shielding and annihilation detectors.

If observed, the phenomenon would be immediate evidence that the ordinary categories of matter and antimatter are less separated than the persistence of a neutron suggests.

Electric dipole moments attack the second door. The neutron EDM, electron EDM and EDMs of atoms and molecules are exquisitely sensitive to CP-violating new physics. Many baryogenesis scenarios that introduce new phases also predict EDMs. The failure to observe them has already killed or squeezed simple parameter choices.

This is one reason “new CP violation” cannot simply be inserted wherever a model needs it. Nature sends invoices.

Collider searches attack several doors at once. New scalar particles could reshape the electroweak phase transition. New fermions could add CP violation. Deviations in Higgs self-coupling or rare decays could expose an extended sector. None has yet produced a clean baryogenesis signature.

Gravitational waves may eventually attack the third door. A strong first-order phase transition can generate a stochastic background through bubble collisions, sound waves and turbulence in the primordial plasma. Proposed space-based observatories such as LISA will probe frequency bands inaccessible to ground-based detectors. If a signal consistent with an electroweak-scale phase transition appeared, it would transform the baryogenesis conversation.

It would not identify the entire mechanism by itself.

Physics is stingy with single-shot explanations.

The human role across these doors is remarkably physical. We dig caverns to shield proton-decay detectors from cosmic rays. We suspend molecules in electric fields to look for microscopic dipoles. We cool neutrons. We maintain kilometers of vacuum. We measure Higgs couplings. We launch interferometers. We let the universe's own expansion perform the high-energy experiment and then build receivers for its debris.

Nothing in Sakharov's conditions mentions consciousness.

That is worth saying because frontier cosmology attracts an observer fetish. The matter-antimatter asymmetry does not require a mind to collapse a wavefunction into baryons. Human observers arrive after billions of years of stellar and chemical evolution. Our relevance is epistemic, not causal: we are the residue becoming able to test the conditions of its residue.

The distinction is severe and sufficient.

There is a historical irony here that does not need polishing. Sakharov helped build a thermonuclear weapon, a machine in which nuclear reactions turn tiny mass differences into catastrophic energy, then helped formulate conditions under which tiny microscopic asymmetries could determine the material content of the cosmos. One should not force a moral symmetry onto those facts. Still, they share a scale lesson: microscopic bookkeeping can govern macroscopic consequence.

The universe is sensitive to small violations when the subsequent dynamics amplify their relevance.

In baryogenesis the amplification is unusual. The asymmetry does not necessarily grow into dominance by reproducing itself. The symmetric population gets removed. A fractional bias survives the purge.

Sakharov gave us a language for asking how the bias could be created.

B violation: can the ledger change?

C and CP violation: can the rates prefer a sign?

Nonequilibrium: can the preference become history rather than being averaged away?

Every mechanism in the chapters ahead chooses its own hardware for those three functions.

Grand unification uses decays of ultraheavy particles.

Electroweak baryogenesis uses bubble walls and sphalerons.

Leptogenesis routes the imbalance through leptons.

Affleck-Dine mechanisms use scalar fields carrying baryon or lepton number.

Other proposals exploit asymmetric dark matter, oscillations, primordial black holes, axion dynamics or sectors that couple weakly to ours. The literature is inventive because the observational fact is simple and the mechanism is absent.

This proliferation is not evidence that physics is lost. It is what an underdetermined frontier looks like. A known output, several necessary ingredients, many candidate circuits.

The task is to find fingerprints that differ.

Does the mechanism predict Majorana neutrinos? A particular EDM range? A first-order phase transition? Proton decay? A new scalar? A relation between dark matter abundance and baryon abundance? A distinctive gravitational-wave spectrum? A low reheating temperature? A collider-accessible state?

A good theory of baryogenesis is not merely able to produce the number. With enough adjustable parameters many theories can produce a small number. A good theory should connect that number to other facts in ways that risk failure.

Sakharov opened the doors.

The frontier consists of putting alarms on each one.
