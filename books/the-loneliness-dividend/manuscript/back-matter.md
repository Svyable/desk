# Back Matter

## A Note on Evidence

This book rests on peer-reviewed research, government advisories and statistics, court filings and regulatory orders, company financial disclosures, and reporting from established news organizations, rather than on anonymous sources or private access. Every specific statistic, study, and case examined in these pages is attributed to a named source in the text itself, and the detailed research trail — organized chapter by chapter, with source URLs and explicit falsification notes describing what each chapter does and does not claim — is kept separately in `research/README.md`, so the narrative can read cleanly while the claims underneath it remain checkable.

Several boundaries are worth stating directly, because this book has tried to name its own limits throughout rather than only at the end. Loneliness and social isolation are measured by different research instruments that do not always agree with each other, and this book has tried to name which specific instrument produced which specific figure rather than treating "loneliness," as measured, as a single stable quantity comparable across every study cited. Several widely circulated statistics in this space — the "fifteen cigarettes a day" mortality comparison, the total dollar cost of workplace loneliness in the United States, and the total size of the "AI companion" market — turn out, on close inspection, to rest on looser evidence than their popularity suggests, and this book has tried to report the more careful, more heavily caveated version of each rather than the version that travels best. Where this book could not trace a widely repeated claim to a verifiable primary source, it says so in the chapter itself rather than silently including or silently omitting the claim.
