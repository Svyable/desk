# Contradiction and Incentive Audit

This file preserves tensions that revision must not smooth away.

## 1. “Training improves remote viewing” vs no quantitative training evidence

The 1984 DIA psychoenergetics summary makes a strong internal claim that appropriate training can improve ability. The 1989 SRI review says quantitative evidence did not support the training hypothesis while also reporting that experienced viewers performed better than general populations. These are not equivalent propositions. Experience effects can arise from selection, attrition, familiarity, practice, or training. The manuscript must not resolve the contradiction by assertion.

**Expansion action:** reconstruct the actual training cohorts, baseline tests, dropout/selection process, target protocols and longitudinal performance. Separate improvement in procedural discipline from target-specific information.

## 2. Very strong aggregate statistics vs weak operational value

Program-associated reviews report large statistical departures from chance across controlled research. The 1995 operational review concludes the phenomenon is too unreliable/inconsistent/sporadic for intelligence use.

**Possible reconciliations:** a small real effect; methodological structure visible only in aggregates; open-world task difficulty; retrospective recognition of correct details; poor calibration; variable performers; scoring differences between laboratory and operations.

**Expansion action:** do not choose a reconciliation prematurely. Normalize laboratory effect sizes and operational decision metrics separately.

## 3. Positive and null sessions coexist in official files

The archive contains sessions program personnel judged strikingly correlated and sessions judged to have little or no target correlation. Later popular accounts preferentially circulate the hits.

**Incentive:** memory, publication and storytelling all select unusual successes. Skeptical summaries may select embarrassing failures in reverse.

**Expansion action:** construct a denominator ledger from a bounded, reconstructable session series rather than a highlight reel.

## 4. Classification can indicate sensitivity without indicating truth

Many remote-viewing records carried SECRET/NOFORN/ORCON or SAP handling. This is sometimes treated publicly as evidence the underlying phenomenon was confirmed.

**Boundary:** classification can protect target identities, methods, participant names, threat assessments, collection priorities, or uncertainty. It cannot be used as an efficacy multiplier.

## 5. Soviet/foreign interest vs foreign capability

U.S. program documents cite Soviet and Chinese anomalous research as a threat concern.

**Incentive:** intelligence services are structurally punished for missing adversary surprise. This rationally lowers the threshold for investigation and can sustain low-cost research even when capability evidence is weak.

**Expansion action:** trace U.S. threat claims to originating Soviet/Russian/Chinese publications and intelligence reports. Identify recursive citation loops.

## 6. Secrecy protects both genuine innovation and methodological weakness

Compartmentation can prevent adversary access and reputational interference. It can also reduce independent replication, narrow the critic pool, and let the same small community train, test, interpret and brief.

**Boundary:** do not infer that secrecy caused error in a particular experiment without evidence. Treat it as an institutional risk variable.

## 7. Utts and Hyman are often presented as opposite verdicts on one question

Utts emphasizes the statistical evidence and regards anomalous functioning as demonstrated. Hyman argues the causal interpretation is not secured by the experimental history and replication record. AIR separately considers operational applications.

**Expansion action:** page-index their actual points of agreement and disagreement. Avoid “Utts proved psi / Hyman denied data” caricatures.

## 8. Famous operational successes vs incomplete contemporaneous chains

Claims about Soviet submarines, facilities, missing aircraft, hostages and other targets are central to the folklore. Some may have substantial contemporaneous support; others are known primarily through later testimony and selective documents.

**Incentives:** participants remember unusual successes; programs use impressive cases to secure support; skeptical reviewers may discount cases lacking complete records even when records were lost for ordinary archival reasons.

**Expansion action:** no famous case becomes a manuscript anchor until tasking → blind conditions → complete response → known-at-time target facts → feedback → action → later confirmation has been reconstructed.

## 9. “Raw intelligence” is appropriately cautious and operationally evasive

Calling transcripts raw intelligence correctly separates collection from analysis. It also pushes responsibility for identifying signal downstream to an analyst who knows the intelligence question, creating room for retrospective matching.

**Expansion action:** measure end-to-end value rather than treating transcript resemblance as the capability endpoint.

## 10. Program persistence vs efficacy

Long duration is used by proponents as implied validation and by critics as evidence of bureaucratic waste.

**Alternative explanation:** low cost + huge imagined upside + foreign-threat uncertainty + committed sponsors + unresolved experimental residue can rationally sustain an exploratory option without establishing a deployable capability.

## 11. Mechanism ignorance cuts both ways

A missing mechanism does not make an observed effect impossible. It does remove boundary predictions that help diagnose artifacts and engineer a capability.

**Boundary:** do not reject positive data merely because no accepted physical channel exists; do not treat the lack of ordinary distance/shielding effects as affirmative evidence for nonlocal consciousness.

## Known disinformation / distortion incentives

- Adversaries can exaggerate exotic research to waste competitor resources or cultivate technological mystique.
- Intelligence collectors may overestimate hidden foreign work because absence of evidence is difficult to distinguish from successful classification.
- Program advocates have incentives to preserve funding and emphasize high-value cases.
- Skeptics have reputational incentives to demonstrate that notorious anomalous claims reduce to error.
- Journal/publication systems select novelty and can suppress nulls.
- Memoirs and media select stories with narrative closure.
- Declassification itself selects what was preserved, reviewed and releasable; the public archive is not a randomized sample of all program activity.

None of these incentives is proof that any specific actor distorted evidence. They are reasons to demand contemporaneous chains and prospective tests.
