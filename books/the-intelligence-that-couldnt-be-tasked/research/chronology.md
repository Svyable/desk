# Working Chronology

This chronology tracks the institutional and evidentiary sequence, not every session. Dates and ownership details should be page-indexed against primary records during expansion.

- **1960s–early 1970s:** U.S. analysts collect reports of Soviet-bloc interest in telepathy, psychokinesis, psychotronics, and related anomalous research. Foreign interest becomes one input to domestic threat assessment; it is not proof of foreign capability.
- **1971:** Harold Puthoff joins Stanford Research Institute (SRI) in Menlo Park.
- **1972:** CIA-sponsored investigation of purported paranormal information acquisition begins at SRI; early work includes subjects such as Ingo Swann and Uri Geller and develops coordinate/remote-viewing approaches.
- **1973–1974:** SRI conducts outbound/local-target remote-viewing experiments and related perceptual work.
- **October 1974:** Russell Targ and Harold Puthoff publish “Information transmission under conditions of sensory shielding” in *Nature*.
- **1975:** SRI delivers *Perceptual Augmentation Techniques* research reports to government sponsors.
- **Mid-1970s:** CIA reduces/directly ends its own sponsorship; research and operational interest continue elsewhere in the defense/intelligence system.
- **1978:** Army INSCOM begins a remote-viewing effort that becomes associated with GRILL FLAME. Surviving training records include both positive assessments and explicit null/poor-correlation sessions.
- **1978:** Marks and Kammann publish a critical *Nature* correspondence/report stating their duplicate experiments did not verify the SRI conclusions.
- **1979–1981:** Remote-viewing sessions are tasked against operational problems including the U.S. Embassy hostage crisis in Iran. Reports routinely label output “raw intelligence.”
- **1980:** Tart, Puthoff and Targ publish a reply in *Nature* addressing criticism of cueing/judging issues.
- **1981:** David Marks publishes “Sensory cues invalidate remote viewing experiments” in *Nature*.
- **Early 1980s:** Army/DIA programs continue operational tasking, training, foreign-assessment work, and method development.
- **1984:** DIA psychoenergetics report summarizes three years of work and makes strong internal claims for reality, trainability and intelligence potential while noting descriptive information is more reliable than analytic/technical content.
- **1984:** Army records document transition of CENTER LANE operational functions toward DIA control after INSCOM decides to discontinue command involvement; the program had Special Access Program handling.
- **1986:** Marks and Scott publish “Remote viewing exposed” in *Nature*.
- **1989:** SRI completes a broad review of psychoenergetic research from 1973–1988, reporting highly significant aggregate results while stating no quantitative evidence supported the training hypothesis.
- **October 23, 1990:** SUN STREAK Special Access Program status is terminated according to later program-summary material.
- **1991:** STAR GATE is presented in official summary material as a reorganized approach combining basic research and national-security applications.
- **Early 1990s:** Research proceeds at Science Applications International Corporation under Edwin May and colleagues; operational tasking continues through DIA.
- **May 1995:** May, Utts and Spottiswoode issue *Decision Augmentation Theory* work within the program research environment.
- **1995:** DIA hands the program back to CIA for review; CIA conditions further consideration on independent evaluation.
- **September 29, 1995:** American Institutes for Research completes *An Evaluation of Remote Viewing: Research and Applications*. Jessica Utts argues the experimental statistics support anomalous functioning; Ray Hyman disputes paranormal attribution and emphasizes methodological/replication concerns; AIR's operational assessment does not find a dependable intelligence capability.
- **1995:** CIA elects not to restore/continue the program as an intelligence capability.
- **Late 1990s onward:** CIA, DIA and Army records are progressively declassified and released; “STAR GATE” becomes a broad public label for a heterogeneous history that had multiple code names and institutional owners.
- **1999:** Milton and Wiseman publish a negative meta-analysis of ganzfeld replication, relevant to the broader anomalous-information-transfer debate but not a direct audit of all remote-viewing work.
- **2023:** Tressoldi and Katz publish a positive 1974–2022 remote-viewing meta-analysis in the *Journal of Scientific Exploration*, illustrating that the statistical controversy remains active outside mainstream consensus.
- **2026 working position:** The archival question is no longer whether governments studied the subject—they did. The unresolved scientific question is whether target-specific anomalous information survives modern adversarial replication. The historical operational record does not establish a calibrated intelligence capability.
