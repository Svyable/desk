# Back Matter

The public STAR GATE record is unusually large and unusually easy to misuse. Thousands of pages now sit in the CIA's FOIA Electronic Reading Room. A document's presence there establishes that the government possessed, produced, collected, or later processed it; it does not certify every claim in the document. Classification markings establish handling status at the time, not scientific validity.

The manuscript therefore uses four evidence classes throughout. “Documented” means an official, archival, contemporaneous, or peer-reviewed record establishes the event, statement, experiment, administrative action, or result being described. “Credible but incomplete” covers reporting or testimony with meaningful provenance whose decisive corroboration is missing. “Disputed” is used for claims with material counterevidence, unresolved methodological problems, or no independent confirmation. “Speculative synthesis” is the author's attempt to connect established facts into a hypothesis that remains open to defeat.

The distinction is especially important here because the same archive contains research reports, intelligence estimates, operational transcripts, program advocacy, critical reviews, administrative memoranda, and later declassification material. Those genres answer different questions. A DIA report can establish what program officers concluded in 1984 without proving their conclusion. A remote-viewing transcript can establish what a viewer said without establishing anomalous acquisition. A user's positive recollection can establish testimony without establishing unique operational value. An AIR statistical assessment can establish an evaluator's analysis without settling mechanism.

The research directory contains the source log, evidence matrix, chronology, contradiction audit, freshness note, and publication gate for this working edition. The links are preserved so claims can be reconstructed rather than inherited from folklore.

## A note on names

The U.S. effort changed organizational homes and code names repeatedly. Public accounts often use “STAR GATE” as a retrospective umbrella. This book sometimes does the same when discussing the overall history, but it distinguishes SCANATE, GRILL FLAME, CENTER LANE, SUN STREAK and STAR GATE when the administrative difference matters. The changing names should not be interpreted as independent confirmations of the underlying phenomenon.

## A note on “psi”

“Psi” is used as a conventional umbrella term for purported anomalous cognition or influence, not as an assertion that such a mechanism has been established. “Remote viewing” refers to the experimental and operational practice described in the historical record. “Anomalous cognition” is retained where sources use it or where the manuscript discusses a residual statistical phenomenon without claiming a sensory or physical mechanism.

## Acknowledgment of uncertainty

This working draft does not claim that remote viewing has been scientifically established, nor that every positive experiment has been explained away. It does not claim that U.S., Soviet, Russian, Chinese, or other state interest demonstrates efficacy. It does not infer classified present-day capabilities from historical programs. It does not treat patents, clearances, funding, secrecy, testimony, or duration as proof.

The unresolved question is narrower and testable: whether rigorously isolated, prospectively scored target-specific information survives independent modern replication, and whether any surviving effect can improve decisions before feedback. The manuscript's thesis is defeated in either direction by sufficiently strong evidence: a robust ordinary explanation for the positive record, or reproducible calibrated operational performance.
