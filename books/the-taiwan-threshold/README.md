The central thesis is that the most dangerous path to a Taiwan conflict is not necessarily a single decision to invade but a sequence of coercive moves below the traditional threshold of war, each individually survivable, that together make the next escalation easier and the off-ramp harder to see.

The book focuses on classification, thresholds, and decision process: quarantine, inspections, exercises, cyber operations, undersea infrastructure, financial pressure, selective seizures, and other acts whose political effect depends partly on what governments decide to call them.

This Desk edition completes the 18-chapter argument as a first full manuscript while preserving the distinction between observed events, official exercises, and scenario analysis.
