# Chapter Briefs

## Prologue — The Day Before the Blockade
Open with shipping and insurance behavior changing before a declared military event.

## 1 — What Counts as War?
Define quarantine, blockade, invasion, and the escalation premium created when civilian-looking coercion invites military-looking response.

## 2 — Gray Is a Strategy
Trace coercion designed to change facts without creating a clean casus belli.

## 3 — Tiny Steps
Show how repeated small actions create cumulative precedent and normalize presence.

## 4 — The Exercise That Does Not End
Study military exercises as signals, rehearsals, intelligence collection, and potential transition mechanisms.

## 5 — The Quarantine
Reconstruct law-enforcement-led inspection scenarios and commercial compliance dynamics.

## 6 — The Blockade
Examine military requirements, economic consequences, escalation pathways, and the difference from quarantine.

## 7 — Cables, Ports, and Invisible Damage
Follow undersea communications, port concentration, repair capability, energy stocks, and attribution.

## 8 — Cyber Before Fire
Examine prepositioning, disruption, espionage, and the problem of interpreting malware found before crisis.

## 9 — The Financial Front
Study insurance, sanctions, payments, markets, shipping finance, and semiconductor supply-chain expectations.

## 10 — Taipei Decides
Reconstruct the domestic political, military, economic, and public-information choices under ambiguous pressure.

## 11 — Washington Decides
Examine authorities, intelligence, military options, economic tools, and strategic ambiguity.

## 12 — Tokyo Decides
Focus on geography, bases, trade routes, law, collective self-defense, and direct exposure.

## 13 — The Alliance Problem
Extend the decision to the Philippines, Australia, Europe, and partners whose roles differ by capability and law.

## 14 — Synthetic Crisis
Study deepfakes, false orders, hacked accounts, automated persuasion, and authentication.

## 15 — The Drone Swarm and the Fishing Fleet
Compare autonomous systems with civilian and paramilitary mass in a crowded maritime environment.

## 16 — The Model in the Situation Room
Test AI decision support against warning overload, uncertainty, and political authority.

## 17 — Deny, Absorb, Recover
Build a theory of resilience as deterrence by denial.

## 18 — Signaling Without Surrender
Design messages and actions that resist precedent while preserving off-ramps.

## 19 — The Cost of Clarity
Ask when explicit red lines stabilize and when they become scripts or commitment traps.

## 20 — The Day Nothing Happened
End with deterrence as the successful non-event created by preparation, resilience, and credible coalition behavior.
