# Book Brief

## Commercial proposition

A Taiwan book for readers who already know invasion is dangerous and want to understand the harder problem: what happens before everyone agrees a war has started.

## Reader promise

By the end, a reader should be able to distinguish quarantine from blockade, identify the economic and infrastructure channels through which coercion operates, understand why alliance responses are difficult under ambiguity, and see how resilience changes deterrence.

## Narrative architecture

The book moves from classification to tools of pressure, then through decisions in Taipei, Washington, Tokyo, and allied capitals, before turning to AI-era crisis management and a practical theory of keeping the threshold high.

## Target scale

20 chapters plus prologue. Complete draft target: 75,000–90,000 manuscript words.

## Kennedy School use

The book should support a multi-session simulation in which students receive ambiguous updates: exercise notices, inspection rules, cable incidents, cyber warnings, shipping cancellations, synthetic media, and intelligence estimates. The learning objective is not to “win” the Taiwan war but to preserve coalition action and decision time before war.

## AI-era relevance

AI affects satellite and sensor analysis, cyber operations, synthetic media, decision support, autonomous systems, and the speed at which interpretations spread. The book must distinguish capability from authority: a model can surface an anomaly; it cannot decide what political threshold the anomaly crosses.

## Central tensions

- clarity versus flexibility
- attribution versus resilience
- denial versus punishment
- commercial risk versus sovereignty
- coalition consensus versus response speed
- signaling versus escalation
- better warning versus automation bias
