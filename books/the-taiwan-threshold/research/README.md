# Research Brief

## Thesis under test

The Taiwan Threshold argues that deterrence is increasingly contested below the point of obvious armed attack. China can use military, paramilitary, legal, cyber, economic, informational, and commercial pressure to alter behavior while forcing Taiwan and its partners to debate what category of event has occurred.

> The operational contest is not only whether China can impose costs. It is whether a coalition can classify an ambiguous action quickly enough to respond together without giving Beijing either a free precedent or an unnecessary escalation opportunity.

## Core empirical tests

1. Which forms of quarantine or blockade are actually feasible with demonstrated Chinese capabilities?
2. How much commercial compliance could be induced without physical interdiction?
3. Which Taiwanese dependencies are most vulnerable to temporary disruption, and which resilience investments materially change the answer?
4. How quickly can the United States, Japan, the Philippines, Australia, and European partners agree on classification and response?
5. Which gray-zone actions create cumulative precedents even when each episode is reversible?
6. Can AI improve anomaly detection and crisis awareness without increasing false confidence or warning overload?
7. Which responses deny coercive benefits without forcing immediate kinetic escalation?

## Falsification and counter-evidence

The book should change if evidence shows that quarantine concepts are operationally implausible, commercial actors would broadly ignore Chinese instructions, coalition decision-making is faster and more unified than assumed, or Taiwan’s resilience has reduced the coercive value of the vulnerabilities emphasized here. It should also preserve evidence that visible military clarity can sometimes be stabilizing and that excessive threshold formalization could create exploitable red lines.

## Evidence lanes

Chinese exercises and coast-guard operations; Taiwan defense and resilience documents; shipping and insurance behavior; port and energy dependence; cable incidents; cyber doctrine; sanctions and export controls; alliance legal authorities; wargames; crisis communications; AI-enabled information operations.

## Source discipline

Separate observed Chinese activity from hypothetical scenarios. Do not convert a wargame outcome into prediction. Treat suspicious cable damage as an attribution problem unless authorities establish cause. Distinguish quarantine, blockade, invasion, and ordinary law enforcement consistently.
