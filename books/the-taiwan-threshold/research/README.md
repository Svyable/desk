# Research Brief

## Thesis under test

The Taiwan Threshold tests whether the most dangerous route to conflict is often cumulative coercion below the conventional threshold of war rather than a single clean decision to invade.

The book must distinguish capability from intent, exercises from operations, and plausible scenarios from observed events. It is not an invasion forecast.

## Core mechanisms

1. **Classification:** the label attached to an act changes legal authorities, alliance expectations, commercial behavior, and escalation risk.
2. **Accumulation:** repeated individually limited actions can normalize a new status quo.
3. **Reversal asymmetry:** undoing a coercive change may require more escalation than creating it.
4. **Resilience:** civilian, commercial, communications, and logistical resilience reduces the political return on coercion.
5. **Coalition thresholds:** deterrence depends partly on whether partners have pre-negotiated responses below declared war.
6. **AI misclassification:** better sensing and synthesis can improve warning while producing false confidence about ambiguous intent.

## Falsification and counter-evidence

The book should change if empirical evidence shows gray-zone pressure is strategically marginal compared with invasion preparation, or if coercive moves consistently produce balancing effects that outweigh gains. Preserve evidence that ambiguity can stabilize crises, that restraint can create off-ramps, and that overreaction can manufacture the escalation an adversary did not initially seek.

Do not infer intent from capability or from every military exercise. Separate Taiwan, PRC, U.S., Japanese, Philippine, commercial, and third-party descriptions of the same event.

## Chapter evidence map

Chapters 1–4 establish the threshold and exercise problem. Chapters 5–11 examine quarantine, inspections, maritime pressure, cables, cyber, finance, and semiconductors. Chapters 12–14 examine partner decision thresholds. Chapters 15–18 examine machine-supported classification, signaling, crisis exercises, and deterrence design.

## Research discipline

Prefer official Taiwan and PRC documents, maritime notices, military activity data, treaty texts, commercial shipping and insurance evidence, cable incident records, cyber reporting, semiconductor filings, and contemporaneous allied policy statements. Scenario work must always be labeled as scenario work.
