# Deep Six — evidence ledger and source trail

## Evidence classes

### 1 — Verified / documented

- GAO, *Chemical Weapons: Obstacles to the Army's Plan to Destroy Obsolete U.S. Stockpile* (NSIAD-90-155, 1990), states that before 1969 the Army used disposal methods including deep-ocean dumping and open-pit burning; it also records the 1969 National Academy recommendation against ocean dumping and the 1972 statutory prohibition on further ocean disposal of chemical agents. https://www.gao.gov/assets/nsiad-90-155.pdf
- GAO, *Stockpile of Lethal Chemical Munitions and Agents—Better Management Needed* (LCD-77-205, 1977), documents the lethal-agent stockpile, storage locations and deficiencies in inspection, maintenance, handling, safety and disposal. https://www.gao.gov/products/lcd-77-205
- National Academies, *Alternative Technologies for the Destruction of Chemical Agents and Munitions*, documents later U.S. chemical-demilitarization experience and the technical tradeoffs of incineration and neutralization. https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/2218/chapter/23
- National Academies, *Recommendations for the Disposal of Chemical Agents and Munitions*, describes the intrinsic hazards of GB, VX and mustard stocks and the continuing risk of storage. https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/2348/chapter/3

### 2 — Credible but incomplete

- Individual Operation CHASE cargo manifests, vessel preparation, dump coordinates and risk calculations need archival reconstruction from Navy/Army records rather than reliance on secondary CHASE lists.
- Exact institutional meaning and contemporary use of the CHASE acronym should be tied to a primary program document before being presented as formal nomenclature.
- Statements about container design, expected rupture depth and release behavior must be test-specific and sourced to contemporary engineering records.

### 3 — Disputed / unconfirmed

- Any assertion that a modern seabed object is a specific CHASE munition without location, imagery, sampling and provenance sufficient to establish identity.
- Any health or ecological effect attributed to a specific dump without a defensible exposure/source pathway.
- Popular totals for chemical munitions dumped at sea when they collapse different services, periods, cargo types or definitions.

### 4 — Speculative synthesis

- Ocean disposal can be understood as trading an inventory problem for an observability problem.
- Distance and inaccessibility functioned as implicit governance technologies: they reduced immediate human contact while also reducing later inspectability.
- The transition from dumping to expensive monitored destruction represents not merely better chemistry but a political change in what counted as acceptable proof of disposal.

## Contradiction ledger

**Storage versus disposal risk.** Aging chemical weapons were themselves hazardous. Do not write as though keeping every munition indefinitely was the safe baseline.

**Dilution versus local exposure.** Reject both caricatures: dilution does not imply zero local effect, while the existence of dumped agent does not establish basin-scale poisoning.

**Corrosion versus release.** A corroded casing is evidence about containment condition, not automatically evidence of a particular agent concentration or ecological outcome.

**Program versus object.** Historical proof that dumping occurred is not provenance for every suspicious seabed cylinder.

## Incentive audit

Military logistics favored removing obsolete inventory and storage hazards. Program offices had incentives to characterize a disposal operation as bounded and complete. Later environmental and political actors can have incentives to maximize uncertain inventories or consequences. Contractors can benefit from remediation. Journalists benefit from vivid hidden-hazard narratives. None of these incentives decides the evidence; each changes which claims require independent corroboration.

## Research queue

1. Locate primary Navy/Army CHASE program histories and operational orders.
2. Build vessel-by-vessel table: hull, date, departure, cargo, packaging, site, depth, source quality.
3. Separate chemical-agent CHASE events from conventional munitions and other hazardous cargo.
4. Reconstruct 1969 NAS recommendation from the original report, not later summaries.
5. Page-index the congressional path to the Marine Protection, Research, and Sanctuaries Act of 1972.
6. Locate contemporary oceanographic assumptions about dilution, hydrolysis, corrosion and benthic effects.
7. Build agent-specific chemistry sheets for mustard, GB and VX in seawater.
8. Identify modern survey/sampling studies at documented U.S. dump sites.
9. Compare U.S. practice with British, Soviet/Russian, German/Baltic and Japanese disposal only after the U.S. record is stable.
10. Trace the transition to monitored incineration/neutralization and the costs created by keeping disposal observable.

## Defeat condition

If the primary record shows CHASE was marginal to U.S. chemical-agent sea disposal, or that the proposed observability thesis is not supported by the operational and scientific record, narrow the book to the broader documented history of U.S. chemical-weapons ocean disposal rather than forcing CHASE to carry the argument.
