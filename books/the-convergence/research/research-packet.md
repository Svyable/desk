## Source-quality ladder

Prefer sources in this order when a claim can be supported at more than one level:

1. original dataset or historical primary record
2. original experimental or empirical paper
3. government, regulator, national-laboratory, or intergovernmental statistical release
4. peer-reviewed synthesis or serious scholarly history
5. technical reports with transparent methodology
6. company technical material, clearly labeled and independently checked when possible
7. journalism and general commentary for discovery/context only

A lower level can be more useful than a higher level when it uniquely documents an event, but it should not silently replace available primary evidence.

## Chapter readiness gate

A chapter is ready to draft only when all of the following are true:

- [ ] Its central empirical claim has at least one primary or authoritative source.
- [ ] At least one serious counter-result has been read and recorded.
- [ ] Any historical scene has a source for date, place, object, and action; no sensory detail or dialogue has been invented.
- [ ] Every quantitative claim has a dataset/table/page or a clear plan to obtain it.
- [ ] Observed data, reconstruction, forecast, scenario, target, and laboratory milestone are labeled separately.
- [ ] Primary energy, final energy, electricity, exergy, useful work, power, and energy are not being used interchangeably.
- [ ] The chapter states what evidence would force its thesis to narrow.
- [ ] A planned figure has defined units and source vintage.
- [ ] Distributional effects are considered where averages could conceal energy poverty or concentrated benefit.
- [ ] External costs—pollution, emissions, land, material, safety, time, labor—are included when relevant.

## Research notes required during chapter writing

Each chapter-writing run should leave a short research note in the PR description or research material covering:

1. **Claim tested** — the narrow proposition the chapter is trying to establish.
2. **Best supporting evidence** — no more than the strongest few sources.
3. **Best counterevidence** — evidence that materially limits the claim.
4. **Definitions** — especially which energy accounting measure is being used.
5. **Fact checks outstanding** — unresolved numbers, dates, attribution, or methodology.
6. **What would make this false?** — one concrete result that would require changing the chapter.

## Writing stance created by the research

The evidence so far supports a book about expanding command of **useful, controllable energy**, not a book that treats raw consumption as a civilization score.

The recurring pattern to test is that major transitions often do four things together:

- increase the scale or accessibility of usable energy;
- improve conversion, control, transport, or storage;
- reorganize institutions and production around the new capability;
- move the binding constraint somewhere else.

The book should remain willing to conclude that energy is a necessary substrate but an incomplete explanation wherever institutions, distribution, knowledge, or external cost dominate the evidence.
