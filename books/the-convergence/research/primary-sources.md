# Primary Source Dossier

This dossier is the starting research shelf for *The Convergence*. It favors original papers, official datasets, and laboratory or agency reports. The notes below describe how each source may be used; they are not chapter-ready claims until the underlying data or text has been checked in context.

## Intellectual lineage

### 1. Alfred J. Lotka — “Contribution to the Energetics of Evolution” (1922)

- **Type:** Original journal article, *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*, 8(6), 147–151.
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.8.6.147
- **Full text:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1085052/
- **Use:** Early formulation of an energetic view of biological evolution and selection. Useful for tracing the idea that systems compete partly through their ability to capture and use available energy.
- **Caution:** Do not turn Lotka’s evolutionary argument into a universal law of civilization without separate evidence.

### 2. Leslie A. White — “Energy and the Evolution of Culture” (1943)

- **Type:** Original journal article, *American Anthropologist*, 45(3), 335–356.
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1525/aa.1943.45.3.02a00010
- **Publisher:** https://anthrosource.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1525/aa.1943.45.3.02a00010
- **Use:** Direct antecedent to the book’s thesis. White explicitly linked cultural development to energy captured per capita and the efficiency with which it was put to work.
- **Caution:** Treat this as an influential hypothesis with a historical context, not as settled anthropology.

### 3. Nikolai S. Kardashev — “Transmission of Information by Extraterrestrial Civilizations” (1964)

- **Type:** Original astronomy paper, translated in *Soviet Astronomy-AJ*, 8(2), 217–221.
- **Scan:** https://adsabs.harvard.edu/pdf/1964SvA.....8..217K
- **Use:** Primary source for the civilization scale later called the Kardashev scale: Type I, II, and III civilizations distinguished by the order of power they can command.
- **Caution:** The scale was introduced in a paper about detectability and communication, not as a complete theory of social progress.

### 4. Eric J. Chaisson — “Energy Rate Density as a Complexity Metric and Evolutionary Driver” (2011)

- **Type:** Original journal article, *Complexity*, 16, 27–40.
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/cplx.20323
- **Use:** A modern attempt to normalize energy flow by system mass and compare complexity across physical, biological, and cultural systems.
- **Caution:** Energy-rate density is a proposed metric; use it as a lens and test its explanatory power rather than presenting it as consensus.

### 5. Robert U. Ayres and Benjamin Warr — “Accounting for growth: the role of physical work” (2005)

- **Type:** Original economic research, *Structural Change and Economic Dynamics*, 16(2), 181–209.
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.strueco.2003.10.003
- **Use:** Critical bridge from raw energy to **useful work**. The paper argues that exergy converted to useful work is more explanatory for long-run U.S. growth than raw energy input alone.
- **Caution:** Compare its model with mainstream growth accounting and subsequent critiques before making a causal claim.

## Long-run history and human capability

### 6. Roger Fouquet and Peter J. G. Pearson — “A Thousand Years of Energy Use in the United Kingdom” (1998)

- **Type:** Original long-run reconstruction, *The Energy Journal*, 19(4), 1–41.
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.5547/ISSN0195-6574-EJ-Vol19-No4-1
- **Publisher:** https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.5547/ISSN0195-6574-EJ-Vol19-No4-1
- **Use:** Deep historical evidence on the transition from biomass and animate power toward coal, petroleum, gas, mechanical power, and electricity.
- **Caution:** Reconstructed historical series have larger uncertainty than modern directly measured data.

### 7. Roger Fouquet and Peter J. G. Pearson — “Seven Centuries of Energy Services: The Price and Use of Light in the United Kingdom (1300–2000)” (2006)

- **Type:** Original long-run energy-services research, *The Energy Journal*, 27(1), 139–178.
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.5547/ISSN0195-6574-EJ-Vol27-No1-8
- **Publisher:** https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.5547/ISSN0195-6574-EJ-Vol27-No1-8
- **Use:** Strong evidence for focusing on the cost and quantity of **energy services**, not fuel alone. Lighting is an unusually clear example of welfare gains from conversion efficiency and technology.
- **Caution:** The UK is not the world; use it as a long-run case study rather than a universal series.

### 8. U.S. Energy Information Administration — historical U.S. primary-energy consumption

- **Type:** Official government statistical series.
- **Current historical overview:** https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=67824
- **Annual data hub:** https://www.eia.gov/totalenergy/data/annual/index.php
- **Use:** A continuous empirical backbone for the United States, including the transition from wood to coal, petroleum, natural gas, nuclear, and modern renewables. The long historical appendix reaches back before the modern statistical era.
- **Caution:** Keep estimated pre-1949 observations distinct from modern measured series.

### 9. Maddison Project Database 2023

- **Type:** Academic historical GDP and population dataset.
- **Dataset DOI:** https://doi.org/10.34894/INZBF2
- **Project page:** https://www.rug.nl/ggdc/historicaldevelopment/maddison/releases/maddison-project-database-2023
- **Use:** Long-run GDP per capita and population series for testing how economic capability co-evolved with energy systems.
- **Caution:** Follow the dataset’s source-attribution requirements, especially when charting country subsets.

### 10. United Nations — World Population Prospects 2024

- **Type:** Official UN demographic estimates and projections.
- **Dataset:** https://www.un.org/development/desa/pd/content/world-population-prospects-2024-dataset
- **Use:** Population denominator for per-capita energy, electricity, and economic measures; projections can support scenario analysis.
- **Caution:** Clearly label projections and variants separately from historical estimates.

## The current global energy transition

### 11. Energy Institute — Statistical Review of World Energy 2026

- **Type:** Global statistical compendium with downloadable data and methodology.
- **Data downloads:** https://www.energyinst.org/statistical-review/resources-and-data-downloads
- **Use:** Global and national energy production, consumption, and generation series, including long historical coverage for the modern energy era.
- **Caution:** The Review compiles many upstream providers. Trace unusually important claims to the originating national or international source when practical.

### 12. International Energy Agency — Global Energy Review 2026

- **Type:** Official international energy assessment with associated dataset.
- **Report:** https://www.iea.org/reports/global-energy-review-2026
- **Use:** First full assessment of 2025 across fuels, electricity, technology deployment, and emissions. Particularly useful for comparing total energy-demand growth with electricity-demand growth and for documenting the record contribution of solar and other low-emissions sources.
- **Caution:** Some values are estimates; preserve the IEA’s units, definitions, and status labels.

### 13. International Energy Agency — Electricity 2026

- **Type:** Official electricity-market analysis and five-year forecast.
- **Report:** https://www.iea.org/reports/electricity-2026
- **Demand chapter:** https://www.iea.org/reports/electricity-2026/demand
- **Use:** Central contemporary source for the “Age of Electricity” thesis. It tracks the reacceleration of demand in advanced economies and forecasts electricity consumption through 2030.
- **Caution:** Separate observed 2025 data from 2026–2030 forecast values.

### 14. International Energy Agency — World Energy Investment 2026

- **Type:** Official global investment assessment and datafile.
- **Report:** https://www.iea.org/reports/world-energy-investment-2026
- **Datafile:** https://www.iea.org/data-and-statistics/data-product/world-energy-investment-2026-datafile
- **Use:** Measures where society is committing capital across fossil supply, renewables, nuclear, grids, storage, efficiency, and electrification. Useful for distinguishing rhetoric from actual resource allocation.
- **License:** Datafile listed by IEA as CC BY 4.0.
- **Caution:** 2026 totals include estimates.

### 15. International Renewable Energy Agency — Renewable Capacity Statistics 2026

- **Type:** Official intergovernmental renewable-capacity statistics.
- **Publication:** https://www.irena.org/Publications/2026/Mar/Renewable-capacity-statistics-2026
- **Database:** https://pxweb.irena.org/pxweb/en/IRENASTAT/
- **Use:** Installed renewable generation capacity by country and technology through 2025.
- **Caution:** Capacity is not generation. Keep MW of installed capacity separate from GWh/TWh of output and from capacity factor.

## Computation, grids, nuclear, and the frontier

### 16. International Energy Agency — Energy and AI (2025)

- **Type:** IEA analysis based on new modelling, datasets, and consultation.
- **Report:** https://www.iea.org/reports/energy-and-ai
- **Use:** Global scenarios for data-center and AI electricity demand, supply implications, security, emissions, and AI applications in the energy system.
- **Caution:** AI demand forecasts depend on hardware, utilization, model architecture, efficiency, and deployment assumptions that can change quickly.

### 17. Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory — 2024 United States Data Center Energy Usage Report

- **Type:** U.S. national-laboratory report prepared in response to the Energy Act of 2020.
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.71468/P1WC7Q
- **LBNL page:** https://bies.lbl.gov/publications/2024-lbnl-data-center-energy-usage-report
- **Use:** Historical U.S. data-center electricity estimates back to 2014 and scenario ranges through 2028. Primary empirical anchor for Chapter 8.
- **Caution:** Scenario ranges are not forecasts with certainty; preserve assumptions and uncertainty.

### 18. Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory — Queued Up, 2026 edition

- **Type:** National-laboratory database compiled from U.S. transmission interconnection queues.
- **Current hub:** https://emp.lbl.gov/queues
- **Use:** Direct evidence for the scale of generation and storage seeking grid connection and for the growing importance of interconnection as a system constraint.
- **Caution:** Queue capacity is not equivalent to capacity that will be built; withdrawal rates are high.

### 19. International Atomic Energy Agency — Power Reactor Information System (PRIS)

- **Type:** Authoritative global reactor database maintained with national contact points.
- **Database:** https://pris.iaea.org/PRIS/Home.aspx
- **Use:** Operating, suspended, under-construction, and historical nuclear reactor statistics. Primary statistical spine for Chapter 10.
- **Caution:** Reactor counts alone do not measure cost, construction performance, capacity factor, or system value.

### 20. Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory — National Ignition Facility ignition record

- **Type:** Primary laboratory record of fusion ignition experiments.
- **Source:** https://lasers.llnl.gov/science/achieving-fusion-ignition
- **Use:** Establishes what inertial-confinement fusion has actually demonstrated experimentally, including repeated target gain above one.
- **Caution:** Target gain is not wall-plug efficiency and does not establish commercial fusion power. The chapter must keep scientific ignition, engineering breakeven, and economic viability separate.

## Source gaps to fill before drafting specific chapters

The hourly chapter loop should add primary material before writing when a chapter depends on one of these areas:

- archaeology and controlled use of fire
- agricultural energy budgets and animal traction
- steam-engine efficiency and coal production during the Industrial Revolution
- early electric power stations, alternating-current networks, and appliance diffusion
- Jevons/rebound evidence using original historical statistics where possible
- transmission build rates, transformer lead times, and utility capital expenditure
- nuclear construction cost and schedule datasets by country
- battery cost, cycle life, and deployment datasets
- geothermal and enhanced-geothermal field data
- commercial fusion performance only when primary operating data exist

## Citation rule for chapter drafting

For quantitative claims, cite the dataset vintage and exact table, series, or page where possible. For conceptual claims, cite the original paper rather than a later summary. Do not use a source merely because it agrees with the book’s thesis; record contrary results in the chapter research notes before drafting prose.
