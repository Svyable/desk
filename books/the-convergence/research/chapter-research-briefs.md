# Chapter Research Briefs

These briefs prepare the twelve planned chapters of *The Convergence* without drafting chapter prose. Each brief identifies the claim to test, the evidence stack, a possible historical scene or object, counter-evidence, and the factual checks that must be completed before prose is written.

## Chapter 1 — Fire Before History

### Question

When did hominins begin extending their biological metabolism through controlled combustion, and what capabilities can the archaeological record actually support?

### Primary evidence stack

- Francesco Berna et al. (2012), “Microstratigraphic evidence of in situ fire in the Acheulean strata of Wonderwerk Cave,” *PNAS*, DOI 10.1073/pnas.1117620109. Microscopic evidence of burned bone and ashed plant remains in an archaeological context around 1.0 Ma.
- Naama Goren-Inbar et al. (2004), “Evidence of Hominin Control of Fire at Gesher Benot Ya‘aqov, Israel,” *Science*, DOI 10.1126/science.1095443. Burned seeds, wood, and flint around 790 ka, with spatial patterning suggestive of hearth locations.
- Alfred Lotka (1922) only after the archaeology is established; use him to widen from fire into a later energetic theory of selection, not to interpret the archaeological evidence itself.

### Scene candidates

- A thin section of Wonderwerk Cave sediment under the microscope: ash and burned bone survive where an open fire itself does not.
- The spatial clustering of burned flint at Gesher Benot Ya‘aqov as a way to show how archaeologists infer behavior from distribution rather than from a preserved hearth.

### Claim to resist

Do not write that humans “invented fire” at a known date. The evidence supports burning in archaeological contexts and, later, repeated controlled use. It does not identify a single invention event or prove every hypothesized benefit at the earliest date.

### Checks before drafting

- Distinguish “evidence of burning,” “controlled use,” “habitual use,” and “fire-making at will.”
- Verify dating ranges and the authors’ exact confidence language.
- Do not use the cooking hypothesis as established fact.

## Chapter 2 — The First Surplus

### Question

Did agriculture expand usable energy surplus, or did its first advantage lie more in population, storage, territorial control, and the ability to concentrate resources?

### Primary evidence stack

- Samuel Bowles (2011), “Cultivation of cereals by the first farmers was not more productive than foraging,” *PNAS*, DOI 10.1073/pnas.1010733108. Direct challenge to a simple productivity story.
- Jean-Pierre Bocquet-Appel (2011), “When the world’s population took off: the springboard of the Neolithic Demographic Transition,” *Science*, DOI 10.1126/science.1208880. Cemetery evidence for a major demographic transition associated with farming.
- Timothy A. Kohler et al. (2017), “Greater post-Neolithic wealth disparities in Eurasia than in North America and Mesoamerica,” *Nature*, DOI 10.1038/nature24646. House-size distributions and the role of domesticated large mammals in agricultural extensification and inequality.

### Scene candidates

- Grain as stored solar energy: not because the first farmer necessarily earned more calories per hour, but because grain could be harvested, stored, counted, taxed, defended, and moved through institutions.
- A house-size distribution from the archaeological inequality dataset as evidence that surplus is also a political technology.

### Claim to resist

Do not equate agriculture with an immediate improvement in health, leisure, or labor productivity. Early agriculture may have expanded population and state capacity while reducing individual welfare on some dimensions.

### Checks before drafting

- Separate labor productivity from land productivity and population carrying capacity.
- Verify which archaeological sequence supports each demographic claim.
- Keep Eurasian large-animal arguments separate from universal claims about agriculture.

## Chapter 3 — Coal and the Compression of Time

### Question

Did coal and steam remove the land, seasonality, and biomass constraints that limited preindustrial economies, and how quickly did that translate into measured growth?

### Primary evidence stack

- UK Department for Energy Security and Net Zero, historical coal production, availability, and consumption series, 1853–2025.
- John Kanefsky, *Early Engines Database*, 2024 version, recording stationary steam engines beginning useful work in the British Isles before 1800.
- Nicholas Crafts (2004), “Steam as a General Purpose Technology: A Growth Accounting Perspective,” *Economic Journal*, DOI 10.1111/j.1468-0297.2003.00200.x.
- William Stanley Jevons, *The Coal Question* (1865/1866), as a contemporary document showing how a Victorian economist understood coal’s systemic role and the possibility that efficiency could increase demand.
- Roger Fouquet and Peter Pearson (1998) for the longer UK energy transition.

### Scene candidates

- A mine pumping engine whose first economic purpose was not factory production but removing water so miners could reach more coal.
- Jevons writing while Britain was already deep inside a coal system, worried not that coal was useless but that its usefulness made the nation dependent on it.

### Claim to resist

Do not make Watt’s patent the instant cause of industrial growth. Crafts finds steam’s measured contribution small before 1830 and peaking much later, with high-pressure steam after 1850 especially important.

### Checks before drafting

- Separate invention date, diffusion date, and macroeconomic impact date.
- Avoid using post-1853 official coal data as though it directly measures eighteenth-century output.
- Validate any earlier coal reconstruction against its original historical source.

## Chapter 4 — The Grid

### Question

What did electricity change that steam shafts, local water power, and isolated generators could not?

### Primary evidence stack

- Thomas A. Edison Papers chronology for 1882, including Holborn Viaduct and the September 4 opening of Pearl Street.
- U.S. Census Bureau, *Central Electric Light and Power Stations, 1902*.
- U.S. Census Bureau, *Central Electric Light and Power Stations and Street and Electric Railways, 1912*.
- Martin Fiszbein, Jeanne Lafortune, Ethan Lewis, and José Tessada, “Powering Up Productivity: The Effects of Electrification on U.S. Manufacturing,” NBER Working Paper 28076, revised 2024.
- Warren Devine (1983), “From Shafts to Wires,” *Journal of Economic History*, for the factory-level organizational shift from line shafts to unit drive.

### Scene candidates

- Pearl Street as a network rather than a bulb story: generation, conductors, meters, loads, finance, and customers had to function together.
- The factory ceiling before electrification: shafts, belts, friction, and machine placement organized around a single mechanical power path.

### Claim to resist

Do not say electricity’s value was merely that it delivered the same energy more efficiently. Its controllability and modularity changed factory layout, capital use, work organization, and where power could be produced and consumed.

### Checks before drafting

- Pull comparable 1902/1907/1912 Census variables before quoting growth rates.
- Verify whether a productivity estimate refers to labor productivity, total factor productivity, output, employment, or wages.

## Chapter 5 — Energy Becomes Invisible

### Question

Why does progress often appear as falling energy-service cost rather than visibly rising fuel consumption?

### Primary evidence stack

- Roger Fouquet and Peter Pearson (2006), “Seven Centuries of Energy Services: The Price and Use of Light in the United Kingdom.”
- William D. Nordhaus (1996/1997), “Do Real-Output and Real-Wage Measures Capture Reality? The History of Lighting Suggests Not.”
- Robert Ayres and Benjamin Warr (2005), useful work rather than raw energy.
- Zsuzsanna Csereklyei, María del Mar Rubio-Varas, and David Stern (2016), “Energy and economic growth: the stylized facts,” *Energy Journal*, DOI 10.5547/01956574.37.2.zcse.

### Scene candidates

- Compare the labor required to purchase a unit of light across candles, gas, incandescent bulbs, and modern electric lighting.
- Follow the same joule through conversion losses to show why primary energy and useful work tell different stories.

### Claim to resist

Do not infer welfare from energy input alone. A civilization can deliver far more light, motion, cooling, or computation with less primary energy per unit of service.

### Checks before drafting

- Normalize lighting comparisons to a consistent service unit.
- Keep nominal price, real price, energy price, and service price distinct.

## Chapter 6 — The Efficiency Paradox

### Question

Can efficiency simultaneously reduce energy needed per service and expand total demand for the service?

### Primary evidence stack

- William Stanley Jevons, *The Coal Question* (1865/1866), especially his argument that more economical use of coal could increase rather than reduce aggregate consumption.
- Harry Saunders (1992), “The Khazzoom-Brookes Postulate and Neoclassical Growth,” *Energy Journal*, DOI 10.5547/ISSN0195-6574-EJ-Vol13-No4-7.
- Csereklyei, Rubio-Varas, and Stern (2016) on declining energy intensity with rising income.
- Allan Mazur (2011), “Does increasing energy or electricity consumption improve quality of life in industrial nations?” *Energy Policy*, DOI 10.1016/j.enpol.2011.02.024.
- Joel Millward-Hopkins et al. (2020), “Providing decent living with minimum energy: A global scenario,” *Global Environmental Change*, DOI 10.1016/j.gloenvcha.2020.102168.

### Scene candidates

- Jevons confronting an apparent contradiction: a more efficient engine can make power cheaper enough that society uses much more of it.
- A modern wealthy country where life expectancy or other quality-of-life indicators saturate even as energy use remains high.

### Claim to resist

Neither “efficiency always saves energy” nor “efficiency always backfires” is defensible. Rebound magnitude depends on technology, price elasticity, income effects, sector, and time horizon.

### Checks before drafting

- Define direct, indirect, economy-wide, and transformational rebound separately.
- Treat Mazur’s industrial-country finding as evidence of saturation, not proof that energy is irrelevant to welfare.

## Chapter 7 — The Age of Electricity

### Question

Is electricity becoming the preferred carrier of a growing share of useful energy because it is unusually controllable, convertible, and compatible with low-carbon generation?

### Primary evidence stack

- IEA, *Electricity 2026*.
- IEA, *Global Energy Review 2026*.
- Energy Institute, *Statistical Review of World Energy 2026*.
- IRENA, *Renewable Capacity Statistics 2026*.
- IRENA, *Renewable Power Generation Costs in 2025* (published 2026).
- National Laboratory of the Rockies, Q1-2025 Solar Photovoltaic System Cost Benchmarks, DOI 10.7799/3009689.
- Goksin Kavlak, James McNerney, and Jessika Trancik (2018), “Evaluating the causes of cost reduction in photovoltaic modules,” *Energy Policy*, DOI 10.1016/j.enpol.2018.08.015.

### Scene candidates

- A heat pump, EV motor, electrolyzer, induction furnace, and GPU cluster as very different end uses sharing the same carrier.
- The grid as an energy API: many generators behind it, many machines ahead of it.

### Claim to resist

Do not call electrification automatically decarbonized. The emissions consequence depends on generation mix, timing, marginal supply, infrastructure, and lifecycle effects.

### Checks before drafting

- Separate capacity additions from actual generation.
- Separate electricity-demand observations from IEA forecasts.
- Use the same primary-energy accounting convention when comparing eras or institutions.

## Chapter 8 — Intelligence Has a Power Bill

### Question

How directly is frontier computation now constrained by electricity delivery, power density, cooling, and grid connection?

### Primary evidence stack

- IEA, *Energy and AI* (2025).
- Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, *2024 United States Data Center Energy Usage Report*, DOI 10.71468/P1WC7Q.
- NERC, *2025 Long-Term Reliability Assessment*, updated 2026.
- DOE, 2026 draft *National Transmission Needs Study*.
- MLCommons power measurement results can be added for specific hardware efficiency claims if a chapter draft needs device-level performance.

### Scene candidates

- A data-center site where the scarce input is not floor space or chips but the date at which hundreds of megawatts can be energized.
- Contrast algorithmic or hardware efficiency gains with total demand growth to make rebound concrete.

### Claim to resist

Do not write that AI demand is known with precision. Current forecasts are unusually sensitive to model architecture, chip efficiency, utilization, inference volume, cooling, and the pace of data-center buildout.

### Checks before drafting

- Preserve scenario ranges.
- Distinguish IT load from total facility electricity and PUE.
- Separate announced data-center capacity from connected and operating load.

## Chapter 9 — The Constraint Is the Network

### Question

As generation technologies become cheaper and faster to deploy, are networks, interconnection, transformers, firm capacity, and permitting becoming the binding constraints?

### Primary evidence stack

- Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, *Queued Up* 2026.
- DOE, 2026 draft *National Transmission Needs Study* and 2023 final study for a stable baseline.
- DOE/NREL/PNNL, *National Transmission Planning Study*.
- FERC Orders 2023 and 1920/1920-A/1920-B.
- NERC, *2025 Long-Term Reliability Assessment*.
- DOE Office of Electricity transformer supply-chain materials: reported distribution-transformer lead times increased from 3–6 months in 2019 to 12–30 months in 2023.

### Scene candidates

- A generation project waiting in an interconnection queue while the panels, turbines, or batteries themselves are commercially available.
- A transformer as an unglamorous object that can become the pacing item for an entire energy system.

### Claim to resist

Queue size is not buildout. Many queued projects withdraw, duplicate alternatives, or never reach construction.

### Checks before drafting

- Use completed-project rates and withdrawal rates alongside gross queue capacity.
- Distinguish transmission, distribution, and interconnection bottlenecks.
- Mark the 2026 DOE Needs Study as draft until final publication.

## Chapter 10 — The Return of the Atom

### Question

What can nuclear fission contribute to a high-electricity system, and what does its mixed construction history imply about scale, standardization, regulation, and institutional capability?

### Primary evidence stack

- IAEA Power Reactor Information System (PRIS).
- Jessica Lovering, Arthur Yip, and Ted Nordhaus (2016), “Historical construction costs of global nuclear power reactors,” *Energy Policy*, DOI 10.1016/j.enpol.2016.01.011.
- IEA electricity and investment reports for contemporary nuclear additions and capital flows.
- Country-level regulator and utility records should be added when the chapter chooses specific construction case studies.

### Scene candidates

- Compare two reactor programs built from similar physics but very different institutional environments and construction outcomes.
- Use PRIS to separate the stock of operating reactors from current construction momentum.

### Claim to resist

Do not flatten nuclear into “cheap” or “expensive.” Historical costs vary sharply by country, era, project structure, financing environment, standardization, and regulatory regime.

### Checks before drafting

- Compare overnight cost separately from financing cost and delivered electricity cost.
- Normalize currency year and purchasing-power assumptions before cross-country comparison.
- Include construction duration and capacity factor alongside cost.

## Chapter 11 — Abundance and the New Frontier

### Question

Which technologies are already producing measurable abundance effects, which are on credible learning curves, and which remain frontier experiments?

### Primary evidence stack

- Micah Ziegler and Jessika Trancik (2021), lithium-ion improvement and cost-decline series, DOI 10.1039/D0EE02681F.
- Micah Ziegler, Juhyun Song, and Jessika Trancik (2021), determinants of lithium-ion cost decline, DOI 10.1039/D1EE01313K.
- Kavlak, McNerney, and Trancik (2018) on PV cost mechanisms.
- NLR Q1-2025 PV system cost benchmark.
- IRENA, *Renewable Power Generation Costs in 2025*.
- DOE Enhanced Geothermal Shot analysis and Utah/Fervo technical results, with company claims treated separately from independently verified field data.
- A. Pak et al. and A. L. Kritcher et al. (2024) on the first NIF target gain above unity.
- EUROfusion/UKAEA JET 2024 deuterium-tritium campaign record.

### Scene candidates

- A lithium-ion cell as a technology whose service improved along several dimensions at once: cost, energy density, cycle performance, manufacturing scale.
- NIF as a lesson in metric discipline: target gain above one is a scientific milestone, not a commercial power plant.

### Claim to resist

Do not put demonstrated solar, batteries, fission, geothermal drilling progress, and experimental fusion on the same readiness level.

### Checks before drafting

- Label every frontier number as observed, target, forecast, or scenario.
- Distinguish cell price from installed storage-system cost.
- Distinguish target gain, engineering breakeven, wall-plug efficiency, plant recirculating power, and commercial cost for fusion.

## Chapter 12 — The Convergence

### Question

After the full historical record, is energy best understood as the deepest physical substrate of expanding capability, or does the thesis fail once efficiency, institutions, distribution, and welfare saturation are included?

### Primary evidence stack

- Lotka (1922), White (1943), Kardashev (1964), and Chaisson (2011) as the explicit intellectual lineage.
- Ayres and Warr (2005), Fouquet and Pearson, and Csereklyei et al. for the energy-quality/useful-work correction.
- Mazur (2011), Rao and Min (2018), and Millward-Hopkins et al. (2020) for the welfare-saturation and sufficiency countercase.
- Tracking SDG7 2026 and WHO household-air-pollution data for unequal access and the human cost of low-quality energy.
- Global Carbon Budget 2025 for the environmental cost of the fossil-fuel era.

### Synthesis test

The conclusion should survive all four of these statements at once:

1. Energy is physically necessary for material action, computation, transport, heat, and industrial transformation.
2. More raw energy is not equivalent to more welfare.
3. Institutions, knowledge, and coordination determine what energy can become.
4. A major form of progress is obtaining more useful capability per unit of primary energy, pollution, land, material, risk, and human labor.

If the thesis only works after ignoring any one of those statements, it is not ready for the final chapter.
