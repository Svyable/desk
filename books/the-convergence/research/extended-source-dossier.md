# Extended Source Dossier

This companion to `primary-sources.md` fills the research gaps identified before chapter drafting. Sources are prioritized because they are original research, historical primary records, official datasets, regulatory records, or laboratory results. Secondary interpretation can still be useful for discovery and context, but quantitative chapter claims should trace back here or to an equivalent primary source.

## Fire and deep history

### 21. Francesco Berna et al. — Wonderwerk Cave fire evidence (2012)

- **Title:** “Microstratigraphic evidence of in situ fire in the Acheulean strata of Wonderwerk Cave, Northern Cape province, South Africa.”
- **Type:** Original archaeological research, *PNAS* 109(20), E1215–E1220.
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1117620109
- **Use:** Strong Chapter 1 anchor. Micromorphology and FTIR identify burned bone and ashed plant remains in an archaeological context around 1.0 Ma.
- **Caution:** Evidence of burning in an archaeological context is not equivalent to a precisely dated “invention of fire.”

### 22. Naama Goren-Inbar et al. — Gesher Benot Ya‘aqov fire evidence (2004)

- **Title:** “Evidence of Hominin Control of Fire at Gesher Benot Ya‘aqov, Israel.”
- **Type:** Original archaeological research, *Science* 304, 725–727.
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1095443
- **PubMed:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15118160/
- **Use:** Burned seeds, wood, and flint around 790,000 years ago, including spatial patterning interpreted as possible hearth locations.
- **Caution:** Use the authors’ language about control and spatial evidence; avoid escalating it into certainty about routine fire-making without later evidence.

## Agriculture, population, and concentrated surplus

### 23. Samuel Bowles — first-farmer productivity (2011)

- **Title:** “Cultivation of cereals by the first farmers was not more productive than foraging.”
- **Type:** Original quantitative research, *PNAS* 108(12), 4760–4765.
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1010733108
- **Full text:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3064343/
- **Use:** Essential counterweight for Chapter 2. Estimates caloric returns per labor hour and finds the evidence inconsistent with the idea that the first farmers simply had higher labor productivity than foragers.
- **Caution:** Estimates are uncertain and depend on ethnographic/historical analogues; use as a challenge to simplistic claims rather than a universal point estimate.

### 24. Jean-Pierre Bocquet-Appel — Neolithic Demographic Transition (2011)

- **Title:** “When the world’s population took off: the springboard of the Neolithic Demographic Transition.”
- **Type:** Original archaeological-demographic research, *Science* 333, 560–561.
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1208880
- **PubMed:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21798934/
- **Use:** Cemetery age-structure evidence for a major fertility/population shift associated with the foraging-to-farming transition.
- **Caution:** Demographic expansion does not itself prove higher individual welfare or labor productivity.

### 25. Timothy A. Kohler et al. — post-Neolithic inequality (2017)

- **Title:** “Greater post-Neolithic wealth disparities in Eurasia than in North America and Mesoamerica.”
- **Type:** Original archaeological comparative research, *Nature* 551, 619–622.
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1038/nature24646
- **Full text:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5714260/
- **Use:** House-size Gini estimates linking domestication, sociopolitical scale, and long-run wealth inequality; useful for showing that concentrated surplus changes institutions and distribution.
- **Caution:** House size is a proxy, and regional coverage is uneven.

## Coal, steam, and industrial diffusion

### 26. John Kanefsky — Early Engines Database (2024 version)

- **Type:** Research database of stationary steam engines that began useful work in the British Isles before 1801.
- **Database:** https://industrial-archaeology.org/EarlyEngines/help.php
- **Use:** Diffusion timeline for Chapter 3; separates the existence of engine designs from actual installations and applications.
- **Caution:** Records first erection/useful work, not full utilization histories.

### 27. Nicholas Crafts — steam and economic growth (2004)

- **Title:** “Steam as a General Purpose Technology: A Growth Accounting Perspective.”
- **Type:** Original economic-history research, *Economic Journal* 114, 338–351.
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-0297.2003.00200.x
- **Use:** Strong correction to invention-centric history. Finds little steam contribution to growth before 1830 and peak effects much later.
- **Caution:** Growth accounting estimates a specific measured contribution and should not be treated as the only possible causal pathway.

### 28. UK DESNZ — historical coal statistics, 1853–2025

- **Type:** Official government statistical dataset.
- **Dataset:** https://www.gov.uk/government/statistical-data-sets/historical-coal-data-coal-production-availability-and-consumption
- **Use:** Direct official series for later British coal production, availability, and consumption; current file updated July 2026.
- **Caution:** The official series begins in 1853 and must not be back-projected into the eighteenth century without another source.

### 29. William Stanley Jevons — *The Coal Question* (1865/1866)

- **Type:** Historical primary text.
- **Facsimile/text access:** https://oll.libertyfund.org/titles/jevons-the-coal-question
- **Use:** Contemporary statement of coal’s systemic role in Victorian Britain and the original intellectual source for what later became known as the Jevons paradox.
- **Caution:** Jevons’ depletion forecasts are historical arguments, not modern reserve estimates.

## Early electrification and productivity

### 30. U.S. Census Bureau — *Central Electric Light and Power Stations, 1902*

- **Type:** Historical government primary statistical report.
- **PDF:** https://www2.census.gov/prod2/decennial/documents/12176799.pdf
- **Use:** Direct baseline for the early U.S. central-station industry.
- **Caution:** Definitions and accounting conventions differ from modern electric-industry statistics.

### 31. U.S. Census Bureau — electrical industries, 1912

- **Type:** Historical government primary statistical report.
- **Catalog:** https://www.census.gov/library/publications/1910/dec/bulletins/manufacturing-and-industry.html
- **Use:** Comparative 1902/1907/1912 statistics for stations, power equipment, lamps, motors, meters, and network growth.
- **Caution:** Verify exact table definitions before reproducing growth rates.

### 32. Thomas A. Edison Papers — 1882 chronology

- **Type:** Scholarly archive of primary Edison records and chronology.
- **Source:** https://edison.rutgers.edu/edit-chronology/chronology-details/1881---1890/1882
- **Use:** Documentary anchor for Holborn Viaduct and the September 4, 1882 opening of Pearl Street.
- **Caution:** Avoid a single-inventor narrative; central-station electrification involved many engineers, firms, financiers, standards, and competing systems.

### 33. Martin Fiszbein, Jeanne Lafortune, Ethan G. Lewis, José Tessada — manufacturing electrification (2020; rev. 2024)

- **Title:** “Powering Up Productivity: The Effects of Electrification on U.S. Manufacturing.”
- **Type:** Original empirical economic-history research, NBER Working Paper 28076.
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.3386/w28076
- **Use:** Identification strategy using historical variation in industry energy intensity and proximity to early hydroelectric plants; finds rapid and lasting productivity effects plus organizational changes.
- **Caution:** Explain the identification strategy; do not substitute a simple national correlation.

## Energy services, efficiency, and rebound

### 34. William D. Nordhaus — history of lighting (1996/1997)

- **Title:** “Do Real-Output and Real-Wage Measures Capture Reality? The History of Lighting Suggests Not.”
- **Type:** Original economic reconstruction in *The Economics of New Goods*.
- **NBER:** https://www.nber.org/books-and-chapters/economics-new-goods/do-real-output-and-real-wage-measures-capture-reality-history-lighting-suggests-not
- **Use:** Chapter 5 evidence for measuring the delivered service—light—rather than fuel or conventional price indexes alone.
- **Caution:** Harmonize service units before comparing with Fouquet/Pearson.

### 35. Harry D. Saunders — rebound in growth theory (1992)

- **Title:** “The Khazzoom-Brookes Postulate and Neoclassical Growth.”
- **Type:** Original theoretical research, *Energy Journal* 13(4), 131–148.
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.5547/ISSN0195-6574-EJ-Vol13-No4-7
- **Use:** Formal bridge from Jevons’ historical observation to economy-wide rebound possibilities.
- **Caution:** A theoretical possibility is not an empirical estimate of rebound magnitude for every technology.

### 36. Zsuzsanna Csereklyei, María del Mar Rubio-Varas, David I. Stern — energy and growth facts (2016)

- **Title:** “Energy and economic growth: the stylized facts.”
- **Type:** Original empirical research, *Energy Journal* 37(2), 223–255.
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.5547/01956574.37.2.zcse
- **Repository:** https://openresearch-repository.anu.edu.au/items/7cec9f3d-9478-4a9f-be61-a0712d6c2c93
- **Use:** Cross-country and historical evidence on income, energy use, declining energy intensity, and rising energy quality.
- **Caution:** Stylized facts constrain theories but do not alone identify causal direction.

## Welfare, sufficiency, access, and external cost

### 37. Allan Mazur — saturation in industrial nations (2011)

- **Title:** “Does increasing energy or electricity consumption improve quality of life in industrial nations?”
- **Type:** Original longitudinal empirical research, *Energy Policy* 39, 2568–2572.
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.enpol.2011.02.024
- **Use:** Important counter-evidence: at already-high energy use, additional per-capita consumption was not associated with further gains in the quality-of-life indicators studied.
- **Caution:** This does not imply energy access is unimportant in lower-income countries.

### 38. Narasimha D. Rao and Jihoon Min — Decent Living Standards (2018)

- **Title:** “Decent Living Standards: Material Prerequisites for Human Wellbeing.”
- **Type:** Original conceptual/quantitative framework, *Social Indicators Research* 138, 225–244.
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s11205-017-1650-0
- **Full text:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6013539/
- **Use:** Separates material prerequisites for basic capability from affluent consumption and income alone.
- **Caution:** A normative framework is not a measured universal energy requirement.

### 39. Joel Millward-Hopkins et al. — minimum-energy decent-living scenario (2020)

- **Title:** “Providing decent living with minimum energy: A global scenario.”
- **Type:** Original bottom-up modelling, *Global Environmental Change* 65, 102168.
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.gloenvcha.2020.102168
- **Open repository:** https://pure.iiasa.ac.at/id/eprint/16764/
- **Use:** Serious challenge to the idea that high human welfare requires present rich-country energy throughput.
- **Caution:** Scenario results depend on advanced technology and strong sufficiency assumptions; present as modeled possibility, not forecast.

### 40. Tracking SDG7 — Energy Progress Report 2026

- **Type:** Joint official statistical report from IEA, IRENA, UN DESA Statistics Division, World Bank, and WHO.
- **Report:** https://www.iea.org/reports/tracking-sdg7-the-energy-progress-report-2026
- **Use:** Current global dashboard for electricity access, clean cooking, energy efficiency, renewables, and international cooperation.
- **Caution:** Use exact indicator years because publication year is newer than many underlying observations.

### 41. World Health Organization — household air pollution (2025)

- **Type:** Official health evidence summary with underlying WHO data.
- **Source:** https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/household-air-pollution-and-health
- **Use:** Makes the cost of low-quality household energy concrete: health burden, time burden, and gendered exposure.
- **Caution:** Preserve the data year attached to mortality and access estimates.

### 42. Global Carbon Budget 2025 (published 2026)

- **Type:** Original community carbon-cycle dataset and data paper, *Earth System Science Data* 18, 3211–3288.
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.5194/essd-18-3211-2026
- **Dataset DOI:** https://doi.org/10.18160/GCP-2025
- **Use:** Quantitative externality spine for fossil CO2 emissions and carbon sinks.
- **Caution:** Fossil emissions, land-use emissions, atmospheric growth, and sink estimates have different uncertainty structures.

## Modern grid constraints

### 43. U.S. Department of Energy — draft National Transmission Needs Study (2026)

- **Type:** Official statutory transmission-needs assessment, draft released July 9, 2026.
- **Source:** https://www.energy.gov/oe/national-transmission-needs-study
- **Use:** Current evidence on load growth, congestion, regional and interregional transmission needs, and AI/manufacturing/electrification pressures.
- **Caution:** **Draft** until final publication. Keep it labeled as such.

### 44. NERC — 2025 Long-Term Reliability Assessment (updated 2026)

- **Type:** Official North American bulk-power reliability assessment.
- **Hub:** https://www.nerc.com/our-work/assessments/long-term-reliability-assessments
- **Report:** https://www.nerc.com/globalassets/our-work/assessments/nerc_ltra_2025.pdf
- **Use:** Ten-year peak-demand and resource-adequacy outlook; identifies data centers and other large loads as major contributors to forecast demand growth.
- **Caution:** Forecast demand includes uncertain proposed large loads and differs across balancing areas.

### 45. U.S. Department of Energy — distribution-transformer supply chain

- **Type:** Official Office of Electricity market/supply-chain analysis.
- **Source:** https://www.energy.gov/oe/supply-chain-and-market-analysis
- **Use:** Concrete example of bottleneck migration. DOE reports distribution-transformer lead times rising from roughly 3–6 months in 2019 to 12–30 months in 2023.
- **Caution:** The 2023 lead-time figure is the latest cited data on the current page, not a 2026 measurement.

### 46. Federal Energy Regulatory Commission — Order No. 1920 series

- **Type:** Primary federal regulatory record.
- **Explainer and links to governing orders:** https://www.ferc.gov/explainer-transmission-planning-and-cost-allocation-final-rule
- **Use:** Primary institutional source for long-term regional transmission planning and cost-allocation reforms.
- **Caution:** For legal claims cite the actual final/rehearing orders, not only the explainer.

## Solar, storage, and technological learning

### 47. Micah S. Ziegler and Jessika E. Trancik — lithium-ion improvement rates (2021)

- **Title:** “Re-examining rates of lithium-ion battery technology improvement and cost decline.”
- **Type:** Original data harmonization and analysis, *Energy & Environmental Science* 14, 1635–1651.
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1039/D0EE02681F
- **Use:** Historical cost and performance series; reports about a 97% real price decline per unit energy capacity from commercial introduction through the study period.
- **Caution:** Cell prices are not installed stationary-storage system costs.

### 48. Micah S. Ziegler, Juhyun Song, Jessika E. Trancik — battery cost mechanisms (2021)

- **Title:** “Determinants of lithium-ion battery technology cost decline.”
- **Type:** Original cost-decomposition analysis, *Energy & Environmental Science* 14, 6074–6098.
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1039/D1EE01313K
- **Use:** Identifies contributions from charge density, materials, R&D, scale, and other mechanisms rather than invoking an unexplained learning curve.
- **Caution:** Decomposition depends on model structure and period.

### 49. Goksin Kavlak, James McNerney, Jessika E. Trancik — photovoltaic cost mechanisms (2018)

- **Title:** “Evaluating the causes of cost reduction in photovoltaic modules.”
- **Type:** Original cost-decomposition analysis, *Energy Policy* 123, 700–710.
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.enpol.2018.08.015
- **Use:** PV analogue to the battery work; identifies efficiency, materials, R&D, scale, and policy-enabled market growth as contributors.
- **Caution:** Module cost is not total installed-system cost.

### 50. National Laboratory of the Rockies — Q1-2025 Solar Photovoltaic System Cost Benchmarks

- **Type:** DOE-sponsored national-laboratory dataset and cost model.
- **Dataset:** https://data.nlr.gov/submissions/304
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.7799/3009689
- **Use:** Current U.S. benchmark model for residential, commercial, and utility PV system costs; dataset updated March 2026.
- **Caution:** U.S. benchmark costs are not global auction prices or realized costs in every market.

### 51. IRENA — Renewable Power Generation Costs in 2025 (published 2026)

- **Type:** Official intergovernmental project-cost database and report.
- **Report/data:** https://www.irena.org/Publications/2026/Jul/Renewable-Power-Generation-Costs-in-2025
- **Use:** Current cost evidence across solar, onshore/offshore wind, hydro, geothermal, CSP, and bioenergy.
- **Caution:** Levelized cost methodology, financing assumptions, and project sample must be preserved when comparing technologies.

## Nuclear construction

### 52. Jessica R. Lovering, Arthur Yip, Ted Nordhaus — historical reactor construction costs (2016)

- **Title:** “Historical construction costs of global nuclear power reactors.”
- **Type:** Original reactor-level dataset and analysis, *Energy Policy* 91, 371–382.
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.enpol.2016.01.011
- **Use:** Covers 349 reactors in seven countries and shows highly heterogeneous cost histories rather than one universal nuclear cost curve.
- **Caution:** Overnight construction costs exclude important financing effects and coverage is incomplete for several major reactor countries.

## Fusion: scientific milestones versus power plants

### 55. A. Pak et al. — first NIF target gain above unity (2024)

- **Title:** “Observations and properties of the first laboratory fusion experiment to exceed a target gain of unity.”
- **Type:** Original experimental paper, *Physical Review E* 109, 025203.
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.109.025203
- **Use:** Experimental evidence for 3.1 ± 0.16 MJ fusion yield from 2.05 MJ delivered laser light in the first target-gain-above-one shot.
- **Caution:** Target gain does not include the facility’s wall-plug energy consumption.

### 56. A. L. Kritcher et al. — NIF gain-above-one design (2024)

- **Title:** “Design of the first fusion experiment to achieve target energy gain G>1.”
- **Type:** Original experimental/design paper, *Physical Review E* 109, 025204.
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.109.025204
- **Use:** Shows the target/laser design changes behind the result and notes later replication at higher target gain.
- **Caution:** Keep target gain distinct from engineering breakeven and commercial generation.

### 57. EUROfusion / UKAEA — JET final deuterium-tritium campaign (2024)

- **Type:** Primary facility/consortium result.
- **EUROfusion:** https://euro-fusion.org/eurofusion-news/dte3record/
- **UKAEA:** https://www.gov.uk/government/news/jets-final-tritium-experiments-yield-new-fusion-energy-record
- **Use:** 69 MJ of fusion energy over a five-second deuterium-tritium pulse, useful as the magnetic-confinement counterpart to NIF.
- **Caution:** JET was an experimental facility, not a net-electric power plant.

## Research rule created by these additions

A chapter should not be considered research-ready merely because it has five sources. It is ready when it has:

1. at least one source capable of supporting the chapter’s central empirical claim;
2. at least one serious result that could narrow or contradict that claim;
3. a source for every numerical historical scene that cannot be observed directly today;
4. a clear distinction between measured data, reconstruction, forecast, policy target, and experimental milestone; and
5. an identified dataset or table for every planned quantitative figure.
