# Working Notes — Reactor Before Us

## Chronology

- **~2.4 billion years ago:** broad Great Oxidation Event interval; changing surface redox conditions become relevant to uranium mobility and concentration.
- **~2.0 billion years ago:** natural uranium contains several percent U-235 rather than today’s ~0.7%, making thermal criticality physically plausible in sufficiently rich ore.
- **~1.8–2.0 billion years ago:** accepted broad interval for operation of the Oklo/Okelobondo natural reactor zones; exact ages vary by zone/model/source.
- **1956:** Paul Kuroda publishes calculations describing conditions under which a natural uranium deposit could sustain a chain reaction.
- **1972:** anomalously depleted U-235 is recognized in uranium from Gabon; fission-product evidence rapidly establishes natural reactor operation.
- **1970s–1990s:** Oklo becomes a major natural-analogue site for reactor physics, isotope geochemistry, radionuclide transport, and waste-disposal research.
- **2004:** Meshik, Hohenberg, and Pravdivtseva publish noble-gas evidence interpreted as ~30-minute active / ~2.5-hour dormant cycling.
- **2004–2006:** reactor-model work sharpens debate over Oklo constraints on possible time variation of alpha.
- **2018:** high-resolution isotope imaging reports fissionogenic Cs/Ba capture in Ru aggregates roughly five years after shutdown, adding unusual temporal resolution to the migration record.
