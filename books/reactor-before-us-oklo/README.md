# Reactor Before Us: Oklo and the Nuclear Machine Earth Built First

**Author:** Sven Hardy Benson  
**Status:** Drafting  
**Chapters:** 18

## Thesis

Two billion years before engineers enriched uranium, machined fuel pellets, designed moderator channels, or wrote reactor-control procedures, a uranium deposit in what is now Gabon crossed criticality and operated as a natural fission system. Oklo matters not because nature built a modern power plant by accident. It matters because one geological site became, at different scales, a reactor-physics experiment, a nuclear-waste migration experiment, an isotope archive, and a two-billion-year test of whether some supposedly fixed quantities in physics have actually remained fixed.

The book follows the evidence outward from one tiny isotopic discrepancy noticed in 1972. It asks what had to be true for Earth to run a chain reaction; how the system apparently cycled without engineered controls; what moved and what stayed put after shutdown; what Oklo can and cannot tell us about geological disposal; and how seriously we should take claims that the fossil reactor constrains changes in the fine-structure constant over deep time.

## Reader promise

A nuclear book in which the reactor has no designer, the control system is groundwater, the operating log is trapped xenon, the spent fuel is stone, and the oldest experiment in the book ran before complex life existed.

## Evidence discipline

1. **Verified / documented:** anomalous U-235 depletion at Oklo; fission-product isotope patterns; multiple reactor zones; natural criticality; neutron-moderation requirements; fission-product and transuranic signatures; published evidence for pulsed operation; measured retention and migration of specific radionuclides.
2. **Credible but model-dependent:** precise reactor temperatures, geometry, power histories, water fractions, total energy production, operating lifetimes, and some reconstructions of neutron spectra.
3. **Disputed / incomplete:** how directly particular Oklo observations transfer to engineered geological repositories; the most precise bounds on time variation of fundamental constants; exact hydrological histories; claims that nature provided a simple proof that nuclear waste is geologically solved.
4. **Speculative synthesis:** Oklo as a naturally executed stress test of nuclear assumptions across time; geological self-regulation as an inversion of engineered safety thinking; the idea that an ore body can become a physical archive with several different scientific meanings depending on which isotope you ask.

## Publication gate

This is a coherent first-pass manuscript project, not a claim that Desk's publication-length gate has been met. Before publication, each chapter must exceed 3,000 words and chapter-only prose must exceed 65,000 words. The next pass must deepen French and Gabonese archival sources, recover original 1972 discovery papers and CEA records, build zone-by-zone burnup and isotope ledgers, and reconstruct the natural-analogue waste literature without turning Oklo into a slogan.
