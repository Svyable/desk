*Personalized Medicine at the Border of Permission.*

A book about what happens when medicine becomes specific enough that the treatment may be designed for one patient, manufactured in one batch, and legally available only through a narrow pathway or a particular jurisdiction. Montana's new experimental-treatment-center framework and Mexico's unevenly enforced but very real regulatory system are the central geographic case studies. The wider argument follows individualized antisense drugs, bespoke gene editing, neoantigen vaccines, personalized phages, autologous cell therapies, microbiome interventions and xenotransplantation to ask where compassionate access ends and uncontrolled experimentation begins.

The book rejects the easy story that innovation lives wherever regulation disappears. The strongest cases often arise from intense oversight used flexibly: emergency INDs, expanded access, platform manufacturing, independent review and rapid regulator-scientist collaboration. The policy question is how to preserve that speed while producing evidence, preventing exploitation and making geography less decisive in who gets a chance.
