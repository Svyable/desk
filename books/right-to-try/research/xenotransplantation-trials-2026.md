# Xenotransplantation trials — 2026 evidence note

Current evidence cutoff: August 31, 2026.

This note supports Chapter 10, **The Organ Before Approval**. It records the prospective trial architecture that now follows the early exceptional-access xenotransplant cases.

The sources below establish trial design, registry status, intended enrollment and follow-up. They do **not** establish efficacy.

## EXPAND — 10 GE Xenokidney

**ClinicalTrials.gov ID:** NCT06878560  
**Official title:** *EXPAND: A Prospective Study to Evaluate the Safety and Efficacy of the 10 GE Xenokidney in Patients With End-stage Renal Disease (ESRD)*  
**Sponsor:** United Therapeutics  
**Registry:** https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06878560

ClinicalTrials.gov describes EXPAND as a multicenter, open-label study of a porcine-derived kidney containing ten intentional genomic alterations for people with end-stage renal disease.

Key registry facts as of this note:

- status: recruiting;
- actual study start: October 29, 2025;
- estimated enrollment: 50 participants;
- initial post-transplant evaluation period: 24 weeks;
- long-term follow-up: lifetime for recipients of the 10 GE Xenokidney;
- long-term measures include participant survival, xenokidney survival and screening for zoonotic infections;
- Group 1 consists of people deemed ineligible for conventional allogeneic kidney transplantation for medical reasons;
- Group 2 consists of people on the OPTN kidney-transplant waitlist who are more likely to die or remain untransplanted within five years than to receive a human kidney.

The registry's detailed description uses a seamless Phase 1/2/3 framing, while the displayed ClinicalTrials.gov phase fields may render as Phase 1 and Phase 2. Preserve the registry language rather than forcing the study into a conventional single-phase shorthand.

**Interpretive cautions:**

- Fifty is estimated enrollment, not evidence that fifty transplants have occurred.
- Recruiting status establishes that a formal trial exists and is open under the registered conditions; it does not establish benefit.
- The population is deliberately selected around poor access to ordinary human kidney transplantation, which limits naive comparison with the broader ESRD population.
- Open-label transplantation cannot conceal which organ a participant received; later causal interpretation will therefore depend on design, endpoints and comparator choices rather than blinding.

## EXTEND — GGTA1 KO Thymokidney

**ClinicalTrials.gov ID:** NCT07224763  
**Official title:** *EXTEND: A Prospective Study to Evaluate the Safety and Efficacy of GGTA1 KO Thymokidney XenoTransplantation in Patients With End-stage Renal Disease (ESRD)*  
**Sponsor:** United Therapeutics  
**Registry:** https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07224763

ClinicalTrials.gov describes EXTEND as a multicenter, open-label Phase 1/2/3 study of a GGTA1-knockout porcine kidney combined with thymic tissue.

Key registry facts as of this note:

- status: recruiting;
- estimated study start: June 2026;
- estimated enrollment: 50 participants;
- Part A: transplantation followed by a 24-week post-transplant evaluation period;
- Part B: long-term follow-up for the recipient's lifetime if the graft remains, or for 52 weeks after nephrectomy if the xenograft must be removed;
- long-term measures include participant survival, thymokidney survival and screening for zoonotic infections;
- enrollment uses the same broad two-group access logic as EXPAND: people medically ineligible for conventional transplantation and waitlisted people with a poor probability of receiving a human kidney before death or continued non-transplantation.

**Interpretive cautions:**

- The trial evaluates a different biological architecture from the 10 GE Xenokidney. Results from one program should not be treated as class-wide evidence for every product called a pig kidney or xenokidney.
- Estimated enrollment and estimated dates can change as the registry is updated.
- Long-term surveillance is part of the treatment architecture, not a minor postscript, because infectious, immunological and graft-survival questions can unfold over years.

## What the two kidney trials establish

Together, EXPAND and EXTEND establish a regulatory transition that matters to the book.

The field has moved beyond isolated exceptional-access kidney cases into prospective, multi-participant clinical development. At the same time, the two registered studies make clear that **xenotransplantation is not one stable product**. Gene-editing strategy, source-organ design, immune engineering, immunosuppression, monitoring and clinical protocol can differ materially.

That means a successful case or even a successful trial cannot automatically validate everything sold under the category name.

The studies also make the longitudinal character of the intervention unusually visible. The operation occurs once. The regulated relationship can last for the recipient's lifetime.

The lifetime follow-up provisions are evidence about the risk model: investigators and regulators are treating participant survival, graft survival and zoonotic surveillance as questions whose relevant clock extends far beyond the headline transplant event.

## What the two kidney trials do not establish

The registry records do not establish:

- that either xenokidney program is effective;
- that a planned sample size has been fully enrolled;
- that one product's safety profile transfers to a biologically different xenotransplant product;
- that early graft survival predicts lifetime graft survival;
- that the selected study population represents all people with end-stage renal disease; or
- that formal trial status resolves every ethical, public-health or reimbursement question raised by xenotransplantation.

Use the registry to establish **what is being tested and how**, not what the answer will be.

## Watch item — UHeart EXPRESS

**Primary source:** United Therapeutics investor-relations announcement, May 15, 2026.  
https://ir.unither.com/press-releases/2026/05-15-2026-230017490

United Therapeutics announced that FDA cleared its IND to proceed with the EXPRESS clinical study of UHeart, a porcine-derived heart with ten gene edits. The sponsor described an initial single-center cohort of up to two participants, with FDA review of safety and efficacy information from the first participant before a second transplant and another review before broader expansion. The announced design uses a 24-week primary post-transplant period followed by lifetime follow-up including survival, graft survival and zoonotic-infection screening.

The company describes the program as a seamless Phase 1/2/3 or “phaseless” study intended to support a future biologics-license application if the evidence supports expansion.

**Source limitation:** this is a sponsor announcement of FDA IND clearance and intended trial design. It is appropriate evidence that the sponsor announced regulatory clearance to proceed. It is **not** evidence that a transplant has occurred, that the study has expanded, that the intervention works, or that every planned feature remained unchanged after the announcement. Check ClinicalTrials.gov and later FDA or peer-reviewed records before using the program as evidence of completed clinical experience.

## Chapter-level inference

The strongest defensible inference is institutional rather than therapeutic:

> Exceptional access can be a bridge into structured development when early cases produce enough operational and biological knowledge to support a prospective trial. The transition does not eliminate uncertainty; it gives the uncertainty a protocol, a denominator and a follow-up clock.

That is the reason these trials belong in *Right To Try*. They are evidence about how an exceptional intervention can stop being only an exception.
