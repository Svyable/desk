# Evidence Ledger

## 1. Verified / declassified / documented

Camp Century was constructed by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers beginning in 1959 on the northwestern Greenland Ice Sheet. CRREL Technical Report 174 is an official engineering history.

PM-2A, part of the Army Nuclear Power Program, supplied power at Camp Century and was later shut down, dismantled and removed.

The Army explored a much larger ice-cap military concept associated with Project Iceworm; historical scholarship based on declassified records describes a dispersed missile network concept. The operational network was not built.

Camp Century drilling recovered a deep ice core and basal/subglacial material in 1966.

Colgan et al. 2016 published a peer-reviewed inventory/modeling study of the abandoned site and possible future waste remobilization under warming scenarios.

Christ et al. 2021 published peer-reviewed multi-proxy evidence that northwestern Greenland was ice-free and vegetated during past Pleistocene periods.

## 2. Credible but incomplete

Exactly which Danish officials knew which details of Iceworm, at which dates, requires archival reconstruction rather than a single binary claim.

Some Camp Century waste quantities are reconstructed from incomplete historical records and should retain uncertainty ranges.

The exact strategic engineering thresholds at which Army planners regarded tunnel deformation as disqualifying require deeper primary-document annotation.

## 3. Disputed / unsupported

No evidence located for an operational completed 600-missile subglacial network.

No basis for claiming the PM-2A reactor core remains buried at Camp Century; official USACE material says the reactor was dismantled and removed.

No basis for claiming current harmful contamination has already emerged from the site absent direct measurements.

## 4. Speculative synthesis

Camp Century can be read as a case of 'assumption debt': a long-lived physical commitment whose environmental model expires before the commitment does.

Classified physical programs generate a parallel archive in infrastructure, wastes, samples and altered landscapes that can outlive documentary secrecy.

The Camp Century core illustrates a general research principle: preserve anomalous or expensive-to-obtain samples because future instrumentation can extract information that the originating program could not anticipate.
