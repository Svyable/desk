# Contradiction and Incentive Audit

The public scientific framing of Camp Century was not simply fake; real engineering and glaciological research occurred. Avoid the lazy formulation that science was only a cover.

The existence of Iceworm does not imply that Denmark was uniformly deceived. Danish knowledge varied by person, office and level of detail. Primary Danish archival work is still needed.

The Army's 1965 engineering report concluded that subsurface camps were feasible and practical even as deformation problems were known. Treat this as evidence of a threshold disagreement about scale and maintenance, not proof of incompetence.

The 2016 waste-remobilization paper used RCP8.5-era projections and explicitly presented scenario-dependent outcomes. A modern revision must compare current surface-mass-balance observations and updated climate scenarios before repeating its timing estimates.

The 2021 basal-sediment result constrains past Greenland states; it does not itself specify twenty-first-century melt rates.

Political incentives cut in several directions. Cold War Army organizations had incentives to demonstrate a strategic role and engineering feasibility. Danish authorities had incentives to preserve sovereignty while maintaining the U.S. alliance. Later environmental politics can reward dramatic contamination framing. Climate-skeptical politics can reward minimizing future remobilization. The manuscript should use measurements and dated records rather than infer truth from those incentives.
