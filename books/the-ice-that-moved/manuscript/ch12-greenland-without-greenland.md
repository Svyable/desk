# Greenland Without Greenland

A piece of moss under nearly a mile of ice is not dramatic until the geography arrives.

Vegetation means light, exposed land, liquid water and time. At Camp Century it means that a place now buried beneath the Greenland Ice Sheet once existed in a radically different state.

The 2021 analysis did not show that Greenland is about to become green. It showed something subtler and more consequential: the ice sheet has crossed large state changes under past climates, and the subglacial landscape retains evidence of those crossings.

This matters to modern climate science because model projections are stronger when confronted with physical evidence of what the Earth system has done before. Marine sediments offer indirect records of ice extent. Material from beneath the ice provides a terrestrial constraint.

The irony is difficult to improve upon. Camp Century was chosen and engineered around an assumption of exploitable persistence. Its deepest scientific product became evidence against simple permanence.

But irony can become lazy causality. The engineers of 1959 were not responsible for knowing a million-year Greenland history that had not yet been recovered. Nor did the later fossil evidence alone invalidate every near-term engineering assumption at the camp.

The point is about scale.

A medium may be stable for a season, difficult over a decade and transient over geologic time. Different projects require different definitions of permanence.

Iceworm asked for strategic reliability over decades.

Waste disposal implicitly asked for much longer.

Climate interpretation asks about hundreds of thousands of years.

Camp Century placed all three timescales in one hole.
