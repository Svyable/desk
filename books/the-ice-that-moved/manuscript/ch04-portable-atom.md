# Portable Atom

The reactor arrived in pieces.

The PM-2A belonged to the Army Nuclear Power Program, an era when the military treated small reactors as logistical machines. The appeal was obvious at Camp Century. Diesel power meant fuel hauled over hundreds of kilometers of ice. A reactor promised a different equation: more engineering complexity at the source in exchange for fewer tons moving across the surface.

USACE's own history lists PM-2A among the Army's experimental reactors and records its service at Camp Century. It was temporary, shut down after several years, dismantled and removed. This matters because Camp Century is often compressed online into the phrase nuclear base, which encourages two opposite errors. One imagines a permanently buried reactor still operating in the ice. The other treats the nuclear element as decorative Cold War theater.

Neither is useful.

The reactor was real infrastructure. It generated power and demonstrated a military theory of distributed nuclear logistics. Its presence also created waste streams and decommissioning problems different from those of diesel machinery. The reactor pressure vessel did not remain abandoned at the site, but radiological material associated with operations entered the waste history later reconstructed by researchers.

Portable nuclear power was itself a wager on institutional maturity. A reactor small enough to ship into extreme environments still required trained operators, shielding, maintenance, emergency procedures and fuel handling. The Army was not only miniaturizing a power plant. It was miniaturizing an organization around it.

Camp Century therefore offers a useful counterweight to modern conversations about microreactors. The historical lesson is neither that small reactors failed nor that they solved remote power. PM-2A did what a prototype is supposed to do: it made a set of tradeoffs physical.

It also demonstrates how technologies inherit the assumptions of their host environment. A reactor can be engineered carefully and still belong to a base whose tunnels are deforming around it.

The atom was portable.

The ground was not ground.
