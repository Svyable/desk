# The Ice Keeps the Receipt

Camp Century was built to test whether Greenland could become infrastructure.

It became evidence that infrastructure is temporary and Greenland is not one thing.

The tunnels deformed. Iceworm remained a proposal. The reactor left. Much of the rest stayed. The core went into storage and eventually told a story older than the institution that drilled it. The abandoned waste entered a climate model. Radar found the buried geometry again.

Nothing about this requires mysticism. That is what makes it so good.

The base is an unusually clean demonstration of how several forms of knowledge age at different speeds. Strategy can expire in a few years. Engineering assumptions can fail in a decade. diplomatic arrangements can be renegotiated across generations. Environmental liabilities can persist for a century. Sediment can wait a million years for the right instrument.

The temptation is to end with a moral about hubris. Camp Century deserves better than that. The people who built it learned real things. The scientists who drilled there created value far beyond the military mission. The later waste problem is not proof that the original engineers were fools. It is proof that a competent institution can still make commitments on a timescale longer than its model.

That is the part worth keeping.

We build into futures we cannot test yet.

Somewhere under Greenland, the old corridors are no longer corridors. The ice has been closing them, carrying them, burying them and, perhaps eventually, returning pieces of them to a world with different politics and better instruments.

The project ended.

The experiment did not.
