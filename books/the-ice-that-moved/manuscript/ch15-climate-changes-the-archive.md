# Climate Changes the Archive

Climate change does not merely create new weather. It changes which parts of history are physically reachable.

Melting glaciers release aircraft wreckage, bodies, pollutants and artifacts. Thawing permafrost exposes archaeology while accelerating decay. Retreating ice reveals landscapes hidden for centuries and can also mobilize contaminants placed under assumptions of permanent cold.

Camp Century belongs to this larger class of unstable archives.

The phrase archive is not metaphorical here. The Greenland Ice Sheet stores atmospheric history, volcanic ash, dust and isotopic records. It also stores human infrastructure. The same process that makes the ice scientifically valuable as a layered record complicates its use as a waste repository.

When the archive warms, preservation and release happen together.

This gives the site a peculiar dual status. Scientists want the ice because it remembers climate. Governments may worry because it also remembers decisions.

There is no need to romanticize the planet as a moral witness. Physical systems have no interest in accountability.

They simply retain consequences longer than institutions retain personnel.
