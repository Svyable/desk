# Back Matter

## Chronology

1951 — United States and Denmark sign the Defense of Greenland Agreement.

1959 — U.S. Army Corps of Engineers begins constructing Camp Century in northwestern Greenland.

1960 — PM-2A portable nuclear reactor enters service at Camp Century.

1962 — Army planning for Project Iceworm develops into a strategic missile-basing proposal.

1963 — Iceworm does not proceed as an operational missile system.

1964 — Year-round Camp Century operations end; seasonal work continues.

1966 — Camp Century drilling reaches the base of the ice sheet and recovers subglacial material.

1967 — Camp Century is abandoned with substantial infrastructure and waste remaining.

1969 — Last reported USACE visit in the historical engineering record.

2016 — Colgan et al. publish a modern assessment of abandoned Camp Century waste under warming-climate scenarios.

2021 — Christ et al. report fossil plants, biomarkers and exposure histories in Camp Century subglacial sediment, supporting repeated Pleistocene ice-free conditions in northwestern Greenland.

## Evidence classes

**Class 1 — documented:** Camp Century construction and operation; PM-2A deployment and removal; Army cold-regions engineering; the recovered ice core; published modern waste and subglacial-sediment studies.

**Class 2 — credible but incomplete:** precise internal Danish-American knowledge boundaries around Iceworm; some reconstructed waste quantities; detailed strategic performance expectations not preserved in one complete public operational record.

**Class 3 — disputed or unsupported:** claims that an operational missile network was secretly completed beneath Greenland; claims that large quantities of reactor fuel remain buried at Camp Century; claims that present contamination is already emerging at harmful levels without supporting measurement.

**Class 4 — synthesis:** Camp Century as a model of intergenerational assumption debt; physical infrastructure as a second archive of classified programs; the value of preserving samples because future measurement can outrun present theory.

## Note on language

This draft uses 'Denmark' for the state actor in mid-century treaty relations while recognizing that Greenlandic people and contemporary Greenlandic institutions are not reducible to Danish policy. A deeper revision should bring Greenlandic-language and Greenland-based scholarship into the center rather than leaving Greenland as terrain in an American-Danish story.
