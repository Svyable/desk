# Research Priorities and Defeat Conditions

## Highest-value publication work

1. Reconstruct the complete ALH84001 claim/counterclaim timeline at paper level, including carbonate thermometry, PAH provenance, nanostructure morphology, magnetite population statistics and shock history.
2. Build a machine-readable table of every proposed ALH84001 biosignature, its strongest abiotic route, required physical conditions and current status.
3. Reproduce key magnetite-forming experiments and quantify morphology distributions rather than representative images.
4. Build Bright Angel analog reactors under Mars-relevant temperature, water activity, redox and mineral constraints.
5. Generate vivianite/greigite/organic reaction fronts abiotically and biologically, blind the images and chemistry, and test classifier specificity.
6. Specify returned-sample isotope measurements across carbon, sulfur, iron and phosphorus with predeclared predictions.
7. Inventory all spacecraft/clean-room contamination relevant to Sapphire Canyon before destructive organic analyses.
8. Use spatially resolved molecular analysis across reaction fronts, not bulk extraction alone.
9. Publish a causal graph showing which observables are statistically dependent under each candidate mechanism.
10. Pre-register what combination of observations would qualify as strong, very strong, or decisive biological evidence.

## Defeat conditions for the book's synthesis

The book's central 'correlated biosignatures' thesis would weaken if independent analysis shows that the major Cheyava Falls observables arise from physically independent processes whose joint abiotic probability is demonstrably tiny. It would also weaken if a single returned-sample observation emerges that is both robustly measured and uniquely biological under Martian conditions.

The biological interpretation of Cheyava Falls should be downgraded if sterile analog experiments reproduce the observed mineral-organic reaction architecture under geologically supported Bright Angel conditions, particularly if isotopic and microstructural details also match.

The biological interpretation should be upgraded if returned material reveals multiple prospectively predicted features that no coherent abiotic model can jointly reproduce without violating measured thermal, fluid or chronological constraints.
