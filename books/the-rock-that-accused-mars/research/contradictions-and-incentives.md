# Contradictions and Incentives

## Contradictions to preserve

1. **Indigenous organics vs biological organics.** Evidence that carbon compounds formed on Mars answers provenance, not origin-by-life.
2. **Biomineral resemblance vs exclusivity.** A crystal can match a bacterial product while an abiotic pathway remains possible.
3. **Low-temperature formation vs later shock.** Different components may record different stages of a rock's history.
4. **Habitable setting vs inhabited setting.** Jezero's ancient lake raises plausibility but does not establish organisms.
5. **Biologically familiar minerals vs abiotic mineral chemistry.** Vivianite and greigite are metabolically interesting without being uniquely biological.
6. **Multiple clues vs multiple causes.** Apparent convergence can be statistically overstated when one process generates several observables.
7. **Public importance vs public certainty.** A result can deserve global attention while remaining provisional.

## Incentives

- NASA and mission teams benefit from scientifically important findings, public support and mission continuity; this is not evidence of exaggeration but it is part of the communication environment.
- Skeptics can gain reputational reward from conventional explanations and may overstate that an abiotic route is established when it is merely possible.
- Proponents can shift from a broad biosignature package to a narrower surviving feature after counterevidence; this may be scientifically legitimate but should be tracked explicitly.
- Sample-return advocates have institutional reasons to emphasize measurements unavailable in situ; the information-value argument should be evaluated independently of program advocacy.

## Disinformation posture

No classified-information or intelligence claim is necessary for this book. The anomaly is scientific and public. Avoid importing secrecy narratives where ordinary scientific incentives suffice.
