# Chapter 9 — Cheyava Falls

The rock is roughly a meter across and arrowhead-shaped. Perseverance encountered it in Neretva Vallis in July 2024. The surface carried pale veins, dark specks and ring-like features that the team called leopard spots.

PIXL mapped elemental chemistry at fine scale. SHERLOC detected organic-molecule signatures. The Bright Angel mudstone contained organic carbon and a chemical inventory rich in sulfur, iron and phosphorus. Within the reaction features, investigators identified ferrous iron phosphate consistent with vivianite and iron sulfide consistent with greigite.

The pattern matters more than any single mineral. Vivianite can occur in sediments associated with organic matter. Greigite can be produced by microbes and can also arise abiotically. The spatial arrangement suggested redox reactions between sediment and organic matter after deposition.

This is the kind of observation astrobiology has been trying to reach for decades: not merely a molecule, not merely a shape, but a microenvironment with chemistry that resembles metabolism.

And still the correct noun is 'potential.'

The 2025 Nature paper did not report a Martian organism. It reported mineral and organic associations that satisfy criteria for potential biosignatures while discussing nonbiological routes. NASA's public language retained that boundary, even amid understandably dramatic statements about proximity to discovering life.

The boundary is important because the rover cannot perform every discriminating experiment. It can map minerals and organics. It cannot turn the sample through the full battery of Earth laboratory methods. The core named Sapphire Canyon therefore matters in a second way: Perseverance did not only observe the rock. It collected a piece of the argument.

There is something almost comic about the situation. Humanity has the rock in a tube on Mars. The most decisive instrument may be a future budget.
