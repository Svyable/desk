# Chapter 15 — What Would Force Biology

Suppose Sapphire Canyon arrives tomorrow. What result would make a skeptical geochemist stop reaching for another mineral reaction?

Not one molecule. Not one isotope ratio. Not one shape.

The strongest case would combine structure, chemistry, isotopes and context in a way that abiotic models cannot jointly satisfy. Imagine repeated cellular-scale architectures embedded within primary sedimentary fabrics, containing complex organic distributions spatially bounded by those structures, with isotopic fractionations consistent across carbon and sulfur, and mineral products arranged as predicted by a specific low-temperature metabolism. Add molecular chirality or polymer-like complexity not plausibly inherited from meteorites or synthesized by the measured geochemistry. Then reproduce the observations in multiple subsamples and independent laboratories.

Even this would invite argument, as it should.

The decisive property is not weirdness. Geology is weird. The decisive property is constrained generativity: biology explains many detailed features with one known class of process while abiotic alternatives require ad hoc, mutually conflicting or experimentally unsuccessful conditions.

The reverse is also true. If a sterile Mars-analog reactor produces the same organics, mineral fronts, isotopic signatures and textures under Bright Angel conditions, biology should lose.

This chapter therefore has defeat conditions on purpose. A book about alien life that cannot describe what would change its mind is not a scientific book. It is a religion with instruments.
