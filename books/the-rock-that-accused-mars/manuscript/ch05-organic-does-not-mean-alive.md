# Chapter 5 — Organic Does Not Mean Alive

'Organic' is one of the most abused words in public astrobiology. In chemistry it describes carbon-containing compounds. In grocery stores it describes an agricultural regime. In headlines it quietly becomes 'life.'

ALH84001 should have cured us of that slippage.

McKay and colleagues reported PAHs on fresh fracture surfaces and argued that contamination studies supported a Martian origin. That was important. Indigenous organics on Mars are scientifically profound even when dead. They reveal carbon chemistry, water-rock reactions, atmospheric inputs and feedstocks from which biology could emerge.

They do not, by themselves, reveal biology.

In 2022 Andrew Steele and colleagues published a Science study connecting ALH84001 organic synthesis to water-rock interactions involving serpentinization and carbonation. The work strengthened an abiotic explanation for at least important organic material in the meteorite. Mars did not need microbes to make interesting carbon chemistry.

That result matters far beyond one old controversy. It establishes an adversarial background model for every rover that detects organics. Mars has had billions of years to make organic molecules geologically. Meteorites also deliver them. Radiation transforms them. Hydrothermal chemistry reorganizes them. Instruments sample mixtures and altered residues.

The right question is not 'are organics present?' It is 'is the distribution of molecules, isotopes, chirality, functional groups and geological context quantitatively more probable under metabolism than under the known abiotic network?'

Perseverance's SHERLOC observations at Bright Angel contain organic signals. That is exciting because they are spatially associated with redox mineral features. Association can add information. But association also creates a danger: the same reaction that changes iron and sulfur may synthesize, mobilize, preserve or concentrate organics.

If one process creates both the mineral anomaly and the organic anomaly, the two observations are not independent witnesses. They are two fingerprints from the same hand.

A returned sample can attack this problem with laboratory instruments that Mars rovers cannot carry: compound-specific isotope analysis, high-resolution mass spectrometry, nanoscale imaging, crystallography, perhaps chirality measurements and destructive microanalysis across reaction fronts. The point of returning the rock is not that Earth laboratories are more prestigious. It is that they can interrogate causality at scales and precisions unavailable in situ.

Organic chemistry is the alphabet. Life is one possible text.
