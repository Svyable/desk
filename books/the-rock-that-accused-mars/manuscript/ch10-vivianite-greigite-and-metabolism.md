# Chapter 10 — Vivianite, Greigite, and Metabolism

Minerals become biologically interesting when organisms change the chemical landscape required to make them.

Microbes do not need sunlight. They need free energy. Electron donors and acceptors separated by chemistry can be enough. Iron and sulfur metabolisms on Earth exploit gradients that would look, from a geochemical distance, like mineral transformations.

Vivianite is an iron phosphate. Greigite is an iron sulfide. Their occurrence with organic matter in reaction fronts invites a metabolic interpretation because microbes can mediate iron and sulfur redox chemistry and alter mineral precipitation.

The temptation is to say that Earth biology makes these minerals, therefore Mars biology may have made these minerals. That is only the first half of the reasoning.

The second half asks whether Mars geology makes them too.

A returned core could provide several discriminants. Are sulfur isotopes fractionated in a pattern difficult to obtain abiotically under the measured conditions? Do iron isotopes vary across reaction fronts in ways consistent with biological reduction? Are organics concentrated at mineral interfaces in molecular distributions suggestive of cellular processing? Does greigite show textures or impurity patterns characteristic of a low-temperature pathway? Are there repeating microscale architectures that track diffusion and growth?

No one measurement deserves magical status. Isotope effects can be abiotic. Organics can be geochemical. Crystal forms can converge. The aim is to build a set of measurements whose abiotic explanations require mutually incompatible conditions.

That phrase—mutually incompatible conditions—is more useful than 'multiple lines of evidence.' If one abiotic model needs 300 degrees Celsius and another needs pristine low-temperature organics preserved in place, the combined data may break the model. If one coherent low-temperature water-rock pathway predicts everything, it does not.

A biosignature becomes strong when geology has to contradict itself to imitate it.
