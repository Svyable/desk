# Chapter 13 — The Abiotic Adversary

Every serious biosignature program should employ an enemy.

Not a skeptic who arrives after publication to find flaws. An experimental adversary whose job begins before the claim and is funded from the same budget.

The adversary receives the geological constraints and tries to produce the candidate biosignature without life. Temperature limits. Pressure. Fluid composition. Mineral inventory. Radiation environment. Time. If the adversary needs conditions ruled out by the rock, biology gains ground. If the adversary reproduces the package cheaply, the biosignature loses specificity.

ALH84001 evolved into such a program informally. Laboratories explored carbonate decomposition, magnetite formation, PAH origins, morphology and water-rock chemistry. The process took years because the adversarial structure was assembled after the claim.

Cheyava Falls gives us a chance to reverse the order.

Before Sapphire Canyon reaches an Earth laboratory, teams can synthesize Bright Angel analog mudstones. They can impose low-temperature redox gradients, organic compounds, iron, phosphate and sulfur under Martian conditions. They can vary acidity and thermal history. They can ask whether vivianite-greigite reaction fronts emerge without cells. Biological analog experiments can run beside them.

Most importantly, both teams can make blind predictions about features not used to tune the model.

This is where anomaly science becomes science rather than a permanent archive of interesting cases. The explanation must risk something.
