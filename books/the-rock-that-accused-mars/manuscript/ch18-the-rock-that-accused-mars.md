# Chapter 18 — The Rock That Accused Mars

ALH84001 did not prove life on Mars. It may have done something almost as valuable for the science that eventually will.

It exposed how evidence changes character when clues share causes. It turned a fossil photograph into crystallography, shock physics, organic geochemistry, contamination control and thermodynamics. It taught us that an indigenous organic molecule can be abiotic, that a bacterium-like crystal can have a mineral route, that a suggestive texture can live at the mercy of imaging and formation history.

Cheyava Falls is not ALH84001 again. The sample provenance is better. The depositional environment is better characterized. The relevant structures are embedded in an ancient Martian sedimentary setting observed in place. The mineral-organic associations are measured by instruments designed for astrobiology. A core exists.

That makes the new evidence stronger, not immune.

The astonishing possibility is that the history of life-detection science has finally arranged its own controlled experiment. We have an old candidate whose lines of evidence were progressively attacked by abiotic mechanisms, and a new candidate whose observations can be evaluated with those lessons already in hand.

The question humanity should ask is not 'did Perseverance find life?' That phrasing asks for a verdict before the trial has begun.

Ask instead: what set of measurements would make a sterile Mars unable to produce this rock?

That is a harder question. It is also how we eventually earn the right to answer the easier one.

If Sapphire Canyon comes home and biology survives every serious attempt to kill it, then the sentence should be short.

Mars lived.

Until then, the most respectful thing we can do for that possibility is make the alternative explanations good.
