# Back Matter

## Chronology

- **1984** — ALH84001 recovered in Antarctica.
- **1993** — meteorite recognized as Martian.
- **August 16, 1996** — McKay et al. publish the multi-line relic-biogenic-activity hypothesis in *Science*.
- **1997–2001** — carbonate-temperature, magnetite, morphology, PAH and contamination disputes intensify.
- **2001** — Thomas-Keprta et al. publish the truncated-hexa-octahedral magnetite biosignature argument.
- **2003** — Treiman and others advance shock/thermal abiotic formation routes; Golden and colleagues report inorganic morphological alternatives.
- **2022** — Steele et al. report abiotic organic synthesis associated with serpentinization and carbonation in ALH84001.
- **July 2024** — Perseverance investigates Cheyava Falls in the Bright Angel formation and collects the Sapphire Canyon core.
- **September 10, 2025** — Hurowitz et al. publish *Redox-driven mineral and organic associations in Jezero Crater, Mars* in *Nature*; NASA describes the features as potential biosignatures.

## The practical rule

A candidate biosignature should be accompanied by five things: its best biological mechanism, its best abiotic mechanism, a causal-dependence map among observations, at least one prospective discriminator, and a statement of what result would defeat the biological interpretation.

## Why this book remains open

The decisive specimen is still on Mars. Until returned-sample measurements exist, any verdict would outrun the evidence.
