# The Rock That Accused Mars

## ALH84001, Cheyava Falls, and the Rules for Recognizing Alien Life

**Sven Hardy Benson**

### Thesis

The strongest lesson of the ALH84001 controversy is not that Mars was dead, nor that the 1996 team was foolish. It is that apparently independent biosignatures can be generated by one geological history and therefore masquerade as convergent biological evidence. Perseverance has now found a new convergence at Cheyava Falls: organic carbon, redox textures, vivianite, greigite, water-deposited mudstone, and an environment once capable of microbial metabolism. This book asks what evidence would actually force biology to win against a sufficiently imaginative abiotic Mars.

### Evidence posture

- **Verified/documented:** ALH84001 is a Martian meteorite; McKay et al. published a multi-line biosignature case in 1996; later work established multiple abiotic routes for key organics/minerals; Perseverance observed the Bright Angel/Cheyava Falls associations and cached Sapphire Canyon; the 2025 Nature paper calls them potential biosignatures, not life detection.
- **Credible but incomplete:** some ALH84001 magnetites retain unusual biomineral-like properties; Cheyava Falls reaction fronts resemble biologically mediated redox mineralization on Earth; returned samples could discriminate among hypotheses unavailable to rover instruments.
- **Disputed/unconfirmed:** ALH84001 contains fossils; its magnetite is uniquely biogenic; Cheyava Falls records ancient Martian metabolism; any current public result establishes past Martian life.
- **Speculative synthesis:** biosignature confidence should be based on causal independence, not merely the number of suggestive observables; Mars sample science needs an explicit abiotic adversary program before extraordinary claims are announced.
