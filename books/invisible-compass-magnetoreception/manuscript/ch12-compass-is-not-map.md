# Compass Is Not Map

A compass tells you which way to go. A map tells you where you are. Animal-navigation literature often compresses those into a single phrase—magnetic sense—because both depend on the geomagnetic field. Mechanistically they may be different senses.

An inclination compass can supply an axis for movement without encoding geographic position. Map information requires spatially varying field properties that an animal can associate with location. Intensity and inclination change across the planet, though not uniquely everywhere. Local anomalies complicate the pattern. A useful magnetic map therefore does not need to resemble latitude and longitude; it can be one component in a learned or inherited navigation system.

Sea turtles have become important here because controlled magnetic-field experiments can expose young animals to combinations of intensity and inclination corresponding to distant locations along migratory routes. Behavioral responses have been interpreted as evidence that the animals recognize regional magnetic signatures.

Salmon, lobsters, and other taxa add different pieces. The question is no longer whether one avian mechanism explains the animal kingdom. It is whether evolution has repeatedly exploited predictable structure in Earth's field, perhaps through multiple receptor chemistries.

This matters to the robin story. Cluster N lesion experiments address a magnetic compass. Trigeminal pathways that are dispensable for that task could still participate in map information. A candidate CRY4 system might be sufficient for an inclination compass without providing the polarity or intensity precision required for another navigational computation.

The urge for a single receptor comes partly from how human sensory organs are named. We say “vision” while retinal rods and cones already divide labor. We say “touch” while mechanoreceptors with different response properties inhabit the skin. Magnetoreception may eventually look ordinary in this respect: several transducers feeding several computations that we prematurely grouped because the external physical field was the same.

The map-compass distinction is therefore not taxonomy. It is experimental protection against asking one mechanism to do work the behavior never required.
