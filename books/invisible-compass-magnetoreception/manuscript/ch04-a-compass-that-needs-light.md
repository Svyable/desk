# A Compass That Needs Light

A magnetic sense sounds as though darkness should help. Remove visual distractions and let the invisible field speak. Migratory birds made the opposite demand.

Decades of orientation work showed that the avian magnetic compass depends on light conditions. The effect is not a trivial statement that birds need to be awake enough to behave. Wavelength and intensity matter. Under some spectral conditions robins orient normally; under others their magnetic orientation becomes altered or unstable.

This pushed the field toward a photochemical mechanism long before one particular cryptochrome could be held up in a cuvette and measured. If the first transduction step involves a light-generated radical pair, the compass is not magnetic in isolation. Light creates the chemical state whose spin dynamics can then be influenced by the geomagnetic field.

The conceptual shift is important. We are used to assigning one stimulus to one organ: photons to vision, pressure waves to hearing. A radical-pair magnetic compass would be a hybrid sense. Photons prepare a molecule; the magnetic field perturbs its reaction dynamics; retinal or associated signaling converts a tiny chemical anisotropy into neural information. The field is not depositing enough energy to function like a conventional trigger. It is changing probabilities inside an already energized reaction.

That helps explain why weak fields are not automatically irrelevant. The correct comparison is not between geomagnetic energy and the total thermal energy of a cell. The physical question is whether spin-state evolution in the relevant radical pair remains coherent and magnetically sensitive long enough for Earth-strength fields to alter a chemical outcome that biology can amplify.

Light dependence is not proof of cryptochrome. A visual system can influence navigation for many reasons, and candidate magnetic chemistry has changed over time. But the light experiments are powerful because they arrived upstream of the fashionable molecule. They created a constraint that a later molecular candidate had to satisfy.

The ideal mechanism in science is not the one that can explain everything after the fact. It is the one that walks into a room already full of awkward observations and discovers that the furniture fits.
