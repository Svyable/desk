# What Artificial Fields Actually Test

The magnetoreception laboratory is a machine for manufacturing invisible environments.

Coils allow experimenters to rotate magnetic north without rotating the room, alter inclination while leaving gravity unchanged, invert components, produce field strengths corresponding to distant geography, add oscillating radiofrequency perturbations, or apply strong pulses intended to affect magnetic particles. Each manipulation is an argument about mechanism.

But artificial fields do not merely reveal the animal. They create edge cases the animal may never encounter in nature. A configuration that inverts the vertical component at a northern site can be physiologically interpretable while ecologically absurd. A strong magnetic pulse can test remanence while also perturbing tissue in ways not yet understood. Broadband radiofrequency noise can constrain spin chemistry while being unlike the spectrum encountered during migration.

This is why dose, frequency, geometry, exposure timing, coil calibration, blinding, and sham conditions matter so much. “Magnetic field” is not a treatment any more than “light” is a treatment. Blue light and infrared light are different interventions. A static field rotation and a megahertz oscillation are different interventions.

The best experiments exploit those differences. A polarity reversal can distinguish an axial compass from one sensitive to north-south sign. A pulse can test for a remanent ferromagnetic component. A light-wavelength manipulation can test whether the compass requires photochemistry. Radiofrequency interference can attack a proposed spin-dynamical mechanism at its vulnerable timescale.

The worst experiments collapse them. They expose an animal to “magnetism,” observe stress or preference, and then infer a magnetic sense without showing that the effect carries directional information or survives nonmagnetic controls.

Magnetoreception therefore deserves unusually technical reporting. The field strength at the center of a coil is not enough. Spatial gradients, harmonics, ambient radiofrequency noise, vibration, heating, switching transients, and acoustic cues can all become alternative explanations.

A sense for a weak planetary field will always be studied with apparatus capable of accidentally producing stronger local cues. The laboratory must prove that the bird is reading the Earth we built around it, not the machine we used to build it.
