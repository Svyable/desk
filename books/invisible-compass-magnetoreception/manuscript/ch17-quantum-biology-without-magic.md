# Quantum Biology Without Magic

Magnetoreception is routinely recruited into arguments it did not volunteer to join.

Because radical-pair chemistry depends on electron spin dynamics, the avian compass has become one of the canonical examples of quantum biology. That label is defensible. It does not mean birds maintain macroscopic quantum superpositions, navigate by consciousness collapsing wavefunctions, or possess privileged access to a hidden layer of reality.

The interesting physics is more disciplined. Photoexcitation creates radicals. Electron spins evolve under hyperfine and Zeeman interactions. Reaction rates and yields can depend on spin state. Molecular orientation can make that dependence anisotropic. If a protein and cell preserve enough of the effect, biology can amplify the chemical difference.

Body temperature is not an automatic objection. Quantum effects are everywhere in chemistry at body temperature. The relevant question is whether the particular coherence or spin correlation required by the mechanism persists on the relevant reaction timescale. That is measurable.

Recent theoretical work continues to explore which radical pairs in cryptochrome could remain sufficiently sensitive to Earth-strength fields, including tightly bound candidates and mechanisms involving asymmetric recombination. These models are valuable when they generate testable chemical predictions. They become less useful when the word quantum is allowed to substitute for a missing cellular pathway.

The 2021 robin CRY4 work is exemplary precisely because it was not philosophical. Purify a protein. Illuminate it. Measure radical yields. Change the magnetic field. Mutate residues along the electron-transfer chain. Compare species. The quantum claim earns its place through spectroscopy and reaction kinetics.

Magnetoreception should make us more impressed by ordinary matter, not less satisfied with it. A migrating bird does not need supernatural physics. It may only need evolution to exploit a piece of physical chemistry humans overlooked because our own senses do not announce the field.
