## A note on evidence

This manuscript treats magnetoreception as a layered evidentiary problem. Behavioral magnetic orientation, molecular magnetosensitivity, anatomical localization, neural processing, conscious perception, and navigational performance are not interchangeable claims. Where the evidence is specific to European robins, pigeons, turtles, salmon, or humans, the book keeps that taxonomic boundary visible.

The phrase *quantum compass* is used only for radical-pair spin chemistry and its biological implementation. It is not used as evidence for claims about consciousness or metaphysics.

## Acknowledged uncertainties

The primary avian magnetoreceptor has not been demonstrated end to end. CRY4 is a strong candidate with direct in-vitro magnetic sensitivity, but the in-vivo transduction and signaling chain remains incomplete. Magnetite-based mechanisms remain plausible for some magnetic tasks despite the failure of the famous pigeon-beak receptor claim. Human magnetic responsiveness is treated as unsettled and is not equated with conscious magnetic navigation.
