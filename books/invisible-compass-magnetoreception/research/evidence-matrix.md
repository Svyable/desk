# Evidence Matrix

| Claim | Class | Current support | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| European robins possess a magnetic compass | Verified/documented | Controlled coil-based orientation experiments | Does not identify receptor |
| Robin compass is inclination-based rather than ordinary polarity compass | Verified/documented | Wiltschko & Wiltschko 1972 | Applies to tested compass behavior |
| Avian magnetic compass depends on visual/light conditions | Verified/documented | Multi-decade orientation literature | Exact receptor photochemistry remains open |
| Cluster N is required for tested robin magnetic compass behavior | Verified/documented | Zapka et al. 2009 lesion study | Does not prove subjective visual percept |
| Ophthalmic trigeminal section leaves tested robin compass intact | Verified/documented | Zapka et al. 2009 | Does not eliminate trigeminal magnetic map functions |
| Famous pigeon-beak iron-rich receptor cells were macrophages | Verified/documented | Treiber et al. 2012 | Does not disprove all magnetite mechanisms |
| Urban/broadband RF noise can disrupt robin magnetic orientation under specified conditions | Verified/documented | Engels et al. 2014 | Ecological population-level consequences not established by that experiment |
| European-robin CRY4 is magnetically sensitive in vitro | Verified/documented | Xu et al. 2021 | In-vivo receptor role not proven end to end |
| Robin CRY4 is the primary avian magnetoreceptor | Credible but incomplete | Molecular sensitivity + retinal localization + behavioral constraints | Requires causal in-vivo perturbation/signaling chain |
| Birds possess multiple magnetic receptor systems for compass and map | Credible but incomplete | Behavioral/neural dissociations and mechanism constraints | Exact division of labor unresolved |
| Human brains respond to Earth-strength field rotations | Credible but incomplete | Wang et al. 2019 EEG | Needs broad independent replication; no conscious perception established |
| Humans possess a useful conscious magnetic compass | Disputed/not established | Inconsistent behavioral literature | Not supported by EEG alone |
| Quantum spin chemistry explains all animal magnetoreception | Disputed/not established | Radical-pair evidence strongest in avian compass context | Other taxa/tasks may use different mechanisms |
| Magnetoreception is best understood as several sensory tasks hidden under one name | Speculative synthesis | Compass/map differences, taxa diversity, receptor uncertainty | Could narrow if a common mechanism ultimately dominates |

## Defeat conditions

The book's central synthesis weakens substantially if a single receptor system is demonstrated end to end across avian compass and map tasks and across major taxa, because receptor elusiveness and mechanistic pluralism would then be historical accidents rather than structural features. The CRY4-centered chapters must narrow if rigorous in-vivo perturbation shows CRY4 is unnecessary for the robin magnetic compass. The human chapter must narrow or be removed if preregistered independent studies fail to reproduce geomagnetic EEG responses under artifact-resistant protocols.
