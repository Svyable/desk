# Chronology

- **1972** — Wolfgang and Roswitha Wiltschko publish the inclination-compass result for European robins in *Science*.
- **1975** — Robin experiments examine interaction between magnetic and stellar compass information.
- **1990s–2000s** — Light wavelength/intensity, magnetic-pulse, and radical-pair hypotheses become increasingly experimentally constrained.
- **2004–2006** — Oldenburg researchers encounter unexplained failures of robin magnetic orientation in urban test huts, beginning the path toward electromagnetic-noise experiments.
- **2009** — Zapka et al. report that cluster N lesions disrupt the robin magnetic compass while ophthalmic trigeminal section does not under the tested conditions.
- **2012** — Treiber et al. show that the celebrated iron-rich cells in the pigeon upper beak are macrophages, not the proposed magnetosensory neurons.
- **2014** — Engels et al. publish double-blind experiments showing disruption of robin magnetic orientation by anthropogenic/broadband electromagnetic noise under specified conditions.
- **2019** — Wang et al. report geometry-specific alpha-band EEG responses to Earth-strength magnetic-field rotations in humans.
- **2020** — European-robin CRY4 isoforms and candidate retinal interaction partners receive more detailed characterization.
- **2021** — Xu et al. demonstrate direct magnetic sensitivity of European-robin CRY4 in vitro and map contributions of the flavin–tryptophan electron-transfer chain.
- **2024** — New spin-chemical modeling expands the plausible radical-pair mechanisms that could respond to Earth-strength fields, while the complete in-vivo receptor pathway remains open.

## Chronology warning

Dates in a mechanism history can create fake inevitability. Radical-pair theory did not automatically predict CRY4; beak-receptor claims did not become obviously wrong before the histology was redone; and 2021 molecular sensitivity did not retroactively prove every earlier behavioral interpretation.
