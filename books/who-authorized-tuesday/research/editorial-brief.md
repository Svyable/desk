# Editorial brief — WHO AUTHORIZED TUESDAY?

**Sven Hardy Benson** · November 17, 2026 · source-led nonfiction

## Question and reader promise

How did specific human choices turn into rules so pervasive that they feel like properties of nature? Twenty real decisions and their material consequences: time, space, units, sizes, logistics, addressing, identity and classification. The reader should leave able to ask not merely who invented a rule but who benefited, what it made possible, whom it left outside, and what replacing it would cost.

The competing answer is that coordination is simply practical and its history irrelevant. The book must resist the opposite facile thesis that all standards are arbitrary or oppressive. A postal code can help an ambulance or an ordinary parcel arrive. An imposed demographic category can misrepresent the people it is supposed to count. The facts change with the mechanism and the institution; the book must permit both effects.

## Architecture

- **1–6: We decide when it is.** Samoa's erased date; Sweden's doubled leap day; British calendar act; railroad time; Washington prime meridian; negotiations over leap seconds. Calendar labels, physical rotation and atomic measures must remain distinct.
- **7–10: We decide how big.** The meter's migration from Earth fraction to light, kilogram from artifact to constant, DIN/ISO paper geometry, and a shoe-fitting device with multiple dimensions. Measure the real thing; don't worship the artifact.
- **11–14: We give objects machine identities.** Container standardization, barcode checkout, ISBN, and ZIP Code. An agreement can be commercially powerful because it travels across firms.
- **15–19: We attach codes to people and places.** Social Security numbers (not initially national identity cards), 911 emergency response, telephone area codes, global geodetic coordinates, and Unicode character encoding. Every identifier works within a scope and can fail outside it.
- **20: A form classifies people.** Census categories change; 1930 'Mexican' classification, self-report and 2024 SPD 15 revisions. Treat race and ethnicity as consequential social categories, not biological types created by an official; quote administrative texts accurately and describe effects with care.

## Authoring rules

No invented historical quotes, motives or eyewitness scenes. Build each chapter from a primary law, standard, patent, science record or preserved object; include secondary context only when needed. Source citations belong within the prose and also in a ledger with falsification questions. Variation of form matters: the legal close reading of Samoa is not an anecdote about chewing gum, and the histories of the meter and Unicode should not force the same three-act prose template.

Full-length one-shot target: >=18 chapters, >=3,000 words each, >=65,000 total (preferred 70,000–90,000); a complete chapter count alone cannot certify editorial quality. Publication requires distinct research, literary and rights review and Sven's explicit decision.
