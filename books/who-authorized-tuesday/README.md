## The reader promise

Every day you live inside agreements made by people you will never meet. The date on your paycheck, the location of noon, the size of your paper, the nine digits assigned to your working life, the number printed on the back of a book, and the box you're required to tick in a form all began as contingent choices. Some improved safety, mobility and equity. Others grew into invisible forms of power. The book follows the *actual negotiations, statutes, archives, tests, technical standards and failures* that turned these choices into ordinary reality.

The twenty cases move through time (calendar, time zones, meridian, leap seconds), measurement (meter, kilogram, paper, shoes), freight and information (container, UPC, ISBN, ZIP), identification and emergency access (SSN, 911, area codes, coordinates, Unicode), and finally public classification (census race and ethnicity). Each chapter distinguishes a convention from the material facts it describes; not everything is arbitrary just because a governing body chose the representation.

Different from *Ideas From The Future & Other Odd Things In My Head* (speculative social futures), *The Evidence Is Ridiculous* (extraordinary historical actions), and *EXPLAIN YOURSELF, EARTH* (natural anomalies). This book is about the **creation and political consequences of interoperability**. It must not read as twenty Wikipedia entries about unusual inventions.

**Evidence standard:** real checkable primary texts and institutional histories; no invented conversations, eyewitness claims, motives or counterfeit certainty. Strongest competing argument: a standard is merely a practical convenience; the book needs to show when such convenience becomes institutional power, and when it remains a genuine public good. Scientific, technical and historical claims must be separated from interpretation. Not all clocks, codes, or human identities are one uniform system.

**Editorial state:** Working Desk manuscript, not authorized for Shelf.
