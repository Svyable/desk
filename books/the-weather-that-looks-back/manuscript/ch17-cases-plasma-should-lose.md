# Cases Plasma Should Lose

A hypothesis earns respect by specifying the cases it should not explain.

Atmospheric plasma should lose when an event establishes a persistent rigid body through independent geometry and multiple physical channels under conditions incompatible with the proposed plasma mechanism.

That sentence needs unpacking.

Suppose two separated calibrated optical stations triangulate a compact object at ten kilometers. Its shape persists through changing aspect. Radar independently measures the same range and coherent Doppler. Infrared shows a stable temperature distribution attached to the geometry rather than a diffuse emission volume. The object performs a maneuver requiring bulk transport, and the surrounding atmosphere records the expected pressure effects or some measurable substitute. The weather is electrically quiet. Broadband RF shows none of the emissions predicted by the candidate plasma model. A later pass produces the same signature.

Calling that plasma would require new evidence, not stubbornness.

Likewise, a recovered object with authenticated chain of custody, manufactured internal structure and materials inconsistent with natural atmospheric production ends the atmospheric argument for that object. The origin could still be human, foreign, classified or unknown. But weather would have lost.

This boundary matters because “natural phenomenon” can become a refuge from evidence just as easily as “advanced technology.” A skeptic who can rename every failure mode after seeing the data is not practicing falsification.

There are softer defeat conditions too. If a ball-lightning theory predicts close association with thunderstorms and a robust class of identical events occurs repeatedly in meteorologically quiet, dry, electrically inactive conditions, the model's domain shrinks. If a Hessdalen electrochemical theory predicts correlations with local geology and the same tightly specified phenomenon occurs over geologically dissimilar sites without the required chemistry, revise it. If plasma radar scattering requires electron densities that would produce conspicuous optical or RF consequences absent from well-calibrated measurements, the theory is in trouble.

Conversely, technology should lose where its own signatures fail. If apparent acceleration collapses after range correction, the propulsion problem disappears. If a bright sphere's spectrum is dominated by local soil elements after a lightning strike, an engineered craft becomes an extravagant explanation. If a radar target tracks a known anomalous-propagation layer, object language should be withdrawn.

The point is not equal-opportunity skepticism as etiquette. It is model selection with consequences.

A book about atmospheric controls would be useless if it simply relocated certainty from extraterrestrial hardware to plasma. There will be cases where atmospheric hypotheses are bad. There will be cases where data are too poor to tell. The mature move is to preserve those distinctions.

The phrase *unidentified* should not be treated as an embarrassment.

Sometimes it is the most accurate result available.
