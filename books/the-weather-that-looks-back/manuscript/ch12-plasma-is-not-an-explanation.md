# Plasma Is Not an Explanation

Say the word *plasma* in an anomaly discussion and watch everyone relax.

The believers hear advanced propulsion. The skeptics hear weather. The physicist should hear a state of matter with parameters.

Ionization fraction. Electron temperature. Electron density. Ion species. Neutral density. Collision rate. Debye length. Geometry. Energy source. Lifetime. Recombination. Radiation spectrum. Magnetic field. Boundary conditions.

Without those, plasma is barely more specific than saying “something electrical.”

This is not pedantry. Atmospheric pressure is hostile to many long-lived plasma configurations. Electrons and ions collide frequently. Recombination can be rapid. A bright compact object lasting seconds needs either stored energy, continuing input, metastable chemistry, aerosol participation, electromagnetic confinement, or some other mechanism that pays the lifetime bill.

Different ball-lightning theories pay differently. Silicon-aerosol models store chemical energy in reactive particles. Microwave models place energy in trapped electromagnetic radiation coupled to plasma. Discharge-channel models use the pressure and flow structure left by the initiating strike. Gatchina-type afterglow work investigates unusually slow decay and possible roles for metastable species and chemi-ionization.

These mechanisms are not synonyms.

They make different predictions about spectrum, radio emission, sensitivity to environment, trajectory and termination. A field event should be able to hurt them.

The same standard should govern UAP explanations. If a luminous event supposedly consists of plasma, calculate the density needed for the observed brightness and radar interaction. If it appears opaque, explain the radiative transfer. If it maintains a hard geometric edge, explain confinement. If it accelerates across kilometers, identify what moves: matter, an ionization front, a pattern, a refractive image, or a source projected onto a medium. Those alternatives have radically different energy requirements.

The distinction between matter velocity and pattern velocity is particularly important. A spot of light on a distant cloud can sweep faster than any material object because the moving entity is the illumination pattern. Ionization fronts can propagate at speeds unrelated to bulk gas flow. A radar track created by changing propagation conditions can appear to move without a compact target carrying mass along the full path.

This does not explain any particular UAP. It explains why “it moved too fast to be natural” is incomplete until the moving quantity is identified.

Plasma physics also offers something anomaly culture rarely tolerates: boring scale dependence. A mechanism plausible across centimeters in a laboratory may fail across meters outdoors. A plasma stable at low pressure may die at sea level. A microwave cavity that works in simulation may require an initiating energy density not available in a quiet sky. A chemical aerosol can persist without supporting spectacular acceleration.

The word is therefore a starting gate.

A serious atmospheric hypothesis should come with a table of expected observables. Visible and infrared spectrum. Polarization. Radar behavior by frequency. RF emissions. Lifetime distribution. Environmental dependence. Acoustic signature. Residues. Geometry. Motion.

Then point instruments at the sky and let the table be wrong.

If a theory survives, it graduates from vocabulary to mechanism.
