# Steal the Machine — working research notes

## Governing thesis

Documented foreign-materiel exploitation supplies an empirical control for evaluating alleged extraordinary retrieval/reverse-engineering programs. The question is not merely whether a program could be hidden, but what physical, organizational, scientific and industrial traces the work itself would require under different hypotheses.

## Evidence classes

### 1. Verified / declassified / documented

- CIA Project AZORIAN: recovery attempt against Soviet K-129; Hughes/Glomar deep-sea-mining cover; purpose-built capture system; partial recovery; public exposure ended follow-on effort. CIA history: https://www.cia.gov/stories/story/the-exposing-of-project-azorian/
- CIA museum technical overview of AZORIAN: https://www.cia.gov/legacy/museum/exhibit/project-azorian/
- DIA public exploitation history: exploitation means examination/analysis, often complete disassembly, of recovered adversary materiel; late-1960s Soviet MiG exploitation at Groom Lake is specifically acknowledged. https://www.dia.mil/About/History/Museum/Staying-Ahead/
- DIA records a multi-agency foreign-materiel exploitation team in Grenada collecting Soviet-made weapons/equipment and documents. https://www.dia.mil/News-Features/Articles/Article-View/Article/567030/dia-during-operation-urgent-fury-grenada/
- AARO Historical Record Report Vol. 1 (2024) says it found no empirical evidence that USG/private companies reverse-engineered extraterrestrial technology and reports cases where interviewees associated authentic classified programs with alien activity. https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/AARO_Historical_Record_Report_Vol_1_2024.pdf

### 2. Credible but incomplete

- Named former officials, military personnel, contractors and journalists have reported or testified to alleged retrieval/reverse-engineering information. Each claim must be decomposed by firsthand/secondhand access, date, claimed program, physical evidence, and independent corroboration before manuscript use.
- AARO's access and conclusions are themselves evidence about an official investigation, not proof of universal nonexistence. Need Volume II/current status and precise statutory/access history before making comprehensive claims.

### 3. Disputed / unconfirmed

- U.S. possession of intact or fragmentary non-human craft.
- Recovered non-human biological material.
- Successful reproduction of non-human propulsion, power, materials or field effects.
- Specific private companies holding alien-derived material under concealed contracts.
- Claims that named conventional SAPs are covers for non-human exploitation absent program-level proof.

### 4. Speculative synthesis / open conjecture

- “Industrial shadow” hypothesis: even highly compartmented exploitation creates observable second-order requirements in metrology, containment, specialist labor, procurement, testing, waste, safety and downstream capability.
- “Legibility gradient”: the more technologically alien an object is, the less likely possession rapidly yields reproducible capability, but the more persistent basic characterization work should become.
- “Cover congruence”: successful cover stories tend to explain the visible infrastructure of the hidden task; extraordinary exploitation should therefore generate cover activities congruent with its physical footprint.

## Contradictions and incentive audit

- Intelligence agencies have institutional incentives to conceal real exploitation programs and capabilities. Official denial cannot be treated as self-authenticating.
- UAP advocates, journalists, witnesses and officials can have incentives ranging from genuine disclosure to status, access, advocacy, audience, bureaucratic rivalry or retrospective meaning-making. Do not infer motive without evidence.
- AARO has both unusually broad statutory access and an institutional position inside DoD. Treat its findings as substantive evidence that must be tested against named contrary claims, not as an oracle.
- Authentic classified programs can seed false extraordinary interpretations. Conversely, the existence of false associations does not establish that every extraordinary allegation is false.

## Quantitative extraction burden

For each historical exploitation control, recover where possible: target mass/dimensions; acquisition/recovery duration; number and kinds of organizations; contractor count; facilities; test infrastructure; elapsed time to useful intelligence; cost in contemporaneous and constant dollars; cover mechanism; known leaks; declassification latency; downstream doctrine/countermeasure/technology effects.

For each extraordinary retrieval claim, extract: alleged date; location; object scale; condition; chain of custody; transporting organization; receiving facility; named personnel; access basis; claimed tests; claimed materials; documentary artifacts; physical artifacts; downstream capability claim; first public appearance; independent corroborators; contradictions; official response.

## Strongest conventional model

The modern retrieval narrative can emerge from a real ecology of classified aerospace, foreign-materiel exploitation, SAP compartmentation, test ranges, contractors and legitimate secrecy. People with partial access encounter authentic fragments, infer a hidden object, exchange stories across overlapping networks, and later repetitions make dependent testimony appear independent. This model predicts many authentic program details but no independently verifiable extraordinary hardware chain.

## Strongest extraordinary model

A genuinely unprecedented recovered object is held in a very small compartment. Basic characterization is centralized; bounded technical tasks are distributed to contractors without origin disclosure; ordinary SAPs provide administrative cover; the object is too technologically illegible for dramatic reproduction, limiting downstream signatures. This model predicts at least some convergent traces in specialist tasking, unusual characterization problems, materials provenance, facility history or documentary/physical artifacts even if the central program name remains hidden.

## Defeat conditions

The book's “industrial shadow” thesis weakens materially if well-documented historical programs of comparable physical/technical scope can be shown to have remained effectively traceless across labor, infrastructure, procurement and downstream effects for many decades, or if compartmented extraordinary exploitation can be demonstrated to require essentially no distinguishable external work.

The skeptical application is defeated if a publicly inspectable artifact with defensible chain of custody demonstrates non-terrestrial/non-human manufacture, or authenticated program records plus independently corroborated physical evidence establish a retrieval/exploitation chain that survives ordinary aerospace, deception, contamination and provenance alternatives.

## Publication holds

- Do not write “AARO disproved crash retrieval.” Its 2024 report states its findings; that is not logically equivalent to proof of nonexistence.
- Do not call testimony physical evidence.
- Do not treat patents as evidence of efficacy.
- Do not use Area 51 itself as evidence for or against extraterrestrial programs; use documented MiG exploitation as the control.
- Do not claim AZORIAN's full intelligence yield without recovering the declassified mission history and surviving payload/accounting detail.
- Do not claim a missing industrial shadow until the expected shadow has been quantitatively derived from controls rather than rhetorically asserted.
