# STEAL THE MACHINE

## How States Learn From Technology They Did Not Build

A Sven Hardy Benson Desk book.

## Thesis

The strongest public control for modern crash-retrieval and reverse-engineering claims is not a UFO story. It is the documented history of foreign-materiel exploitation: states really do locate, acquire, conceal, transport, disassemble and exploit adversary hardware. Project AZORIAN is the spectacular case because the United States built an enormous cover enterprise to recover Soviet strategic technology from more than three miles of ocean. The lesson cuts both ways. Retrieval programs are institutionally plausible. But real retrieval also generates an engineering stack: localization, access, custody, transport, facilities, specialists, documentation, failure modes, budgets, cover stories and downstream technical consequences. A claim that borrows the first fact while producing none of the second has not yet earned the analogy.

## Reader promise

This is a book about the machinery between **we found it** and **we learned from it**. It reconstructs documented exploitation programs, then uses them as controls against modern allegations of recovered anomalous technology. It does not assume that non-human technology exists, and it does not assume that secrecy makes such a program impossible.

## Publication holds

Do not state that AZORIAN recovered an intact submarine, nuclear warheads, codebooks or cryptographic equipment unless the specific claim is supported by released primary material. Do not infer modern UAP retrieval programs from historical foreign-materiel programs. Patents establish filing, not working capability. Testimony establishes that a person made a claim, not that the claimed program or material exists. Any alleged anomalous sample must survive provenance, custody and independent-analysis scrutiny before its composition can carry origin claims.
