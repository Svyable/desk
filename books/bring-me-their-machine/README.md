# Bring Me Their Machine

## What Real Reverse Engineering Teaches Us About Extraordinary Retrieval Claims

**Sven Hardy Benson**

### Governing thesis

States really do steal, buy, salvage, defect with, recover, disassemble, fly, instrument, exploit, copy, and quietly bury adversary technology. The documented history is better than the mythology: captured MiGs flown against American pilots; Soviet hardware acquired through allies and defectors; an intelligence service building an implausible ship to claw a ballistic-missile submarine from three miles of Pacific water. Foreign materiel exploitation is not a hypothetical bureaucratic capability. It is a mature intelligence practice.

That fact does not prove modern allegations of recovered non-human craft. It gives us something more useful than belief or ridicule: a control group.

This book reconstructs what genuine secret retrieval and exploitation programs leave behind when they eventually become visible—budgets, engineering constraints, acquisition chains, contractors, test ranges, training changes, accidents, security compartments, cover stories, documents, hardware, institutional descendants, and measurable effects on doctrine. It then applies that historical signature to contemporary crash-retrieval and reverse-engineering allegations. The question is not whether governments *could* hide recovered technology. They demonstrably have. The question is what kind of evidence a program of the alleged scale and duration should leak into the lawful public record, and whether the evidence we actually have resembles the historical controls.

### Evidence classes

1. **Verified/declassified/documented:** U.S. acquisition and exploitation of foreign aircraft; HAVE DOUGHNUT/HAVE DRILL and CONSTANT PEG; Project AZORIAN; foreign technical-document acquisition; the ordinary existence of compartmented exploitation, cover mechanisms, contractors, test infrastructure, and operational feedback.
2. **Credible but incomplete:** attributed testimony and investigative reporting alleging modern retrieval/exploitation activity where witnesses may have relevant access but the public record does not independently establish the claimed hardware, origin, chain of custody, or program scope.
3. **Disputed/unconfirmed:** specific claims of recovered non-human vehicles, biologics, exotic materials, successful reverse engineering, hidden SAPs created to conceal such material, or technological breakthroughs derived from it when no independently inspectable evidence establishes those claims.
4. **Speculative synthesis:** historical foreign-materiel exploitation may function as an empirical baseline for estimating what genuine extraordinary retrieval programs should look like from outside the compartment—and for identifying where secrecy can itself manufacture a misleading evidentiary ecology.

### Publication hold

Never reason from “governments have secretly exploited foreign machines” to “therefore they possess non-human machines.” Historical capability establishes possibility of organization, not identity of recovered material. For every modern allegation, separate witness access, firsthand versus hearsay content, documentary corroboration, chain of custody, physical inspectability, program naming, legal/oversight claims, and independently observable technological consequence. Patents prove filing, not working hardware. Classification proves secrecy, not exotic origin.
