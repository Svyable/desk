# Evidence ledger — Bring Me Their Machine

## Working rule

The historical cases are controls, not analogies licensed to prove the modern claim. A real exploitation program can tell us what bureaucratic and technical traces are possible under secrecy. It cannot tell us that an alleged program exists.

## Class 1 — verified / declassified / documented

### Project AZORIAN

CIA publicly describes AZORIAN as a six-year effort to recover Soviet submarine K-129 after the United States located it on the Pacific floor. CIA and Defense Department support produced the Hughes Glomar Explorer, a capture vehicle, a deep-ocean lift system, and a Howard Hughes/deep-sea-mining cover. CIA's account says a section of the submarine was recovered in August 1974 after part of the wreck broke away during the lift. The engineering history is itself declassified.

Primary/public anchors:
- CIA Museum, Project AZORIAN: https://www.cia.gov/legacy/museum/exhibit/project-azorian/
- CIA, “The Exposing of Project AZORIAN,” 17 March 2020: https://www.cia.gov/stories/story/the-exposing-of-project-azorian/
- CIA Reading Room, “Engineering for AZORIAN,” C05607353: https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/DOC_0005607353.pdf

Boundary: AZORIAN proves that the United States has undertaken an extraordinarily difficult covert physical retrieval of adversary strategic hardware. It does not establish any non-human retrieval.

### HAVE DOUGHNUT / HAVE DRILL / CONSTANT PEG

The National Museum of the U.S. Air Force states that the Air Force secretly acquired and tested MiG-17 and MiG-21 aircraft in HAVE DRILL and HAVE DOUGHNUT in the late 1960s, producing complete technical breakdowns, and later flew acquired MiGs in CONSTANT PEG. The 4477th Test and Evaluation Squadron used actual adversary aircraft to change American aircrew training.

Anchor:
- National Museum of the U.S. Air Force, “CONSTANT PEG: Secret MiGs in the Desert”: https://www.nationalmuseum.af.mil/Visit/Museum-Exhibits/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/1682967/constant-peg-secret-migs-in-the-desert/

Boundary: possession, test flying, technical exploitation, secrecy, and operational feedback are documented. This is a particularly useful control because exploitation created downstream effects in training even while the source aircraft remained secret.

### Technical-document acquisition

CIA Reading Room contains a 5 June 1969 record explicitly titled “Acquisition of Soviet Technical Manuals for the MIG-21PFM Aircraft.” This is mundane compared with stealing a whole aircraft, and therefore useful: exploitation is an information pipeline, not merely a dramatic crash-recovery event.

Anchor:
- CIA Reading Room document 0000772121: https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/0000772121

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete reporting/testimony

Modern witnesses and officials may credibly describe conversations, briefings, access disputes, program allegations, or attempts to obtain oversight. Their credibility and access must be assessed person by person and claim by claim. Testimony under oath increases accountability; it does not convert hearsay into physical proof.

Acquisition queue: official congressional hearing transcripts and video; inspector-general correspondence that is lawfully public; named firsthand witnesses; official AARO historical reports and appendices; documentary rebuttals; serious investigative reporting that identifies sources and distinguishes firsthand from secondhand claims.

## Class 3 — disputed allegations

Hold here unless independently confirmed:
- recovered craft of non-human origin;
- recovered “biologics” of non-human origin;
- successful exploitation of exotic propulsion or power systems;
- specific contractor possession claims without inspectable provenance;
- alleged waived/unacknowledged SAP structures invoked as proof of the underlying material;
- claims that familiar technologies were seeded by recovered non-human hardware.

A program name is not evidence of a program if provenance cannot be established. A witness saying another person told them about a program is evidence that the conversation occurred if corroborated; it is not evidence that the program's claimed contents exist.

## Class 4 — speculative synthesis / open conjecture

### The signature hypothesis

A long-running extraordinary exploitation program should create some combination of: acquisition logistics; storage/security architecture; specialized materials characterization; unusual test infrastructure; contractor interfaces; safety events; compartmented budgets; technical staffing; oversight friction; derivative requirements; and eventually downstream capability or doctrine. Not every trace must become public. But historical programs let us ask which traces persisted despite serious secrecy.

### The secrecy-ecology hypothesis

Compartmentalization can create false convergence. People with adjacent classified access may know that real exotic-looking programs exist, know that foreign hardware is secretly exploited, and know that access denials are normal. Rumor then moves through a population trained to treat missing information as potentially significant. Independent-sounding stories can share an upstream source. The result can look like corroboration without independent physical evidence.

This hypothesis must also be allowed to lose. If independently authenticated records, inspectable materials with documented chain of custody, multiple genuinely independent firsthand witnesses, or reproducible technical effects converge, “secrecy ecology” becomes inadequate.

## Contradictions and incentives to track

- Agencies have incentives both to conceal real collection methods and to deny embarrassing or unlawful activity.
- Intelligence personnel may accurately know compartmentation practice while being wrong about what lies behind a denied compartment.
- Foreign-materiel programs benefit from adversary uncertainty; misleading stories about capabilities can be operationally useful.
- Contractors may possess legitimate proprietary or classified technology that outsiders misidentify.
- Skeptical official reviews can be incomplete because access is incomplete; allegation-friendly narratives can be incomplete because testimony is hearsay.
- Declassification is selective and delayed. Absence from today's archive is not proof of absence. But indefinite appeals to future declassification cannot substitute for present evidence.

## Defeat condition

The book's central historical-control method fails if the alleged extraordinary program is structured so differently from known exploitation programs that no meaningful trace comparison is possible. If so, the manuscript must say so rather than manufacturing a score. Conversely, a robust public chain of custody to genuinely anomalous hardware would outrank the signature method immediately: inspect the object.
