# Research Brief — Single Points of Failure

## Purpose

This book argues that global economic power increasingly concentrates at narrow, hard-to-substitute points in supply chains, payment systems, and infrastructure networks, rather than tracking aggregate economic scale. Each chapter opens with a specific, dated event and traces the mechanism of concentration behind it.

## Evidence hierarchy

Prefer, in order:

1. Government agencies, courts, and official regulatory filings (e.g., SEC filings, GAO reports, EIA data, State Department historical archives).
2. International bodies and industry data consortia (IEA, ITU, TeleGeography, USGS).
3. Trade and industry-research publications with a track record of sourcing primary data (TrendForce, Counterpoint, Fastmarkets, Benchmark Mineral Intelligence, SemiAnalysis-adjacent outlets).
4. High-quality investigative journalism and books built on original reporting (Siddharth Kara's *Cobalt Red*, Chris Miller's *Chip War*, reporting from Reuters, CNBC, the *Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists*, *Rest of World*).
5. Secondary aggregation and explainer coverage, used only for context or when higher-tier sources were not found in the drafting window.

## Claim rules

- Figures on market share, export-control status, prices, and stockpile levels move quickly in this subject area. Where possible, the manuscript names the approximate reporting month or year next to a volatile figure rather than presenting it as a permanent fact.
- Where sources disagree (for example, different outlets citing DRC cobalt's share of global production as anywhere from 67 to 78 percent), the manuscript gives a range rather than a single false-precise number.
- Distinguish reserves/deposits (where a resource exists in the ground) from processing/refining capacity (the ability to turn it into a usable material) — conflating the two was identified as the single most common error in secondary coverage of both the rare-earths and cobalt chapters.
- Distinguish a company's or country's stated position from an independently verified outcome, especially in ongoing trade and export-control disputes where policy reverses quickly.
- Do not present a contested strategic theory (e.g., the "silicon shield") as settled fact.

## Falsification notes

- **Silicon shield (Chapter 3):** Presented explicitly as a contested strategic theory, not a proven deterrent effect. Some analysts argue TSMC's concentration could make Taiwan a more attractive target rather than a protected one; the manuscript does not adjudicate this.
- **Cobalt traceability (Chapter 7):** Corporate responsible-sourcing claims are described as contested and only partially verified by independent researchers, rather than accepted at face value.
- **Export-control policy (Chapter 8):** Presented as a fast-moving and reversible political variable rather than a settled state of affairs; the manuscript notes the December 2025 H200 reversal specifically to avoid freezing a temporary policy state into an apparent permanent fact.
- **MP Materials and critical-minerals stockpiling (Chapter 11):** Explicitly framed as an unproven, still-unfolding bet rather than a demonstrated policy success, since the underlying goal (cost-competitive non-Chinese rare-earth processing at scale) had not yet been achieved as of the drafting window.
