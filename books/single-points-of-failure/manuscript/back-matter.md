# Back Matter

## A Note on Evidence

This book leans heavily on figures — market shares, tonnages, dollar values, dates of export-control decisions — that were current, or as current as public reporting allowed, at the time each chapter was drafted in 2026. Several of the numbers in this book will be out of date by the time it is read, and a few of them may already have moved by the time it goes to print. That is not a flaw to apologize for; it is close to the point of the book. A market controlled by one company at 100 percent, a country's export quota, a chip company's chip-export legal status, and a memory manufacturer's market share are all, in their own ways, chokepoints in motion. Freezing them into permanent facts would have made the book feel more authoritative and would have made it more wrong.

Where a figure could plausibly be disputed depending on the source or the exact month cited, the manuscript tries to say so — "roughly," "on the order of," "estimates ranging from" — rather than presenting a single precise number as beyond question. The full source trail, including publication dates for the most time-sensitive figures, is maintained in [research/README.md](../research/README.md).

A recurring caution threads through every chapter: concentration is not, by itself, evidence of wrongdoing, and fragility is not, by itself, evidence of mismanagement. Several of the chokepoints in this book — ASML's decades of EUV investment, TSMC's yield discipline, the accumulated expertise of a canal pilot — are concentrated because someone solved a hard problem unusually well, not because a shortcut was taken. Other chokepoints in this book — the labor conditions in parts of the Congolese cobalt supply chain, the environmental costs shifted onto communities near early rare-earth processing sites — are concentrated partly because a real cost was avoided by someone who was never made to pay it. The book tries to keep those two very different origins visible rather than collapsing every case of scarcity into a single moral story.

## A Small Glossary

**Artisanal and small-scale mining (ASM)** — Mining conducted by individuals or small groups using largely manual methods and limited mechanization, typically outside formal industrial licensing and safety regulation, common in parts of the Democratic Republic of Congo's cobalt sector and in other mineral economies worldwide.

**Chokepoint** — As used in this book, any point in an economic system through which an unusually large share of value passes and for which no fast substitute exists, whether that point is a physical passage, a company, a mineral-processing capability, a standard, or a body of specialized human knowledge.

**Correspondent banking** — A system in which banks hold accounts with one another, or with shared intermediary institutions, allowing funds to move across borders and between institutions without every pair of banks needing a direct relationship.

**EUV (extreme ultraviolet) lithography** — A chip-manufacturing technique using 13.5-nanometer light, reflected through a chain of specialized mirrors in a near-vacuum, to pattern the smallest transistor features on advanced semiconductors. As of the mid-2020s, only ASML manufactures working EUV systems.

**Fabless** — A semiconductor company that designs chips but does not manufacture them, instead contracting fabrication out to a foundry such as TSMC.

**Foundry** — A semiconductor manufacturer that fabricates chips designed by other companies, as distinct from an integrated device manufacturer that designs and fabricates its own chips.

**High-bandwidth memory (HBM)** — Memory built by vertically stacking multiple chip layers and mounting them immediately adjacent to a processor, used to relieve the "memory wall" bottleneck in AI accelerators. Dominated by SK Hynix, with Samsung and Micron as smaller competitors.

**Rare-earth elements** — A group of seventeen chemically similar elements used in magnets, electronics, and other technologies. Not geologically rare; scarce mainly in the sense that the chemical separation of one rare earth from its neighbors is difficult, costly, and concentrated overwhelmingly in Chinese processing facilities.

**Silicon shield** — The theory that Taiwan's outsized importance to global advanced semiconductor manufacturing gives other countries, and especially China and the United States, a strong incentive to avoid actions that would disrupt Taiwan's stability. A contested and unproven theory, not an established fact.

**Tacit knowledge** — Know-how built through direct experience that resists being fully written down or transferred, such as the judgment a veteran field-service engineer develops for diagnosing an idiosyncratic equipment failure.

**Yield** — In semiconductor manufacturing, the percentage of chips produced on a wafer that function correctly. A major source of competitive advantage between fabrication companies using otherwise similar equipment.

## Closing Note

A chokepoint is not always a place you can visit. Sometimes it is a molecule, a contract, or a person who has not yet trained a replacement. What every chokepoint in this book shares is that it was invisible, to almost everyone, right up until the day it wasn't — and that on the other 364 days of the year, the invisibility was not a failure of attention. It was the chokepoint working exactly as well as it was built to.
