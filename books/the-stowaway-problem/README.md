# The Stowaway Problem

*Planetary Protection, Panspermia, and the Life We Bring With Us*

## Reader promise

A book about a peculiar collision in astrobiology: the evidence that makes interplanetary transfer of life physically less absurd also makes our own life-detection experiments easier to contaminate. The hardiest organisms are simultaneously evidence for biological persistence and the worst possible stowaways.

## Distinct contribution

This is not a generic panspermia book and not a procedural history of planetary protection. It follows the same organism through two literatures that are usually kept apart: extremophile survival experiments and contamination-control engineering. The central claim is narrower than “life can travel between planets.” Rock exchange between Mars and Earth is established; microbial survival through every stage remains conditional. What is established on the other side is that spacecraft assembly facilities harbor persistent, sometimes unusually resistant organisms despite stringent cleaning. The epistemic problem is therefore concrete: a future life-detection signal must survive not only abiotic alternatives, but terrestrial provenance alternatives.

## Strongest competing explanation

The strongest objection is that planetary-protection controls are already designed around this problem, modern molecular methods can identify terrestrial contaminants, and laboratory demonstrations of microbial survival do not imply successful natural lithopanspermia. The book accepts all three points. Its argument survives only if contamination remains a meaningful scientific variable even under modern controls and if survival experiments are treated as boundary conditions rather than proof of interplanetary colonization.

## Evidence classes

1. **Verified/documented:** planetary-protection policies; Viking sterilization; Mars meteorite transfer; spacecraft cleanroom microbiomes; specific sterilization-resistant isolates; EXPOSE-class survival experiments; terrestrial contamination demonstrated in some meteorite organics.
2. **Credible but incomplete:** long-duration transfer survival under realistic shielding; persistence of low-biomass spacecraft contaminants after complete mission chains; some molecular methods' ability to reconstruct provenance after years in space.
3. **Disputed/not established:** successful natural transfer of viable organisms between Mars and Earth; indigenous martian biology; ALH84001 as evidence of past martian life; a cleanroom organism surviving all the way to a habitable extraterrestrial niche.
4. **Speculative synthesis:** the strongest practical evidence for panspermia may come from the same biological traits that make future biosignatures hardest to trust.
