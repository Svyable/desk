# Chronology

- **1960s–1970s:** Planetary-protection requirements become integral to Mars mission design; Viking landers receive stringent biological-cleanliness treatment, including dry heat.
- **1984:** ALH84001 is recovered in Antarctica.
- **1996:** A team reports possible signs consistent with ancient martian life in ALH84001, provoking sustained scrutiny of abiotic and contamination alternatives.
- **1998:** Amino-acid and isotopic studies report terrestrial contamination in portions of ALH84001's organic inventory.
- **2003–2005:** Spacecraft-assembly isolates including unusually UV-resistant *Bacillus pumilus* strains are characterized; SAFR-032 becomes a planetary-protection reference organism.
- **2007:** The strain later named *Tersicoccus phoenicis* is collected during Phoenix-related cleanroom monitoring; a related isolate is recognized from a second continental cleanroom.
- **2008–2009:** EXPOSE-E begins long-duration external ISS exposure experiments.
- **2011:** Shock-recovery work further quantifies survival envelopes relevant to lithopanspermia.
- **2012:** ADAPT/EXPOSE-E results report strong dependence of spore survival on UV exposure and shielding; molecular surveys of Mars-bound hardware show persistent cleanroom diversity.
- **2013:** *Tersicoccus phoenicis* is formally described.
- **2014:** Extreme-tolerant Phoenix-assembly isolates are deposited in a public culture collection; EXPOSE-R SPORES results sharpen the meteorite-shielding boundary.
- **2021:** High-resolution cleanroom microbiome work around the JPL Spacecraft Assembly Facility continues to show that assay choice and community composition matter to bioburden interpretation.
- **November 7, 2025:** COSPAR Bureau approves the current planetary-protection policy referenced in this draft.
- **2026:** Planetary protection remains an active research field as robotic life detection, sample return, private missions and eventual human Mars exploration push against assumptions built for earlier mission architectures.
