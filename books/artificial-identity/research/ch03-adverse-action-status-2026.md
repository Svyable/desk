# Chapter 3 research note — adverse-action explanations after the CFPB guidance withdrawal

## Why this note exists

Chapter 3 uses credit underwriting to make a narrow point: a complex predictive model does not erase the institution's responsibility for the decision it makes with that model. The existing research packet cites CFPB Circulars 2022-03 and 2023-03 as the clearest AI-specific articulation of that idea.

That citation now needs a current-law boundary.

## What changed

On May 12, 2025, the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau withdrew both Circular 2022-03 and Circular 2023-03 along with a larger set of guidance documents. Those circulars should therefore be treated as historical CFPB interpretations, not current guidance.

The withdrawal does not itself repeal the Equal Credit Opportunity Act or Regulation B's adverse-action notice requirement. The current Official Interpretations to Regulation B § 1002.9 still say that the principal reasons for adverse action must relate to and accurately describe the factors actually considered or scored by the creditor. They also say that, when a creditor relies on a credit-scoring system, no principal factor may be excluded from disclosure.

The current legal anchor for the chapter should therefore be the regulation and its official interpretation, not a withdrawn circular.

## Strongest counterargument

The withdrawn circulars contained the most explicit federal language tying these duties to black-box and AI credit models. Removing them matters. The manuscript should not imply that the CFPB's present enforcement position on complex-model explainability is identical to the Bureau's 2022–2023 position merely because the underlying notice rule remains.

The safer claim is narrower: where Regulation B requires a statement of reasons for adverse action, the reasons must be specific and tied to the factors actually considered. That is enough to support the chapter's institutional point without overstating a current AI-specific policy.

## Manuscript implication

The existing sentence that the CFPB “has had to clarify” the principle remains historically accurate, but later revisions should not cite Circular 2023-03 as live guidance. If the passage is refreshed, ground the continuing obligation in § 1002.9 and its Official Interpretations, and identify the AI-specific circulars as withdrawn historical guidance if they are mentioned at all.
