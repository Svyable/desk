# 14. Four Baselines

The strongest experiment in this field may not exist in one laboratory.

It may be the consistency relation among four baselines.

Oklo integrates over geological time and depends on nuclear resonances, reactor physics and geology. Quasar spectroscopy reaches cosmological distances and depends on atomic structure, astrophysical clouds and wavelength calibration. Laboratory clocks probe the present with extraordinary short-term precision and depend on controlled systematics. Thorium nuclear clocks promise a new sensitivity vector involving nuclear structure.

Each has weaknesses. Their weaknesses barely overlap.

That is the opportunity.

A monotonic scalar-field drift that produces a quasar-scale change must not violate Oklo. An oscillating ultralight field can evade a secular geological bound but should produce frequency-domain structure in clocks. A spatial gradient hinted by quasars must survive local equivalence-principle constraints and predict how Earth moves through that gradient. A coupling that strongly shifts thorium but weakly shifts atomic clocks should have a calculable pattern across species.

The field should stop presenting constraints as separate bar charts and start presenting global model trajectories.

For a given scalar model, evolve the field cosmologically. Predict alpha, mass-ratio and QCD-sensitive observables at Oklo time. Predict quasar absorption shifts as a function of redshift and environment. Predict present-day drift and oscillation spectra. Predict equivalence-principle violation. Predict thorium-to-atomic clock ratios.

Then ask whether one parameter set survives all of them.

This is not merely data combination. It is forcing a hypothetical field to live one life across two billion years instead of adopting a different personality in every paper.
