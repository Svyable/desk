# 18. The Number That Must Not Move

Physics uses constants because equations need anchors.

The mistake is thinking the notation guarantees the anchor is metaphysical.

Alpha may be constant. The electron-proton mass ratio may be constant. Every properly controlled experiment so far may continue to return null. That outcome would still teach us something important: any new scalar fields must couple weakly, hide through screening, oscillate outside searched bands, live at inaccessible masses, or otherwise respect an increasingly bizarre set of constraints from rocks, stars, clocks and nuclei.

But the opposite possibility is worth taking seriously because the experiment has changed.

For most of the twentieth century, variation of constants lived partly in cosmological speculation. Now it lives in a laboratory ecosystem with optical clocks, frequency combs, global timing links, precision spectroscopy, atom interferometry and a nuclear transition that laser physicists can finally reach. Oklo provides a geological anchor nobody designed. Quasar absorbers provide a cosmological anchor nobody can rebuild. Thorium provides a new nuclear ruler nobody expected nature to place at optical energy.

These are not separate curiosities. Together they form a detector for dynamical law.

If a dimensionless constant changes, the phrase 'law of nature' needs a footnote. The equations may still be local laws, but some quantities we treated as parameters would become fields with histories, gradients and dynamics. Particle physics, gravitation and cosmology would become less separable. The equivalence principle would move from background assumption to active diagnostic. Metrology would become astronomy without looking outward.

There is also a methodological lesson worth keeping even if every result is null.

The most powerful experiments often arise when disciplines exchange their discarded objects. Nuclear geology hands spent fuel to fundamental physics. Astronomy hands calibration headaches to metrology. Clock builders hand noise spectra to dark-matter theory. Nuclear spectroscopy hands an accidental cancellation to particle physics.

No one owns the question because the question is larger than the instrumentation.

The number must not move if our present framework is complete in the relevant way. So we should build instruments exquisitely capable of catching it in the act.
