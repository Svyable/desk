# Back Matter

## Chronology

- **1870s:** spectroscopy and electromagnetism make combinations later recognizable as dimensionless couplings physically meaningful.
- **1937:** Dirac publicly develops large-number ideas motivating possible variation of constants.
- **1972:** anomalous uranium isotopic composition leads to recognition of the Oklo natural reactors in Gabon.
- **1970s–1990s:** Oklo isotopic data become a probe of historical nuclear resonance energies and possible constant variation.
- **1996:** Damour and Dyson publish a major reanalysis of the Oklo alpha bound.
- **1999–2012:** increasingly sensitive quasar absorption analyses produce both null constraints and controversial evidence for spatial variation.
- **2006:** detailed Oklo reactor-neutronics calculations by Petrov and collaborators report no evidence for resonance change within their modeled range.
- **2010s:** optical clocks reach sensitivity where comparisons strongly constrain present-day drift and begin probing ultralight-field models.
- **2021–2024:** ESPRESSO and related work improve quasar calibration and expose analysis-pipeline systematics.
- **2024:** direct laser spectroscopy of the thorium-229 nuclear transition and frequency connection to optical clocks mark a transition from proposal to experimental platform.
- **2025:** solid-state thorium temperature sensitivity and new nuclear-sensitivity analyses sharpen the path toward fundamental-physics searches.
- **2026:** NIST publicly frames nuclear clocks as probes of dark matter, fifth forces and variation of constants.

## Defeat conditions

The book's strongest synthesis would weaken if: (1) improved nuclear theory showed thorium-229 has little usable sensitivity to underlying coupling variation; (2) Oklo bounds proved dominated by irreducible geochemical uncertainty; (3) quasar absorption methods could not control calibration and kinematic-model systematics below claimed sensitivity; or (4) clock-network systematics produced correlated false positives indistinguishable from proposed field signatures.

None of these would make the individual measurement programs unimportant. They would change the claim that their combination creates a uniquely powerful detector for dynamical constants.

## Research appendix priorities

1. Reconstruct Oklo RZ2 and RZ10 with current evaluated nuclear data and two independent Monte Carlo codes.
2. Publish a common sensitivity-vector table for major atomic, molecular and nuclear clock candidates.
3. Reanalyze benchmark quasar absorbers with common synthetic-injection tests and convergence diagnostics.
4. Propagate measured thorium-229 nuclear observables into alpha/strong-sector sensitivity with multiple nuclear models.
5. Design a preregistered global transient search across heterogeneous clock species.
