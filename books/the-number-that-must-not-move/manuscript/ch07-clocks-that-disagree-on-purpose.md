# 7. Clocks That Disagree on Purpose

The best clock experiment for varying constants is not the best clock.

It is two clocks that would respond differently if the underlying physics moved.

Modern optical clocks reach fractional uncertainties so small that gravitational height differences, blackbody radiation, electric fields and motion all become visible. That achievement creates a second use for clocks. Instead of asking which oscillator keeps time most faithfully, one asks whether two unlike oscillators keep the same ratio.

A frequency ratio is dimensionless. If a strontium optical transition and a cesium hyperfine transition drift relative to each other after every known systematic has been removed, the result cannot be explained by redefining the second. The ratio itself changed.

Different clock species have different sensitivity coefficients. Relativistic effects make some heavy-ion transitions particularly responsive to alpha. Hyperfine frequencies add dependence on nuclear magnetic moments and mass ratios. Molecules can be sensitive to electron-proton mass changes. Highly charged ions and nearly degenerate transitions can provide large enhancement factors.

This turns the periodic table into a detector array.

The danger is that environmental sensitivities can also differ. A clock highly sensitive to alpha may be highly sensitive to electric fields. A network distributed across continents encounters different gravitational potentials, temperatures, magnetic environments, transfer-link errors and local oscillator noise. Searching for an exotic correlated signal requires understanding ordinary correlations better than ordinary timekeeping often demands.

The 2023 analysis by Sherrill and collaborators used Sr, Yb+ and Cs clock data to constrain model-independent variations on timescales from minutes to nearly a day, then translated those limits into constraints on ultralight dark matter and axion-like scenarios. The translation matters: the raw observable is a clock ratio; dark matter enters only through a model of coupling and field behavior.

That distinction should never be compressed away.

The clock does not detect dark matter. It detects—or fails to detect—a structured variation in a frequency ratio. Theory maps that pattern to particle or field parameter space.

This discipline is what makes clocks dangerous to speculative physics in the best possible way. They convert metaphors about hidden fields into time series.
