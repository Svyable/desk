# The Number That Must Not Move

## Oklo, Nuclear Clocks, and the Possibility That Physics Drifts

Sven Hardy Benson

## Author's note

There is a cheap version of this subject and an expensive one. The cheap version says that maybe the constants of nature change and therefore everything is mysterious. The expensive version begins by asking which constants can change in a way that means anything at all.

If the speed of light changes while the meter and the second are defined using the speed of light, language has tied its own shoelaces together. Dimensionful constants live partly inside our unit conventions. Dimensionless ratios do not give us that escape. The fine-structure constant, alpha, is a pure number. So is the electron-to-proton mass ratio. If either differs between two properly compared experiments, no change of rulers can hide it.

This book is about the strange fact that the universe has already performed some of the experiments for us.

In Gabon, uranium ore assembled itself into reactors before multicellular animals existed. Those reactors burned samarium and other isotopes in ways that preserved information about neutron resonances. Quasar light lets us compare atomic transition patterns across billions of years. Atomic clocks can compare different dependence on alpha and mass ratios now. Thorium-229 has handed nuclear physics an absurd gift: a nuclear excitation low enough to address with laser light.

None of these has established that a fundamental constant varies. That negative result is not boring. It is a map of where entire classes of new physics may still hide.

The point is not to make the constants move by rhetoric. The point is to make the hypothesis expensive enough that nature has to pay for it.
