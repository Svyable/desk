# 15. How to Fake a Changing Universe

A field this sensitive to tiny shifts needs an encyclopedia of false universes.

In Oklo, the fake universe is a slightly wrong reactor temperature, a migrated isotope or an oversimplified neutron spectrum. In quasar spectroscopy, it is a wavelength distortion, an unresolved cloud component, isotopic abundance evolution or numerical non-convergence. In atomic clocks, it is blackbody shift, gravitational potential, magnetic field, transfer-link noise or local oscillator behavior. In a solid-state thorium clock, it may be crystal temperature, electric-field gradients or radiation damage.

Notice what is beautiful about this list: the frauds are different.

A genuine underlying field is one of the few hypotheses capable of producing the correct pattern across all systems. That does not mean cross-platform correlation automatically proves new physics. Common time standards, shared calibration databases, analysis software and environmental forcing can create hidden correlations. But heterogeneity makes conspiracy by nuisance harder.

The experimental program should deliberately cultivate this heterogeneity.

Use multiple clock species and independent transfer methods. Use different quasar spectrographs on overlapping targets. Reanalyze Oklo with independent reactor codes and nuclear libraries. Compare trapped-ion and crystal thorium implementations. Archive raw data and calibration states so future theories can be tested without re-running ancient experiments.

Most importantly, inject fake signals.

Analysis pipelines should routinely be challenged with synthetic alpha shifts, transient field crossings and oscillatory patterns unknown to the analysts. A pipeline that cannot recover injected signals without inventing extras has not earned the right to constrain nature.

Precision physics has traditionally treated systematics as dirt to remove. At the frontier, systematics should be turned into an adversarial training set.
