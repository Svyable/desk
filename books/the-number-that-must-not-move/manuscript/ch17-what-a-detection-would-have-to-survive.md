# 17. What a Detection Would Have to Survive

A real detection of varying constants would be one of the largest discoveries in modern physics. The evidentiary threshold should be correspondingly rude.

First, the observable must be stated before the interpretation. A frequency ratio changed. A set of absorption lines exhibited a differential shift. An isotope pattern implied a resonance displacement. Do not begin with 'dark matter detected.'

Second, the signal must reproduce in an instrument with different dominant systematics. A clock-ratio oscillation seen only through one frequency-transfer chain is not enough. A quasar result from one spectrograph is not enough. A thorium shift in one crystal host is not enough.

Third, sensitivity scaling must work. If two transitions have known coefficients K1 and K2, the observed amplitudes should follow the predicted relationship within uncertainty. A candidate that ignores the sensitivity vector is probably an instrument complaint.

Fourth, spacetime scaling must work. A claimed annual modulation should have a phase and environmental dependence distinguishable from seasons. A transient field crossing should propagate across a network consistently. A cosmological gradient should remain fixed on the sky under instrument changes.

Fifth, neighboring theories must pay. A scalar coupling large enough to move alpha may predict equivalence-principle violation, fifth forces or astrophysical effects. Those cannot be ignored because the preferred experiment saw something exciting.

Sixth, the full pipeline should recover blinded injections and reject realistic confounders.

Finally, independent groups should be able to reproduce the analysis from raw or minimally processed data.

The discovery paper should be almost disappointingly explicit about ways the result could die.

That is how a claim this large becomes believable: not by sounding certain, but by surviving a carefully engineered campaign of attempted murder.
