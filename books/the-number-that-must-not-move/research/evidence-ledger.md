# Evidence Ledger

## Class 1 — verified / documented

- Natural self-sustaining fission reactors operated at Oklo, Gabon roughly two billion years ago; multiple reaction zones and distinctive isotopic burnup are documented.
- Sm-149 and other neutron-capture isotopes preserve information about reactor neutron spectra and resonance behavior.
- Modern Oklo calculations find no established evidence that alpha differed from its present value, though exact bounds depend on reactor and nuclear modeling.
- Atomic-clock frequency-ratio comparisons constrain present-day variation of dimensionless constants and can be interpreted in ultralight-field models.
- Quasar absorption spectra enable differential tests of alpha over cosmological lookback times.
- ESPRESSO has produced parts-per-million-level alpha constraints with improved wavelength calibration.
- In 2024, direct laser access to the thorium-229 nuclear isomer transition and frequency comparison with an Sr atomic clock were demonstrated.
- Solid-state thorium spectroscopy has measured environmental sensitivities including temperature shifts.

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete

- Oklo may constrain relative alpha change at or below ~10^-7 over ~2 billion years, with some recent analyses claiming tighter bounds; model dependence remains material.
- Thorium-229 may have alpha sensitivity enhanced by factors in the thousands to ~10^4 because of nuclear-energy cancellations; exact coefficient remains model-dependent.
- Heterogeneous clock networks can probe oscillatory or transient scalar dark matter through correlated frequency-ratio signatures.

## Class 3 — disputed / unconfirmed

- Claims that quasar data establish a spatial dipole in alpha.
- Any positive detection of temporal variation in alpha or electron-proton mass ratio.
- Any identification of clock anomalies with dark matter rather than instrumental or environmental causes.
- Claims that Oklo alone proves a unique bound independent of strong-interaction assumptions.

## Class 4 — speculative synthesis / open conjecture

- A joint Oklo–quasar–atomic-clock–nuclear-clock likelihood may constrain classes of scalar-field models more powerfully than the usual separate-limit presentation reveals.
- Geological nuclear archives may contain other underused fundamental-physics observables.
- A global heterogeneous clock network could function as an Earth-scale detector for transient scalar structures.
- Thorium nuclear clocks might eventually separate electromagnetic from strong-sector variation when combined with atomic and molecular references.
