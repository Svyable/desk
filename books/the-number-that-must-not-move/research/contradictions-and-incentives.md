# Contradictions and Incentives

## Oklo precision versus model dependence

The literature often compresses Oklo into a spectacular alpha bound. The isotopic measurements are real, but the conversion into alpha requires reactor-temperature estimates, neutron-spectrum modeling, burnup reconstruction, nuclear resonance calculations and assumptions about which underlying parameters vary. Incentive risk: a single tight number is easier to cite than a layered uncertainty budget.

## Quasar anomaly versus spectrograph systematics

Large archival samples produced provocative evidence for variation, including a proposed spatial dipole. Later calibration work identified long-range wavelength distortions capable of producing comparable shifts. Incentive risk runs both ways: anomaly papers receive attention; instrument teams may prefer systematics to cosmology. Resolution requires cross-instrument geometry, not motive-reading.

## Blinding versus numerical convergence

Blinding is intended to reduce analyst bias, but 2024 work showed that a specific approach fixing alpha during model construction could interfere with nonlinear convergence. The lesson is not anti-blinding; it is that bias controls can generate algorithmic side effects and must be validated with injections.

## Thorium enhancement versus nuclear uncertainty

Large enhancement factors make nuclear clocks exciting new-physics sensors. The same enhancement arises from cancellations that are difficult to model. Incentive risk: clock advocacy can favor the largest sensitivity estimate; nuclear theorists can favor models that make auxiliary observables tractable. Empirical nuclear measurements should arbitrate.

## Dark-matter interpretation versus raw clock observables

Clock experiments measure ratios, not dark matter. Translation into dark-matter parameter space depends on assumed field coherence, local density and couplings. Incentive risk: exclusion plots can visually erase model assumptions.

## Null-result incentive

The field's strongest result may continue to be non-detection. Precision nulls are scientifically valuable but compete poorly for attention against hints of drifting constants. The book should preserve null results as constraints on whole classes of dynamical-field models rather than treating them as failed discovery attempts.
