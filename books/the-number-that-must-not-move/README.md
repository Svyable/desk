# The Number That Must Not Move

## Oklo, Nuclear Clocks, and the Possibility That Physics Drifts

**Sven Hardy Benson**

### Thesis

Physics writes its laws with constants and then quietly promotes that notation into ontology. But a dimensionless constant such as the fine-structure constant, alpha, need not be a Platonic number. In many extensions of the Standard Model and general relativity it can be the observable shadow of a dynamical field. If that field moves, even slightly, then chemistry, spectra, nuclear resonances, clock ratios and the equivalence principle all become detectors.

The underwritten collision is that humanity already possesses four radically different experiments on this question: a natural fission reactor that ran in Gabon roughly two billion years ago; ancient light crossing intergalactic space through quasar absorption systems; laboratory clocks that compare dissimilar atoms today; and the emerging thorium-229 nuclear clock, whose low-energy nuclear transition may amplify sensitivity to changes in electromagnetic and strong-sector parameters. These are not four versions of the same measurement. Their systematics are almost perversely different. That is exactly why they belong in one book.

### Evidence posture

- **Verified/documented:** Oklo natural reactors and isotopic burnup; precision atomic-clock comparisons; quasar absorption measurements; 2024 direct laser access to the thorium-229 nuclear transition and frequency-ratio measurements to optical clocks.
- **Credible but incomplete:** present constraints on alpha and mass-ratio drift; model-dependent enhancement of thorium-229 sensitivity; interpretation of Oklo resonance shifts after reactor-temperature and nuclear-model corrections.
- **Disputed/unconfirmed:** historical claims of a cosmological alpha dipole; any positive detection of temporal or spatial fundamental-constant variation; specific dark-matter or dark-energy field identification.
- **Speculative synthesis:** constants as local readouts of dynamical fields; a global clock-and-astronomy network as a detector for transient scalar structures; geological nuclear archives as an underused branch of precision fundamental physics.
