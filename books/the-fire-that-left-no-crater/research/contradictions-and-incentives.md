# Contradictions and Incentives

The historical controversy is not reducible to believers versus skeptics. The missing crater created a legitimate physical problem, and airburst modeling offered a legitimate solution class. Later high-pressure evidence changed the balance.

## Live contradictions

- Crater-forming impact is favored by shock evidence, yet no agreed parent crater exists.
- Some specimens emphasize high-pressure histories; others constrain primarily high-temperature/low-pressure crystallization or quench.
- The target is overwhelmingly terrestrial, but simple sampled modern sands/sandstones do not fully reproduce all glass chemistry.
- Meteoritic signatures occur in some components, while other visually suggestive metal-rich streaks are terrestrial.
- Archaeological use is secure at least for Tutankhamun’s scarab, but the collection route is not.

## Incentives and narrative traps

A craterless catastrophe is attractive to planetary-defense discourse because it dramatizes airburst hazard. A pharaonic artifact made from impact glass is attractive to popular archaeology because it invites lost-knowledge stories. A missing crater is attractive to anomaly literature because absence creates narrative space. None of these incentives determines the science, but each can shape which results receive attention.

The manuscript therefore gives greatest weight to discriminating mineral physics, geochemistry, and spatial field evidence.
