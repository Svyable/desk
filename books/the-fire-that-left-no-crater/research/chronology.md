# Chronology

- ~28.5 million years ago — Libyan Desert Glass forms during an extreme high-temperature event in the Great Sand Sea region.
- Ancient Egyptian New Kingdom — a piece of LDG is fashioned into the scarab used in Tutankhamun’s pectoral.
- 1922 — Howard Carter’s team discovers Tutankhamun’s tomb.
- 1932 — Patrick Clayton’s expedition brings modern scientific attention to the LDG field.
- Late twentieth century — impact and giant-airburst models compete as mineralogical, chemical, and geographic evidence accumulates.
- 2010 — detailed inclusion work strengthens the rapid high-temperature melting picture.
- 2018 — mullite study adds constraints on high-temperature formation and argues for impact-related ejecta.
- 2019 — reported former-reidite microstructures in zircon provide high-pressure evidence; separate bedrock work reports shocked quartz in regional outcrops.
- 2020 — trace-element and Sr-isotope work further constrains terrestrial source materials.
- 2026 — dendritic-zircon work refines thermal conditions while explicitly noting that its specimen alone does not discriminate every impact-versus-airburst scenario.
