# Source Log

## Primary / peer-reviewed anchors

1. Christian Koeberl and Ludovic Ferrière, “Libyan Desert Glass area in western Egypt: Shocked quartz in bedrock points to a possible deeply eroded impact structure in the region,” *Meteoritics & Planetary Science* 54 (2019). https://doi.org/10.1111/maps.13250 — Class 1 for reported shocked bedrock and impact interpretation; crater location remains inference.
2. Aaron J. Cavosie and Christian Koeberl, “Overestimation of threat from 100 Mt-class airbursts? High-pressure evidence from zircon in Libyan Desert Glass,” *Geology* 47 (2019). https://doi.org/10.1130/G45974.1 — Class 1 for described zircon microstructures interpreted as former reidite; strong evidence against a purely radiative, no-ground-shock airburst origin.
3. Ansgar Greshake et al., “Brownish inclusions and dark streaks in Libyan Desert Glass: Evidence for high-temperature melting of the target rock,” *Meteoritics & Planetary Science* (2010). https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1945-5100.2010.01283.x — Class 1 for high-temperature mineralogy and terrestrial composition of specific streak populations.
4. Ansgar Greshake et al., “Mullite in Libyan Desert Glass: Evidence for high-temperature/low-pressure formation,” *Meteoritics & Planetary Science* (2018). https://doi.org/10.1111/maps.13030 — Class 1 for mullite observations; authors favor impact-related origin.
5. Elio Sighinolfi et al., “Terrestrial target and melting site of Libyan Desert Glass: New evidence from trace elements and Sr isotopes,” *Meteoritics & Planetary Science* (2020). https://doi.org/10.1111/maps.13550 — Class 1/2 for target-source constraints and isotopic comparisons.
6. Magnani et al., “New evidence on the formation conditions of the Libyan Desert Glass: Clues from a dendritic zircon inclusion,” *Meteoritics & Planetary Science* (2026). https://doi.org/10.1111/maps.70094 — Class 1 for observed zircon inclusion and reconstructed thermal history; authors state this specimen alone cannot discriminate impact melting from airburst.
7. Rita Simon, “The curious history of Tutankhamun’s scarab,” in *Aegyptus et Pannonia IX* (2025/2026 repository release). https://doi.org/10.71067/AePIX-2025-205-266 — Secondary archaeological synthesis for discovery history and the scarab’s material identity/context.
8. Mark B. E. Boslough and David A. Crawford, “Low-altitude airbursts and the impact threat,” *International Journal of Impact Engineering* 35 (2008). https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijimpeng.2008.07.053 — Class 2 numerical/physical modeling for low-altitude airburst energy deposition and downward hot-jet phenomenology; physical mechanism, not event-specific proof for LDG.
9. Vladimir Svetsov, Valery Shuvalov, and Igor Kosarev, “Formation of Libyan Desert Glass: Numerical simulations of melting of silica due to radiation from near-surface airbursts,” *Meteoritics & Planetary Science* 55 (2020). https://doi.org/10.1111/maps.13470 — Class 2 numerical feasibility for radiative silica melting in craterless and crater-forming variants. Important claim boundary: the authors explicitly incorporate the post-2019 high-pressure evidence and allow a hybrid case in which atmospheric breakup is followed by fragment impacts; the modeled parameter sets are not a historical reconstruction of the LDG event.

## Comparative event anchors

10. NASA/JPL and peer-reviewed Chelyabinsk literature should be used in the publication pass for instrumented airburst energetics; no claim in the present draft depends on a specific casualty or energy number.
11. Tunguska is used only as a documented craterless atmospheric-explosion analog, not as evidence that LDG itself formed by airburst.

## Source discipline

No source is used to claim a discovered LDG crater. No archaeological source is used to infer ancient knowledge of impacts. No meteoritic tracer is treated as proof of projectile taxonomy without convergence from multiple systems. “Airburst” is not treated as synonymous with “no ground contact”: some published LDG airburst models include atmospheric fragmentation followed by surface impacts, so high-pressure evidence constrains a purely radiative model more directly than it constrains every hybrid atmospheric/ground-coupled scenario.
