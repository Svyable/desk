# The Missing Source Rock

There is another missing object in this story, quieter than the crater: the exact rock that became the glass.

Libyan Desert Glass is silica-rich, often extraordinarily so. The obvious candidate is local quartz-rich sand or sandstone. Yet geochemical comparisons have repeatedly found that no single sampled modern material reproduces the glass cleanly. Trace elements and isotopes complicate the simple story of dunes flash-melted where they sit.

This is not evidence for extraterrestrial glass. The dominant material is terrestrial. The mismatch is a provenance problem.

The target twenty-eight million years ago was not the landscape seen today. Erosion has removed strata. Climate cycles redistributed sediment. Basement rocks and dykes may have contributed components now exposed differently or gone. An impact can preferentially melt some minerals and leave others. Vaporization can fractionate volatile elements. Immiscible melts and incomplete mixing can preserve schlieren.

Greshake and colleagues’ work on brown inclusions and dark streaks is particularly useful because it shows that visually minor components can carry target information. Some inclusions point toward high-temperature melting of unusual terrestrial phases such as Al-rich orthopyroxene. Other dark streaks have nonchondritic elemental ratios and appear terrestrial despite their metal enrichment.

The source question should therefore be attacked like a mixture problem. Build a library of plausible regional lithologies, including subsurface and eroded units reconstructed from regional geology. Measure major elements, trace elements, Sr-Nd-Pb-Os isotopes where appropriate, zircon age populations, and mineral inclusions. Then ask whether combinations can reproduce glass populations.

This approach can also constrain location. If a distinctive basement lithology contributed to the glass, mapped occurrences of that rock narrow the source region. If zircon age spectra point toward one terrane, that becomes geographical evidence independent of crater morphology.

The missing source rock and missing crater may be the same problem viewed from opposite directions. Find the target, and the crater search shrinks. Map shock, and the target search improves.

The desert surface is not the baseline. It is the latest page of a geological document with many pages torn out.
