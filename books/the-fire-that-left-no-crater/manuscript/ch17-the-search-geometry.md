# The Search Geometry

If the crater is missing, the next expedition should begin with a probability map, not a hunch.

The inputs already exist in scattered form: modern glass-density maps, fragment chemistry, target-rock candidates, shocked-bedrock localities, regional structural geology, satellite imagery, gravity and magnetic data, erosion surfaces, sediment thickness, and numerical ejecta models. The problem is that each literature tends to optimize its own variable.

A proper search would combine them.

Start with trajectory families capable of generating the observed pressure and thermal record. For each family, simulate impact or terminal disruption and predict excavation center, melt production, ejecta footprint, and shock decay. Then degrade the synthetic landscape through plausible 28.5-million-year erosion and burial histories.

Next compare those outputs to field data. Where should shocked bedrock remain? Which target lithologies should be exposed? What gravity anomaly might a buried or deeply eroded structure retain? Which zircon age populations should appear in glass if the impact center lies over one terrane rather than another?

The result is not one circle. It is a ranked landscape.

Field teams can then sample transects designed to maximize information gain between hypotheses. A location where impact model A predicts 20 GPa shock, model B predicts none, and the airburst model predicts thermal alteration only is worth more than another random attractive outcrop.

This approach also clarifies what would count against the impact-crater interpretation. If high-quality mapping finds no spatial organization in shock intensity, no plausible source lithology, no geophysical remnants, and no trajectory capable of connecting candidate regions to the glass distribution, the model should lose probability.

Likewise, an airburst model would need to reproduce not just surface heating but the high-pressure mineral record through a physically credible coupling mechanism. If it cannot, crater absence cannot rescue it.

The search geometry turns an old mystery into experimental design. That is the point where a good anomaly stops being a story and becomes a program.
