# Shock Has a Signature

Heat is promiscuous. Shock is pickier.

Quartz can be heated in many ways. It can melt, recrystallize, crack, and become glass under several geological or technological processes. But certain microscopic deformation structures require pressures reached on timescales associated with hypervelocity impact. Planar deformation features are one such family of evidence. High-pressure polymorphs are another.

This distinction is the reason impact geology became a forensic science rather than a catalog of circular holes. Before shock metamorphism was understood, impact structures could be confused with volcanic or tectonic features. Once diagnostic shock effects were recognized, the argument moved inside minerals.

For the desert-glass problem, that move is decisive. An atmospheric explosion can produce strong winds and enormous thermal flux at the surface. It cannot casually generate tens of gigapascals throughout target minerals. If a glass fragment contains a phase history requiring those pressures, the airburst must either couple to the ground in a way approaching impact or cease to be an airburst-only explanation.

This is where language becomes important. “Airburst” can mean several physical regimes. Chelyabinsk fragmented high in the atmosphere and caused blast damage without excavating a crater remotely commensurate with its energy. Tunguska flattened forest over a huge area and likewise left no accepted large impact crater. A very low-altitude disruption of a much larger body may couple energy to the ground far more strongly. At some point the categorical distinction between exploding just above the surface and striking it becomes less useful than the pressure-temperature-time history actually delivered.

The minerals do not care what we name the event.

They record thresholds. Reidite forms from zircon at high pressure. Baddeleyite can record high-temperature decomposition. Quartz PDFs preserve crystallographic orientations associated with shock. Melt textures constrain heating and quench. Together these are more informative than one label.

A disciplined reconstruction therefore uses shock markers as clocks with badly calibrated hands. Each one says the material crossed a region of pressure-temperature space. Some transformations are reversible. Some leave orientation relationships after the high-pressure phase disappears. Some can be mimicked by non-impact processes and require careful discrimination. The strongest case comes from multiple independent markers whose formation conditions overlap in one event history.

The desert glass has gradually accumulated such markers. That accumulation explains why older debates can sound strangely balanced when read today. At one time the missing crater carried enough weight to keep a pure airburst model competitive. As high-pressure evidence improved, the prior changed.

Science often changes this way—not by a single dramatic paper killing an idea, but by making the idea pay more and more explanatory rent until a cheaper model wins.
