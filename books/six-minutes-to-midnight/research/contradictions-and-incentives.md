# Contradictions and incentives

## Contradictions to keep alive

**The safeguards worked / the incidents were dangerous.** The 1979 warning was rejected rapidly and official records stress that no strategic units advanced alert. This is evidence for resilience. It does not erase the fact that operational channels carried a fictitious attack.

**Able Archer was an exercise / Soviet concern was real.** NATO intent and Soviet interpretation can both be accurately described without converting Soviet fear into proof that war was imminent.

**Human judgment can prevent automation error / human judgment can create error.** Petrov folklore must not become an anti-technology parable. Humans are part of both safety and failure modes.

**More sensor fusion can improve detection / fusion can correlate failure.** Independence must be evaluated by physical and digital dependency, not number of feeds.

**AI can improve warning / AI can increase automation bias.** Public official statements support AI as decision support with human control; claims about specific operational systems require public evidence.

## Incentives and distortions

Military institutions have incentives to reassure publics and allies about command reliability while also seeking modernization funding by emphasizing vulnerability. Arms-control advocates can gain attention by dramatizing near misses. Nuclear hawks can minimize them as harmless safeguards working. Memoir subjects and journalists have incentives toward hero-centered stories. Intelligence agencies have legitimate secrecy interests that also obstruct independent safety assessment. Historians working from declassification face survivorship bias: what becomes public is not a random sample.

## Language rules

Use `false warning` when official records establish erroneous warning. Use `war scare` for the documented 1983 episode but attribute judgments about severity. Reserve `near miss` for cases where the pathway to irreversible action is evidenced rather than imagined. Never convert `could have` into `almost did` without showing the intermediate decisions.
