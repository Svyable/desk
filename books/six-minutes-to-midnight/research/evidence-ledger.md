# Evidence ledger

## Class 1 — verified / declassified / documented

- 9 Nov 1979: a simulated missile-attack scenario was erroneously introduced into NORAD operational warning channels; U.S. commands identified it as false in under six minutes; official State Department reporting said no strategic nuclear units advanced alert.
- 3 Jun 1980: computer-system problems generated erroneous attack indications while direct missile-warning sensors showed no hostile activity; precautionary measures occurred and corrective steps followed.
- 1981–83: Soviet/KGB leadership established intelligence requirements for indicators of Western nuclear attack; a Feb 1983 KGB directive explicitly considered maneuvers/training possible cover.
- Nov 1983: Able Archer practiced NATO nuclear release procedures; declassified Western and former Soviet-bloc records document unusual Soviet concern/reaction.
- Jan 1989: Leonard Perroots's retrospective addendum warned that reciprocal U.S. precautionary force generation during Able Archer might have produced a dangerous escalatory situation.
- Public U.S. defense statements describe modern NC3 as facing aging-architecture, cyber and space challenges and state that AI may support processing while humans remain in control.

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete

- The 26 Sep 1983 Soviet Oko warning episode and Stanislav Petrov's rejection of it are supported by substantial retrospective testimony/reporting, but the open primary documentary record is materially thinner than for the U.S. 1979/1980 events.
- Sunlight/high-cloud geometry is a widely reported technical explanation for the Oko false detections; obtain Soviet technical primary documentation before treating detailed mechanism as fully established.
- Able Archer materially increased nuclear risk. Strong archival evidence supports genuine Soviet concern, but the magnitude of counterfactual risk cannot be measured directly.

## Class 3 — disputed / unconfirmed

- Claims that any named false alarm put the world exactly N minutes from nuclear war.
- Claims that a particular officer single-handedly 'saved the world.'
- Claims about current classified launch procedures, decision thresholds, AI models, cyber vulnerabilities or exact warning timelines beyond official public material.
- Claims that Able Archer would probably or certainly have produced nuclear exchange if one specific decision changed.

## Class 4 — speculative synthesis / open conjecture

- Increasingly accurate AI fusion may increase tail risk if it converts heterogeneous uncertain evidence into a persuasive single confidence score that humans cannot interrogate under time pressure.
- Strategic survivability should be evaluated partly as 'epistemic reserve': the minutes it buys leaders to seek confirmation.
- Future arms-control/risk-reduction regimes should explicitly treat semantic ambiguity, exercises, cyber interference with NC3, machine-generated warning and protected clarification channels as risk variables.

## Defeat conditions

The manuscript's central synthesis weakens if historical review shows false warning was almost always trivial, instantly recognized, and structurally incapable of influencing nuclear decisions; if current postures have eliminated meaningful decision-time compression; or if machine fusion demonstrably increases both accuracy and human contestability without creating correlated dependencies. These are empirical questions, not articles of faith.
