# 3 — Forty-Six Cents of Certainty

On 3 June 1980 the warning machinery lied again, but differently.

The Department of Defense fact sheet is wonderfully dry. Technical problems in the computer system feeding the National Command System generated erroneous indications of a missile attack. The key warning sensors themselves showed no hostile missile activity. Precautionary measures were taken. The disagreement between the generated display and the physical sensors exposed the falsehood.

Later accounts commonly attach the story to a failed computer chip and sometimes to its trivial purchase price. The exact price is less important than the engineering humiliation: strategic consequence is not proportional to component cost. A cheap part can sit at a semantic choke point.

This incident also destroys the comforting theory that once the 1979 test-tape pathway was fixed, the class of problem was fixed. November had been contamination between simulation and operations. June was a technical failure producing erroneous attack data. Same apparent proposition—missiles are coming—different causal route.

That distinction matters for modern machine learning. A safety case built around the last failure tends to become a museum of patched doors. The next error arrives through the wall. Robustness comes from asking what evidence would have to exist in the world if the proposition were true. Incoming missiles should leave multiple physically independent signatures. If the central display screams while the sky's dedicated witnesses remain silent, silence is evidence.

There is a subtle danger here. Operators can also become habituated to false alarms. A system with too many erroneous warnings teaches humans to discount the very alert it exists to deliver. Reliability is therefore not merely an engineering virtue. It shapes human belief. Every false positive changes the prior of the person at the console.

The June event is documented fact. Claims about exactly how close particular officials came to launch decisions require more care. The useful story does not need embellishment. The most heavily armed state on Earth had built a machine capable of rendering nonexistent missiles into operationally meaningful data, and the machine's defense was another machine looking at a different piece of physics.
