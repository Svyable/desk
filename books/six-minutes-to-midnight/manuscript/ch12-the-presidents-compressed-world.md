# 12 — The President's Compressed World

A national leader does not receive the sky. The leader receives a compressed representation of the sky.

Sensors become tracks. Tracks become assessments. Assessments become briefings. Briefings become options. At every stage information is discarded so a decision can fit inside time.

Compression is unavoidable. It is also political. Whoever chooses what survives compression chooses which uncertainties remain visible. A display that says HIGH CONFIDENCE may conceal disagreement between phenomenologies, a known sensor anomaly, or a model assumption that would matter enormously if spoken aloud.

The right interface for nuclear warning should therefore preserve provenance under pressure. Not more data—more lineage. Which sensors independently observed what? Which conclusions are model outputs? What contradicts the leading hypothesis? What changed in the last sixty seconds? What would we expect next if the attack were real?

This is not a proposal for a busier screen. It is a proposal that uncertainty remain attached to the claim rather than evaporating as the claim moves upward.

The president's world will always be compressed. Humanity's survival may depend on refusing to compress away the reasons it might be wrong.
