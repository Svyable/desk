# Watching It Fall

The most unsettling accidents are not always surprises. Sometimes the system is visible while it is becoming uncontrollable.

By January 1978, Cosmos 954's orbital trouble was known outside the Soviet Union. Tracking networks could see a decaying object. They could calculate possible windows and regions. What they could not do was turn orbital observation into a precise address before re-entry. An object moving at orbital velocity can be well observed and still have an uncertain terrestrial destination because small differences in atmospheric drag, attitude and breakup timing produce enormous differences downrange.

This created a peculiar form of warning. Governments had time to worry but not enough positional certainty to stage one ordinary local response. Emergency responders had to think in corridors and probabilities. The IAEA now describes the incident as the world's first experience with the uncontrolled re-entry of a space object carrying radioactive material, a prototype for later international preparedness.

That word, prototype, conceals the improvisation. There was no mature global playbook for a reactor falling from orbit. Canada had federal expertise in radiation, geology, defence, transport and northern operations, but Health Canada's retrospective says no single federal department was responsible for such an event. Responsibility had to be assembled while the object was descending.

There was also a diplomatic asymmetry. The Soviet Union knew the spacecraft. Canada controlled the territory where debris might land. The United States possessed tracking and nuclear-emergency capabilities that could help. Information therefore had strategic value even before a fragment was recovered.

The accident is a reminder that detection and control are different achievements. A tracked satellite can still be uncontrolled. A predicted re-entry can still have a huge uncertainty ellipse. A government can know that a hazard is coming and still not know where to put the trucks.

Preparedness often means building a useful response before the location becomes a fact.
