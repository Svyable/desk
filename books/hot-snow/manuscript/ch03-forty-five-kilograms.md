# Forty-Five Kilograms

Modern IAEA emergency-preparedness material describes Cosmos 954 as carrying a reactor fueled with roughly forty-five kilograms of highly enriched uranium. That figure is useful and dangerous. Useful because it communicates why governments cared. Dangerous because forty-five kilograms of uranium is not forty-five kilograms of uniformly recoverable radioactive debris.

A reactor after atmospheric breakup is a materials problem. Fuel composition, cladding, activation products, fission products, structural alloys, burnup, heating and aerodynamic fragmentation all matter. Some material may survive in recognizable pieces. Some may be dispersed into particles. Some may remain in components that do not advertise themselves except through a detector. Some signals can be confused with naturally radioactive terrain.

The public imagination likes lost objects. Investigators had something worse: an uncertain source term.

That distinction is visible in the later search. Recovery teams did not simply walk outward from a crater because there was no single crater holding the answer. They flew. They sampled. They used radiation instruments that could discriminate energy signatures. They divided likely impact regions. They handled fragments as radiological objects and evidence at the same time.

The reactor also created a communications problem. Officials needed to tell people enough to reduce exposure without pretending they knew more than they did. A highly enriched uranium inventory sounds apocalyptic to a public trained by weapons language, but reactor fuel scattered by re-entry is not a nuclear detonation. Conversely, saying that no explosion occurred says almost nothing about localized contamination from intensely radioactive fragments.

Good emergency language has to survive that middle territory.

One reason Cosmos 954 remains useful is that it punishes the single-number instinct. The fuel mass does not tell you the dose. The fraction recovered does not tell you the fraction that reached the surface intact. The mapped footprint does not mean every square kilometre was equivalently contaminated. The absence of observed health effects does not retroactively make the search unnecessary.

A source term is not a verdict. It is the beginning of an accounting problem.
