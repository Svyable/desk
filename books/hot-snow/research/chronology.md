# Chronology

- 1960s–1970s — Soviet Union develops and operates reactor-powered Radar Ocean Reconnaissance Satellites; Canada develops airborne gamma-ray spectrometry for geological work.
- September 18, 1977 — Cosmos 954 launched.
- Late 1977–January 1978 — orbital trouble and decay become an international tracking concern.
- January 24, 1978 — Cosmos 954 re-enters and disintegrates over northern Canada.
- January 25 onward — airborne and ground search intensifies under Operation Morning Light.
- 1978 — radioactive debris is detected, recovered, characterized and removed; search continues into October.
- 1978 — international debate expands over safety of nuclear-powered satellites.
- 1979 — Canada formally presents compensation claim to USSR under international agreements and general international law.
- April 2, 1981 — USSR agrees to pay Canada C$3 million in full and final settlement.
- 1983 — Cosmos 1402 reactor core re-enters over South Atlantic, providing an important comparison case.
- December 14, 1992 — UN General Assembly adopts Principles Relevant to the Use of Nuclear Power Sources in Outer Space.
- Present — Cosmos 954 remains a standard case in space-liability and radiological-emergency preparedness literature.
