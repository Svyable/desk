# Source log

## Canadian official

- Health Canada, `Previous nuclear incidents and accidents: COSMOS 954`: https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/health-risks-safety/radiation/radiological-nuclear-emergencies/previous-incidents-accidents/cosmos-954.html
  - January 24, 1978 crash; 124,000 km² figure; Operation Morning Light through October 1978; estimated recovery of about 0.1% of power source; subsequent international policy attention.
- Geological Survey of Canada / ISED, `Operation Morning Light (1978)`: https://www.ised-isde.canada.ca/site/science/en/educational-resources/history-geological-survey-canada-175-objects/123-operation-morning-light-1978
  - Hundreds of Canadian participants; 120-person U.S. NEST; airborne gamma-ray spectrometer; first debris identification near Fort Reliance; geological-survey lineage.
- Government of Canada Treaty Information, 1981 settlement text: https://www.treaty-accord.gc.ca/text-texte.aspx?id=103429
  - C$3,000,000 full and final settlement signed April 2, 1981; the protocol contains no admission of liability or treaty interpretation.

## International law

- United Nations International Law Commission materials discussing the Canadian claim and approximately C$13.97 million response costs: https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/494252/files/A_CN.4_531-EN.pdf
- United Nations materials noting the 1981 C$3 million settlement: https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/538650/files/A_CN.4_543-EN.pdf
- United Nations International Law Commission, `A/CN.4/L.608/Add.4`: https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/446162/files/A_CN-4_L-608_Add-4-EN.pdf
  - Records Canada's reliance on the Liability Convention and general international law; preserves the reasonable/proximate/calculable cost standard and later characterization of the settlement payment as ex gratia.
- JAXA legal-material reproduction, `Settlement of Claim between Canada and USSR for Damage Caused by Cosmos 954`: https://www.jaxa.jp/library/space_law/chapter_3/3-2-2-1_e.html
  - Reproduces the Canadian claim figures used in Chapter 14: C$6,041,174.70 claimed; Phase I costs of C$12,048,239.11 with C$4,414,348.86 claimed; Phase II costs of C$1,921,904.55 with C$1,626,825.84 claimed. Treat as a reproduction pending recovery of the original authenticated Canadian note.
- United Nations Committee on the Peaceful Uses of Outer Space, 1991 report containing the agreed nuclear-power-source principles: https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/135717/files/A_46_20-EN.pdf
  - Principle 9 explicitly includes duly substantiated search, recovery and clean-up expenses, including assistance received from third parties.
- United Nations General Assembly Resolution 47/68, `Principles Relevant to the Use of Nuclear Power Sources in Outer Space`, adopted December 14, 1992: https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/282974
  - Final adoption of the principles; do not infer from textual resemblance alone that Cosmos 954 caused Principle 9.

## Chapter 14 claim boundaries

- The 1981 protocol settled the dispute but did not adjudicate the meaning of `damage` under the Liability Convention.
- The C$3 million payment should not be narrated as a court-awarded fraction of a C$6.04 million liability. Canada itself had already excluded substantial response spending from its formal claim, and the diplomatic settlement supplied no judicial allocation or reasoned damages ruling.
- Principle 9 is strong evidence that search, recovery and cleanup later became explicit compensation categories in UN nuclear-power-source principles. Causal attribution from Cosmos 954 to that text remains a separate historical question requiring the negotiating record.
