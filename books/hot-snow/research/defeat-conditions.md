# Defeat conditions

The book's central synthesis should weaken if primary records show that Canada already had a mature, single-owner operational plan specifically for uncontrolled nuclear-satellite re-entry and that Morning Light required little institutional improvisation.

The argument that geological sensing capability transferred decisively should weaken if operations logs show that Canadian airborne spectrometry added little relative to U.S. systems or ground search.

The legal-afterlife argument should weaken if later nuclear-power-source rules and compensation doctrine developed independently with little reliance on Cosmos 954.

Claims about community disruption should be reduced or removed if local records show negligible warning, interruption or concern; they must not be inferred from the accident's technical seriousness.

Claims about Soviet secrecy increasing response uncertainty should be limited to specific documented withheld or delayed information, not deduced from the Soviet system's general secrecy.

The book should explicitly preserve evidence that emergency response was proportionate and health consequences limited if that is what the best epidemiological and environmental record supports.
