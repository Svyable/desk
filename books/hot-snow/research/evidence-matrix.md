# Evidence matrix

## Class 1 — verified / documented

- Cosmos 954 was a Soviet nuclear-powered surveillance satellite.
- It re-entered uncontrollably on January 24, 1978 and scattered radioactive debris across northern Canada.
- Canada and the United States conducted Operation Morning Light.
- Canadian airborne gamma-ray spectrometry was used to identify debris against natural background.
- Health Canada describes an affected/search geography of at least 124,000 km² and estimates recovery at about 0.1% of the power source.
- Canada presented an international compensation claim.
- The USSR agreed on April 2, 1981 to pay C$3 million in full and final settlement.
- Cosmos 954 became a major reference point in international work on nuclear power sources in outer space.

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete

- Exact pre-entry intelligence-sharing chronology among USSR, Canada and United States.
- Complete accounting of reactor material after atmospheric breakup.
- Intelligence exploitation of recovered components.
- Exact local disruption, warning and risk perception in individual northern communities.
- Protocol-level search thresholds and false-positive burden across all sectors.

## Class 3 — disputed / not established in this draft

- Claims that most of the reactor remained intact but hidden.
- Claims of significant unacknowledged population health injury caused by Cosmos 954.
- Claims that the Soviet Union deliberately concealed a known imminent Canadian impact point.
- Claims that recovered components yielded a specific classified intelligence breakthrough.
- Claims that the C$3 million payment judicially validated every Canadian legal theory.

## Class 4 — speculative synthesis

- Cosmos 954 as an interoperability stress test showing that rare emergencies are failures of institutional categorization as much as hardware.
- Geological anomaly-detection expertise as the decisive transferable capability in Morning Light.
- Emergency search as a general epistemic model: act before certainty while progressively tightening claims.
- Accident debris as a historical control case for what genuine exotic material should look like under increasingly close measurement.
