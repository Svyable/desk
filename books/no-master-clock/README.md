## Working thesis

Physics has steadily demoted the master clock. Relativity replaces one universal time with local proper times; precision metrology exposes the physical cost of comparing them; quantum gravity inherits a deeper problem because ordinary quantum theory assumes the external time that gravity makes dynamical. Several frontier programs therefore treat time as relational, conditional, thermal, or otherwise emergent rather than as a universal background parameter. None of that means clocks are arbitrary. It means clocks are physical systems, synchronization is an operation, and common time is an achievement.

The human role developed here is correspondingly modest and large: civilization is a synchronization machine. We build conventions, instruments, networks, and institutions that let local clocks coordinate without pretending the universe supplied a single cosmic wristwatch.
