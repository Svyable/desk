# Chapter 1 research — Find the Uncarried Bag

## Chapter purpose

Establish the book's core move through a real, ordinary worker rather than a celebrity case: useful people notice the work that sits between formal roles. The chapter should make that claim concrete, then complicate it. Unclaimed work can create learning, trust, and leverage, but it can also be an organization's way of exporting cost to conscientious employees, patients, and families.

The chapter uses Ana Melendez as the primary contemporary character, patient navigation as the historical case, and research on administrative burden and invisible/articulation work as the explanatory backbone.

## Primary character: Ana Melendez

### Alliance for Equity in Cancer Care — "Day in the Life: How a patient navigator balances the needs of the Latinx community with her own"

https://www.equityincancercare.org/news/day-in-the-life-how-a-patient-navigator-balances-the-needs-of-the-latinx-community-with-her-own/

Use for:

- Melendez's current roles as lead in Community Health & Supportive Services at El Centro de Servicios Sociales and as a cancer navigator through Case Comprehensive Cancer Center.
- Her description of barriers involving finances, language, medical-system complexity, and access to local resources.
- Her path into El Centro: grew up on the same block; grandmother used El Centro services and did not speak or write English; left an 11-year job during COVID; entered El Centro through a receptionist opening encouraged by her aunt.
- Reported daily details: alarm at 5:30 but usually awake by 4; three children; medicine for her oldest daughter; school drop-off; Spanish music or a call with a friend on the morning commute; team check-in; email/voicemail; nutrition classes; appointment support and interpretation; patient portals; Medicaid and SNAP applications; calls, walk-ins, referrals.
- Thursday senior socialization lunch, café, and pan con mantequilla.
- Her practice of driving home in quiet to decompress.
- El Centro's self-reported scale: roughly 15 employees and more than 10,000 individuals served in 2025.

Caution:

This is an organizational interview published by an Alliance program connected to her work. Treat it as a first-person source for her biography and descriptions of her work, not as independent verification of institutional impact claims. The 10,000-person service figure is explicitly framed in the manuscript as El Centro's own account.

## Administrative burden

### Moynihan, Herd & Harvey (2015), Journal of Public Administration Research and Theory — "Administrative Burden: Learning, Psychological, and Compliance Costs in Citizen-State Interactions"

https://doi.org/10.1093/jopart/muu009

Use for:

- The three-part framework of learning, compliance, and psychological costs.
- The important caution that administrative burden is not always accidental; burden levels and distribution can reflect policy choices.

Do not overextend a citizen-state framework into a universal theory of every workplace. The chapter uses it as a lens for patient experience and then draws an explicitly analogical connection to organizations.

### Kyle & Frakt (2021), Health Services Research — "Patient administrative burden in the US health care system"

https://doi.org/10.1111/1475-6773.13861
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34498259/

Use for:

- National survey of 4,155 insured, nonelderly adults.
- 73% reported at least one of five common administrative tasks in the prior year.
- 24.4% of respondents overall reported delayed or foregone care due to an administrative task.
- Five task categories: scheduling, obtaining information, prior authorization, billing problems, premium problems.

Caution:

The survey measures insured nonelderly adults and should not be generalized to all patients. The manuscript says exactly that. The chapter also distinguishes administration from burden; administrative tasks can be necessary and useful.

## Patient navigation history and evidence

### Harold P. Freeman — history and principles of patient navigation

Freeman HP. "Patient Navigation: A Community Based Strategy to Reduce Cancer Disparities." Journal of Urban Health (2006).
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2527166/

Freeman HP, Rodriguez RL. "History and Principles of Patient Navigation." Cancer (2011).
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4557777/

Use for:

- American Cancer Society hearings on cancer in the poor and the barriers identified there.
- Freeman's launch of the first patient navigation program at Harlem Hospital in 1990.
- Barrier categories: financial, communication/information, medical-system, fear/distrust/emotional.
- Historical Harlem cohort figures: earlier five-year survival 39%, later five-year survival 70%; early-stage disease also increased substantially after screening plus navigation.

Critical caution:

Do not write that navigation alone caused the Harlem survival improvement. The intervention combined free/low-cost screening, navigation, outreach, and culturally responsive education, and the compared cohorts came from different time periods. The chapter explicitly rejects the clean causal story.

### Patient Navigation Research Program — multisite controlled evidence

Freund KM et al. "Impact of Patient Navigation on Timely Cancer Care: The Patient Navigation Research Program." Journal of the National Cancer Institute (2014).
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4072900/
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24938303/

Use for:

- 10,521 participants with abnormal screening tests and 2,105 with cancer or precancer diagnoses.
- No benefit in the first 90 days, but moderate benefit from 91–365 days for diagnostic resolution and treatment initiation.
- Greatest benefit in centers with the longest usual-care delays.

This result is valuable because it resists a universal success narrative: navigation is especially useful where ordinary systems create delay.

### Chan et al. (2023), CA: A Cancer Journal for Clinicians — overview of systematic reviews

https://acsjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.3322/caac.21788
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37358040/

Use for:

- 61 systematic reviews plus emerging literature.
- Broad conclusion that navigation improves cancer-screening participation and reduces time from screening to diagnosis and diagnosis to treatment initiation.
- Evidence is thinner in some later parts of the care continuum, including palliative care.

## Invisible and articulation work

### Susan Leigh Star & Anselm Strauss (1999) — "Layers of Silence, Arenas of Voice: The Ecology of Visible and Invisible Work"

https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1008651105359

Use for:

- The argument that no work is inherently visible or invisible; visibility depends on the indicators institutions and observers choose to recognize.
- "Articulation work" as a lens for the background coordination needed to make interdependent work cohere.

### Anselm Strauss (1988) — "The Articulation of Project Work: An Organizational Process"

https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1533-8525.1988.tb01249.x

Use for:

- Historical grounding of articulation work as the process of aligning interlocking and sequential elements of project work.

Caution:

The manuscript paraphrases these concepts rather than dropping academic quotations into trade prose. The reader should feel the research underneath the chapter, not be asked to admire the bibliography.

## Falsification and anti-myth notes

- Ana Melendez is not presented as a solitary hero who fixes health care. Her work is described as collaborative and embedded in a network of clinics, community organizations, hospitals, food resources, and other navigators.
- The chapter does not claim every unclaimed task is an opportunity. It explicitly treats invisible work as a potential site of exploitation and cost-shifting.
- The chapter does not claim patient navigation alone caused the Harlem survival improvement.
- The chapter does not claim administrative work is inherently wasteful. Necessary administration is distinguished from burdens that delay or prevent access.
- The chapter does not claim AI or automation cannot handle context or exceptions. It argues only that automation moves the boundary of where human judgment and responsibility are needed, and that the durable career skill is understanding outcomes and exceptions rather than defending a specific manual task.
- El Centro's 2025 service volume is identified as the organization's own report rather than independently audited data.

## Voice check

The chapter should avoid:

- motivational stage directions
- "here's the secret" reveals
- stacked antithesis
- invented dialogue
- celebrity cameos
- framework-heavy subheadings
- neat claims that one behavior guarantees career advancement
- ending every case with a moral

The intended texture is closer to the strongest Desk manuscripts: concrete observation, reported detail, an explanatory model introduced only after the reader has seen the problem, counter-evidence left intact, and an ending that returns to the ordinary work rather than shouting a slogan.
