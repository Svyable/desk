# Chapter 2 Research — Be Early, Not Loud

## Chapter claim under test

A useful worker often creates disproportionate value by seeing the next constraint before it becomes the current emergency. That does not mean indiscriminate initiative. The stronger claim is narrower: anticipation becomes valuable when it rests on real craft knowledge, situational judgment, respect for safety and authority boundaries, and enough social awareness to improve the work without turning initiative into a status performance.

The chapter should distinguish four things that are easy to collapse into one another:

- being proactive
- being competent
- being authorized
- being visible

The book's preferred behavior is not “always take charge.” It is learning enough about what is trying to happen that you can make the next sensible step easier, including escalating early when the problem belongs to somebody else.

## Recurring character: Tonya Hicks

Tonya Hicks is the founder/CEO of Power Solutions, Inc. in Atlanta and the founder of Women Do Everything. The chapter uses her first-person account as the narrative spine because it contains an unusually clear example of informal operational leadership before formal authority.

### Strongly usable first-person details

Primary interview/transcript:

- MCJ Skilled Labor Series / Inevitable podcast, interview with Tonya Hicks, January 2023: https://mcj.vc/inevitable-podcast/tonya-hicks
- Hicks says she attended Central State University on a mathematics scholarship and left after becoming interested in electricians' use of math on an industrial construction site.
- She describes the IBEW apprenticeship as five years, combining full-time work with night classes.
- She says she became the first woman journeyman wireman in IBEW Local 917 in Meridian, Mississippi, and the first Black woman IBEW journeyman electrician in Mississippi.
- She says that during the apprenticeship she had to travel out of state for work because local employers would not give her enough hours, while still returning for required classes.
- She says she was graduated several months before her class specifically so she would not appear in the graduation photograph. This is a serious allegation about discrimination and should remain explicitly attributed to Hicks unless independent union records or contemporaneous documentation are found.
- Around minutes 13–15, Hicks says she was never the foreman or lead electrician but routinely told coworkers how she thought the crew should divide the work. She attributes their willingness to listen partly to her competence as an electrician and the credibility of the plan.
- She recalls a coworker telling her that if she was going to tell everyone what to do, she might as well own the company. She says she decided that day to start a business.
- She describes buying a Gateway computer, making early business cards with clip art, and setting up a desk in the sunroom of her apartment.
- Later in the interview she says women she met through an Atlanta Women's Chamber event helped introduce her to early business opportunities.
- She says that after the first year she moved away from doing all the field work herself and focused more on getting business and hiring strong people.

Supporting company/business records:

- Power Solutions company history: https://www.powersolutionsintl.com/about-us/
- Better Business Bureau business profile records Power Solutions, Inc. as starting December 5, 2000 and lists Tonya Hicks as Founder/CEO: https://www.bbb.org/us/ga/atlanta/profile/electrical-contractors/power-solutions-inc-0443-28129639
- 2026 CBS Atlanta profile marking the company's 25-year milestone and repeating Hicks's account of entering the trade: https://www.cbsnews.com/atlanta/news/atlanta-business-owner-paves-the-way-for-young-people-of-color-in-the-electrical-field/
- 2016 NPR/WBEZ profile documents that Hicks's Atlanta electrical business expanded, contracted during the recession, and later began adding workers again: https://www.wbez.org/morning-edition/2016/09/26/divided-states-female-business-owner-in-georgia-favors-clinton

### Caution on “first” claims

Hicks's own biography, company materials, conference biographies, and multiple interviews repeat the first-woman/first-Black-woman claims. These are useful corroborating repeats but not independent archival verification because most appear to derive from Hicks or her supplied biography. Until IBEW Local 917 records or another independent contemporaneous source is obtained, phrase the claims as “Hicks says” or “according to Hicks.”

## Research: personal initiative and proactive behavior

### Frese and Fay — personal initiative

Michael Frese and Doris Fay, “Personal initiative: An active performance concept for work in the 21st century,” Research in Organizational Behavior 23 (2001), 133–187.

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/S0191-3085(01)23005-6
- Defines personal initiative as self-starting and proactive behavior that overcomes barriers to achieve a goal.
- Their model includes goals, information collection, planning, action, and feedback, which is useful for resisting the shallow equation of initiative with enthusiasm or constant volunteering.
- The chapter uses this research to support the claim that useful initiative begins before visible action.

### Grant and Ashford — dynamics of proactivity

Adam M. Grant and Susan J. Ashford, “The dynamics of proactivity at work,” Research in Organizational Behavior 28 (2008), 3–34.

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.riob.2008.04.002
- Integrates forms of proactive behavior and emphasizes anticipatory, future-focused action shaped by situational and psychological conditions.
- Useful as support for the idea that proactivity is context-sensitive rather than a universal instruction to intervene.

### Situational judgment as a boundary condition

David Chan, “Interactive effects of situational judgment effectiveness and proactive personality on work perceptions and work outcomes,” Journal of Applied Psychology 91(2) (2006), 475–481.

- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16551198/
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1037/0021-9010.91.2.475
- In a sample of 139 employees, proactive personality predicted more positive work perceptions/outcomes among people with high situational-judgment effectiveness but negative outcomes among those low in situational judgment.
- This is a useful empirical caution against treating proactivity as unconditionally beneficial.
- Do not inflate this one study into a universal causal law.

### Social costs of taking charge

Melody Jun Zhang, Kenneth S. Law, and Lin Wang, “The risks and benefits of initiating change at work: Social consequences for proactive employees who take charge,” Personnel Psychology 74(4) (2021), 721–750.

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/peps.12423
- Research summary: https://research.cuhk.edu.hk/en/publications/the-risks-and-benefits-of-initiating-change-at-work-social-conseq-2/
- Wiley page: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/peps.12423
- Three-wave multisource study plus follow-up experiment.
- Employees who took charge more than teammates tended to be perceived as more competent and were more likely to emerge as informal leaders, but were also perceived as less warm and received less coworker support, especially in low-initiative climates.
- Useful because it makes “Be Early, Not Loud” a social argument as well as an operational one: taking charge can improve status and simultaneously create relational costs.

### Network-building mechanism

Jeffery A. Thompson, “Proactive personality and job performance: a social capital perspective,” Journal of Applied Psychology 90(5) (2005), 1011–1017.

- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16162073/
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1037/0021-9010.90.5.1011
- Study of 126 employee-supervisor dyads; suggests performance benefits of proactive personality were mediated by network building and initiative taking.
- Useful later in the book for the reputation/access chain, but not necessary to lean on heavily in this chapter.

## Construction research: anticipation and making work ready

The construction literature gives the chapter a concrete operational vocabulary for the difference between a schedule and executable work.

### Lookahead planning and constraints

Farook Hamzeh, PhD dissertation, “Improving Construction Workflow — The Role of Production Planning and Control,” University of California, Berkeley (2009): https://escholarship.org/uc/item/1wr2c7mv

- Describes the Last Planner System and the role of lookahead planning in connecting higher-level schedules to executable weekly work.
- Highlights the importance of identifying and removing constraints before planned work reaches the crew.

Farook Hamzeh, Emile Zankoul, and Fatima Alsakka, “Removing Constraints to Make Tasks Ready in Weekly Work Planning,” Procedia Engineering 164 (2016), 68–74.

- DOI/source: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1877705816339340
- Open-access paper; studies “Tasks Made Ready” (TMR) as a measure of how well lookahead planning identifies and removes constraints before execution.
- Simulation results in the paper find TMR a useful predictor of project duration.

Farook Hamzeh et al., “Understanding the role of ‘tasks anticipated’ in lookahead planning through simulation,” Automation in Construction 49 (2015).

- Source: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0926580514002039
- Describes lookahead planning as breaking work into operations, designing operations, identifying constraints, assigning responsibility, and making assignments ready.
- Useful for the chapter's central distinction between work that SHOULD be done and work that CAN be done.

Caution: Last Planner research includes simulations, case studies, and action research; do not present every reported association as settled universal causality across all construction contexts.

## Automation / AI and lookahead planning

Ranjith K. Soman and Miguel Molina-Solana, “Automating look-ahead schedule generation for construction using linked-data based constraint checking and reinforcement learning,” Automation in Construction (2022), 104069.

- DOI/source: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0926580521005203
- Combines linked-data constraint checking with reinforcement learning to generate conflict-free lookahead schedules more quickly than conventional methods in the study setting.
- Supports the claim that codified constraints are increasingly automatable and should be.

“Construction schedule augmentation with implicit dependency constraints and automated generation of lookahead plan revisions,” Automation in Construction (2023).

- Source: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0926580523001565
- Notes that traditional schedules often fail to capture the implicit rationale and flexibility behind dependencies and that some planning knowledge is transferred verbally rather than represented directly in schedules.
- Useful for the durable AI argument: automation improves once constraints are represented; people still add value by noticing, eliciting, and taking responsibility for context that has not yet been captured.

Do not turn this into a claim that machines “cannot” discover implicit constraints. The correct claim is temporal and operational: many relevant constraints today remain outside formal representations, while research is actively moving toward encoding and learning more of them.

## Safety boundary

OSHA 29 CFR 1910.333, “Selection and use of work practices”: https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.333

- Requires, among other things, that only qualified persons work on electrical circuit parts or equipment that have not been deenergized under the applicable procedures.
- Useful as a concrete demonstration that safety-critical work deliberately limits initiative by competence and authority.

OSHA 29 CFR 1926.960, “Working on or near exposed energized parts”: https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.960

- Contains qualified-employee requirements for certain electric-power construction work.

Keep the legal/regulatory claim narrow and avoid turning this chapter into electrical safety advice.

## Falsification / counter-evidence notes

- Do not claim that proactivity reliably produces promotion, leadership, or career success. Research shows benefits plus substantial boundary conditions and social costs.
- Do not claim that quiet people are inherently more effective than visible people. “Not loud” means avoiding performative initiative, not prescribing personality.
- Do not imply Hicks's success was caused by one coworker's suggestion to start a company. Her later business depended on trade skill, customer acquisition, networks, labor, market conditions, and years of operating experience.
- Do not imply discrimination strengthened Hicks or was somehow necessary to her competence. It imposed costs and reduced opportunity; the chapter should not convert barriers into motivational scenery.
- Do not independently assert the Local 917 graduation-photo allegation without further corroboration. Attribute it to Hicks.
- Do not use Last Planner System research to imply that every workplace can or should formalize every constraint. The analogy is about anticipatory preparation, not a universal management method.
- Do not romanticize “seeing around corners.” Most anticipation is learned pattern recognition plus checking dependencies, not intuition or clairvoyance.
- Do not suggest employees should act outside legal, professional, safety, or organizational authority. Early escalation is often the useful action.
