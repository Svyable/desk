# Chapter 4 Research — Learn the Whole Machine

## Chapter claim

Useful people often become more valuable when they understand the dependencies around their own task: where information originates, who has authority, what the next role needs, where work waits, and what the person outside the organization experiences when a handoff fails.

The chapter should not argue that everybody should become a generalist or that crossing boundaries is always good. The evidence supports a narrower claim: coordination and knowledge spanning can improve performance, while also creating role stress, ambiguity, and overload. Formal authority, task uncertainty, and context matter.

## Boundary-spanning research

### Leicht-Deobald et al. — team boundary management meta-analysis

Ulrich Leicht-Deobald, Julia Backmann, Thomas A. de Vries, Matthias Weiss, Sebastian Hohmann, Frank Walter, Gerben S. van der Vegt, Martin Hoegl, “A Contingency Framework for the Performance Consequences of Team Boundary Management: A Meta-Analysis of 30 Years of Research.” Journal of Management.

Open-access version:
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11705025/

Publisher:
https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/01492063231206107

Use for:

- Meta-analysis synthesizes 85 primary studies and 10,848 teams.
- Positive overall relationship between team boundary management and team performance.
- Benefits are stronger for boundary-spanning activities such as coordination, representation, and information search than for boundary-strengthening/buffering activities.
- Effects depend on who carries the activity and what kind of boundary management is performed.
- Formal team leaders were more effective carriers of some boundary-management activity than ordinary members.

Caution / falsification:

- This is team-level evidence, not proof that any individual employee who crosses boundaries will perform better.
- Boundary management includes different behaviors with different costs and outcomes.
- The chapter should not turn “boundary spanning” into a universal career prescription.

### Employee boundary-spanning meta-analysis

“Benefits and costs of employee boundary-spanning behavior: A meta-analytic review.” Acta Psychologica Sinica (2022).

https://journal.psych.ac.cn/acps/EN/10.3724/SP.J.1041.2022.00665

Use for:

- Meta-analysis of 50 studies / 52 independent samples, total N = 14,366.
- Reports positive relationships with job satisfaction, organizational commitment, performance, and innovation.
- Also reports role-stress costs.
- Relationships vary by employee type and cultural/contextual moderators.

Caution:

- Do not describe boundary spanning as cost-free citizenship.
- The role-stress finding is important to the chapter’s “human glue” warning.
- Correlational/meta-analytic associations do not establish that boundary spanning alone causes the positive outcomes.

### Faraj & Yan — boundary work in knowledge teams

Samer Faraj and Aimin Yan, “Boundary work in knowledge teams.” Journal of Applied Psychology 94(3), 2009, 604–617.

PubMed:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19450002/

DOI: 10.1037/a0014367

Use for:

- Develops and tests a model of boundary spanning, buffering, and reinforcement.
- Study used 64 software-development teams.
- Boundary activities related to team performance and psychological safety, with effects moderated by task uncertainty and resource scarcity.

Caution:

- Software teams are a specific context.
- Useful mainly to reinforce that boundary work has contingencies rather than a single linear effect.

## Relational coordination

### Bolton, Logan & Gittell — systematic review

Rendelle Bolton, Caroline Logan, Jody Hoffer Gittell, “Revisiting Relational Coordination: A Systematic Review.” Journal of Applied Behavioral Science, 2021.

https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0021886321991597

Use for:

- Relational coordination theory argues that shared goals, shared knowledge, and mutual respect support frequent, timely, accurate, problem-solving communication in interdependent work.
- Gives the chapter a way to explain why understanding another role does not require becoming that role.

Caution:

- Do not imply that “shared knowledge” means universal expertise.
- The chapter uses relational coordination as a coordination lens, not as a branded framework the reader is required to memorize.

## Cross-domain support-work evidence

### Knowledge boundary spanning in enterprise-system support

“Knowledge boundary spanning and productivity in information systems support community.” Decision Support Systems.

https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167923615001827

Use for:

- Archival study of 36 information-systems support workers handling 23,450 requests from 4,568 end users during the first 13 months after a large SAP/R3 implementation.
- Examines experience spanning business-unit and technical-system knowledge boundaries.
- Finds both network centrality and boundary-spanning experience positively associated with productivity; boundary-spanning experience was substantially more important than centrality in this setting.

Caution / falsification:

- One organization and 36 support workers: do not generalize the magnitude universally.
- “More important than centrality” is specific to the study’s measures and context.
- Use as a concrete example of why cross-domain exposure can help people diagnose problems whose symptoms appear in one system but originate elsewhere.

## AI / organizational knowledge

### NIST AI 600-1 — Generative AI Profile

Chloe Autio et al., “Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework: Generative Artificial Intelligence Profile,” NIST AI 600-1, July 2024, updated April 2026.

Landing page:
https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-generative-artificial-intelligence

PDF:
https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf

DOI: 10.6028/NIST.AI.600-1

Use for:

- NIST defines “confabulation” as GAI systems confidently generating erroneous or false content, including internally inconsistent output.
- NIST notes particular concern in highly contextual, expertise-dependent, and consequential domains.
- Supports the chapter’s warning that fluent synthesis of organizational documents should not automatically be treated as authoritative system understanding.

Caution:

- Do not imply all AI summaries are unreliable.
- The durable claim is that authoritative source, scope, currency, exception handling, and accountability remain important when acting on synthesized information.

### Xu et al. — tacit knowledge integration with generative AI (2026 conference research)

Aiden Zheqi Xu, Kun Zhang, Luye Bao, Yuchen Wang, Wei Wei, “Beyond Codification: Generative AI and the Integration of Tacit Knowledge in Organizations.” Academy of Management Proceedings, 2026.

https://journals.aom.org/doi/10.5465/AMPROC.2026.286bp

Use cautiously for:

- Emerging evidence that generative AI may help organizations integrate tacit knowledge at scale rather than merely search explicit documentation.
- The study reports 66 semi-structured interviews across 52 organizations and proposes pattern-based mediation as one mechanism.
- Supports the chapter’s refusal to claim humans will permanently monopolize tacit organizational context.

Caution:

- Conference-proceedings research, recent and early.
- Do not treat findings as settled or use them to make quantitative productivity claims.
- The manuscript therefore states only that research is beginning to examine this possibility.

## Claims deliberately avoided

- “Hicks became successful because she read receipts.” Too causal; evidence supports only that she says the experience taught her about the business and later helped.
- “Boundary spanners always outperform specialists.” Not supported.
- “Cross-functional workers are better leaders.” Too broad.
- “AI cannot understand tacit knowledge.” The chapter explicitly rejects this static claim.
- “AI will eliminate boundary-spanning work.” Also unsupported.
- “Silos are bad.” Some boundaries protect expertise, focus, safety, confidentiality, and accountability.
- “Everyone should rotate through every department.” Not supported and operationally unrealistic.
