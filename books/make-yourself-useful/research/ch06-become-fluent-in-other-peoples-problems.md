# Chapter 6 research — Become Fluent in Other People's Problems

## Narrative purpose and possible disconfirmation

Chapters 1–5 have established unowned coordination, anticipation, help with boundaries, cross-system literacy, and promises sized to reality. This chapter changes the unit of analysis: two people can describe the same process accurately while counting different costs. A shift planner may see labor coverage; the employee sees childcare, class enrollment, bus connections, sleep, and uncertainty. Fluency requires finding costs that the local metric does not record, testing explanations with the people affected, and changing an actual decision rather than merely adopting an empathetic vocabulary.

The argument must not become "workers know everything and managers know nothing." Customer demand and labor budgets are real constraints; good scheduling and staffed service require tradeoffs. Nor is interviewing one worker a representative study. The difficult proposition is that some apparent efficiency is cost transfer, and the relevant costs are knowable enough to put back into the decision.

## The mechanism: risk moves, people pay

**Daniel Schneider and Kristen Harknett, "Consequences of Routine Work-Schedule Instability for Worker Health and Well-Being," American Sociological Review 84(1), 2019, pp. 82–114.**
https://shift.hks.harvard.edu/onsequences-of-routine-work-schedule-instability-for-worker-health-and-well-being/
Research brief with sampling, denominators: https://shift.hks.harvard.edu/consequences-of-routine-work-schedule-instability-for-worker-health-and-wellbeing/
- Descriptive survey sample 27,792 hourly employees at 80 large food-service and retail firms surveyed 2016–17.
- About two-thirds received less than two weeks' notice; about a third less than one week's; 50% reported "clopening" shifts in measurement, 26% on-call; ~32% variation in hours between most and least busy week over prior month.
- Associations: schedule instability related to distress, poor sleep and unhappiness, with work–life conflict major mediator; correlation/mediation cannot conclusively establish isolated causation. Cannot generalize directly to every US worker, sector, or era.

**Elizabeth O. Ananat and Anna Gassman-Pines, "Work Schedule Unpredictability: Daily Occurrence and Effects on Working Parents' Well-Being," Journal of Marriage and Family 83(1), 2021 (first published 2020).**
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8651235/
https://doi.org/10.1111/jomf.12696
- 92 workers with children aged 2–7, daily surveys for 30 days; 2,221 worker-days analyzed.
- Unanticipated schedule change on 13.3% of observed days; 87% participants experienced at least one in a month.
- Within-family daily association with negative mood and worse perceived sleep quality; modest sample and some marginal associations, not universal causal effect.
- The daily scale is useful to distinguish weekly averages from life disruptions on the day.

**Schneider & Harknett, "Maternal Exposure to Work Schedule Unpredictability and Child Behavior," Journal of Marriage and Family, 2022.**
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9293031/
- 2,613 mothers in retail/food service, 2017–19; unstable schedules associated with children's internalizing/externalizing behavior. Mediation analysis suggests economic insecurity, parenting time and maternal well-being; cannot claim that a given mother's schedule caused any individual child's symptoms.
- Optional; do not overload manuscript with numbers.

## Counterevidence: working on the schedule can work, but effects are qualified

**Stable Scheduling Study, Center for WorkLife Law with Gap, Inc., UC Hastings, UChicago, UNC, March 2018.**
https://worklifelaw.org/projects/stable-scheduling-study/report/
https://news.uchicago.edu/story/stable-scheduling-increases-sales-and-employee-productivity-study-finds
**Formal peer-reviewed report: Saravanan Kesavan et al., "Doing Well by Doing Good: Improving Retail Store Performance with Responsible Scheduling Practices at the Gap, Inc.", Management Science (published online March 2, 2022).**
https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2021.4291
- 28 participating stores; 19 randomly assigned treatment, 9 controls, nine-month pilot 2015–16 in SF/Chicago.
- All stores already adopted advance two-week notice and eliminated on-call shifts, while intervention stores tested a package with shift swapping, stable times, core scheduling, some hours guarantees and targeted staffing. It therefore **cannot isolate** one scheduling intervention.
- 2018 report estimated a 7% improvement in median sales and ~5% labor productivity. Peer-reviewed 2022 paper reports **intent-to-treat 5.1% increase in productivity, driven by +3.3% sales and –1.8% labor**. These are *different outcomes/estimands*; never mix the median sales change with the ITT sales figure as if identical.
- Researchers found only 30% of week-to-week payroll-hours variability explained by customer traffic; managers cited inaccurate shipment info, late promotions, and corporate visits as other drivers. This undermines simple "demand made us do it" account in those stores.
- Important boundaries: retail apparel not cafes or hospitals, small sample of stores, package treatment and manager effort, heterogeneous staffing constraints; not proof that stable shifts universally increase profits.

**Ananat, Gassman-Pines & Fitz-Henley II, "The Effects of the Emeryville Fair Workweek Ordinance on the Daily Lives of Low-Wage Workers and Their Families," RSF Journal (2022).**
https://www.rsfjournal.org/content/8/5/45
https://www.nber.org/papers/w29792
- 96 workers with young children, 78 in longitudinal analyses, before/after; treated firms vs smaller exempt stores.
- Greater stability and improved parent well-being/sleep; fewer workdays with more hours per workday, total hours roughly unchanged.
- Not a randomized trial; small geographically bounded sample. More evidence of feasibility than universal promise.

## Complementary mechanism: labels and targets are not the person

**Elizabeth N. Liao et al., "Using a human-centered, mixed methods approach to understand the patient waiting experience and its impact on medically underserved populations," BMC Health Services Research 22 (2022).**
https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12913-022-08792-8
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9682738/
- Zuckerberg San Francisco General outpatient surgical clinic. 96 timed observations and 43 interviews; average visit 95.8 minutes, 68.5% of time waiting, 27% of visits > two hours.
- Travel barriers, distant neighborhoods, work and childcare constraints; patients late to appointments could be labeled "no-show" and appointment canceled.
- Study reports parking restrictions and worry about losing place when moving car. Do not generalize the mean to all hospitals, imply patient waits always unnecessary, or assume a no-show label proves no attempt to attend.
- Human-centered qualitative investigation can expose absent metrics but still requires representative processes, consent/privacy, resource allocation and testing. "Empathy" alone is not measurement or entitlement to collect sensitive histories.

## Customer effort, as contrast not universal proof

**Matthew Dixon, Karen Freeman & Nicholas Toman, "Stop Trying to Delight Your Customers," Harvard Business Review (July–August 2010).**
https://hbr.org/archive-toc/BR1007
Interview clarifying mechanism and self-service nuance: https://hbr.org/podcast/2010/07/why-delighting-your-customers
- Research program reported >75,000 contact-center and self-service respondents; conceptual argument that easy resolution and repeat-contact avoidance can matter more than manufactured delight.
- Commercial/customer-service research: methodological detail limited in public excerpts. Use as a provocative managerial account, not conclusive independent evidence. Some customers genuinely need a human to help; low-effort is not identical to lowest employer cost.
- The repeated-explanation burden overlaps prior chapters; focus on **whose cost is omitted** rather than repeating "close the loop" of Chapter 5.

## AI / algorithms: what changes, what does not

- Forecasting demand is useful; optimized staffing can reduce wasted hours. But the objective function may omit childcare, commute, sleep, turnover costs, employee control, inaccessible channels or downstream losses. The omission is a managerial choice and a measurement problem, not proof an algorithm is malicious.
- More data / models can model many formerly neglected constraints. Avoid claiming permanently human-only empathy or that algorithms inevitably worsen scheduling.
- Systems can support preference capture, constraints, stable shift patterns, workload alerts, and exception escalation. Reported policy compliance must be distinguished from actual impact on affected people.
- Cheap synthetic service language is not a substitute for meaningful correction of the schedule/appointment/access. The chapter's concrete test: after hearing the affected person's explanation, did a different decision become possible?
- Privacy and autonomy: do not require employees or clients to narrate their private lives to earn schedule stability or usable care. Ask for limits and preferences they choose to share; design a fair default.

## Claims/falsification check

- Navarro's pay/savings/schedule are historical 2014 reportage; cannot assume current circumstances.
- Preserve Kantor's explicit statement that Navarro's breakup had multiple causes; avoid a causal morality play.
- Distinguish company policy declaration from outcomes. Company later claims improvement and alternative benefits; do not erase competing evidence.
- Gap RCT: cluster-level, multicomponent, limited geography, distinct published metrics. No universal profit claim.
- Shift survey associative with select big-chain hourly workers; daily diary small.
- Emeryville before/after is quasi-experimental and small.
- Hospital study is one safety-net outpatient site; observation does not license sweeping national claims.
- Empathy can be paternalistic, exhausting, biased, and insufficient without authority. Inquire without demanding disclosure; corroborate accounts; put costs into concrete decisions.
- Do not invent speech, thoughts, stores, rooms, meeting dialogue, or managerial responses.
- Make the human character consequential without turning her personal hardship into another person's career-development fable.
