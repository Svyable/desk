# Recurring Character Slate

This is a working slate, not a locked cast. The goal is five to seven real people with enough documentary evidence to recur across chapters without forcing their lives into a predetermined moral.

## Ana Melendez — community health and cancer navigator

Why she fits: Melendez entered El Centro de Servicios Sociales in Lorain, Ohio through a receptionist opening after leaving an eleven-year job during the COVID period. She now leads Community Health & Supportive Services and works as a cancer navigator. Her documented day crosses referrals, portals, benefits, interpretation, appointments, food access, and community partnerships.

Possible returns: Chapters 1, 6, 12.

Sources:
- https://www.equityincancercare.org/news/day-in-the-life-how-a-patient-navigator-balances-the-needs-of-the-latinx-community-with-her-own/
- https://www.equityincancercare.org/news/community-embedded-patient-navigation/

## Tonya Hicks — electrician and business owner

Why she fits: Hicks entered the electrical trades when women, and especially Black women, were rare in the field. During an apprenticeship in which she was sometimes sidelined into office or cleanup work, she used the office time to study receipts and learn how the business operated. She later became a journeyman electrician and founded Power Solutions. Her story contains both quiet learning and the later conversion of individual capability into opportunity for other women.

Possible returns: Chapters 2, 4, 8, 12.

Sources:
- Canary Media, “Only 2% of US electricians are women. Tonya Hicks wants to change that”: https://www.canarymedia.com/articles/clean-energy-jobs/only-2-of-us-electricians-are-women-tonya-hicks-wants-to-change-that
- Georgia Public Broadcasting profile, April 30, 2026: https://www.gpb.org/blogs/hullingers-musings/2026/04/30/tonya-hicks-atlanta-based-business-owner-and-entrepreneur
- Power Solutions company history: https://www.powersolutionsintl.com/about-us/

Caution: company biographies are useful for dates and current roles but should not be the sole evidence for causal claims or self-promotional success narratives.

## Amanda Perry — librarian and digital navigator

Why she fits: Perry started at Salt Lake City Public Library as an aide and progressed to associate librarian and Digital Navigator. During the pandemic-era digital-navigation pilot, she worked with people who lacked devices, internet access, or basic digital skills and stayed in contact until a concrete goal was met. The work turns the “two-minute favor” into something more disciplined: a small act that can reveal a recurring access problem and become a service model.

Possible returns: Chapters 3, 6, 10.

Sources:
- Urban Libraries Council profile: https://www.urbanlibraries.org/blog/digital-navigator-profile-amanda-perry
- Urban Libraries Council Digital Navigators initiative: https://www.urbanlibraries.org/initiatives/racial-equity/digital-navigators
- Digital Navigators Toolkit (participant cases and Amanda Perry interview): https://metro.org/do/978/iiif/f416aa39-2753-4cb2-b80d-625c945da8da/full/full/0/Digital-Navigators-Toolkit-Final.pdf

## Valerie Langston — neighborhood organizer

Why she fits: Langston has lived in Milwaukee’s Merrill Park/Hilltopper area for decades and helped co-found and lead a block-watch effort. Her documented work includes neighborhood cleanups, meetings, walking the neighborhood with public officials, and repeated local organizing rather than one headline event. She offers a way to write about reliability as accumulated local trust.

Possible returns: Chapters 3, 5, 9, 12.

Sources:
- Near West Side Partners resident profile: https://nearwestsidemke.org/hilltopper-community-resident-spotlight-valerie-langston/
- TMJ4 profile of her AARP Wisconsin volunteer award: https://www.tmj4.com/news/local-news/milwaukee-woman-wins-aarp-wisconsins-top-volunteer-award
- Near West Side transformation-plan material and ongoing community updates can be used for corroboration.

Caution: award language tends to be promotional. Prefer documented activities, dates, and repeat involvement over praise quoted from award announcements.

## Teresa Flores and Erika Gonzalez — small-business owner and advisor

Why they fit: Flores owns Little Munchkins Produce in Chicago’s Back of the Yards neighborhood with her family. She connected with Gonzalez, executive director of 36Squared Business Incubator, while trying to apply for an Illinois grant. Flores did not receive the grant, which makes the story more useful than a simple success narrative: the value lay partly in learning the process, building an advisory relationship, meeting other owners, and beginning to improve delegation and control inside the business.

Possible returns: Chapters 4, 6, 9.

Source:
- Cook County Small Business Source, “Little Munchkins: A small corner store with big plans,” March 11, 2025: https://cookcountysmallbiz.org/little-munchkins-a-small-corner-store-with-big-plans/

Caution: this is a county economic-development story and therefore partly promotional. Treat it as a lead supported by a direct interview, not as independent evaluation of business outcomes.

## Open slot — analyst or technologist

Still needed: an early-career analyst, operations technologist, or civic-data worker whose documented value came from taking on a neglected data/process problem, understanding how the inputs were produced, and making uncertainty or definitions visible rather than simply building a prettier dashboard.

Do not fill this slot with a prominent chief data officer merely because a case study is easy to find. Prefer a person closer to the actual repair work.

## Cast test

Before promoting any candidate into the recurring cast, confirm:

- At least two independent or differently situated sources where practical.
- Enough chronology to distinguish what the person knew then from what became obvious later.
- At least one operational detail that changes the reader’s understanding of the work.
- Other people and institutions that deserve causal credit.
- A chapter return that reveals a genuinely different facet of the person rather than retelling the same anecdote.
- No need to invent dialogue, motives, or sensory detail to make the story work.
