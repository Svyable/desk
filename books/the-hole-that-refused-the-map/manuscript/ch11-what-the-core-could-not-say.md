# What the Core Could Not Say

Direct evidence has its own mythology.

Once a borehole produces rock that can be held in the hand, it is tempting to treat the sample as the final court of appeal. But a recovered core is not the crust. It is a displaced fragment that has been depressurized, cooled, handled, cut and curated.

Some information survives beautifully: mineral assemblages, textures, isotopic systems, inclusions, structural fabrics. Other information is damaged or lost. Fracture aperture changes when pressure drops. Fluids escape. gases exsolve. weak zones may not recover at all. Drilling-induced fractures can be confused with natural ones if orientation and stress information are poor.

Kola therefore forces a useful hierarchy of evidence without allowing any one layer to become sovereign. Core constrains logs. Logs constrain intervals with poor recovery. Seismic data provide scale. drilling behavior supplies mechanical clues. Regional mapping provides tectonic context.

The strongest interpretation is the one that survives these channels simultaneously.

This is also the answer to a recurring question about anomalous evidence: what would direct proof look like? Direct proof is rarely one object detached from context. A fragment without provenance may be less informative than a boring chain of custody plus multiple measurements. Kola's scientific power came from correlation—depth, branch, lithology, log, temperature, seismic response—not from possession of impressive rocks.

A core is powerful because it is located.

Remove the location and it becomes a specimen.

Remove the measurement history and it becomes a souvenir.
