# The Hole Splits Into Branches

The popular diagram lies by necessity.

A single vertical black line descending to 12,262 metres is how Kola is remembered. The actual well system branched. Deep failures forced sidetracks. One trajectory became inaccessible and another began from above the obstruction. The final geometry was a record of adversity.

This matters scientifically because 'depth' hides path dependence. The deepest sample, the deepest temperature and the deepest logging run may not all belong to one uninterrupted physical tube. A statement about what happened 'at twelve kilometres' has to be anchored to a branch and a measurement history.

There is a broader epistemic point here. Instruments accumulate biography. Telescopes are recoated. satellites lose detectors. clinical assays change reagents. intelligence programs change collection platforms. If the data are presented as one seamless series, hardware history becomes invisible and false continuity enters the analysis.

Kola's branching makes continuity impossible to pretend.

The borehole also shows why failed equipment is evidence. A stuck string tells us something about geometry, stress or hole condition. Repeated collapse identifies mechanically difficult intervals. Lost circulation identifies permeability. Drilling speed records hardness, abrasiveness and tool performance. The operational log is a shadow geological log.

This does not mean every engineering failure can be inverted uniquely into crustal properties. Far from it. Human procedure, equipment quality and simple bad luck matter. But discarding the drilling history while interpreting only the recovered core would remove one of the experiment's richest channels.

The Earth answered Kola partly by breaking it.
