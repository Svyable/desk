## Thesis

The Kola Superdeep Borehole matters less because it was deep than because it forced geology to compare elegant indirect models with twelve kilometres of inconvenient direct evidence: predicted compositional boundaries dissolved into changes of state, supposedly dry crystalline crust carried fluids, temperature outran engineering assumptions, and the world's deepest scientific hole later acquired a folklore life more famous than its actual discoveries. Modern ultradeep drilling in China makes Kola newly relevant as a case study in how direct access changes both Earth science and the stories societies tell around inaccessible knowledge.
