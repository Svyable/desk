# Chronology

- **1950s–1960s** — U.S. Project Mohole develops deep-ocean drilling methods but is canceled before reaching the mantle.
- **1970** — drilling begins at Kola SG-3 on the Baltic Shield.
- **1970s–1980s** — Kola deepens through Pechenga Proterozoic rocks and Archean basement; multiple scientific and engineering programs accumulate around the well.
- **1984** — major Soviet technical synthesis edited by E. M. Kozlovsky appears; Kola is showcased internationally.
- **1989** — deepest SG-3 branch reaches **12,262 m**; English-language 'Well to Hell' folklore begins circulating in religious media around the same period.
- **1990–1994** — deep drilling continues through sidetracks/branches under increasingly difficult thermal, mechanical and financial conditions.
- **1991** — Soviet Union dissolves, destabilizing funding and institutions supporting the project.
- **1992** — Pavlenkova publishes a major synthesis emphasizing heterogeneous physical properties, fluids, porosity, anisotropy and the non-simple nature of seismic boundaries.
- **1992** — Kola-92 international seismic work uses the borehole as direct control on crustal reflection interpretations.
- **1994–1995** — final drilling efforts end; the project is mothballed.
- **1998** — Ganchin et al. publish integrated seismic studies linking reflections to compositional layering, shear zones and fluid-filled structures.
- **2020** — deep Kola fluid inclusions are reported to contain exceptionally high concentrations of gold nanoparticles.
- **2021** — 50th-anniversary retrospective reviews scientific and engineering achievements.
- **2023** — Charlotte Wrigley publishes a historical/geographical study of Kola as excavation, collaboration and post-Soviet ruin.
- **2023–2025** — China's Shenditake-1 ultradeep well is drilled in the Tarim Basin, ultimately reaching 10,910 m.
- **2026** — new reviews and >10 km Chinese drilling records reposition ultradeep access as an active scientific and engineering frontier rather than a closed Cold War chapter.
