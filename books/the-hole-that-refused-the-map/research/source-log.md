# Source Log

## Primary / serious technical sources

- N. I. Pavlenkova (1992), **The Kola Superdeep Drillhole and the nature of seismic boundaries**, *Terra Nova* 4:117–123. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-3121.1992.tb00456.x — key synthesis on Proterozoic/Archean section, porosity, fluids, anisotropy, velocity inversion and uncertain deep boundaries. Important claim boundary: the subhorizontal boundary near 7 km is explicitly described as of uncertain origin; core observations associate it with higher porosity and velocity anisotropy rather than proving a single causal mechanism.
- Lev Vernik, Stephen Hickman, David Lockner & Michael Rusanov (1994), **Ultrasonic velocities in cores from the Kola superdeep well and the nature of subhorizontal seismic reflections**, *Journal of Geophysical Research: Solid Earth* 99. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1029/94JB01236 — measurements on 12 cores across 0–12 km at 2–100 MPa, dry and water-saturated, compared with sonic-log/VSP data. Strong counterevidence to a simple "water caused the Kola low-velocity zone" story: the authors argue that the inversion below ~4.3 km is most likely due to bulk-composition variation plus foliation-induced anisotropy, while explicitly reviewing the enhanced-porosity/free-water hypothesis as a competing interpretation.
- J. Digranes et al. (1996), **Analysis of shear waves observed in VSP data from the superdeep well at Kola, Russia**, *Geophysical Journal International* 126:545–562. https://academic.oup.com/gji/article/126/2/545/624421 — two VSPs from ~2.15–6.0 km; shear-wave splitting below ~4.4 km; estimated anisotropy ~4–5%, locally ~10% in the Luchlompolsky fault zone. Both aligned hornblende fabric and aligned vertical cracks are considered plausible explanations, illustrating mechanism non-uniqueness even with borehole control.
- Y. V. Ganchin et al. (1998), **Seismic studies around the Kola Superdeep Borehole, Russia**, *Tectonophysics* 288:1–16. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/S0040-1951(97)00280-1 — Kola-92 reflection/VSP experiment; 38-km ~20-fold CDP line plus two VSPs recorded at 25-m intervals from ~2.2–6.0 km. Useful for separating reflectors tied to lithologic layering from shear/fault zones, cataclastic fabrics, anisotropy and deeper subhorizontal structures.
- E. M. Kozlovsky, ed. (1984), **The Superdeep Well of the Kola Peninsula — Exploration of the Deep Continental Crust**, Springer — foundational Soviet-era technical compilation; full extraction remains required. An English USGS translation/compilation is available as Open-File Report 86-517: https://doi.org/10.3133/ofr86517
- K. V. Lobanov, M. V. Chicherov, N. V. Sharov (2021), **The 50th Anniversary of the Start of Drilling the Kola Superdeep Well**, *Arctic and North* 44. DOI: https://doi.org/10.37482/issn2221-2698.2021.44.267 — retrospective on drilling technology, thermal gradient, mineralization and scientific results.
- 2020 *Scientific Reports*, **Exceptional Concentrations of Gold Nanoparticles in 1.7 Ga Fluid Inclusions From the Kola Superdeep Borehole, Northwest Russia**: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-020-58020-8 — deep fluid inclusions and gold nanoparticle concentrations.
- Charlotte Wrigley (2023), **Going deep: Excavation, collaboration and imagination at the Kola Superdeep Borehole**, *Environment and Planning D*. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1177/02637758231185134 — history, site organization, post-Soviet afterlife and cultural reconstruction.

## Current ultradeep drilling

- Guangyou Zhu & Haiping Huang (2026), **Ultradeep drilling beyond 10 km revealing new insights into Earth systems and resources**, *Communications Earth & Environment* 7, 124. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s43247-026-03246-z — current global ultradeep context; SDTK-1 at 10,910 m, ~220°C and >145 MPa.
- State Council / Xinhua, 4 March 2024, **China's landmark deep-Earth borehole drilling exceeds 10,000 meters**: https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202403/04/content_WS65e573e2c6d0868f4e8e499a.html — official contemporaneous Shenditake-1 milestone.
- Tan et al. (2026), **World's fastest 10,000-m breakthrough: The XY-22 well redefines deep-earth access in the Tarim Basin**, *Deep Underground Science and Engineering*. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1002/dug2.70115 — >10 km drilling speed, HPHT system and preserved deep organic matter.
