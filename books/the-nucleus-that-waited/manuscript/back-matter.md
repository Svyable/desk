# Back Matter

## Evidence note

This book treats nuclear isomers as established nuclear physics, the late-1990s hafnium-178m2 triggering claims as published but not independently confirmed at the reported magnitude, DARPA interest as documented evidence of government investigation rather than efficacy, and thorium-229 laser spectroscopy as an experimentally distinct modern success. Where nonpublic DARPA results are alleged, the manuscript preserves the allegation without using it to overturn public replication evidence.

## Chronology

- **1970s–1990s** — Nuclear-isomer spectroscopy and gamma-ray-laser concepts develop across mainstream nuclear physics.
- **1990s** — Hafnium-178m2 draws attention because of its ~31-year half-life and high excitation energy.
- **1998–1999** — Carl Collins and collaborators report x-ray-induced enhancement of gamma emission from 178m2Hf.
- **2001** — LLNL/LANL/Argonne team reports no detectable triggering at the Advanced Photon Source and places limits far below the claimed effect.
- **Early 2000s** — DARPA investigates stimulated isomer energy release and production/triggering questions.
- **2000s** — Independent criticism, replication failures, theoretical objections and application reviews erode the case for practical hafnium energy release.
- **2010s** — Thorium-229 transition-energy measurements progressively narrow the optical search region.
- **2024** — Multiple teams report decisive thorium-229 laser-spectroscopy milestones, including direct resonant excitation and high-precision frequency measurement.
- **2024** — Frequency comparison between the 229mTh nuclear transition and a strontium atomic clock is reported.
- **2025** — Solid-state temperature sensitivity and improved fundamental-constant sensitivity analyses push the thorium system toward clock engineering and new-physics tests.

## What would change the book

A reproducible independent demonstration of large, low-energy-triggered decay enhancement in hafnium-178m2 would require rewriting the central historical judgment. So would public data showing net-gain stimulated release at rates relevant to an engineered system. Conversely, persistent inability to reproduce the thorium-229 optical transition across independent laboratories would undermine the book's contrast; the present record already contains multiple independent measurements, making that reversal unlikely.
