# Milky Sea — source notes

## Primary research spine

1. Miller, S. D., Haddock, S. H. D., Elvidge, C. D., & Lee, T. F. (2006/2007). “Twenty thousand leagues over the seas: the first satellite perspective on bioluminescent ‘milky seas’.” *International Journal of Remote Sensing* 27(23), 5131–5143. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1080/01431160600554298
   - First published satellite perspective on the 1995 event corresponding to the *Lima* report.
   - Use for OLS instrument limitations, event geometry, persistence and radiance-derived population estimate.

2. Miller, S. D. et al. (2021). “Honing in on bioluminescent milky seas from space.” *Scientific Reports* 11, 15443. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-021-94823-z
   - Open access.
   - Twelve VIIRS-detected events in 2012–2021 search.
   - Explicit exclusion logic: thermal explanation, clouds/airglow, daytime reflectance, persistence and current-consistent motion.
   - 2019 Java event is central narrative case.

3. Nealson, K. H. & Hastings, J. W. (2006). “Quorum Sensing on a Global Scale: Massive Numbers of Bioluminescent Bacteria Make Milky Seas.” *Applied and Environmental Microbiology* 72(4), 2295–2297. https://doi.org/10.1128/AEM.72.4.2295-2297.2006
   - Mechanistic commentary, not direct proof of the causal ecology.
   - Excellent source for the scale problem, luminous-bacteria logic, historical bucket reports, *Phaeocystis*/*Vibrio* hypotheses and predictions.

4. NASA Earth Observatory (2 Nov 2021), “Hunting Milky Seas by Satellite.” https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/hunting-milky-seas-by-satellite-149017/
   - Authoritative explanatory bridge and chronology.
   - Notes >200 historical mentions assembled by Miller; 100,000 km² scale for 2019 event; only rare direct sampling; emphasizes unresolved composition/ecology.

5. NOAA Institutional Repository record for Miller et al. 2021: https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/41844
   - Stable government repository metadata and DOI trail.
