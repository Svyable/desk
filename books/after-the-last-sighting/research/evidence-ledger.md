# Evidence ledger — After the Last Sighting

This ledger separates evidence state from narrative interest. Class 1 = verified/declassified/documented facts or strong direct evidence. Class 2 = credible but incomplete evidence or testimony. Class 3 = disputed allegations without independent confirmation. Class 4 = speculative synthesis/open conjecture.

## Class 1 — verified / documented / direct

- Living coelacanth specimen recovered in 1938; subsequent populations and observations documented. Smithsonian: https://ocean.si.edu/ocean-life/fish/coelacanth
- Megamouth shark first specimen became entangled with a U.S. Navy research vessel's sea anchor off Hawaii in 1976 and was subsequently described as a new shark. Smithsonian: https://ocean.si.edu/ocean-life/sharks-rays/original-megamouth
- Saola entered formal zoological knowledge after surveyors encountered distinctive skulls/horns in Vietnam in 1992; IUCN describes it as a major twentieth-century mammal discovery. https://iucn.org/content/saola-still-a-mystery-20-years-after-its-spectacular-debut
- First photographs of a living giant squid in natural deep habitat obtained in 2004; tissue from a severed tentacle supported identification. Smithsonian/Nature background: https://ocean.si.edu/ocean-life/invertebrates/giant-squid-baited-camera and https://www.nature.com/news/2005/050926/full/news050926-7.html
- Olinguito described in 2013 after museum, genetic, historical, and field work; specimens had been misidentified in collections and at least one individual had lived in zoos. Smithsonian: https://www.si.edu/stories/olinguito-discovery
- Attenborough's long-beaked echidna photographed by an Oxford-led expedition in 2023 after no scientific record since 1961. Oxford: https://www.ox.ac.uk/news/2023-11-10-found-last-bizarre-egg-laying-mammal-finally-rediscovered-after-60-years-1
- USFWS proposed in 2021 to delist ivory-billed woodpecker due to extinction after a 2019 review; in 2023 it finalized 21 other extinction delistings but continued reviewing the ivory-bill. https://www.fws.gov/press-release/2021-09/us-fish-and-wildlife-service-proposes-delisting-23-species-endangered-species and https://www.fws.gov/press-release/2023-10/21-species-delisted-endangered-species-act-due-extinction
- Solow 1993 formally analyzed extinction inference from sighting records. https://doi.org/10.2307/1940821
- Solow et al. 2012 applied a Bayesian uncertain-sighting model to ivory-bill records and reported a Bayes factor favoring extinction under that model. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1523-1739.2011.01743.x
- Solow & Beet 2014 showed the ivory-bill inference can change under plausible alternative models of certain/uncertain sighting processes. https://doi.org/10.1111/cobi.12309
- Darling, Jerde & Sepulveda 2021 distinguished sample-level false-positive eDNA detection from site-level false inference. https://doi.org/10.1002/edn3.194
- Sykes et al. 2014 sequenced 30 hair samples attributed to anomalous primates; most resolved to known mammals. https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2014.0161
- Edwards & Barnett 2015 challenged the interpretation of the surprising Himalayan bear sequences in Sykes et al.; this contradiction is retained rather than suppressed. https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2014.1712
- University of Otago-led Loch Ness eDNA project collected 250 samples and reported no eDNA evidence for giant reptile hypotheses while detecting abundant eel DNA. https://www.otago.ac.nz/news/newsroom/first-edna-study-of-loch-ness-points-to-something-fishy

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete

- 2004–2005 Arkansas ivory-bill sightings, brief video, and acoustic evidence judged persuasive by the Cornell-led team and USFWS at the time. This is class 1 evidence that the observations/material existed and institutions interpreted them as rediscovery, but class 2 for the proposition that the bird was definitively present because identification remains disputed. USFWS 2005: https://www.fws.gov/story/2005-04/once-thought-extinct-ivory-billed-woodpecker-rediscovered-arkansas
- Latta et al. 2023 Louisiana ivory-bill evidence package is peer-reviewed and serious enough to analyze, but imagery/interpretation remain disputed. https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ece3.10017
- Qualified post-1936 thylacine reports may justify targeted search, but testimony without diagnostic physical material does not establish population persistence.
- Local ecological knowledge can be highly predictive for search placement, as in saola/echidna contexts, but testimony should not be converted into a specimen claim.

## Class 3 — disputed allegations

- Contemporary survival of wild thylacines: no universally accepted post-1936 specimen, diagnostic genome, or clear repeatable imagery is established in the public record used here.
- Bigfoot/Sasquatch as an undiscovered North American great ape: many reports and alleged traces exist, but no authenticated body, diagnostic recent fossil, or independently verified genome establishes the species. The 2014 anomalous-primate hair survey mostly resolved submitted material to known mammals.
- Loch Ness plesiosaur or giant reptile: no independently confirmed physical evidence; 2019 broad eDNA results provide negative evidence against giant reptile hypotheses under the study's sampling assumptions.
- Claims that hidden animals deliberately avoid electronics, bury every dead individual, or possess extraordinary cognition are treated as ad hoc unless independently evidenced.

## Class 4 — speculative synthesis / open conjecture

- The book's "hiddenness budget": a heuristic that a population's body size, abundance, metabolism, mortality, movement, habitat, and interaction with sensors jointly determine expected evidence production.
- The proposed rule that a hidden-animal claim becomes scientifically mature when investigators preregister how null results reduce specific population hypotheses.
- A multi-modal last-sighting protocol combining camera networks, eDNA, acoustic sensors, witness scoring, specimen-chain standards, and prospective defeat conditions.
- The prediction that genuine rediscoveries generally become less dependent on testimony after first diagnostic evidence because repeatable biological channels open; weak claims remain trapped in the same ambiguous evidence class.

## Contradictions and disinformation / incentive audit

- Ivory-bill: 2005 institutional rediscovery claim versus later USFWS extinction proposal and ongoing expert disagreement. Government action is not used as a substitute for species identification in either direction.
- Ivory-bill statistics: Solow et al. 2012 versus Solow & Beet 2014 demonstrate model dependence. No single Bayes factor is presented as a metaphysical extinction verdict.
- Yeti hair: Sykes et al. 2014 reported two samples matching a short Pleistocene polar-bear sequence; follow-up criticism challenged that interpretation. The manuscript preserves both.
- eDNA: powerful positive/negative tool, but false-positive inference, transport, contamination, degradation, primer mismatch, and low shedding can all break naive conclusions.
- Camera traps: detection varies by species, placement, lure, trigger, and habitat. Camera absence is never treated as universal absence.
- Conservation incentives: charismatic lost species can attract resources and habitat protection; this creates both legitimate precautionary reasons to search and incentives to keep ambiguous cases culturally alive.
- Media/tourism incentives: famous cryptids create economic value from unresolved mystery. This is tracked as an incentive, not used as evidence that witnesses are dishonest.
- De-extinction companies have branding incentives around words such as "revived" or "de-extincted"; technical achievements are separated from species-identity claims.
