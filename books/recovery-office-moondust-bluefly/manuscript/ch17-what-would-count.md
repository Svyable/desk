# What Would Count

The easiest way to keep an extraordinary claim alive forever is to avoid saying what would settle it.

So let us say what would count.

The gold-standard evidence for a non-human crash-retrieval program would join provenance, material, analysis, and institutional records in one chain. No single element has to be perfect if the others independently constrain it, but the chain must resist ordinary explanations.

Start with provenance. A sample should connect to a specific recovery event through contemporaneous records or multiple independent witnesses with direct custodial roles. The chain should identify where the material was found, who controlled it, how it was packaged, where it moved, and when samples were subdivided. Gaps do not automatically destroy the case, but unexplained gaps lower confidence.

Then material analysis. Independent laboratories should characterize composition, isotopes, microstructure, manufacturing traces, and relevant physical properties using calibrated methods and controls. The interesting result is not “unusual.” It is a result that remains difficult to reconcile with terrestrial natural or industrial processes after experts from the relevant fields have tried.

Then engineering context. A piece of layered metal is weaker than a component whose structure, interfaces, and function cohere as part of a technological system. Manufacturing evidence matters. Tool marks, deposition patterns, lattice structure, joining methods, and contamination can distinguish engineered novelty from damaged aerospace debris.

Then institutional linkage. Documents, program records, budgets, security guides, contracts, or authenticated communications should connect the material to the alleged exploitation effort. A laboratory result and a program rumor floating separately do not become stronger by being placed on the same slide.

Then replication and adversarial access. Experts who do not share the original investigators’ expectations should be allowed to test portions of the claim. The point is not radical openness with sensitive national-security details. The point is preventing one closed group from becoming both source and validator.

What would count on the documentary side if physical material remained inaccessible?

Authenticated program records could be decisive if they were sufficiently specific and internally supported. An authorization memorandum saying “recover foreign technology” would not be enough. A detailed technical report describing a recovered object with measurements that cannot be explained by known systems, cross-referenced to custody records and independent program documentation, would be much stronger.

What about testimony?

Firsthand testimony from multiple people occupying non-overlapping roles can become powerful when their accounts converge on details not publicly available beforehand. A recovery operator, transport officer, materials scientist, security manager, and contracting official would each see different pieces. Their testimony should fit without being identical.

What would not count by itself?

A patent. Patents establish that someone filed a claim, not that the claimed device works.

A code name. Programs can have dramatic names and ordinary missions.

A classified refusal. Withholding establishes sensitivity, not content.

An unexplained sighting. A sighting can motivate investigation but does not establish a recovered artifact.

An unusual alloy without custody. Interesting, not dispositive.

A famous witness repeating what trusted colleagues told them. Worth recording, still secondhand.

A government denial. Also not dispositive.

The symmetry matters. We should not demand impossible proof from one side and accept atmosphere from the other.

The most useful standard is one that could embarrass our preferred answer.

For the exotic hypothesis, the embarrassment would be a complete Moon Dust-style chain ending in mundane identification. For the mundane hypothesis, the embarrassment would be authenticated, independently testable material whose properties and provenance survive every serious terrestrial explanation.

Either result would be progress.

The recovery office gives us the template because it already solved the boring part: how to take something from a field and turn it into an intelligence object.

What would count is evidence that survives the rest of the trip.
