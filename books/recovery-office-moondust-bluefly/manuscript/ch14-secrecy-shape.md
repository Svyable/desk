# Secrecy Has a Shape

“Classified” is often treated as a depth measurement.

It is not.

Classification controls access to information according to expected damage from disclosure. Special access controls can narrow distribution further. Sensitive compartmented information uses separate channels. Nuclear information has its own statutory regimes. Contractors can hold pieces of programs under security rules that prevent neighboring teams from seeing them. The result is not one basement beneath ordinary government. It is a map of rooms with different locks.

This matters because crash-retrieval stories frequently use secrecy as a universal solvent. Any missing evidence can be explained by deeper classification. Any contradiction between witnesses can be explained by compartmentation. Any official denial can be explained by lack of access or participation in concealment.

All three explanations can sometimes be true.

They cannot be allowed to explain everything automatically.

Real compartmentation produces patterned ignorance. People know some details and not others according to role. A pilot may know the aircraft but not the acquisition channel. A metallurgist may know the sample but not the event. A security officer may know who has access but not the underlying physics. A senior official may know that a program exists while receiving only sanitized technical conclusions.

These patterns can be tested against testimony.

A witness who claims direct knowledge should be able to describe the scope of that directness. What did clearance permit? What was observed personally? What was learned from program records? What was repeated by colleagues? Which statements are inferences from unusual security behavior?

The distinction becomes especially important when credentials are strong. Intelligence officers and military personnel can be excellent witnesses to what they were briefed on while remaining unable to verify whether the briefing itself was accurate. Governments contain rumors too.

Secrecy can make rumors harder to kill because ordinary correction channels are blocked. If someone hears that a restricted program studies “off-world material,” they may be unable to inspect the program directly. They also cannot discuss the claim widely enough to find the person who knows the phrase referred to meteorites, foreign spacecraft debris, or a cover story. Compartments preserve information and misinformation by the same mechanism: limiting comparison.

This is one way secrecy creates a false evidentiary ecology without any central disinformation plan.

The ecology becomes more distorted when genuine deception exists. Intelligence organizations use cover stories. Classified aircraft have been denied or concealed. Sources and methods are protected. Officials sometimes answer narrow questions literally while allowing broader false impressions to stand.

The public learns from these episodes, rationally, that denial is not proof.

The dangerous next step is to conclude that denial is evidence of the denied proposition.

That inversion makes secrecy unfalsifiable.

A better approach asks what shape the alleged secret would need. How many disciplines? How many facilities? What statutory authorities? Which contracting pathways? What kinds of clearances? How would samples move? Which oversight bodies should receive notice? Where could illegal concealment occur, and what records would that concealment itself generate?

This is not glamorous work. It is how a claim becomes investigable.

Moon Dust and Blue Fly help because they have shape. We can see diplomatic traffic, technical-intelligence destinations, operational language, and specific recovered objects. HAVE DOUGHNUT has shape. We can see aircraft, tests, facilities, and consequences. The historical programs were hidden, but not shapeless.

A modern retrieval allegation should eventually acquire similar edges.

If it never does, secrecy may be protecting the program.

Or secrecy may be protecting the story from disconfirmation.

Those possibilities have to remain separate until evidence joins them.
