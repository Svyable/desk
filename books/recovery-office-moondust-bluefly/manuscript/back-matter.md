# Back Matter

## A note on evidence

This manuscript uses four evidence classes throughout: verified/declassified/documented facts; credible but incomplete reporting or testimony; disputed allegations without independent confirmation; and speculative synthesis. A document can prove that an agency or witness made a claim without proving the underlying claim. A patent is evidence of filing, not efficacy. Classification is evidence of sensitivity, not exotic content.

## Core public sources

Primary and authoritative sources used for the first-pass research spine include the U.S. Government Accountability Office’s 1995 Roswell records search and MJ-12 assessment; National Archives Project Blue Book guidance; official Air Force and DIA histories of foreign materiel exploitation, HAVE DOUGHNUT/HAVE DRILL/HAVE FERRY, and NASIC lineage; the July 26, 2023 House UAP hearing and David Grusch’s submitted statement; and the Department of Defense/AARO 2024 historical report.

The Moon Dust/Blue Fly document trail is preserved in declassified State Department, USAF, and DIA material held in National Archives Record Group 59 and in FOIA-derived document collections. Because several public web hosts are secondary repositories rather than originating agencies, the research notes distinguish archival provenance from hosting provenance and mark page-level recovery as a next-pass task.

## Claim boundary

Nothing in this manuscript asserts that the United States has recovered non-human technology. It also does not assert that such recovery is impossible. The narrower thesis is historical and epistemic: real foreign-technology recovery infrastructure existed, which makes recovery logistics plausible while making it especially important not to use infrastructure as a substitute for proof of payload.
