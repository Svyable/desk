# Back Matter

## Chronology

**5 August 1963** — United States, Soviet Union and United Kingdom sign the Limited Test Ban Treaty, prohibiting nuclear explosions in the atmosphere, outer space and underwater for parties.

**1963** — First Vela Hotel satellites are launched as part of U.S. nuclear-test monitoring.

**1960s** — U.S. concern grows over Israel's Dimona nuclear complex. Inspection history later shows that U.S. teams did not discover the full underground reprocessing capability.

**1969** — Nixon administration debates Israel's emerging nuclear status. The subsequent U.S.-Israeli posture becomes associated with nuclear opacity rather than overt declaration.

**1974** — India conducts a nuclear explosion described as a peaceful nuclear explosion, intensifying international nonproliferation concerns.

**1977** — Foreign intelligence detects South African preparations at a Kalahari test site; international pressure follows and no test is conducted there.

**22 September 1979** — Vela 6911 records Event 747, a suspicious optical double flash over the far southern Atlantic/Indian Ocean region.

**10 October 1979** — State Department intelligence memorandum records conclusions from a CIA-sponsored scientific review by Harold Agnew, Richard Garwin and Stephen Lukasik: the signal is consistent with an atmospheric nuclear explosion, though anomalous sensor behavior requires further investigation.

**1–2 November 1979** — White House Nuclear Explosion Panel chaired by Jack Ruina meets to evaluate nuclear, natural and technical explanations.

**8–9 November 1979** — Department of Energy and White House reporting circulate information about an unusual Arecibo ionospheric disturbance considered a possible corroborative lead.

**December 1979** — Interagency Intelligence Memorandum *The 22 September 1979 Event* states that the nature and origin of the event cannot be determined with certainty and analyzes possible actors under the assumption of a nuclear explosion.

**27 February 1980** — President Jimmy Carter records in his diary that U.S. scientists are increasingly convinced the event was an Israeli nuclear explosion near southern Africa.

**1980** — The Ruina panel's non-nuclear interpretation, including a meteoroid-related spacecraft event, becomes the dominant semi-official public explanation.

**1986** — Mordechai Vanunu provides detailed information about Israel's Dimona program to the *Sunday Times*, greatly expanding public understanding of Israel's nuclear capability. This event is relevant to the broader opacity context, not evidence specific to Vela.

**1991** — South Africa accedes to the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty after dismantling its nuclear weapons.

**March 1993** — President F. W. de Klerk publicly discloses South Africa's former nuclear-weapons program.

**1993–1994** — IAEA verification work supports the conclusion that South Africa's declared weapons program has been terminated and dismantled.

**1996** — Comprehensive Nuclear-Test-Ban Treaty opens for signature, establishing the basis for the International Monitoring System.

**2017** — Wright and De Geer publish a modern reanalysis of the Vela double flash arguing that the meteoroid-reflection explanation is less physically compelling than often portrayed and that the optical record remains consistent with a nuclear explosion.

**2018** — De Geer and Wright publish an analysis of hydroacoustic and radionuclide evidence arguing that a low-yield nuclear test remains a plausible interpretation.

**2019** — National Security Archive publishes additional declassified Vela materials, including reporting that CIA had assessed the probability of a nuclear test at greater than ninety percent. A Wilson Center oral-history conference revisits the unresolved Israeli/South African attribution question.

**2026** — CTBTO's International Monitoring System is roughly ninety percent operational, with four complementary technologies providing a global verification architecture that did not exist in 1979.

## Notes on language

“Vela incident,” “Event 747,” and “Vela flash” refer to the detected September 22, 1979 event and its investigation, not to an established nuclear explosion.

“Israeli test,” “South African test,” and “joint Israeli–South African test” are attributed hypotheses unless a sentence explicitly discusses a source's belief.

“Opacity” refers to the historically documented policy environment surrounding Israel's nuclear status. It is not evidence that Israel participated in Event 747.

“Meteoroid explanation” refers to the Ruina panel's preferred non-nuclear class of explanation; no recovered impactor or uniquely identifying spacecraft evidence is publicly available.

## What would change the book

A full release of the Ruina panel's technical dataset and drafts could materially change the balance of the optical argument.

Raw hydroacoustic records and station metadata could change the cumulative event-level inference.

A detailed reconstruction of U.S. atmospheric sampling could make the missing-fallout evidence much stronger or weaker.

A high-quality Israeli or South African operational record naming a test platform, device, yield or bilateral command chain would radically change attribution.

A quantitative Vela-geometry simulation showing that plausible meteoroid impacts frequently reproduce Event 747-like double pulses would substantially strengthen the non-nuclear interpretation.

The book's claims should move if those records appear.
