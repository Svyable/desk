## Thesis

The 1979 Vela double flash is most useful not as a solved conspiracy or a debunked false alarm but as a rare, well-documented collision between treaty-verification physics and political epistemology: optical, ionospheric, hydroacoustic, radionuclide, intelligence, and archival evidence support different layers of inference, and the case shows why detecting an event, deciding it was nuclear, and attributing it to a state require separate standards.

## Evidence posture

**Declassified-primary-heavy / nuclear-monitoring-heavy / nuclear-event interpretation open and taken seriously / Israeli–South African attribution conservative / cover-up claims unproven.**

Research notes use four classes:

1. verified/declassified/documented facts;
2. credible but incomplete reporting or technical evidence;
3. disputed allegations without independent confirmation;
4. speculative synthesis/open conjecture.

The manuscript does not claim that Event 747 is proven to have been nuclear; that Israel or South Africa is proven to have conducted it; that a CIA assessment is physical proof; that President Carter's diary proves perpetrator identity; that Australian iodine-131 uniquely identifies a Vela-source test; or that the Ruina panel was deliberately falsified for political reasons.
