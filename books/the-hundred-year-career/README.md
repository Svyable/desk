*Work, Identity, and Reinvention in a Life That No Longer Has One Profession*

A hundred-year career is not a hundred years spent doing the same job. It is an economically active life long enough to contain several professions, repeated periods of learning, multiple peaks of productivity, changing forms of ownership, and identities that would once have belonged to several different people.

The working thesis is that the future will reward people less for choosing one profession correctly than for becoming unusually good at rebuilding themselves as technology, institutions, and the economics of intelligence change around them.

## Working thesis

The great career question of the twentieth century was what you wanted to be when you grew up. The great career question of a longer-lived, faster-changing century may be what you can become next.

Education therefore moves into adulthood. Occupational identity becomes more portable. Retirement becomes less binary. AI gives individuals institutional-scale leverage. And the most consequential inequality may increasingly be optionality: who can afford to leave, learn, experiment, own, and begin again.
