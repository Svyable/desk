# The New Inequality

This future will not automatically be fair.

The ability to reinvent yourself is itself a form of wealth.

Someone with savings can retrain.

Someone with strong relationships can switch industries.

Someone with good health can work longer.

Someone with ownership can survive periods of low income.

Someone with access to excellent AI tools can dramatically amplify their abilities.

Someone without these advantages may become trapped in declining work.

The defining inequality of a long-career society may therefore be less about who has a job and more about who possesses **optionality**.

Can you leave?

Can you learn?

Can you relocate?

Can you survive a year with lower income?

Can you start over?

Can you experiment?

Can you own part of what you create?

Policy will eventually have to recognize this.

Education finance, retirement accounts, unemployment systems, benefits, and taxation were designed around stable employment.

A hundred-year-career society needs institutions designed around repeated transitions.

The social safety net of the future may need to become a social trampoline.

Its purpose cannot merely be preventing people from falling.

It must help them bounce into something new.
