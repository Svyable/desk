# The Hundred-Year Career

*Work, Identity, and Reinvention in a Life That No Longer Has One Profession*

**Sven Hardy Benson**

---

For most of modern history, a career was imagined as a line.

You learned something when you were young. You became useful at it. You found an employer or profession. You accumulated experience. Your income rose. Eventually you retired.

The line might contain a few interruptions, promotions, layoffs, or changes of employer, but the basic assumption remained intact: the person who entered the workforce at twenty-five would be recognizably similar to the person leaving it forty years later.

That assumption is collapsing.

A child born today may live well into the next century. During that lifetime, industries will appear and disappear. Entire categories of intellectual work will become automated. New professions will emerge that cannot yet be named. Education acquired at twenty-two will expire long before the person who acquired it does.

The great career question of the twentieth century was:

**What do you want to be when you grow up?**

The great career question of the twenty-first will be:

**What will you become next?**

The future belongs less to people who choose correctly once than to people who can repeatedly rebuild themselves.

This changes almost everything.

Education can no longer be concentrated at the beginning of life.

Retirement can no longer be treated as the only alternative to employment.

A résumé can no longer be understood simply as evidence of continuity.

Age can no longer reliably predict professional seniority.

And identity can no longer be safely attached to a single occupation.

We are moving from the forty-year career toward the hundred-year career.

Not because everyone will work for one hundred years.

Because our economically active lives will increasingly contain several careers, repeated periods of learning, multiple peaks of productivity, different kinds of work, and identities that would once have belonged to several different people.

The future career will not be a ladder.

It will be a portfolio.
