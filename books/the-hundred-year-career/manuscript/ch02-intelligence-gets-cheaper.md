# Intelligence Gets Cheaper

Every economy is organized around scarcity.

When something becomes abundant, value moves elsewhere.

For centuries, literacy was scarce.

Then literacy spread.

Information remained scarce.

Then the internet made information abundant.

Expertise remained scarce.

Artificial intelligence is now beginning to make portions of expertise abundant as well.

A person who once needed an accountant, designer, researcher, programmer, translator, tutor, and junior analyst may increasingly possess software capable of performing parts of all six functions.

This does not necessarily mean that humans become economically useless.

It means that the source of human value moves.

When calculation became cheap, mathematicians did not disappear.

When photography became cheap, visual culture exploded.

When publishing became cheap, writing multiplied.

The same may happen to intelligence.

The important career question therefore becomes:

**What becomes valuable when competent cognitive output becomes cheap?**

Several answers appear immediately.

Judgment.

Taste.

Responsibility.

Trust.

Relationships.

Initiative.

Originality.

Physical presence.

Leadership.

The ability to define the problem rather than merely solve it.

These abilities have always mattered.

But they become disproportionately valuable when machines can execute routine intellectual tasks.

The most resilient future worker may therefore not be the person who knows the most.

It may be the person who knows what deserves doing.
