# Become Difficult to Finish

There is a familiar anxiety surrounding the future of work.

Will AI replace me?

The question is understandable.

It may also be too narrow.

Every technology replaces tasks.

Some replace occupations.

Few replace human ambition.

The more useful question is:

**What kind of person remains valuable across multiple technological eras?**

Probably not the person with the most stable expertise.

Probably the person with the greatest capacity for reinvention.

Curiosity becomes career insurance.

Relationships become career infrastructure.

Ownership becomes career resilience.

Judgment becomes career leverage.

Learning becomes a permanent activity.

Identity becomes portable.

The objective is no longer to find a career that lasts forever.

There may be no such thing.

The objective is to build a self that can survive the end of one.

A hundred-year life does not require a hundred-year plan.

It requires the opposite.

Enough direction to move.

Enough flexibility to change.

Enough confidence to become a beginner repeatedly.

Our grandparents could ask what they wanted to be.

Our children may ask a stranger question.

What are all the people I might still become?

That is not the death of the career.

It is its expansion.

The career of the future is not a road we discover.

It is a life we repeatedly redesign.
