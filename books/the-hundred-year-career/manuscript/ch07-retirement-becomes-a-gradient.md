# Retirement Becomes a Gradient

Retirement was designed around a binary distinction.

Working.

Not working.

But knowledge work already makes this division increasingly artificial.

A future sixty-eight-year-old might work two days a week, advise three companies, teach one course, run a small investment portfolio, and spend months each year traveling.

Are they retired?

The question becomes meaningless.

Retirement may fragment into periods.

A person works intensely for ten years.

Takes eighteen months away.

Returns in another field.

Works part-time.

Starts something.

Stops.

Teaches.

Returns again.

Instead of one retirement at the end of life, people may have several sabbatical periods throughout it.

This could be economically difficult.

Our pension systems, health systems, corporate structures, and cultural expectations were not designed for irregular careers.

But psychologically, it may be healthier.

The possibility of rest no longer requires waiting until sixty-five.

And meaningful contribution no longer has to stop there.
