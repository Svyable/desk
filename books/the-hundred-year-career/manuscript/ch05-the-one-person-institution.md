# The One-Person Institution

Technology historically made large organizations more powerful.

Factories required capital.

Mass media required distribution.

Software companies required engineering departments.

Professional services required teams of specialists.

Artificial intelligence may reverse part of that dynamic.

An individual could increasingly possess a virtual staff.

Research.

Legal drafting.

Design.

Programming.

Bookkeeping.

Marketing.

Analysis.

Customer support.

Scheduling.

Translation.

A future entrepreneur may not ask, “How many employees do I need?”

They may ask, “Which decisions must remain mine?”

This creates the possibility of the one-person institution: an individual whose productive capacity resembles that of a small twentieth-century firm.

The implications extend far beyond entrepreneurship.

Employees become more leveraged.

Researchers operate larger intellectual projects.

Artists build businesses around themselves.

Consultants become miniature firms.

The distinction between employment and enterprise blurs.

Career resilience may increasingly depend on possessing something outside employment.

An audience.

A reputation.

Intellectual property.

A business.

A professional network.

A body of work.

Capital.

A community.

A system that can generate opportunity without a single employer's permission.

The safest career may eventually be the one containing several independent sources of usefulness.
