# Education Moves Into Adulthood

Our education system is built around a strange assumption.

Humans need their most concentrated period of formal learning when they know the least about themselves.

Then, once they have accumulated decades of experience, responsibilities, curiosity, and context, structured education largely disappears.

The hundred-year career will invert this logic.

A person may receive major periods of education at twenty, thirty-five, fifty, sixty-five, and eighty.

Universities may increasingly serve people repeatedly rather than once.

Companies may become partial schools.

AI tutors may make continuous individualized education inexpensive.

Credentials may become smaller and more frequent.

Learning may cease to be preparation for work and become part of work itself.

Consider the difference.

Today, a forty-five-year-old returning to school can feel as though something has gone wrong.

In the future, not returning to structured learning may feel unusual.

We may eventually view the idea of receiving one concentrated dose of education before sixty years of employment the way we now view a doctor who stopped reading medical literature after graduating.

The half-life of professional knowledge is shrinking.

The solution is not learning everything.

It is becoming extremely good at learning again.
