# RESIDUAL

*How a Spacecraft Taught Us to Distrust New Physics*

Sven Hardy Benson

A spacecraft is a terrible place to discover a new law of nature.

Not because spacecraft are imprecise. The opposite. Deep-space navigation is among the most exquisite acts of measurement humans routinely perform. We throw a machine past a planet, let it disappear into a radio point, and infer its motion from a carrier wave received across billions of kilometres. The problem is that the machine doing the experiment is also part of the experiment. It warms. It cools. It leaks. It transmits. It expels propellant. Its plutonium decays. Its louvers move. Its electronics age. Its geometry determines where photons leave.

This book is about the Pioneer anomaly, but the anomaly is the specimen, not the thesis.

For years Pioneer 10 and 11 appeared to experience a small unexplained acceleration roughly toward the inner solar system. Serious physicists asked serious questions about new gravity. The residual was real enough to deserve that attention. It was also eventually accounted for, to the precision the data could support, by a force so ordinary it had been living inside the spacecraft all along: anisotropic thermal radiation.

That story matters far beyond Pioneer. Frontier science, UAP analysis, anomalous aerospace, classified propulsion claims, fifth-force searches and precision tests of gravity all share a dangerous temptation: subtract the apparatus conceptually before the apparatus has been subtracted quantitatively.

The rule of this book is therefore severe. An unexplained residual is evidence for an unexplained residual. It is not yet evidence for the explanation we most want.

## Evidence classes

**Class 1 — verified, documented, or experimentally established.** Mission telemetry, navigation data, spacecraft engineering records, peer-reviewed orbit determination, published thermal models, official mission histories, and independently reproducible physics belong here.

**Class 2 — credible but incomplete.** Serious interpretations that fit part of the data but lack decisive discrimination; recollections whose underlying records are incomplete; engineering reconstructions whose parameter uncertainty remains material.

**Class 3 — disputed or unconfirmed claims.** Assertions that the Pioneer anomaly established modified gravity, dark-matter drag, clock acceleration, a new fundamental force, or evidence of hidden propulsion belong here unless and until discriminating evidence survives conventional systematics.

**Class 4 — speculative synthesis.** The book's broader proposal—that anomaly science should carry an explicit `apparatus debt`, the amount of unclosed physical accounting between a residual and a claim about nature—is an interpretive framework. It is useful only if it predicts better investigations. It is not a discovered law.

Patents are evidence that somebody filed a patent, not that the device works. Program goals are evidence of intended work, not achieved capability. Testimony is testimony. Classification can explain missing public information; it cannot supply missing experimental discrimination.

The standard is not cynicism. Pioneer deserved investigation. The standard is to let an anomaly remain anomalous long enough to learn what produced it.
