# Gateway author-position audit

Bounded primary-source pass for the opening and later provenance chapter. This note does **not** revise the active manuscript in PR #2298.

## Why this matters

The opening currently presents Wayne M. McDonnell mainly as an Army intelligence officer trying to make Gateway legible. The originating memorandum adds a consequential fact: McDonnell says he had taken Gateway training himself. That changes the epistemic geometry of the document. It was not an outside laboratory validation, and it was not merely an intelligence officer summarizing somebody else's claims. It was an assessment written by a participant who was trying to construct an explanatory model for a system he had experienced.

That fact should eventually complicate, not discredit, the report. First-person exposure can improve descriptive understanding while also making explanatory independence harder to assume.

## What the memorandum itself establishes

On the first sheet McDonnell tells his commander that he was tasked to assess Gateway's mechanics and practicality. In the same paragraph he says that a physician **“took the Gateway training with me”** and that he initially relied on Itzhak Bentov's biomedical models before turning to quantum mechanics and theoretical physics to construct his explanation.

That sequence matters. The physics was not reported as the result of an experiment that independently established Gateway's mechanism. It was part of McDonnell's attempt to build a model capable of explaining the process.

On the second sheet McDonnell describes the paper as a guide for other USAINSCOM personnel who were **required to take Gateway training or work with Gateway materials**. The intended audience was therefore not simply a distant policy readership. At least some personnel in the organization were expected to encounter the training or its materials directly.

The signature block identifies McDonnell as LTC, MI, Commander, Det O.

## Claims this supports

1. **Participant-assessor, not independent validator.** McDonnell's own sentence establishes that he took the training. Later prose should make that visible before readers infer that the memorandum represents an external government test of Monroe's claims.
2. **Model construction is not mechanism measurement.** McDonnell explicitly describes moving through Bentov, quantum mechanics and theoretical physics because he needed an explanatory model. The report can be historically serious while its physics remains an argument rather than an experimentally demonstrated causal chain.
3. **Institutional interest was more concrete than mere file custody.** His reference to other USAINSCOM personnel being required to take the training or work with the materials is stronger evidence of organizational engagement than the fact that a copy later sat in the CIA archive. It still does not establish efficacy.
4. **Archive custody should be separated from authorship.** The released scan carries a CIA release stamp, while the document header and GovInfo catalog identify the Army/INSCOM provenance. “CIA report” is therefore misleading shorthand unless the text is explicitly discussing archival custody.

## Strongest counterargument / disconfirming interpretation

Participation does not make McDonnell's assessment worthless. An evaluator who has actually undergone a procedure may understand its instructions, phenomenology and practical demands better than an evaluator working only from brochures. Nor does participation prove he was credulous: the memorandum says he intended an assessment and expected its construction to face rational critique.

The fair inference is narrower: his experience improves the report as evidence for **what the training was like and how an informed Army participant tried to understand it**, while weakening any attempt to treat the document itself as independent experimental validation of the mechanism or cosmology.

Likewise, the sentence about personnel being required to take training establishes organizational use/engagement, not how many people participated, how they were selected, what outcomes were measured, whether the requirement was broad or narrow, or whether commanders endorsed McDonnell's conclusions. Those denominators still need primary reconstruction.

## Publication holds

- Do not call the document a “CIA study,” “CIA experiment,” or “CIA proof.”
- Do not infer original classification level from the 2003 CIA release stamp alone. The preserved scan visibly establishes release processing; classification history requires its own record.
- Do not turn “required to take the Gateway training or work with Gateway materials” into a claim that all USAINSCOM personnel were trained.
- Do not describe McDonnell as an independent scientific evaluator once his own participant status is known.
- Do not infer efficacy from organizational participation.

## Editorial opportunity

The eventual prose has a better human turn available than another abstract warning about archive authority: the officer assigned to explain Gateway had already sat through Gateway. That is not a gotcha. It is a more interesting question of standpoint. The same fact makes him a better witness to the procedure and a less independent witness to its explanatory frame.

A later manuscript pass can use that tension to sharpen the opening's central distinction: the government did more than store a strange paper, but it did less than independently validate the universe described inside it.
