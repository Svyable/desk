# Imenent Chapters 4–6 — authority, descendants, and speed controls

Purpose: prepare one coherent manuscript pass across Chapters 4–6 without widening the book into a generic agent-security survey. The shared question is whether formal human authority remains operationally usable as permissions compose, delegation propagates, and decision tempo accelerates.

## Chapter 4 — Permission Is Power

### Revision target

Make the connection between permission design and re-entry more mechanical. The chapter already explains least privilege, standing consent, authority composition, revocation lag, provenance, and transaction-specific authority. The next pass should show that a permission architecture determines not only what an agent may do during normal operation, but how much authority a human can actually recover during an abnormal one.

### Mechanisms to sharpen

**Revocation completeness.** A successful revoke is not merely disabling the visible agent identity. Recovery requires identifying descendant credentials, sessions, queued work, scheduled jobs, delegated scopes, external commitments, and durable state changes created under the original mandate. Formal revocation can therefore precede practical revocation.

**Independent authority map.** A human cannot reliably recover authority if the only current map of permissions and descendants must be generated by the agent layer being constrained. Preserve the distinction between machine-assisted explanation and independently inspectable records. Machine assistance can make the map legible; it should not be the sole source of truth for the map.

**Authority conservation under delegation.** Avoid implying a literal conservation law. The useful design test is narrower: decomposition should not silently multiply a principal's intended aggregate limits. Ten descendants each receiving the parent's full spending or action allowance can create more effective authority than the principal granted.

**Re-entry latency.** Permission systems should be judged partly by the time required to contract an agent's effective action envelope under stress. A theoretically revocable authority that takes hours to discover and unwind may be operationally irrevocable for a process whose consequential window is minutes.

### Counterpressure to preserve

Do not imply that broad permissions are always irresponsible. Emergency response, security containment, infrastructure recovery, and other time-sensitive work can be made safer by delegated action. Strong identity, short-lived credentials, scoped capabilities, durable provenance, and automated revocation can make machine authority easier to govern than informal human authority.

### Boundary

Do not turn the chapter into a standards catalog. NIST identity/authorization work and established delegation concepts support the infrastructure claim; they do not by themselves establish the book's re-entry thesis.

## Chapter 5 — Delegation Has Descendants

### Revision target

Push from individual subdelegation risk to the recoverability of a changing authority graph. The key question is not whether agents can spawn subagents. It is whether the principal can still discover, constrain, and assume the consequential work after the original mandate has produced descendants across systems and organizations.

### Mechanisms to sharpen

**Shared limits across a family tree.** Local compliance can produce aggregate violation. Spending, transaction, rate, exposure, and change budgets may need to bind a mandate across descendants rather than reset at each node.

**Commitment discovery.** Re-entry requires a ledger of obligations as well as a list of active identities. Orders, reservations, contracts, disclosures, queued jobs, shipments, recurring payments, and external promises can survive revocation. Some can be cancelled; some can only be compensated for.

**Cross-organizational propagation.** Internal revocation is easier than revocation after a mandate has crossed into suppliers, marketplaces, payment systems, or other principals' agents. The manuscript should distinguish technical authority from obligations that a counterparty may reasonably treat as valid.

**Topology versus state.** Knowing that a child agent existed is not enough. Recovery also requires knowing what it changed, what depends on those changes, and which later actors inherited or relied on the result.

**Bounded inheritance.** Objective inheritance and authority inheritance should remain separate. A descendant can receive the task context needed to solve a subproblem without automatically inheriting every capability of its parent.

### Counterpressure to preserve

Interoperability and machine-readable delegation can improve control. Standardized provenance, scoped credentials, shared-budget semantics, revocation propagation, and durable commitment records can make authority chains more visible than today's human subcontracting chains. The chapter should argue for governable delegation, not isolation.

### Falsification pressure

If consequential deployments converge on shallow delegation, non-transitive permissions, short-lived credentials, strong aggregate limits, and reliable descendant cleanup, the chapter's escalation mechanism weakens materially.

## Chapter 6 — The Speed Gap

### Revision target

Make the chapter's speed argument explicitly conditional on intervention windows. Machine speed is not itself loss of control. The relevant failure occurs when the time required for humans to understand, decide, coordinate, and act exceeds the time before consequential state becomes difficult or impossible to reverse.

### Mechanisms to sharpen

**Intervention-window test.** Compare human re-entry latency with the system's consequence horizon. A market, attack, deployment, physical process, or contractual chain can have very different windows. Avoid one universal threshold.

**Graceful deceleration.** Treat rate limits, freeze points, read-only modes, transaction staging, safe states, and pauses under uncertainty as re-entry infrastructure. The goal is not to make humans machine-fast; it is to make selected consequential processes slow enough for human judgment to become causally relevant again.

**Environmental tempo.** Preserve the distinction between internal and environmental re-entry. A company may retain a manual mode and still find it unusable if counterparties, customers, attackers, or markets operate at a tempo the manual organization cannot meet.

**Independent observability under time pressure.** A summary generated by the operating agent can be useful, but a human veto is weaker when there is no timely route to underlying evidence or an independent view. The problem is not machine summarization; it is epistemic dependence during a compressed decision window.

**Governance lag.** Technical capability can change faster than staffing, training, audit practice, insurance, procurement controls, and incident doctrine. Stable control primitives—identity, scope, evidence, reversibility, criticality, escalation—can let institutional learning survive model turnover.

### Counterpressure to preserve

Automation can widen effective intervention windows by detecting problems earlier, simulating outcomes, executing safe containment immediately, and presenting state more clearly. Machine-speed defense can be essential against machine-speed failure or attack. The thesis is therefore not that human-speed execution should dominate; it is that systems should retain a path for human authority to become effective at the appropriate abstraction level.

### Evidence discipline

METR task horizons support the claim that useful autonomous task length is changing on measured technical task suites. They are not direct measures of safe real-world operating duration and should not be converted into a countdown to general autonomous control.

## Cross-chapter continuity

The sequence should advance rather than repeat:

- Chapter 4: authority becomes consequential through permissions and must remain contractible.
- Chapter 5: authority propagates through descendants and must remain discoverable across the resulting graph and commitments.
- Chapter 6: even discoverable, revocable authority may cease to be practically recoverable when consequence moves faster than re-entry.

The shared re-entry test for this band is therefore: can accountable humans identify the relevant authority, stop its propagation, discover the obligations it created, obtain an independent enough view of state, and become causally effective before the consequence window closes?

## Prose guardrails for the manuscript pass

Keep the existing calm, concrete voice. Do not add another named framework unless it genuinely compresses recurring material. Avoid turning the control ideas above into checklist prose inside the chapters. Prefer one or two concrete consequences per mechanism and let the re-entry argument emerge from them. Preserve uncertainty and the strongest design countercases. Do not invent incidents, quotations, or precise performance claims.
