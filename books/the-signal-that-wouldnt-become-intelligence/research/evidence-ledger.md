# Evidence ledger

## Class 1 — verified / declassified / documented

- CIA publicly acknowledges U.S. intelligence research into remote viewing and describes the later transfer to DIA and 1995 independent evaluation. https://www.cia.gov/stories/story/ask-molly-did-cia-really-study-psychic-powers/
- Declassified GRILL FLAME / INSCOM session reports document operational tasking, including sessions connected to the U.S. Embassy hostage crisis in Tehran. Example: https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp96-00788r000800760001-1
- Those records explicitly describe viewer impressions as raw intelligence data requiring interpretation by the requester; this proves institutional handling, not accuracy.
- Program files also preserve poor outcomes. A 1978 training report states there was “absolutely no target correlation”; a 1982 training report records “very little correlation.” https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp96-00788r000900390001-1 and https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp96-00788r001400250001-0
- The 1995 evaluation generated a real expert disagreement over experimental evidence and a much less favorable judgment about operational utility. Obtain and page-index the complete AIR report before detailed treatment.

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete

- Participant and investigator descriptions of individual operational successes where contemporaneous tasking, target pool, full transcript, scoring, feedback timing, and analyst use are not all available.
- Claims about which results persuaded particular sponsors to continue funding, pending memoranda and budget/briefing records.
- Soviet/Russian program descriptions preserved through U.S. intelligence translations or later participant reporting, pending original-language provenance and scientific documentation.

## Class 3 — disputed / not independently established

- Any claim that STARGATE or predecessor programs demonstrated a dependable operational psychic spying capability.
- Famous “hits” reconstructed only after target disclosure or from participant memoir.
- Claims that Soviet psychotronic research produced operational mind-control or comparable capabilities.
- Claims that program termination proves suppression of a successful capability.
- Claims that classification status establishes scientific validity.

## Class 4 — synthesis / conjecture

- A weak anomalous effect, if real, may fail to cross the engineering threshold required for intelligence use.
- The persistence of the program may be explained by a mixture of anomalous-looking results, low investigation cost, Soviet-threat mirroring, sponsor incentives, secrecy, and asymmetric strategic upside rather than any single cause.
- Remote viewing is a useful case study in the difference between statistical evidence and instrumentality.

# Contradiction audit

| Claim | Counterweight |
|---|---|
| Government funding implies officials saw something compelling. | Intelligence services rationally investigate low-probability/high-impact adversary capabilities; funding alone proves little. |
| Laboratory statistics establish remote viewing. | Methodological independence, judging, sensory leakage, selective reporting, replication, effect size and prospective scoring remain separate burdens. |
| Operational anecdotes prove utility. | Utility requires full tasking and miss records, novelty, specificity, timeliness and evidence that a decision improved. |
| Cancellation proves failure. | Cancellation establishes an institutional judgment, not necessarily the nonexistence of every experimental anomaly. |
| A statistically non-chance effect would imply extraordinary cognition. | Artifacts, protocol weaknesses, optional analysis and human judging must be exhausted first. |

# Incentive audit

- **Program advocates:** funding, scientific reputation, institutional survival, genuine conviction.
- **Intelligence sponsors:** cheap asymmetric collection upside, adversary fear, bureaucratic ownership, career risk if a dismissed capability later proves real.
- **Skeptics/evaluators:** methodological rigor, reputational incentives, possible prior commitments.
- **Participants:** memory compression, selection of vivid successes, identity and later commercial incentives.
- **Agencies after declassification:** simplify complicated histories into defensible institutional narratives.
- **Popular media:** spectacular successes and spectacular debunkings both outperform ambiguous effect sizes.

# Defeat condition

The central thesis weakens substantially if a prospective, well-controlled operational record demonstrates reliable, specific, novel remote-viewing intelligence that repeatedly changed decisions at useful rates. It also weakens from the opposite direction if the best experimental record collapses under protocol, leakage, scoring or replication audit so completely that no residual anomaly needs explaining. The book must be willing to end in either place.
