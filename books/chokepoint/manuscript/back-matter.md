## About the Book

*Chokepoint* is a book about a distinction that mineral geopolitics makes easy to blur: the difference between where a resource is found and where its leverage actually sits.

These materials are abundant enough in the crust that geology alone rarely explains the leverage. The chokepoint usually appears later: in workable ore bodies, processing capacity, qualified chemical products, infrastructure, permits, and the years of capital needed to connect them. That is why a mine in the Andes, a smelter in Sulawesi, or a separation plant in Inner Mongolia can matter more than a headline reserve total. *Chokepoint* follows those bottlenecks without pretending they amount to a new OPEC or that markets will dissolve them on schedule.

## Notes and Sources

The manuscript was researched chapter by chapter using a source ledger maintained alongside the book.

The sourcing hierarchy is deliberate. USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries, IEA reports, company filings, government press releases and regulatory documents, and original academic research are preferred for claims they can answer directly. Contemporaneous reporting is used where the event itself is the subject. Historical claims are supported by historical records and modern scholarship rather than by repeated but unverified anecdotes — including, where a popular claim's sourcing turned out to be thin, saying so directly rather than repeating it uncritically.

Mineral prices, export-control regimes, and company disclosures are snapshots, not permanent facts. The source ledger records dates so a reader can distinguish an observed result from a forecast, an announced project from completed capacity, and a policy target from a policy outcome.

Where evidence cuts both ways, the manuscript keeps the counterevidence. The goal is not to win an argument by removing inconvenient facts. It is to make the argument strong enough to survive them.

The research files accompanying this manuscript contain the chapter-level source ledger with source titles, dates, URLs, claim summaries, and caveats. Those materials are the canonical source record for the draft and can be converted into publication-format endnotes during final production.
