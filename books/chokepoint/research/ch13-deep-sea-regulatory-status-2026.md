# Chapter 13 research note — deep-sea mining regulatory status, September 2026

## Why this belongs in the chapter

Chapter 13 is strongest when it distinguishes a politically accelerated permitting process from an operating commercial mine. That distinction is unusually easy to lose in headline language because, in 2026, the U.S. process moved quickly while the multilateral International Seabed Authority process remained unfinished.

The durable claim is not that deep-sea mining has been "approved" or "blocked." It is that the route from resource to commercial supply is itself a chokepoint made of jurisdiction, environmental review, public process, technical standards, and legitimacy.

## Primary sources checked

### NOAA — current U.S. DSHMRA status

NOAA's current deep-seabed-mining page states that the United States is not a party to the Law of the Sea Convention and that, under the Deep Seabed Hard Mineral Resources Act (DSHMRA), the United States may issue exploration licenses and commercial recovery permits to U.S. companies for areas beyond national jurisdiction if statutory and regulatory requirements are met.

As of September 2026, NOAA's own permit table lists existing DSHMRA commercial recovery permits as **N/A**.

NOAA also lists The Metals Company USA's consolidated application for an exploration license and commercial recovery permit. The Federal Register notice was posted **August 19, 2026**. The public-comment period runs **August 19 through October 19, 2026**, and NOAA lists a virtual public hearing for **October 13, 2026**.

Source: NOAA, "Deep Seabed Hard Minerals Mining," current page accessed September 2026.
https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/deep-seabed-mineral-resources/deep-seabed-mining/

### NOAA — January 21, 2026 final rule

NOAA's January 21, 2026 final rule established a consolidated application and review process under which a qualified applicant can seek an exploration license and commercial recovery permit through one application rather than two sequential applications.

The consolidation changes process, not the substantive existence of the two authorizations. NOAA's compliance guide says the other DSHMRA exploration and commercial-recovery regulations still apply, public comment and at least one public hearing remain required, and the Administrator may still issue separate license and permit proposals, conditions, and final authorizations.

### International Seabed Authority — Mining Code status

On March 31, 2026, the International Seabed Authority reported that its Council had discussed **29 of 32 outstanding issues** in the draft exploitation regulations during Part I of its 31st Session and had reached agreement in principle on several, including an Equalisation Mechanism and a Compliance Committee.

The ISA's current media guidance still describes completion of the Mining Code as a prerequisite, within the multilateral framework, for future commercial activities in the Area.

## Strongest counterargument / boundary

The U.S. route should not be described as fictitious merely because no commercial permit has yet issued. NOAA has materially accelerated the process, is reviewing real applications, and has already advanced some exploration applications through certification and environmental-review steps. That is genuine regulatory movement.

The opposite overstatement is equally misleading: an application, a certification step, or an environmental-review notice is not a commercial recovery permit. As of September 2026, NOAA itself lists no existing DSHMRA commercial recovery permits.

The legal disagreement also deserves care. NOAA describes a domestic statutory route for U.S. companies because the United States is not a party to the Law of the Sea Convention. The ISA maintains that commercial activity in the Area should proceed through the multilateral regime and after completion of the Mining Code. The chapter should describe the competing legal and institutional routes rather than resolving that dispute by assertion.

## Editorial use

The current manuscript's line — "The application is real. The permit is not." — is well supported and should be preserved.

If Chapter 13 receives a later factual update, the most useful added specificity would be the **August 19, 2026** notice date and the **October 19, 2026** comment deadline. Those facts make the difference between regulatory motion and commercial authorization visible without adding legalistic clutter.

The deeper argument is stronger than either camp's headline: deep-sea minerals may diversify geology while creating new chokepoints in permission, environmental review, ships, processing, standards, and legitimacy. Regulation here is not an administrative footnote to supply. It is part of supply.
