## Chapter 14 — Friend-Shoring and the New Alliances

### Primary / authoritative sources checked

1. **IRS, Internal Revenue Bulletin 2023-16 / Section 30D critical-minerals guidance.** The statutory schedule used in the chapter is 40% for vehicles placed in service after April 17, 2023 and before 2024; 50% in 2024; 60% in 2025; 70% in 2026; and 80% after 2026. Later legislation changed the consumer-credit landscape, so the chapter deliberately describes this as the original design rather than implying the credit remains unchanged in September 2026.
   - https://www.irs.gov/irb/2023-16_IRB

2. **European Commission, Critical Raw Materials Act strategic projects.** The Commission approved 47 EU strategic projects on March 25, 2025 and 13 non-EU projects on June 4, 2025. The second application cut-off closed January 15, 2026; the Commission reported more than 160 applications. Strategic-project status supports permitting attention and financing guidance but is not equivalent to an operating project.
   - https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/sectors/raw-materials/areas-specific-interest/critical-raw-materials/strategic-projects-under-crma_en

3. **European Commission, Raw Materials Mechanism, April 2026.** The first diversification round aggregates buyer demand and connects buyers with suppliers, financial institutions, storage providers, and strategic projects. It covers all 17 strategic raw materials, with emphasis on rare earths, battery materials, and defense inputs.
   - https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/news/commission-launches-platform-aggregate-demand-raw-materials-and-boost-diversification-2026-04-13_en

4. **U.S. Department of Energy, 2026 critical-minerals selections.** DOE announced $134 million for two rare-earth recovery/refining projects on June 2, $162 million for nine industrial-feedstock recovery projects on August 18, and $500 million for seven critical-mineral processing/battery/recycling projects on August 20. These announcements support the chapter's claim that policy increasingly spans multiple supply-chain stages rather than mine development alone.

   - https://www.energy.gov/cmei/articles/does-office-critical-minerals-and-energy-innovation-announces-162-million-accelerate

### Falsification / counterevidence

Friend-shoring should not be presented as empty diplomacy. The EU has designated real projects, DOE is funding real processing and recovery capacity, and demand aggregation can address a genuine bankability problem. The counterweight is execution: designations, grants, and agreements do not guarantee commissioning, qualification, competitive operating cost, or survival through a commodity-price downturn. The chapter therefore treats policy as scaffolding rather than completed diversification.

## Chapter 15 — China's Long Game

Chapter 15 draws primarily on the existing `ch08-15-refining-monopoly-china-strategy.md` research brief, which contains the detailed Mountain Pass, Lynas, Baotou, Chinese consolidation, processing-share, and MP Materials sourcing trail.

### Verification boundaries preserved

- The familiar Deng Xiaoping line comparing Middle Eastern oil with Chinese rare earths is treated as a widely repeated paraphrase whose exact polished English wording is difficult to tie to a clean contemporaneous transcript. The chapter does not place the polished English sentence in quotation marks as a verified verbatim Deng quotation.
- China's advantage is described as an ecosystem of mining, separation, refining, magnets, downstream manufacturing, policy, and accumulated technical capability rather than being attributed to one policy or one leader.
- Environmental tolerance is included as part of the historical explanation but not as a China-only pathology; Mountain Pass and Lynas are retained as counterexamples showing that rare-earth separation creates waste and political conflict outside China as well.
- Exact Chinese processing shares vary with year, stage, and whether a source measures capacity or output. The chapter therefore avoids false precision and states only the robust relationship: China's share of rare-earth processing is even more concentrated than its already dominant mining share.
- Consolidation and industrial policy are presented with counterevidence: they can improve governance and technical investment while also reducing competition, preserving weak investments, generating overcapacity, and encouraging diversification abroad.

### Additional current context

IEA's 2025–26 critical-minerals work continues to identify high concentration in critical-mineral refining and processing as a major supply-chain vulnerability. This is consistent with the chapter's emphasis on the middle of the chain rather than reserves alone.

The handoff to Chapter 16 is also empirically grounded: IEA's Global EV Outlook 2025 reported that LFP batteries were nearly half of the global EV battery market in 2024, demonstrating that chemistry substitution can materially change cobalt and nickel demand exposure rather than remaining a laboratory possibility.
- https://www.iea.org/reports/global-ev-outlook-2025/electric-vehicle-batteries
