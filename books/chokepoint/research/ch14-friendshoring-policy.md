## Key facts and sources

### IRA critical minerals / FEOC rules

1. **The IRA's Section 30D critical-minerals sourcing threshold is a phased annual percentage**: 40% for vehicles placed in service before Jan 1, 2024; 50% for 2024; 60% for 2025; 70% for 2026; 80% for vehicles placed in service after Dec 31, 2026. The battery-components threshold runs on a parallel but distinct schedule (50% in 2023, rising ~10 points/year toward 100%). Source: search-aggregated summary of the U.S. Treasury/IRS final rule, "Clean Vehicle Credits Under Sections 25E and 30D... Foreign Entities of Concern," Federal Register, May 6, 2024, https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/05/06/2024-09094/clean-vehicle-credits-under-sections-25e-and-30d-transfer-of-credits-critical-minerals-and-battery — **primary source, but I could not directly fetch it; verify exact percentages against the Federal Register text before quoting.** Used for: explaining the mechanics of the $7,500 credit split ($3,750 for critical minerals, $3,750 for battery components).

2. **"Foreign entity of concern" (FEOC) battery-component ban took effect January 1, 2024; the critical-minerals FEOC ban took effect January 1, 2025.** From 2025 onward, a vehicle is ineligible for any credit if any applicable critical mineral was extracted, processed, or recycled by an FEOC (a category covering China, Russia, Iran, North Korea and entities they control 25%+). Source: Congressional Research Service, "Foreign Entity of Concern Requirements in the Section 30D Clean Vehicle Credit," IN12322, https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IN12322 (primary/CRS — could not directly fetch, verify). Used for: dating the "hard" FEOC cutoff that made Chinese-linked battery supply chains ineligible.

3. **DOE finalized its interpretive guidance defining "foreign entity of concern"** for critical-minerals purposes. Source: U.S. Department of Energy, "DOE Releases Final Interpretive Guidance on the Definition of Foreign Entity of Concern," energy.gov, https://www.energy.gov/articles/doe-releases-final-interpretive-guidance-definition-foreign-entity-concern (primary — could not fetch directly; get exact date and definition text before using).

4. **Sen. Joe Manchin publicly criticized the Treasury/IRS FEOC guidance as containing "workarounds" that would let Chinese critical-mineral supply chains continue qualifying** (his objection was to leniency in how "FEOC-controlled" entities were defined, e.g., licensing arrangements). Source: aggregated reporting cited via search results referencing Treasury press release jy1379 and related coverage (The Hill, "What to know about 2024 changes to the electric vehicle tax credit," https://thehill.com/policy/energy-environment/4379404-what-to-know-electric-vehicle-tax-credit-2024-changes/). Used for: narrative material — a senator-vs-Treasury dispute over how porous the "de-China-ified" supply chain rules really are. **Needs direct verification of quote and date.**

5. **The U.S.-Japan Critical Minerals Agreement (CMA) was signed March 28, 2023, and entered into force immediately**, covering five minerals (lithium, graphite, manganese, cobalt, nickel) so that minerals processed in Japan could count toward IRA sourcing requirements despite Japan having no comprehensive US free-trade agreement. Source: Congressional Research Service, "U.S.-Japan Critical Minerals Agreement," IF12517/R48676, https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF12517 and https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R48676 (primary CRS product — verify directly). Used for: illustrating how allied governments negotiated narrow "mini-FTAs" specifically to unlock IRA eligibility — a template later eyed by the EU and UK.

### Minerals Security Partnership (MSP)

6. **The MSP was officially launched/announced in June 2022 in Toronto, Canada**, on the sidelines of the PDAC mining convention, led by the U.S. State Department. Source: search-aggregated from U.S. Dept. of State, "Minerals Security Partnership," https://2021-2025.state.gov/minerals-security-partnership/ (primary, State Dept archived page — could not fetch directly, verify launch date/venue).

7. **MSP membership grew from an initial core group to 15 partner countries plus the EU by 2024**: Australia, Canada, Estonia (joined March 3, 2024), Finland, France, Germany, India (joined June 2023), Italy (joined July 2023), Japan, Norway, South Korea, Sweden, the UK, the US, and the European Commission. Source: aggregated from European Commission DG Trade, "EU and US welcome new members to Minerals Security Partnership," Sept 27, 2024, https://policy.trade.ec.europa.eu/news/eu-and-us-welcome-new-members-minerals-security-partnership-2024-09-27_en (primary EU source — verify directly). Used for: showing MSP's expansion trajectory as a signal of Western alignment.

8. **By September 2024, the MSP had endorsed/publicly supported 32 projects** spanning Africa (13 sites), the Americas (8 sites), Asia-Pacific (6 sites), and Europe (5 sites), across upstream mining, midstream processing, and downstream recycling. Source: aggregated search summary referencing MSP official statements and coverage (e.g., American Ceramic Society, "Video: Minerals Security Partnership welcomes new members and initiatives," https://ceramics.org/ceramic-tech-today/video-minerals-security-partnership-welcomes-new-members-and-initiatives/). Used for: quantifying MSP's project pipeline — note this is a count of *endorsed* projects, not completed ones, an important caveat for the "too slow" critique.

9. **The MSP Finance Network was launched September 23, 2024, on the sidelines of the UN General Assembly**, bringing together development finance institutions and export credit agencies of MSP members to co-finance projects. Source: U.S. Department of State, "Joint Statement on Establishment of the Minerals Security Partnership Finance Network," https://2021-2025.state.gov/joint-statement-on-establishment-of-the-minerals-security-partnership-finance-network/ (primary — verify directly).

10. **KoBold Metals' Mingomba copper project in Zambia** (backed by Bill Gates, Jeff Bezos, and Sam Altman among others) is cited as an MSP-linked milestone project; KoBold broke ground on the mine with a planned $2.3–2.5 billion investment, targeting 300,000+ tonnes/year copper production in the early 2030s, described as the largest private investment in Zambian history and part of President Hakainde Hichilema's plan to triple national copper output. Sources: Mining Indaba, "KoBold Metals breaks ground on $2.3 billion Mingomba mine," https://miningindaba.com/articles/kobold-metals-breaks-ground-on-23-billion-min; KoBold Metals, "Breaking Ground at Mingomba: A New Chapter for the Copperbelt," May 21, 2026, https://koboldmetals.com/2026/05/21/breaking-ground-at-mingomba-a-new-chapter-for-the-copperbelt/. Used for: strong narrative anecdote — Silicon Valley billionaires + AI-driven exploration + African copper + MSP diplomatic backing, all in one story. Note: verify the *specific* MSP linkage (KoBold/Mingomba is widely cited as an MSP "milestone project" in secondary sources, but confirm this in an MSP official document, not just news coverage).

### EU Critical Raw Materials Act

11. **The CRMA entered into force in May 2024** and sets non-binding 2030 benchmarks: at least 10% of EU annual consumption from domestic extraction, at least 40% from domestic processing/refining, and at least 25% from recycling, plus a cap that no single third country should supply more than 65% of the EU's annual consumption of any strategic raw material at any processing stage. Source: aggregated from Council of the EU, "Critical raw materials act," consilium.europa.eu, https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/infographics/critical-raw-materials/, and European Commission single-market-economy pages (primary EU sources — could not fetch directly; verify exact benchmark wording and the 65%-single-country cap, which is a distinct and important provision).

12. **On March 25, 2025, the European Commission designated the first batch of 47 Strategic Projects** under the CRMA across 13 EU member states, covering extraction (25 projects), processing (24), recycling (10), and substitution (2) — note these sum to more than 47 because some projects span multiple categories; total estimated capital investment ~€22.5 billion; lithium (22 projects), nickel (12), and cobalt (10) were the most represented minerals. Source: White & Case LLP, "Strategic Projects for the EU: list of 47 Strategic Projects announced," https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/strategic-projects-eu-list-47-strategic-projects-announced (law-firm secondary source summarizing the EC announcement — reliable but verify against the European Commission's own press release). Used for: giving the book a concrete, named first cohort of "picked winners" (e.g., Barroso lithium project in Portugal, run by Savannah Lithium).

13. **By 2025, the Commission had approved a cumulative 60 strategic projects under the CRMA, spanning 13 EU member states and 13 third (non-EU) countries, covering 14 of the 17 officially designated strategic raw materials** across the full value chain. Source: aggregated search summary citing Ecomondo, "Critical raw materials: EU backs 60 strategic projects," https://www.ecomondo.com/en/news-detail/critical-raw-materials-eu-backs-60-strategic-projects?newsId=4191515 (secondary industry-news source — verify against EC official list). Used for: showing growth from 47 (March 2025) to 60 (later 2025) projects, and the extension outside the EU's own borders.

14. **The CRMA's designated Strategic Projects receive expedited permitting timeframes: 24 months for extraction permits and 12 months for processing/recycling permits**, plus streamlined access to EU financing instruments. Source: aggregated search summary (multiple sources referencing EU Regulation 2024/1252 text). Used for: explaining the CRMA's core regulatory lever (permitting speed), which is also its main point of criticism (see counterevidence).

15. **Criticism of the CRMA: it sets targets and streamlines permitting but provides comparatively little dedicated EU funding.** The complementary "RESourceEU" Action Plan (2025) aims to mobilize up to €3 billion alongside European Investment Bank support, which critics say is a fraction of what individual strategic projects (often requiring billions each) need. Source: aggregated from ODI, "Europe's critical raw materials bet: a reality check on the EU's ambitions," https://odi.org/en/insights/europes-critical-raw-materials-bet-a-reality-check-on-the-eus-ambitions/ (reputable think-tank analysis). Used for: counterevidence — policy ambition vs. financing gap.

16. **Permitting delays for new EU mining projects average 5-10 years in practice, undercutting the CRMA's stated goal of a 24-month permitting window for extraction**, and the second batch of Strategic Project designations (expected mid-2025) was pushed back into autumn amid reports of delay. Source: The Metalnomist, "EU CRMA Strategic Projects Delay Raises Questions Over Critical Minerals Execution," Aug 2026, https://www.metalnomist.com/2026/08/eu-crma-strategic-projects-delay-raises.html (trade-press secondary source — verify). Used for: counterevidence — implementation gap between legal targets and ground reality (e.g., permitting fights around Portugal's Barroso project cited as a specific case of social opposition and financing complexity).

### US-Australia / Quad / MP Materials / Lynas

17. **On October 20, 2025, President Trump and Australian PM Anthony Albanese signed the U.S.-Australia Critical Minerals Framework**, after roughly five months of negotiation, targeting an ~$8.5 billion pipeline of priority critical-minerals and rare-earth projects, with each government committing to provide at least $1 billion of investment within six months. Cited project examples: the NT (Northern Territory) Nolans neodymium project (used in night-vision devices and guided missiles), plus Victoria/Queensland/NSW titanium and zircon projects. Sources: CNBC, "U.S. and Australia sign critical minerals agreement with $8.5 billion project pipeline," Oct 20, 2025, https://www.cnbc.com/2025/10/20/trump-australia-albanese-minerals-rare-earths-china.html; Prime Minister of Australia (official), "Historic critical minerals framework signed by President Trump and Prime Minister Albanese," https://www.pm.gov.au/media/historic-critical-minerals-framework-signed-president-trump-and-prime-minister-albanese (primary, Australian PM's office — could not fetch directly, verify dollar figures and named projects). Used for: a strong, quotable, dateable set-piece for the chapter — a signing ceremony explicitly framed (per Trump's own quoted remarks about the "race against China") as a geopolitical response to Chinese rare-earth leverage.

18. **On July 10, 2025, MP Materials announced a "transformational public-private partnership" with the U.S. Department of Defense** — the first time the U.S. federal government became a major equity shareholder in a critical-minerals company. Terms: a $400 million DoD equity investment via newly issued Series A Preferred Stock; a commitment for up to $350 million in additional Series A Preferred funding; a $150 million DoD loan; a 10-year DoD price floor for NdPr (neodymium-praseodymium) products; a 10-year DoD offtake agreement for magnet output from MP's planned "10X Facility" (a second U.S. magnet plant); and warrants giving DoD a resulting ~15% as-converted equity stake in MP Materials. The deal also funds new samarium production capability at MP's Mountain Pass, California mine. Sources: MP Materials investor relations, "MP Materials Announces Transformational Public-Private Partnership with the Department of Defense...," July 10, 2025, https://investors.mpmaterials.com/investor-news/news-details/2025/MP-Materials-Announces-Transformational-Public-Private-Partnership-with-the-Department-of-Defense-to-Accelerate-U-S--Rare-Earth-Magnet-Independence/default.aspx (primary, company press release — could not fetch directly, verify all dollar figures and the exact equity percentage before quoting); MP Materials SEC Form 8-K, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1801368/000119312525157310/d43796d8k.htm (primary SEC filing). Used for: the single most vivid "state capitalism meets critical minerals" anecdote in Western policy — Pentagon as shareholder, price-floor guarantor, and customer of last resort, all at once.

19. **Lynas Rare Earths (Australia) signed a binding letter of intent with the U.S. Department of War (renamed from Department of Defense) for a roughly US$96 million rare-earths offtake and price-floor deal**, with an NdPr floor price around US$110/kg over a four-year supply period; this reportedly redirected funds originally earmarked (by March 16, 2026) for a Lynas heavy-rare-earth processing plant at Seadrift, Texas, which faces construction uncertainty, toward direct government procurement instead. (Note: some sources cite the deal value as $137 million rather than $96 million — reconcile before use.) Sources: Capital Brief, "Lynas Rare Earths signs $137m offtake, floor price with US Department of War," https://www.capitalbrief.com/briefing/lynas-rare-earths-signs-137m-offtake-floor-price-with-us-department-of-war-34c6a671-72fb-42ac-85a9-0c61f3a5f226/; Benchmark Minerals, "Lynas Rare Earths signs US$96m rare earth deal with DoW," https://source.benchmarkminerals.com/article/lynas-rare-earths-signs-us-96m-rare-earth-offtake-and-price-floor-commitment-with-dow; CNBC, "Lynas flags uncertainty over Texas rare earths plant, posts profit slump," Aug 28, 2025, https://www.cnbc.com/2025/08/28/lynas-flags-uncertainty-over-texas-rare-earths-plant-posts-profit-slump.html. Used for: counterevidence/complication — shows a flagship "friend-shored" processing facility (Seadrift, TX) running into real-world uncertainty and Washington pivoting from building capacity to just buying output, which is a materially weaker form of derisking. **Reconcile the $96M vs $137M figures before drafting.**

20. **The Quad Critical Minerals Initiative Framework (U.S., Japan, Australia, India) was announced May 26, 2026, at the Quad Foreign Ministers' Meeting in New Delhi**, aiming to mobilize up to $20 billion for secure critical-minerals supply chains, with role division described as: Australia (raw materials + extraction/processing expertise), Japan (public-private investment model, drawing on JOGMEC experience), India (processing/refining hub ambitions), U.S. (capital + market access). Source: U.S. Department of State, "Quad Critical Minerals Initiative Framework Among the United States, Japan, Australia, and India," https://www.state.gov/releases/office-of-the-spokesperson/2026/05/quad-critical-minerals-initiative-framework-among-the-united-states-japan-australia-and-india (primary — could not fetch directly, verify $20B figure and date); The Diplomat, "Operationalizing a Quad Critical Minerals Pact," July 2025, https://thediplomat.com/2025/07/operationalizing-a-quad-critical-minerals-pact/ (earlier framing/analysis piece — note this predates the May 2026 announcement, so there may be an earlier framework iteration; reconcile the timeline). Used for: shows the Indo-Pacific "mini-lateral" dimension distinct from the transatlantic MSP/CRMA track.

21. **Japan's JOGMEC (Japan Organization for Metals and Energy Security) is METI's primary vehicle for mineral security**: it manages Japan's national rare-metal stockpile, provides equity/loans/guarantees for overseas projects, and — under Japan's 2020 International Resource Strategy — raised national stockpiling targets to 60 days of supply for standard critical minerals and up to 180 days for high-risk ones. Between 2004 and 2020, JOGMEC backed 100+ overseas projects with $600M+ combined financial support; by 2024 it had offices in 15 countries running 30+ active projects. Source: New Security Beat (Wilson Center), "How to Diversify Mineral Supply Chains – A Japanese Agency has Lessons for All," Aug 2024, https://www.newsecuritybeat.org/2024/08/how-to-diversify-mineral-supply-chains-a-japanese-agency-has-lessons-for-all/ (reputable think-tank secondary source, but with specific figures attributable to JOGMEC's own reporting — verify against JOGMEC/METI primary documents). Used for: giving the book a non-U.S., decades-old model of stockpiling/state industrial finance predating the post-2022 Western scramble — useful for showing Japan got here first.

22. **JOGMEC has backed rare-earth separation capacity outside China, including investment tied to Lynas's Malaysia/Australia operations and the France-based Caremag project; via its Lynas relationship Japan has secured offtake rights covering roughly 30% of its domestic heavy rare earth demand.** Source: same New Security Beat piece above, and SFA (Oxford), "Japan–US Rare Earths Deal," https://www.sfa-oxford.com/market-news-and-insights/japan-us-rare-earths-deal-critical-minerals-supply-chain/ (secondary). Used for: narrative material — Japan's post-2010 (Senkaku shock) rare-earth diversification as the direct ancestor of the current Western "friend-shoring" push; a good historical bridge/contrast within the chapter.

### National Defense Stockpile (US)

23. **The "One Big Beautiful Bill Act" (P.L. 119-21), signed into law July 4, 2025, appropriated $2 billion for FY2025 to expand the U.S. National Defense Stockpile, plus $5 billion to a Defense Department "Industrial Base Fund" (IBF) to support critical-minerals supply chains, and an additional $500 million to the Defense Department's Credit Program Account.** In 2025 DoD also announced intent to procure up to $1 billion in stockpile materials, issuing multiple Requests for Information. The NDS (managed by the Defense Logistics Agency) list includes copper, nickel, lithium, antimony, and 16 rare-earth elements. Source: Inside Government Contracts (law firm blog), "Federal Push for Critical Minerals Stockpiling: 2025 in Review and Outlook for 2026," https://www.insidegovernmentcontracts.com/2026/02/federal-push-for-critical-minerals-stockpiling-2025-in-review-and-outlook-for-2026/ (secondary, but from a law firm tracking the legislation directly — verify dollar figures against the bill text/DLA announcements). Used for: quantifying the scale of the U.S. stockpiling push and dating it precisely to a named piece of legislation (good primary-document anchor to chase down: P.L. 119-21).

### US-China 2025 trade truce (rare earths)

24. **On October 30, 2025, in South Korea, Presidents Trump and Xi agreed to a one-year trade truce**: China agreed to suspend for one year the sweeping rare-earth export controls it had announced October 9, 2025, plus related controls on lithium-ion battery materials and superhard materials; in exchange the U.S. cut the "fentanyl tariff" on Chinese imports from 20% to 10% (lowering the overall China tariff rate from 41% to 31%) and extended a suspension of 24% "reciprocal" tariffs until November 10, 2026. Sources: CNBC, "What Trump and Xi agreed to in the U.S.-China trade truce," Oct 30, 2025, https://www.cnbc.com/2025/10/30/trump-xi-south-korea-rare-earth-tariff-trade-war-nvidia.html; Morrison Foerster, "United States and China Reach Trade Agreement: Takeaways for Export and Supply Chain Controls," Nov 13, 2025, https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/251113-united-states-and-china-reach-trade-agreement (law firm analysis, reliable for legal/trade detail); Reuters via TradingView, "What did Trump, Xi agree to on tariffs, export controls and fentanyl," https://de.tradingview.com/news/reuters.com,2025:newsml_L1N3WD08M:0-what-did-trump-xi-agree-to-on-tariffs-export-controls-and-fentanyl. Used for: the chapter's central "did friend-shoring even matter" pivot point — China's October 9, 2025 export-control threat and its one-year suspension shows Beijing retains the whip hand and can dial pressure up or down largely independent of Western alliance-building; this belongs at the emotional/analytical climax of the chapter.

25. **Analysts characterize the October 2025 truce as tactical, not strategic — a pause, not a resolution**, with major disputes (especially over semiconductors and rare earths) unresolved and the arrangement widely described as fragile. Source: Coface, "US-China tactical deal: Tariffs, tech, and rare earths," https://www.coface.com/news-economy-and-insights/us-china-trade-agreement-a-tactical-truce-not-a-strategic-shift (economic research firm — reasonably reliable, treat as analysis/opinion not primary fact). Used for: counterevidence — undercuts any triumphalist framing that the truce "solved" Western mineral vulnerability.

### China's baseline dominance (context/counterevidence)

26. **China controlled roughly 91% of global rare-earth refining capacity and about 94% of global permanent-magnet (sintered) production in 2024**, up from roughly 50% magnet share in 2005; China also accounted for about 60% of global mined production of "magnet rare earths" in 2024. Source: aggregated from IEA data, "China's share in rare earth magnet production, 2024," https://www.iea.org/data-and-statistics/charts/china-s-share-in-rare-earth-magnet-production-2024 (primary IEA chart — could not fetch directly, verify exact percentages); also referenced via Chatham House, "China's new restrictions on rare earth exports send a stark warning to the West," Oct 2025, https://www.chathamhouse.org/2025/10/chinas-new-restrictions-rare-earth-exports-send-stark-warning-west. Used for: the baseline number against which every friend-shoring initiative in this chapter must be measured — essential for the "is this actually working" through-line.

27. **China also holds roughly 60%+ of world lithium *processing* capacity** even though it mines a much smaller share of raw lithium — illustrating that Beijing's chokepoint is concentrated at the midstream (refining/processing) stage, which is exactly the stage the CRMA's 40%-processing target and the MSP's "midstream" project focus are aimed at. Source: aggregated search summary citing multiple outlets (East Asia Forum, "China's critical mineral strategy beyond geopolitics," March 2026, https://eastasiaforum.org/2026/03/03/chinas-critical-mineral-strategy-beyond-geopolitics/). Used for: explaining *why* Western policy (CRMA, MSP, MP Materials magnet plants) targets processing/midstream specifically rather than mining.

28. **A Jacques Delors Centre / general expert consensus, echoed in EU Parliament and think-tank analysis, holds that "meaningful reductions in [EU] dependence on China will not materialize before the 2030s"** even with the CRMA and RESourceEU fully implemented, given multi-year permitting and construction timelines for extraction/processing facilities. Source: aggregated from search summary referencing Jacques Delors Centre, "The EU's critical raw materials predicament: ReSourceEU to the Rescue?," https://www.delorscentre.eu/en/publications/detail/publication/the-eus-critical-raw-materials-predicament (reputable EU-focused think tank). Used for: the chapter's core "too slow" thesis, stated by a credentialed European policy institute rather than just journalistic framing — worth chasing the primary PDF for a direct quotable line.

## Narrative material

- **The Pentagon-as-shareholder scene (MP Materials, July 10 2025):** For the first time in modern U.S. history, the Defense Department became a significant equity owner ($400M+ up to $750M possible) of a publicly traded mining/materials company, plus a price-floor guarantor and a captive 10-year customer, in one negotiated package. The company: MP Materials, owner of the Mountain Pass mine in California — the only significant rare-earth mine operating in the U.S., previously a symbol of American industrial decline (it went bankrupt in 2015 as Molycorp before being revived). Good scene-setting: Mountain Pass's history (Cold War-era mine, lost to Chinese competition in the 1990s-2000s, revived post-2015) bookends neatly with the government literally buying back into it in 2025.

- **The Lynas pivot from "build a plant" to "just buy the output" (2025-2026):** Washington originally committed money to help Lynas build a heavy-rare-earth separation plant at Seadrift, Texas — the kind of visible, photogenic "reshoring" infrastructure project. By March 2026, construction uncertainty led the U.S. Department of War (renamed from DoD) to redirect the money into a straightforward offtake/price-floor deal instead — paying Lynas to ship rare earths to the U.S. rather than funding a U.S. factory. This is a strong, concrete illustration of the "friend-shoring is easier to announce than to build" thesis, and it also captures the renaming of the Pentagon to the "Department of War" as an interesting period detail.

- **The Trump-Albanese signing (October 20, 2025):** A head-of-state signing ceremony, explicit in its own framing (Trump quoted describing it as being about the "race against China"), tied to a named project (Nolans neodymium mine, Northern Territory) whose output is explicitly linked to "night vision devices and guided missiles" — a vivid civilian/military dual-use detail.

- **The October 9 → October 30, 2025 whiplash:** China announces sweeping new rare-earth export controls on October 9, 2025; three weeks later, at a Trump-Xi summit in South Korea, China agrees to suspend them for a year in exchange for tariff relief. This 21-day arc is a compact, dramatizable sequence showing how quickly Beijing can escalate and de-escalate mineral leverage — useful as a "meanwhile, back in Beijing" counterpoint to the slower-moving Western institution-building described elsewhere in the chapter.

- **KoBold Metals in Zambia:** Silicon Valley money (Gates, Bezos, and reportedly Sam Altman-adjacent backers) using AI-driven mineral exploration to find what's described as the largest copper discovery in a century, now breaking ground on a $2.3-2.5B mine with MSP diplomatic backing — an interesting "AI eating its own supply chain" angle, given the book's AI-data-center framing.

- **Japan's decades-long head start:** JOGMEC's stockpiling and offtake strategy (100+ projects, $600M+ since 2004) began well before 2022's Western scramble, seeded by the 2010 Senkaku/Diaoyu Islands dispute when China briefly halted rare-earth exports to Japan. This gives the chapter a useful "Japan already lived through this once" throughline and a source of hard-won expertise other Quad partners are now leaning on.

- **Numbers worth using as chapter anchors:** China ~91% of rare-earth refining, ~94% of magnet production (2024); MSP's 32 endorsed projects vs. EU's 47-then-60 "strategic projects"; the U.S. National Defense Stockpile's $2B (FY25) + $5B Industrial Base Fund infusion via the "One Big Beautiful Bill Act."

## Counterevidence / complications

- **Too slow, structurally:** Multiple analysts (Jacques Delors Centre, ODI, Chatham House) converge on the view that meaningful reductions in Western dependence on China will not materialize until the 2030s even under optimistic implementation of the CRMA, MSP, and IRA — because mine and refinery construction, plus permitting, run 5-10+ years, while China's dominance is already built and operating. https://www.delorscentre.eu/en/publications/detail/publication/the-eus-critical-raw-materials-predicament ; https://odi.org/en/insights/europes-critical-raw-materials-bet-a-reality-check-on-the-eus-ambitions/

- **Underfunded relative to need:** The EU's CRMA sets targets and streamlines permitting but its companion funding vehicle (RESourceEU, up to ~€3B) is small relative to the €22.5B+ investment need identified in just the first 47 strategic projects. https://odi.org/en/insights/europes-critical-raw-materials-bet-a-reality-check-on-the-eus-ambitions/

- **Permitting delays persist despite the law:** Real-world EU mine permitting still averages 5-10 years, not the CRMA's stated 24-month target; the second round of CRMA strategic-project designations was delayed from mid-2025 into autumn. https://www.metalnomist.com/2026/08/eu-crma-strategic-projects-delay-raises.html

- **China retains and exercises real-time leverage:** The October 9, 2025 Chinese export-control announcement — three weeks before the Trump-Xi truce — shows Beijing can still unilaterally shock global rare-earth markets at a moment of its choosing, and the resulting "truce" is explicitly one-year and widely described by analysts as tactical/fragile rather than a durable resolution. https://www.cnbc.com/2025/10/30/trump-xi-south-korea-rare-earth-tariff-trade-war-nvidia.html ; https://www.coface.com/news-economy-and-insights/us-china-trade-agreement-a-tactical-truce-not-a-strategic-shift

- **Flagship "reshoring" projects wobble:** Lynas's Seadrift, Texas heavy-rare-earth plant — originally the marquee example of U.S.-funded processing capacity outside China — ran into "significant uncertainty" over whether construction would even proceed, and by March 2026 Washington had redirected the earmarked funds into an offtake deal instead. This is a concrete instance of the "announcement ≠ execution" pattern skeptics warn about. https://www.cnbc.com/2025/08/28/lynas-flags-uncertainty-over-texas-rare-earths-plant-posts-profit-slump.html

- **Domestic reserve constraints limit how far "onshoring" can go regardless of policy:** The Carnegie Endowment's October 2025 report argues the U.S. does not have sufficient domestic reserves of some critical minerals to meet its own demand even with maximal onshoring effort, meaning a purely domestic (as opposed to allied/"friend-shored") strategy is mathematically impossible for at least some minerals (their worked example: copper). https://carnegieendowment.org/research/2025/10/securing-americas-critical-minerals-supply?lang=en (also PDF: https://carnegie-production-assets.s3.amazonaws.com/static/files/Allan%20and%20Goldman_US%20CM%20Supply.pdf)

- **MSP's own numbers show limited maturity:** 32 "endorsed" projects (as of Sept 2024) is a count of political/diplomatic support, not of projects actually in production — a distinction worth making explicit in the manuscript, since press coverage sometimes conflates "MSP-endorsed" with "MSP-built."

- **Successes worth counting fairly (for balance):** The MP Materials-DoD deal (equity stake + 10-year price floor + 10-year offtake) is a genuinely novel and large-scale U.S. policy instrument with few precedents; the US-Australia framework moved from negotiation to a signed $8.5B pipeline in about five months; and Japan's JOGMEC model (pre-dating the 2022 Western turn by over a decade) demonstrates that patient, state-backed stockpiling and offtake investment can measurably reduce single-country exposure over a ~15-year horizon (Japan's ~30% heavy-rare-earth offtake coverage via Lynas). These are legitimate "it can work, but only over a long horizon and with sustained state commitment" counterpoints to the "too slow" critique.
