# Chapter 5 research addendum — state ownership is not immediate operating control

## Why this note

Chapter 5 is strongest when Chile's lithium strategy is treated as an institutional design problem rather than a binary choice between nationalization and laissez-faire. The current manuscript says Codelco moved into lithium through partnerships and describes the Codelco–SQM Salar de Atacama venture as evidence that the actual model is more complicated than either political slogan. That argument can now be made more concrete because the venture has completed its regulatory process and begun operating.

## Primary-source update

Chile's Fiscalía Nacional Económica approved the Codelco–SQM joint venture on April 24, 2025, subject to mitigation measures aimed at competition and information-sharing risks. The FNE's description contains an important governance detail that sharpens the chapter's state-versus-market argument: the venture has two control periods. SQM controls it from 2025 through 2030; Codelco controls it from 2031 through 2060.

Source: Fiscalía Nacional Económica, "FNE Approves Codelco–SQM Joint Venture Subject to Mitigation Measures," April 24, 2025.
https://www.fne.gob.cl/en/fne-approves-codelco-sqm-joint-venture-subject-to-mitigation-measures/

Codelco later reported that the final condition precedent was completed on December 19, 2025, when Chile's Comptroller General approved the relevant Corfo–Minera Tarar contracts. Codelco and SQM formally created NovaAndino Litio on December 27, 2025, and its first board meeting was held December 29. The company has six directors, three representing Codelco and three SQM. Codelco describes the venture as having majority state participation; the earlier agreement specified 50 percent plus one share for the state side.

## Argument this permits

The interesting Chilean experiment is not simply "more state." It separates ownership, operating control, technical capability, and time.

At Atacama, the state can hold the majority economic position while an incumbent private operator retains control during the transition period. Codelco's control begins only in 2031. At Maricunga, Codelco again keeps a bare majority while buying access to outside capital and operating knowledge through a large private partner.

That makes the chapter's political-economy point more specific. A government does not capture more value merely by owning a larger percentage on paper. It also has to decide which capabilities it can build, which it should contract or partner for, how quickly control should transfer, and what competition or governance problems the partnership itself creates.

## Strongest counterargument / disconfirming evidence

Majority state participation does not establish that the model will outperform a more private system. The FNE found enough potential competition risk to require mitigation measures, including restrictions around commercially sensitive information. The venture also preserves SQM's control through 2030, which cuts against any simplistic description of the arrangement as immediate state takeover.

Nor does the completed legal structure prove environmental success. NovaAndino's formation and Maricunga's DLE plan establish governance and technology choices, not their eventual hydrological outcomes. Chapter 5 should keep its existing caution that the relevant environmental question is what a specific extraction process does to a specific salar over time.

The fair claim is narrower: Chile is testing a hybrid model in which the state tries to retain long-run control and a larger share of value without pretending that ownership instantly creates operating capability.

## Suggested insertion point

Immediately after the paragraph beginning "The actual model has been more complicated than either slogan."

Possible manuscript language, to be adapted rather than pasted mechanically:

> The complication is visible in the calendar. Chile's competition authority approved the Codelco–SQM venture with one company controlling it through 2030 and the other taking control from 2031 to 2060. By the end of 2025 the venture, NovaAndino Litio, had formally begun operating with majority state participation, but not with immediate state operating control. That distinction is easy to lose in arguments about nationalization. Ownership can be transferred with signatures. Operating knowledge has a slower clock.

This gives the chapter a concrete institutional example without turning the book into a defense of the venture. The FNE's competition remedies and the delayed control transfer are useful precisely because they prevent the example from becoming a triumphalist state-capacity anecdote.
