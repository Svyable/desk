*Hiding Signal in Artificial Noise*

The working thesis is that generative AI changes the information problem from scarcity to saturation. When plausible text, images, explanations, reviews, arguments, and evidence-like artifacts become cheap, truth does not necessarily disappear. It becomes harder to distinguish from the material surrounding it. Signal can be hidden without being censored: bury it under enough competent-looking noise.

*Gradient Ascent* uses optimization as both a technical idea and a social metaphor. Systems climb toward whatever objective we make legible. Institutions, markets, creators, and algorithms do the same. The danger is not simply that optimization fails. It is that optimization succeeds against a proxy until the proxy becomes abundant and the underlying thing we cared about becomes harder to see.

This is deliberately not a handbook for covert communications, evasion, or deception. It is a book about epistemic infrastructure: why optimization produces artificial noise, how synthetic abundance changes the economics of verification, and what people and institutions can do to keep reality legible.
