# Sea Trials — evidence ledger

## Research rule

No test-level claim enters the manuscript from a program-level summary if a test-specific fact sheet or report can be located. No individual-health claim is inferred from mere presence on a participating ship. Agent, simulant, tracer and decontaminant remain separate categories. “Classified” never means “sinister” by itself; “declassified” never means complete.

## Class 1 — verified / documented

- VA: Project 112/SHAD ran from 1962 into the 1970s and included land- and sea-based chemical/biological vulnerability tests. https://www.publichealth.va.gov/exposures/shad/basics.asp
- VA current eligibility page: participants in Project 112/SHAD testing may qualify for compensation when a health condition is linked to testing. Updated July 27, 2026. https://www.va.gov/disability/eligibility/hazardous-materials-exposure/project-112-shad/
- Health.mil maintains declassified test fact sheets by fiscal year and test name. https://health.mil/Military-Health-Topics/Environmental-Exposures-Hub/Environmental-Exposures-Topics/Project-112-SHAD/Fact-Sheets
- Health.mil states that original classified SHAD information was not completely cataloged or centralized; Deseret Test Center had closed; later investigation required searching dispersed decades-old records and declassifying medically relevant information.
- Health.mil states that nasal swabs and throat gargles were used in some tests as an informal, largely undocumented supplement to mechanical sampling; Autumn Gold records include sampling of masked crewmen but readings are not linked to named individuals. https://health.mil/Military-Health-Topics/Environmental-Exposures-Hub/Environmental-Exposures-Topics/Project-112-SHAD
- VA May 23, 2002 release: medically significant information from twelve tests involving approximately 4,300 participants had then been declassified and released to VA. https://news.va.gov/press-room/va-contacts-project-shad-veterans/
- National Academies 2016: approximately 5,900 military personnel, primarily Navy and Marine Corps, were reported included in SHAD testing between 1963 and 1969; tests used active chemical/biological agents, simulants, tracers and decontaminants. DOI 10.17226/21846. https://www.nationalacademies.org/publications/21846
- VA public-health summary currently says there is no clear evidence of specific long-term health problems associated with SHAD participation as a class. https://www.publichealth.va.gov/exposures/shad/
- GAO-08-366 (Feb. 28, 2008): tens of thousands of military personnel and civilians were potentially exposed in DoD chemical/biological tests since WWII; GAO evaluated identification and notification efforts, including Project 112. https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-08-366
- Health.mil's warfare-exposure FAQ currently says its Project 112/SHAD database contains about 6,400 names and is updated when additional participants are discovered. Treat this as a current administrative count, not a fixed historical denominator.
- GAO-26-108483 (July 22, 2026): DoD/VA's Individual Longitudinal Exposure Record links military toxic-exposure information for health care, claims and research. Useful final-chapter comparator; it does not retroactively solve SHAD's missing provenance. https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-26-108483

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete

- Veteran testimony about being unaware of test details can establish remembered disclosure conditions but must be reconstructed test by test. Do not generalize one crew's account to all SHAD tests.
- Retrospective recollection of symptoms is evidence of experience, not by itself causal attribution.
- Congressional testimony can establish what witnesses told Congress and what oversight questions existed; use contemporaneous ship/test records for technical reconstruction.

## Class 3 — disputed / unconfirmed

- Claims that all SHAD participants were deliberately used as unwitting human test subjects in the same sense as a clinical dosing experiment.
- Claims that a particular veteran's later cancer, respiratory disease or neurologic illness was caused by SHAD absent individual exposure and medical evidence.
- Claims of undisclosed SHAD tests based solely on memory or internet lists.
- Claims that an inconclusive epidemiological result demonstrates record destruction or a cover-up.

## Class 4 — synthesis / conjecture

- **Classification debt:** secrecy can create a deferred administrative liability when medically relevant provenance is stored according to operational sensitivity rather than person-level longitudinal health needs.
- **Ontology collision:** engineering, intelligence, medicine and veterans-benefit systems index the same event differently; later causal reconstruction fails at the joins.
- **The body crossing the classified boundary:** a person may be unable to know facts about his own exposure because those facts are embedded in a classified operational system. This is a normative interpretation, not a claim that all information should have been immediately public.

## Contradiction ledger

### “SHAD was defensive, therefore harmless.”
Defensive purpose does not determine exposure or ethics. Test-specific materials, protective conditions and sampling must be reconstructed.

### “Live agents were used, therefore every sailor was dosed.”
False inference. Program inventory is not individual absorbed dose.

### “Veterans later became ill, therefore SHAD caused it.”
False without causal evidence. Population epidemiology and individual causation are distinct.

### “No clear long-term syndrome was found, therefore secrecy did no harm.”
Also false. Health effect and recordkeeping/notification failure are separable questions.

### “The participant count changed, therefore officials hid names.”
Not established. Later discovery from dispersed records can change counts without deliberate concealment. The delay and dispersal still deserve scrutiny.

## Incentive audit

- **Cold War test organizations:** incentive to protect vulnerabilities, methods and capabilities; operational success metrics dominate later clinical utility.
- **Services/DoD after declassification pressure:** incentive to demonstrate diligent reconstruction and bound liability; official summaries require comparison with underlying reports.
- **VA:** incentive to identify participants and adjudicate health claims while avoiding unsupported presumptions.
- **Veterans:** legitimate incentive to obtain exposure histories, care and compensation; memory may carry details absent from records but is not automatically technical proof.
- **Plaintiffs/advocates:** incentive toward broad causal framing; valuable leads require documentary and epidemiological checking.
- **Historians/journalists:** incentive toward the most dramatic version; resist collapsing simulants, live agents and individual dose.
