# Sea Trials

## Project SHAD, Secret Exposure, and the Sailors Inside the Experiment

**Sven Hardy Benson**

### Thesis

Project SHAD is not best understood as a lurid story in which the United States secretly attacked its own sailors, nor as a harmless readiness exercise later inflated by memory. It is a harder story. The military needed to learn whether warships could survive chemical and biological attack. It therefore built realistic vulnerability experiments around ships, crews, live agents, simulants, tracers, protective equipment and decontamination. The resulting knowledge was classified. Decades later, the people whose bodies had occupied the test environment discovered that the government's operational record and their medical record had been separated by secrecy.

The book asks a narrow question with large consequences: **what happens when a state has a legitimate reason to keep an experiment secret but the secret contains information that may matter to the health of the people inside it?**

This is a book about Project 112 and especially Shipboard Hazard and Defense, not a generalized catalogue of Cold War wrongdoing. It reconstructs test design, ships, agents, samplers, nasal swabs, masks, decontamination, classification, record dispersal, veteran discovery, epidemiology and compensation. It refuses to manufacture a health verdict the evidence cannot support. The National Academies' later epidemiology did not establish a simple SHAD-caused syndrome. That uncertainty is part of the story, because delayed reconstruction made the denominator, dose and individual exposure history harder to recover.

## Evidence classes

1. **Verified / documented:** DoD and VA records; declassified test fact sheets; ships, units, dates, agents and simulants identified by official investigation; congressional and GAO records; National Academies epidemiology.
2. **Credible but incomplete:** participant recollections, shipboard testimony and veteran accounts where contemporaneous records are incomplete or cannot be linked to individuals.
3. **Disputed / unconfirmed:** specific dose claims, individual causal-health claims, or assertions about undisclosed tests that lack independent documentary support.
4. **Speculative synthesis:** the argument that classification created a second-order exposure problem by separating operational knowledge from later clinical knowledge. This is interpretation, not proof of an institutional plan to conceal harm.

## Structure

1. **A Ship Inside a Cloud** — vulnerability testing as an engineering problem with human beings physically inside the instrument.
2. **Deseret** — Project 112, Deseret Test Center and the institutional machine that designed the tests.
3. **Why Test a Warship?** — Cold War chemical/biological threat assumptions and the difference between offensive use and defensive vulnerability research.
4. **Eager Bell** — an early SHAD test reconstructed from the surviving record.
5. **Autumn Gold** — masks, samplers, nasal swabs and the uncomfortable boundary between crew and measurement device.
6. **Live Agent** — what was actually used: biological agents, chemical agents, simulants, tracers and decontaminants, without collapsing them into one category.
7. **Dose Unknown** — exposure reconstruction and why presence is not dose.
8. **A Sailor Is Not a Sampler** — human-subject ethics, operational testing and the category problem that let crew participation sit outside ordinary research narratives.
9. **Classified for a Reason** — what secrecy protected operationally.
10. **Classified Too Long** — when legitimate secrecy begins degrading health reconstruction and accountability.
11. **Forty-Year Filing Cabinet** — the closure of Deseret Test Center and the later search across dispersed records.
12. **Who Was There?** — the participant denominator, rosters, missing names and notification.
13. **What Did They Know?** — participant knowledge reconstructed test by test rather than assumed globally.
14. **The Veterans Come Back** — the 1990s/2000s pressure that forced SHAD into public view.
15. **Epidemiology After Secrecy** — IOM/National Academies studies, comparison populations and inferential limits.
16. **No Clear Syndrome** — what negative or equivocal population evidence does and does not establish for individuals.
17. **Exposure Record** — SHAD in the longer history of military toxic-exposure recordkeeping, through the modern Individual Longitudinal Exposure Record.
18. **Secrets With Half-Lives** — a design argument for classification systems that preserve operational secrecy without destroying medically relevant provenance.

## Defeat condition

The book fails if it needs to imply that all SHAD sailors were unknowingly dosed with lethal agents, that every later illness was caused by SHAD, or that inconclusive epidemiology proves a cover-up. It succeeds only if the documented machinery is disturbing enough without inflation.
