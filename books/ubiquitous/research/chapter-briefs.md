# Chapter Briefs and Falsification Notes

These notes keep the empirical burden of *Ubiquitous* attached to the chapter where it matters. Source IDs refer to `source-ledger.csv`. Product descriptions and occupational projections are time-sensitive and should be rechecked before release.

## 1 — The Floor Rises

**Evidence burden:** Establish that generative AI can improve speed and quality while disproportionately helping weaker, novice, or less-experienced workers; distinguish task exposure from whole-job elimination.

**Core sources:** `brynjolfsson-2023-genai-work`, `noy-zhang-2023`, `ilo-2025-genai-jobs`, `autor-2015-polarization`.

**Caution:** Do not generalize two productivity studies into a universal law. Gains depend on task, model, workflow, and evaluation method.

**What would make this false?** A broad evidence base showing that strong performers systematically gain more from AI than weaker performers, leaving or widening performance gaps across most economically important tasks.

## 2 — A Good Enough Expert Everywhere

**Evidence burden:** Separate expertise-leveling from expert augmentation and from creation of new tasks. Show why direct access to competent first-pass interpretation can change professional demand before full substitution becomes reliable.

**Core sources:** `acemoglu-autor-johnson-2026`, `brynjolfsson-2023-genai-work`, `acemoglu-restrepo-2019-new-tasks`.

**Caution:** “Good enough” is domain dependent. High-stakes law, medicine, safety, and engineering can remain professionally gated even when preliminary interpretation becomes cheap.

**What would make this false?** Evidence that widespread general-model access leaves the division of labor between clients, juniors, and experts largely unchanged because reliability or institutional barriers prevent meaningful task transfer.

## 3 — The Harness Eats the Interface

**Evidence burden:** Document the current shift from chat-only models toward systems with tools, files, connectors, permissions, and multi-step action. Treat Claude Desktop/MCP as an example, not a permanent winner.

**Core sources:** `anthropic-2026-local-mcp`, `anthropic-2026-connectors`, `anthropic-2025-economic-index`, `anthropic-2026-cadences`.

**Caution:** Current product architecture changes quickly. Preserve the general claim—intent is increasingly translated into software actions—while rechecking named features before release.

**What would make this false?** A reversal toward narrowly isolated AI applications in which general models rarely receive safe, useful access to external tools or enterprise context.

## 4 — The Vanishing Junior Rung

**Evidence burden:** Show the mechanism by which routine work functions simultaneously as production and apprenticeship, and why automating the first does not automatically replace the second.

**Core sources:** `dellacqua-2026-jagged-frontier`, `brynjolfsson-2023-genai-work`, `acemoglu-autor-johnson-2026`, `anthropic-2026-81k`.

**Caution:** The junior-rung problem is partly prospective. Do not claim a profession-wide collapse without hiring and training evidence.

**What would make this false?** Employers create enough new junior tasks, simulation, supervised ownership, and formal training that entry pathways remain as plentiful and effective as before routine cognitive automation.

## 5 — The Vertical Specialist Becomes a Feature

**Evidence burden:** Explain how stable input-to-output procedures migrate into software and why routine-task exposure differs from disappearance of the profession around the procedure.

**Core sources:** `autor-2015-polarization`, `acemoglu-restrepo-2019-new-tasks`, `oecd-2026-ai-exposure`, `acemoglu-restrepo-2021-wages`.

**Caution:** Deep expertise is not reducible to rule count. Local facts, tacit knowledge, responsibility, and feedback loops can keep a specialty economically distinct.

**What would make this false?** General models remain unable to execute useful portions of specialist workflows without continuous specialist intervention, so professional boundaries remain mostly intact.

## 6 — The Jagged Frontier

**Evidence burden:** Establish that model capability is uneven enough that assistance can improve performance on one task while increasing error on another nearby task.

**Core sources:** `dellacqua-2026-jagged-frontier`, `oecd-2026-ai-exposure`.

**Caution:** The exact frontier moves with every model and tool. The chapter should defend the need to locate the frontier, not freeze its current location.

**What would make this false?** Models become reliably calibrated and broadly competent across open-ended professional work, with failures obvious enough that users rarely become worse through misplaced trust.

## 7 — Curiosity Is an Economic Skill

**Evidence burden:** Support the claim that changing technology increases the value of learning agility, technological literacy, and cross-domain adaptation without treating employer surveys as prophecy.

**Core sources:** `wef-2025-skills`, `oecd-2024-changing-skills`, `autor-2015-polarization`.

**Caution:** Curiosity without depth, time, access, or real-world feedback can produce little economic value.

**What would make this false?** Labor markets increasingly reward stable narrow specialization while cross-domain learning provides little measurable advantage once AI handles translation between fields.

## 8 — The Renaissance Worker

**Evidence burden:** Test the stronger claim that people with real depth plus cross-domain integration become more valuable as general cognitive capability gets cheaper.

**Core sources:** `acemoglu-autor-johnson-2026`, `oecd-2024-changing-skills`, `anthropic-2026-economic-index`.

**Caution:** This chapter is partly synthesis rather than a directly measured occupational category. Avoid presenting the renaissance-worker label as an empirically established job class.

**What would make this false?** Models integrate specialties more reliably and cheaply than humans while organizations show little premium for workers who combine multiple real domains.

## 9 — The Physical World Pushes Back

**Evidence burden:** Distinguish variable physical intervention from repetitive physical production and show that “physical” occupations have mixed exposure to automation.

**Core sources:** `wef-2025-skills`, `bls-2026-machinists`, `bls-2026-industrial-maintenance`, `bls-2026-drafters`, `bls-2026-welders`, `bls-2026-carpenters`.

**Caution:** Robotics can advance quickly, especially when environments can be standardized or capital intensity is justified.

**What would make this false?** General-purpose robotics becomes economical and reliable across variable service, repair, retrofit, and construction environments fast enough to materially erase the open-world advantage within the decade.

## 10 — Electricians for the Compute Century

**Evidence burden:** Ground the electrical argument in current projections and in the physical requirements of electrification, data centers, power generation, controls, and maintenance.

**Core sources:** `bls-2026-electricians`, `bls-2026-hvac`, `bls-2026-industrial-engineers`, `bls-2026-solar-installers`, `bls-2026-occupational-projections`.

**Caution:** National projections are not local guarantees, and data-center growth can be geographically concentrated or cyclical.

**What would make this false?** Electrical demand grows without corresponding skilled-labor demand because prefabrication, robotics, standardized systems, or construction productivity absorb nearly all incremental work.

## 11 — Water Does Not Care About the Demo

**Evidence burden:** Show how service plumbing, building water systems, and public water operations combine local physical diagnosis with increasing instrumentation and automation.

**Core sources:** `bls-2026-plumbers`, `bls-2026-water-operators`, `bls-2026-hvac`, `bls-2026-occupational-projections`.

**Caution:** Water-system work is heterogeneous. Centralized treatment operations may automate faster than distributed repair and retrofit work.

**What would make this false?** Plumbing and water-system employment broadly contracts as sensing, prefabrication, robotics, and remote operation make local diagnosis and intervention far less labor intensive.

## 12 — Machines Still Need Machinists

**Evidence burden:** Show how CNC, CAM, probing, robotics, and process automation shift skill toward setup, metrology, maintenance, quality, repair, and process economics while some machinist employment declines.

**Core sources:** `bls-2026-machinists`, `bls-2026-industrial-maintenance`, `bls-2026-industrial-engineers`, `bls-2026-welders`.

**Caution:** Do not romanticize machining as protected craft; high-volume stable processes can become highly automated.

**What would make this false?** Automated process planning, workholding, inspection, adaptive control, and robotic handling become economical across high-mix low-volume work, sharply reducing the value of human setup intelligence.

## 13 — Housing Is a Systems Problem

**Evidence burden:** Support the claim that housing delivery crosses design, construction, finance, infrastructure, regulation, and local conditions, while documenting which construction roles remain in demand and which drafting tasks compress.

**Core sources:** `bls-2026-construction-managers`, `bls-2026-carpenters`, `bls-2026-drafters`, `bls-2026-occupational-projections`.

**Caution:** The chapter is about project-system integration, not a claim that any one housing policy or technology solves affordability.

**What would make this false?** Standardization and integrated AI workflows make housing development sufficiently routine that cross-domain project coordination becomes a minor rather than central source of value.

## 14 — Public Engineering and Consequence

**Evidence burden:** Ground civil engineering and construction management in current labor projections while showing the institutional layers—procurement, inspection, public process, maintenance, accountability—that surround technical design.

**Core sources:** `bls-2026-civil-engineers`, `bls-2026-construction-managers`, `bls-2026-drafters`, `bls-2026-occupational-projections`.

**Caution:** Licensure and public process are current institutions, not immutable barriers to automation.

**What would make this false?** Public infrastructure agencies successfully automate most design, coordination, review, and prioritization while retaining accountability, causing broad contraction in engineering and construction-management demand.

## 15 — Verification Becomes the Job

**Evidence burden:** Connect probabilistic generation and tool-using agents to concrete needs for evaluation, permissions, provenance, testing, rollback, audit, and human escalation.

**Core sources:** `dellacqua-2026-jagged-frontier`, `nist-2024-ai-rmf-genai`, `anthropic-2026-connectors`.

**Caution:** Verification itself can be automated. The claim is that control and evidence become more important, not that humans must manually inspect every output forever.

**What would make this false?** Model reliability and automated evaluation improve enough that verification becomes an invisible commodity with little remaining domain-specific human work.

## 16 — Taste After Competence

**Evidence burden:** Treat abundant candidate generation as a selection problem and connect it to older economics of automation and task redesign rather than presenting “taste” as mystical immunity.

**Core sources:** `autor-2015-polarization`, `acemoglu-restrepo-2019-new-tasks`, `oecd-2024-changing-skills`.

**Caution:** Taste can be imitated, benchmarked, and partially encoded. Its defensible form is preference accountable to outcomes.

**What would make this false?** Automated ranking and optimization consistently choose among generated alternatives as well as or better than experienced humans across real multi-objective settings.

## 17 — Trust, Sales, and Local Knowledge

**Evidence burden:** Explain why cheaper information can shift value toward relationships, accountability, private context, demand discovery, and customer ownership.

**Core sources:** `oecd-2026-ai-exposure`, `anthropic-2026-economic-index`, `ilo-2025-genai-jobs`, `anthropic-2026-81k`.

**Caution:** Trust can be intermediated by platforms, brands, ratings, insurance, and automated guarantees; locality is not always a moat.

**What would make this false?** Customers broadly prefer fully automated providers once competence and guarantees are available, and human relationship or local context commands little remaining premium.

## 18 — The New Apprenticeship

**Evidence burden:** Show how AI can make explanation and feedback cheap while preserving the need for attempts, supervised responsibility, and real consequences.

**Core sources:** `brynjolfsson-2023-genai-work`, `dellacqua-2026-jagged-frontier`, `acemoglu-autor-johnson-2026`.

**Caution:** The proposed apprenticeship model is normative and partly prospective. Distinguish evidence about learning effects from recommendations about institutional design.

**What would make this false?** Workers acquire robust expert judgment through AI-mediated explanation and simulation alone, with little need for real supervised practice or consequential experience.

## 19 — The Skills That Last

**Evidence burden:** Synthesize the occupation and task evidence into durable skill structures rather than a static safe-jobs list.

**Core sources:** `oecd-2026-ai-exposure`, `wef-2025-skills`, `ilo-2025-genai-jobs`, `bls-2026-electricians`, `bls-2026-plumbers`, `bls-2026-industrial-maintenance`, `bls-2026-machinists`, `bls-2026-civil-engineers`, `bls-2026-construction-managers`.

**Caution:** “Durable” means complementary under the book's current ten-year horizon, not future-proof.

**What would make this false?** The identified complements—diagnosis, verification, integration, skilled intervention, trust, ownership, taste, and cross-domain judgment—show rapid broad substitution with no corresponding creation of higher-level work.

## 20 — Ubiquitous

**Evidence burden:** Reconcile the whole argument: rising floor, compressed scarcity, harnessed execution, physical complementarity, cross-domain learning, and distribution of gains.

**Core sources:** `anthropic-2025-economic-index`, `anthropic-2026-economic-index`, `anthropic-2026-cadences`, `ilo-2025-genai-jobs`, `acemoglu-autor-johnson-2026`, `acemoglu-restrepo-2021-wages`.

**Caution:** Ubiquity is a direction, not a claim that all workers or regions receive equal access, reliability, infrastructure, bargaining power, or benefit.

**What would make this false?** AI remains a bounded specialist application rather than becoming a general layer across ordinary software and work, or its economic impact proves concentrated enough that the proposed labor-market reorganization does not generalize.
