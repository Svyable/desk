# Release review — 2026-09-04

## Scope

This review checks whether evidence published or materially updated after the first *Ubiquitous* release changes the book’s claims about AI harnesses, durable expertise, apprenticeship, and labor-market adaptation.

The review is intentionally narrow. The September 1 release review already refreshed the 2025–2035 BLS occupational projections, Anthropic’s June 2026 Economic Index work, the June 2026 agentic-coding expertise study, and the August 2026 retraining evidence.

## What changed

The clearest new movement is at the harness and interface layer.

Anthropic’s current interactive-connector documentation describes connected services that can render live, interactive application surfaces directly inside Claude conversations. Its current plugin documentation describes packages that can combine skills, connectors, and sub-agents into a ready-made role or company workflow. These product changes strengthen Chapter 3’s architectural claim: routine interface knowledge is increasingly being absorbed into model-and-tool harnesses rather than remaining a distinct source of occupational scarcity.

OpenAI’s June 25, 2026 economic research on agents provides a second, product-independent signal. It describes agentic work as delegated, long-horizon execution involving tool calls and environment interaction, and reports use spreading inside OpenAI beyond software engineering into nontechnical functions including Legal and Recruiting. The durable point is not a vendor comparison. It is the migration from one-shot assistance toward delegated execution across departmental boundaries.

## Labor and apprenticeship cross-check

No newer Anthropic Economic Index report has displaced the June 26, 2026 *Cadences* report as of this review. The June 16 agentic-coding study therefore remains the strongest directly relevant usage evidence in the ledger for the book’s claim that AI can lower technical entry barriers while preserving returns to domain expertise.

That study analyzed roughly 400,000 Claude Code sessions. People outside software occupations achieved coding-task success rates close to software engineers on average, while greater domain expertise remained associated with higher verified success and more effective delegation. This continues to support the book’s distinction between cheaper execution and scarce judgment.

Anthropic’s August 12 review of 56 randomized U.S. job-training studies remains the newest directly relevant retraining evidence located for this review. Average effects were positive but modest, while some employer-linked sector programs produced larger gains that were difficult to reproduce consistently. This supports the book’s emphasis on apprenticeship tied to real employers, systems, and consequences rather than generic explanation alone.

## Policy cross-check

The OECD’s July 24, 2026 review of recent AI labor-market policy finds that governments are still using existing labor-market frameworks for many AI issues, with more targeted approaches uneven across skills and adoption, privacy, non-discrimination, occupational safety, transparency, explainability, accountability, and social dialogue.

This does not change the book’s skill thesis. It reinforces the distinction between technical capability and institutional authority: models and harnesses can move faster than the rules governing responsibility, worker rights, and acceptable delegation.

## Editorial decision

The evidence does not require rewriting the twenty core chapters.

It does justify a dated back-matter update because the product surface has moved far enough to make one of the book’s central mechanisms more visible: the harness is absorbing not only commands and tool calls but portions of the application interface and packaged role workflow.

The September 2026 update therefore adds no new protected-job list and makes no new forecast. It sharpens the existing conclusion:

- routine navigation, drafting, synthesis, coding, classification, and coordination continue moving deeper into harnesses;
- domain expertise still earns returns through planning, steering, diagnosis, recovery, and verification;
- apprenticeship remains constrained by access to consequence rather than access to explanation;
- durable value remains concentrated around ownership, real-world intervention, system integration, trust, and judgment across conflicting objectives.
