# What Would Falsify a Preferred-Partition Thesis

A thesis that cannot be falsified is not physics. It is metaphysics. The preferred-partition thesis is that the division between system and environment does physical work that is not eliminable from quantum mechanics, that this work is done by observers in a broad sense including biological and institutional systems, and that the work has measurable thermodynamic, informational, and structural consequences. If this thesis is to be taken seriously as physics rather than philosophy, it must make predictions that could be wrong.

The predictions are not about whether quantum mechanics is correct. Quantum mechanics is extraordinarily well-validated. The predictions are about whether the partition is merely descriptive or whether it is operative—whether it is bookkeeping or structure. A thesis that treats the partition as structure makes different claims than a thesis that treats it as bookkeeping. The claims are not about individual experiments. They are about the pattern of constraints, costs, and limitations that appear when partitions are manipulated, maintained, or measured. A structural thesis predicts that the partition has physics attached to it. A bookkeeping thesis predicts that the partition can be moved without physical cost as long as the formalism is applied correctly. The difference is testable.

The tests are subtle. They are not single dramatic experiments. They are cumulative patterns across many domains. The falsification of a structural partition thesis would look like the following: any combination of observations that demonstrate the partition is arbitrary in the sense that it can be freely moved without consequences, that it costs nothing to maintain, that observers play no role in its instantiation, and that the thermodynamic signature of partition maintenance is negligible or absent. These are separate tests. Each one can be evaluated independently.

The first test is arbitrariness. If the partition is merely descriptive, then moving it should not change any physical prediction. Different partitions should be equivalent in the sense that they all reproduce the same observable outcomes. This is true within the quantum formalism. Different choices of system-environment decomposition give the same predictions for measurements conditioned on the partition. But the conditioning is the key. If the partition is structural, then changing the partition changes what is measurable. A degree of freedom that is traced over in one partition is inaccessible. A degree of freedom that is retained in another partition is accessible. The accessibility is physical. It depends on the measurement apparatus, the resources available, and the state of the observer. If the partition is bookkeeping, accessibility is a matter of choice. If the partition is structural, accessibility is a matter of physics.

The difference can be tested by attempting to access traced-over degrees of freedom. In principle, any degree of freedom that has been traced over can be re-measured if the environment is accessible. In practice, the environment is almost never fully accessible. The inaccessibility is not a limitation of human capability. It is a consequence of thermodynamics. Accessing the environment requires reversing decoherence. Reversing decoherence requires controlling every degree of freedom that became entangled with the system. The control requires energy and time that grow exponentially with the size of the environment and the time since decoherence occurred. The growth is physical. It is set by the Hamiltonian and the entropy. If the growth makes access impossible within the age of the universe, the degree of freedom is operationally inaccessible. It is environment in a structural sense, not merely in a bookkeeping sense. A falsification would require showing that such degrees of freedom can be accessed routinely without exponential cost. No such demonstration exists. The absence is evidence that the partition is not arbitrary. It is constrained by physics.

The second test is cost. If the partition is bookkeeping, maintaining it should be thermodynamically free. Choosing a partition is a mathematical operation—deciding which Hilbert space factor is system and which is environment—and mathematical operations do not consume energy. But physical partitions are not mathematical choices. They are enforced by isolation, control, and measurement. Isolation requires energy to build barriers, reduce thermal coupling, and filter environmental modes. Control requires energy to perform operations, correct errors, and maintain stability. Measurement requires energy to amplify signals, record outcomes, and dissipate irreversible entropy. All of these have thermodynamic costs. The costs are measurable. A quantum computer dissipates heat. A refrigerator consumes power. A cell metabolizes nutrients. The dissipation, consumption, and metabolism are the physical signatures of partition maintenance. If these costs could be driven to zero while maintaining the partition, the partition would be bookkeeping. If the costs have a nonzero lower bound set by physical principles, the partition is structural.

The lower bounds are set by the second law of thermodynamics, the Landauer principle, and the uncertainty principle. The Landauer principle says that erasing one bit of information requires dissipating at least $k_B T \ln 2$ of energy. Measurement typically involves erasure—old information in the measuring device must be cleared to make room for new information. The erasure is irreversible. The irreversibility generates entropy. The entropy must be exported to an environment. The export requires coupling to a heat sink. The coupling is physical. It cannot be eliminated without violating the second law. A falsification would require demonstrating reversible measurement that does not generate entropy, or demonstrating partition maintenance without energy cost. Neither has been observed. The absence is evidence that the cost is structural.

The third test is the role of observers. If the partition is bookkeeping, observers are not necessary. The quantum formalism can be applied without reference to observers. The system evolves according to the Schrödinger equation. The environment is traced over as a mathematical operation. The reduced density matrix is computed. Probabilities are extracted via the Born rule. None of these steps requires an observer in the formalism. But in practice, every step involves choices made by observers. The system is defined by the observer. The environment is chosen by the observer. The measurement is performed by the observer. The probabilities are interpreted by the observer. These choices are not external to the physics. They are implemented physically. The implementation requires building apparatus, running experiments, recording data, and analyzing results. The apparatus, experiments, data, and analysis are physical processes. They are performed by observers. Observers are physical systems. Removing the observer from the account requires showing that the partition can be instantiated without any physical system playing the observer's role. No such demonstration exists. The partition always involves a physical system that establishes correlations, records information, and dissipates entropy. That system is the observer in the operational sense. A falsification would require showing that partitions can exist and be maintained without any such system. This would require a formulation of quantum mechanics that generates definite outcomes from unitary evolution alone, without measurement or environmental coupling. No such formulation has been found. The absence is evidence that observers are not eliminable. They are structural.

The fourth test is thermodynamic signature. If the partition is bookkeeping, the entropy changes associated with tracing over the environment should be purely formal. They should not correspond to physical entropy increases in any measurable system. If the partition is structural, the entropy changes should be physical. They should correspond to heat dissipation, irreversible processes, and increasing disorder in the environment. The difference is measurable. Physical entropy is extensive. It scales with the size of the system. It is associated with temperature, heat flow, and dissipation. Formal entropy is a property of the quantum state. It can be calculated from the density matrix. The two kinds of entropy are related but not identical. A pure state has zero formal entropy but can have nonzero physical entropy if it is entangled with an environment. The relationship between the two is mediated by decoherence. Decoherence transfers information from system to environment. The transfer is irreversible. Irreversibility increases physical entropy. The increase is measurable. It appears as heat. Heat is energy in disordered form. Disorder is physical entropy. A falsification would require showing that decoherence occurs without any corresponding increase in physical entropy. This would violate the second law. The second law has never been violated in a controlled experiment. The absence of violation is evidence that the entropy associated with decoherence is physical, not merely formal.

The fifth test is uniqueness. If the partition is bookkeeping, there should be many equivalent ways to partition the Hilbert space, all giving the same predictions. If the partition is structural, there should be a preferred partition or a narrow class of viable partitions for any given physical situation. The preference is determined by the Hamiltonian, the environment, and the measurement strategy. Change any of these and the preferred partition changes. But for a fixed setup, the partition should be approximately unique. The uniqueness is testable. It corresponds to the stability of decoherence basis and the consistency of measurement outcomes. Different experimental setups that nominally measure the same observable should use partitions that give consistent results. If the partitions are arbitrary, different setups could give inconsistent results depending on how the partition is drawn. If the partitions are structural, different setups should converge on compatible partitions. The convergence is observed in practice. Position measurements consistently decohere in the position basis. Energy measurements consistently decohere in the energy basis. The consistency is not arbitrary. It is determined by the interaction Hamiltonians. The determination is physical. A falsification would require showing that the preferred basis is arbitrary or that different choices give contradictory results. No such contradiction has been found. The absence is evidence that the preferred basis is not arbitrary. It is structural.

The sixth test is scale. If the partition is bookkeeping, it should apply equally at all scales. The formalism is scale-independent. A qubit and a galaxy are both quantum systems. They are both described by wavefunctions. They are both subject to the Schrödinger equation. But in practice, partitions are scale-dependent. Microscopic systems are treated as quantum. Macroscopic systems are treated as classical. The transition is not sharp. It is not at a fixed size or energy. It is determined by the decoherence rate, which depends on environmental coupling. Environmental coupling scales with system size, temperature, and the density of environmental degrees of freedom. The scaling is physical. It predicts that maintaining quantum coherence becomes harder as systems grow larger, hotter, or more strongly coupled to their surroundings. The prediction is validated. Large molecules decohere faster than small ones. Hot systems decohere faster than cold ones. Strongly coupled systems decohere faster than weakly coupled ones. The rates are quantitative. They match the predictions of decoherence theory. A falsification would require showing that large, hot, strongly coupled systems can maintain coherence as easily as small, cold, weakly coupled systems. No such demonstration exists. The absence is evidence that the partition is scale-dependent. Scale dependence is physical. It is not bookkeeping.

The seventh test is recoverability. If the partition is bookkeeping, information traced over the environment should be recoverable in principle. The total state is pure if the initial state was pure. Unitary evolution preserves purity. Information is conserved. Recovering the information should be possible with sufficient control over the environment. If the partition is structural, recovery should become impossible in practice beyond a certain scale. The scale is set by the thermodynamic cost of accessing the environment. The cost grows exponentially. Exponential growth makes recovery infeasible beyond microscopic scales and short timescales. The infeasibility is testable. It corresponds to the irreversibility of macroscopic measurements. Macroscopic measurements produce permanent records. The records cannot be undone without enormous energy expenditure. The expenditure is prohibitive. A falsification would require demonstrating that macroscopic measurements can be reversed routinely. Quantum erasers demonstrate reversal at microscopic scales. No experiment has demonstrated reversal at macroscopic scales. The absence is evidence that macroscopic measurements are irreversible. Irreversibility is physical. It is not bookkeeping.

The eighth test is interpretation-independence. If the partition is bookkeeping, its significance should depend on interpretation. Many-worlds treats branches as real. Copenhagen treats outcomes as unique. QBism treats states as personal. The interpretations differ on what the partition means. But they agree on how the partition is used. All interpretations trace over the environment. All compute reduced density matrices. All apply the Born rule. The procedures are interpretation-independent. If the partition is structural, its physical consequences should also be interpretation-independent. The thermodynamic cost of maintaining a partition should be the same regardless of whether the physicist believes in many worlds or Copenhagen. The entropy dissipated during measurement should not depend on whether the quantum state is considered objective or subjective. These predictions are testable. They correspond to the universality of thermodynamic constraints. Thermodynamics does not care about interpretation. A falsification would require showing that thermodynamic costs depend on interpretation. No such dependence has been observed. The absence is evidence that the partition's physical aspects are interpretation-independent. Interpretation-independence is evidence of structure.

These eight tests are not exhaustive. They are representative. Each test can be refined into specific experimental questions. The refinement is ongoing. It is part of the research program in quantum foundations, quantum information, and quantum thermodynamics. The program is not complete. Some tests have been performed. Others are conceptual. None has falsified the structural partition thesis. The absence of falsification is not proof. It is evidence. Evidence accumulates. Accumulation builds confidence. Confidence is not certainty. Certainty is not available in empirical science. Science proceeds by testing, refining, and testing again. The preferred-partition thesis is subject to this process. If it survives continued testing, it becomes part of the working understanding of quantum mechanics. If it fails a test, it is revised or discarded. The process is standard. It is how physics works. The preferred partition is not a metaphysical claim. It is a physical hypothesis. It makes predictions. The predictions are testable. The tests are ongoing. The outcome is uncertain. That uncertainty is the hallmark of a scientific claim. The claim is that the partition is structural. The structure has consequences. The consequences are measurable.
