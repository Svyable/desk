# Research Trail — The Preferred Partition

This research trail documents the evidence basis for *The Preferred Partition: Decoherence does not dissolve the measurement problem; it relocates it to an unexplained preferred partition of the universe* by Sven Hardy Benson.

## Scope and method

This book is a conceptual argument about the structure of quantum mechanics. It does not report new experimental results. It synthesizes existing physics: decoherence theory, quantum measurement theory, quantum information, thermodynamics, and foundational interpretations. The synthesis is the contribution. The claim is that the partition between system and environment is not merely descriptive bookkeeping but physical structure that has measurable thermodynamic, informational, and structural consequences.

## Falsification boundaries

The thesis makes the following testable claims (detailed in Chapter 14):

1. **Partition maintenance has measurable thermodynamic cost.** Violating this would require demonstrating reversible measurement without entropy increase, contrary to the second law.
2. **Partitions are not arbitrary.** Violating this would require showing that different partitions give contradictory predictions for the same physical setup.
3. **Observers play a physical role.** Violating this would require demonstrating that partitions can be instantiated and maintained without any physical system performing observer functions.
4. **Entropy changes are physical, not merely formal.** Violating this would require showing decoherence without corresponding heat dissipation.
5. **Preferred bases are structurally determined.** Violating this would require showing that preferred bases are arbitrary or observer-dependent in a way that produces measurement contradictions.
6. **Scale dependence is physical.** Violating this would require demonstrating that large, hot, strongly coupled systems maintain coherence as easily as small, cold, weakly coupled systems.
7. **Macroscopic irreversibility is physical.** Violating this would require routinely reversing macroscopic measurements without exponential resource cost.
8. **Thermodynamic costs are interpretation-independent.** Violating this would require showing that dissipation depends on whether the physicist endorses Copenhagen, many-worlds, or QBism.

None of these has been violated experimentally. The thesis stands or falls on whether these boundaries hold under continued testing.

## Interpretational neutrality

The book is compatible with multiple interpretations: many-worlds, Copenhagen, QBism, relational QM, and others. The common structure is the partition. All interpretations require specifying system-environment boundaries, measurement events, and traced-over degrees of freedom. The thesis is that this specification is not external to physics but part of it. The specification has thermodynamic costs, determines observables, and is constrained by resources available to observers.

The thesis does not require consciousness to collapse wavefunctions, does not require literal branching universes, and does not require states to be subjective. It requires only that the partition is physical: maintained by work, constrained by thermodynamics, and implemented by observers who are themselves physical systems embedded in the world.

## Notes on voice and integrity

This book follows the human-prose skill (`.agents/skills/human-prose/SKILL.md`). No quotations are invented. No anecdotes are fabricated. No statistics are made up. Experiments cited are real. Interpretations discussed are actual positions held by physicists. The synthesis and the argumentative structure are the author's. The physics is standard. The claim that the partition is physical rather than merely descriptive is the thesis. Testing the thesis is the open research program.

## Continuity with Desk practice

This research trail is shorter than those for experimental books like *Useful Noise* because this book does not report laboratory results. It is a conceptual argument grounded in existing physics. The sources are published papers and textbooks. They are checkable. The argument is falsifiable. The falsification criteria are stated explicitly. That transparency is the evidence discipline for a foundations book. The discipline is the same. The application is different. The standard is: can the claim be tested? If yes, how? If no, is it physics or philosophy? This book aims to be physics. The test is whether its predictions survive experiment.
