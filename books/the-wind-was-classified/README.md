## Thesis

Between 1949 and 1969 the United States conducted a large open-air biological-warfare vulnerability program in which cities, transit systems, military installations, roads, coastlines, and buildings became instruments for measuring aerosol and microbial dispersion. The central historical problem is not solved by calling these releases either harmless tests or covert poisoning. The useful scientific result and the ethical breach came from the same design choice: the experiments had to occur in real inhabited environments to answer the questions planners cared about, while the people inhabiting those environments were denied the information required to consent, object, protect themselves, or even recognize an unusual exposure.
