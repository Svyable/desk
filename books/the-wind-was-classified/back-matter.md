# Back Matter

The central claim of this book is narrower than the mythology surrounding Cold War biological testing and stronger because of that narrowness.

The United States conducted secret open-air biological-warfare vulnerability tests in populated environments. The program used microbial simulants, fluorescent particles, and other surrogates to study dispersion and vulnerability. These experiments produced real technical knowledge. They also relied on public nonawareness as an experimental condition. Some materials later appeared less benign than contemporary classifications suggested. Health claims arising from particular releases vary sharply in evidentiary strength.

The book therefore refuses two easy endings. It does not describe every simulant release as a covert lethal attack, and it does not accept the historical label harmless as a timeless scientific fact.

## Evidence classes

**1. Verified / declassified / documented facts.** The 1942–1969 U.S. biological warfare program; the 1950 San Francisco Sea-Spray release; use of *Serratia marcescens* and *Bacillus globigii* as simulants; 1966 New York subway dissemination tests; fluorescent-particle field tests; military mosquito-dispersal experiments; controlled Fort Detrick aerosol and volunteer research; Nixon's 1969 renunciation of offensive biological weapons; the 1977 Senate hearings and Army history; later disclosure of Project SHAD/Project 112.

**2. Credible but incomplete reporting or inference.** Population-scale exposure estimates derived from environmental recovery; exact individual inhaled doses; health-risk reconstruction of historical tracer quantities; completeness of surviving program inventories; causal influence of particular field results on later doctrine.

**3. Disputed allegations.** That Sea-Spray caused the 1950–51 Stanford *Serratia* cluster or Edward Nevin's death; that particular tracer programs produced specific later chronic diseases without individual exposure evidence; claims that all open-air tests used actual pathogens.

**4. Speculative synthesis / open conjecture.** Long-term ecological persistence of released lineages in clinically meaningful form; undisclosed programs inferred only from the existence of known compartments; broader claims that covert testing created durable regional disease patterns absent molecular or epidemiological evidence.

## Defeat conditions

The book's thesis would need substantial revision if primary records showed that the major public simulant releases were openly disclosed in advance to affected communities; if the relevant programs had robust independent public-health surveillance capable of resolving adverse events contemporaneously; or if records showed that population ignorance was irrelevant to experimental realism and secrecy served only unrelated administrative convenience.

A stronger causal claim about Sea-Spray and the Stanford cluster would be justified by preserved isolates or other evidence capable of demonstrating a specific lineage connection. A stronger exculpatory claim would require evidence showing the clinical strains were unrelated to the released strain or that exposure at the relevant patients' locations was implausible.

## Next research program

The highest-value work is document-level extraction of the 1977 Army volumes, a test-by-test matrix of the 239 open-air tests rather than repetition of the headline number, full reconstruction of the 1966 subway sampling geometry, toxicological re-analysis of zinc cadmium sulfide formulations and doses, primary-source reconstruction of mosquito tests, and a legally precise review of *Nevin v. United States*. A second pass should compare Cold War governance with modern urban tracer experiments and biodefense exercises.
