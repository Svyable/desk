- *Nevin v. United States*, 696 F.2d 1229 (9th Cir. 1983). Primary legal anchor for the Sea-Spray wrongful-death litigation and the government's planning/safety-decision record. The appellate majority held that the bacterial-strain/site decision fell within the FTCA discretionary-function exception and ordered dismissal for lack of subject-matter jurisdiction. Judge Norris's dissent would have found jurisdiction but affirmed the district court's factual finding that the released bacteria did not cause Nevin's death. Do not summarize the appellate case as a merits holding that scientifically proved or disproved causation. https://openjurist.org/696/f2d/1229/nevin-v-united-states

## Sea-Spray / Stanford claim boundaries

- The release happened, the Stanford cluster happened, and *S. marcescens* is capable of serious opportunistic infection.
- The clinical isolates were not preserved for modern strain-level comparison with the release organism; the surviving record identified so far therefore cannot establish a genomic chain from military batch to patient.
- The 1951 cluster involved medically vulnerable/instrumented patients and remains compatible with nosocomial acquisition; modern *Serratia* outbreak literature makes this a substantive alternative, not a generic caveat.
- The Ninth Circuit majority's 1983 ruling is jurisdictional, based on the FTCA discretionary-function exception. The district court also made a no-causation factual finding, but the appellate majority did not need to endorse that finding to dispose of the case.
- The court record shows that safety was considered at a high level. The manuscript should therefore criticize the adequacy/testability of the safety model and the absence of integrated health surveillance, not claim that decision-makers performed no safety assessment.

## Research cautions

- The 239-test figure refers to the Army's broad open-air testing inventory and should not be rewritten as 239 releases of pathogenic organisms over civilians.
- Sea-Spray exposure estimates are model/recovery-derived population estimates, not individual dosimetry.
- The Stanford cluster and Nevin death are temporally relevant but not molecularly linked to the released strain by surviving evidence identified so far.
- Project SHAD/112 is related through vulnerability-testing culture and disclosure history but differs materially from public-city simulant releases; do not merge their exposure categories.
- Zinc cadmium sulfide risk claims require dose- and formulation-specific treatment; the 1997 NRC review should anchor the toxicology section.
