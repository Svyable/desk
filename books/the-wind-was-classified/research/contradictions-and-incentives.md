# Contradictions, incentives, and disinformation audit

## Institutional incentives

The military had incentives to demonstrate vulnerability clearly enough to justify defensive/offensive research while minimizing the perceived health risk of simulants. Classification reduced adversary insight and public interference but also insulated safety assumptions from external challenge.

Congressional investigators in the 1970s had incentives to surface abuse after Watergate-era revelations. That does not invalidate hearing evidence, but dramatic aggregate numbers such as 239 tests can obscure differences among materials and settings.

Plaintiffs and families harmed by unexplained illness had understandable incentives to connect newly revealed exposures to medical events. Temporal proximity deserves investigation but is not a substitute for strain-level causation.

Agencies defending historical programs had incentives to emphasize contemporaneous safety judgments and lack of proven injury. Lack of proof after delayed disclosure should not automatically be treated as proof of no effect.

## Contradictions to preserve

- Sea-Spray could have been technically informative and ethically unacceptable.
- *Serratia* could have been reasonably considered low-risk in 1950 and still have represented a real opportunistic hazard not appreciated at population scale.
- The Nevin causal claim can remain unproved while secrecy itself still constitutes a serious governance failure.
- The 239-test inventory can be genuinely large without representing 239 pathogen attacks on civilians.
- Zinc cadmium sulfide can contain toxic elements while historical exposure estimates remain below expected harmful doses in major reviews.
- Human volunteer research at Fort Detrick can have stronger consent procedures than lower-risk public environmental testing without making either program automatically ethical or unethical.
- Project SHAD's delayed disclosure supports caution about claims of archival completeness but does not validate unspecified hidden-program allegations.

## Known uncertainty / misinformation channels

Popular accounts frequently merge Army, Navy, CIA, and Project SHAD activities. They also merge live pathogens, microbial simulants, and chemical/particulate tracers. Future revisions should keep test IDs, sponsoring organization, material, location, purpose, and evidence of exposure in a single test matrix to prevent narrative contamination.
