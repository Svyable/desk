# INPUT FAMINE

## What the brain manufactures when the world goes quiet

**Sven Hardy Benson**

Status: working draft

## Reader promise

Cold War intelligence agencies became interested in isolation, sensory deprivation, drugs, hypnosis and other ways of changing human cognition. At the same time, experimental psychologists were discovering something less cinematic and more durable: when patterned sensory input is reduced, perception does not simply switch off. People can experience imagery, distortions, altered time, cognitive impairment and hallucination-like phenomena.

INPUT FAMINE follows the collision between classified behavior-control research and the ordinary neuroscience of a prediction-making brain. Its governing question is deliberately narrower than "mind control": **when the external evidence stream becomes poor, what does the nervous system manufacture from priors, noise, memory and expectation—and how easily can an interrogator, experimenter, experiencer or later historian misattribute the source?**

The book does not treat MKULTRA funding as evidence that coercive reprogramming worked. It does not treat hallucination as proof of external stimulus, psi, implanted information or manipulation. It separates the documented abuses and research programs from their technical efficacy, and the existence of altered perception from the ontology assigned to the percept.

## Chapter progression

1. **The Room Goes Quiet** — deprivation does not produce nothing.
2. **Unpatterned Light** — the laboratory technology of reducing information without removing sensation.
3. **The Brain Hates a Vacuum** — imagery, pseudo-hallucination and internally generated structure.
4. **Criteria** — why early researchers fought over what counted as a hallucination.
5. **Fourteen Days** — EEG, motivation and the physiology of prolonged perceptual deprivation.
6. **BLUEBIRD** — intelligence interest before MKULTRA.
7. **ARTICHOKE** — interrogation, hypnosis, drugs and the fantasy of reliable control.
8. **MKULTRA** — what the surviving records establish and what document destruction prevents us from claiming.
9. **The Cutout** — universities, foundations, hospitals and unequal knowledge.
10. **Montreal** — Cameron, depatterning and the difference between harming a mind and rewriting one.
11. **Unwitting** — consent failure as an epistemic failure, not only an ethical one.
12. **Ganzfeld** — homogeneous stimulation and perception at low external information.
13. **The Source-Monitoring Problem** — a real experience can have the wrong attributed cause.
14. **Interrogation Is Not Measurement** — compliance, confabulation, suggestibility and contaminated evidence.
15. **The Weapon That Wasn't** — program interest versus demonstrated controllable capability.
16. **Anomalous Experience** — UAP, apparitions, psi and other reports under conditions where perception must be reconstructed carefully.
17. **Input Famine** — a framework for separating experience, stimulus and explanation.
18. **Turn the World Back On** — what returns when patterned evidence returns.
