# Working notes: False Eye

## Thesis

Project STORMFURY is a case of causal attribution failing in a naturally self-mimicking system. Several seeded hurricanes displayed wind reductions and structural changes compatible with the intervention theory, but later observations showed both that hurricanes often form outer eyewalls naturally and that their clouds generally contain less supercooled liquid water than the silver-iodide mechanism required.

## Four evidence classes

### 1. Verified/declassified/documented facts

- Hurricane seeding preceded STORMFURY in Project Cirrus.
- Esther was seeded in 1961; STORMFURY was formally organized in 1962.
- Beulah, Debbie and Ginger were later seeded; four hurricanes were modified on eight days according to NOAA's historical summary.
- On four experimental days reported winds fell roughly 10–30 percent.
- The 1969 Debbie experiments were regarded as the project's most encouraging.
- Ginger in 1971 was the last hurricane seeded under the program.
- Later observations established natural concentric-eyewall/eyewall-replacement behavior in unseeded hurricanes.
- Later cloud measurements showed insufficient supercooled liquid water for the original seeding concept to be reliably effective.
- Project STORMFURY ended in 1983; a major scientific retrospective appeared in 1985.

### 2. Credible but incomplete/model-dependent

- Exact causal effect, if any, of seeding on individual experimental days.
- Magnitude and uncertainty of historical maximum-wind changes reconstructed from aircraft sampling.
- Whether particular failed days reflect bad targeting, inadequate seeding, unsuitable microphysics, or no treatment effect.
- How much the intervention could have accelerated a naturally developing eyewall replacement cycle.

### 3. Disputed/not independently established

- Project Cirrus caused the 1947 hurricane's track reversal.
- STORMFURY demonstrated a reliable operational capability to weaken hurricanes.
- Later destructive hurricanes were secretly steered or weakened with mature descendants of STORMFURY technology.
- Silver iodide supplied storm-scale energy rather than attempting to perturb energy already in the system.

### 4. Speculative synthesis/open conjecture

- STORMFURY is a particularly clean model of an intervention whose expected endpoint is endogenously generated by the untreated system.
- The program's most durable contribution may be methodological: an intervention effort improved observation until the observation system became the strongest argument against the intervention.
- Similar causal traps may occur in medicine, ecology, economics and other systems with strong internal dynamics, but those analogies are explanatory rather than evidence about hurricanes.

## Contradictions and incentives

Program scientists had incentives to interpret favorable experimental responses as evidence worth continued testing, but also professional incentives to improve hurricane science independent of modification. Public agencies faced safety and political constraints after the 1947 Cirrus controversy. Retrospective NOAA accounts have institutional proximity to the program but are unusually explicit about its failure; primary experiment papers and archived correspondence remain necessary for a publication-grade reconstruction.

Conspiracy narratives have the opposite incentive structure: the documented existence of a government program can be used to support later undocumented claims. The manuscript must never let proof of experimentation stand in for proof of capability.

## Chronology

- 1947: Project Cirrus seeds a hurricane with dry ice; subsequent track change becomes politically controversial.
- 1950s: expanded federal hurricane research follows destructive U.S. hurricane seasons.
- 1961: Hurricane Esther seeded by Navy/Weather Bureau aircraft.
- 1962: Project STORMFURY formally organized.
- 1963: Hurricane Beulah experiments.
- 1969: extensive Hurricane Debbie experiments produce the most encouraging apparent results.
- 1971: Hurricane Ginger becomes the last seeded hurricane.
- 1970s–early 1980s: observing emphasis increasingly exposes natural variability and cloud-microphysics limitations.
- 1983: STORMFURY ends.
- 1985: Willoughby, Jorgensen, Black and Rosenthal publish the scientific chronicle reassessing the program.

## Defeat conditions

The book's central synthesis should be narrowed or abandoned if primary experiment records show that the major apparent successes included independent treatment tracers or cloud-scale mediator measurements that make natural eyewall replacement an implausible explanation; if later reanalyses demonstrate a statistically robust seeded-versus-unseeded effect after accounting for natural eyewall cycles; or if modern microphysical work establishes that the historical storms likely possessed enough supercooled water for the claimed seeding mechanism at the relevant locations and times.
