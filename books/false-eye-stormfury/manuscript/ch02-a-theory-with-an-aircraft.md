# A Theory With an Aircraft

Weather modification becomes less ridiculous the closer one gets to the actual proposal. STORMFURY was not an attempt to push a hurricane away with explosives or supply energy comparable to the storm. It was a cloud-physics intervention: find a phase transition already poised to happen, seed it, and let the hurricane's own circulation amplify the consequence.

Silver iodide was attractive because its crystal structure can act as an ice nucleus at subfreezing temperatures. In a cloud containing supercooled liquid water, introducing suitable nuclei can change when and where droplets freeze. The release of latent heat was the intended lever. The lever was small. The atmospheric machine around it was not.

The theory therefore depended on geography inside the storm. Seeding the eye would not do. Seeding arbitrary rainbands would not necessarily do. The target was convection outside the main eyewall, where induced growth might establish a competing ring. If that new ring consolidated farther from the center, angular-momentum arguments suggested that the maximum tangential wind could decline as the radius of maximum wind expanded.

This was why aircraft mattered as more than delivery trucks. A hurricane modification experiment required reconnaissance, targeting, seeding, repeated wind measurements, radar structure, thermodynamic sampling, and enough temporal coverage to say what changed. The project inherited and accelerated an airborne research culture in which the storm itself was repeatedly penetrated rather than inferred only from shore stations and ship reports.

The machinery also exposed the central weakness. A clean laboratory experiment can often isolate a variable by holding other things still. STORMFURY could not hold the sea-surface fluxes still, could not freeze environmental shear, could not replay yesterday's hurricane without seeding, and could not ask the same storm to split into treatment and control copies. Every flight produced more knowledge and another reminder that the object being manipulated was also moving through its own life history.

The program's safety criteria made the statistical problem worse. Candidate storms had to be intense enough to possess the desired structure, reachable by aircraft, and sufficiently unlikely to threaten land during the experiment. Those are sensible ethical constraints. They also make eligible cases rare. A field that needs many replicates can become starved by the very precautions that make the experiment acceptable.

The aircraft gave STORMFURY an experimental hand. They never gave it a second hurricane to use as a counterfactual.
