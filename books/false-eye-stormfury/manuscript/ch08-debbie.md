# Debbie

Hurricane Debbie in August 1969 became STORMFURY's best-known apparent success. The project seeded the storm on August 18 and again on August 20. NOAA's later histories describe the operation as the program's most extensive experiment and the one that produced its most encouraging results.

Reported maximum winds fell after the seedings. The storm's eye and wind structure changed in ways that researchers considered compatible with the hypothesis. If STORMFURY needed one case to persuade a skeptical observer that something interesting might be happening, Debbie was it.

The timing was almost perversely good for belief. The project had spent years refining aircraft, instrumentation and procedures. A mature experiment finally found a suitable hurricane and obtained a response that resembled the predicted one. The result did not look like a random anecdote. It looked like a mechanism becoming visible.

Yet Debbie is precisely where hindsight should be most disciplined. Later knowledge does not permit us to say the measurements were fake. Nor does it permit us to say the scientists were irrational to take them seriously. It changes the set of plausible causes.

Once natural concentric eyewall cycles were recognized as common features of intense hurricanes, Debbie's outward structural change ceased to be diagnostic. A process that had been interpreted as the storm obeying the intervention could occur without intervention. At the same time, improved cloud measurements weakened confidence that silver iodide had enough supercooled liquid water to exploit.

The old data therefore survived into a new causal world. That is rare enough to notice. Usually a famous experimental claim collapses because the measurement cannot be reproduced, the instrument was wrong, or the statistical analysis fails. Debbie is different. The observed storm evolution remained meteorologically plausible. What disappeared was the exclusivity of the explanation.

The case is a warning against asking only whether a treatment produced the predicted outcome. A good causal test also asks how often the untreated system produces that outcome, by what mechanisms, and on what timescale. STORMFURY learned that denominator late.

Debbie looked like evidence because the hurricane did what the theory said a seeded hurricane should do. Later science discovered that this was also what a hurricane sometimes does when nobody is trying to teach it anything.
