# Intervention Without Attribution

STORMFURY failed at a task that sounds simpler than changing a hurricane: proving that a change belonged to the intervention.

The program's difficulty can be stated without meteorological romance. The treatment was physically plausible but weak. The outcome was valuable but naturally variable. Eligible experiments were rare. The causal signature was later discovered to occur spontaneously. Measurements along the proposed mechanism were incomplete. Under those conditions, a sequence of apparent successes can remain permanently ambiguous.

This is not an argument against intervention. It is an argument for designing interventions around attribution as deliberately as around effect. A treatment that cannot be distinguished from endogenous variation may be operationally useful if repeated experience eventually establishes its value, but it will be scientifically difficult to validate and politically difficult to govern.

Hurricane modification adds another complication: the cost of a false positive and the cost of a false negative differ. A false positive can produce confidence in an ineffective intervention and perhaps encourage risky deployment. A false negative can discard a real method that reduces damage. Scientists therefore need evidence strong enough to survive both desires: the desire to do something about catastrophic storms and the desire not to mistake activity for control.

The modern lesson is not that complex systems should be left alone. Medicine, ecology, climate adaptation, finance and engineering all intervene in systems with noisy baselines. The lesson is to ask what observation would remain specific to the intervention after the natural system has been watched long enough.

STORMFURY chose outer-eyewall formation because it was mechanistically meaningful. It later turned out not to be specific. That is the cruelest possible outcome for an experiment: the endpoint made sense, occurred at the right time, and still could not identify the cause.

A better future weather-modification program, if one becomes physically plausible, will need more than a modeled effect. It will need predeclared causal intermediates, independent measurements, untreated baselines, explicit stopping rules and an account of natural processes capable of producing the same response.

Otherwise the atmosphere can pass the experiment on our behalf.
