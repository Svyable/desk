# The Missing Control Storm

A hurricane cannot be cloned, and that simple fact sits underneath much of STORMFURY's trouble.

The ideal counterfactual would be the same hurricane in the same ocean and atmosphere, observed over the same hours, once with silver iodide and once without it. There is no such object. Every substitute differs in some way. Another storm has a different vortex, environment and history. A later period in the same storm inherits what happened before. A before-and-after comparison confounds treatment with natural evolution.

Researchers can compensate with repeated cases, statistical models and carefully chosen controls, but only if they know the distribution of untreated behavior well enough. STORMFURY began before the natural frequency of concentric eyewall cycles was properly appreciated. That made its implicit control model too narrow.

The difficulty resembles medicine before the natural history of a disease is understood. A patient improves after treatment. That observation matters. But without knowing how often comparable patients improve untreated, and on what schedule, the treatment effect remains uncertain. In a hurricane, the patient is also changing shape while flying across the ocean.

The program experimented with timing and operational comparisons, but scarcity remained brutal. Only four hurricanes were seeded over the program's life. Safety rules and logistics prevented casual repetition. Even if every seeded storm had weakened, four storms would still leave large uncertainty about mechanism and generality.

This is where retrospective ridicule misses the point. The control problem was not hidden from competent scientists; it was structurally difficult. The mistake was subtler: the field's model of untreated hurricane behavior was incomplete enough that a natural process could occupy the exact evidentiary slot reserved for a treatment response.

Once natural eyewall replacement cycles became better documented, old experiments acquired a new control group—not a set of paired storms, but a body of observations showing that unseeded hurricanes routinely reorganized in similar ways. The baseline arrived after the intervention program.

Science often imagines controls as something designed before an experiment. Sometimes an entire later observing era becomes the control the earlier experiment did not know it needed.
