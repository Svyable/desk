*The Most Important Athlete of Every Decade — and the Stories That Made a Nation*

Who was the most important American sports player of each decade across the country’s first 250 years?

Not the best. Not the statistical GOAT transported through a time machine. The most important: the athlete who most changed the sport, the culture around it, or the range of possibilities available to the athletes who followed.

*America, Played* begins before baseball, before modern leagues, and before the United States had finished becoming the United States. Its first selection is Austin Curtis, an enslaved Black jockey whose career crosses the 1770s, 1780s, and 1790s. It ends, provisionally, with Simone Biles and a new form of athlete sovereignty: the right to stop an unsafe performance, return on the athlete’s own terms, and remain great.

Across the sequence, a second history emerges beneath the decade picks: **who owns the athlete?** The answer moves from literal human ownership through labor control, exclusion, amateurism, state claims, media, endorsement, collective organization, athlete branding, business ownership, and bodily autonomy.

The 2020s selection remains explicitly provisional until the decade is complete. Early-decade choices carry explicit archival uncertainty where records do not support modern statistical confidence. Famous stories that are cleaner than the surviving evidence are labeled rather than improved into fiction.

## Editorial principle

The scoreboard can prove performance. It cannot force society to accept what the performance means.

This book therefore treats the surrounding reaction—rules, access, media, labor, race, gender, business, law, politics, and memory—as part of sports history rather than contamination of it. Where the archive is thin, the prose says so. Where a famous story is disputed, the dispute stays visible. Where a decade has a serious challenger, the challenger is named.
