# Decade board — America, Played

This is the canonical selection matrix for the book. It records the current pick, the strongest challenger considered in the drafting pass, and the confidence level of the historical judgment.

Confidence is about the **selection**, not whether the athlete existed or mattered. A lower-confidence decade usually means the archive is thinner, the sporting landscape is more fragmented, or a rival case is unusually strong.

The governing test is:

1. **Competitive authority** — was the athlete demonstrably elite in the sporting world available to them?
2. **Institutional effect** — did rules, leagues, tours, labor arrangements, access, or organizational behavior change around the athlete?
3. **Cultural reach** — did the athlete become legible beyond the immediate contest?
4. **Downstream inheritance** — did later athletes operate in a meaningfully different landscape?
5. **Decade fit** — is the effect distinctive to this decade rather than simply borrowed from a better fit in an adjacent one?

No single factor decides the selection mechanically.

## Why Austin Curtis appears three times

The repetition is intentional.

The earliest decades have thinner records than the modern era, and forcing three different names into the 1770s, 1780s, and 1790s would create false precision. Curtis’s documented arc changes meaning across the three decades: enslaved elite athlete; Revolutionary-era horseman whose service is remembered; then a free horseman after the 1791 emancipation act.

A future archival discovery could change one or more of those selections. The book should welcome that correction rather than protect visual neatness.

## Why Robinson is the 1950s pick rather than the 1940s

The first Major League game is April 15, 1947, but importance is not identical to first occurrence.

The 1950s show the change becoming institutionally irreversible. Robinson becomes an established star, other Black stars reshape competitive quality, delayed clubs incur an increasingly obvious talent cost, and Robinson’s public role expands beyond the initial restraint required by the integration strategy.

Joe Louis better captures the distinctive sports politics of the 1940s: war, anti-fascism, national unity, military segregation, and Black patriotic symbolism.

## Why Ali is not repeated in the 1970s

Ali’s 1970s fights are among the greatest events in sports history, and a pure boxing-history list could reasonably give him both decades.

The book instead gives the 1960s to the transformation of athlete citizenship and the 1970s to Billie Jean King’s transformation of women’s sports institutions and bargaining power. The aim is not artificial diversity; it is to identify the most consequential mechanism distinctive to each decade.

## Why Jordan beats Tiger for the 1990s

Tiger’s 1997 Masters victory is already transformative. Jordan still owns the decade because his six championships, the 1992 Dream Team, the NBA’s international growth, Air Jordan’s product ecosystem, and the conversion of an athlete into global intellectual property are broader across the full 1990s.

Tiger’s most complete decade-level case is the 2000s, beginning with the 2000 U.S. Open and the Tiger Slam.

## Why Serena beats LeBron for the 2010s

LeBron James is an exceptionally strong challenger: competitive dominance, player agency, direct voice, the 2010 free-agency spectacle, athlete-owned media, investment, and political engagement all matter.

Serena narrowly wins because the decade forces more institutional categories to move simultaneously around one athlete: aging, race, gender, body politics, motherhood, health, clothing, officiating, prize history, global individual-sport longevity, business ownership, and the right to define the end of a career. The 2026 return further demonstrates that her 2022 “evolution” language was not a conventional retirement script.

## Why the 2020s must stay provisional

Any edition published before January 1, 2030 must keep the provisional label.

The decade already contains plausible eventual winners whose cases may strengthen dramatically. Ohtani can change baseball’s understanding of two-way elite value. Mahomes can extend a championship and tactical era. Clark can continue accelerating the economics of women’s basketball. Ledecky can extend unprecedented distance-swimming longevity. Someone not yet central to the book can still create the defining sports event of 2027, 2028, or 2029.

The method requires the uncertainty to remain visible.
