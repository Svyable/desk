# Chapter 9 — Twenty Seconds

A progressive collapse is terrifying partly because gravity supplies the next load.

Once a floor or connection fails, falling mass strikes what remains below. The structure is no longer carrying the loads for which it was designed; it is receiving impacts from pieces of itself. Local failure can become a cascade.

Descriptions of Sampoong often use the phrase `pancake collapse`. The image is useful but incomplete. Real debris fields are irregular. Voids survive. Columns tilt. Slabs bridge. Walls and furnishings create pockets. The same disorder that kills also creates the spaces in which some people remain alive.

The forensic investigation had to work backward from this destroyed state. Concrete and steel were sampled. Original documents were compared with actual conditions. Collapse geometry was surveyed. Competing explanations—including ground failure and explosion—had to be tested and rejected or bounded.

That process matters to the thesis. After catastrophe, institutions suddenly become hungry for information they previously tolerated being fragmented. Drawings matter. maintenance records matter. eyewitness timing matters. material strength matters. Every undocumented alteration becomes an uncertainty tax.

Sampoong's rubble was not mute. It was an archive that had been violently rearranged.
