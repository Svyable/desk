# Chapter 15 — Rescue as Engineering

The collapsed store became a temporary city with its own infrastructure.

Search teams needed lighting, lifting, shoring, medical support, communications, food, rest cycles and maps. Heavy equipment could accelerate debris removal and destroy survivable voids. Fire and leaking fluids created hazards. Crowds, media and grieving families pressed against the perimeter.

Contemporary reports count hundreds of specialist rescuers and volunteers. U.S. military support and specialized search equipment were requested. The response exposed limitations in Korea's rescue coordination, which later historical accounts link to the development of stronger centralized rescue capability.

The temptation is to narrate rescue as heroism alone. Heroism is real. Systems matter because heroism is scarce, exhaustible and difficult to coordinate.

Sampoong helped make visible that urban rescue is a technical discipline. Structural assessment, canine search, acoustic detection, confined-space access, medical triage and debris management can work against one another unless command is coherent.

The archive should allow a more exact reconstruction than English accounts usually provide. Seoul's repeated situation reports, response files and compensation records can show the operational rhythm after the cameras' first night.
