# Contradictions and Incentives

## Collapse time

Korean sources report approximately 17:52 and 17:57. Preserve the discrepancy until dispatch logs, CCTV/broadcast timestamps, emergency calls and official investigation records are compared.

## Opening / completion chronology

Sources variously describe late-1989 completion and July 1990 public opening. Do not flatten construction completion, temporary approval, formal completion inspection and retail opening into one date.

## Casualty accounting

The canonical Korean figure is 502 dead, 6 missing and 937 injured, but contemporary totals changed as bodies, hospital patients, duplicate missing reports and presumed deaths were reconciled. Use date-stamped numbers in narrative passages about the response.

## Management knowledge

Public retellings often assert that management knew collapse was imminent and personally fled. The broad failure to evacuate despite severe warning is well supported. Exact mental state and departure timing must be tied to testimony and judgments before stronger claims enter Class 1.

## Policy causation

Do not say Sampoong caused the Facilities Safety Act. The act followed Seongsu and predated Sampoong's June 1995 collapse. Sampoong intensified inspections, rescue reform and safety politics but belongs to a chain, not a single-origin myth.

## Incentive structure

- Owners faced immediate revenue loss from closure and uncertain loss from continued operation.
- Technical advisers faced limited authority if recommendations were advisory.
- Public officials faced incentives distorted by relationships, approvals and corruption allegations.
- Workers and tenants had local observations but asymmetric power.
- Customers relied heavily on the implicit safety signal of an open, licensed store.
