# Evidence Matrix

## Class 1 — Verified / documented

- Collapse on June 29, 1995 in Seocho, Seoul.
- 502 deaths, 6 missing, 937 injured in standard Korean historical accounting.
- Reinforced-concrete flat-slab structure.
- Investigation found overlapping design, construction and management failures.
- Serious pre-collapse distress was observed on the final day while lower-level business continued.
- Twenty-five defendants were prosecuted in the principal criminal proceedings.
- Municipal response generated repeated situation, casualty, missing-person and compensation records.
- Late survivors were recovered many days after collapse.

## Class 2 — Credible but incomplete

- Exact minute-by-minute final-day management chronology.
- Exact wording and strength of each expert's evacuation recommendation.
- Fine-grained history of each unauthorized or inadequately reviewed alteration.
- Individual employee attempts to warn or evacuate customers.
- Detailed void geometry and survival conditions for late survivors.

## Class 3 — Disputed / not established

- Any single-cause account of the collapse.
- Claims that rooftop air-conditioning equipment alone caused failure.
- Claims that all executives possessed identical knowledge of imminent collapse.
- Claims that Sampoong itself created the 1995 Facilities Safety Act; enactment chronology predates the collapse.
- Exact popularized casualty or compensation figures not reconciled to official records.

## Class 4 — Speculative synthesis

- Sampoong as an information-routing failure in which true warnings existed without a reliable path to emergency authority.
- Corruption as counterfeit trust: regulatory approval transmitting false safety information to the public.
- Emergency evacuation authority as a circuit breaker that should trigger before proof of imminent collapse.
- The municipal aftermath as an inverse information problem: reconstructing one event from fragmented names, records, rubble and testimony.
