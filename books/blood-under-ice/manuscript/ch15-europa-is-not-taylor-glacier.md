# Europa Is Not Taylor Glacier

Europa invites comparison because an ice shell may cover a global ocean. Blood Falls offers a terrestrial example of liquid, salts and biology associated with ice. The resemblance is useful until scale and physics are restored.

Taylor Glacier is kilometers of terrestrial ice over rock and sediment in a valley with a known geologic history. Europa's ice shell may be orders of magnitude different in thickness and thermal structure, over an ocean shaped by tidal energy, radiation chemistry and a planetary environment unlike Antarctica.

Still, Blood Falls teaches mission design. A surface deposit can be chemically transformed relative to its source. Material moving through fractures may fractionate or react. A plume or chaos-terrain deposit may not preserve ocean composition transparently. Detecting salts or organics at the surface therefore requires a transport model.

The analogue earns its value by revealing how analogy fails. If we can identify the variables that make Taylor Glacier unlike Europa, we can design measurements around those differences instead of pretending them away.
