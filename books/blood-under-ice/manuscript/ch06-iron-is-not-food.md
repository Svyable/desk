# Iron Is Not Food

Popular summaries often say that microbes at Blood Falls eat iron. It is a useful compression and a dangerous one. Iron participates in redox chemistry that organisms can exploit, but a cell cannot live on a noun. Metabolism requires coupled reactions, electron donors, electron acceptors, carbon sources, nutrients and enough free energy to maintain and reproduce cellular machinery.

Subglacial weathering can liberate iron from bedrock. Microbes can accelerate or redirect those reactions. Sulfur and iron cycles become entangled. The chemistry is not a buffet of elements but a network of oxidation states and reactions.

This matters because planetary habitability discussions frequently leap from mineral detection to metabolic possibility. Reduced iron exists, therefore iron metabolism. Sulfate exists, therefore sulfate reduction. Methane exists, therefore methanogens. Each inference is a hypothesis generator, not an organism detector.

Blood Falls is unusually useful because the chemistry and biology can be measured in the same terrestrial system. Researchers can ask whether genomic potential matches geochemical gradients, whether reaction products accumulate where predicted, whether incubations reproduce transformations, and whether alternative abiotic routes can explain the same compounds.

A good analogue is not a place that looks like another world. It is a place where we can practice being wrong in ways that another world will not forgive.
