# Release review — 2026-09-02

## Purpose

This memo freezes the factual and editorial posture of *Adjacency* for a first Shelf release. It records the claims the manuscript can responsibly make from its current source packet, the boundaries that must survive publication, and the remaining questions that belong to later editions rather than blocking this one.

## Release scope

The candidate edition is the complete 25-chapter Desk manuscript with front matter, back matter, the research brief and chapter evidence map, the source ledger, and the book-level rights metadata.

The release review does not convert the Desk copy to `Published`. Desk remains the working edition; Shelf receives a deliberate snapshot.

## Evidence posture

The book's durable claim is narrower than a triumphalist "vectors replace search" story: learned representations make fuzzy relation cheap enough to become infrastructure, expanding candidate sets while moving scarce judgment downstream toward authority, verification, experimental validation, governance, and the choice of how far from the familiar to search.

The research packet is built around primary papers and explicit counterevidence. The manuscript and back matter preserve the distinctions most important for release:

- vector similarity is not truth, probability, causality, legality, authority, or fairness;
- dense retrieval complements rather than abolishes lexical search, structured filters, databases, graphs, permissions, and source inspection;
- approximate nearest-neighbor systems trade resources and latency against recall;
- learned neighborhoods inherit the objectives, omissions, biases, and coverage of their training process;
- computational or literature-generated scientific candidates remain candidates until appropriate empirical or theoretical validation;
- recommendation and retrieval allocate attention but do not establish a person's stable preference or a source's correctness;
- retrieved memory can be stale, private, unauthorized, or wrong even when it is semantically close.

## Claims cleared for release

The first edition can responsibly argue that:

1. learned vector spaces have become a durable computational primitive for semantic retrieval, recommendation, multimodal matching, scientific literature navigation, memory retrieval, and other forms of candidate generation;
2. the usefulness of a neighborhood depends on the representation, task objective, metric, filters, reranking, and evaluation regime rather than on a universal geometry of "meaning";
3. approximate indexing and compression make large-scale search practical while introducing measurable recall, latency, memory, and failure-mode tradeoffs;
4. vector and graph representations increasingly complement one another, with fuzzy proximity and typed relation solving different problems;
5. lowering discovery costs can expand what people and systems are able to consider, but verification, authority, taste, experiment, and institutional judgment remain binding downstream constraints.

## Claims not cleared for release

The edition should not claim or imply that:

- vector databases replace relational databases or conventional search;
- embeddings are literal meaning or understanding;
- cosine similarity measures truth;
- RAG eliminates hallucination;
- a computationally predicted material, molecule, protein, or research direction is validated by virtue of being retrieved or generated;
- model-level debiasing makes a downstream system fair;
- recommendation accuracy is equivalent to usefulness, welfare, diversity, or user intent;
- the nearest result is necessarily the best result;
- long-context models make retrieval obsolete.

These exclusions already match the research brief and back matter and therefore do not require a release-blocking rewrite.

## Publication judgment

The manuscript is ready for a first Shelf release.

It is structurally complete, has the required front and back matter, has an explicit rights boundary, carries a substantial source ledger, and makes its strongest counterevidence part of the argument rather than hiding it. No explicit unresolved factual or research blocker is recorded in the current publication package.

The field is moving quickly, so later Desk editions should revisit contemporary examples and product claims before replacement release. That ordinary nonfiction update risk is not a reason to withhold this first edition.

The release discipline is simple: keep adjacency as a map for candidate generation, not a substitute for evidence.
