## What changed after the first edition

The first Shelf edition was released on September 2, 2026. The second-edition pass materially deepened the back half of the manuscript while preserving the original thesis and chapter architecture.

The largest additions are concentrated in Chapters 16–25:

- agent retrieval now separates investigative reach from committing authority and adds reversibility, auditability, and procedural-memory boundaries;
- serendipity now treats discovery as a process that can change the future user rather than merely predict a static preference;
- the economics chapter now treats shortlisting, portability, supplier visibility, contestability, and commercial influence as parts of discovery power;
- map governance now includes versioned geography, regression comparison, rollback, scope boundaries, and the distributive effects of moving neighborhoods;
- analogy now includes counter-analogy, mechanism tests, boundary conditions, and the discipline of transferring questions rather than predetermined endings;
- graph/vector systems now preserve the epistemic status and provenance of inferred relations before they harden into explicit edges;
- organizational search now distinguishes canonical knowledge from searchable exhaust, carries permission and retention provenance, and surfaces unresolved contradiction rather than smoothing it away;
- education now measures independent capability after assistance is withdrawn and treats learner models as uncertain, revisable, and sensitive to the provenance of help;
- creativity now rejects semantic distance as a standalone novelty score and treats crowded neighborhoods, self-imitation, source provenance, and escape from local style as design problems;
- the conclusion now makes discovery literacy explicit: inspect the neighborhood, recover the source, distinguish candidate from authority, and return from representation to reality.

These additions are primarily analytical and operational. They do not introduce a new named empirical literature or new quantitative product-performance claims that require a separate evidence lane beyond the existing research packet.

## Evidence posture retained

The second edition keeps the same durable thesis: learned representations make fuzzy relation cheap enough to become infrastructure, expanding candidate sets while moving scarce judgment downstream toward authority, verification, empirical validation, governance, and the choice of how far from the familiar to search.

The edition continues to preserve the boundaries that matter most:

- similarity is not truth, causality, probability, fairness, legality, authority, novelty, or usefulness by itself;
- dense retrieval complements lexical retrieval, structured filters, graphs, permissions, databases, and source inspection rather than abolishing them;
- approximate nearest-neighbor systems trade resources and latency against recall;
- scientific candidates remain candidates until the relevant empirical or theoretical validation occurs;
- recommendation and personalization can allocate attention without revealing a person's stable or complete preference;
- agent retrieval can expand what an agent can consider without expanding what it is authorized to do;
- searchable memory can be stale, superseded, private, unauthorized, or wrong even when it is semantically close;
- distance can support exploration without becoming a calibrated measure of creativity or originality.
