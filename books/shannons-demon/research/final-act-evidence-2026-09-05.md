# Final-act evidence note — 2026-09-05

This note records the evidence and falsification boundaries used for the September 5 depth pass on Chapters 16, 18, and 19 of *Shannon's Demon*. It supplements the canonical `source-ledger.csv`; it does not replace the chapter-level sources already recorded there.

## Chapter 16 — rebalancing, diversification, and the demon

### Added source

- Keith Cuthbertson, Simon Hayley, Nick Motson, and Dirk Nitzsche, “What Does Rebalancing Really Achieve?”, *International Journal of Finance & Economics* 21(3), 2016, pp. 224–240. DOI: `10.1002/ijfe.1545`. Open accepted version: https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/13733/

### Claim boundary

The paper is used as counterevidence against the loose claim that all apparent growth in a diversified constant-weight portfolio should be called a positive “rebalancing return.” It distinguishes diversification return from return attributable specifically to the act of rebalancing and warns that infinite-horizon arguments can give a misleading picture of finite-horizon experience.

The manuscript therefore treats the 50/50 doubling/halving example as an illustrative path-dependent construction, not a universal investment law. It preserves cases where a persistent trend makes buy-and-hold outperform a constant-weight rule, where transaction costs and taxes consume the effect, and where asset impairment makes repeated buying destructive.

### Falsification test

If evidence showed that rebalancing itself necessarily adds positive return for any finite sequence of asset returns, regardless of costs, correlations, trends, or asset impairment, the chapter’s countercase would be wrong. The cited literature does not support that universal claim.

The historical provenance boundary from `SD-046` remains controlling: this pass still does not claim a canonical Shannon-authored paper titled “Shannon's Demon” or a single definitive published formulation by Shannon.

## Chapter 18 — from existence theorem to practical codes

### Added source

- Erico Guizzo, “Closing In on the Perfect Code,” *IEEE Spectrum*, 2003: https://spectrum.ieee.org/turbo-codes

### Claim boundary

The article is used for the engineering bridge between Shannon’s 1948 channel-coding existence result and later practical capacity-approaching constructions. It documents the long gap between theorem and implementation, the 1993 introduction of turbo codes by Claude Berrou, Alain Glavieux, and Punya Thitimajshima, and the later practical importance of LDPC codes.

The chapter gives Shannon credit for the frontier, not for later code families. Hamming, Reed–Solomon, convolutional, turbo, LDPC, decoder, hardware, and standards work remain separate contributions by later researchers and engineering communities.

The chapter also preserves a model boundary: “channel capacity” is a family of model-specific results, not one universal formula that can be applied unchanged to every physical channel. The familiar bandwidth/SNR expression belongs to a particular channel model.

### Falsification test

If Shannon’s 1948 paper contained a practical finite-complexity construction that later engineers merely implemented unchanged, the theorem-versus-construction distinction would be overstated. The primary paper and the engineering history instead show a long research program devoted to finding workable codes approaching the theoretical limits.

## Chapter 19 — Alzheimer’s disease and the limits of information metaphor

### Added source

- National Institute on Aging, “What Is Alzheimer’s Disease?”, reviewed March 4, 2025: https://www.nia.nih.gov/health/alzheimers-and-dementia/what-alzheimers-disease

### Claim boundary

The NIA source is used only for general clinical framing: Alzheimer’s disease is a progressive brain disorder that damages memory and thinking and, as it advances, affects language, reasoning, judgment, behavior, and daily functioning. Shannon-specific diagnosis and chronology remain controlled by the MIT obituary already recorded as `SD-053`.

The medical source is not used to infer Shannon’s private symptoms, exact clinical timeline, subjective experience, or cause of any earlier professional behavior. The chapter explicitly resists turning Alzheimer’s into a Shannon-entropy analogy or treating a human mind as a storage device with a rising bit-error rate.

### Falsification test

If a clinical source established that Alzheimer’s can be fully and usefully modeled as ordinary digital information loss, the chapter’s metaphor boundary would need revision. Current medical descriptions do not support that reduction.

No invented dialogue, bedside scene, private thought, or Shannon-specific medical detail was added. No modern finance result is attributed retrospectively to Shannon. No later error-correcting code is treated as Shannon’s invention. The pass uses later scholarship to narrow claims, not to enlarge the hero narrative.
