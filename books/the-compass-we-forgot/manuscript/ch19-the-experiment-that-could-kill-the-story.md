# 19. The Experiment That Could Kill the Story

A field becomes healthy when its proponents can describe the result that would make them stop.

For human magnetoreception, the decisive experiment is not another clever positive study. It is a coordinated attempt to reproduce the 2019 EEG signature under conditions designed in advance to discriminate the major mechanism and artifact classes.

The protocol should be multicenter and adversarial. At least one laboratory should include investigators skeptical of the original interpretation. Hardware designs should be open. Coil fields should be independently mapped. Analysis code should be frozen before unblinding.

The primary replication outcome can be simple: does the preregistered magnetic rotation produce the predicted alpha-band desynchronization relative to matched sham conditions?

But the experiment should contain nested mechanism tests.

**Polarity:** repeat matched rotations with the static vertical component reversed. If the original asymmetry is real, the direction of the effect must be specified prospectively.

**Hemisphere:** recruit cohorts raised primarily under Northern and Southern Hemisphere inclinations. A developmental-ecology hypothesis must state whether the response should reverse, remain fixed or depend on current residence.

**Light:** run controlled spectral conditions, including darkness and wavelength bands relevant to cryptochrome photochemistry. A light-independent effect weakens simple retinal radical-pair accounts.

**Radiofrequency perturbation:** add calibrated weak oscillating fields at frequencies selected from radical-pair predictions and animal literature. The result must show frequency and amplitude structure, not merely “some RF effect.”

**Coordinate frame:** compare field rotation around a stationary head with physical head rotation through a stationary field trajectory when vestibular controls allow. A biological compass should be describable in head-centered or world-centered variables; a coil artifact follows hardware.

**Instrumentation:** record microphones, accelerometers, temperature probes, pickup coils and independent magnetometers. Every sensory side channel becomes a covariate and a potential exclusion criterion defined before analysis.

**Behavior:** include a forced-choice judgment whose stimulus mapping is concealed and whose analysis is independent of EEG. Behavior is secondary at first; it should not be allowed to rescue a failed neural replication through post hoc task selection.

The defeat conditions should be explicit.

If several adequately powered, technically validated sites fail to reproduce the preregistered alpha response, the 2019 human EEG claim should be treated as unconfirmed rather than continually modified. If the effect appears but follows hardware rather than magnetic geometry, it is artifact until shown otherwise. If the effect replicates but fails every mechanism fingerprint, “magnetoreception” should remain provisional; generic weak-field sensitivity is a different claim. If the response is reproducible but unstable within individuals and unrelated to any biological covariate, the vestigial-sense story weakens.

Conversely, a successful result would have to be uncomfortable for skeptics, not merely satisfying to proponents. It should reproduce at independent sites. It should follow magnetic variables rather than coil commands. It should display a coherent mechanism fingerprint. It should predict held-out trials. It should survive direct scrutiny of side channels.

Only then does receptor hunting become the next major investment.

The experiment is expensive by the standards of a niche sensory question and cheap by the standards of discovering a new human sensory channel.

That asymmetry is exactly why it should be done once, well enough to lose.
