# 13. The Southern Hemisphere Test

The cleanest experiment in this book may require nothing more exotic than an airplane ticket.

The 2019 EEG result was not symmetric with respect to the vertical component of the magnetic field. The response appeared under a downward-pointing field resembling normal Northern Hemisphere geomagnetic inclination and disappeared when that vertical component was reversed.

If that dependency reflects biological calibration to the local geomagnetic environment, people raised in the Southern Hemisphere become a natural experiment.

There are several possibilities, and they do not all tell the same story.

A hardwired polarity-sensitive receptor might produce the same intrinsic response in every population regardless of birthplace. A developmentally calibrated system might reverse its preferred vertical configuration in people raised where the geomagnetic field points upward. A flexible adult system might recalibrate after years in a new hemisphere. A vestigial system might show no clean population pattern at all.

The experiment therefore should not merely recruit “southerners.” It should reconstruct geomagnetic biography.

Birthplace. Childhood latitude. Long-term residence. Migration age. Time spent near the magnetic equator where inclination approaches horizontal. Occupational exposure. Urban versus rural environment. Even the field inside homes and schools could matter if developmental calibration exists.

Then the protocol should be identical across sites and portable enough that the apparatus, not the laboratory, moves. The same coil geometry, amplifiers, electrode caps, software, timing and sham conditions should be used in northern and southern laboratories. Hardware should be swapped between sites so an apparent hemisphere effect cannot be assigned to one chamber.

The preregistered prediction must be explicit before data collection.

If the original ecological interpretation is right, which field rotations should trigger alpha-ERD in participants raised under upward inclination? Does the response follow current residence or developmental origin? What happens to people raised near the equator? What happens to identical twins who moved at different ages?

This experiment is powerful because artifact hypotheses often make different predictions. A coil artifact should travel with hardware and current sequence, not childhood latitude. A universal receptor should travel with the participant but not necessarily reverse. A learned calibration model predicts systematic interaction between participant history and field geometry.

There is also a more radical outcome: the original vertical-polarity effect may not replicate anywhere, including California. That would demote the entire ecological story before researchers spend years explaining it.

This is what good anomalous science looks like when it becomes ambitious. It chooses a result whose most interesting interpretation creates an inconvenient, geographically expensive test.

The magnetic field itself gives us the intervention.

Nature reverses the sign of inclination across the equator. Biology, if it learned the field, should carry some trace of that history.

If it does not, the theory should get smaller.
