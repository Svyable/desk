# Back Matter

## A note on claims

This book treats magnetoreception as a layered problem rather than a yes/no referendum. Robust animal magnetic orientation does not establish human magnetoreception. Magnetic material in human tissue does not establish a receptor. A molecule capable of magnetic effects in another organism does not establish that humans use it. A neural response does not establish conscious perception or useful navigation.

The strongest human claim presently discussed is the published 2019 EEG result from Wang et al. and related neurophysiological work. Independent replication remains a central requirement.

## Evidence classes

1. **Verified/documented:** directly established experimental, archival or institutional facts.
2. **Credible but incomplete:** serious evidence that has not yet independently converged or that underdetermines mechanism.
3. **Disputed/unconfirmed:** claims with substantial uncertainty, failed replication, methodological controversy or missing independent confirmation.
4. **Speculative synthesis/open conjecture:** book-level hypotheses offered as testable interpretations, not facts.

## Research principle

A strange claim does not deserve weaker controls because it is strange. It deserves experiments whose failure modes are more explicit than ordinary work because the prior literature is already full of seductive artifacts.

## Decisive next step

The highest-value experiment is a preregistered, multicenter reproduction of the human alpha-ERD result with hardware swaps, direct side-channel instrumentation, Northern/Southern Hemisphere cohorts, mechanism-specific light/RF/polarity tests, and a frozen analysis pipeline.

If that experiment fails across technically validated sites, the current human neural magnetoreception claim should contract sharply.

If it succeeds, the field earns the expensive next question: where is the receptor?
