# Contradictions and Incentives

## Contradiction: animal certainty, receptor uncertainty

Magnetic orientation is behaviorally persuasive across many animals while the physical receptor remains controversial in major vertebrate systems. Do not infer that mechanism uncertainty negates behavior; do not infer that behavior licenses any favored receptor.

## Contradiction: human CRY2 capability versus human EEG polarity

Human CRY2 can participate in magnetosensitivity in Drosophila, supporting molecular capability. Wang et al. reported human EEG polarity sensitivity that does not fit the simplest axial radical-pair compass. These findings can coexist if the fly assay demonstrates unused capacity, humans use another mechanism, multiple mechanisms coexist, downstream circuitry creates asymmetry, or the EEG result is not robust.

## Contradiction: magnetite is present but receptor anatomy is absent

Human brain magnetic minerals are real, but pollution-derived magnetite is also real and no accepted human magnetite receptor has been identified. Presence cannot carry function.

## Contradiction: strong neural claim, weak conscious behavior

The 2019 EEG study reports an unconscious neural response while reliable human magnetic navigation remains unestablished. This can reflect a vestigial input, a weak nonsensory magnetic effect, task mismatch, or failure of the EEG result to replicate.

## Incentive: novelty

A “new human sense” is a high-attention framing. Press coverage can compress the claim ladder. Manuscript language should keep neural response, perception and navigation separate.

## Incentive: skepticism after embarrassment

Baker-era failures create a rationally high evidentiary bar but can also make researchers reluctant to revisit neighboring questions. Historical embarrassment is not itself negative evidence against a new assay.

## Incentive: defense interest

DARPA has incentives to test unusual mechanisms with potential sensing/communications value before consensus forms. That raises the prior probability of government investigation, not the probability that a capability works.

## Incentive: quantum language

Radical-pair chemistry legitimately involves quantum spin dynamics. “Quantum” also increases rhetorical appeal and can cause mechanism claims to outrun quantitative constraints.

## Incentive: environmental-health narratives

A validated weak-field sensory pathway could be misused to support broad claims about electromagnetic injury. Sensory detection, behavioral disruption and toxicity must remain distinct.

## Incentive: engineering payoff

Magnetogenetics and biomimetic sensing create commercial and defense value for a solved receptor mechanism. This can accelerate good work and encourage premature mechanism stories.

## Disinformation posture

No evidence presently justifies a claim that human magnetoreception is a classified military capability, that public nulls are cover stories, or that DARPA RadioBio concealed an operational compass system. Treat such assertions as Class 3 unless primary documentation emerges.
