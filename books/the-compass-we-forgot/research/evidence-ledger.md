# Evidence Ledger

## Class 1 — verified / documented

| Claim | Evidence | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Magnetoreception occurs across diverse animal taxa | Large replicated behavioral literature summarized in modern reviews | Does not establish one universal mechanism or human capacity |
| Human brain tissue contains magnetite/ferrimagnetic material | Kirschvink et al. 1992 and later mineralogical work | Presence is not receptor identity |
| Human brains also contain combustion/pollution-consistent magnetite nanoparticles | Maher et al. 2016 | Does not prove all brain magnetite is exogenous |
| Human CRY2 can support light-dependent magnetosensitivity in a Drosophila assay | Foley et al. 2011 | Does not show CRY2 mediates a human magnetic sense |
| A 2019 study reported selective alpha-ERD to controlled Earth-strength magnetic rotations | Wang et al. 2019 | Single research program; independent replication still required |
| The 2019 response depended on field geometry/polarity under the reported protocol | Wang et al. 2019 | Mechanistic interpretation remains provisional |
| DARPA publicly ran RadioBio to test claims of biological electromagnetic signaling | DARPA program records | Funding is not validation or evidence of weaponization |
| Several proposed vertebrate magnetite receptor structures have failed under improved cell identification/contamination control | Primary literature summarized in Lam & Malkemper 2026 | Does not rule out every magnetite receptor architecture |

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete

- The 2019 alpha-ERD is a genuine sensory transduction response rather than an unrecognized apparatus interaction.
- Some human visual or neurophysiological variables are modulated by Earth-strength magnetic fields.
- A residual human magnetic input is unconscious or vestigial rather than behaviorally useful.
- Individual differences in EEG response reflect stable biology rather than sampling/analysis noise.

## Class 3 — disputed / unconfirmed

- Humans can reliably navigate or home using geomagnetic information alone.
- A specific human magnetite-containing receptor cell has been identified.
- Human retinal cryptochrome is the operative human magnetic receptor.
- Weak ambient electromagnetic fields produce broad health effects by disrupting a human magnetic sense.
- Existing ferritin-based magnetogenetic systems provide robust remote neural control through the originally proposed mechanisms.

## Class 4 — speculative synthesis / open conjecture

- Human magnetoreception may be a degraded ancestral input that still reaches cortex but rarely reaches conscious spatial behavior.
- Developmental geomagnetic environment may calibrate response polarity or inclination sensitivity.
- Anthropogenic electromagnetic noise may modulate a real magnetic sensory pathway in some environments.
- Pollution-derived magnetic nanoparticles may interfere with or obscure a genuine receptor system.
- A distributed receptor architecture may explain why anatomical searches for a single magnetic organ have repeatedly failed.

## Rules for promotion

No Class 2–4 claim is promoted because it fits the book’s thesis. Promotion requires independent evidence directed at the specific claim. Testimony, institutional interest, a compatible mechanism, or a neighboring verified phenomenon does not inherit proof across layers.
