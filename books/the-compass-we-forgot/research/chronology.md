# Chronology

- **19th–early 20th century** — animal orientation by unknown environmental cues attracts speculation; geomagnetic explanations remain difficult to isolate experimentally.
- **Mid-20th century** — behavioral evidence for animal magnetic orientation strengthens across birds and other taxa.
- **1970s–1980s** — Robin Baker conducts chair, bus and walkabout experiments claiming human magnetic orientation; attempted replications produce inconsistent or negative results.
- **Early 1980s** — claims of magnetic material associated with human sinus structures become entangled with contamination concerns; the episode helps discredit strong human-compass claims.
- **1985** — Kirschvink reports failure to replicate a version of Baker’s human chair experiment, part of a broader reproducibility problem.
- **1992** — Kirschvink, Kobayashi-Kirschvink and Woodford report magnetite biomineralization/ferrimagnetic material in human brain tissue in *PNAS*.
- **2000** — Ritz, Adem and Schulten publish an influential radical-pair model for photoreceptor-based avian magnetoreception.
- **2011** — Foley, Gegear and Reppert report that human CRY2 can restore light-dependent magnetosensitivity in a Drosophila system.
- **2016** — Maher et al. identify abundant pollution-consistent magnetite nanoparticles in human brain tissue, complicating inference from magnetic material to biological receptor.
- **2017** — DARPA announces RadioBio, explicitly designed to determine whether functional electromagnetic signaling between biological cells exists and, if so, characterize mechanism and information transfer.
- **2019, March 18** — Wang et al. publish *Transduction of the Geomagnetic Field as Evidenced from Alpha-band Activity in the Human Brain* in *eNeuro*, reporting alpha-ERD to selected Earth-strength field rotations.
- **2025–2026** — modern magnetosensation reviews synthesize animal mechanism work, failed receptor identifications, human EEG evidence and magnetogenetic replication/physics problems.
- **2026** — Lam and Malkemper’s *Physiological Reviews* synthesis characterizes human EEG evidence as suggestive of subconscious magnetosensitivity while emphasizing that independent replication remains necessary.

## Chronology boundary

This chronology records publications and documented research events. It does not turn publication order into proof accumulation. A later positive paper does not retroactively authenticate early behavioral claims; a failed early receptor candidate does not negate later physiological evidence.
