# DARPA Buys the Question

DARPA's budget documents make the institutional fact unusually clean. The Stimulated Isomer Energy Release program existed. It received millions of dollars. Its stated goal was to develop a technique to control energy release from nuclear isomers such as hafnium-178m2.

This is where evidence classification prevents two equal and opposite mistakes.

The first mistake says government funding proves the underlying effect. It does not. DARPA exists in part to fund uncertain technology. A program line is evidence of institutional interest, not a laboratory result.

The second mistake says funding a disputed effect is self-evidently foolish. That also goes too far. If a claim implies a radically new energy-storage regime or weapon class, buying a decisive experiment can be prudent even when the prior probability is low. Technological surprise has asymmetric costs.

The interesting question is program design.

A good high-risk program turns uncertainty into milestones. If the claimed cross section is real, demonstrate it under independent, controlled conditions. If triggering is real but inefficient, quantify the efficiency. If useful release is possible, establish an energy balance. If material production dominates cost and energy, include it in the system boundary. Each stage should narrow the next.

Public DARPA budget language around SIER emphasized controlled release and a breakeven experiment. That wording is revealing. It moved the conversation away from a slight spectral enhancement and toward net energy accounting. This was the correct engineering direction, although the phrase “breakeven” can itself hide choices about what counts as input.

Do we count only the trigger pulse? The energy required to produce the isomer? Target preparation? Cooling? Shielding? Replacement? A military device does not get to ignore the factory that makes its fuel.

DARPA was therefore buying more than a physics question. It was buying a boundary-setting exercise.

The program later disappeared from the budget line. That absence should not be romanticized as evidence of classification. Public records already provide a simpler explanation: the dramatic x-ray-triggering claims had not survived independent tests at anything like their original strength.

Black-budget imagination loves missing programs. Sometimes a missing program is just a terminated program.
