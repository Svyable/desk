# Back Matter

## Chronology

**1999** — C. B. Collins and collaborators report x-ray-induced accelerated emission from the 31-year hafnium-178m2 isomer in *Physical Review Letters*.

**2000** — Multiple technical comments challenge aspects of the interpretation and implied nuclear strength.

**2001** — Argonne/Livermore/Los Alamos collaboration reports a high-intensity synchrotron test with no triggering signal at the reported strength.

**2003** — Further Advanced Photon Source work reports upper limits far below the triggering cross sections claimed by the Collins group.

**2004–2005** — DARPA budget documents list the Stimulated Isomer Energy Release program and millions of dollars in funding.

**2006** — DARPA budget justification records no further funding for SIER and describes the program in past tense.

**2024** — Modern work revisits nuclear-isomer excitation in laser-generated plasmas in a different isotope/system, illustrating that nuclear-isomer control remains an active scientific subject even though the hafnium weapon narrative did not become established technology.

No classified information was used or implied. The book treats patents, program names and budget lines as evidence that people filed, funded or investigated ideas—not as proof that the ideas worked.
