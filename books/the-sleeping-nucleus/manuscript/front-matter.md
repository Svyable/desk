# The Sleeping Nucleus

*Hafnium-178m2, Gamma-Ray Dreams, and the Pentagon's Experiment with Stored Nuclear Energy*

Sven Hardy Benson

A nucleus can be excited and remain that way for years. That sentence is already strange enough. The trouble began when another sentence followed it: perhaps the energy could be released on command.

This book is about the distance between those two sentences.

The first belongs to ordinary nuclear physics. The second became, for a few years, a Pentagon research program, a weapons argument, a press sensation and a scientific feud. Hafnium-178m2 was not imaginary. Its long-lived excited state was not imaginary. The energy stored in that state was not imaginary. A 1999 *Physical Review Letters* paper reporting accelerated gamma emission under x-ray irradiation was also real. So were the later national-laboratory experiments that did not reproduce the claimed effect. So was DARPA's Stimulated Isomer Energy Release program.

The temptation is to turn this history into a morality play about foolish defense spending or suppressed breakthroughs. Neither version is adequate. The interesting object is the machine that formed between an anomalous result and an institution built to care about technological surprise.

Throughout, I separate four kinds of evidence: documented measurements and official records; credible but incomplete interpretation; disputed claims that lack independent confirmation; and open synthesis. Government funding proves interest. It does not prove the effect. Publication proves that referees found a paper publishable. It does not make the result reproducible. A failed replication weighs against a claim, but does not by itself explain how the original signal arose.

There is a useful discipline in leaving those distinctions intact. Strange physics deserves experiments capable of killing it.
