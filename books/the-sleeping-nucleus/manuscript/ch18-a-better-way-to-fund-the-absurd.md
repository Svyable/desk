# A Better Way to Fund the Absurd

Governments should fund absurd ideas.

Not all of them. Not forever. But some fraction of public research money should be allowed to touch claims that embarrass tidy roadmaps. Technological surprise exists. Consensus has failed before. Institutions that fund only what review panels already believe will optimize yesterday.

The hafnium episode does not argue against that appetite. It argues for a stricter form of it.

High-risk programs should begin with an evidence map. What is measured? What is one-lab evidence? What is disputed? What is purely speculative? The program should then spend money moving claims upward or killing them.

Applications should be gated behind physical milestones. A weapon concept does not mature because someone drew it. A funding line does not become evidence of efficacy because it was classified. A prestigious paper does not become a material property until another apparatus can recover the effect.

The best frontier agencies should be almost rude about this. Give the claimant excellent equipment. Give the critic access to the same sample. Predefine the result that would change each side's mind. Pay for the null. Publish as much as classification allows. If the effect survives, increase the bet.

This is not cautious science. It is aggressive science.

The public argument around exotic research usually offers two bad characters: the visionary who believes too much and the skeptic who refuses to look. The more useful figure is the experimentalist who wants the strange claim placed in a machine that can break it.

Hafnium-178m2 still carries its excitation. That fact is beautiful. The failed dream around it is useful too. For a few years, a sleeping nucleus forced physicists, weapons analysts and research agencies to decide how much evidence a radical technology should owe before imagination becomes procurement.

We still have not standardized the answer.

We should.
