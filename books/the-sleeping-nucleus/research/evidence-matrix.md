# Evidence matrix and source log

## Class 1 — verified / documented

- Hafnium-178m2 is a long-lived nuclear isomer with roughly 31-year half-life and about 2.446 MeV excitation energy.
- Collins et al. published a 1999 *Physical Review Letters* paper reporting x-ray-induced enhancement of gamma emission. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.82.695
- Formal technical critiques followed in *Physical Review Letters*, including McNabb et al. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.84.2542
- Argonne/LANL/LLNL teams performed higher-intensity tests and reported no corresponding triggering signal, with upper limits many orders below the original inferred cross section. https://www.llnl.gov/article/28056/physicists-challenge-reports-accelerated- and https://www3.aps.anl.gov/News/Conferences/2003/UC_Review/Posters/user_posters.htm
- DARPA publicly budgeted a Stimulated Isomer Energy Release program. FY2005 documentation lists $3M in FY2004 and $4M in FY2005; FY2006-era documentation shows no subsequent funding and describes the program in past tense. https://comptroller.defense.gov/Portals/45/Documents/defbudget/fy2007/budget_justification/pdfs/03_RDT_and_E/Vol_1_DARPA/FY06_RDTE_DARPA_DW.pdf

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete

- Public accounts and technical retrospectives describe internal defense interest extending beyond the easily visible budget line. These are useful for institutional history but should not be treated as proof of secret successful experiments.
- Claims about the exact reasons particular managers continued or ended funding may rely on recollection unless supported by program records.

## Class 3 — disputed / unconfirmed

- The original magnitude of x-ray-triggered depletion remains unsupported by independent high-intensity replication.
- Claims that a practical “hafnium bomb” was demonstrated are not supported by the public experimental record.
- Claims that the public program merely moved into a successful classified compartment lack independently verifiable evidence.

## Class 4 — speculative synthesis

- The book proposes that high-payoff frontier programs should be evaluated by “uncertainty retired per dollar.” This is an analytic framing, not an official DARPA metric.
- The manuscript argues that production/logistics signatures can constrain claims of mature hidden physical technologies. This is a comparative inference, not a proof rule.

## Contradictions / incentives

- Original triggering claims versus APS null results are the central contradiction; do not smooth it over.
- The original authors revised mechanism discussions across subsequent work; mechanism migration must not be treated as independent confirmation.
- Defense agencies have incentives both to investigate low-probability surprise and to preserve option value; neither motive establishes efficacy.
- Critics may be correct on the physics and still overstate the death of adjacent nuclear-isomer research.
- Press labels such as “hafnium bomb” compress multiple unproven engineering steps and should be attributed, not adopted as a technical category.
