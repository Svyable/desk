# Research Appendix

## High-value Russian-language trail

The Russian Electronic Library of Historical Documents exposes a particularly valuable collection: *Немецкие авиационные специалисты в г. Куйбышеве в первые послевоенные годы (1946–1953): Сб. док.* Its index lists 169 documents, including ministerial orders moving German bureaus, combining design groups, transferring specialists between plants, reports on political/cultural work, and management assessments of whether foreign personnel remained useful.

This collection should become the backbone of the next aviation expansion pass because it can replace broad secondary claims with dated administrative decisions.

S. M. Dudkin's Russian-language study of German specialists on a Soviet plant is useful for everyday management evidence. It quotes factory orders evaluating specific specialists' initiative and absenteeism, material that complicates both “prisoner” and “ordinary employee” caricatures.

Russian Academy of Sciences / Space Research Institute material on Gröttrup and OSOAVIAKhIM supplies another non-English lane, especially for sequence and the rocket community's move from German reconstruction work to Soviet territory.

## High-value German-language trail

The APuZ specialist-transfer study is unusually important because it uses personnel-card and archival evidence to reconstruct selection before the October removal, the share transferred in the operation, and effects on the SBZ/DDR after return. It also cautions that the operation's familiar name may lack a Soviet archival basis.

Bundesarchiv's Invenio system should be used in a subsequent research pass for personnel files, occupation administration records, industrial dismantlement records, and return/reintegration material.

## High-value U.S. intelligence trail

CIA records should be mined as source collections rather than treated as conclusions. Returned specialists provide details on Gorodomlya, Podlipki, Zeiss machinery, guidance projects, and design studies. For each report, the next pass should record source access, date of observation, date of reporting, whether the source knew the matter directly, and what the source could not have known because of Soviet compartmentation.

## Technical lineage audit

For every claimed German-to-Soviet technology lineage, build a table with:

- German system or proposal
- German author/team
- date
- receiving Soviet organization
- documentary evidence of transfer
- Soviet project allegedly influenced
- physical resemblance
- shared equations/methods
- personnel continuity
- explicit Soviet adoption/rejection record
- competing indigenous or international source
- confidence class

This is especially important for R-10/R-14/R-15 claims, guidance work, jet engines, swept-wing aircraft, optics, and later Soviet missile systems.

## Human-history audit

Build a family-level index where public records permit: specialist, spouse, children, prewar employer, political record if documented, October 1946 location, Soviet destination, role, return date, post-return career, memoir/testimony sources.

Do not infer Nazi commitment from employment in a wartime firm alone. Distinguish party membership, SS affiliation, war-crime implication, ordinary technical employment, and unknown status.
