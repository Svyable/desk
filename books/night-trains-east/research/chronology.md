# Chronology

- **1943–1944** — Allied technical-intelligence interest in German advanced weapons grows as V-2 and jet programs become visible.
- **Spring 1945** — U.S., British and Soviet forces seize German technical sites, records, equipment and personnel.
- **1945** — Soviet authorities organize German specialists in the occupation zone into institutes and design groups, including rocket reconstruction around Nordhausen/Bleicherode.
- **August–September 1946** — U.S. Paperclip policy is expanded; Soviet authorities finalize selection and transfer preparations for German specialists.
- **21–22 October 1946** — Coordinated removals from the Soviet occupation zone and East Berlin; specialists and families are transported to the USSR.
- **1946–1947** — German aviation, missile, optics and other specialist groups are installed in Soviet plants and research organizations.
- **1947** — V-2-derived Soviet test activity begins at Kapustin Yar.
- **1948–1949** — German rocket groups on Gorodomlya produce improved V-2 and longer-range design studies; aviation groups are reorganized and reassigned.
- **1948** — German/Soviet organizations record both productive specialist work and personnel-management problems.
- **1950–1953** — Soviet ministries increasingly judge foreign teams against diminishing marginal value; repatriation accelerates.
- **1952–1955** — Western intelligence interviews returned specialists and produces detailed reports on Gorodomlya, Zeiss, guidance and other programs.
- **1957** — U.S. National Intelligence Estimate assesses German exploitation as important but largely supplementary to native Soviet missile development.
- **1990s onward** — German and Russian archival openings permit more systematic reconstruction of selection, transfer, industrial assignments and return.
