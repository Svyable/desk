# Evidence and source spine

## Working rule

This book treats *physical coupling*, *observed anomaly*, *precursor*, *forecast skill*, and *operational prediction* as five different claims. Sources must not be promoted from one category to the next by rhetoric.

## Four-class matrix

| Claim | Class | Posture |
|---|---|---|
| Earthquakes can produce coseismic electric/magnetic effects | 1 — documented | Strong; not evidence of a pre-event warning |
| Luminous phenomena can occur in association with earthquakes | 1/2 | Documented reports and serious mechanisms exist; event attribution varies |
| Stress-activated charge carriers in rocks are a possible mechanism for some luminous/EM phenomena | 2 | Laboratory/mechanistic literature; field-scale predictive significance unclosed |
| Loma Prieta had a useful ULF magnetic precursor | 3 | Famous original claim; USGS-linked reanalysis attributes reported anomaly to sensor-system malfunction |
| Hector Mine had a useful TEC precursor | 3 | Published claim; USGS reanalysis reproduced the time series but found the supposed anomaly within normal global TEC variation |
| QuakeFinder pulses predicted Alum Rock earthquakes | 3 | USGS-linked 2021 assessment found threshold sensitivity, non-replication for 2010, and strong evidence for local cultural noise |
| DEMETER statistics contain pre-seismic ion-density structure | 2/3 | Peer-reviewed statistical studies report structure; translation to prospective prediction remains unresolved |
| EM precursors currently permit reliable earthquake prediction | 3 | USGS states there is no convincing evidence of electromagnetic earthquake precursors |
| A heterogeneous precursor ensemble could eventually improve probabilistic forecasts | 4 | Testable conjecture, not present capability |

## Contradictions and incentives audit

**Retrospective windowing.** If the event date is known, investigators can inspect many frequencies, stations, spatial radii and lead windows. A visually unusual feature can acquire significance after the fact. Expansion must record which thresholds were specified before inspection and which were selected afterward.

**Instrument pathology.** A precursor detector is also a machine in weather, soil, power systems, telemetry networks and human infrastructure. Corralitos is valuable because the alleged geophysical signal and the health of the instrument cannot be separated by storytelling.

**Space weather.** TEC and geomagnetic variables have enormous non-seismic drivers. Solar and geomagnetic controls are not nuisance footnotes; they are competing causal models.

**Cultural noise.** Power systems, vehicles, pumps, industrial equipment and nearby human activity can generate electromagnetic signatures. The QuakeFinder reassessment is especially useful because pulse counts increased during peak human-activity hours.

**Positive-event selection.** A spectacular earthquake attracts analysis. The correct denominator includes quiet periods, earthquakes without anomalies, anomalies without earthquakes, and sensors that failed silently.

**Institutional asymmetry.** Prediction success is memorable; false alarms and null results are less culturally sticky. Skeptics have an opposite incentive risk: treating the failure of one claimed precursor as evidence that every possible coupling mechanism is impossible. The manuscript must resist both moves.

## Chronology spine

- 1970s–1980s: earthquake-light and tectonomagnetic mechanisms receive sustained scientific attention.
- 1989: Loma Prieta earthquake; Corralitos ULF observations later become a canonical precursor claim.
- 1990s: GPS/TEC and increasingly dense digital geophysics enlarge the anomaly search space.
- 1999: Hector Mine earthquake later becomes a prominent ionospheric-precursor case.
- 2004–2010: DEMETER operates as a dedicated low-Earth-orbit mission studying electromagnetic/ionospheric phenomena, including possible seismic associations.
- 2008: Wenchuan earthquake becomes a major multisensor seismo-ionospheric case study.
- 2009: Thomas, Love & Johnston publish their Corralitos reanalysis.
- 2012: Thomas et al. publish the Hector Mine TEC reanalysis.
- 2017–2018: DEMETER statistical analyses report population-level ionospheric structure associated with seismic activity.
- 2021: Wang et al. publish the QuakeFinder/Alum Rock reassessment.
- 2025: USGS researcher Susan Hough revisits luminous phenomena near Summerville, South Carolina, arguing that at least some local “ghost light” reports may have natural seismic explanations.

1. Recover and page-index the original Fraser-Smith and Bernardi Loma Prieta papers alongside the 2009 reanalysis.
2. Reconstruct Corralitos station hardware, maintenance log, calibration state and the full 21-month denominator.
3. Build a Hector Mine TEC ledger containing near/far stations, solar/geomagnetic controls, analysis windows and the exact disagreement between original and reanalysis papers.
4. Build a DEMETER mission ledger: orbit, local-time sampling, instruments, event-selection rules, earthquake magnitude/depth cuts, background construction and multiple-comparison handling for the major statistical papers.
5. Quantify the Alum Rock/QuakeFinder pulse thresholds and reproduce the human-activity argument from the published data/methods.
6. Add a serious radon/hydrological chapter spine from review literature and prospective networks, including null/negative evidence.
7. Separate earthquake lights observed during/near shaking from genuinely pre-seismic luminous claims; build event-level chain-of-observation records.
8. Recover laboratory positive-hole, electrokinetic, piezomagnetic, streaming-potential and microfracture-emission literature and distinguish mechanism feasibility from field detectability.
9. Audit animal-behavior prediction studies prospectively, with automated sensor studies separated from anecdote.
10. Reconstruct Parkfield as the control case for deterministic prediction and the transition toward probabilistic forecasting and earthquake early warning.
11. Define a prospective scoring protocol: lead-time bins, spatial radius, magnitude threshold, alarm duration, false alarms, misses, precision/recall, Brier/log scores, and comparison to ETAS or another accepted seismic baseline.
12. Expand all 18 chapters past 3,000 words with event-level evidence and primary-source links, then run whole-book link, source, repetition and anti-slop checks.
