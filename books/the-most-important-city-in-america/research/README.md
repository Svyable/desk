# Research Brief — The Most Important City in America

## Purpose

This book makes one deliberately contestable selection for each decade from the 1770s through the 2020s: the American city where a nationally consequential force became unusually concentrated, visible, and transferable.

The research burden is therefore two-layered.

The first layer is factual: what happened in the city, when, through which institutions, and with what measurable consequences.

The second layer is comparative: why those facts make this city a better lens on the decade than its strongest rival.

A source can establish the first layer. No archival document can mechanically establish the second. The winner is an interpretive judgment that must be argued from evidence, rival cases, transmission mechanisms, and costs.

## Selection method

A city earns a decade when it performs unusually strongly on four tests:

1. **Concentration.** A defining national force is unusually dense there: political authority, industrial capacity, finance, migration, communications, transportation, cultural production, movement organization, energy, technology, or crisis.
2. **Transmission.** What happens locally changes choices elsewhere through law, markets, infrastructure, imitation, media, migration, capital, organizational practice, or political reaction.
3. **Counterfactual pressure.** A serious rival city has to explain the decade less completely, or the national story becomes meaningfully harder to tell if the winning city is removed.
4. **Cost visibility.** The chapter can identify who paid for the city's importance: workers, displaced residents, excluded citizens, downstream communities, taxpayers, consumers, environments, or future generations. Cost is part of mechanism, not an afterthought.

Population, wealth, fame, moral virtue, and municipal boundaries are not selection rules. A smaller city can beat a larger one. A metropolitan system can be represented by its principal city when the relevant economy exceeds legal city limits. A city may win more than once for different mechanisms.

## Evidence hierarchy

Prefer, in order:

1. Contemporaneous primary records: statutes, proceedings, correspondence, government reports, company records, maps, census material, speeches, court records, and period journalism used with appropriate caution.
2. Public archives and historical agencies: National Archives, Library of Congress, National Park Service, Smithsonian institutions, Federal Reserve History, FDIC, Social Security Administration, state archives, municipal archives, and established historical societies.
3. Peer-reviewed scholarship and university-press history for interpretation and contested causation.
4. High-quality institutional histories and serious secondary reporting for context, especially for recent decades where archives are incomplete.
5. Company material only for bounded claims about the company's own announced investments, facilities, products, or chronology; do not use corporate self-description as independent proof of national importance.

## Claim rules

- Distinguish the city from its metropolitan region when the relevant geography is wider.
- Distinguish event date from later symbolic importance.
- Do not turn correlation into causation: a city can reveal a national change without single-handedly causing it.
- Treat legislation as multi-causal. No protest, president, company, or city gets sole credit for a national statute.
- Treat migration and expansion as experiences of both arrival and dispossession.
- Treat infrastructure as a distribution system for benefits and burdens.
- Treat numbers in recent chapters as dated snapshots and record the source-check date.
- Avoid invented quotations, composite scenes, unverified dialogue, or inner thoughts.
- Preserve genuine rival claims rather than weakening them to make the winner look inevitable.

## Prior-art boundary

The book does **not** claim that nobody has written urban histories of the United States, histories organized around influential cities, rankings of important cities, or city-by-city accounts of American development. Those are established forms.

The working novelty claim is narrower: this manuscript uses one explicitly argued city for each decade of the United States' first 250 years as a repeated selection device, and requires every selection to state a transmission mechanism, strongest rival, and distribution of cost. That is a positioning claim, not a legally or bibliographically exhaustive assertion of first publication. Any marketing language such as "never before written" requires a separate prior-art search before release.

## Chapter source trail and falsification notes

### Chapter 1 — 1770s: Philadelphia

Core evidence:

- National Archives, **Declaration of Independence: A Transcription**. The Continental Congress adopted the Declaration in Philadelphia on July 4, 1776. https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/declaration-transcript
- National Archives, **How Did it Happen?** The Second Continental Congress convened in Philadelphia in 1775 and became the institution through which intercolonial resistance moved toward independence. https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/declaration/how-did-it-happen
- Library of Congress and National Park Service collections on the Continental Congress, Independence Hall, and revolutionary Philadelphia should be used in the next line edit for local institutional detail.

Falsification: Boston is the strongest rival. Switch the winner if the chapter cannot show that Philadelphia's congressional, diplomatic, and declaration functions transmitted rebellion into an intercolonial sovereign claim more consequentially than Boston's role in radicalization and armed resistance.

### Chapter 2 — 1780s: Philadelphia

Core evidence:

- National Archives, **Constitution of the United States**. The Constitutional Convention met in Philadelphia in 1787 and produced the signed Constitution. https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/constitution
- National Archives, **The Constitution: How Did it Happen?** Background on the Confederation's weaknesses and the Philadelphia Convention. https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/constitution/how-did-it-happen

Falsification: New York and Annapolis are serious institutional rivals in the Confederation story. Philadelphia remains the winner only if constitutional design—not merely postwar recovery—is judged the decade's most transferable national development.

### Chapter 3 — 1790s: Philadelphia

Core evidence:

- U.S. Senate historical material on Congress's Philadelphia residence and the federal government's operation before the 1800 move to Washington. https://www.senate.gov/artandhistory/history/common/generic/Philadelphia.htm
- National Archives, **Bill of Rights**. The first Congress proposed constitutional amendments in 1789; ratification concluded in 1791 as the new federal system took institutional form. https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/bill-of-rights
- Federal institutional records for the First Bank, federal fiscal system, diplomacy, and the 1793 yellow-fever crisis should be checked claim by claim in the next factual pass.

Falsification: New York wins if finance and the opening phase of the Washington administration prove more nationally constitutive than Philadelphia's longer role as the functioning national capital during the decade.

### Chapter 4 — 1800s: Washington

Core evidence:

- U.S. Senate, **Meeting Places and Quarters**. The federal government moved to Washington in 1800; the Capitol and city remained unfinished, and enslaved labor was part of their construction. https://www.senate.gov/artandhistory/history/common/briefing/Meeting_Places.htm
- U.S. Senate historical record of Congress first meeting in Washington on November 17, 1800. https://www.senate.gov/artandhistory/history/minute/Congress_Moves_to_Washington.htm

Falsification: New York, New Orleans, and frontier gateways matter more economically. Washington wins only if making a permanent federal capital operational is treated as the decade's most durable institutional act.

### Chapter 5 — 1810s: New Orleans

Core evidence:

- National Park Service, **Chalmette Battlefield**. New Orleans was a vital port and gateway to the Mississippi system; the January 1815 battle protected that strategic geography. https://www.nps.gov/jela/learn/historyculture/chalmette-battlefield.htm
- National Park Service materials on the Battle of New Orleans and Mississippi River commerce should anchor military chronology and the city's strategic role.

Falsification: Baltimore is the strongest rival through the War of 1812, privateering, Fort McHenry, and the national anthem. New Orleans wins only if control of the Mississippi gateway and the battle's political afterlife make it the stronger continental lens.

### Chapter 6 — 1820s: New York

Core evidence:

- New York State Museum, **The Erie Canal**. The canal opened in 1825, sharply reduced shipping costs, and connected western producers to New York's market. https://www.nysm.nysed.gov/research-collections/history/erie-canal
- New York State and Library of Congress canal maps and shipping records should support the mechanism by which New York changed economic distance rather than merely gaining traffic.

Falsification: Baltimore and Philadelphia remain major commercial rivals. New York wins if the canal's market-integration effect explains the decade more powerfully than rival transport and finance systems.

### Chapter 7 — 1830s: Lowell

Core evidence:

- National Park Service, Lowell National Historical Park, **History & Culture**. Lowell concentrated water power, capital, machinery, labor, and production in a system that helped transform American industrialization. https://www.nps.gov/lowe/learn/historyculture/index.htm
- National Park Service, **The Mill Girls of Lowell**. The workforce and boardinghouse system illuminate both labor innovation and worker resistance. https://www.nps.gov/lowe/learn/historyculture/the-mill-girls-of-lowell.htm

Falsification: New York and Philadelphia were economically larger. Lowell wins only if the factory system itself—not total output—is judged the decade's more transferable institutional innovation.

### Chapter 8 — 1840s: St. Louis

Core evidence:

- National Park Service, Gateway Arch National Park history. By the 1840s St. Louis functioned as a major outfitting and transfer point for western movement. https://www.nps.gov/jeff/learn/historyculture/index.htm
- National Park Service material on Independence and overland trails documents travelers arriving via Missouri River steamboats and provisioning for routes west. https://www.nps.gov/hstr/learn/historyculture/trails.htm

Falsification: San Francisco becomes the obvious rival late in the decade through the gold rush, while New York remains stronger in finance and immigration. St. Louis wins only if the decade is interpreted primarily as the operationalization of continental expansion rather than the destination boom at its end.

### Chapter 9 — 1850s: Chicago

Core evidence:

- Encyclopedia of Chicago / Chicago History Museum material on railroad development: by the 1850s Chicago had become a terminus and transfer point for major eastern and western rail lines. http://www.encyclopedia.chicagohistory.org/pages/1039.html
- Chicago Board of Trade historical material on grain grading and standardized commodity markets should support the argument that Chicago converted geography into exchange infrastructure.

Falsification: New York remains the dominant port and financial center; Cincinnati and St. Louis are western rivals. Chicago wins if rail, grain, telegraphy, and standardized exchange together explain the emergence of a national market better than sheer commercial scale.

### Chapter 10 — 1860s: Washington

Core evidence:

- National Archives, **Emancipation Proclamation**. The January 1, 1863 proclamation changed the legal and military character of the war and authorized Black military service. https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/emancipation-proclamation
- U.S. Senate historical material on the Capitol during the Civil War documents the capital's transformation into barracks, hospital, administrative center, and symbol of Union continuity. https://www.senate.gov/artandhistory/history/common/generic/Civil_War_Capitol.htm

Falsification: Richmond is the strongest rival as Confederate capital and military target; New York matters through finance, draft resistance, and commerce. Washington wins if expansion of federal war, emancipation, taxation, conscription, administration, and citizenship capacity is the decade's dominant national transformation.

### Chapter 11 — 1870s: New Orleans

Core evidence:

- 64 Parishes / Louisiana Endowment for the Humanities, **Battle of Liberty Place**. On September 14, 1874, the White League attacked Republican forces, temporarily deposed the state government, and surrendered control only after federal intervention. https://64parishes.org/entry/battle-of-liberty-place
- 64 Parishes, **White League**. The paramilitary organization explicitly sought white political rule and used violence against Reconstruction governments and Republican constituencies. https://64parishes.org/entry/white-league
- National Park Service Reconstruction resources provide the broader context of anti-Black and anti-Republican violence across the South. https://www.nps.gov/subjects/reconstruction/index.htm

Falsification: Columbia, South Carolina, and other Reconstruction capitals are powerful rivals; Washington determined federal enforcement. New Orleans wins only if its interracial politics, urban armed overthrow attempt, federal restoration, and later retreat provide the clearest concentrated test of whether Reconstruction democracy could survive organized violence.

### Chapter 12 — 1880s: Chicago

Core evidence:

- Library of Congress, **Today in History — May 1**. Chicago's 1886 labor movement for the eight-hour day and the Haymarket events became enduring international labor symbols. https://www.loc.gov/item/today-in-history/may-01/
- Library of Congress Haymarket collections and trial records should anchor chronology and prevent mythology from replacing evidence about the bombing, police response, prosecution, and later pardons.

Falsification: Pittsburgh and other industrial cities can beat Chicago on production or strike history. Chicago wins if Haymarket's transmission into national and international labor politics makes the city's industrial conflict unusually consequential.

### Chapter 13 — 1890s: New York

Core evidence:

- Library of Congress, **Immigration to the United States, 1851 to 1900**. More than 70 percent of immigrants in the late nineteenth-century period entered through New York; Ellis Island opened in 1892. https://www.loc.gov/classroom-materials/united-states-history-primary-source-timeline/rise-of-industrial-america-1876-1900/immigration-to-united-states-1851-1900/
- Library of Congress, **New York City at the Turn of the Century**. The collection identifies immigration, 1898 consolidation, infrastructure growth, and construction as components of the emerging metropolis. https://www.loc.gov/collections/early-films-of-new-york-1898-to-1906/articles-and-essays/new-york-city-at-the-turn-of-the-century/
- Library of Congress / HABS, **Ellis Island**. The federal immigration station opened in 1892; the first complex burned in 1897 and was replaced by the 1900 facility. https://www.loc.gov/item/ny1252/

Falsification: Chicago may better represent industrial urbanism; Washington better represents imperial expansion at decade's end. New York wins if immigration, finance, media, consolidation, and density together best explain the emergence of the modern metropolitan nation.

### Chapter 14 — 1900s: Pittsburgh

Core evidence:

- National Park Service labor-history and Rivers of Steel materials on the Pittsburgh district should anchor steel output, integrated mills, labor, and the industrial landscape. https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/rivers-of-steel-national-heritage-area.htm
- U.S. Geological Survey and Census historical statistics should be used for any exact national steel share rather than repeating civic promotional figures.

Falsification: New York is the strongest rival through finance, immigration, corporate consolidation, and mass media. Pittsburgh wins only if steel as an enabling material for rail, buildings, machinery, bridges, and industrial scale is the more foundational mechanism.

### Chapter 15 — 1910s: Detroit

Core evidence:

- Ford historical materials on Highland Park document the 1913 moving assembly line and its dramatic reduction in Model T assembly time. https://corporate.ford.com/articles/history/moving-assembly-line.html
- Ford and labor-history sources on the 1914 five-dollar day should be used with the distinction between wage policy, turnover reduction, eligibility restrictions, and the company's intrusive social program.

Falsification: New York and Washington carry finance, migration, World War I, and federal mobilization. Detroit wins if the moving assembly line's transfer into mass-production practice is judged the decade's most durable economic operating change.

### Chapter 16 — 1920s: New York

Core evidence:

- Library of Congress, **Harlem Renaissance**. The 1920s and 1930s saw an exceptional concentration of Black literary, musical, visual, and intellectual production in Harlem. https://guides.loc.gov/harlem-renaissance
- Federal Reserve History, **Stock Market Crash of 1929**. The 1920s equity boom was centered on the New York Stock Exchange; the crash began the decade's final financial rupture. https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/stock-market-crash-of-1929

Falsification: Detroit represents mass consumption and Los Angeles mass entertainment. New York wins if the decade's most revealing bundle is the coexistence of cultural modernism, mass media, migration, consumer credit, finance, and speculative fragility.

### Chapter 17 — 1930s: Washington

Core evidence:

- FDIC, **1930–1939**. Roosevelt declared the national bank holiday in March 1933; the Emergency Banking Act established reopening rules; the Banking Act of 1933 created the FDIC. https://www.fdic.gov/history/1930-1939
- Social Security Administration, **Historical Development**. The Social Security Act of 1935 established national old-age insurance and federal-state unemployment insurance and expanded federal responsibility for economic security. https://www.ssa.gov/policy/docs/progdesc/sspus/histdev.html
- National Archives New Deal collections should support relief, regulatory, labor, and public-works claims.

Falsification: Detroit, New York, and Dust Bowl migration centers reveal the Depression's lived costs more directly. Washington wins if the permanent expansion of federal economic governance is the decade's most transferable institutional change.

### Chapter 18 — 1940s: Detroit

Core evidence:

- Detroit Historical Society, **The Arsenal of Democracy** and wartime industry materials. Detroit converted automobile manufacturing capacity into enormous military production. https://www.detroithistorical.org/learn/encyclopedia-of-detroit/arsenal-democracy
- Detroit Historical Society, **Race Riot of 1943**. Wartime prosperity coexisted with segregated housing, racial exclusion, migration pressure, and major violence. https://www.detroithistorical.org/learn/encyclopedia-of-detroit/race-riot-1943

Falsification: Washington directed mobilization; Los Alamos represents the atomic age; New York remained finance and media center. Detroit wins if converting mass-production capability into war materiel while exposing the racial limits of mobilization best captures the domestic war economy.

### Chapter 19 — 1950s: Los Angeles

Core evidence:

- Federal Highway Administration / Caltrans histories of the Interstate era and California freeway development should anchor highway chronology and federal financing. https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/interstate/history.cfm
- National Archives, **Servicemen's Readjustment Act (1944)** materials. The GI Bill expanded education and mortgage opportunity, while Black veterans frequently faced discriminatory implementation in housing and finance. https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/servicemens-readjustment-act
- Los Angeles City Planning historical housing material should support restrictive land-use, segregation, suburbanization, and postwar growth claims. https://planning.lacity.gov/resources/housing-element

Falsification: New York dominates television and corporate media; Detroit remains central to automobiles; Washington directs Cold War and Interstate spending. Los Angeles wins if the freeway-suburb-defense-aerospace-entertainment bundle best explains the postwar metropolitan model the country copied.

### Chapter 20 — 1960s: Birmingham

Core evidence:

- National Park Service, **Birmingham Civil Rights National Monument**. The A.G. Gaston Motel served as movement headquarters; Project C, mass protest, police dogs and fire hoses, and the 16th Street Baptist Church bombing made Birmingham a central national civil-rights site. https://www.nps.gov/bicr/learn/historyculture/index.htm
- National Park Service, presidential proclamation establishing the monument, which states that the 1963 campaign helped create momentum for the Civil Rights Act of 1964. https://www.nps.gov/bicr/learn/management/presidential-proclamation.htm
- National Archives, **Civil Rights Act of 1964**. https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/civil-rights-act

Falsification: Washington is the strongest decade-wide rival; Selma is the strongest movement rival. Birmingham wins only if local movement infrastructure plus nationally transmitted repression changed the political cost of federal delay more clearly than any other city. Do not claim Birmingham alone caused the Civil Rights Act.

### Chapter 21 — 1970s: Houston

Core evidence:

- U.S. Energy Information Administration historical energy data for oil prices, production, imports, and consumption. https://www.eia.gov/totalenergy/data/annual/
- Houston historical and municipal sources on the 1970s energy boom should support claims about headquarters, engineering, construction, and Sun Belt growth; exact market shares require EIA, Census, or industry statistical sources.
- NASA Johnson Space Center history provides the federal science and engineering layer that complicates a purely private-market Houston story. https://www.nasa.gov/johnson/

Falsification: Washington is the strongest rival through Watergate, Vietnam's aftermath, environmental law, monetary and energy policy; New York's fiscal crisis is a rival urban model. Houston wins if energy vulnerability, oil wealth, engineering, and Sun Belt growth form the decade's most consequential cross-system bundle.

### Chapter 22 — 1980s: New York

Core evidence:

- Federal Reserve and SEC historical sources should anchor takeover finance, high-yield debt, market structure, and the 1987 crash; precise claims about financial innovation require transaction-specific scholarship rather than retrospective shorthand.
- CDC, 1980s AIDS surveillance. New York was among the hardest-hit jurisdictions; contemporary surveillance documents the scale and unequal burden of the epidemic. https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/00001477.htm
- ACT UP New York archival material should support the movement's 1987 founding, tactics, and technical engagement with drug approval, pricing, and public health. https://actupny.com/

Falsification: Washington reshaped national tax, labor, defense, and regulatory policy; Silicon Valley represents the personal-computing transition. New York wins if financialization, postindustrial urban revival, AIDS, inequality, crime, and cultural production together provide the decade's most transferable metropolitan model.

### Chapter 23 — 1990s: Seattle

Core evidence:

- Seattle Municipal Archives, **WTO / 1999 protests**. The ministerial drew one of the city's largest political protests and generated emergency restrictions, arrests, and later criticism of policing. https://www.seattle.gov/cityarchives/exhibits-and-education/online-exhibits/wto
- World Trade Organization, **Third WTO Ministerial Conference, Seattle, 1999**. The meeting ran November 30–December 3, 1999. https://www.wto.org/english/thewto_e/minist_e/min99_e/min99_e.htm
- Company and port histories can establish Boeing, Microsoft, Amazon, Starbucks, and trans-Pacific trade chronology, but should not be used as neutral proof of Seattle's national rank.

Falsification: Silicon Valley is the strongest technology rival; New York remains finance/media center; Washington defines the post-Cold War state. Seattle wins only if the city genuinely concentrates globalization's beneficiaries, material infrastructure, digital economy, and organized backlash in one metropolitan system. Do not claim street protest alone caused the ministerial's negotiating failure; member-state disagreement was independently decisive.

### Chapter 24 — 2000s: New York

Core evidence:

- Federal Reserve History and Federal Reserve Bank of New York materials on September 11 document the physical, payment, liquidity, and market disruptions and emergency response. https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/federal-reserve-response-to-911
- Federal Reserve History, **Lehman Brothers Bankruptcy** and related crisis essays document the September 2008 systemic shock. https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/lehman-brothers-bankruptcy
- National Commission on Terrorist Attacks Upon the United States, **9/11 Commission Report**, for attack chronology and institutional findings. https://www.9-11commission.gov/report/
- Financial Crisis Inquiry Commission, **Final Report**, for mortgage securitization, leverage, shadow banking, ratings, regulation, and crisis transmission. https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-FCIC/pdf/GPO-FCIC.pdf

Falsification: Washington is the strongest rival because it made the wars, security laws, and rescue decisions. New York wins only if the decade's defining characteristic is the concentration and transmission of national risk through security-symbolic and financial networks based in the city. The chapter must preserve the moral distinction between terrorist attack and systemic financial failure.

### Chapter 25 — 2010s: San Francisco

Core evidence:

- California Public Utilities Commission, December 2012 rulemaking on online-enabled transportation services. The state opened a formal proceeding around companies including Lyft, Sidecar, and Uber, demonstrating that app-based urban services were already colliding with safety and carrier regulation. https://docs.cpuc.ca.gov/PublishedDocs/Published/G000/M039/K594/39594708.PDF
- San Francisco Planning Department, 2014 short-term-rental staff material. Listings on one platform rose from 1,595 in 2011 to 6,960 by January 2014, and the city treated platform-enabled short-term rental as a land-use and enforcement problem. https://commissions.sfplanning.org/cpcpackets/2014.0707T.pdf
- San Francisco Planning Department, **Housing Needs and Trends Report** (2018). High-wage employment growth outpaced regional housing production and intensified affordability and displacement pressure. https://sfplanning.org/resource/san-francisco-housing-needs-and-trends-report
- San Francisco Planning, **Community Stabilization**. The city explicitly links economic growth, income inequality, inadequate housing production, and displacement. https://sfplanning.org/community-stabilization-strategy

Falsification: Washington is the strongest political rival; the broader Silicon Valley region is the strongest technology rival. San Francisco wins only if platforms' translation of software into transportation, lodging, labor, housing, curb use, and regulation is more explanatory of the decade's everyday operating model than the Valley's hardware/software innovation considered more broadly.

### Chapter 26 — 2020s: Phoenix

Core evidence:

- National Institute of Standards and Technology / CHIPS for America, July 16, 2026. TSMC announced an additional $100 billion Arizona commitment, bringing planned U.S./Arizona investment to $265 billion. https://www.nist.gov/chips/tsmc-arizona-expands-us-investment-265-billion
- TSMC Arizona current project chronology: Phoenix selected in 2020; the first Arizona fab entered volume production in Q4 2024; later fabs and packaging capacity are under construction or planned. https://www.tsmc.com/static/english/arizona/index.htm
- Arizona Department of Water Resources, Phoenix Active Management Area groundwater model. Current 100-year analysis identifies substantial unmet groundwater demand under modeled conditions and constrains new assured-water-supply determinations based solely on groundwater. https://www.azwater.gov/phoenix-ama-groundwater-model
- City of Phoenix, **Heat Response Plan**. Phoenix now treats extreme heat through dedicated annual municipal planning, cooling, outreach, shade, and mitigation actions. https://www.phoenix.gov/administration/departments/heat/heat-response-programs/heat-response-plan.html
- U.S. Department of Commerce, November 2024 CHIPS award to TSMC Arizona, for the earlier three-fab / $65 billion phase and federal industrial-policy rationale. https://www.commerce.gov/news/press-releases/2024/11/biden-harris-administration-announces-chips-incentives-award-tsmc

Falsification: Washington is the leading rival and can still win the decade if constitutional conflict, federal political realignment, war, or another national institutional rupture proves more durable than the industrial-policy/climate-growth bundle. Austin, Detroit, New York, and Miami are secondary rivals. Phoenix remains the choice only if semiconductors, AI hardware demand, strategic supply-chain resilience, heat adaptation, water governance, housing, and Sun Belt growth continue to converge there through the decade's end.

## Book-level falsification tests

The thesis should be weakened or the selection method revised if any of the following survives serious review:

- The chosen city is merely the site of a famous event and cannot demonstrate a plausible transmission mechanism beyond publicity.
- A rival city carries more of the decade's decisive mechanisms without requiring special pleading.
- The chapter attributes a national law or economic transition to the city when the causal chain is demonstrably broader and the city is only incidental.
- The chapter's cost accounting is detachable from the mechanism rather than part of how the system operated.
- Repeated winners result from author familiarity rather than genuine institutional path dependence.
- Metropolitan-region evidence is being misleadingly presented as action by a municipal government.
- The 2020s chapter uses later announcements, proposed investment, or planning targets as if they were completed production or guaranteed outcomes.

## Remaining editorial verification queue

Before a publication candidate is frozen:

- mechanically count every numbered chapter with the repository's `check-book-length.py` regex and deepen any chapter below 3,000 words with new evidence or countercases rather than padding;
- line-check exact figures, dates, casualty counts, production shares, population figures, investment amounts, and superlatives against the source ledger;
- remove any heading structure inside chapter files that violates Desk's single-`#` chapter convention;
- perform a dedicated prior-art / comparable-title search before using novelty language in marketing;
- refresh every time-sensitive 2020s source and preserve the date of the final factual freeze;
- re-evaluate Phoenix against Washington and other rivals at final publication review rather than treating the current provisional verdict as locked.
