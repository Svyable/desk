# Research trail and evidentiary matrix

## Evidence class 1 — documented / directly established

- Diverse animals use geomagnetic information in controlled behavioral experiments. The exact sensory mechanisms differ or remain unresolved by taxon.
- European robin cryptochrome 4 shows magnetically sensitive photochemistry in vitro. Xu et al., *Nature* 2021: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-03618-9
- Weak anthropogenic electromagnetic noise disrupted European-robin magnetic compass orientation in a blinded, reversible shield/noise experiment. Engels et al., *Nature* 2014: https://doi.org/10.1038/nature13290
- The famous iron-rich pigeon-beak cells previously proposed as magnetosensitive neurons were shown to be macrophages. Treiber et al., *Nature* 2012: https://doi.org/10.1038/nature11046
- Loggerhead turtles can learn magnetic signatures, and 2025 experiments reported a dissociation in which radiofrequency oscillating fields disrupted compass behavior while learned magnetic map responses were not equivalently disrupted. Goforth et al., *Nature* 2025: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08554-y
- Human CRY2 can support a light-dependent magnetic behavioral response when expressed in a Drosophila system. Foley et al., *Nature Communications* 2011: https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms1364

## Evidence class 2 — credible but incomplete

- Wang et al. reported alpha-band EEG desynchronization in humans after particular ecologically relevant rotations of Earth-strength magnetic fields. The result used active-sham coil design and released data/code, but it does not establish conscious magnetic perception or navigation and requires independent multi-site replication. *eNeuro* 2019: https://doi.org/10.1523/ENEURO.0483-18.2019
- Avian compass behavior, light dependence, inclination sensitivity, CRY4 biophysics and radiofrequency disruption jointly support a radical-pair mechanism, but the complete in-vivo receptor-to-neuron chain has not been demonstrated.
- Trigeminal involvement in magnetic map processing in birds has substantial behavioral/neural evidence, while the peripheral receptor remains uncertain.
- Magnetite and other magnetic iron phases occur biologically, including reports in human brain tissue, but sensory function in humans is not established.

## Evidence class 3 — disputed / unresolved

- The precise anatomical identity of a magnetite-based vertebrate receptor.
- The exact in-vivo radical pair and whether CRY4 is the decisive avian compass receptor rather than one component of a larger system.
- The reproducibility, spectral thresholds and cross-laboratory generality of some radiofrequency-disruption effects.
- Whether reported human geomagnetic EEG responses replicate independently and reflect biological transduction rather than unrecognized artifact.
- Whether human cryptochromes contribute to any native magnetic sensing pathway.

## Evidence class 4 — speculative synthesis / open conjecture

- Humans may retain a weak or vestigial magnetic input that rarely reaches conscious awareness.
- Human magnetic transduction, if confirmed, could use a particle-based mechanism, a cryptochrome-based mechanism, more than one mechanism, or another mechanism not yet identified.
- The loss of useful magnetic navigation in humans could occur mainly through downstream neural reweighting rather than deletion of every receptor component.
- Modern electromagnetic and built environments could reduce the ecological usefulness of a weak magnetic input; this is a hypothesis, not an established cause of sensory loss.

## Contradictions and confounders

- A successful in-vitro cryptochrome experiment does not prove receptor deployment in a living animal.
- A magnetic mineral in tissue does not prove sensory function; contamination, iron metabolism and immune cells are major confounders.
- RF disruption of animal navigation is not evidence of injury or of human health effects at comparable field strengths.
- A magnetic behavior can be real while a favored receptor explanation is wrong; the pigeon-beak macrophage correction is the canonical example.
- Human EEG effects can be affected by induction, equipment, vibration, acoustic leakage, analysis flexibility or participant heterogeneity; active sham and independent hardware remain essential.
- Geomagnetic storms, local ferromagnetic structures, building wiring and radiofrequency background can alter experimental conditions.

## Research queue for deepening

- Wiltschko & Wiltschko primary 1972 compass work and later inclination-compass experiments.
- Ritz et al. 2004 radiofrequency resonance work plus blinded replication history.
- 2021–2026 CRY4 structural, expression and in-vivo candidate literature.
- Independent replications or failures of Wang et al. 2019 human EEG findings.
- Comparative trigeminal-map experiments and candidate receptor anatomy.
- Sea-turtle magnetic map learning and geomagnetic imprinting literature before and after the 2025 Nature paper.
- Salmon magnetic map/imprinting primary experiments.
- Subterranean mammal polarity-compass evidence.
- Magnetite biomineral provenance in human tissue and environmental magnetite contamination.
- Quantitative spin-chemistry constraints on radical lifetimes, hyperfine couplings, RF disruption windows and biological amplification.

## Source-quality rule

Patents, popular claims, wellness literature, anecdotal orientation ability and self-reported electromagnetic sensitivity are not treated as evidence of magnetoreception. Mechanism claims require peer-reviewed physical, molecular, anatomical, neural or behavioral support.
