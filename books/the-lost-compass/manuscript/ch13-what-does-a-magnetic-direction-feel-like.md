# What Does a Magnetic Direction Feel Like?

No one knows what a robin's magnetic field looks like to a robin.

The sentence sounds philosophical but it contains an experimental problem. A receptor produces signals; a nervous system turns those signals into behavior. We can measure the behavior without knowing the percept.

The radical-pair model is often illustrated as a visual pattern superimposed on the bird's normal field of view, perhaps a dim or bright modulation depending on head direction. This is a useful intuition, not an established subjective report. A retinal receptor could influence visual processing without producing anything like a conscious overlay.

The distinction matters in humans. If geomagnetic rotations alter alpha activity, subjects may still experience nothing reportable. Sensory transduction and sensory awareness are different stages.

Neuroscience already gives us analogies. Blindsight patients can respond to visual stimuli they deny seeing. Vestibular signals stabilize gaze without appearing as a separate conscious object. Proprioception is continuously used while rarely noticed. The brain can treat an input as a calibration variable rather than a thing.

A magnetic sense might work that way even in species that rely on it strongly. The field could bias directional circuits, visual salience, head-direction cells or spatial memory without becoming a named percept.

This creates a trap for experiments built around introspection. Asking a human to describe magnetic sensation assumes the signal enters awareness in a form language can capture. If the pathway instead adjusts spatial representations silently, performance tasks or neural measures may be more sensitive.

It also creates a trap for storytelling. We should not imagine what magnetic north “looks like” and then mistake the image for biology.

The receptor question and the qualia question are independent.

First establish transduction. Then establish neural pathway. Then behavior. Conscious experience comes last, if it comes at all.
