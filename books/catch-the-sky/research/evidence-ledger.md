# CATCH THE SKY — evidence ledger

Working rule: classification is a condition on access, not a substitute for mechanism.

## Evidence class 1 — verified / declassified / documented

### CORONA / DISCOVERER architecture

- President Eisenhower approved the CORONA film-return reconnaissance effort on 7 February 1958 according to the National Reconnaissance Office historical account.
- The public-facing DISCOVERER program supplied cover for the classified CORONA reconnaissance mission. CIA and U.S. Air Force institutional sources both describe this relationship.
- The first fully successful CORONA photographic mission launched 18 August 1960; its recovery vehicle returned the next day.
- A modified USAF C-119 recovered Discoverer XIV’s descending capsule in midair southwest of Hawaii. The National Museum of the U.S. Air Force describes this as the first midair recovery of an object returning from space.
- NRO history says the first successful photographic mission returned roughly 3,000 feet of film and covered about 1.65 million square miles of Soviet territory.
- NRO history records 190 CORONA film capsules intended for recovery, 165 recovered, and 161 recovered with operational quantities of exposed film.
- CORONA remained operational for almost twelve years and 145 missions; later systems improved ground resolution and used multiple return capsules.
- CORONA was declassified in 1995 and large imagery holdings were transferred for public archival/scientific use.

### Failure evidence

- Official CIA/NRO accounts document repeated early failures before usable imagery returned.
- The Air Force museum holds a parachute fragment from Discoverer XLIII associated with a failed midair catch in June 1962. This is useful because it physically documents a recovery failure without implying the whole mission architecture failed.

## Evidence class 2 — credible but incomplete reporting / testimony

At this opening stage the book does not require testimonial evidence to establish CORONA’s existence or basic recovery architecture. Oral histories of recovery crews and program personnel may become valuable for operational texture, but individual recollection will be checked against mission records where possible.

Potential lane:
- NRO `CORONA Star Catchers` oral-history material on the 6593rd Test Squadron recovery crews.

Do not use retrospective memory alone for exact mission numbers, dates, altitudes, payload masses or technical performance when program records exist.

## Evidence class 3 — disputed allegations / claims without independent confirmation

The following are explicitly outside the verified CORONA spine unless case-specific evidence is added:

- Claims that particular historical UFO sightings were CORONA/DISCOVERER recovery vehicles.
- Claims that modern UAP reports are descendants or covers for reconnaissance recovery systems.
- Claims that current U.S. intelligence programs use film-return or analogous physical-capture architectures.
- Claims that satellite recovery history establishes any alleged non-human crash-retrieval program.
- Claims about undisclosed CORONA payloads or missions not supported by released program records.

Historical possibility is not case attribution.

## Evidence class 4 — speculative synthesis / open conjecture

These are authorial tools, not historical findings:

### Interface burden
A working capability must cross interfaces with the world. Classification can restrict observation of those interfaces but cannot abolish interfaces required by the mechanism.

### The claim has joints
Extraordinary claims become more testable when decomposed at points where material, information, energy, custody or authority must pass from one subsystem to another. Joints create failure modes and possible receipts.

### Failure is mechanism-rich
Successful operations are often summarized as capability. Failures expose the actual sequence of dependencies. This is a methodological claim to be tested across cases, not assumed universally.

### Return path is part of the sensor
For CORONA specifically, collection without successful return and processing did not produce usable photographic intelligence. For modern digital sensors, the analogous path may be downlink/storage/processing rather than physical recovery. Do not universalize the film bucket.

## Contradiction and incentive audit

1. **Institutional histories celebrate successful programs.** CIA, NRO and Air Force museum sources have incentives to emphasize ingenuity and strategic value. Use their records heavily for architecture and dates, but seek contemporaneous/declassified mission records for performance claims and contested causal claims.
2. **Failure-count wording varies.** NRO describes twelve failed missions plus a successful test vehicle without film; CIA says the first thirteen missions returned no usable imagery. Preserve the difference rather than manufacturing a single failure taxonomy.
3. **Coverage statistics vary by definition.** CIA pages use different aggregate image/area figures depending on what is counted. Avoid mixing lifetime image counts, unique ground area, cumulative photographed area and mission coverage.
4. **`Missile gap` language can become too clean.** CORONA imagery materially improved strategic-force estimates, but the political history of the missile gap involved prior intelligence, public rhetoric and estimation disputes. Do not write `one photograph ended the missile gap`.
5. **Cover is not total deception.** DISCOVERER involved real launches and real technology. The hidden purpose does not make every public statement false.

## Publication holds

Do not publish as fact until primary reconstruction supports them:

- exact recovery altitude for every early capsule;
- exact number of aircraft passes for missions other than Discoverer XIV;
- exact share of capsules caught in air versus recovered by water contingency;
- claims that a specific named UFO case was a CORONA recovery vehicle;
- current classified reconnaissance return/recovery practices;
- claims about modern UAP sensor records not actually released;
- causal claims that CORONA alone determined a named presidential arms-control decision.

## Defeat condition

The book’s broad methodological thesis should be revised if comparison with later digital reconnaissance shows that the proposed `interface burden` is mostly an artifact of film-return technology and does not generalize in a useful way. The narrower CORONA history survives either way. The book should also abandon any implication that visible residue scales simply with program size if documented black programs show durable, high-tempo capability with materially fewer external interfaces than the framework predicts.
