# Chronology

- **1950s–1970s:** U.S. strategic-submarine communications research drives increasingly low-frequency concepts, including Project Sanguine and later ELF architectures.
- **April 5, 1979:** GAO publicly reviews Navy strategic communications, including TACAMO and the Navy’s ELF case.
- **2000s:** Long-baseline accelerator-neutrino facilities demonstrate routine transmission of neutrino beams through substantial Earth over hundreds of kilometers for physics research.
- **September 2009:** Patrick Huber posts *Submarine neutrino communication*, analyzing a high-energy neutrino concept using a muon storage ring and submarine-scale detection approaches.
- **March–April 2012:** MINERvA/NuMI collaboration reports the first experimental digital communication link using neutrinos. The encoded word is `neutrino`; the link spans 1.035 km including 240 m of earth and achieves about 0.1 decoded bit/s with ~1% bit-error rate.
- **2019:** MINERvA completes its main physics data-taking program; Fermilab documentation continues to preserve the communication demonstration as an ancillary proof of concept.
- **2026:** No public evidence located in this run establishes an operational naval neutrino communications system. The public technical record remains dominated by proof-of-concept demonstration and feasibility analysis rather than fleet deployment.
