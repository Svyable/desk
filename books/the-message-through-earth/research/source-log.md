# Source Log

Checked 2026-09-14. Sources below are public and lawfully available.

## Primary / authoritative technical

1. D. D. Stancil et al., “Demonstration of Communication using Neutrinos,” *Modern Physics Letters A* 27 (2012), DOI 10.1142/S0217732312500770. Experimental neutrino communications link; 1.035 km path including 240 m of earth; decoded rate 0.1 bit/s; bit-error rate about 1%. https://doi.org/10.1142/S0217732312500770
2. Fermilab MINERvA, “Photos and graphics,” including the collaboration’s description of the first neutrino communication test and encoding of the word `neutrino`. https://minerva.fnal.gov/photos-and-graphics/
3. Fermilab MINERvA, “How it works,” for NuMI beam production, shielding/rock path, detector location, and interaction/reconstruction context. https://minerva.fnal.gov/how-it-works/
4. Patrick Huber, “Submarine neutrino communication,” arXiv:0909.4554. Engineering proposal for one-way communication to submerged submarines using high-energy neutrinos from a muon storage ring; discusses direct muon and Cherenkov-based receiver concepts. https://arxiv.org/abs/0909.4554

## Government / strategic communications

5. U.S. Government Accountability Office, *Navy’s Strategic Communications System*, April 5, 1979. Contemporary review of Navy strategic submarine communications, TACAMO and ELF arguments. https://www.gao.gov/products/109021
6. National Academies, *Distributed Remote Sensing for Naval Undersea Warfare: Abbreviated Version* (2007). Public executive-level evidence for continuing naval interest in undersea sensing and for the existence of classified technical detail in the broader domain; not evidence of neutrino communications deployment. https://doi.org/10.17226/11927

## Scientific context

7. Fermilab MINERvA homepage and experiment documentation, for detector mission and neutrino-interaction measurements. https://minerva.fnal.gov/
8. Fermilab, “MINERvA successfully completes its physics run” (2019), for experiment history and infrastructure context. https://news.fnal.gov/2019/04/minerva-successfully-completes-its-physics-run/

## Claim boundaries

- A published proposal is evidence of proposal, not deployment.
- The 2012 Fermilab communication experiment is evidence that information can be encoded in an accelerator neutrino beam and decoded after passage through substantial matter. It is not evidence of a practical submarine system.
- The GAO record establishes the strategic communications problem and historical ELF/TACAMO comparison; it does not establish neutrino-program interest.
- No source in this ledger is used to claim a classified operational neutrino communications system.
- Patents, if added later, will be treated as evidence of filing and disclosed concept only, not efficacy.
