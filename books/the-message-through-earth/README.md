**Reader promise.** This book follows one of the strangest communications ideas ever taken seriously: using neutrinos to send information through rock, ocean, and potentially the planet itself. The physics is real. A digital neutrino link was demonstrated at Fermilab in 2012. The strategic problem is also real: submerged ballistic-missile submarines are difficult to reach without asking them to expose themselves. The surprise is that the property that makes neutrinos nearly ideal as carriers—their indifference to matter—also makes them ruinously difficult to create and detect.

**Distinct contribution.** The book treats neutrino communication as a control case for claims about exotic military technology. It separates physical possibility from system utility, and asks what infrastructure, energy, geometry, receiver burden, signaling doctrine, and survivability would have to exist before a laboratory proof became a strategic capability.

**Strongest competing explanation.** The topic is less a hidden military technology than an elegant physics demonstration attached to a communication niche already served by VLF/ELF, TACAMO, satellites, buoys, and operational doctrine. If so, neutrino communication matters chiefly because it reveals why penetrating signals are expensive.
