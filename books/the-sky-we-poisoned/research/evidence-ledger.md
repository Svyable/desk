# Evidence ledger

## Class 1 — verified / declassified / documented

- Operation Argus detonated three low-yield nuclear devices at high altitude over the South Atlantic in August–September 1958 to investigate artificial radiation belts. Defense Nuclear Agency retrospective history: https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/GOVPUB-D15-PURL-gpo222813
- Artificial belts from Argus were directly measured; a NASA historical review places the narrow belts near L≈1.7, 2.1 and 2.17 and notes lifetimes on the order of weeks. https://ntrs.nasa.gov/archive/nasa/casi.ntrs.nasa.gov/19720009975.pdf
- Starfish Prime detonated July 9, 1962, at approximately 400 km altitude with a yield around 1.4 Mt. It produced an intense artificial electron belt persisting for more than a year. https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19630004838 ; https://doi.org/10.1029/2026JA035724
- O'Brien, Laughlin and Van Allen reported geomagnetically trapped radiation following Starfish in *Nature* in September 1962. https://doi.org/10.1038/195939a0
- Contemporaneous measurements documented ionospheric, geomagnetic, VLF/LF, radio-propagation and artificial-auroral effects over large distances.
- The Limited Test Ban Treaty was signed August 5, 1963 and entered into force October 10, 1963, prohibiting nuclear explosions in the atmosphere, underwater and outer space for parties. https://2001-2009.state.gov/t/ac/trt/4797.htm
- Lawrence Livermore's Topanga simulations reproduced important features of the Starfish diamagnetic cavity and E3 EMP; peer-reviewed 2025 JGR paper: https://doi.org/10.1029/2024JA033681
- Olifer & Mann (2026) reconstructed historical electron fluxes and found evidence consistent with Kennel–Petschek-like self-limiting behavior below ~2 MeV. https://doi.org/10.1029/2026JA035724

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete

- Telstar 1's ultimate failure was materially accelerated by enhanced trapped radiation following Starfish, but the spacecraft's component-level history must be treated separately from a simple “Starfish killed Telstar” slogan. See Lanzerotti 2011: https://doi.org/10.1029/2011SW000739
- Historical lists attribute failures or degradation of Ariel 1, TRAAC, Transit 4B, Injun 1 and other spacecraft to Starfish-enhanced radiation; each case needs its own engineering record and timing analysis.
- Starfish electron injection estimates of order 10^26 beta electrons are model-based reconstructions rather than direct particle counts.
- Soviet 1962 high-altitude tests also produced artificial belts, but English-language open documentation is thinner than for U.S. tests.

## Class 3 — disputed / simplified / unconfirmed

- “Starfish knocked out one-third of all satellites” is a useful warning but not a primary failure analysis. APS summarizes that at least one-third of the 24 spacecraft then in orbit were damaged; the manuscript will not convert that aggregate statement into certainty about every named satellite without engineering records.
- “Starfish blacked out Hawaii” is overbroad. Historical accounts document streetlight failures, alarm/communications disruption and electrical effects, not a modern island-wide grid-collapse analogue.
- Precise projections for a contemporary high-altitude nuclear burst are scenario-dependent and cannot be established by scaling 1962 outcomes linearly.
- Public discussion of modern HANE weapons effects should not be treated as evidence of current classified operational plans.

## Class 4 — speculative synthesis / open conjecture

- Artificial radiation belts constitute an early form of deliberate/inadvertent planetary engineering: a local detonation created a nonlocal, persistent operating environment.
- Modern orbital density converts a once-limited technological externality into a potentially systemic infrastructure risk.
- Natural plasma self-limiting processes may cap some artificial-belt fluxes, but a cap is not safety; duration, energy spectrum and orbital intersection can still matter enormously.
- Historical nuclear-space data may be uniquely valuable as validation data for models of extreme natural radiation events, because modern experiments cannot ethically reproduce the same forcing.
