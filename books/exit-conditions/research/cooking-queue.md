# Cooking queue — Exit Conditions

This is the next-draft queue, not a claim that research is complete.

## Next cluster: Chapters 2–4

### Chapter 2 — Starts Have Owners; Endings Have Orphans

Find three end-of-life cases where the actor responsible for closure differs from the actor that captured the productive-phase benefit. Prefer one industrial/infrastructure case, one technology/service case, and one institutional case. Research orphaned obligations, bonding/trust mechanisms, successor liability, and final-user transition.

### Chapter 3 — The Sunk-Cost Machine

Build from primary escalation-of-commitment research into documented organizational cases. Separate the psychological sunk-cost effect from structural incentives such as appropriations, career identity, contractual milestones, political reputation, and accounting treatment.

### Chapter 4 — Kill Criteria

Find organizations that publish or document predeclared stop rules for experiments, R&D programs, clinical development, or capital projects. The chapter needs at least one case where a kill rule prevented escalation and one where a rigid stop would plausibly have destroyed a valuable project.

## Engineering cluster: Chapters 5–8

Research emergency-stop standards, process-safety shutdown systems, graceful service termination, automatic protective trips, and NASA/aviation abort windows. Favor official standards, regulator incident reports, and postmortems over vendor explainers.

The central question for the cluster is: what state must exist **after** the stop for the stop to count as successful?

## Institutional cluster: Chapters 9–13

For sunset clauses, use actual statutory text and reauthorization history.

For permanent programs, choose a case with an explicitly temporary or bounded original rationale and trace how the rationale changed. Avoid "old therefore bad."

For bankruptcy, distinguish Chapter 7, Chapter 11, receivership, and ordinary dissolution.

For war termination, research objectives and decision processes rather than writing a retrospective "they should have left on date X" argument.

For corporate wind-down, find a company whose final obligations materially outlasted demand for its product.

## Residue cluster: Chapters 14–18

Prioritize decommissioning funds, reclamation bonds, Superfund/orphan liabilities, end-of-life product support, clinical stopping/transition protocols, and data custody after shutdown.

The cluster should make the phrase **residual obligation** concrete enough that it is not merely metaphor.

## Final synthesis: Chapters 19–21

Do not draft the final framework until at least fifteen evidence chapters exist. The synthesis should be induced from cases rather than imposed on them.

Target final test case: one system where both continuation and stopping were defensible in real time, the downside of each was meaningful, and later outcome knowledge must be carefully bracketed.
