# The Psychic That Wasn't Useful

## Why Intelligence Agencies Kept Testing an Effect They Could Not Operationalize

**Sven Hardy Benson**

### Thesis

The most interesting fact about the U.S. government's remote-viewing programs is neither that intelligence agencies studied psychics nor that the programs were eventually terminated. It is that the evidentiary record permits two propositions to coexist without resolving into either believer folklore or skeptical dismissal: some controlled anomalous-cognition experiments produced statistical results that serious evaluators regarded as difficult to reduce to chance, while the same phenomenon remained too inconsistent, underspecified, and operationally noisy to become dependable intelligence. The book asks what happens when an apparent effect can survive a laboratory argument but fail the user's test.

This is not a book claiming psychic powers are proven. It is not a book claiming STARGATE was a sham. It treats the program as an unusually rich natural experiment in the difference between **statistical anomaly, mechanism, replication, task performance, and operational utility**.

### Evidence classes

1. **Verified / documented:** CIA, DIA and Army sponsorship; GRILL FLAME/STARGATE records; operational tasking records; the 1995 AIR evaluation; program termination; published ganzfeld and anomalous-cognition experiments and critiques.
2. **Credible but incomplete:** evaluator judgments that some laboratory results were statistically anomalous; practitioner and intelligence-user recollections; claims of individual operational successes where records permit partial reconstruction.
3. **Disputed / unconfirmed:** robust extrasensory information transfer as a settled scientific phenomenon; famous operational hits reconstructed retrospectively; claims of reliable intelligence-grade remote viewing; claims of a known physical mechanism.
4. **Speculative synthesis:** anomalous cognition may represent a weak effect, an unresolved methodological artifact, an interaction between selection and scoring, or some combination not captured by the binary psychic/not-psychic fight. The intelligence program matters because it forces the harder question: what would an anomaly have to do before anyone should trust it?

### Central defeat condition

The book's thesis fails if the primary record shows either that the experimental anomaly disappears under adequately powered, preregistered, independently replicated protocols, **or** that blinded prospective operational trials demonstrate repeatable decision advantage large enough to survive base rates, analyst comparison, cue leakage, and scoring discretion. Either result would simplify the story. The surviving historical record does not yet do so.
