# Evidence ledger — The Copper Sky

## Class 1: verified / documented

- MIT Lincoln Laboratory records Project West Ford as a passive orbital communications experiment beginning from a 1958 concept.
- Lincoln Laboratory records the October 1961 deployment as unsuccessful and the May 1963 deployment as substantially successful.
- Lincoln Laboratory reports communication through the belt at data rates up to 20,000 bps during favorable early density.
- Contemporary scientific reporting records organized astronomical criticism and subsequent Space Science Board assessment.
- Active communications satellites displaced passive dipole-belt communication.

## Class 2: credible but incomplete

- Exact national-security weighting of cable-cut, jamming, solar-disturbance, and nuclear-effects scenarios.
- Full chain of U.S. diplomatic consultation before each launch.
- Exact long-tail debris population attributable to failed dispersion/clumping.

## Class 3: disputed or unsupported

- West Ford permanently contaminated orbit with hundreds of millions of intact needles.
- West Ford caused serious demonstrated optical or radio astronomy damage.
- West Ford alone produced specific Outer Space Treaty clauses.

## Class 4: synthesis / conjecture

- West Ford can be read as an early abundance-based orbital network whose environmental politics prefigure modern constellation disputes.
- Technical controllability and institutional legibility may be underappreciated reasons active satellites became easier to govern than passive belts.

## Defeat conditions

The book's environmental-governance thesis weakens if archival records show extensive international and astronomical consultation before deployment comparable to modern impact review; if the program's scale or performance was substantially misreported by official technical histories; or if the supposed connection to later consultation norms disappears under primary treaty-drafting records.
