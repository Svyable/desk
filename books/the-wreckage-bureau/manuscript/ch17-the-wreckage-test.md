# The Wreckage Test

By now the method can be stated without mystery.

A crash-retrieval claim should be evaluated as a claim about an exploitation system, not as a referendum on whether UFOs are real.

The first step is event identity.

What exactly is the alleged recovery? Date, place, object count, condition, first responders, sponsoring authority, and the earliest documented account. Do not begin with origin. Begin with geometry and time.

The second step is acquisition path.

Who took custody? Under what authority? Was the recovery domestic or foreign? Which unit, agency, or contractor had physical possession first? What transport was used? Were local institutions displaced from the scene? Did any known military crash-recovery, hazardous-material, nuclear, intelligence, or technical-exploitation organization plausibly fit the described activity?

The third step is custody.

Where did the object go next? Which facilities could physically accept it? Which people signed for it or were briefed? If only fragments were recovered, how were they tagged and divided? If biological material existed, how was it preserved?

The fourth step is exploitation.

What measurements were performed? Which specialties participated? What were the first hypotheses? What failed? Which results replicated? What instrument data survives? Were independent labs used? Which specimens were consumed?

The fifth step is program structure.

What was the sponsoring authority? Was the effort a SAP, CAP, SCI compartment, special project, contractor program, or something else? Was it formally established or merely proposed? Who had oversight? What budget mechanism sustained it? How did the program change names or sponsors over time?

The sixth step is downstream consequence.

What knowledge escaped the compartment in sanitized form? Did tactics, sensors, materials research, manufacturing, threat assessments, or operational procedures change? If the program supposedly achieved reverse engineering, what did “achieved” mean in practice?

The seventh step is origin.

Only now ask whether the object was non-human.

This order is deliberately inconvenient. It prevents origin from doing explanatory work before the evidence earns it.

Each step can be assigned an evidence class.

Class one: verified, declassified, documented, or physically demonstrated.

Class two: credible but incomplete reporting or testimony, including claims from people plausibly positioned to know but lacking public corroboration.

Class three: disputed allegation without independent confirmation.

Class four: speculative synthesis or open conjecture.

The classes belong to individual propositions, not whole witnesses.

A witness can provide class-one evidence that a program existed and class-three evidence about what the program contained. A government report can provide class-one evidence that an investigation occurred and only class-two evidence for its negative conclusion if the underlying classified search cannot be independently audited. A laboratory can provide class-one composition data for a sample while provenance remains class three.

This granular classification is the only way I know to keep controversial subjects from turning into teams.

Apply the test to HAVE DOUGHNUT.

Event identity: known aircraft, known acquisition pathway through an ally.

Custody: documented U.S. possession at Groom Lake.

Exploitation: declassified flight testing, analysis, disassembly, tactical evaluation.

Program structure: DIA, Air Force, Navy, Foreign Technology Division involvement.

Downstream consequence: combat tactics and training.

Origin: Soviet-designed MiG-21.

The chain is dense.

Apply it to AZORIAN.

Target identity: K-129 wreck.

Acquisition path: U.S. intelligence located it after Soviet search failure.

Custody: partial recovery by purpose-built ship.

Exploitation: much remains classified, but the intelligence objective and partial success are documented.

Program structure: CIA with Defense Department support and private contractors under commercial cover.

Downstream consequence: intelligence gained and engineering capabilities developed.

Origin: Soviet submarine, independently coherent with the vessel’s history.

Again, dense.

Now apply the test to the strongest public version of a multi-decade non-human crash-retrieval program.

Program existence: alleged by Grusch and others; denied by AARO after investigation.

Physical inventory: alleged craft and biologics; no publicly available specimen with established government crash-retrieval custody and demonstrated non-human origin.

Facilities and contractors: alleged, with some details reportedly provided in classified channels; public proof incomplete.

Exploitation: alleged reverse-engineering activity; technical outputs not established publicly.

Oversight: contested, with allegations of improper concealment and government assertions that authentic programs were appropriately reported.

Origin: non-human, based publicly on attributed assessments rather than open physical demonstration.

The chain is sparse where the claim is strongest.

That does not mean false.

It means unresolved at the public level.

This language matters because “unresolved” is not a consolation prize for believers or skeptics. It is a precise description of an evidentiary state. A claim can be important enough to investigate and too weak to assert.

The wreckage test also predicts what would change my mind.

A firsthand technical witness with verifiable access could move several propositions upward, especially if specific program records corroborated the account.

A specimen with continuous chain of custody from an official recovery and reproducible properties inconsistent with known terrestrial manufacture would move the physical case dramatically.

Independent laboratories replicating an anomalous functional property would matter more than exotic composition alone.

A secure congressional or inspector-general finding that verified a specific hidden custody program could establish program existence even before technical details were public.

Conversely, a detailed source-genealogy showing that key crash-retrieval allegations all trace to a small circular network would reduce confidence.

Records demonstrating that named programs were unrelated black projects would reduce confidence in those specific claims.

Material samples failing adversarial testing should be removed from the positive corpus.

A theory that cannot say what would lower its confidence is not investigating. It is recruiting.

The wreckage test is intentionally boring because boring methods are the only ones strong enough for extraordinary material.

If non-human technology is sitting in a warehouse, it deserves better than belief.

It deserves inventory.
