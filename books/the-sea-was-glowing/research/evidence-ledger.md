# Evidence ledger — THE SEA WAS GLOWING

## Governing thesis

Milky seas are a control case for anomaly science: a historically folkloric witness class survived translation into satellite measurement, but confirmation of the event class did not close its biological mechanism. The book asks when an anecdote becomes retrospectively testable because it preserved time, place and phenomenology, and when observational geometry—not small physical scale—explains why science samples a phenomenon poorly.

## Class 1 — verified / documented

- Miller et al., PNAS (2005), “Detection of a bioluminescent milky sea from space.” The DMSP Operational Linescan System archival imagery contains a ~15,400 km² luminous feature off Somalia corresponding to the S.S. Lima’s 25 January 1995 report and persisting across multiple nights. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0507253102
- Nealson & Hastings, AEM (2006), commentary on bacterial quorum sensing and the scale problem. https://doi.org/10.1128/AEM.72.4.2295-2297.2006
- Miller et al., Scientific Reports (2021), “Honing in on bioluminescent milky seas from space.” Modern VIIRS Day/Night Band candidate detections include the enormous 2019 Java event and review the accidental 1985 research-vessel encounter. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-021-94823-z
- Miller, PNAS (2022), “Boat encounter with the 2019 Java bioluminescent milky sea: Views from on-deck confirm satellite detection.” Ganesha crew testimony, vessel track and photographs independently connect a surface encounter to the satellite-observed 2019 Java event. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2207612119
- NASA Earth Observatory (2021), “Hunting Milky Seas by Satellite,” useful authoritative history of the DMSP→VIIRS detection path and historical-report compilation. https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/hunting-milky-seas-by-satellite-149017/
- Hudson et al., Earth and Space Science (2025), “From Sailors to Satellites: A Curated Database of Bioluminescent Milky Seas Spanning 1600-Present.” Current historical/satellite database and explicit curation methods. https://doi.org/10.1029/2024EA004082

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete

- Historical ship reports with recoverable date/location/phenomenology but no independent instrumental record.
- The 1985 research-vessel encounter as evidence for luminous bacteria associated with a milky-sea event; exact taxonomic history and sampling chain need primary-paper reconstruction before chapter-level claims.
- Bacterial quorum sensing as a plausible component of sustained emission. The scale, substrate, population density, optical depth and persistence required for full events remain a quantitative burden.
- Geographic clustering in the northwestern Indian Ocean / Maritime Continent. Must be corrected for shipping lanes, archive availability, cloud cover, satellite duty cycle and detection thresholds before ecological inference.

## Class 3 — disputed / unconfirmed

- Every historical “milky sea” account belongs to the same physical phenomenon.
- A single bacterial species explains all events.
- Satellite candidates without surface confirmation are all bioluminescent milky seas.
- Historical rarity equals physical rarity.
- Literary references are independent observations rather than retellings.

## Class 4 — speculative synthesis / open conjecture

**Sampling-regime anomaly.** Some large anomalies remain poorly integrated because they are rare in time, remote in geography, and mismatched to the duty cycle of the instruments capable of mechanism-level sampling.

**Archive as delayed instrument.** A structured witness record can acquire new evidentiary value when later sensors permit independent retrospective testing. This does not upgrade unstructured or unfalsifiable testimony.

**Translation survival.** A claim becomes more interesting when peculiar features survive across sensor classes: location, boundary, scale, persistence and steady luminosity. Features that do not translate remain witness-level claims.

**Pixel-to-bottle closure.** The event class will materially advance when a satellite-detected event is intercepted prospectively by an instrumented vessel that measures radiance/spectrum, water-column structure, microbial community, chemistry, currents and controls across the boundary.

## Contradictions / incentives / traps

- Targeted archival searching after reading the 1995 Lima report can inflate apparent evidentiary independence; the 2019 satellite-first/crew-later sequence has a different dependency structure and should not be rhetorically merged with 1995.
- Modern sensors may preferentially detect large, persistent, cloud-free events. Historical witnesses may preferentially report spectacular events on busy routes. Both archives are selected.
- “Bioluminescent” is an emission category, not a completed ecological mechanism.
- A bacterial sample from one encounter cannot establish the cause of every event.
- Do not use the case as a generic warrant for UFO, cryptid, psi or other anomaly testimony. Its lesson is narrower: preserve testable structure and demand independent translation.

## Publication holds

Before quantitative prose beyond Chapter 1, extract for each satellite event: start/end dates; cloud screening; lunar illumination; sensor spectral response; radiance threshold; area-estimation method; current/eddy association; false-positive controls; surface corroboration; and candidate classification rule.

For historical reports: original source, transcription chain, vessel, date/time, coordinates or route reconstruction, moon/weather, duration, boundary language, wake response, color, number of observers, independence from earlier accounts.

For mechanism: organism identification method, historical taxonomy, concentration, quorum threshold, substrate association, emission spectrum, per-cell photon budget, optical attenuation, mixed-layer depth, biomass requirement and whether the observed radiance is quantitatively achievable.

## Strongest countermodel

There is no single deep anomaly. “Milky sea” may be a human category spanning multiple luminous processes. The best-confirmed large events may be ordinary microbial ecology seen under rare conditions; historical clustering may largely follow shipping/reporting bias; and the apparent centuries-long scientific delay may reflect rational sampling economics rather than disciplinary neglect. The book loses its stronger sampling-regime claim if corrected event rates, observing effort and interception costs show that conventional marine science had a realistic probability of repeated mechanism-quality encounters and simply ignored them.

## Defeat-capable test

Preregister a prospective satellite alert/intercept protocol. For candidate events, freeze the satellite classification before ship arrival. Sample luminous core, boundary, nearby dark control and depth profile; sequence microbial communities; measure calibrated spectrum/radiance and physical oceanography. Repeat across multiple events. If candidate satellite signatures repeatedly fail to correspond to the surface phenomenology, or if mutually incompatible mechanisms dominate different events, narrow or abandon the unified class. If a reproducible biological/physical mechanism closes the chain, retire “anomaly” rather than protecting it.
