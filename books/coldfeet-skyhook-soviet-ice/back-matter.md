# Back Matter

## A note on names

U.S. sources variously render the Soviet drifting station as NP-8, NP8, SP-8, or North Pole 8 depending on transliteration and publication context. This draft uses **NP-8** except when preserving the terminology of a cited source.

## Evidentiary boundary

COLDFEET is unusually well documented as an operation and incompletely documented in public as a claim-by-claim technical exploitation result. The manuscript therefore treats the existence, personnel, dates, extraction system, general haul, and broad intelligence finding as strongly documented while reserving judgment on the exact significance of particular recovered instruments until the contemporary ONI/exploitation record is fully page-indexed.

## Further reading

The core narrative should ultimately rest on William M. Leary's history of Fulton and COLDFEET, the declassified contemporary ONI review, CIA historical and museum material, Leonard LeSchack's published account of Skyhook retrieval, and Soviet/Western Arctic scientific archives rather than on later spy-history retellings.

The scientific context belongs equally to the history of Arctic observation. NSIDC's archived Soviet drifting-station tracks, rawinsonde observations, sea-ice records, and Environmental Working Group atlases are especially useful for preventing the station from being reduced to a spy-story prop.
