# Chronology

- **1937** — Soviet Union establishes its first North Pole drifting station.
- **1940s–1950s** — Airborne pickup concepts evolve from mail and wartime recovery systems; Robert Fulton develops a balloon-line airborne recovery method with support connected to U.S. naval research.
- **1954 onward** — Soviet drifting-station operations become recurrent/continuous enough to create long Arctic observational series.
- **1958** — *USS Nautilus* transits beneath the North Pole, demonstrating a new strategic submarine geography. Fulton system reaches human-test maturity; CIA histories identify Staff Sgt. Levi Woods as first human Skyhook test pickup on August 12.
- **1959** — NP-8 established on Arctic pack ice.
- **1961** — U.S. planners pursue an abandoned Soviet drift-station exploitation concept; later CIA history describes ONR as initial sponsor and a subsequent target change.
- **March 19, 1962** — Period U.S. naval account reports NP-8 abandoned, probably because runway breakup made operation untenable.
- **Spring 1962** — CIA assumes responsibility after ONR funding is exhausted; Intermountain Aviation and a Fulton-equipped B-17 are brought into the operation.
- **May 28, 1962** — James F. Smith and Leonard A. LeSchack parachute onto NP-8.
- **May 28–June 2, 1962** — Team photographs station, examines facilities, selects documents/samples/equipment; planned stay extends because of weather.
- **June 2, 1962** — B-17 performs Skyhook recoveries of intelligence cargo and both men in poor visibility/high winds.
- **August 1962** — John Cadwalader's contemporary *ONI Review* article, `Operation Coldfeet: An Investigation of the Abandoned Soviet Arctic Drift Station NP 8`, appears; complete text remains a primary recovery target for this draft.
- **March 1966** — LeSchack publishes `Skyhook Retrieval` in *U.S. Naval Institute Proceedings*.
- **1990s** — William M. Leary reconstructs Fulton/Skyhook/COLDFEET history using participant interviews and declassified Navy material; publication appears through CIA's *Studies in Intelligence*.
- **1995 onward** — U.S.-Russian Environmental Working Group effort releases and combines substantial Cold War Arctic scientific observations for research use.
- **2008** — CIA commemorates COLDFEET participants and unveils artwork depicting the operation.
- **2020s** — CIA museum/history pages continue to release operational details and the technological lineage of Skyhook, while Arctic scientific archives preserve Soviet drifting-station records for modern research.
