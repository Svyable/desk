## Author’s Note

This book starts from a simple asymmetry: machine intelligence may become cheap much faster than electricity, land, housing, water, construction, permits, maintenance, and institutional coordination.

It is not a prediction that one AI capability arrives on one timetable. It is an argument about complements. If cognition becomes cheaper, the physical and institutional systems required to act on it become more important. If AI progress is slower than expected, those systems remain useful anyway.

The book therefore treats public works as durable shared capacity rather than a jobs program or a monument to spending. Its question is practical: if the country becomes more productive, what should become easier to build, maintain, connect, and use?

Forecasts in the chapters are scenarios, not certainties. The source trail and falsification notes live in the project’s research directory.
