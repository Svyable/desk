Shared compromise risk is not an argument for isolation; it is an argument for segmentation, diversity, rapid revocation, and rehearsed degradation. Islanded forces avoid some contagion and accept blindness. The adult design accepts contagion risk and prices the insurance.

Cyber effects on port terminals, rail schedulers, and warehouse systems turn software into terrain. Alliances that exercise only against kinetic attacks on bridges will be surprised by ransomware that achieves similar delay with fewer photographs.

Cable geography returns in every cloud briefing that pretends otherwise. Landing stations, repair ships, and diverse routes are alliance infrastructure. So are agreements about who repairs what under fire or under gray-zone cutting.

Influence operations that exaggerate partner unreliability aim at the trust layer. Authentication, liaison, and refusal to reward panic are countermeasures as real as firewalls.

Over-sharing and under-sharing are twin vulnerabilities. One burns sources; the other burns time. Playbooks that define both edges reduce improvisation under television lights.

Quantum risk on stored traffic argues for crypto agility now, across vendors and nations, before harvest-now-decrypt-later campaigns mature. Waiting for perfect standards is how networks age into fragility.

Mapping vulnerabilities jointly requires its own security. A shared vulnerability catalog that leaks becomes a shared targeting folder. Handle it like intelligence—because it is.

Contractors and integrators at the edge need clearance cultures and logging equal to the networks they touch. Soft administrative edges undo hard perimeter stories.

False precision from machine summaries shared widely creates confident error. Norms for expressing uncertainty across allies are vulnerability management for the mind.

A network that cannot fight hurt will not fight long. Denying vulnerabilities reserves them for opening night. Naming them in peacetime is how conversion stays honest.
