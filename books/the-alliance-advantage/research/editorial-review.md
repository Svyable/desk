# Editorial Review

## Release decision — September 5, 2026

**Remain blocked pending bounded evidence remediation.** The manuscript is structurally complete at 18 of 18 numbered chapters plus prologue, with front matter, back matter, book-level rights metadata, a research brief, and a normalized source ledger. The current research packet is not yet sufficient to clear the book for Shelf publication.

The blocker is specific: the research brief says the thesis must be tested against implementation rather than strategy documents and should use exercises, deployment timelines, production, repair, acquisition, and actual cross-national use. The current source ledger contains eight official sources, concentrated on NATO digital strategy/implementation/industry documents and AUKUS policy or signature-project material. That is useful foundation evidence, but it does not yet provide the implementation evidence the book's own research standard requires across the logistics, munitions, industrial-base, Indo-Pacific, sovereignty-cost, vulnerability, and coalition-speed claims.

## Evidence checked this pass

The current NATO Alliance Digital Strategy was rechecked against NATO's official January 13, 2026 text. It directly supports the manuscript's bounded claims that NATO is pursuing interoperable digital capabilities, federated platforms, data-centric operations, shared standards, hybrid/federated cloud, AI integration, digital engineering, and measurable implementation. It also reinforces the manuscript's own caution: the strategy is an implementation direction and governance framework, not proof that every national system is already integrated operationally.

The book's existing AUKUS sources are appropriately first-party and useful for the shared-standards, control-system, data/AI, experimentation, and defense-trade portions of the argument. They should remain, but publication clearance requires adding evidence of demonstrated implementation or measured delivery rather than relying mainly on announced architecture and policy.

## Concrete remediations required

1. Add at least one authoritative implementation source for the logistics / munitions / industrial chapters that provides measured production, delivery, exercise, transfer, repair, or sustainment evidence rather than only conceptual argument.
2. Add at least one authoritative implementation source for the Indo-Pacific network chapter and one for AUKUS showing actual cross-national use, fielding, trials, delivery, or operational integration where available.
3. Add at least one strong countercase in which centralized integration or national control outperformed alliance coordination on speed, interoperability, or deployment, and preserve the conditions under which that countercase weakens the thesis.
4. Run a claim-level pass over Chapters 7–18 against those sources and record any dated or quantitative claim that requires revision. Do not treat broad strategy language as evidence that implementation has already occurred.
5. After substantive remediation, run the standard mechanical length check and Desk repository integrity check on the same frozen Desk commit considered for release.
