# Research Brief

## Thesis under test

The Alliance Advantage tests whether interoperable networks of sovereign allies can generate more usable strategic power than centralized rivals, especially as AI, software, data, and industrial production become integral to military capability.

> Alliances create advantage when political trust is converted into technical, industrial, informational, and legal interoperability before crisis. Nominal combined capability overstates power when the conversion system is missing.

## Core empirical tests

1. Does interoperability measurably improve readiness, replenishment, decision speed, and operational resilience?
2. Which seams create the largest coalition tax: data, classification, munitions, logistics, software, procurement, export controls, or national caveats?
3. Do common standards increase industrial capacity by widening markets and aggregating demand?
4. Can distributed allied networks remain functional when the U.S. hub is degraded or politically constrained?
5. Does AI amplify alliance advantage through shared data and models or magnify fragmentation?
6. When does centralization give authoritarian states a genuine speed advantage?
7. Which forms of sovereignty must allies pool, and which should remain deliberately national?

## Falsification and counter-evidence

The thesis weakens if highly centralized systems consistently field and adapt technology faster without severe information or resilience costs; if allied standards create lowest-common-denominator capability; if data-sharing creates unacceptable security leakage; or if industrial-national interests repeatedly defeat meaningful pooling.

## Evidence lanes

NATO standards and strategies; exercises; readiness reports; munitions production; cross-border logistics; Five Eyes intelligence; AUKUS technology cooperation; Indo-Pacific alliances; cloud and data systems; procurement and export controls; coalition operations; historical failures of interoperability.

## Source discipline

Distinguish alliance intent from fielded capability. A strategy document is evidence of priorities, not implementation. Prefer exercise results, procurement outcomes, production data, operational after-action reporting, and technical standards where available.
