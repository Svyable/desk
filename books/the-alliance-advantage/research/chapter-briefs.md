# Chapter Briefs

## Prologue — The Plug That Did Not Fit
Make interoperability visible through an exercise where political cooperation meets a technical seam.

## 1 — An Alliance Is a System
Define the alliance as a conversion system that turns national capabilities into combined effects.

## 2 — The Treaty Is Not the Capability
Separate legal commitment, political credibility, force availability, and operational integration.

## 3 — NATO’s Second Founding
Trace post-2022 enlargement, force planning, and the shift from expeditionary habits back toward collective defense.

## 4 — The Data Problem
Examine data quality, metadata, provenance, access, and coalition AI.

## 5 — The Permission Problem
Follow classification, caveats, export rules, and legal authorities that determine who may act.

## 6 — The Cloud Problem
Study common digital services, edge computing, cyber resilience, and dependence on commercial providers.

## 7 — The Munition Problem
Show how common calibers, certification, stockpiles, and cross-loading convert industrial output into endurance.

## 8 — The Logistics Problem
Follow ports, rail, roads, fuel, maintenance, customs, and host-nation support.

## 9 — The Standard
Explain standards as strategic agreements that trade local freedom for network scale.

## 10 — Five Eyes and the Intelligence Layer
Use a mature intelligence network to examine trust, compartmentation, and asymmetric access.

## 11 — AUKUS and the Technology Bet
Study whether advanced-capability sharing can move faster than traditional export and procurement systems.

## 12 — Japan, Korea, and the Hub-and-Spokes Inheritance
Trace the shift from bilateral alliances toward additional lateral links.

## 13 — The Philippines and Geography
Show how access, bases, local infrastructure, and political permission turn geography into capability.

## 14 — Europe in the Indo-Pacific
Test how far allied obligations, economic interests, and deployable capability travel beyond home regions.

## 15 — Industrial Mobilization
Examine demand aggregation, long-term contracts, surge capacity, labor, and reciprocal production.

## 16 — The Coalition Software Stack
Follow identity, APIs, updates, zero trust, models, and software-defined capability.

## 17 — When the Network Breaks
Use failures and degraded communications to test resilience rather than ideal interoperability.

## 18 — The Authoritarian Advantage, Tested
Take centralized speed seriously and identify where command outperforms agreement.

## 19 — The Alliance Advantage
Specify when diversity plus interoperability creates a durable structural edge.

## 20 — The Network That Can Fight
End with a practical definition of alliance readiness visible before war.
