# The Cat That Would Not Take Orders

Acoustikitty is usually told backward. The ending comes first: CIA wired a cat, the cat failed, government foolishness was revealed. The more interesting sequence begins with the engineering requirement.

A conversation on a park bench is acoustically easy and operationally difficult. Put a microphone close enough and the sound problem improves. Put a human close enough and the counterintelligence problem gets worse. A stray cat solves the visibility problem by being visible in a way nobody cares about.

CIA now says that beginning in 1964 it explored implanting a microphone in a cat's ear, a small transmitter beneath loose skin, and an antenna in the fur. According to the Agency's 2024 retrospective, the electronics worked. The animal did not become a controllable platform. In limited field tests it went where it wanted rather than where handlers needed it to go. CIA says the program ended in 1967 and was never used operationally.

That is a better failure than the famous taxi story, because it is about autonomy. CIA also says the tale that the cat was flattened by a vehicle during a test originated in a former official's joke. The joke survived because it was narratively perfect. A ridiculous machine meets an equally ridiculous death. The actual problem is less cinematic and much more durable: sensing capability without taskability is not an intelligence system.

There is an ethical question here that cannot be filed beneath engineering. CIA's current account says implantation and removal were performed under humane veterinary conditions and the animal was unharmed. That is an official retrospective assertion and should be checked against contemporaneous veterinary and program records before it is treated as the last word. “Humane” is not a property a secret organization can confer on itself by adjective.

But the control problem survives regardless of that audit. Engineers could miniaturize the radio enough to put it on an animal before they could miniaturize an autonomous vehicle capable of behaving naturally around a target. So they borrowed the animal's body and discovered that the behavior came attached.

The cat was not defective. It was insufficiently machine-like. That distinction is the whole project in miniature.
