# Umm Qasr

In March 2003, supply and hospital ships needed access to Umm Qasr. Mines made the approach a problem of time as much as detection.

ONR's contemporary account says REMUS vehicles surveyed the channel with side-scan sonar, dolphins inspected potential targets, and Navy and Marine Corps personnel followed with demolition work. Several mines were tagged and destroyed. The channel then supported the movement of badly needed supplies.

This chapter needs a fuller operational reconstruction before publication: exact dates, mine types, search area, false contacts, dolphin sorties, UUV sorties, diver actions, and what other coalition mine-countermeasure assets contributed. The official account is evidence of deployment, not permission to assign every success to one component.

Even in skeletal form, Umm Qasr breaks one persistent misconception. The animal program was not merely an eccentric Cold War research inheritance. Dolphins were integrated with contemporary autonomous hardware in an actual war.

That integration also supplies the fairest benchmark. If a modern UUV is said to replace the dolphin, compare it not with a dolphin in isolation but with the task the dolphin occupied inside the 2003 system: reacquisition and discrimination after broad-area machine search. Replacement claims become much more precise when the old job is specified honestly.
