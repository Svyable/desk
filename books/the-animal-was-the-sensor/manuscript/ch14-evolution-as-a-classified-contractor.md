# Evolution as a Classified Contractor

The military did not invent dolphin sonar, pigeon navigation or canine olfaction. It found them.

That difference changes the economics of research. A biological capability can arrive before a human theory adequate to reproduce it. Engineers can exploit the black box while scientists are still arguing about mechanism. In that narrow sense evolution behaves like a contractor that delivers a mature prototype with no drawings.

The metaphor has limits. Evolution has no customer and no design intent. But the procurement asymmetry is real. Building an underwater autonomous system requires materials, transducers, software, batteries, navigation and manufacturing. Recruiting an animal requires a different infrastructure: training, care, transport and an acceptance that the platform was optimized for a life rather than a mission.

The historical temptation was to borrow the finished system. The scientific dividend was that military use then generated questions about hearing, diving physiology, cognition and health. The Navy says work around its marine mammals has produced more than a thousand scientific publications.

The loop is peculiar: biology supplies a capability to engineering; engineering turns the capability into a task; the task creates experiments that send knowledge back to biology.
