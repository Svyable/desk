# Weapon Stories

“Killer dolphin” is a nearly perfect rumor. It combines secrecy, animal intelligence, military technology and cruelty in two words.

The Navy publicly denies that its marine mammals are trained as offensive weapons. That denial is evidence of the Navy's position, not metaphysical proof that no offensive experiment ever occurred anywhere. The responsible response is neither credulity nor the theatrical skepticism that treats every classified gap as proof of innocence.

Instead, ask what an offensive claim would leave behind. Training doctrine. Hardware. Veterinary risk. Handler procedures. Rules of engagement. Accidents. Procurement. Deployment records. Multiple independent witnesses whose accounts converge on technical particulars rather than movie imagery. A lethal animal system is not made secret by calling the animal classified.

This is the same discipline applied to extraordinary aerospace claims: specify the expected industrial and operational trace. Secrecy can hide documents. It cannot abolish causality.

The chapter's purpose is not to prove a negative. It is to prevent the book from using allegation as atmosphere. The stranger the claim, the more boring the evidentiary work should become.
