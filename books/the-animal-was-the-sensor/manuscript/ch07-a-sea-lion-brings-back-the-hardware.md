# A Sea Lion Brings Back the Hardware

The sea lion gets less mythology because its job is harder to romanticize. It retrieves things.

That plainness is useful. Navy sea lions are trained to locate objects underwater and attach recovery lines so humans can bring the hardware back. Low-light vision, directional hearing, diving ability and trainability become a recovery system. The mission exposes a category mistake in the phrase “animal sensor.” Detection is only one step. Somebody has to reacquire the target, approach it, manipulate something near it and communicate success.

Machines are often introduced into this chain as replacements and then discovered to be replacements for one link. A side-scan sonar may survey efficiently. A remotely operated vehicle may inspect. A diver may attach a line. A sea lion may collapse several steps under particular conditions.

The comparison therefore needs task accounting rather than species romance. How deep? How long? What current? What visibility? What object geometry? What support crew? What recovery rate? The answer may favor the animal in one envelope and the machine in another.

The sea lion's historical importance is that it makes the book's thesis embarrassingly concrete. Intelligence is not always an inference about geopolitics. Sometimes it is knowing where the expensive object fell and getting a rope onto it.
