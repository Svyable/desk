# The Autonomy Problem

The cat wandering away is funny until the machine does it.

Autonomy is sold as freedom from operators. Operationally it is also freedom from continuous correction. That means the system must decide what matters when reality departs from the plan.

Animals have been doing this for hundreds of millions of years. They are consequently excellent demonstrations of both the attraction and danger of autonomous platforms. A dolphin can adapt its search. A pigeon can navigate home. A cat can ignore the mission.

The difference between those outcomes is not intelligence in the abstract. It is alignment between the organism's learned behavior, motivation, environment and the handler's objective. That is why the animal programs deserve a place in the prehistory of autonomy. They forced institutions to manage capable agents whose internal state could not be fully inspected or commanded.

The analogy to artificial intelligence should stop before it becomes metaphysics. No claim about machine consciousness follows. The useful inheritance is engineering humility. Capability does not imply obedience. Performance in training does not imply performance in every field condition. A system can perceive something the operator cannot and still make the wrong operational choice.

Acoustikitty was not the first alignment failure. It was simply furry enough that nobody mistook the problem for a theorem.
