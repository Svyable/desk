# Biomimicry Is Not Biology

CIA eventually built fish that were not fish and an insect that was not an insect.

Its robotic catfish, Charlie and Charlene, explored unmanned underwater capabilities including maneuvering, depth control, communications and covert water sampling. Insectothopter imitated a dragonfly and was intended as a tiny listening platform. CIA says crosswinds above roughly five miles per hour made the latter operationally impractical.

These devices belong beside the living-animal programs because they reveal the direction of desire. Intelligence agencies wanted the access profile of animals without the autonomy problem of animals. A robotic fish could be commanded. A mechanical insect would not become hungry or decide the target was boring.

But copying appearance is cheaper than copying competence. The dragonfly's body is not merely a disguise; a real dragonfly contains flight control that handles the air in which the imitation failed. Biomimicry can therefore expose how much of an animal's useful technology is invisible.

The long arc from pigeon to drone is not a story of replacing feathers with carbon fiber. It is the slow reproduction of navigation, stabilization, sensing, control and autonomy that the feathers had been carrying all along.
