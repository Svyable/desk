# The Animal and the Machine

The clean replacement story says technology advances until the animal becomes unnecessary. Umm Qasr produced a messier diagram.

In 2003 the Office of Naval Research described a wartime team in Iraq composed of REMUS autonomous underwater vehicles, dolphins, divers, Navy personnel and Marines. REMUS performed broad sonar surveys. Dolphins reacquired potential targets. Humans handled demolition. The machine did the tedious coverage. The animal did discrimination. People made consequential decisions.

This is not a transitional embarrassment on the road to full autonomy. It is systems engineering. Different agents occupy different parts of the error surface.

The point matters because public arguments about automation often compare idealized wholes: robot versus human, sonar versus dolphin, AI versus analyst. Operations compare failure modes. A battery-limited vehicle can search without fatigue until its battery is gone. An animal tires but can adapt. A human diver is flexible but physiologically constrained and placed at risk. The useful system may be the one that assigns each weakness somewhere it hurts least.

Biohybrid intelligence is an unfashionable phrase because it sounds futuristic. The older reality was more literal: boat, laptop, robot, dolphin, diver. The future had already arrived and it smelled like fish.
