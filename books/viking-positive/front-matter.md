# Viking Positive
## The Experiment That Found Life on Mars and Wasn't Allowed to Mean It

**Sven Hardy Benson**

## A note about the word *positive*

Positive is not the same word as proven.

The Viking Labeled Release experiment produced a positive experimental response. That is a historical and instrumental statement. Whether the active agent was biological remains disputed. This book will not improve the case for life by quietly changing one sentence into the other.

The evidence is carried in four classes throughout:

1. **Verified / documented.** Viking flight data, instrument design and controls; NASA mission records; peer-reviewed chemistry; Phoenix perchlorate measurements; later Martian-organics detections.
2. **Credible but incomplete.** Retrospective interpretations whose premises are serious but whose full causal chain is not experimentally closed; claims about how particular institutional judgments were made where surviving deliberative records are incomplete.
3. **Disputed / unconfirmed.** The claim that the LR response was caused by extant Martian organisms; any specific abiotic mechanism not demonstrated under the actual Viking-site conditions; claims that NASA suppressed a known discovery of life.
4. **Speculative synthesis.** The book's central proposal that Viking is a case of *evidentiary dependency*: one experiment's interpretation was materially constrained by another experiment's apparent negative, and later discoveries can therefore weaken the old inference without retroactively proving the first experiment right.

A patent proves filing, not function. A scientist's conviction proves conviction, not Mars. Consensus is evidence about expert judgment, not a substitute for the experiment. Minority status is not evidence of courage or correctness.

The book has one hard rule: every time the biological interpretation gains a point, the strongest chemical alternative gets to answer.
