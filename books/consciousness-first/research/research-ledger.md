# Research ledger — Consciousness First

## Governing question

Can a single defensible ontology support all three claims—**consciousness is fundamental; spacetime is a perceptual interface; physical reality is emergent**—without borrowing certainty from physics, perception science, or philosophy that those fields have not actually earned?

## Distinction from Spacetime Is a Lie

*Spacetime Is a Lie* begins with established and active physics, asks how spacetime might emerge, and only later asks whether consciousness belongs in the deeper ontology. *Consciousness First* reverses the burden. It provisionally adopts a consciousness-first ontology and asks whether that ontology can reproduce the physical world, explain the tight brain–experience relation, justify interface language, and make empirical contact.

The first book asks: **if spacetime is not fundamental, what might be?**

This book asks: **suppose consciousness is fundamental—can that claim survive contact with everything else we know?**

## Evidence classes

### Class 1 — established empirical results

1. Human conscious state and content covary strongly with brain state; anesthesia, lesions, stimulation, psychoactive drugs, sleep, seizures and brain injury provide many causal interventions.
2. Relativity removes any universal frame-independent notion of simultaneity.
3. Quantum theory and quantum field theory are experimentally successful without requiring a consensus consciousness-based interpretation.
4. Perception is constructive and mediated by sensory/neural processing; experienced qualities are not simple copies of external variables.

### Class 2 — serious active theory

1. Connes and Rovelli's thermal time hypothesis proposes that physical time-flow in generally covariant quantum theory can be determined by the state through modular automorphism flow.
2. Jacobson's thermodynamic derivation treats Einstein's equation as an equation of state under specific horizon/entropy assumptions.
3. Ryu–Takayanagi and related holographic work connect entanglement entropy and geometric area in AdS/CFT.
4. Van Raamsdonk and subsequent work investigate relations between entanglement and emergent spacetime connectivity.
5. These results motivate emergent-spacetime research; none establishes consciousness as the source of spacetime.

### Class 3 — empirical/philosophical consciousness programs

1. Global workspace, recurrent-processing, higher-order, predictive-processing and related neuroscientific theories explain aspects of conscious access/content without treating consciousness as fundamental.
2. Integrated Information Theory treats consciousness in terms of intrinsic causal structure; its ontological implications remain contested.
3. Panpsychism, panprotopsychism, Russellian monism, neutral monism and idealism treat mentality or a mind-adjacent substrate as fundamental in different senses. These are philosophical positions, not established empirical findings.
4. The hard problem is influential but contested; rejecting reductive physicalism is an argument, not a scientific result.

### Class 4 — interface hypotheses

1. Hoffman, Singh and Prakash's Interface Theory of Perception argues from evolutionary modeling that fitness can favor useful perceptual encodings over veridical representation.
2. Published commentaries dispute parts of the theory, including its treatment of veridicality and the mapping from evolutionary games to real perceptual systems.
3. Even if ITP succeeds, it does not by itself establish that spacetime specifically is the interface, or that consciousness is the fundamental substrate behind it.

### Class 5 — speculative synthesis

1. Spacetime is an agent-facing data structure rather than fundamental ontology.
2. Physical objects are stable interface regularities produced by relations among conscious agents or a deeper experiential substrate.
3. Temporal flow is state-relative in a way that connects physical modular flow to experienced time.
4. Consciousness-first ontology can reproduce the intersubjective mathematical stability of physics.
5. A deeper consciousness-first theory yields empirically distinct predictions.

## Contradiction ledger

### Emergent spacetime vs consciousness-first

Physics can make spacetime emergent while leaving consciousness wholly dependent on biological organization inside the emergent world. This is the central rival, not a footnote.

### Constructed perception vs non-veridical ontology

The brain constructs perceptual experience. That fact alone does not show that perception fails to track real structure or that spacetime is merely a user interface. Useful representation can preserve genuine relational structure without resembling its substrate.

### Brain dependence vs fundamental consciousness

If consciousness is fundamental, why do small physical interventions produce large, lawful changes in experience? Any viable theory must explain anesthesia, lesions, stimulation and development at least as well as neural emergence theories.

### Thermal time vs experienced time

A state-defined modular flow is a mathematical/physical proposal. The felt passage of time and the thermodynamic arrow are additional problems. Do not identify them without mechanism.

### Formal definition vs ontological discovery

A theory can define a mathematical quantity as “consciousness” and prove theorems about it. That establishes consequences of the formalism, not the empirical identification of the quantity with subjective experience.

## Strongest physicalist account

Spacetime may be emergent at a quantum-gravity level while consciousness remains a biological process realized by certain information-processing architectures in brains. Perception is an adaptive model of the environment, not a transparent copy, but it tracks enough real structure to support successful action and cumulative science. The hard problem either yields to a future functional/neural explanation or reflects a misleading demand for a kind of explanation science does not owe.

This account wins if it continues absorbing consciousness phenomena with increasing predictive precision while consciousness-first theories fail to generate unique, testable consequences.

## Strongest consciousness-first account

Subjective experience is not generated from non-experiential ingredients; it is a primitive feature of reality or of the deeper substrate. Organisms do not create consciousness ex nihilo but organize, constrain, integrate or localize it. Perception presents an adaptive interface rather than the underlying ontology. The mathematical stability of physics describes reliable structure within that interface or among the deeper relations generating it. Spacetime is emergent because it is a representational geometry of those relations rather than the base layer.

This account wins only if it can explain brain dependence, intersubjective agreement, physical causal closure and the quantitative success of physics while also producing explanatory or predictive gains unavailable to its rivals.

## Defeat conditions

The consciousness-first program loses scientific necessity if a mature physicalist theory predicts the presence, absence, content and structure of experience from physical/functional organization with no residual explanatory or empirical anomaly, while consciousness-first theories add no independent prediction.

The “spacetime as interface” claim weakens if interface models fail to recover the stable relational and causal structure measured by physics, or if their evolutionary arguments against veridical perception do not survive criticism.

The thermal-time bridge must be dropped if no principled connection can be made between modular flow and experienced temporal structure beyond metaphor.

## Publication holds

- Do not state that quantum gravity has shown spacetime to be emergent in our universe.
- Do not state that thermal time is established physical time.
- Do not describe modular flow as inherently irreversible without a separate argument.
- Do not say physics requires consciousness to solve the measurement problem.
- Do not say Interface Theory of Perception proves spacetime is a desktop.
- Do not call the hard problem “solved” by definition.
- Do not use “information” as a substance without specifying whether the term is Shannon, quantum, semantic, causal, or ontological.
- Do not use first-person certainty (“experience is all we know directly”) as proof that only experience exists.
- Give neuroscience causal evidence full weight.
- Require every consciousness-first proposal to say what observation could count against it.
