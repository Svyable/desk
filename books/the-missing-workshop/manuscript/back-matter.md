# Back Matter

## Chronology

- **4th century BCE:** Greek epigraphic evidence discussed by modern researchers indicates bronze turning and sophisticated metalworking practices before the Antikythera mechanism.
- **3rd century BCE:** Archimedes works in the Hellenistic world; later literary traditions associate him with advanced mechanics and defensive devices. Specific machines vary in evidentiary strength.
- **2nd century BCE:** The Antikythera mechanism is constructed, commonly dated toward the late second century BCE on current scholarship.
- **Early 1st century BCE:** The Antikythera wreck sinks while carrying a diverse cargo across the Mediterranean.
- **1st century BCE–1st century CE:** Vitruvius and later Hero of Alexandria preserve descriptions of hydraulic, mechanical and automated devices.
- **2nd century CE:** The Barbegal complex in Roman Gaul operates sixteen waterwheels in two cascades.
- **1900–1901:** Sponge divers recover artifacts from the Antikythera wreck.
- **1902 onward:** The corroded mechanism fragments are recognized as containing geared machinery.
- **1974:** Derek de Solla Price publishes *Gears from the Greeks*, a foundational modern study.
- **2005–2006:** High-resolution X-ray CT and surface imaging transform analysis of the fragments; the major results appear in *Nature* in 2006.
- **2008:** Further *Nature* work identifies calendrical, eclipse and Games-display functions.
- **2018–2024:** Barbegal carbonate studies reconstruct aspects of vanished wooden mill machinery and its operating history.
- **2021:** UCL researchers publish a new reconstruction model for the Antikythera front display while identifying manufacturability questions that still require testing.
- **2024:** Statistical work revisits the likely hole count of the Antikythera calendar ring; the Greek-Swiss excavation recovers a significant portion of the ancient hull.
- **2025:** The ongoing Antikythera program raises another portion of the ship and continues 3D documentation.

## Evidence posture

The central conclusion is intentionally narrower than the strongest rhetoric available.

**Established:** Antikythera is a genuinely ancient, sophisticated geared astronomical/calendrical mechanism. Barbegal demonstrates large ancient mechanical systems whose organic machinery can vanish while indirect proxies survive. Ancient copper alloys were recycled. Technical literature preserves mechanical concepts not matched one-for-one by surviving hardware.

**Credible inference:** Antikythera required a substantial local craft ecology and probably inherited techniques rather than emerging from a single isolated act of invention. Other sophisticated geared instruments may have existed without surviving.

**Disputed:** exact front reconstruction, exact workshop location/lineage, prevalence of sibling mechanisms, specific legendary Archimedean machines, electrical function of the Baghdad vessels.

**Speculative synthesis:** the archaeological record systematically overstates the uniqueness of some high-end technological objects because it deletes their production ecology faster than their most durable outputs. This is a model to test, not a recovered historical fact.

## What would falsify or weaken the book

The missing-workshop thesis weakens if systematic searches show that the required adjacent capabilities were absent; if proposed manufacturing processes prove incompatible with observed tool marks/materials; if future dating/context moves the mechanism outside its assumed technical setting; or if increasing survey sensitivity repeatedly fails to find any plausible intermediate mechanical tradition, pushing the evidence toward a rarer one-off object.

The thesis does **not** survive by saying all missing evidence was conveniently recycled or rotted. At sufficiently large claimed scale, absent durable externalities become evidence against the claim.
