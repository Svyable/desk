# Clementine

The capture vehicle needed a name because eventually every large secret machine acquires language that makes it manageable.

Accounts of AZORIAN commonly call the giant claw-like device Clementine. The nickname risks making it cute. It was not cute.

The capture vehicle had to descend beneath the Glomar Explorer, straddle a wrecked submarine section, close around it, support it, and survive the beginning of a lift through miles of water. CIA imagery shows an industrial skeleton with articulated gripping arms rather than anything resembling a delicate scientific instrument.

Its job was made harder by the condition of the target. K-129 was not a factory-fresh cylinder resting on a prepared cradle. It was a submarine that had suffered a catastrophic sinking, struck the seabed, remained under immense pressure, and corroded for years. A recovery mechanism could be strong enough to lift a designed mass and still fail because the mass no longer behaved as a coherent object.

That distinction becomes crucial later.

Engineering strength is relational. A claw can be stronger without making the thing in its grip stronger. A lift system can satisfy its own structural requirements while the recovered hull fails around it.

Clementine also had to disappear when it was not supposed to be seen. The specially built Hughes Mining Barge allowed the capture vehicle to be positioned beneath the ship under cover. Water and roof structure hid the most revealing component from overhead reconnaissance and harbor observation.

The barge is one of the project’s purest expressions of security engineering. Instead of telling observers not to look, the system denied them the useful angle.

A satellite photograph of a giant claw would have demolished the mining story. A satellite photograph of a large ship and support barge could remain ambiguous.

The capture vehicle therefore existed in two technical systems: lifting and concealment. Its shape was determined by one; its handling procedure by both.

Modern discussions of secret hardware often imagine that classification sits outside engineering as a rule imposed after design. AZORIAN makes the opposite visible. Security requirements can alter the hardware, facilities, transfer methods, schedule, and cost. A thing designed to remain secret is a different thing.

Clementine had to grab K-129.

Before that, it had to arrive beneath the ship without explaining itself.
