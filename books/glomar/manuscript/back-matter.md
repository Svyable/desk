# Back Matter

## Evidence note

This book treats declassified CIA material and the Department of State's *Foreign Relations of the United States* documentary series as the primary spine for the operation's chronology, authorization, cover, recovery attempt, exposure, and cancellation. Those records are authoritative evidence of what the U.S. government has chosen to declassify, not proof that the declassified record is complete.

Later participant recollections, investigative reporting, books, and documentaries can add detail but are not silently promoted to the same evidentiary class. Claims about the precise contents recovered from K-129, the cause of the submarine's loss, and still-classified intelligence gains remain bounded in the text.

## Claim discipline

A government statement that AZORIAN was an intelligence success is evidence of the government's assessment, not a complete public accounting of value obtained. Press reporting that a particular object or document was recovered is reporting until independently documented. The phrase “neither confirm nor deny” is treated as a legal-administrative technique whose scope depends on statutory exemptions and judicial doctrine, not as an unlimited executive power.
