# Evidence Matrix

## Class 1 — verified / declassified / documented

- K-129 disappeared in March 1968 and was located by the United States later that year.
- CIA was charged with leading an effort to recover strategically valuable material from the wreck.
- Hughes/Glomar deep-sea mining was used as the public cover.
- A purpose-built ship, hidden capture vehicle, pipe-handling system, and moon pool supported the recovery attempt.
- The 1974 lift recovered only part of the intended target after the wreck broke during ascent.
- Six Soviet submariners' remains were recovered and buried at sea.
- A second recovery mission was planned.
- Press exposure, increased Soviet attention, and executive decisions ended the follow-on effort.
- CIA adopted a neither-confirm-nor-deny posture that became known as the Glomar response.
- Glomar responses remain part of U.S. FOIA practice in 2026.

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete reporting / testimony

- Participant recollections of specific items recovered from the submarine beyond what CIA publicly acknowledges.
- Detailed attributions about which engineering failure initiated the loss of the unrecovered section unless supported by declassified technical records.
- Exact internal intelligence value assigned to each recovered object where the assessments remain classified or redacted.

## Class 3 — disputed allegations without independent confirmation

- Precise claims that particular cryptographic materials, warheads, missiles, or code systems were recovered in forms not officially acknowledged.
- Claims that the publicly described partial failure was chiefly a deception masking near-total success.
- Definitive claims about the cause of K-129's sinking where public evidence does not resolve competing theories.

## Class 4 — speculative synthesis / open conjecture

- AZORIAN as a control case for evaluating modern secret-retrieval allegations: not proof or disproof of any modern claim, but a historical example of the administrative and industrial residue generated by a genuine strategic recovery program.
- The idea that AZORIAN's most consequential product was institutional rather than material: a durable doctrine for withholding the existence-status of records.
- “Interpretive security” as a useful description of cover systems that permit an object to be visible while steering outsiders toward the wrong explanation.
