# Chronology

- March 1968 — Soviet ballistic-missile submarine K-129 disappears in the Pacific.
- 1968 — Soviet search fails; U.S. search subsequently locates the wreck. FRUS dates U.S. location to August.
- August 1970 — Richard Helms makes recovery a top U.S. intelligence priority according to FRUS.
- 1971 — construction work on the recovery vessel proceeds under the deep-sea mining cover.
- September 14, 1972 — Helms recommends continuation to Kissinger, explicitly discussing technical risk, cost, cover commitments, and contractor relationships.
- May–June 1974 — final review and presidential authorization for recovery; FRUS states Nixon authorized the plan June 7.
- June 1974 — burglary of a Hughes office steals documents that later contribute to exposure risk.
- July 4, 1974 — CIA public history places Glomar Explorer over the recovery site.
- Summer 1974 — capture and lift; target breaks; a portion is recovered, including six Soviet sailors' remains.
- Late 1974–early 1975 — planning for another recovery continues while rumors spread.
- February 7, 1975 — Los Angeles Times publishes connection among Hughes, CIA, and Glomar.
- March 18, 1975 — Jack Anderson broadcasts the story nationally according to CIA chronology.
- June 1975 — follow-on recovery is terminated; FRUS states Ford approved termination June 16.
- 1981 — D.C. Circuit decision in Phillippi v. CIA helps formalize the “Glomar” response in FOIA law.
- 1992 — CIA Director Robert Gates gives Boris Yeltsin film of the burial at sea of recovered Soviet sailors.
- 2010 — CIA declassifies a substantive internal history of AZORIAN.
- 2026 — DOJ continues to track and describe Glomar responses as active FOIA practice.
