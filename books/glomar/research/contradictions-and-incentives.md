# Contradictions and Incentive Audit

CIA has an institutional incentive to remember AZORIAN as ingenuity, audacity, and intelligence success. Critics have incentives to emphasize cost, partial recovery, and bureaucratic overreach. Journalists have incentives to reward disclosure and conflict. Participants may remember technically vivid details better than compartment boundaries or exact documentary status decades later.

Do not resolve those incentives by averaging them. Resolve claims proposition by proposition.

Key contradictions to preserve:

- Engineering success vs. incomplete target recovery.
- Extraordinary physical accomplishment vs. uncertain publicly documented intelligence yield.
- Commercial cover based on a real emerging field vs. a venture whose scale and timing depended on government participation.
- Widespread press reporting vs. continued official refusal to authenticate facts.
- Transparency law vs. a doctrine allowing agencies to withhold even the existence-status of responsive records.
- CIA's present public celebration of AZORIAN vs. remaining redactions and unacknowledged operational details.

Disinformation incentive: once the project became partly public, both truthful silence and misleading ambiguity could serve security. Therefore later public absence of confirmation cannot be treated mechanically as evidence of falsity or truth.
