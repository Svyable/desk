# Defeat Conditions

The book's central thesis should weaken if primary records show that the famous “neither confirm nor deny” posture was not materially connected to the Glomar/AZORIAN disclosure fight, or that Phillippi's connection to the operation is merely retrospective naming rather than procedural lineage.

The engineering-secrecy synthesis should weaken if technical records show that cover requirements had little or no effect on ship architecture, barge handling, capture-vehicle concealment, contracting, or operational procedure.

The contractor-commitment argument should be narrowed if Helms's 1972 memorandum proves anomalous rather than representative of decision-making elsewhere in the project.

Claims about modern relevance should be cut if current DOJ or case law no longer treats Glomar as an active FOIA response category. Current 2026 DOJ material presently defeats that concern.

The manuscript should not imply that AZORIAN proves modern crash-retrieval or reverse-engineering allegations. If the analogy begins doing evidentiary work instead of methodological work, remove it.
