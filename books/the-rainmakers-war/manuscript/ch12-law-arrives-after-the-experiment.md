# Chapter 12 — Law Arrives After the Experiment

Law is often late to technology because technology does not ask permission before becoming imaginable.

Weather modification was particularly awkward. The underlying techniques had peaceful uses. Rain enhancement, fog dispersal, hail suppression, and storm research could be framed as public benefit. The same physical intervention could become hostile when aimed at another state's logistics, agriculture, hydrology, or infrastructure.

The dual-use problem was not hypothetical after Popeye.

In the early 1970s, the United States publicly renounced hostile climate modification and Congress pushed for international constraints. U.S.-Soviet discussions followed. The result was the Convention on the Prohibition of Military or Any Other Hostile Use of Environmental Modification Techniques—ENMOD—adopted by the U.N. General Assembly in 1976 and opened for signature in 1977.

It is tempting to narrate this as technology causing treaty.

The causal chain is messier. Popeye was one catalyst among broader concerns about environmental warfare, geophysical manipulation, and technological escalation. But the program gave the debate a concrete object. States no longer had to imagine whether someone might try to weaponize environmental processes. Someone had.

The interesting feature of ENMOD is that it regulates purpose and effect thresholds, not all environmental modification.

Peaceful use remained permissible. Research remained possible. Weather modification did not become illegal as a category.

This mirrors the physics. The same seeding flare does not know whether it was launched for a ski basin or a supply route.

Intent lives outside the cloud.

That means governance must follow institutions, targets, scale, and expected effects rather than chemistry alone.

Popeye thus produced an early version of a problem now returning through solar geoengineering. A technique can be technically continuous across benign research, commercial operation, emergency intervention, unilateral deployment, and hostile use while being politically discontinuous across those categories.

The aerosol does not carry a flag.

The authority structure does.
