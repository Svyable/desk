# Chronology

- **1946:** Vincent Schaefer demonstrates freezing of supercooled cloud droplets with dry ice, catalyzing modern cloud-seeding research.
- **1947:** Project Cirrus seeds a hurricane; subsequent track change becomes an early attribution controversy.
- **1961–1971:** Hurricane seeding experiments associated with the research lineage later known as Project STORMFURY.
- **October 1966:** Popeye test phase conducted in the Lao panhandle; later FRUS record says more than 50 cloud-seeding experiments were performed.
- **January 13, 1967:** State Department memorandum considers Defense request to begin operational weather modification in North Vietnam and Laos.
- **1967–1972:** Operational military cloud seeding continues in Southeast Asia under classified arrangements.
- **1971–1972:** Press and congressional scrutiny intensify around weather warfare.
- **1972:** U.S. policy renounces hostile climate modification; Congress considers restrictions and treaty action.
- **1973–1974:** Senate action and hearings push weather/environmental modification into arms-control debate; Defense publicly acknowledges the Southeast Asia program.
- **1974–1975:** U.S.-Soviet bilateral discussions develop environmental-modification arms-control concepts.
- **December 10, 1976:** U.N. General Assembly adopts ENMOD.
- **May 18, 1977:** ENMOD opens for signature.
- **October 5, 1978:** ENMOD enters into force.
- **1983:** STORMFURY officially ends after observations undermine key assumptions and make natural hurricane behavior a stronger explanation for earlier apparent effects.
- **2020:** SNOWIE researchers publish direct radar/gauge quantification of snowfall produced by cloud seeding under suitable orographic conditions.
- **December 19, 2024:** GAO publishes modern cloud-seeding technology assessment, emphasizing possible effects alongside unresolved effectiveness limitations.
- **February 23, 2026:** GAO reports significant weaknesses in NOAA's weather-modification reporting oversight and data quality.
- **March 24, 2026:** GAO publishes current solar-geoengineering technology spotlight emphasizing uncertainty and governance risk.
