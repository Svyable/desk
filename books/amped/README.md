## Working thesis

Data centers are becoming a new kind of grid actor: industrial-scale loads with software economics, global balance sheets, unusually short deployment clocks, and at least some ability to choose where and when computation occurs. Their demand is forcing utilities, grid operators, regulators, generators, equipment suppliers, and communities to redesign how large loads are forecast, connected, priced, financed, equipped, and sometimes curtailed.

The book follows that demand shock through forecasting, interconnection, rate design, generation, transmission, flexible compute, equipment bottlenecks, and local political economy while preserving the downside case: the AI boom can also strand assets, shift costs, increase fuel dependence, and build around forecasts that later prove wrong.
