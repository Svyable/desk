# Infostructure — evidence and source log

## Evidence classes

**Class 1 — documented / established:** peer-reviewed empirical results, archival records, established historical facts, and directly inspectable institutional or technical standards.

**Class 2 — credible but incomplete:** serious historical reconstruction, observational inference, or empirical work whose interpretation remains contested or whose generality is uncertain.

**Class 3 — disputed:** claims with meaningful scholarly disagreement, weak identification, or insufficient independent confirmation. These belong in the book only with the dispute visible.

**Class 4 — synthesis / conjecture:** the book's own proposed model of infostructure, including retrieval latency, translation distance, cultural adjacency and institutional storage as a unified explanatory frame.

## Initial source trail

1. Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, “Cultural Evolution.” Overview of cultural-evolution traditions, social learning, inheritance and conceptual disputes. https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/evolution-cultural/ — Class 1/2 background.
2. Boyd & Richerson, *Culture and the Evolutionary Process* (1985). Foundational dual-inheritance/cultural-evolution modeling. — Class 1 scholarly source; model claims remain model-dependent.
3. Richerson & Boyd, *Not by Genes Alone* (2005). Cultural information and social learning. — Class 1/2 synthesis.
4. Henrich, *The Secret of Our Success* (2015). Cumulative culture, distributed know-how and social learning. — Class 2 synthesis drawing on empirical literature.
5. Polanyi, *The Tacit Dimension* (1966). Tacit knowledge framework. — Class 2 conceptual source.
6. Kolodny, Creanza & Feldman (2015), “Evolution in Leaps.” Models punctuated accumulation/loss and differential distribution of cultural knowledge. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1520492112 — Class 1 publication; simulations are not direct historical proof.
7. Pawar et al. (2023), “Learning Few-Shot Imitation as Cultural Transmission.” Demonstrates a computational cultural-transmission setup in artificial agents. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-42875-2 — Class 1 experimental publication; not evidence that human culture reduces to the model.
8. Jablonka & Lamb, *Evolution in Four Dimensions* (2005). Symbolic inheritance and latent cultural information as part of a broader inheritance framework. — Class 2 synthesis.

## Contradictions and cautions

- “Information” is not a settled ontology of culture. Critics argue that informational language can conceal power, history, embodiment and local causal detail. The manuscript must not turn the metaphor into an unexplained substance.
- Population-size explanations for cultural complexity are debated and context-dependent. Connectivity, environment, transmission fidelity and institutional structure can confound simple demographic stories.
- Simultaneous invention can support prerequisite/adjacency accounts but does not prove determinism or erase individual agency.
- Tacit knowledge is often invoked too broadly. Distinguish truly hard-to-verbalize perceptual or embodied competence from knowledge that is merely undocumented.
- Citation and patent networks observe recorded traces, not the full cultural substrate. They are proxies, not direct measures of infostructure.
- AI-assisted retrieval may increase cross-domain search while also increasing homogenization, false synthesis and provenance loss. These are open empirical questions.

## Thesis status

The existence of cumulative culture, social learning, tacit knowledge, knowledge loss, standards, archives and distributed production capability is well supported. **Infostructure as a unified causal construct is Class 4: a proposed synthesis.** The book should earn the term by producing discriminating measures and case predictions, not by relabeling familiar sociology.
