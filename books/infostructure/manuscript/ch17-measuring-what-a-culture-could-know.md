# Measuring What a Culture Could Know

If infostructure is more than a metaphor, it should eventually generate measurements.

Not one index. The temptation to compress cultural capability into a single score should be resisted. The object is multidimensional.

We could measure retention: how often important knowledge survives personnel turnover or institutional death. Retrieval latency: how long it takes a competent searcher to find relevant prior work. Translation distance: how difficult it is to map equivalent mechanisms across vocabularies. Recombination rate: how often innovations draw on distant domains. Tacit dependence: how much performance degrades when experienced practitioners are removed. Provenance quality: how much of the record remains connected to methods and source evidence. Experimental throughput: how cheaply uncertain combinations can be tested.

Existing research already measures pieces of this landscape through citation networks, patent recombination, labor mobility, scientific collaboration, archaeological transmission models and economic complexity. None is identical to infostructure, which is useful precisely because it asks how the pieces interact.

A culture with excellent storage and terrible retrieval is different from one with rapid retrieval and poor verification. Both may produce the same number of documents.

The most interesting experiments would be comparative. Give matched technical teams the same hard problem but different access architectures. Measure which prior art they recover, how diverse their candidate solutions become, how quickly false analogies are discarded, and whether machine-assisted cross-domain search changes outcomes.

At institutional scale, study what happens after closures, mergers and retirements. Which capabilities survive? Which require relearning? What kinds of documentation predict recovery?

The theory should be allowed to fail. If measures of access topology add nothing beyond education, R&D spending, population, capital and ordinary network variables, then infostructure is only a new label.

A useful concept earns its name by making a prediction somebody could have missed.
