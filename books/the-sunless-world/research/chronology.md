# Chronology

- **Late 19th–20th century:** Mining, petroleum microbiology and groundwater studies repeatedly encounter microorganisms and biologically influenced chemistry below ordinary soils.
- **1968 onward:** Deep Sea Drilling Project and successor ocean drilling programs create systematic access to subseafloor sediments and crust; microbiological sampling becomes increasingly contamination-aware.
- **1990s:** Molecular techniques and direct cell counts expand evidence for a large marine subsurface biosphere; global biomass estimates become a major scientific question.
- **2000:** ODP contamination work formalizes fluorescent microsphere and perfluorocarbon-tracer approaches for microbiological coring.
- **2005:** Lin et al. publish quantitative evidence/modeling for radiolytic H2 as an energy source in South African deep fracture waters.
- **2006–2008:** South African mine work converges on deeply isolated chemolithoautotrophic communities; Chivian et al. report the near-single-species *Candidatus Desulforudis audaxviator* ecosystem in Mponeng fracture water.
- **2013:** Holland et al. report noble-gas evidence for Precambrian-age residence components in deep fracture fluids near Timmins/Kidd Creek, Ontario.
- **2013:** Low-temperature water-rock H2 generation work broadens nonphotosynthetic energy mechanisms relevant to subsurface habitability.
- **2010s:** Deep Carbon Observatory and related programs synthesize continental and marine deep-life evidence; global biomass estimates are revised as sampling and models improve.
- **2018:** Magnabosco et al. estimate ~2–6 × 10^29 cells in the continental subsurface and revise global prokaryotic biomass estimates.
- **2020:** Ocean-drilling work pushes active/present-cell observations toward the known upper temperature boundary in deeply buried sediment, sharpening the presence-versus-activity problem.
- **2021:** Oman Drilling Project work documents active microbial ecosystems in serpentinizing rock with large spatial variation in cell abundance and measurable sulfate reduction.
- **2021:** Tarnas et al. model radiolytic redox energy in Martian crust and identify subsurface energetic habitability where groundwater exists.
- **2022:** Mars shallow-subsurface irradiation experiments show rapid geologic-time degradation of amino acids relative to preservation hopes, strengthening the case for depth-sensitive biosignature searches.
- **2026:** Raman-SIP work reports direct growth-rate constraints in an active serpentinization-fueled subsurface system, illustrating how some deep communities can be more active than classic ultra-slow sediment models.
- **2026:** Repository-relevant bentonite microbiology experiments further quantify conditions under which microbial consortia can accelerate carbon-steel corrosion, while leaving field extrapolation dependent on repository conditions.

## Chronology warning

The chronology tracks publication and measurement, not a march from error to truth. Older studies can remain foundational; newer methods often address different environments and timescales rather than superseding them directly.
