# Source Log

Checked for this Author-agent pass on 2026-09-14. Links are public landing pages or DOI targets; a link establishes source identity, not unrestricted full-text access.

## Primary scientific spine

1. Holland, G. et al. (2013), **Deep fracture fluids isolated in the crust since the Precambrian era**, *Nature* 497, 357–360. https://doi.org/10.1038/nature12127
   - Use: noble-gas constraints on 1.5-billion-year minimum mean residence time and older fluid-system components at Kidd Creek/Timmins.
   - Boundary: fluid residence time is not cell age.

2. Chivian, D. et al. (2008), **Environmental genomics reveals a single-species ecosystem deep within Earth**, *Science* 322, 275–278. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1155495
   - DOE/JGI archive: https://archive.jgi.doe.gov/publication/environmental-genomics-reveals-single-species-ecosystem-deep-within-earth/
   - Use: >99.9% dominance of *Candidatus Desulforudis audaxviator* in sampled fracture fluid; genome-based metabolic reconstruction.
   - Boundary: sampled-fluid dominance is not proof that every connected niche contains exactly one species.

3. Lin, L.-H. et al. (2005), **Radiolytic H2 in continental crust: Nuclear power for deep subsurface microbial communities**, *Geochemistry, Geophysics, Geosystems* 6. https://doi.org/10.1029/2004GC000907
   - Use: measured H2 and radiolytic production model in Witwatersrand fracture waters.
   - Boundary: radiolytic sufficiency at a site does not prove all subsurface H2 is radiolytic.

4. Magnabosco, C. et al. (2018), **The biomass and number of cells in deep continental subsurface**, *Nature Geoscience* 11, 707–717. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41561-018-0221-6
   - Use: global continental-subsurface cell estimate ~2–6 × 10^29; revised global prokaryotic biomass estimate.
   - Boundary: global extrapolation from sparse and geographically biased sampling.

5. Templeton, A. S. et al. (2021), **Accessing the Subsurface Biosphere Within Rocks Undergoing Active Low-Temperature Serpentinization in the Samail Ophiolite (Oman Drilling Project)**, *JGR Biogeosciences*. https://doi.org/10.1029/2021JG006315
   - Use: redox/pH gradients, cell abundances spanning ~10^1 to 10^7 cells/g, detected sulfate reduction in serpentinite cores.
   - Boundary: local Oman system is not a universal serpentinization ecology.

6. International/Ocean Drilling contamination-tracer methods, ODP Leg 185 deep-biosphere methods. https://iodp.tamu.edu/publications/185_IR/chap_01/c1_11.htm
   - Use: fluorescent microspheres and perfluorocarbon tracers; quantified drilling-fluid intrusion and preparation contamination.
   - Boundary: absence of one tracer does not prove zero contamination by every route.

7. Tarnas, J. D. et al. (2021), **Earth-like Habitable Environments in the Subsurface of Mars**, *Astrobiology* 21(6), 741–756. https://doi.org/10.1089/ast.2020.2386
   - Use: modeled radiolytic redox energy from Martian meteorite source compositions.
   - Boundary: energetic habitability is not evidence of extant Martian organisms or accessible groundwater.

8. Mayhew, L. E. et al. (2013), **Hydrogen generation from low-temperature water-rock reactions**, *Nature Geoscience*. NASA summary: https://astrobiology.nasa.gov/news/water-rock-reactions-could-be-food-for-life-on-mars/
   - Use: low-temperature mineral-water H2 generation as another geological energy source.

9. Ellison, E. T. et al. (2021), **Low-Temperature Hydrogen Formation During Aqueous Alteration of Serpentinized Peridotite in the Samail Ophiolite**, *JGR Solid Earth*. https://doi.org/10.1029/2021JB021981
   - Use: mineralogical mechanism and continuing low-temperature H2 production questions.

10. Pavlov, A. A. et al. (2022), **Rapid Radiolytic Degradation of Amino Acids in the Martian Shallow Subsurface**, *Astrobiology* 22(9). https://doi.org/10.1089/ast.2021.0166
    - Use: cosmic-radiation destruction of shallow organic biosignatures and dependence on minerals/perchlorates.
    - Boundary: amino-acid preservation model does not directly define extant-life depth.

## Repository microbiology

11. Swedish Nuclear Fuel and Waste Management Co. (SKB), SFR repository overview. https://skb.com/our-operations/sfr/
    - Use: public description of deep geological waste infrastructure; institutional context.

12. **Microbiologically influenced corrosion (MIC) potential of bentonite microorganisms: implications for a deep geological repository for nuclear waste** (2026), *World Journal of Microbiology and Biotechnology*. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11274-026-05039-0
    - Use: laboratory mechanism evidence for bentonite-associated communities influencing steel corrosion, including strong flow/nutrient dependence.
    - Boundary: selected laboratory conditions are not a field canister lifetime.

## Current frontier/context

13. **Microbial growth rates captured using Raman-SIP reveal a highly active subsurface biosphere fueled by serpentinization** (2026), *Nature Communications*. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-70622-w
    - Use: modern direct growth-rate methods and contrast between active serpentinization systems and ultra-slow deep sediment.
    - Boundary: growth-rate method and setting differ from older maintenance-power estimates.

14. NASA Astrobiology, **Life Without the Sun** (2008). https://astrobiology.nasa.gov/news/life-without-the-sun/
    - Use: historical NASA framing of *D. audaxviator* and astrobiological implications.
    - Boundary: outreach summary; cite Chivian et al. for scientific claims.

## Source discipline

Patents, press releases, repository marketing pages, and outreach summaries are never used here as evidence that a scientific mechanism is efficacious beyond what underlying technical evidence supports. Habitability calculations remain separate from detections. Laboratory corrosion remains separate from repository performance. Geological age remains separate from biological age.
