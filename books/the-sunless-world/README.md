# The Sunless World

## Life Powered by Stone Beneath the Surface of Earth

**Author:** Sven Hardy Benson

## Thesis

Earth contains a vast, slow, largely invisible biosphere in rock and deep sediment whose most interesting ecosystems can persist with little or no direct dependence on contemporary sunlight. In some settings, radioactive decay, water-rock reactions, geologic hydrogen, sulfate, iron, methane, and ancient fracture fluids provide enough chemical disequilibrium to maintain life over extraordinary timescales. The provocative claim is not that we have discovered a second origin of life underground. We have not. It is that surface biology is a biased sample of what living can look like—and that the hardest problem in learning from the deep biosphere is often not finding a signal but proving that the signal did not arrive on the drill bit.

## Reader promise

This book follows life downward: through South African gold mines, Canadian Shield fractures, ocean drilling cores, Oman serpentinite, hot subseafloor sediment, nuclear-waste repository experiments, and the astrobiological argument for looking beneath Mars rather than only across it. It treats contamination control, energy accounting, residence-time inference, metabolism-versus-dormancy, and habitability-versus-inhabitation as first-class scientific problems.

## Evidence classes

1. **Verified/documented:** sampled cells, genomes, isotope measurements, measured geochemistry, drilling-contamination tracers, laboratory corrosion experiments, peer-reviewed biomass estimates.
2. **Credible but incomplete:** residence-time reconstructions, metabolic-rate inference at ultra-low power, global extrapolations from sparse boreholes, claims that particular communities are fully isolated from surface carbon.
3. **Disputed/unconfirmed:** a genuinely independent second genesis, perfectly closed ecosystems over geologic time, life presently existing on Mars, or any inference that chemical habitability establishes biology.
4. **Speculative synthesis:** the deep biosphere is a better conceptual template than the surface for thinking about the modal form of life on rocky worlds.
