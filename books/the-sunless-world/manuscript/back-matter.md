# Back Matter

## A note on evidence

The deep biosphere has an unusually clean way to punish sloppy language: many of its central claims come in pairs that sound similar and are scientifically different.

- ancient rock / ancient fluid
- ancient fluid / ancient organism
- cells present / cells active
- metabolism possible / metabolism measured
- geological energy available / community primarily powered by that energy
- low diversity / single-species ecosystem
- habitability / inhabitation
- microbial corrosion possible / repository failure probable
- surface-independent metabolism / independent origin of life

The book treats those slashes as load-bearing walls.

## What is established

Microorganisms inhabit deep continental and marine subsurface environments. Some communities exploit hydrogen, sulfur, methane, iron and other compounds generated or maintained by water-rock chemistry. Radiolysis of water by natural radioactivity can generate metabolically useful redox products. Serpentinization can generate hydrogen-rich reducing fluids. Deep fracture waters can remain isolated on million- to billion-year timescales. Microbes can influence mineral alteration and corrosion. Contamination during drilling and low-biomass molecular analysis is measurable and must be controlled.

## What is not established

No known deep-biosphere organism demonstrates an independent second origin of life. No observation of radiolytic habitability proves extant life on Mars. No single methane, hydrogen, mineral or organic signature is uniquely biological in every context. The age of a fluid does not establish the age of a living cell. Global biomass estimates are model-based extrapolations from sparse sampling.

## Research principle

The strongest claims in this field are not the strangest ones. They are the claims for which the route from sample to conclusion is most visible.
