# SANGUINE

## The Navy's Impossible Radio and the Argument Over What a Field Can Do

**Author:** Sven Hardy Benson

**Status:** Drafting

## Thesis

Project Sanguine began with an ugly physical fact: seawater is very good at hiding submarines and very good at hiding radio from them. The Navy's answer was not a better mast. It was to make a transmitter so geographically large that part of the upper Midwest became the antenna. That engineering choice collided with another problem that was much harder to measure: what, if anything, extremely low frequency electromagnetic fields do to living systems at the weak exposures created around such a network.

This book is about the collision, not a verdict smuggled in through vocabulary. The strategic communications requirement was real. The proposed and later reduced ELF systems were real. Public opposition was real. Navy-sponsored biological research was real. So were disagreements over military necessity. None of those facts, alone, proves either biological injury or biological harmlessness.

The deeper thesis is that Sanguine became an unusually clean laboratory for a recurring frontier-science failure: **detectability, biological interaction, biological effect, adverse effect, and operationally meaningful harm are different propositions.** Institutions and opponents alike had incentives to collapse them.

## Architecture

1. **A Radio the Size of Wisconsin** — why ordinary radio and seawater do not cooperate.
2. **Sanguine** — the first continental-scale conception.
3. **One Bit, Very Slowly** — what ELF could and could not communicate.
4. **The Submarine Has to Hear You** — deterrence, command, and the hidden assumptions inside survivability.
5. **Copper in the Ground** — electrodes, geology, current, and the Earth as circuit.
6. **1969** — the Wisconsin Test Facility and the moment an abstraction became local infrastructure.
7. **A Field Is Not a Dose** — exposure metrics before health claims.
8. **What Counts as an Effect?** — cells, organisms, behavior, reproduction, and statistical noise.
9. **The Navy Studies Itself** — sponsored research, independence, incentives, and what sponsorship does and does not invalidate.
10. **No Effect Is Also a Claim** — statistical power, endpoints, replication, and the burden carried by null results.
11. **Seafarer** — shrinking the dream without abandoning the requirement.
12. **PISCES** — the strange proposal to borrow a power grid for strategic communication.
13. **Congress Enters the Circuit** — cost, necessity, alternatives, and GAO's skepticism.
14. **The Fence Hums** — interference, induced currents, farms, telephones, and the politics of mundane coupling.
15. **Electromagnetic Folklore** — how a documented military field project became available to larger stories about mind control, weather, and secret influence.
16. **What the Literature Can Carry** — the evidence hierarchy for weak-field bioeffects.
17. **Antenna Down** — what happened to the operational system and what its end does not prove.
18. **Signal / Effect / Harm** — the epistemic machinery Sanguine leaves behind.
