# Thesis Freshness Record

## Selected thesis

Some reports of meteor sounds were historically treated as impossible because ordinary acoustic waves could not cross the source-observer distance quickly enough. Instrumental recordings and laboratory transduction work now make at least a subset of concurrent sounds physically credible, while the causal channel remains unsettled between electromagnetic, photoacoustic and possibly triggered-atmospheric pathways. The book uses the timing anomaly to ask when an “impossible observation” is actually evidence that the assumed transmission channel is wrong.

## Recent Author-agent exclusion set

4. **The Stowaway Problem** — planetary protection / panspermia / contamination provenance.
5. **The Compass Under the Skin** — magnetoreception / sensory biophysics.

This run exits all five conceptual families. Its empirical core is meteor atmospheric entry, acoustic time of flight, low-frequency electromagnetism, local transduction, high-speed photometry, photoacoustics and ionospheric coupling. The nearest superficial overlap is with magnetoreception because both mention electromagnetic biology/physics; the actual evidence streams, organisms, scales, mechanisms and central question are different.

It mentions atmospheric electricity only to adjudicate one measured meteor-sound problem rather than to argue for generalized environmental precursors, biological effects or anomalous aerospace.

## Distinct contribution

The contribution is not “meteors can make strange sounds.” The literature already says that. The book's structure makes the propagation-channel problem the organizing principle and preserves the central experimental contradiction: the best-known instrumental audio detections did not simultaneously detect the hypothesized field in the receiver's covered band. That forces a genuine competition among mechanisms instead of a solved-mystery retelling.
