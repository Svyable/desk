# Publication / Expansion Gate

This run completes the full 18-chapter argument, but the manuscript is **not yet at Desk's full-book length gate** and is not a release candidate. The current chapters are substantial first-pass prose, not 3,000-word chapters. Do not pad them to pass a counter.

## Required substantive expansion before claiming the 54,000+ target

### Chapter 1 — The Sound Before the Boom

Reconstruct Chelyabinsk from primary/peer-reviewed physical papers and eyewitness methods. Separate synchronized video evidence for the delayed shock from retrospective concurrent-sound testimony. Reproduce the survey denominator and interview chronology from the original source, not secondary summaries.

### Chapter 2 — Halley’s Clock

Recover and page-index Montanari/Halley/Olmsted primary texts. Establish what was actually reported, in what language, with what timing precision. Remove any historical detail that survives only by repetition in later electrophonic reviews.

### Chapter 3 — What Sound Is Allowed to Do

Add a quantitative worked example of acoustic travel time through a stratified atmosphere, including source altitude, slant range, temperature dependence, refraction and why none can compress minutes into simultaneity. Deepen meteor shock/infrasound physics with ReVelle and modern bolide literature.

### Chapter 4 — Witnesses Who Should Have Been Wrong

Build a pre-theory versus post-theory witness ledger. Analyze selection effects, prompted vocabulary and the probability of coincident environmental sounds. Compare lone witnesses, groups and instrumented cases without treating testimony as a single evidence class.

### Chapter 5 — Colin Keay’s Natural Antenna

Reconstruct Keay's 1980 model from equations and original event geometry. Explain turbulent-wake magnetic-field trapping, predicted field strengths and the meteor regimes in which the assumptions apply. Include strong technical criticism.

### Chapter 6 — The 1978 Fireball

Recover the original New South Wales event record, witness distribution, meteor brightness/trajectory and acoustic chronology. Do not rely on the 1980 abstract alone.

### Chapter 7 — Making Objects Speak

Page-index Keay/Ostwald laboratory apparatus, field strengths, materials and human threshold distribution. Quantify transduction efficiency and compare it with later photoacoustic efficiency on common acoustic units.

### Chapter 8 — Hunting the Radio Ghost

Build a complete positive/null meteor ELF/VLF ledger with receiver bandwidth, sensitivity, site noise, meteor brightness, event count and false-coincidence method. Reconstruct the 1999 Price/Blum analysis and any subsequent replications or critiques.

### Chapter 9 — The Leonid Trap

Reconstruct campaign logistics and preregistered/a-priori event criteria from the full 2002 paper and predecessor reports. Establish the denominator of bright meteors and environmental acoustic baseline.

### Chapter 10 — Two Pops in Mongolia

Reproduce the two optical/audio waveforms numerically from published figures or archived data if available. Quantify timing uncertainty, SPL calibration, spectra, meteor altitude at sound onset and observer geometry.

### Chapter 11 — The Missing Signal

Map receiver transfer functions and lower-frequency blind spots. Calculate what field would be required to generate the measured acoustic outputs for plausible local transducers. State exactly what the null does and does not exclude.

### Chapter 12 — When a Meteor Touches the Ionosphere

Deepen meteor-trail plasma chemistry, E-region electrodynamics, sprite triggering and null population evidence. Reconstruct the Suszcynsky event and later TLE/meter-flux work without turning coincidence into general causation.

### Chapter 13 — The Photoacoustic Turn

Reproduce the 2017 thermal model and bench measurements in reader-accessible form. Explain optical absorption depth, thermal diffusivity, modulation frequency and acoustic pressure. Search for independent replication, criticism or natural-event validation after 2017.

### Chapter 14 — Hair, Leaves, and the Full-Moon Problem

Build explicit irradiance and magnitude conversions. Compare full moon, −9, −12 and Chelyabinsk-class apparent brightness. Model several materials and masking noise conditions. Clarify apparent versus absolute meteor magnitude where needed.

### Chapter 15 — An Electric Sky Anyway

Reproduce Kelley–Price's ambipolar-field/Hall-current calculation and list falsifiable geometric predictions. Compare predicted ground power flux with Keay transduction thresholds and measured natural VLF background.

### Chapter 16 — Chelyabinsk Had Two Clocks

Use the Popova et al. physical dataset, original eyewitness/interview study and synchronized videos. Map concurrent reports spatially against trajectory, brightness and urban/rural noise. Search for surviving raw audio that predates survey prompting.

### Chapter 17 — The Experiment the Sky Owes Us

Turn the conceptual design into an executable preregistration: sensor bandwidths, sample rates, dynamic range, station spacing, candidate transducer materials, controls, event thresholds, null handling, statistical false-coincidence test and decision rules for mechanism attribution.

### Chapter 18 — Impossible Witnesses

Add carefully bounded philosophy/history comparisons where an apparent impossibility was resolved by identifying a hidden channel or coupling mechanism. Do not use sprites, ball lightning, UAP or other anomalies as rhetorical proof by analogy. Each comparison must earn itself.

## Release-blocking unresolved questions

- Is there an independent instrumental replication of a concurrent meteor sound after the 2002 publication?
- Are the Mongolia raw audio/video/field data publicly recoverable?
- What exact field strength and spectrum does each electromagnetic source model predict at the observer for the two Mongolia events?
- Can the 2017 photoacoustic model reproduce the short low-frequency Mongolia pops at their estimated meteor brightness and observed timing?
- Do post-2017 studies materially favor photoacoustic, electromagnetic or plural mechanisms?
- Can Chelyabinsk concurrent reports be tied to any unprompted synchronized recordings?

Until these are answered, the book should remain explicit that the physical category is stronger than the mechanism assignment.
