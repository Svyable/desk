# Back Matter

This working edition separates the physical existence of concurrent meteor sounds from any single mechanism proposed to explain them. Readers should keep three distinctions in view when following the cited literature: ordinary delayed acoustic waves versus concurrent local sound; proof that a transduction mechanism can work versus proof that it operated during a natural event; and a meteor-correlated signal versus a demonstrated causal chain from meteor to field or light to local acoustic output.

The central primary and peer-reviewed sources are listed in the book's research source log with live links. Historical witness material is treated as phenomenology unless an instrumental record exists. The 1998 Mongolia observations receive greater weight because the acoustic events were recorded under a purpose-built campaign with synchronized optical observation. The absence of a coincident ELF/VLF detection in the receiver's measured band remains part of the result rather than being silently converted into support for an unmeasured lower-frequency field.

The 2017 photoacoustic experiments establish that rapidly modulated light can generate audible local sound in suitable materials under controlled conditions. They do not establish the mechanism of every electrophonic report. Likewise, the 2017 ionospheric current model establishes a quantitative theoretical pathway worth testing, not a measured cause for the historical cases.

The most valuable future evidence would be a synchronized natural-event dataset containing high-speed photometry, broadband electric and magnetic fields, calibrated audio, infrasound, local transducer controls and precise meteor geometry. A denominator of silent bright meteors is as important as the positive events.

Sven Hardy Benson
