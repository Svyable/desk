# The Expiration Principle

## Why the World Works Because Things End

The central thesis is that expiration is hidden civilization infrastructure. DNS answers, certificates, passwords, emergency powers, patents, drug exclusivities, options, leases, passports, product dates, gift cards, public-benefit renewals, identity documents, data retention, consent, and AI-agent permissions all answer the same question: **How long should yesterday's answer remain good enough?**

The book argues that expiration improves a system only when renewal produces useful new information. Otherwise a clock can become churn, bureaucracy, artificial scarcity, exclusion, or synchronized failure. The counter-thesis is equally important: some things should not expire. Durable rights, durable human status, archives, and other hard-to-reconstruct goods may require continuity precisely because termination is more dangerous than staleness.
