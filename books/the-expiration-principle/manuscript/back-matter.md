# Back Matter

## Research Note

*The Expiration Principle* is a work of synthesis. It connects technical expiration mechanisms, legal sunsets, market exclusivities, administrative renewals, product dating, data-retention rules, archives, consent, and machine authorization around one governing question: how long should an earlier decision remain sufficient without being reconsidered?

The manuscript draws primarily on standards, statutes, regulations, agency guidance, official technical documentation, and institutional research. The working source ledger is maintained in `research/source-ledger.csv`; chapter-level hypotheses and falsification tests are maintained in `research/chapter-briefs.md` and `research/README.md`.

Examples are used to illuminate the governing mechanism rather than to provide legal, medical, financial, or security advice. Rules can vary by jurisdiction and change over time. Readers making consequential decisions should consult the current controlling authority and qualified professionals where appropriate.

## A Note on Originality

The book's originality claim is deliberately narrow. Expiration, sunsets, credential lifetimes, patent terms, retention schedules, renewal burdens, and related mechanisms have substantial literatures of their own. This book does not claim to have discovered those mechanisms. Its contribution is the cross-domain synthesis: treating expiration as a general institutional primitive for deciding how long the past may govern the future without new information.

Research conducted during drafting did not identify a general-audience trade book organized around this exact synthesis. That search is evidence of differentiation, not proof that no comparable work exists.

## Source Discipline

Where the manuscript relies on time-sensitive rules, publication preparation should verify the current primary source immediately before release. Particular attention should be paid to certificate-validity rules, identity-document procedures, benefits administration, privacy law, drug guidance, financial regulation, and any other regime capable of changing after the manuscript's research date.
