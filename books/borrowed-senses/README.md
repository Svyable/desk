# Borrowed Senses

## How Intelligence Agencies Learned to Recruit Biology Before They Could Build It

**Sven Hardy Benson**

### Governing thesis

The strangest history of military sensing is not a history of machines becoming animal-like. It is the opposite. For decades, states repeatedly recruited animals because biology already possessed sensing, navigation, discrimination, stealth, endurance, and autonomy that engineered systems could not cheaply reproduce. Pigeons became low-altitude imaging platforms. Dolphins became mine-hunters. Sea lions became underwater recovery systems. Intelligence services tested cats, ravens and other animals because the animal was not merely a carrier: sometimes its evolved sensory and behavioral machinery was the capability.

That history matters now because robotics and AI are usually narrated as replacement. The more interesting record is hybridization: organism + handler + instrument + mission doctrine. The book asks what intelligence institutions learned by borrowing senses they did not understand well enough to manufacture, and what that implies for modern biohybrid systems, autonomous sensing, and our habit of defining intelligence around human cognition.

### Evidence classes

1. **Verified/declassified/documented:** CIA pigeon-camera development; Navy Marine Mammal Program history and present mine/recovery missions; documented wartime dolphin/REMUS mine-countermeasure teaming; declassified institutional histories.
2. **Credible but incomplete:** operational details acknowledged by agencies but still partly classified; historical mission accounts with incomplete technical records.
3. **Disputed/unconfirmed:** sensational offensive-animal claims, assassination stories, and program capabilities not supported by released records.
4. **Speculative synthesis:** the proposition that animal programs constitute an overlooked lineage of biohybrid autonomous systems and a useful conceptual ancestor of embodied AI.

### Publication hold

Never let the comic surface of spy pigeons or dolphins substitute for technical reconstruction. Every animal capability must be separated into evolved sensor, trained behavior, attached instrument, handler inference, environmental constraint, and mission outcome.
