# BORROWED SENSES

## When intelligence agencies recruit biology

**Sven Hardy Benson**

Status: working draft

## Reader promise

A pigeon is not a camera platform until somebody makes a camera light enough, predicts the bird's route, accepts that the bird has preferences, and gets the film back. A cat is not a microphone because an engineer can implant electronics in it. Biology is not hardware with fur on it.

BORROWED SENSES follows the strange, documented edge where intelligence engineering tries to borrow capabilities evolution already built: flight, homing, camouflage, hearing, sensing and access. The book uses CIA animal-borne collection experiments as a hard historical control for claims about exotic sensing and biological augmentation. It asks a simple question that turns out to be brutal: where does the sensing actually happen?

The answer separates a receptor from a useful sensor, a physiological response from perception, perception from behavior, and behavior from an operational intelligence system.

## Central question

What do documented attempts to turn living organisms into collection systems teach us about extraordinary claims for hidden human senses, bioelectromagnetic effects and biological surveillance?

## Evidence posture

Declassified/official-intelligence-history plus peer-reviewed sensory-neuroscience heavy. CIA's pigeon camera is documented; details of missions remain classified and the Agency says the approach was ultimately ineffective because birds did not reliably fly over required locations. Human geomagnetic EEG work is treated as evidence of a reported neural response, not proof of conscious magnetoreception or navigational ability. Claims about psi, psychotronics, directed-energy mind control or operational human magnetic sensing require independent evidence.
