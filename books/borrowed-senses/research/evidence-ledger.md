# Borrowed Senses — evidence ledger

## Book claim under test

Military and intelligence animal programs are most revealing when treated as early autonomy systems: institutions borrowed evolved sensing, navigation, concealment, and behavior that machines could not yet reproduce, but the organism's own agency prevented a clean separation between capability and control.

Competing explanation: these programs are mostly a collection of unrelated Cold War curiosities. If primary records show no recurring engineering logic across species/programs, the book should narrow rather than forcing a unifying autonomy thesis.

## Evidence classes

### 1. Verified / declassified / documented

- CIA publicly dates Acoustikitty exploration to 1964, describes the implanted microphone/transmitter/antenna configuration, says limited field tests exposed control problems, and says the project ended in 1967 without operational use. Source: https://www.cia.gov/stories/story/natural-spies-animals-in-espionage/
- CIA says the famous vehicle-death story arose from a former official's joke and says the animal was unharmed. Treat as CIA's documented institutional account pending underlying-record reconstruction, not omniscient fact.
- CIA Museum documents a pigeon-borne camera measuring 4.7 x 2.4 x 2.2 cm, motor-driven film advance, timed photography, and low-altitude reconnaissance rationale. It says mission details remain classified. Source: https://www.cia.gov/legacy/museum/artifact/pigeon-camera/
- U.S. Navy NIWC Pacific says the Marine Mammal Program has trained dolphins and sea lions since 1959; early work examined more than a dozen marine mammal species plus sharks, rays, sea turtles and marine birds; current operational species are bottlenose dolphins and California sea lions. Source: https://www.niwcpacific.navy.mil/About/Departments/Intelligence-Surveillance-and-Reconnaissance/Marine-Mammal-Program/
- Navy documents current roles in mine/object detection, recovery, and swimmer/diver detection, and states that dolphin biosonar remains useful in shallow/cluttered environments difficult for electronic sonar.
- Office of Naval Research independently describes the program as beginning in the 1960s after multiple species were tested before settling on dolphins and sea lions. Source: https://www.nre.navy.mil/organization/departments/code-34/division-342/marine-mammal-health
- CIA publicly describes robotic catfish Charlie/Charlene as unmanned underwater vehicles developed to explore aquatic robot technology and water-sample collection. Source: CIA Natural Spies page above.

### 2. Credible but incomplete

- Participant histories of CIA animal programs where underlying technical and operational records remain partly classified or unrecovered.
- Operational performance details for pigeon reconnaissance. The artifact and concept are documented; CIA explicitly says mission details remain classified.
- Comparative Soviet/Russian military-animal programs. Require Russian-language primary records, institutional histories, or strong archival scholarship before manuscript claims.

### 3. Disputed / unconfirmed

- Claims that U.S. Navy dolphins were trained to carry offensive weapons or kill enemy divers. Navy explicitly denies the offensive-weapons narrative; contrary claims require independent records rather than folklore.
- Variants of the Acoustikitty taxi/bus death story. CIA's 2024 account rejects them and traces the story to a joke. Preserve provenance rather than repeating as event.
- Any claim that animal intelligence programs demonstrate mind control, direct brain-machine control, or exotic biological manipulation. No such inference follows from the current source set.

### 4. Speculative synthesis / open conjecture

- Animal programs can be read as a prehistory of autonomous systems because autonomy was imported rather than engineered.
- The key technical trade is not biology versus machinery but borrowed capability versus controllability.
- Classification can turn a bounded strange program into an evidentiary seed for larger myths because outsiders correctly infer that something unusual was hidden while incorrectly inferring its scope.
- Modern biomimetic robotics may represent a second phase: retain the morphology or sensing principle while removing the organism and its independent agency. This needs technical literature, not analogy alone.

## Contradictions and incentive audit

- CIA and Navy public histories have reputational incentives: they may emphasize ingenuity, humane treatment, successful bounded missions, and debunking of sensational stories. Use them strongly for what the institution acknowledges, more cautiously for claims that would exculpate the institution.
- Former officers have incentives toward memorable storytelling. Recollections should be dated and compared with contemporaneous records.
- Animal-rights advocacy can surface real records and ethical problems but may also select the most alarming interpretation. Follow citations back to source documents.
- Popular histories have strong incentives to preserve the funniest version of Acoustikitty and the most cinematic version of military dolphins.
- Classification itself is not evidence of extraordinary capability. It can conceal performance, collection geometry, targets, vulnerabilities, or mundane operational details.

## Defeat conditions

The central thesis weakens materially if: (a) animal programs prove too operationally and technically dissimilar to support a common autonomy/control argument; (b) primary records show animals were mainly passive payload carriers rather than sensing/decision elements; or (c) machine alternatives of the same periods already matched the relevant capability and animals were selected mostly for institutional accident rather than performance, concealment, or access.

The book strengthens if independent primary histories repeatedly show the same bargain: superior biological sensing/mobility/access paired with training, reliability, welfare, or command-and-control constraints.
