# Back Matter

*Unconsciously Competent: How Mastery Disappears Into the Mind—and What Happens When Machines Take the Reps*

**Sven Hardy Benson**

This book distinguishes automaticity, procedural learning, expertise, intuition, habit, tacit knowledge, and cognitive offloading rather than treating them as synonyms. The research literature behind those distinctions is documented in the book's research trail.

The argument is intentionally narrower than a claim that automation or artificial intelligence makes people less intelligent. Humans have always offloaded cognition into tools, language, writing, maps, institutions, other people, and machines. Offloading can expand what a person or a group is capable of doing. The risk examined here is more specific: when a tool substitutes for the repetitions that would otherwise create a skill, the immediate performance benefit may be purchased partly with foregone learning or future skill decay. The size and importance of that trade depend on the task, the learner, the feedback environment, and how the tool is used.

The practical rule that emerges is not “do everything yourself.” It is simpler and harder: know which repetitions are building the person you will need later, and be careful about giving those away.
