# Back Matter

## Evidence posture

This manuscript is archival-heavy and ethics-history-heavy. It treats the Fernald nutrition studies as documented low-dose, nontherapeutic radioisotope research involving institutionalized boys, not as a proven radiation-injury event or a covert weapons experiment.

The strongest factual spine comes from the 1994 Massachusetts task-force review and the 1995 Advisory Committee on Human Radiation Experiments reconstruction. Later testimony, journalism, litigation reporting, and retrospective scholarship are used for experience, chronology, and institutional response, not to override primary records.

## Claim boundary

Nothing in this manuscript should be read as claiming that the Fernald tracer studies caused cancer or other specific illnesses in former subjects. ACHRE judged physical harm from the doses extremely unlikely. The book's central claim is ethical and institutional: subject selection, incomplete disclosure, presumed parental permission, and the use of scarce privileges to recruit institutionalized children remain serious even when physical risk is low.

## Defeat conditions

The book's thesis should be materially revised if original records establish that parents were routinely and clearly told about the radioactive tracers through communications not considered by the state and federal reviews; if the boys' Science Club benefits were unrelated to research recruitment; if the studies were therapeutic for the individual participants rather than nontherapeutic nutrition research; or if original protocols show that child refusal was systematically respected despite the 1953 correspondence quoted by ACHRE.

The book should also revise any institutional-responsibility claim when original funding, protocol, or correspondence records show that a named institution played a smaller or larger operational role than later retellings imply.
