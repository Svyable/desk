## Thesis

At the Walter E. Fernald State School, radioactive-tracer nutrition studies did not require a secret laboratory or a monstrous dose. They required something more ordinary and, in retrospect, more useful to understand: a population of institutionalized boys whose diets could be controlled, whose cooperation could be purchased with privileges that barely counted as privileges outside the institution, and whose parents or guardians received letters that did not say radioactive isotopes were involved. The experiments were scientifically legible, publicly funded in part, and apparently low-dose. That is precisely why they matter. Fernald shows how human-subject abuse can occur inside respectable science without depending on sadism, spectacular injury, or a conspiracy to poison anyone. Convenience, hierarchy, euphemism, and unequal bargaining power can do most of the work.
