# Chronology

- **1930s–1940s:** Radioisotopes become increasingly useful biological and medical tracers; institutionalized children are already part of a broader American history of pediatric research.
- **1946:** ACHRE reports a Fernald nutrition study exposing 17 young male residents to radioactive iron.
- **1947:** AEC General Manager Carroll Wilson issues consent-related correspondence for certain human applications of potentially harmful substances. Later reviewers find the scope and application to tracer studies ambiguous.
- **November 2, 1949:** Fernald superintendent Malcolm J. Farrell sends a parent/guardian letter describing special diet and blood testing. Surviving letter does not mention a radioisotope. Permission form refers back to the described project.
- **1950–1953:** ACHRE reports 57 subjects exposed to radioactive calcium across 17 related subexperiments.
- **May 1, 1953:** MIT professor Robert S. Harris writes to Fernald clinical director Clemens Benda regarding subjects who objected and the possibility of emphasizing the Science Club angle.
- **May 28, 1953:** Benda sends parent letter describing volunteer participation, test meals, blood samples and Science Club privileges; no radioisotope disclosed; silence is treated as permission.
- **1954:** Journal of Nutrition publishes a calcium-45 breakfast study involving 19 institutionalized adolescent boys and describing 0.85 microcurie labeled breakfasts.
- **1950s–1960s:** Debate over pediatric research and use of institutionalized populations becomes more explicit; radiation-risk knowledge and human-subject governance continue evolving.
- **1970s:** U.S. human-subject regulation becomes substantially more formalized; additional protections for children develop after national debate and commission work.
- **1993–1994:** Human-radiation revelations trigger federal and state review. Massachusetts convenes Task Force to Review Human Subject Research.
- **May 1994:** Massachusetts task force reports no significant health effects from the nutrition tracer studies in the best judgment of its experts while finding serious rights/consent problems.
- **1994–1995:** ACHRE hears former subjects and reconstructs documents, standards, dose and ethics.
- **October 3, 1995:** President Bill Clinton accepts the ACHRE report and apologizes for wrongs identified in the broader human-radiation history.
- **1995:** Former Fernald subjects pursue class litigation against MIT and Quaker Oats.
- **December 1997–1998:** Proposed $1.85 million class settlement is reported and receives federal court approval; institutional statements continue to distinguish low physical dose from disclosure failures.
- **Present research posture:** Fernald is best documented as a low-dose human-subject ethics failure centered on disclosure, selection, assent and institutional power; disease-causation claims remain a different evidentiary question.
