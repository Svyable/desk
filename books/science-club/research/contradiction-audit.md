# Contradiction and Incentive Audit

## Low dose versus serious wrong

**Apparent contradiction:** Later official reviews say physical harm was extremely unlikely, while the same reviews condemn the studies ethically.

**Resolution:** Different propositions. Dose addresses expected/actual biological injury. Disclosure, assent and fair selection address respect and justice. Do not use either to cancel the other.

## Permission form versus lack of informed consent

**Apparent contradiction:** Parents/guardians were asked for permission, so claims of `no consent` can overstate the record.

**Resolution:** Use `inadequate/incomplete disclosure` unless a specific subject is documented as having no valid guardian authorization. The surviving letters omitted radioisotope use and blurred research with benefit.

## 17 + 57 versus 62 versus popular totals

**Apparent contradiction:** Different sources report different subject numbers.

**Likely explanation:** overlapping participants and different counting units: unique people, experiment subjects, or exposures. Do not sum study counts into a unique-person total.

## Commercial motive

**Claim pressure:** The cereal-company connection invites a simple story in which children were irradiated to improve advertising.

**Evidence rule:** Funding, fellowship support and cereal supply may be documented; operational control and advertising intent require original correspondence. Litigation advocacy is not enough by itself.

## Federal-government framing

**Claim pressure:** `The U.S. government experimented on children` is rhetorically efficient.

**Evidence rule:** Separate federal isotope infrastructure/funding, state institutional authority, university research design/execution and private sponsorship. Attribute verbs.

## Secrecy framing

**Claim pressure:** Cold War radiation history encourages classification explanations.

**Evidence rule:** Do not call Fernald classified or a national-security secret without classification records. The stronger documented mechanism is nondisclosure to subjects/families and low public visibility.

## Health claims

**Claim pressure:** Former subjects' later illnesses naturally become linked in memory to undisclosed radiation exposure.

**Evidence rule:** Testimony establishes experience and concern; disease causation requires dose and epidemiology. Preserve ACHRE/task-force low-harm finding unless stronger evidence displaces it.

## Institutional incentives

- Researchers: scientific result, protocol completion, stable cohort, publication.
- Fernald administrators: prestige/cooperation with universities, possible institutional nutrition knowledge, operational ease.
- Federal atomic agencies: isotope applications, biomedical knowledge, peaceful-atom legitimacy, program growth.
- Commercial sponsor: nutrition/product knowledge and competitive value.
- Later institutions: minimize legal/reputational liability while acknowledging undeniable disclosure problems.
- Plaintiffs/lawyers: maximize legally cognizable injury and institutional responsibility.
- Journalists: compress a complicated program into a memorable headline.

None of these incentives proves dishonesty. Each is a reason to seek independent documents.
