# Source Log

## Primary / authoritative spine

1. Advisory Committee on Human Radiation Experiments, Final Report, Chapter 7, `The Studies at the Fernald School`.
   - https://ehss.energy.gov/OHRE/roadmap/achre/chap7_5.html
   - Documents 17 subjects exposed to radioactive iron in 1946 and 57 subjects exposed to radioactive calcium across related subexperiments from 1950–1953; treats the doses as low and physical harm as extremely unlikely; reproduces and analyzes the 1949 and 1953 parental letters; discusses Science Club privileges and Robert S. Harris correspondence about boys who objected.

2. ACHRE Chapter 7 footnotes.
   - https://ehss.energy.gov/OHRE/roadmap/achre/chap7_fn.html
   - Provides document identifiers for the parental permission form, Benda letter, Harris letter, AEC human-applications minutes and Carroll Wilson consent correspondence; identifies the Massachusetts task-force report and relevant pediatric-research literature.

3. Massachusetts Department of Mental Retardation Task Force to Review Human Subject Research, `A Report on the Use of Radioactive Materials in Human Subject Research that Involved Residents of State-Operated Facilities within the Commonwealth of Massachusetts from 1943 through 1973` (1994).
   - NLM catalog record: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/nlmcatalog/100948838
   - Full archival copy should be obtained for the next expansion pass; ACHRE quotes and cites it extensively.

## Source conflicts to preserve

- Counts differ across retrospective summaries because the studies overlap, reports count experiment participations differently, and some journalism conflates unique individuals with exposures. The manuscript currently uses ACHRE's 17 iron-study subjects and 57 calcium-study subjects as study counts and does **not** add them to claim 74 unique boys.
- MIT's 1994 archival summary says 62 youths in all across nutrition studies; later popular accounts report other totals. Resolve with original subject rosters and published-paper overlap before asserting a unique-subject number.
- Litigation allegations that some subjects exceeded federal limits conflict with state/federal later risk reconstructions emphasizing low dose. Preserve the allegation as disputed unless original dosimetry and the historically applicable limit establish it.
- Commercial sponsorship is documented, but claims about Quaker Oats' operational control, advertising intent, or responsibility for recruitment require proposition-specific proof.
