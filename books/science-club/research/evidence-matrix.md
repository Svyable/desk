# Evidence Matrix

## Class 1 — verified / declassified / documented

- MIT-linked researchers and senior Fernald staff conducted nontherapeutic nutrition studies using radioactive tracers with young male Fernald residents in the late 1940s and early 1950s.
- ACHRE reports 17 subjects exposed to radioactive iron in 1946 and 57 subjects exposed to radioactive calcium in related subexperiments from 1950–1953.
- ACHRE judged the doses low and physical harm extremely unlikely.
- Surviving 1949 and 1953 parental communications described diet, blood sampling and/or calcium but did not disclose radioisotope use.
- The 1953 letter described Science Club benefits including milk, a baseball game, the beach and outside dinners.
- Robert S. Harris wrote about three subjects who objected and suggested the Science Club angle could help induce them to change their minds.
- The Massachusetts task force found the nutritional studies nontherapeutic and the disclosure inadequate for informed decision-making.
- AEC consent-related correspondence existed in 1947, though its precise applicability to low-dose tracer research on healthy/institutionalized children was ambiguous.
- MIT publicly apologized/expressed sorrow over the disclosure failures in 1994-era statements.
- A class-action settlement involving MIT and Quaker Oats was approved in 1998 after a proposed $1.85 million agreement.

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete

- Specific former subjects' recollections of what they understood at the time and why they joined the Science Club.
- Degree to which Science Club rewards changed individual decisions.
- Exact institutional division of responsibility among MIT investigators, Fernald administration, state officials, federal funders/isotope programs, and commercial sponsors for each subexperiment.
- Extent to which all parents/guardians received only the surviving form letters versus additional oral explanation.
- How strongly the 1947 AEC consent rules were known, interpreted, and enforced in this research channel.
- Exact number of unique boys across overlapping iron/calcium studies.

## Class 3 — disputed / not independently established

- Claims that the Fernald nutrition studies caused specific later cancers, gastrointestinal disease, or other illnesses in former subjects.
- Claims that investigators knowingly administered doses above an applicable federal limit.
- Claims that the nutrition work was a covert weapons-effects experiment.
- Claims that national-security classification was used specifically to conceal the Fernald nutrition studies from subjects or families.
- Claims that Quaker Oats directed subject recruitment or consent practices merely because it supported cereal research.
- Any assertion that every boy was a ward of the state, every boy lacked cognitive capacity, or every parent was completely absent from the decision.

## Class 4 — speculative synthesis / open inference

- Fernald as a model of `convenience capture`: institutional dependency creates methodological advantages that make a vulnerable population unusually attractive to researchers.
- The hypothesis that low perceived physical risk made investigators more likely to treat radioactivity as technically irrelevant to parental disclosure.
- The hypothesis that fragmented responsibility across university, state institution, federal atomic infrastructure and commercial sponsorship reduced the chance that any one actor would own the full ethical question.
- The proposition that the deepest legacy of Fernald is not radiation injury but the persistence of research systems that turn constrained environments into clean experimental settings.

## Defeat tests

- Recover every original parent/guardian communication. If radioactivity was routinely disclosed through other materials or conversations, revise the consent thesis.
- Reconstruct unique participant rosters. If Science Club membership and experimental participation were materially different from the current reconstruction, revise recruitment chapters.
- Recover original protocol and sponsor correspondence. If commercial or federal control differs from current bounded language, revise responsibility allocation.
- Recompute doses from administered activity, subject mass, biokinetics and applicable contemporary standards; keep health claims tied to the strongest dosimetry rather than later rhetoric.
