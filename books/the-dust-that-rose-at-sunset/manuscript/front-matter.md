# The Dust That Rose at Sunset

## Horizon Glow and the Electric Moon

Sven Hardy Benson

The Moon is supposed to be the easy dead world.

No air. No weather. No biology. No wind to move a grain.

Then Surveyor photographed a line of light after sunset. Apollo astronauts sketched glow and streamers above the lunar limb. Surface instruments registered strange events around sunrise. For decades, the elegant explanation was electrical: ultraviolet sunlight and plasma charged lunar soil, strong fields developed near light-shadow boundaries, and dust rose into the vacuum.

Then NASA flew through the cloud.

LADEE found dust around the Moon, but not quite the dust the old story predicted. Its particle detector mapped a permanent asymmetric cloud generated by micrometeoroid impacts. A dedicated search found no evidence, within its sensitivity, for the classic high-altitude population of approximately tenth-micron electrostatically lofted grains from roughly three to 250 kilometers above the terminator. Optical measurements did not reveal the human-eye-visible dust glow the simplest Apollo interpretation implied. Later ultraviolet analysis complicated the result again by finding evidence consistent with a much smaller nanodust population. Laboratory work continued to show that electrical charging can move grains near rough surfaces.

The mystery did not survive intact.

It stratified.

That is the subject of this book.

The phrase *lunar dust levitation* hides several different claims: grains acquire charge; local electric fields exist; those fields overcome adhesion; grains hop near the surface; some reach meters; some reach kilometers; enough are present to scatter visible sunlight; a global population persists long enough to be a dust exosphere. Evidence strong enough for one layer is routinely borrowed by the next.

I am interested in the borrowing.

The book separates four evidence classes throughout the research trail.

**Class 1 — verified/documented.** Surveyor horizon-brightness observations exist in the mission literature. Apollo 17 crew members recorded high-altitude glow observations. Lunar grains charge in plasma and ultraviolet environments. LADEE/LDEX directly detected a permanent impact-generated lunar dust cloud. LDEX reported no evidence for the classic electrostatic population in its tested altitude/size regime. Laboratory experiments have demonstrated dust charging and motion under controlled vacuum conditions.

**Class 2 — credible but incomplete.** Some Surveyor brightness may have been forward scattering from near-surface dust. Natural microfield enhancement may mobilize a measurable fraction of grains at shadow boundaries. Nanodust may occupy regimes poorly sampled by larger-grain impact detectors. Natural electrostatic transport may affect near-surface regolith evolution even if it contributes little to the high-altitude cloud.

**Class 3 — disputed or overextended.** A dense persistent electrostatic dust fountain extending tens of kilometers; treating Apollo visual glow as uniquely diagnostic of dust; treating the presence of any electrostatic grain motion as confirmation of the Apollo interpretation; treating LADEE’s orbital null as proof that no electrical dust motion occurs near the ground.

**Class 4 — speculative synthesis.** The original horizon-glow mystery may have accidentally fused several real but distinct populations: impact ejecta, nanodust, near-surface electrical motion, and an optical phenomenon with another source. Future lunar infrastructure may create artificial electrostatic and mechanical dust regimes stronger locally than the natural effect people spent fifty years trying to detect.

The method is deliberately asymmetrical. Old claims do not receive extra credit for surviving a long time. New nulls do not receive extra reach merely because their instruments are better. Every result is confined to what it actually measured.

That matters because lunar exploration is about to make the question practical again. Long-lived surface systems will put seals, optics, radiators, solar arrays, joints, habitats and human lungs inside the first few meters above the regolith—the exact domain modern orbital missions constrained least directly.

Apollo gave us moments.

The next lunar era can give us distributions.

A good ending to this story is not belief or debunking. It is a surface experiment with synchronized dust tracking, electric-field measurement, plasma diagnostics, optical scattering, impact monitoring, and controlled shadows. The surviving near-surface hypothesis is now specific enough to risk.

The Moon has waited long enough for us to put the instrument in the gap.
