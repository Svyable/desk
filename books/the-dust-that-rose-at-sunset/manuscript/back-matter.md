# Back Matter

## A note on evidence

This book is built around a claim that became less unified as measurement improved. That makes source discipline more important than rhetorical closure.

The strongest documented facts are not the most dramatic ones. Surveyor spacecraft recorded low-level horizon brightness after local sunset. Apollo crews reported and sketched high-altitude glow. Lunar materials charge in ultraviolet light and plasma. LADEE’s LDEX directly measured a permanent asymmetric dust cloud produced by micrometeoroid impacts. A dedicated LDEX search found no evidence for the classic approximately 0.1-micrometer electrostatically lofted population over roughly 3–250 km within its detection model. Modern laboratory work continues to demonstrate charging and mobilization under relevant forcing, including combined vacuum-ultraviolet and plasma conditions.

The least secure statement is the one popular summaries often imply most strongly: that the Apollo orbital glow was a directly observed high-altitude electrostatic dust fountain. The optical observation is documented. The physical attribution remains disputed and constrained by later data.

The same separation applies to the near-surface problem. Demonstrating electrostatic dust motion in a laboratory is not equivalent to measuring its natural lunar flux. Conversely, an orbital null beginning kilometers above the surface does not establish zero transport in the first meter.

The book therefore uses four evidence classes in its working notes:

1. **Verified/declassified/documented:** direct mission records, NASA archives, instrument results, peer-reviewed measurements, and reproducible laboratory results.
2. **Credible but incomplete:** physically plausible interpretations with serious supporting evidence but missing direct convergence, such as a natural near-surface electrostatic dust flux large enough to matter operationally.
3. **Disputed/overextended:** claims that turn an interpretation into a direct observation, use one scale to authenticate another, or ignore modern upper limits.
4. **Speculative synthesis/open conjecture:** broader ideas about long-timescale regolith sorting, artificial lunar dust weather around future infrastructure, and how several historical observations may have fused distinct populations.

The classes are not permanent labels. A good future experiment should move claims between them.

## The result I would most like to be wrong about

My working synthesis is that the classic tall electrostatic fountain has lost most of its original territory, while near-surface electrical transport remains an undermeasured but physically serious regime. That synthesis would be wrong in an interesting way if a modern, well-calibrated surface-to-orbit campaign detected a reproducible population whose size, density and altitude simultaneously reproduced Apollo’s visible glow and survived LADEE-era constraints.

I would welcome that result.

The point of the book is not to protect the contraction thesis. It is to make the surviving claims specific enough that nature can take more territory away—or give some back.

## Useful starting sources

The complete source trail is in `research/source-log.md`. The central documents include NASA Technical Reports Server records for Surveyor horizon glow and Apollo-era modeling; the 2015 *Nature* report of the permanent impact-generated lunar dust cloud; the 2015 LDEX electrostatic-lofting null; the 2016 *Nature Geoscience* nanodust analysis; controlled charging/transport experiments; and recent 2025 work on lunar cavity charging and combined VUV/plasma dust mobilization.

The most valuable next source is not another review article.

It is a dataset from a modern lunar surface station watching individual grains move through an electric field.
