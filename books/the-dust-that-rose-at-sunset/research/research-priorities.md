## Highest-value historical research

### Surveyor reconstruction

Recover the original Surveyor 5, 6 and 7 image frames used in horizon-glow analyses, camera calibration documents, exposure settings, transmission/processing history, and exact lunar geometry. Reproduce the brightness reduction from raw or earliest-available data.

**Why it matters:** later literature often treats the inferred dust cloud as though the camera measured grains. A modern reconstruction can quantify how much of the original inference depends on calibration and scattering assumptions.

### Apollo orbital observation reconstruction

Build an event table from mission transcripts, crew technical debriefs, sketches, photographs, spacecraft attitude, window used, solar elongation, local time, dark-adaptation state and contemporaneous discussion.

**Why it matters:** the Apollo 17 glow is the bridge from near-surface dust to kilometer-scale population. The bridge should be rebuilt from primary mission records.

### Apollo surface dust-detector reanalysis

Locate event-level telemetry, housekeeping channels, calibration reports and thermal histories for the lunar dust/meteor instruments associated with sunrise/sunset anomalies.

**Why it matters:** a modern pulse-shape and correlation analysis could strengthen or weaken one of the few surface particle datasets relevant to natural electrostatic motion.

## Highest-value quantitative synthesis

### One exclusion map

Build a common model space with:

- number density,
- launch velocity,
- local time,
- optical wavelength,
- source mechanism.

Overlay allowed/excluded regions from Surveyor photometry, Apollo optical interpretation, Clementine, LRO, LADEE/LDEX, LADEE/UVS and laboratory launch-rate constraints.

**Goal:** replace the phrase “lunar dust levitation remains controversial” with a visible parameter-space map.

### Natural versus engineered field comparison

Extract measured field strengths, frequencies and geometries used by electrodynamic dust shields and laboratory mobilization experiments. Compare with plasma/charging model distributions for natural lunar surfaces, especially 2025 cavity-charging and VUV+plasma results.

**Goal:** quantify how far engineering demonstrations are from natural release conditions.

## Decisive experiment

Deploy a long-duration lunar surface station with synchronized:

- stereoscopic high-speed dust tracking,
- laser-sheet or holographic particle imaging,
- multiple-height dust counters,
- electric-field and surface-potential sensing,
- electron/ion plasma diagnostics,
- UV/visible calibrated photometry and polarization,
- micrometeoroid/impact monitoring,
- substrate vibration and temperature monitoring,
- a mechanically isolated controlled shadow generator,
- controlled UV illumination for intervention trials.

The primary endpoint should be the change in upward grain flux after randomized local illumination transitions, with mechanical/thermal sham controls.

## Defeat conditions

### Defeat condition A — near-surface natural electrostatic transport is operationally negligible

If a properly powered, long-duration surface experiment observes no reproducible increase in grain launch or trajectory deflection under natural or controlled charging transitions, despite measured local fields spanning the range predicted to mobilize representative grains, then the book’s Class 2 claim of meaningful natural near-surface electrostatic transport should be downgraded sharply.

A mechanism demonstrated only in special laboratory contacts would remain real but environmentally minor.

### Defeat condition B — the classic high-altitude interpretation returns

If a future surface-to-orbit experiment measures a repeatable grain population whose observed size/charge/velocity distribution, propagated through lunar fields, produces the Apollo optical brightness at kilometer-scale altitudes while also fitting LADEE/LRO upper limits, then the manuscript’s contraction thesis is too skeptical. The classic interpretation should be rehabilitated to the degree supported by the quantitative match.

### Defeat condition C — impact ejecta explains essentially all historical optical brightness

If forward modeling of the measured LADEE impact-ejecta population plus known optical backgrounds reproduces Surveyor and Apollo brightness within uncertainties, the electrostatic interpretation of the historical glow loses most of its evidentiary motivation even if near-surface charging still moves grains.

### Defeat condition D — original optical observations fail forensic reconstruction

If original Surveyor/Apollo data show calibration, stray-light, window-reflection or geometry problems sufficient to explain the reported brightness without an external lunar source, the horizon-glow mystery should be demoted rather than protected by later dust physics.

### Defeat condition E — nanodust bridges the whole gap

If independent optical and in situ nanodust measurements establish a population that both reproduces historical visible glow and shows the predicted electrical dependence, then treating nanodust as merely a separate complication is wrong. It becomes the unifying population.

## Predictions worth registering now

1. If microfield charging dominates natural electrostatic release, controlled centimeter-to-meter shadow boundaries should alter dust flux more strongly than global local-time alone after controlling for plasma state.
2. If impacts dominate all meaningful near-surface dust motion, dust bursts should correlate primarily with impact/seismic/meteoroid indicators and show no additional repeatable response to controlled illumination transitions.
3. If combined UV+plasma “charge pumping” from 2025 laboratory work is environmentally important, mobilization should depend nonlinearly on simultaneous photon and plasma conditions rather than on either variable alone.
4. If cavity size controls charging as 2025 simulations predict, launch events should cluster by measurable surface microgeometry and show a scale dependence relative to sheath conditions.
5. If natural electrostatic transport is mostly a geological sorting mechanism rather than a cloud source, repeated high-resolution surface maps should show slow size-selective redistribution without a corresponding kilometer-altitude enhancement.
