# Decisive Experiment Appendix

## Working title

**Lunar Near-Surface Dust Causality Station (LNDCS)**

This appendix is deliberately written as a preregistration skeleton rather than a mission sales pitch.

## Primary question

Does changing local illumination and electric-field structure on undisturbed lunar regolith cause a reproducible change in natural near-surface dust launch flux?

## Primary hypothesis

After controlling for vibration, temperature, impact activity and ambient plasma variation, creation of a sharp local shadow boundary will change the upward flux of 0.5–10 μm grains through 10 cm altitude within a specified post-intervention window.

Direction and magnitude should be supplied by competing charging models before data collection.

## Primary endpoint

Difference in upward grain crossings per square meter per second through a virtual 10 cm plane above the regolith during:

- 0–5 seconds,
- 5–60 seconds,
- 1–10 minutes

after randomized shadow-on transitions compared with matched sham transitions.

## Secondary endpoints

- grain radius distribution,
- reconstructed launch velocity,
- maximum observed height,
- lateral displacement,
- estimated charge-to-mass ratio,
- electric-field transient,
- surface-potential change,
- optical scattering signal,
- plasma electron/ion distribution,
- dependence on solar elevation,
- dependence on solar-wind state,
- dependence on surface microgeometry,
- occurrence during magnetotail passage,
- occurrence during meteoroid-stream periods.

## Instrument stack

### Dust trajectories

Three or more synchronized cameras with overlapping fields of view reconstruct three-dimensional tracks. A thin laser or structured-light sheet can increase sensitivity to small grains. Imaging geometry must avoid heating the target and must be calibrated with known particles before flight.

### Independent dust counters

Impact or induction sensors at several heights provide flux measurements not dependent on optical reconstruction.

### Electric field / potential

Multiple miniature sensors at different heights and lateral distances, with full electrostatic modeling of sensor perturbation. No single probe should be treated as the undisturbed surface field.

### Plasma

Electron and ion density/energy measurements sufficient to identify major changes in charging currents.

### Optical glow

Calibrated horizon photometry at multiple wavelengths and polarization where practical. The goal is to connect measured grain populations to actual radiance rather than infer density from brightness alone.

### Impact discrimination

Seismic and optical-impact monitoring, plus high-speed event clustering, to identify dust bursts associated with micrometeoroid strikes.

### Mechanical controls

Accelerometers on all actuated structures and the instrument platform. Shadow-maker motion without illumination change serves as a sham condition.

## Intervention design

A suspended shadow blade moves without contacting the regolith. Trials are randomized among:

1. true shadow onset,
2. true shadow removal,
3. mechanical sham movement with no target illumination change,
4. no-motion control,
5. controlled UV illumination of a dark patch,
6. visible/thermal control illumination with matched heating but reduced photoelectric effectiveness.

Trial identity should be hidden from first-pass event classification where feasible.

## Site plan

The primary science patch should be outside the immediate landing-plume disturbance zone and characterized before intervention. A second disturbed patch provides an engineering comparison. High-resolution topography and grain-size mapping should be performed before operations.

## Duration

Minimum useful duration: several lunar day/night transitions.

Preferred duration: one Earth year or more, capturing multiple solar-wind states, magnetotail passages and meteoroid-source variations.

## Predefined positive result

A positive natural electrostatic-mobilization result requires all of the following:

1. statistically and physically significant change in dust flux after electrical/illumination intervention,
2. replication across multiple cycles,
3. effect absent or materially weaker in mechanical/thermal sham conditions,
4. measured field/potential change in the direction predicted by at least one preregistered model,
5. grain trajectories physically consistent with the measured force regime,
6. no coincident impact burst sufficient to explain the event.

A single spectacular grain is not a positive result.

## Predefined null

If the station samples field/potential conditions spanning the preregistered launch regime for a sufficiently powered number of trials and no excess flux appears above the defined effect size, the tested electrostatic detachment model is rejected for that site/material regime.

Do not rescue it by moving the grain-size range, field threshold or time window after looking at the data unless the revised model is explicitly labeled post hoc and tested on a later independent dataset.

## Bridge to historical observations

Any measured population is fed forward through realistic optical scattering models into:

- Surveyor 5/6/7 camera geometry,
- Apollo 15–17 orbital viewing geometry,
- LADEE/LDEX sensitivity,
- LADEE/UVS sensitivity,
- LRO optical constraints.

A near-surface positive result does **not** count as explanation of Apollo glow unless this cross-instrument forward model closes quantitatively.

## Why this experiment is decisive

The surviving controversy is no longer primarily about whether charge exists or whether dust can move in a laboratory. It is about natural flux and vertical transfer on the actual Moon.

This station measures both directly at the scale where modern orbital instruments leave the largest gap.
