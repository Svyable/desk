# Source Log

Checked or refreshed September 11, 2026 unless otherwise noted. Links are primary, official, peer-reviewed, or serious technical sources where available.

## Surveyor horizon glow / early electrostatic interpretation

- J. J. Rennilson & D. R. Criswell, **Surveyor observations of lunar horizon-glow** (1973), NASA Technical Reports Server. Reports Surveyor 5, 6 and 7 post-sunset horizon brightness and interprets it as forward scattering by a temporary near-surface dust cloud near sunlight/shadow boundaries. The paper’s electrostatic mechanism is an interpretation, not a direct electric-field measurement: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19730051136
- D. R. Criswell, **Lunar dust motion** (1972), NASA Technical Reports Server. Early quantitative treatment of Surveyor 7 glow and electrostatic levitation near the surface: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19730035060

## Apollo high-altitude glow

- H. A. Zook & J. E. McCoy, **Large scale lunar horizon glow and a high altitude lunar dust exosphere**, *Geophysical Research Letters* 18 (1991), 2117–2120. Models Apollo 17 crew sketches with submicron grains and an approximately 5–20 km scale height; this is a model fit to optical observations, not direct particle counting: https://doi.org/10.1029/91GL02235
- NASA Technical Reports Server record for the same Zook & McCoy paper: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19920030340

## LADEE / direct dust environment

- M. Horányi et al., **A permanent, asymmetric dust cloud around the Moon**, *Nature* 522 (2015), 324–326. Direct LDEX evidence for a persistent lunar ejecta cloud sustained by interplanetary micrometeoroid impacts: https://doi.org/10.1038/nature14479
- J. R. Szalay & M. Horányi, **The search for electrostatically lofted grains above the Moon with the Lunar Dust Experiment**, *Geophysical Research Letters* 42 (2015), 5141–5146. Dedicated LDEX search; no evidence for the putative ~0.1 μm electrostatic population within the tested 3–250 km altitude range and instrument model: https://doi.org/10.1002/2015GL064324
- J. R. Szalay & M. Horányi, **Annual variation and synodic modulation of the sporadic meteoroid flux to the Moon**, *Geophysical Research Letters* (2015). Connects the observed ejecta cloud’s local-time structure to sporadic meteoroid source geometry: https://doi.org/10.1002/2015GL066908
- D. Janches et al., **Constraining the Ratio of Micrometeoroids From Short- and Long-Period Comets at 1 AU From LADEE Observations of the Lunar Dust Cloud**, *Geophysical Research Letters* (2018): https://doi.org/10.1002/2017GL076065
- NASA, **What is LADEE, the Lunar Atmosphere and Dust Environment Explorer?** Official mission summary and result boundary: LADEE confirmed an impact-generated dust cloud but did not find an effect corresponding to the human-eye-visible Apollo glow: https://www.nasa.gov/general/what-is-ladee-the-lunar-atmosphere-and-dust-environment-explorer/
- NASA Science, **LADEE Outcome**: https://science.nasa.gov/mission/ladee/outcome/

## Nanodust / optical constraints

- D. H. Wooden et al., **Evidence for a dynamic nanodust cloud enveloping the Moon**, *Nature Geoscience* 9 (2016), 665–668. UVS-based evidence for a tenuous nanodust population; treat separately from LDEX’s larger impact ejecta and the classic high-altitude electrostatic population: https://doi.org/10.1038/ngeo2779
- M. K. Barker, **Searching for Lunar Horizon Glow With the Lunar Orbiter Laser Altimeter**, *Journal of Geophysical Research: Planets* (2019). Two-year LRO campaign searching for forward-scattered sunlight from exospheric dust, including meteor-stream periods: https://doi.org/10.1029/2019JE006020
- H. Sharma et al., **Constraining Low-Altitude Lunar Dust Using the LADEE-UVS Data**, *Journal of Geophysical Research: Planets* (2021): https://doi.org/10.1029/2021JE006935

## Laboratory charging and transport

- A. Champlain et al., **Lunar dust simulant charging and transport under UV irradiation in vacuum: Experiments and numerical modeling**, *Journal of Geophysical Research: Space Physics* 121 (2016), 103–116. Shows laboratory mobilization and argues microscopic surface irregularities can amplify fields enough to assist ejection; explicitly focused on near-surface conditions below LADEE’s high-altitude search: https://doi.org/10.1002/2015JA021738
- X. Wang et al. (2007), **A laboratory model of the lunar surface potential near boundaries between sunlit and shadowed regions**, *Geophysical Research Letters*. Demonstrates that differential photoelectron charging at light-shadow boundaries can produce fields much larger than the few-V/m large-scale sheath field; laboratory evidence for a mechanism, not a natural lunar flux measurement: https://doi.org/10.1029/2007GL030766
- Kuznetsov et al. (2025), **Experimental Simulation of Electrostatic Activation of Lunar Regolith Dust at the Light–Shadow Boundary**, *Astronomy Reports* 69(S1), S21–S27. Vacuum-chamber simulation using lunar-regolith-like dust at a light-shadow boundary; relevant to Ch 19’s controlled-shadow proposal, but still a laboratory result rather than an in-situ Moon measurement: https://www.pleiades.online/cgi-perl/search.pl?name=astengs&number=1&page=S21&type=abstract&year=25
- Richmond et al., **Enhanced Charging and Mobilization of Photoemitting Dust Particles in a Low-Density Plasma**, *Geophysical Research Letters* (2025). Laboratory experiments find stronger dust mobilization under combined vacuum-ultraviolet illumination and plasma than under either forcing alone; proposed “plasma charge pumping” is a laboratory mechanism, not yet a measured natural lunar flux: https://doi.org/10.1029/2024GL113715
- J. Nakazono & Y. Miyake, **Size-Dependent Surface Charging of Lunar Cavities Exposed to the Solar Wind**, *Journal of Geophysical Research: Space Physics* 130 (2025), e2024JA033490. PIC simulations show cavity size changes potential structure; useful for microfield hypotheses, but simulation results are not direct lunar grain-motion observations: https://doi.org/10.1029/2024JA033490

## Current lunar dust engineering and upcoming field measurement

- NASA, **Electrodynamic Dust Shield Heading to Moon on Firefly Lander** (January 8, 2025). Describes the EDS surface-demonstration plan and engineering rationale: https://www.nasa.gov/missions/artemis/clps/electrodynamic-dust-shield-heading-to-moon-on-firefly-lander/
- NASA, **NASA’s Dust Shield Successfully Repels Lunar Regolith on Moon** (March 27, 2025). Reports successful EDS removal of regolith from glass and thermal-radiator surfaces during Firefly Aerospace’s Blue Ghost Mission 1. This demonstrates engineered electrostatic dust transport/mitigation on the Moon, not the magnitude of natural electrostatic lofting: https://www.nasa.gov/image-article/nasas-dust-shield-successfully-repels-lunar-regolith-on-moon/
- NASA Science, **NASA Selects 2 Instruments for Artemis IV Lunar Surface Science** (2025). DUSTER (DUst and plaSma environmenT survEyoR) is a rover-mounted investigation selected to characterize natural and human-disturbed lunar dust/plasma. Its Electrostatic Dust Analyzer is intended to measure charge, velocity, size and flux of lofted particles; RESOLVE will characterize electron density. This is the closest selected mission architecture to the book’s proposed near-surface observational gap, but NASA’s public description does not include the controlled light/shadow intervention proposed in Ch 19: https://science.nasa.gov/missions/artemis/artemis-4/nasa-selects-2-instruments-for-artemis-iv-lunar-surface-science/
- NASA TechPort, **Electrostatic Dust Lofting via Photoionization Under Artificial Lunar Gravity** (updated April 30, 2026). Completed Flight Opportunities project using UV illumination, lunar-regolith simulant and high-speed imaging to test electrostatic repulsion under reduced gravity; explicitly intended to validate dust-lofting models that cannot be fully tested at one-g: https://techport.nasa.gov/projects/106723
- NASA NTRS, **Electrostatic Dust Lofting (EDL) Suborbital Flight Payload Overview** (2025). Technical overview of the UV/vacuum/high-speed-imaging reduced-gravity payload: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20250004477
- NASA, **Lunar Surface Technology** (updated 2026). Current agency overview of dust mitigation and lunar-surface technology, including EDS and plume-surface imaging: https://www.nasa.gov/lunar-surface-technology/
- NASA Johnson Space Center, **Lunar Development and Test Facility Prepares Artemis Hardware for Moon** (August 7, 2026). Current hardware-testing context and electrostatic-dust mitigation work: https://www.nasa.gov/centers-and-facilities/johnson/nasas-lunar-development-and-test-facility-prepares-artemis-hardware-for-moon/

## Claim boundaries

- “Surveyor saw levitating dust” is shorthand and should not be used as a literal observational statement. Surveyor recorded horizon brightness; dust and electrostatic lofting were the interpretation.
- “Apollo observed a high-altitude dust cloud” similarly compresses an optical observation and later scattering model into one sentence. Preserve the distinction.
- “LADEE disproved electrostatic lunar dust” is too broad. It strongly constrained a particular high-altitude grain population and found no human-eye-visible glow, while near-surface electrical transport remained outside the strongest LDEX altitude constraint.
- “LADEE confirmed Apollo” is also too broad. It found an impact-generated dust cloud and later UVS analysis supported nanodust, neither automatically equivalent to the classic Apollo interpretation.
- NASA’s 2025 EDS success proves an engineered electric field can remove lunar regolith from selected surfaces. It is not evidence that natural lunar fields produce the same flux.
- DUSTER is selected to measure the near-surface dust/plasma environment; selection is not a scientific result, and its observational measurements should not be described in advance as a causal test of electrostatic launch.
- Reduced-gravity UV-lofting experiments validate portions of the charging/transport model chain. They do not substitute for an in-situ intervention on undisturbed lunar regolith.
