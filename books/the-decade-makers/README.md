*The Most Important Institution of Every American Decade—and the Stories That Built 250 Years*

The working thesis is that America is easier to understand when its history is organized around the institutions that solved each era’s hardest coordination problem. The winner need not be a government agency and need not be morally admirable. It can be a temporary convention, an army, a railroad, a labor organization, a bank, a regulator, a corporation, a software platform, or an AI laboratory. The test is whether other institutions had to reorganize around it.

Each chapter names one winner, presents the human story behind its rise, tests the strongest runner-up, and asks who the winning institution empowered, excluded, disciplined, enriched, impoverished, or made dependent on it. The 2020s choice is explicitly provisional and is dated to the manuscript’s current evidence snapshot.
