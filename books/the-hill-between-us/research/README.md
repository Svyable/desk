# Research Brief — The Hill Between Us

## Purpose

This research trail supports a book that treats telepathy as an unestablished information-transfer hypothesis. The manuscript begins with reports from nonspeaking spelling communities, including reports of a shared mental place called the Hill, but never treats the existence of a report as evidence that the proposed external mechanism occurred.

The research task is to keep four claims separate:

1. **Report** — somebody says an experience occurred.
2. **Authorship / validated behavior** — the person demonstrated causal control over the response under conditions capable of distinguishing partner influence.
3. **Transfer** — novel target-specific information reached the receiver beyond the tested ordinary channels.
4. **Mechanism** — a source, encoding process, carrier or coupling process, receiver/transducer, bandwidth, noise profile, and perturbation explain the transfer.

Evidence does not automatically climb from one level to the next.

## Evidence hierarchy

Prefer, in order:

1. Original controlled studies, systematic reviews, and preregistered empirical work.
2. Government laboratories and authoritative technical references for measurement claims.
3. Professional clinical bodies for scope-of-practice, AAC, and evidence-position claims.
4. Methodological reviews and journal research-integrity guidance for experimental design.
5. First-person, family, practitioner, podcast, or media sources only to document what was reported, never as sole support for an external telepathy mechanism.

The canonical source metadata are in [source-ledger.csv](source-ledger.csv).

## Claim rules

- Do not state or imply that telepathy has been scientifically demonstrated in nonspeaking autistic people.
- Do not infer intelligence, language, or personhood from the success or failure of a disputed communication method.
- Do not use the 2026 empty systematic-review result as proof that every RPM/S2C output is facilitator-authored; it establishes a validation gap under the review's criteria.
- Do not use the 2020 eye-tracking study as a blinded message-passing authorship demonstration; it supplies evidence about gaze, timing, and agency in nine experienced participants.
- Treat Facilitated Communication, RPM, S2C, independent typing, eye gaze, and other AAC access configurations as empirically distinguishable rather than interchangeable labels.
- Separate synchrony from semantic transfer. Temporal alignment between bodies or brains can arise through reciprocal interaction, shared stimuli, common task structure, movement, or other common causes.
- A measurable brain field is not a demonstrated broadcast channel. State the measurement scale, distance, noise, encoding, and receiver problem.
- Animal magnetoreception or molecular magnetosensitivity demonstrates biological possibility of magnetic sensing in some systems, not human mind-to-mind communication.
- Quantum mechanics is not an explanatory placeholder. Any quantum proposal must identify degrees of freedom, coupling, relevant timescales, and a prediction that differs from classical alternatives.
- Experimental designs should make null and positive results interpretable before data collection. Report misses, exclusions, attrition, and sender-absent controls.

## Chapter 1 — The Hill

**Sources:** `hill-ttt-2025`, `hill-koray-2026`, `hill-kane-2026`.

These sources document that the term *the Hill* and associated telepathy/spiritual interpretations are circulating in The Telepathy Tapes community. They are report-layer sources, not independent evidence for a shared external realm or target-specific transfer.

**Falsification note:** The chapter's external claim remains deliberately unendorsed. Shared terminology can arise through culture, repeated storytelling, community contact, or media exposure. A scientifically stronger Hill claim requires prospectively concealed novel information that was not already present in the shared vocabulary.

## Chapter 2 — A Mind Without a Mouth

**Sources:** `asha-aac`, `asha-autism`.

ASHA's AAC and autism materials support the distinction between speech output, language, communication access, and individualized multimodal supports. They support a zero-exclusion orientation toward AAC rather than any claim of universally preserved cognition.

**Falsification note:** Limited speech does not determine a person's language profile, but neither does the possibility of hidden competence justify presuming any specific unmeasured ability. Individual assessment remains necessary.

## Chapter 3 — A Finger, a Board, a Second Person

**Sources:** `asha-rpm-2018`, `asha-fc-2018`, `jaswal-2020`.

The professional position statements establish why partner knowledge and prompt dependency matter. Jaswal et al. supplies a contrasting empirical result: in nine experienced users, gaze often preceded pointing in a way the authors interpreted as agency.

**Falsification note:** Visible non-contact between partner and speller does not by itself establish semantic independence. Conversely, the presence of partner movement does not prove partner authorship. Knowledge-separation and temporal-control experiments are needed.

## Chapter 4 — The Authorship Problem

**Sources:** `schlosser-2026`, `schlosser-fc-2014`, `jaswal-2020`, `asha-rpm-2018`.

The 2026 systematic review found no studies meeting its preregistered criteria for controlled empirical demonstration of authorship in RPM/S2C and variants. Earlier FC reviews found strong facilitator-control evidence in that method. The eye-tracking study asks a different question and should remain visible rather than being collapsed into either conclusion.

**Falsification note:** A participant-only knowledge condition in which a target-blind support person cannot know the answer can directly strengthen an individual authorship inference. A pattern that follows support-person-only knowledge weakens it.

## Chapter 5 — What Telepathy Would Have to Mean

**Sources:** `timme-2018`, `brainnet-2019`.

Information theory supplies the distinction between state change and target-specific information. BrainNet provides a reality check: experimentally engineered brain-to-brain transfer used an explicit encoder, electronic communication path, and receiver stimulation.

**Falsification note:** A telepathy claim becomes unnecessary if target information is available through a known sensory, behavioral, technological, procedural, or inferential route. A surviving anomaly should first be described operationally rather than metaphysically.

## Chapter 6 — The Channels We Forget

**Sources:** `burgess-1998`, `asha-rpm-2018`, `asha-aac`.

The chapter's point is that human interaction contains subtle visual, auditory, tactile, proprioceptive, timing, and prompting information. Burgess et al. demonstrates how facilitator-only information can enter sincere output without conscious intention in an experimental analogue.

**Falsification note:** An ordinary-channel account weakens when every person and device available to the receiver is genuinely target-blind and sender-absent/apparatus-only controls remain at chance.

## Chapter 7 — Eyes Before Fingers

**Sources:** `jaswal-2020`.

The study reported that participants frequently fixated letters before pointing and that timing showed features interpreted as planning and agency. The sample was small and the task was not a blinded target-knowledge dissociation.

**Falsification note:** Anticipatory gaze should be tested when participant and partner knowledge are experimentally separated. If partner-only knowledge predicts selections despite participant gaze patterns, gaze alone is insufficient to establish semantic authorship.

## Chapter 8 — The Motor Bottleneck

**Sources:** `asha-aac`, `asha-autism`, `jaswal-2020`.

AAC guidance supports individualized access and the possibility that motor or speech constraints can coexist with stronger receptive or language abilities. The eye-tracking work provides an example of using motor timing rather than speech output to interrogate control.

**Falsification note:** The motor-bottleneck interpretation should predict performance changes when motor supports change while target knowledge remains blinded. A collapse only when the support person loses target knowledge is not a motor-only pattern.

## Chapter 9 — Two Nervous Systems in One Room

**Sources:** `gordon-bartsch-2026`, `hakim-2023`.

Interpersonal physiological synchrony and inter-brain coupling are measurable research topics. Their existence does not identify a direct person-to-person information channel.

**Falsification note:** Pseudo-pairs, shared-stimulus controls, and sender-absent trials should estimate how much coupling appears without reciprocal target transfer. Target identity must be decodable above those controls before synchrony can support a content claim.

## Chapter 10 — Bodies That Keep Time Together

**Sources:** `gordon-bartsch-2026`.

The review documents substantial research on temporal coordination of physiology while emphasizing heterogeneity and ambiguity of psychological meaning.

**Falsification note:** If breathing, heart rate, or electrodermal synchrony rises during interaction but does not distinguish concealed targets, it may index shared state or engagement rather than semantic transfer.

## Chapter 11 — The Brain That Predicts Another Brain

**Sources:** `hakim-2023`, `berni-2026`.

Hyperscanning studies measure inter-brain relationships during interaction, but methodological reviews emphasize diverse metrics and the need to distinguish reciprocal coupling from common input and task structure.

**Falsification note:** Inter-brain effects that disappear when shared sensory timing is modeled, or appear equally in pseudo-pairs exposed to the same stimulus sequence, cannot by themselves support direct brain-to-brain transfer.

## Chapter 12 — The Synchrony Trap

**Sources:** `gordon-bartsch-2026`, `hakim-2023`, `berni-2026`.

These reviews support the manuscript's recurring warning: synchrony is a statistical relationship whose psychological meaning depends on design and controls.

**Falsification note:** A synchrony-based telepathy mechanism should contain target-specific information and should change under perturbations predicted by the proposed coupling. Correlation without target information is not enough.

## Chapter 13 — The Fields We Actually Emit

**Sources:** `nimh-meg`, `buzsaki-2012`, `anastassiou-2011`.

NIMH documents MEG detection of brain-generated magnetic fields outside the head at approximately 10^-14 tesla using specialized SQUID instrumentation. The electrophysiology sources describe how endogenous neural currents create extracellular fields and how local ephaptic effects can occur in tissue.

**Falsification note:** Local field effects inside neural tissue cannot be scaled directly to cross-person communication. A candidate field must contain target information at the receiver's location and survive a measured distance/noise comparison.

## Chapter 14 — The Electromagnetic Problem

**Sources:** `nimh-meg`, `buzsaki-2012`, `brainnet-2019`.

The chapter separates field existence from strength, coding, and biological reception. Engineered BCI transfer illustrates how much decoding and hardware can be required even when sensors have direct access to the sender.

**Falsification note:** If a proposed external field is strongly attenuated or experimentally blocked while behavioral transfer remains unchanged, that specific carrier model weakens. Artificial reproduction of the field should affect a real receptor in the predicted direction.

## Chapter 15 — The Quantum Temptation

**Sources:** `tegmark-2000`, `timme-2018`.

Tegmark supplies a quantitative classical/decoherence challenge to some long-lived neural quantum-coherence proposals. Information theory keeps the behavioral demand independent of the metaphysical label.

**Falsification note:** A quantum telepathy proposal must predict an outcome different from classical alternatives and identify a perturbation that selectively affects the proposed quantum process. The word *quantum* without such a prediction does no explanatory work.

## Chapter 16 — A Place That Is Not a Place

**Sources:** `hill-ttt-2025`, `hill-koray-2026`, `hill-kane-2026`.

The source trail here documents recurring Hill language only. The manuscript treats shared mental geography as compatible with several ordinary cognitive and social explanations unless prospective concealed information demonstrates more.

**Falsification note:** A stable shared vocabulary, media exposure, and post-experience discussion can explain convergence without transfer. Randomly injected novel features generated after isolation are the stronger test.

## Chapter 17 — Dreams, Maps, and Shared Worlds

**Sources:** `hill-ttt-2025`, `hill-koray-2026`.

The book does not cite Hill testimony as proof of shared dreaming. It uses the reports as the phenomenon to be operationalized.

**Falsification note:** Similar free reports are weak evidence when target space is unconstrained. Blinded ranking against matched decoys and prospectively generated landmarks reduce interpretive flexibility.

## Chapter 18 — The Receiver Hypothesis

**Sources:** `wang-2019`, `foley-2011`, `matthews-2024`, `anastassiou-2011`.

These studies demonstrate, respectively, a reported human EEG response to certain geomagnetic rotations, molecular magnetosensitivity of human CRY2 in a fly assay, weak-signal enhancement through stochastic resonance in some sensory systems, and local ephaptic effects. None establishes a human telepathy receptor.

**Falsification note:** A receiver hypothesis should yield a threshold, tuning curve, adaptation profile, or artificial-stimulus response. Failure to detect a substantially amplified controlled version of the candidate signal is evidence against that receptor model.

## Chapter 19 — The Experiment That Could Change Everything

**Sources:** `registered-reports-2021`, `registered-reports-guidelines`, `timme-2018`, `brainnet-2019`.

The chapter's one-bit-first design follows the logic of outcome-neutral preregistration and measurable information transfer. BrainNet shows that direct transfer is experimentally tractable when the pathway is explicit.

**Falsification note:** The primary result should be fixed before data collection. Chance performance across adequately sensitive replications weakens the tested transfer claim under those conditions; an effect surviving sender-absent controls requires an apparatus/leakage explanation before a sender mechanism.

## Chapter 20 — Building a Lab Around Dignity

**Sources:** `asha-aac`, `asha-autism`, `registered-reports-guidelines`.

AAC access can be individualized without surrendering target blinding. Research transparency can coexist with privacy and reasonable accommodations.

**Falsification note:** If a control condition destroys a participant's validated ability to communicate even when no telepathy task is involved, that condition cannot cleanly test transfer. Access needs should be distinguished from target knowledge.

## Chapter 21 — If the Signal Survives

**Sources:** `timme-2018`, `nimh-meg`, `wang-2019`, `brainnet-2019`.

A positive concealed-target result would justify mechanism work but not unlimited mind-reading claims. The next steps are information capacity, range, timing, candidate physical measurements, artificial reproduction, and perturbation.

**Falsification note:** A replicated behavioral anomaly can coexist with a failed electromagnetic or magnetic mechanism. Mechanism-specific failures should narrow the theory rather than erase or inflate the behavioral result.

## Chapter 22 — If It Doesn't

**Sources:** `asha-aac`, `schlosser-2026`, `gordon-bartsch-2026`.

A null telepathy result has no bearing on human worth and does not by itself answer every AAC authorship question. It can still motivate useful work on communication access, predictive coordination, and physiological synchrony.

**Falsification note:** The null interpretation is only as strong as the study's ability to detect an effect under conditions that preserve valid response access. Floor effects, failed communication access, or protocol noncompliance can make a null uninformative.

## Chapter 23 — The Clever Hans Problem

**Sources:** `burgess-1998`, `schlosser-fc-2014`, `asha-fc-2018`, `jaswal-2020`.

Burgess et al. demonstrates an ideomotor route by which sincere facilitators can produce information available to themselves while attributing output elsewhere. The chapter balances this with evidence that individual motor/gaze dynamics can support an agency interpretation in specific users.

**Falsification note:** A cueing account weakens when the support person cannot know the target, target information is absent from measured partner behavior, and the participant succeeds on participant-only knowledge trials across partners and interfaces.

## Chapter 24 — The Information Budget

**Sources:** `timme-2018`, `brainnet-2019`.

Information theory supplies the chapter's target-entropy and capacity logic. Meaningful context can allow a small transmitted variable to drive rich inference, so subjective richness cannot substitute for measured bits.

**Falsification note:** A proposed carrier must contain enough target-discriminating information to support the receiver's observed performance. Increasing target entropy should reveal a capacity/error curve if a stable channel exists.

## Chapter 25 — A Biology of Weak Signals

**Sources:** `matthews-2024`, `anastassiou-2011`, `buzsaki-2012`.

Stochastic resonance and local ephaptic coupling show that weak physical signals can matter in biological systems under specific conditions. They do not establish arbitrary sensitivity to another person's brain activity.

**Falsification note:** Borrowing a known weak-signal mechanism commits the hypothesis to its characteristic dependencies: thresholds, tuning, noise curves, adaptation, or spatial constraints. If those dependencies are absent, the analogy weakens.

## Chapter 26 — Magnetoreception Without Magic

**Sources:** `wang-2019`, `foley-2011`, `nimh-meg`.

Wang et al. reported EEG alpha responses to particular Earth-strength field rotations; Foley et al. showed human CRY2 can support magnetosensitivity in a Drosophila assay. NIMH's MEG reference anchors the much smaller scale of brain-generated magnetic signals measured outside the head.

**Falsification note:** Human magnetic sensitivity, if replicated, still needs a threshold and coding bridge to telepathy. A natural sender signal far below measured receptor sensitivity, or failure of stronger artificial candidate patterns to affect receivers, weakens direct magnetic transfer.

## Chapter 27 — Distance Is a Mechanism

**Sources:** `nimh-meg`, `brainnet-2019`, `timme-2018`.

The chapter treats range as a discriminating variable rather than a storytelling detail. Engineered BrainNet is explicitly mediated by electronic infrastructure; a natural local-field hypothesis should instead show a physical scaling relationship.

**Falsification note:** If behavior is invariant across distances at which a measured candidate carrier changes drastically, that carrier is a poor explanation unless another propagation process is specified and tested. Digital isolation must accompany geographic separation.

## Chapter 28 — The Adversarial Lab

**Sources:** `registered-reports-2021`, `registered-reports-guidelines`, `schlosser-2026`, `asha-aac`.

Registered Reports provide a concrete model for reviewing methods and analyses before outcomes are known. The authorship literature and AAC guidance motivate separating valid response access from target knowledge.

**Falsification note:** Both believers and skeptics should state update conditions before data collection. A protocol is not adversarially useful if either side can redefine success after seeing the result.

## Chapter 29 — The Hill Challenge

**Sources:** `hill-ttt-2025`, `hill-koray-2026`, `hill-kane-2026`, `registered-reports-guidelines`, `timme-2018`.

The Hill sources define the report to be tested; they do not support the test outcome. The methodological sources support preregistration, matched alternatives, information accounting, and outcome-neutral publication.

**Falsification note:** The central test is prospective: generate a novel landmark after isolation, keep the receiver site target-blind, lock a free report before forced choice, compare against matched decoys, include sender-absent trials, and replicate independently. Chance performance weakens reliable concealed-landmark transfer under those conditions.

## Chapter 30 — The First Honest Unknown

**Sources:** `schlosser-2026`, `jaswal-2020`, `gordon-bartsch-2026`, `nimh-meg`, `registered-reports-2021`.

The closing chapter deliberately preserves a bounded uncertainty. Current evidence can justify claims about reports, specific motor/gaze findings, synchrony, weak fields, and methodological gaps without promoting those findings into demonstrated telepathy.

**Falsification note:** The book's thesis is methodological rather than paranormal: extraordinary reports should be made vulnerable to experiments that can distinguish authorship, access, transfer, and mechanism. A future replicated concealed-target effect would require the evidentiary boundary to move; repeated well-powered null results would narrow it.

## Research status

This is a working research trail, not a claim that every adjacent literature has been exhausted. Before release, fact-check any new quantitative statement against the source of record, preserve corrections and version dates for 2026 papers, and add new sources to the canonical ledger rather than inserting uncataloged citations into prose.

The manuscript should remain readable without footnote clutter, but the factual burden is not optional. The stranger the proposed mechanism, the more precise the boundary between measured fact, plausible analogy, report, and speculation must become.
