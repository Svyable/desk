# Back Matter — A Protocol for the Impossible

## Evidence note

This book does not present telepathy as an established human ability.

It begins from reports made in and around communities of nonspeaking people who use spelling-based communication, including descriptions of a shared mental location often called “the Hill,” and asks what would have to be true for target-specific information to move between people without a known sensory, behavioral, technological, or inferential route.

The distinction between **documenting a claim** and **supporting a claim** is essential.

A podcast episode, interview, family account, or independently authored first-person report can document what somebody says they experienced. It does not by itself establish that the proposed external mechanism occurred. Scientific evidence enters when an observation is produced under conditions that can distinguish competing explanations.

The same rule applies to skepticism. Evidence that facilitator influence has occurred in Facilitated Communication does not prove that every output produced through every superficially similar interface is facilitator-authored. Evidence of anticipatory gaze in a small sample of experienced letterboard users does not establish independent authorship for every user, and it does not establish telepathy. Each claim needs the experiment that belongs to it.

The research notes accompanying this manuscript therefore separate four levels whenever possible:

1. **Report:** what a participant, family, practitioner, or media source says occurred.
2. **Validated behavior:** what a person can demonstrate under controlled conditions, including independent authorship.
3. **Transfer:** whether novel target-specific information reaches a receiver beyond known channels.
4. **Mechanism:** the source, encoding, carrier or coupling process, receiver/transducer, bandwidth, noise constraints, and perturbations that explain the transfer.

Evidence cannot be promoted automatically from one level to the next.

## The one-bit protocol

If the strongest version of the claim is true, the cleanest first experiment does not need language.

A sender and receiver enter separate rooms. After separation, software randomly selects one of two targets—for example, a left-facing or right-facing arrow. Only the sender sees the target. Nobody with the receiver knows it. The receiver records a choice through a communication method whose authorship has already been independently established for that person.

A serious first protocol should include:

- target generation after isolation;
- concealed allocation so neither the receiver nor anyone interacting with the receiver knows the answer;
- sender-absent trials that look identical to the receiver;
- prespecified trial count, stopping rules, exclusion rules, and primary analysis;
- automatic timestamping of target generation, sender exposure, and receiver response;
- no live correctness feedback during the session;
- an inventory and audit of phones, wearables, radios, networked devices, cameras, displays, and shared equipment;
- environmental control or monitoring appropriate to the proposed channel;
- all attempted trials retained in the analysis unless a preregistered exclusion criterion is met;
- independent replication.

One reproducible bit would be enough to justify a research program.

It would not establish unlimited “mind reading.” It would establish a constrained information-transfer anomaly.

## The authorship gate

Telepathy testing with spelling output should not be used to validate the authorship of the spelling itself.

The receiver’s output channel needs an independent authorship test first. A useful design separates participant knowledge from communication-partner knowledge:

- participant sees target, partner does not;
- partner sees target, participant does not;
- both see the same target;
- neither sees a constrained target.

The order is randomized and the person scoring the response is blind. If a board is held or moved, eye, hand, board, prompt, and timing data can be recorded to identify how the dyad controls the interface.

A participant who succeeds when only they know the target has evidence of independent semantic control under those conditions. A pattern that tracks partner-only knowledge indicates partner influence. A collapse specific to participant-only trials identifies a different problem than generalized fatigue, anxiety, or motor difficulty.

A failed authorship test is not an intelligence test.

It is a result about an output method under stated conditions.

## The Hill landmark protocol

The Hill can be tested without deciding whether it is a spiritual realm, dream landscape, shared cognitive schema, social mythology, or something else.

Two participants who report meeting there first provide private baseline maps or descriptions over multiple sessions. Publicly circulating Hill features are coded in advance so they cannot later be mistaken for novel convergence.

For a transfer trial:

1. Sender and receiver are isolated.
2. Software selects a novel landmark from a large target bank only after isolation.
3. The landmark is designed to have scorable features and low overlap with common Hill imagery—for example, a transparent blue arch containing three orange spheres.
4. Only the sender receives the target.
5. The sender attends to or places the landmark within a standardized Hill task.
6. The receiver produces a report before any contact with the sender or knowledgeable researcher.
7. Independent judges, blind to the correct target, rank the receiver report against the true landmark and matched decoys.
8. Sender-absent, delayed, and sham-target trials are interleaved.
9. All reports are timestamped and retained.

If the true target is ranked above decoys at a preregistered rate across many trials and independent laboratories, the next question is mechanism.

If it is not, the result does not erase the Hill as a subjective, cultural, dreamlike, or community experience.

## A mechanism worksheet

Any proposed physiobiological pathway should be forced through the same questions.

**Source:** What changes in the sender when target A replaces target B?

**Encoding:** Which measurable difference carries target identity rather than merely arousal or timing?

**Carrier or coupling:** What crosses the physical gap, or what shared process couples the two systems?

**Range:** How should signal strength or performance change with distance?

**Receiver:** What tissue, receptor, or neural process detects the difference?

**Transduction:** How does that detection alter neural activity in the receiver?

**Bandwidth:** How many reliably distinguishable alternatives can the system carry per unit time?

**Noise:** What ordinary environmental or physiological signals compete with it?

**Gating:** Why is the receiver not continuously overwhelmed by every nearby person?

**Perturbation:** What manipulation should weaken or eliminate the effect if the proposed pathway is causal?

**Artificial reproduction:** Can a controlled version of the candidate signal reproduce the receiver effect without the sender?

**Replication:** Can an independent team produce the same dependency pattern?

A candidate mechanism that cannot answer these questions may still be an idea worth exploring. It is not yet an explanation.

## Glossary

**AAC — Augmentative and alternative communication.** Communication methods, strategies, and technologies used to supplement or replace speech, including symbol systems, keyboards, speech-generating devices, eye gaze, switches, signs, and other access methods.

**Authorship.** Causal control over the information-bearing choices that form a message. Authorship is not identical to physical independence; a person may use substantial access support while still controlling semantic content.

**Bandwidth.** The amount of information a channel can carry over time. In this book, bandwidth is used conceptually and, where possible, in information-theoretic terms rather than as a metaphor for intensity.

**Blinding.** Preventing a participant, facilitator, experimenter, scorer, or analyst from knowing information that could consciously or unconsciously influence the result.

**Facilitated Communication (FC).** A method in which a facilitator physically supports or guides a disabled person while the person points or types. Controlled authorship studies historically found facilitator influence, and major professional organizations including ASHA describe FC as discredited.

**Hyperscanning.** Simultaneous measurement of neural activity from two or more people during interaction, using methods such as EEG, fNIRS, or fMRI.

**Inter-brain synchrony.** A statistical temporal relationship between neural signals recorded from different people. It can arise through reciprocal interaction, shared sensory input, common task timing, joint attention, motor mirroring, or other common causes; it is not synonymous with direct information transfer.

**Interpersonal physiological synchrony.** Temporal association between physiological measures from different people, such as heart rate, respiration, or electrodermal activity. Synchrony can be socially meaningful without carrying target-specific semantic content.

**MEG — Magnetoencephalography.** Measurement of weak magnetic fields produced by coordinated neural activity, typically with highly sensitive sensors positioned close to the head.

**Nonspeaking.** A broad descriptive term for people who do not use reliable speech as their primary communication output. It does not specify cognition, receptive language, motor profile, or preferred AAC method.

**Preregistration.** Publicly time-stamping hypotheses, methods, exclusion rules, outcome measures, and analysis plans before inspecting the target results, reducing the ability to reshape the test after seeing the data.

**Pseudo-pair.** An analytical control formed by pairing recordings from people who were not actually interacting, used to estimate how much apparent synchrony can arise from shared task structure, slow trends, or analysis methods.

**Rapid Prompting Method (RPM) / Spelling to Communicate (S2C).** Spelling-based practices in which a communication partner typically presents, holds, or moves a letterboard and may provide prompts. Techniques vary. Their relationship to FC is contested by advocates, while professional reviews have emphasized the unresolved need for rigorous independent authorship testing. Independent typing without another person influencing selection is a different evidentiary situation.

**Target-specific information.** Information that distinguishes the actual concealed target from plausible alternatives. A rise in arousal may indicate that “something happened”; it does not identify whether the sender saw an orchid, bicycle, or random number.

**Telepathy, operational definition used here.** Reproducible transfer of target-specific information from one person to another when that information is not available through known sensory, behavioral, technological, procedural, or inferential channels.

**The Hill.** A term used in accounts circulating around nonspeaking spelling communities and *The Telepathy Tapes* for a reported shared nonphysical or mental meeting place. This book treats “the Hill” first as a report and experimental paradigm, not as an established external realm.

**Transduction.** Conversion of one form of physical signal into biological activity usable by the nervous system. Every established human sense depends on some form of transduction.

## What a positive result would mean

A replicated concealed-target effect would mean that the specific tested information-transfer phenomenon requires explanation.

It would not, by itself, establish:

- unrestricted access to another person’s thoughts;
- survival of consciousness after death;
- precognition;
- remote viewing as a general ability;
- quantum consciousness;
- a collective mind;
- every interpretation associated with the Hill;
- the validity of every spelling method or every message produced through one.

Those are separate claims.

## What a negative result would mean

Repeated chance performance under well-designed conditions would weaken the claim that target-specific telepathic transfer occurs under those conditions.

It would not establish:

- that a nonspeaking person lacks an inner life;
- that they lack language;
- that every communication method they use is invalid;
- that every unusual interpersonal experience is meaningless;
- that a shared Hill experience cannot exist as dream, imagery, culture, metaphor, or community practice.

A negative target-transfer result may still reveal useful physiology: motor supports, anticipatory gaze, partner regulation, shared timing, subtle sensory cueing, predictive expertise, autonomic coordination, or characteristics of a recurring internal state.

## Closing note

The book’s standard is intentionally asymmetric in one way and symmetric in another.

The claim is not accepted merely because a story is astonishing.

The claim is not rejected merely because it is astonishing either.

What matters is whether an experiment can separate authorship from access, access from transfer, and transfer from mechanism.

If the information remains after the ordinary roads are closed, the unknown has earned a map.
