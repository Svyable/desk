# Evidence Ledger

## Class 1 — Verified / documented

- The Eötvös–Pekár–Fekete torsion-balance experiments are historical precision tests of equivalence.
- Fischbach et al. published a 1986 reanalysis reporting apparent composition dependence and proposing an intermediate-range force.
- Chu and Dicke published a thermal-gradient explanation of the old data in 1986.
- Extensive later torsion and inverse-square experiments did not reproduce the original fifth-force interpretation over its favored region.
- Atom interferometers have directly measured attraction from small laboratory source masses and constrained screened scalars.
- MICROSCOPE's final result found no weak-equivalence-principle violation for its titanium/platinum test pair at the ~10^-15 level.
- Minute-scale lattice atom-interferometer coherence was demonstrated in 2024.
- A 2022 levitated-force-sensor result strongly excluded the basic cosmologically viable chameleon model it tested.
- A 2025 Reviews of Modern Physics article reviews spin-dependent exotic-force searches.
- 2026 peer-reviewed papers analyze screened dark energy in near-Earth space and propose long-baseline atom-interferometer searches.

## Class 2 — Credible but incomplete

- Some screened scalar models can evade classes of laboratory constraints through nonlinear environmental dependence.
- Long-baseline atom interferometers can materially improve sensitivity to selected chameleon and symmetron parameter space if projected systematic control is achieved.
- Orbital clock/Sagnac concepts may access screening regimes unavailable to terrestrial experiments.

## Class 3 — Disputed / unconfirmed

- The 1986 Eötvös composition correlation as evidence of a real fifth force.
- Any present claim that chameleon, symmetron, dilaton, axionlike, or related fields have been detected as new fundamental forces.
- Claims that screening in general has been ruled out by one experiment.
- Claims that a null equivalence-principle test excludes every spin-dependent or environment-dependent interaction.

## Class 4 — Speculative synthesis

- A deliberately matched terrestrial/orbital pair of quantum-force experiments could turn screening's environmental dependence into a positive fingerprint rather than a loophole.
- Geometry modulation may become as important as sensitivity improvement in the next generation of fifth-force searches.
- Cross-platform exclusion maps should treat apparatus geometry and environmental boundary conditions as first-class experimental metadata.
