# Contradictions and Incentives

## Historical anomaly versus later nulls

The 1986 Fischbach interpretation gave the old Eötvös data new significance. Chu–Dicke and later experiments supplied conventional/systematic explanations and failed to confirm the proposed force. The manuscript treats the historical claim as a target generator, not surviving positive evidence.

## Cosmological motivation versus laboratory exclusion

Dark-energy scalars are theoretically motivated in some frameworks, but motivation does not establish existence. Screening mechanisms were partly developed because unscreened light scalars face severe local constraints. This creates an incentive for increasingly complicated survival regions; experimental tests must therefore target model-specific predictions rather than admire theoretical flexibility.

## Experimental sensitivity versus theoretical translation

Published exclusion contours depend on the source/test geometry, environmental density, assumed potential, coupling structure, and approximations used to solve nonlinear field profiles. A stronger acceleration sensitivity does not automatically imply a stronger constraint on every screened model.

## Null enthusiasm

Experimental groups gain legitimate scientific value from excluding parameter space. But “rules out chameleon dark energy” can be broader in headlines than the model actually tested. The manuscript keeps claims attached to explicit constructions and parameter regions.

## Discovery incentives

A fifth-force detection would be career-defining. This raises the cost of confirmation: blind analysis, source reversal, environmental scaling, independent apparatus, and replication are necessary before an unexplained acceleration becomes new physics.
