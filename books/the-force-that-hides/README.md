# The Force That Hides

## Chameleons, Fifth Forces, and the Experiment Gravity Keeps Escaping

**Sven Hardy Benson**

### Thesis

If a new light field couples to ordinary matter, precision gravity experiments should usually have seen it. Screening theories make the opposite wager: the field can become heavy, weak, or otherwise self-suppressed in dense environments while remaining active in vacuum, orbit, or cosmological space. The scientifically interesting question is therefore no longer simply whether a fifth force exists, but whether our best null experiments have unknowingly been performed inside the very conditions that hide it.

This book follows that argument from the Eötvös torsion balance and the 1986 fifth-force controversy through Eöt-Wash, MICROSCOPE, atom interferometry, levitated sensors, screened dark-energy models, spin-dependent searches, and the 2026 push toward long-baseline and orbital tests.

The book does not claim discovery. Most searches are null, and several simple chameleon models have been severely constrained or ruled out in their original cosmological role. The live physics lies in the shrinking, model-dependent spaces where screening changes what different instruments actually test.

## Evidence posture

Peer-reviewed experimental physics heavy. Historical fifth-force claims are treated as disputed and largely superseded by later null tests. Screened-scalar mechanisms are established theoretical constructions, not established constituents of nature. Current chameleon, symmetron, dilaton, axionlike, dark-photon, and related force searches are kept model-specific. No null experiment is converted into a universal statement about all possible new interactions.
