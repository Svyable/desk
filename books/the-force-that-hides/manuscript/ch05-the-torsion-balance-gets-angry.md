# 5. The Torsion Balance Gets Angry

The modern torsion balance is a machine built by people who have been lied to by the laboratory for decades.

The Eöt-Wash program at the University of Washington turned that suspicion into an experimental culture. Their balances have tested equivalence, inverse-square behavior, Lorentz symmetry, spin-dependent forces, and other departures from known physics. A torsion fiber is simple enough to explain to a student and cruel enough to occupy a career.

The strength of the torsion balance is not that it is immune to disturbances. It is that many disturbances have different symmetries from the desired signal. Rotate a source mass with carefully chosen mass multipoles and a Newtonian background can be suppressed at the signal harmonic. Arrange different materials into a composition dipole and rotate the apparatus relative to Earth, Sun, or Galaxy. A real equivalence-principle violation should track the external attractor with a calculable phase.

Over time these experiments erased much of the parameter space that had made the 1986 fifth force exciting.

They also pushed Newton's inverse-square law to submillimeter distances. At those scales gravity is absurdly weak compared with electromagnetic contamination. The apparatus therefore becomes an exercise in making ordinary physics cancel itself.

The null results matter because many beyond-standard-model scenarios produce Yukawa-like corrections. Extra dimensions, moduli, scalar fields, and new bosons can all map onto an effective strength-range plot. Each experiment carves away a region.

But a screened scalar does not always behave like a fixed Yukawa force. The source may develop a thin shell. The test mass may be partially screened. The vacuum chamber wall may set the field value inside. The effective interaction can depend nonlinearly on object size, density, geometry, and ambient matter.

This is where an instrument optimized for ordinary fifth forces can become a different experiment than the theorist first imagines.

The balance did not fail. The theory changed the question.
