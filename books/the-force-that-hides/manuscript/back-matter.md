# Back Matter

## Chronology

- 1889–1922 — Eötvös, Pekár, and Fekete develop increasingly precise torsion-balance equivalence tests.
- 1970s–1980s — precision inverse-square and equivalence experiments tighten non-Newtonian constraints.
- January 6, 1986 — Fischbach et al. publish the Eötvös reanalysis proposing an intermediate-range composition-dependent interaction.
- October 1986 — Chu and Dicke publish a thermal-gradient alternative for the historical correlation.
- Late 1980s–1990s — dedicated fifth-force experiments largely exclude the original favored parameter region.
- 1998 — discovery of accelerating cosmic expansion sharpens interest in dynamical dark energy.
- 2004 — Khoury and Weltman develop the modern chameleon screening mechanism.
- 2006 — Upadhye, Gubser, and Khoury work out direct short-range tests of chameleon fields.
- 2015 — atom-interferometry searches strongly constrain chameleon parameter space.
- 2017 — atom interferometry measures attraction from a miniature in-vacuum source mass and improves screened-force limits.
- 2022 — MICROSCOPE publishes final weak-equivalence-principle results; a levitated-sensor experiment strongly constrains basic chameleon dark energy.
- 2024 — lattice atom interferometry measures gravitational attraction and demonstrates minute-scale coherence.
- June 24, 2025 — Reviews of Modern Physics publishes a broad review of spin-dependent exotic interactions.
- March 11, 2026 — Physical Review Letters publishes near-Earth space constraints on screened dark energy.
- April 22, 2026 — Physical Review D publishes the long-baseline screened-scalar source-mass/Q-flip proposal.

## What would change the book

A reproducible force signal with predicted environmental scaling would change the conclusion immediately. So would a theoretical no-go result showing that every remaining screened-scalar model capable of relevant cosmology is incompatible with existing data. The book is intentionally parameter-space-sensitive rather than metaphysical.

## Highest-value research next

Reconstruct a common machine-readable exclusion map from torsion, atom, levitated, orbital, clock, neutron, Casimir, and spin experiments; propagate each experiment's geometry and screening assumptions rather than copying published contour plots; compare chameleon, symmetron, dilaton, and Vainshtein mechanisms without collapsing them into one category; and preregister cross-environment experiments where the same microscopic coupling predicts measurably different terrestrial and orbital responses.
