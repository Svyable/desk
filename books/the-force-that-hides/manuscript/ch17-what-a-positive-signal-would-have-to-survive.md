# 17. What a Positive Signal Would Have to Survive

A positive fifth-force signal would be one of the largest discoveries in modern physics and one of the easiest ways to ruin a career with a cable.

The standard should therefore be cruel.

A candidate acceleration should reverse when the source configuration reverses and preserve the predicted phase. It should scale with separation according to the proposed field equation, not merely decrease. It should respond to source composition if the coupling predicts composition dependence. It should survive electrostatic patch mapping, magnetic reversal, thermal modulation, vibration injection, tilt calibration, wavefront analysis, and blind analysis choices.

For a screened field, there is an additional requirement: the environmental dependence must appear.

Change the chamber size. Change the source density at fixed Newtonian gravity where possible. Change the test-body size. Change ambient density. Move to a different gravitational potential. Use a second instrument whose dominant backgrounds are unrelated.

This turns screening from an escape hatch into a fingerprint.

A real chameleon-like signal should not merely survive an experimental change. It should change by the amount the nonlinear solution predicts.

The history of the 1986 fifth force gives the warning. A retrospective correlation can be exciting. A plausible systematic can be embarrassing. A prospective, modulated, independently replicated environmental scaling law is much harder to fake.

If the force hides, force it to move while we watch.
