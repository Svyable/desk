# 16. The 2026 Geometry

By 2026, the fifth-force frontier is becoming geometrical.

The easy versions of the question have already been asked: does a centimeter-scale mass create a simple extra acceleration? Do titanium and platinum fall differently? Does gravity become stronger below a millimeter?

The surviving screened models demand more deliberately chosen geometries.

Banks and collaborators' April 2026 Physical Review D proposal is a good example. Put an annular planar source mass inside a ten-meter-scale atom interferometer. Choose the shape so the hypothetical screened force and Newtonian field have distinguishable spatial structure. Alternate pulse sequences—the Q-flip—to modulate the response without physically moving every part of the apparatus. Calibrate the Newtonian source field in situ rather than subtracting it from a simulation and hoping the machining tolerances behave.

The proposal is aimed at facilities such as AION-10 and VLBAI. Its projected sensitivity reaches roughly one to one-and-a-half orders of magnitude beyond existing bounds for selected chameleon and symmetron models.

This is not yet a detection. It is more valuable at this stage as an experimental design document.

The 2026 space-based analysis by Feleppa and colleagues creates a complementary geometry: Earth orbit itself. They show that screened-field corrections can be mapped into geodetic precession, pericenter advance, and Sagnac observables. Their prospective nuclear-clock-level Sagnac sensitivity reaches parameter space not naturally attacked by the same terrestrial apparatus.

One experiment changes source shape. The other changes the environment around the whole experiment.

That is the mature version of the screening question. Stop asking where the field is hiding. Build two places where it cannot hide the same way.
