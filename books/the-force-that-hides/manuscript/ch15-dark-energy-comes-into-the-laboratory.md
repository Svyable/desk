# 15. Dark Energy Comes Into the Laboratory

Dark energy is normally introduced with a supernova and a cosmological scale factor.

The chameleon proposal brought it into a vacuum chamber beside a metal sphere.

That move deserves more attention than it gets. If cosmic acceleration comes from a scalar field with a mass of order the Hubble scale, the field is fantastically light. A light scalar generically mediates a macroscopic force if it couples to matter. Local tests then become a direct assault on dynamical dark-energy models.

Screening was invented to survive that assault.

The survival has become increasingly expensive. Atom interferometers have removed large regions. Torsion balances constrain others. Neutron experiments, Casimir-force measurements, astrophysical systems, equivalence tests, and levitated sensors add different boundaries.

A 2022 Nature Physics experiment using a diamagnetically levitated force sensor reported nearly two orders of magnitude improvement over prior tests in its regime and argued that the basic cosmologically viable chameleon model it targeted was decisively excluded as an explanation of dark energy.

That statement should be neither softened into “anything is possible” nor inflated into “screening is dead.” The experiment rules out the model space it actually covers. Other screened scalar constructions remain, but the most elegant original story has been squeezed hard.

This is how theory should pay rent. A mechanism invented to avoid local tests must eventually face local tests built specifically around the mechanism.

The cosmological mystery remains. The laboratory has merely made one class of explanations less comfortable.
