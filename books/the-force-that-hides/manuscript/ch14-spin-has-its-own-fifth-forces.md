# 14. Spin Has Its Own Fifth Forces

Not every fifth force pulls on kilograms.

Some exotic bosons generate spin-dependent interactions. Axions and axionlike particles, new vector bosons, dark photons, and other mediators can produce potentials that depend on one or both particles' spin, velocity, or relative orientation.

These forces can be nearly invisible to ordinary equivalence tests. A bulk unpolarized mass averages many spins away. A torsion balance designed for composition dependence may have little leverage on an interaction that couples to electron spin.

The experimental toolkit therefore looks different: spin-polarized torsion pendula, atomic comagnetometers, nuclear magnetic resonance, precision spectroscopy, trapped ions, nitrogen-vacancy centers, ultracold neutrons.

A 2025 Reviews of Modern Physics article by Cong and collaborators organized this landscape into a systematic set of spin-dependent exotic potentials and experimental constraints. The field has become mature enough that “fifth force” no longer refers to one anomalous acceleration. It is a library of possible low-energy interactions mapped onto distinct experimental observables.

This matters to the screened-scalar story for two reasons.

First, null statements must be interaction-specific. MICROSCOPE's spectacular equivalence limit does not automatically kill a pseudoscalar spin-spin force. A comagnetometer null does not automatically kill a universal scalar coupling.

Second, different mediator structures produce different environmental behavior. Some interactions are screened by material boundaries in ways others are not. Some are coherent over bulk matter; some depend on polarization. Some oscillate because the source is an ambient dark-matter field rather than a laboratory mass.

There is no single “fifth-force experiment.” There is only a network of mismatched instruments whose overlap is where confidence comes from.
