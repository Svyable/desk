# Coherence Without Incantation

Coherence is one of those words that can do science in the laboratory and magic in a sentence.

In optics, coherence describes statistical relationships in a field. Temporal coherence relates to phase correlations over time and is connected to spectral width. Spatial coherence concerns phase relationships across positions. Photon statistics can reveal whether light resembles thermal emission, coherent states or other sources. These are measured properties.

Outside optics, coherence often becomes a compliment. A healthy organism is called coherent. A synchronized brain is coherent. A theory is coherent. From there it is an easy slide to saying that biological light is coherent and therefore coordinates the organism. The same word has crossed three disciplines without carrying its instrument panel.

A serious biophoton claim has to bring the instrument panel back.

If emission is unusually coherent, specify relative to what null source. Chemiluminescent reactions can have nontrivial temporal dynamics. Detector dead time alters photon-count statistics. Afterpulsing creates correlations. Delayed luminescence after illumination can produce decay patterns. Biological oscillations modulate source intensity. None of these require laser-like organization.

The appropriate tests come from quantum and statistical optics. Measure second-order correlation functions with detector configurations that characterize instrumental artifacts. Compare live tissue with matched chemical reactions producing similar spectra and intensities. Vary temperature and metabolic state. Use calibrated coherent and thermal sources through the same optical path. Test whether any claimed nonclassical statistic survives detector corrections and independent replication.

If a living source does show unusual photon statistics, the question then becomes chemical. Which molecular processes generate them? A non-Poissonian count distribution might reflect bursty source reactions rather than a global organizing field. The mechanism should be driven downward into molecules before it is driven upward into consciousness.

There is no insult in this. Lasers themselves became useful because coherence acquired equations, cavities, gain media and threshold conditions. The word stopped being mystical and became engineering.

Biophoton research needs the same treatment. If cells possess an optical cavity, identify it. If there is gain, measure it. If emission is phase-correlated, report coherence times. If entangled pairs are generated, perform a nonclassical-correlation test appropriate to the state. If those photons alter another biological system, demonstrate the receiver.

The word *quantum* requires the same discipline. Quantum models of biological structures are worthwhile when they make predictions that classical models do not. Otherwise quantum language merely restates that matter is quantum mechanical.

Warm, wet biology can support quantum effects; photosynthetic energy transfer, radical-pair chemistry and tunneling debates have made that much clear. But each successful case is narrow. The environment, timescale and molecular architecture matter. One cannot import quantum robustness from a radical pair in cryptochrome into a centimeter-scale neuronal communication theory by analogy.

The most attractive possibility is also the most modest: perhaps some endogenous optical processes have correlations or transport properties that are more structured than bulk chemiluminescence models predict. That would be a discovery even before any signaling function was established.

Science gets stranger when vocabulary gets smaller. Remove *life force*, *field consciousness* and *quantum coherence* as all-purpose explanations and what remains is a photon counter asking whether two detection events are related. That is enough.
