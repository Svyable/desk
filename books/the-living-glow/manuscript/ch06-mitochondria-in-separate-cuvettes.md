# Mitochondria in Separate Cuvettes

The experiment I keep coming back to involves two cuvettes.

In work published in 2023, researchers reported that isolated mitochondria placed in separate, physically distinct cuvettes appeared to influence one another's respiration. When mitochondria in one cuvette were stressed with the electron-transport inhibitor antimycin, oxygen consumption changed in mitochondria in an adjacent cuvette. The effect depended on cell-line origin and on ambient light conditions. The authors considered non-chemical signaling and raised ultraweak photon emission among the possible mechanisms.

This is exactly the kind of paper that deserves neither a sneer nor a coronation.

If the result is robust, something crosses the boundary between preparations or some shared variable changes them both. Those are very different propositions. A glass wall blocks bulk chemical exchange but does not block every physical influence. Temperature gradients cross it. Ordinary light crosses it. Infrared radiation crosses it. Mechanical vibration reaches both preparations. Electrical equipment can create common-mode perturbations. Volatile compounds can travel through headspace depending on the geometry. Timing and handling can create correlated drift. A result that survives chemical separation has ruled out one family of explanations, not all families.

The mitochondria experiment is valuable because it puts the problem into a tractable scale. There is no need to invoke consciousness, organisms sensing intention or whole-body fields. Two samples, known metabolic machinery, measurable oxygen consumption, controllable optical paths. This is where an extraordinary-sounding claim can be turned into ordinary laboratory engineering.

The next experiment practically writes itself. Put sender and receiver cuvettes in independently temperature-controlled chambers. Measure UPE from the sender simultaneously. Place a tunable optical channel between them rather than relying on adjacent glass. Run conditions with a transparent window, an opaque barrier, wavelength-selective filters and a fiber path that transmits measured photons while eliminating direct thermal coupling. Add sham senders. Randomize which sender is stressed. Blind the analyst. Monitor vibration, acoustic energy, electric and magnetic fields, temperature and volatile composition. Reproduce the sender's measured photon pattern with an external source at matched intensity.

The external-source condition matters. If the receiver response follows the biological sender when the optical path is open, disappears behind an opaque barrier, returns when the biological photon stream is recreated artificially, and shows the same wavelength dependence as a candidate photoreceptor, the case becomes difficult to wave away. If the effect persists when photons are blocked, biophotons were the wrong mechanism even if non-chemical coupling remains.

That last outcome would still be interesting. Good experiments are allowed to change the mystery.

There is a broader lesson here about anomalous biology. Mechanism claims often arrive too early because they make a result easier to narrate. A strange correlation becomes "photonic communication" before anyone has established that photons mediate it. Then critics attack the mechanism and enthusiasts defend the phenomenon as though the two are inseparable. They are not.

The two-cuvette problem should be decomposed ruthlessly: is there an effect; what physical channel carries it; what source variable encodes it; what receiver transduces it; and does the coupling matter in a living system outside the apparatus?

Mitochondria are especially provocative because they sit near the main accepted source of UPE: oxidative metabolism. If any intracellular structure produces a changing optical field, mitochondria are plausible contributors. But proximity to the source does not make them receivers. The same organelle can emit photons as a by-product without possessing any machinery to interpret them.

This chapter stays with the cuvettes because the geometry is honest. There are only a few centimeters between ordinary chemistry and an extraordinary claim. Every centimeter can be instrumented.
