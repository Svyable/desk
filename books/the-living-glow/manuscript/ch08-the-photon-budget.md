# The Photon Budget

Every hidden biological channel eventually meets arithmetic.

Suppose a tissue produces ultraweak photons at a rate consistent with reported measurements. The raw number sounds impressive because photons are countable objects. But a communication claim needs the number that arrives at the receiver, not the number generated somewhere in the tissue.

Start with geometry. Emission is not automatically directed. If photons leave a microscopic source roughly isotropically, intensity falls as they spread. Then comes absorption. Proteins, pigments, water, lipids and blood all have wavelength-dependent optical properties. Then scattering. Biological tissue is not empty glass. Photons ricochet, lengthening their paths and making source localization difficult. Then comes the receiver's absorption cross-section and quantum efficiency. Finally there is biochemical gain: does one absorbed photon change enough molecules downstream to alter behavior?

The budget can fail at any step.

Advocates sometimes answer that biology detects single photons. That statement is true in specialized systems and misleading as a general argument. A dark-adapted vertebrate retina has a remarkable cascade built around rhodopsin, rod cells, neural convergence and noise suppression. The relevant question is not whether biology *can* detect one photon but whether the tissue under discussion contains a comparable receiver and whether endogenous photons reach it at a useful rate.

There is another twist. External measurements may drastically underestimate local production because most internally generated photons never escape the body. A detector outside skin samples only survivors. Within a cell, distances are micrometers rather than centimeters. An optical effect that is hopeless as long-range tissue communication could be plausible across a mitochondrion, membrane or organelle contact. Scale changes the budget.

That is why "biophoton signaling" may be too broad a category. There are at least three geometries: intracellular, cell-to-cell and organism-to-organism. A mechanism feasible at one scale says little about the others. A photon traveling two micrometers through cytoplasm is not a photon traveling across a skull or between plants.

The budget also forces wavelength specificity. Ultraweak emission spans broad bands because multiple excited species contribute. A receiver sensitive to one narrow region sees only a fraction. Conversely, spectral filtering by tissue might create local windows where certain wavelengths travel farther. Any serious model should predict where the usable window lies before the detector is pointed.

Signal-to-noise is not simply external darkness. Living tissue has its own optical background. If oxidative metabolism continuously generates photons, a coded message would ride on stochastic emission. Perhaps timing matters: bursts synchronized with metabolic events might be more detectable than mean intensity. Perhaps spatial confinement matters. Perhaps there is no code at all.

A theory should put numbers on these alternatives. How many photons are produced per source volume per second? What fraction enters the relevant mode? What path length? What attenuation coefficient? What receiver density? What probability of absorption? What spontaneous activation rate does the receptor have without photons? What response threshold follows?

This sounds reductionist only if one confuses wonder with vagueness. Numbers make the hypothesis more interesting because they show where evolution would have to work. If the natural flux is six orders of magnitude too small for a proposed receptor, the model is probably wrong. If it is within an order of magnitude, there is an experiment worth doing.

The photon budget is also where quantum language must pay rent. Entanglement, coherence or waveguiding cannot be invoked as generic amplifiers. Each proposed effect has to change a term in the budget in a measurable way: improve directional transport, reduce loss, alter correlations, or increase receiver sensitivity.

Until then, the budget remains brutally classical. That is not a defect. A hidden channel that cannot survive ordinary accounting is not made stronger by calling it quantum.
