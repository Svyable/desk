# The Human Veto

There will be a temptation to define successful government by efficiency.

Faster permits.

Better forecasts.

Lower fraud.

Smarter budgets.

More precise regulations.

These are valuable goals.

But democracy exists for something deeper than administrative performance.

It exists because people should possess agency over the systems governing them.

A perfectly optimized dictatorship remains a dictatorship.

An AI capable of producing excellent policies does not automatically possess the right to impose them.

The defining political principle of the AI era should therefore be simple:

**Machines may advise, administer, simulate, detect, summarize, and negotiate. Humans must retain the final legitimate veto.**

That veto may sometimes be inefficient.

Citizens may reject an objectively better policy.

Legislatures may ignore excellent recommendations.

Voters may make mistakes.

That is not a bug democracy can completely eliminate.

It is part of what self-government means.

Freedom includes the freedom to make decisions imperfectly.
