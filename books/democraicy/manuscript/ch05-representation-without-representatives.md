# Representation Without Representatives

Representative democracy exists partly because direct democracy does not scale.

Citizens cannot spend every afternoon studying legislation.

So we elect people to do it for us.

AI weakens this constraint.

A personal political agent could theoretically understand thousands of issues.

It could know your preferences.

It could compare proposals with those preferences.

It could ask you questions when uncertain.

It could eventually cast advisory votes on your behalf.

This creates a spectrum between representative and direct democracy that previously did not exist.

Imagine citizens delegating different political questions differently.

You trust one environmental organization on climate policy.

A local business association on municipal regulation.

Your own AI on routine administrative matters.

Yourself on major constitutional questions.

Political representation becomes modular.

Delegation becomes revocable and specific.

Democracy begins behaving less like an election every few years and more like a continuously updating network of consent.

There are reasons to fear this.

Politics is not merely preference aggregation.

Leadership sometimes requires telling citizens what they do not want to hear.

Compromise requires accepting packages rather than optimizing every issue independently.

And human representatives can be held morally responsible in a way software cannot.

Nevertheless, the pressure will grow.

Once citizens experience highly responsive systems everywhere else in life, waiting four years to express political preferences may begin to feel strangely primitive.
