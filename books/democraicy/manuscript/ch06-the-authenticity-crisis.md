# The Authenticity Crisis

Democracy depends on knowing that certain things actually happened.

A candidate said this.

A police officer did that.

A crowd gathered here.

A missile landed there.

A witness saw something.

For most of modern history, recordings strengthened this evidentiary environment.

Photography appeared to capture reality.

Then video.

Then audio.

Synthetic media reverses the relationship.

Soon the existence of a convincing recording may prove very little by itself.

Political systems will therefore need to move from a culture of **seeing is believing** toward a culture of verified provenance.

Who created this?

Which device recorded it?

Was it modified?

Can its chain of custody be demonstrated?

Authentication infrastructure may become as important to democracy as the printing press once was.

This creates an irony.

AI makes fabrication cheap.

Therefore proof becomes valuable.

The future internet may contain vastly more information while producing dramatically less default trust.

Institutions capable of establishing authenticity may gain power.

Journalists.

Courts.

Cryptographic systems.

Trusted networks.

Reputable archives.

Identity providers.

The political struggle will increasingly concern not merely what is true, but which systems society trusts to establish truth.
