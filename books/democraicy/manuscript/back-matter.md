# Democracy After Intelligence

The first democracies were built in a world where information moved at the speed of a person.

Modern democracies emerged in a world of newspapers, railways, telegraphs, radio, television, and eventually the internet.

Every communications revolution changed politics.

Artificial intelligence is different because it does not merely move information.

It interprets it.

Generates it.

Argues with it.

Acts upon it.

And eventually negotiates with other intelligence.

The central political conflict of the coming decades will not simply be humans versus machines.

It will be humans using machines against other humans using machines.

Citizens with agents.

Companies with agents.

Governments with agents.

Campaigns with agents.

Foreign states with agents.

Journalists with agents.

Regulators with agents.

And somewhere in the center of this enormous computational contest remains the old democratic question:

**Who has legitimate power?**

The technology may change dramatically.

The answer should not.

People do.

The challenge of DemocrAIcy is therefore not to construct a government intelligent enough to replace human judgment.

It is to use artificial intelligence to make human self-government more capable without allowing capability to become control.

AI could make democracy more informed.

More accessible.

More responsive.

More participatory.

It could also make propaganda individualized, bureaucracy inscrutable, lobbying infinite, surveillance effortless, and political manipulation extraordinarily precise.

Both futures are technologically plausible.

Technology will not choose between them.

Institutions will.

Citizens will.

We will.

The great political project of the AI century is not teaching machines how to govern humans.

It is teaching humans how to remain sovereign in a world full of machines that can.
