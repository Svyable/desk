# When Everyone Has a Lobbyist

Lobbying has a bad reputation because access is unequal.

Large institutions can hire experts to watch legislation, meet officials, draft policy language, and defend their interests.

Ordinary citizens usually cannot.

AI could democratize lobbying.

Your personal agent could monitor every regulation affecting you.

A restaurant owner could know the moment a proposed health rule changes.

A teacher could follow education legislation automatically.

A patient with a rare disease could coordinate with thousands of similar families.

A neighborhood could instantly analyze zoning proposals.

In this sense, AI could create the most politically empowered citizenry in history.

But there is a catch.

Corporations receive the same technology.

A company with ten thousand employees may deploy ten thousand specialized political agents.

Industry associations can generate endless regulatory comments.

Interest groups can challenge every administrative decision.

Governments themselves can automate political communication.

We could move from a world in which lobbying is scarce to one in which lobbying is infinite.

When everyone can speak continuously, the scarce resource becomes something else.

Attention.

Officials will need AI systems simply to process the AI-generated requests arriving from everyone else.

Politics becomes machine-mediated on both sides.

One agent petitions.

Another evaluates the petition.

A third searches for manipulation.

A fourth summarizes the conflict for a human.

The bureaucracy begins to resemble a network of negotiating machines with human sovereignty sitting somewhere above it.
