# The Constitutional Layer

Every major technological era eventually pressures institutions built for the previous one.

Industrialization transformed labor law.

Broadcast media transformed campaign regulation.

Nuclear weapons transformed executive power.

Artificial intelligence will create constitutional questions of its own.

Can an AI possess speech rights?

Can a million automated agents petition the government simultaneously?

Does a citizen have the right to know when political communication was generated by a machine?

Can government require models to reveal reasoning relevant to administrative decisions?

Can a politician delegate official duties to an AI?

Who is liable when an autonomous public system causes harm?

Can foreign AI systems participate in domestic political discourse?

Can an artificial agent make political donations?

Can it form an organization?

Can it negotiate legislation?

These questions sound strange only because the technology is young.

Previous generations confronted equally strange questions.

Could a corporation possess legal personhood?

Could broadcasting spectrum be regulated?

Could governments intercept telephone calls?

Could software be protected as speech?

Institutions eventually absorb new technology.

The dangerous period is the transition, when technological reality moves faster than political categories.
