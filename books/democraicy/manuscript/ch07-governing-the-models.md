# Governing the Models

The political importance of an AI system does not begin when government buys it.

A widely used private model may become part of society's political infrastructure simply because millions of citizens ask it political questions.

What does this law do?

Which candidate supports this policy?

Is this claim true?

How should I think about immigration?

What caused this war?

Should taxes be higher?

The answers do not have to endorse a candidate to shape political understanding.

Selection matters.

Framing matters.

What evidence appears first matters.

What uncertainty is emphasized matters.

This creates an uncomfortable reality.

Some of the most consequential political institutions of the future may formally be technology companies.

Governments will therefore face a difficult balance.

Too little oversight and enormously influential systems operate without democratic accountability.

Too much control and governments acquire dangerous power over the information citizens receive.

Neither corporate sovereignty nor state-controlled intelligence is attractive.

DemocrAIcy will require pluralism.

Multiple models.

Transparent standards.

Independent evaluation.

Academic access.

Competition.

Public-interest systems.

The ability to move between providers.

No democracy should want a single machine through which reality must pass.
