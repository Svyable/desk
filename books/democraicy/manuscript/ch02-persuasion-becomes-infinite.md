# Persuasion Becomes Infinite

Political persuasion has historically been expensive.

A candidate could make a speech.

Print a leaflet.

Buy a television advertisement.

Send campaign workers door to door.

Digital advertising improved targeting.

AI changes its fundamental economics.

Imagine a political campaign capable of conducting a private conversation with every voter.

Not one advertisement for a demographic.

One argument for one person.

The system knows that one voter cares about childcare costs.

Another worries about crime.

Another owns a small business.

Another has a child entering university.

The political message adapts continuously.

Its tone changes.

Its examples change.

Its emotional framing changes.

Its language changes.

Eventually its apparent personality may change.

Political rhetoric stops being broadcast and becomes generated.

The danger is not merely misinformation.

Humans have always lied in politics.

The larger change is **persuasion at scale**.

Democracy evolved in a world where influence required enormous human effort.

When personalized persuasion becomes nearly free, attention becomes a battlefield populated by machines.

The constitutional right to speak may collide with the technological capacity to speak billions of times.
