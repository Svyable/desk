# Democracy's Unexpected Advantage

Authoritarian systems may initially appear well suited to AI.

They can deploy surveillance rapidly.

Centralize data.

Automate censorship.

Coordinate institutions.

Experiment without lengthy debate.

Democracy is slower.

Messier.

More adversarial.

And filled with institutions capable of saying no.

These characteristics can appear inefficient.

They may also become advantages.

Complex AI systems are difficult to understand.

The safest method for governing powerful systems may be exactly what democracies already do when functioning well:

Distribute power.

Permit criticism.

Encourage competing institutions.

Protect whistleblowers.

Allow independent researchers.

Require legal appeals.

Expose decisions to adversarial challenge.

Replace leaders peacefully.

The same friction that makes democracy frustrating can make it resilient.

A centralized society can deploy technology faster.

It can also make one enormous mistake faster.

The democratic advantage is not superior intelligence.

It is error correction.

That may become increasingly important in a world filled with intelligent machines.
