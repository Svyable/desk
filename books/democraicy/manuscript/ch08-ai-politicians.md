# AI Politicians

Eventually someone will ask the obvious question.

Why not elect an AI?

The machine never sleeps.

It can read every briefing.

It cannot become physically exhausted.

It can theoretically communicate individually with every constituent.

It can analyze every budget line.

It does not need campaign donations for personal consumption.

It cannot secretly purchase a vacation home.

At first glance, the proposition may sound compelling.

It is also based on a misunderstanding of politics.

Political leadership is not merely optimization.

Politics involves moral responsibility.

When a painful decision must be made, society needs someone who can legitimately own it.

A machine can recommend sending soldiers into danger.

It cannot sacrifice anything itself.

A machine can recommend raising taxes.

It does not pay them.

A machine can recommend closing a hospital.

It will never need one.

Authority without vulnerability is dangerous.

The future may therefore contain AI political advisers of extraordinary power without granting those systems final sovereignty.

Machines can calculate.

Humans must remain accountable.

This principle sounds obvious now.

It may become surprisingly difficult to maintain once machine recommendations consistently outperform human judgment.
