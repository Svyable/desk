# Evidence ledger — THE LIGHTS THAT STAY

This ledger is deliberately stricter than the manuscript. A recurring light is not automatically one phenomenon. A radar return is not automatically the light. A proposed plasma mechanism is not an identification. A UAP label is a measurement state, not an ontology.

## Class 1 — verified / documented

- Project Hessdalen conducted an instrumented field campaign in the valley from 21 January to 26 February 1984. Its technical archive describes visual stations and instruments including radar, cameras, diffraction gratings, spectrum analysis, magnetometer, seismograph, Geiger counter, infrared viewer and laser. Primary archive: https://old.hessdalen.org/rapporter/hprapport84.shtml
- The project archive reports 53 visual observations categorized as unknown lights during the campaign and preserves spectrographic, photographic and radar records. The spectrographic archive says 23 grating photographs were taken, five as known-light references, and only three unknown-light frames were sufficiently clean for further spectral work. https://old.hessdalen.org/reports/Spectrographic_records.shtml
- A surviving 1984 optical-spectrum page gives measured grating positions and estimated wavelengths near 710, 500 and 430 nm for photographs taken at 03:08 on 26 February 1984. This is evidence that spectral measurement was attempted and recorded, not by itself a validated composition diagnosis. https://old.hessdalen.org/reports/Optical_specter.shtml
- Later EMBLA field work used optical, radio and radar methods. Massimo Teodorani's 2004 survey describes recurrent luminous phenomena and explicitly states that no self-consistent definitive theory could yet account quantitatively for all reported characteristics. ADS record: https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2004JSE....18..217T/abstract
- A dusty-plasma mechanism involving ionized air/dust and radon decay was proposed by Paiva and Taft in 2010. The paper is a hypothesis/model, not confirmation of the mechanism. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jastp.2010.07.022
- NASA's 2023 UAP independent study explicitly centered data quality, prospective collection, calibration, metadata and physical constraints, and stated that the limited number of high-quality observations prevents firm scientific conclusions about UAP nature. https://science.nasa.gov/uap/

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete

- The 1984 technical record reports radar returns during the campaign, including returns associated in time with visual observations. Exact event-by-event sensor geometry, clutter rejection, operator procedure and raw radar data need reconstruction before any radar/optical coincidence is promoted to a single physical object.
- EMBLA reports describe optical/radio/radar anomalies and estimate substantial radiant power for some luminous events. These are field-study results from a difficult observing environment; independent replication with modern calibrated instruments is incomplete.
- Some researchers have proposed electrochemical, plasma, dusty-plasma and geological/electrical mechanisms. Several may explain subsets of observations. No mechanism presently earns the right to absorb every Hessdalen report into one class.

## Class 3 — disputed / unconfirmed

- Claims that Hessdalen radar tracks establish solid craft, extraordinary acceleration, or technology are unconfirmed. Radar can constrain a hypothesis only after association, geometry, clutter, propagation and instrument behavior are established.
- Claims that lights respond intelligently to lasers are not established by the small historical interaction record. Temporal coincidence and observer interpretation remain serious alternatives.
- Claims that Hessdalen is evidence of extraterrestrial/non-human technology are unsupported by the available public evidence.
- Claims that every Hessdalen light is anomalous are false as a working assumption. Ordinary aircraft, astronomical sources, vehicles and other conventional lights are part of the observational environment and must remain in the control set.

## Class 4 — speculative synthesis / open conjecture

- **Recurrence advantage:** an anomaly that recurs in a bounded geography should be treated differently from a one-off UAP report because it permits prospective instrumentation, preregistered triggers and repeated control observations.
- **Category fracture:** `the Hessdalen phenomenon` may be an observational bucket containing several ordinary classes plus one or more unusual atmospheric processes. The residual can shrink without disappearing.
- **Association debt:** every time two sensors are said to have observed `the same thing`, the analysis incurs a debt that must be paid with timing, geometry, field of view, calibration and alternative-source exclusion.
- **Unknown as measurement state:** `unidentified` should expire when new evidence arrives. It is not a durable property of an object and certainly not a synonym for technology.
- **Stay-until-repeat test:** the strongest anomaly science should prefer targets where investigators can remain long enough for nature to produce another trial.

## Contradictions to preserve

1. Recurrence improves testability but also creates a large denominator of mundane events.
2. Multi-sensor observations can be much stronger than eyewitness testimony, but only if the sensors are demonstrably associated with the same event.
3. Plasma models are physically plausible classes, not automatic explanations for arbitrary motion or radar behavior.
4. Historical instrument records are valuable precisely because they are imperfect; modern retellings often promote ambiguous measurements into capabilities the original data did not establish.
5. A residual after conventional identifications is not evidence for a preferred extraordinary hypothesis. It is a residual.

## Publication holds

Do not publish as fact without primary reconstruction:

- exact counts of radar/visual coincidences;
- extraordinary speed values sometimes repeated from Hessdalen summaries;
- radiant-power maxima unless calculation assumptions and distance estimates are exposed;
- `laser response` as evidence of agency;
- metallic-particle deposition as causally linked to a luminous event;
- any claim that radar proves solidity;
- any single-mechanism account of all Hessdalen observations.

## Strongest countermodel

There may be no singular Hessdalen phenomenon. A long-running observation program in a valley with roads, aircraft, stars, weather, unusual sight lines and motivated observers will accumulate heterogeneous misidentifications plus instrument artifacts; the remaining luminous events may be uncommon but ordinary atmospheric/electrical processes. If careful modern prospective observation classifies essentially all events into known sources or known atmospheric mechanisms, the book's residual-anomaly argument should collapse. That would be a scientific success, not a narrative failure.

## Defeat condition

The book's central claim is defeated if recurrence does not materially improve discriminating evidence—if decades of prospective instrumentation cannot produce better event association, calibrated spectra, geometry, distance, timing or physical constraints than one-off reports. In that case Hessdalen is not a model laboratory for UAP science; it is primarily a case study in how observation programs can preserve ambiguity.
