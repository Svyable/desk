# Back Matter

*The Compatibility Dividend* began with a small observation about things that fit.

A bolt made by one company can repair a machine made by another. A container packed inland can be lifted by a crane across an ocean. A string of characters can survive movement between operating systems. A packet can cross networks its sender did not know existed. Each success looks ordinary because the agreement underneath has become ordinary.

That ordinariness is the achievement.

The book's claim is not that civilization should standardize everything. Uniformity can erase useful diversity, propagate common failures, protect incumbents and freeze yesterday's assumptions into tomorrow's infrastructure. The harder and more useful task is to identify the seam where shared constraints enlarge the field of independent action.

The historical cases suggest several durable principles. Standardize interfaces before internals. Make meaning portable where ambiguity is expensive. Preserve multiple implementations around a narrow common waist. Treat migration and conformity testing as part of the standard rather than afterthoughts. Keep authority separable from identity and intelligence. Make errors, versions and revocation legible. Give successful standards a constitutional mechanism for change.

The final implication reaches beyond machinery and software.

Civilization is itself a compatibility project. Money, measurement, contracts, calendars, corporations, credentials, property, accounting and language are reusable agreements that let people coordinate beyond personal relationships. They reduce the amount of bespoke negotiation required before strangers can trust a result enough to act.

Autonomous agents do not escape that inheritance. They intensify it.

As machine intelligence becomes cheaper, the scarce resource shifts toward reliable relationships among intelligent actors: who may act, for whom, under which limits, with what evidence, through which settlement system, and with what route for revocation or dispute. An economy of agents will therefore depend less on one universal intelligence than on a plural stack of agreements that lets different intelligences meet without becoming one system under one owner.

That is the compatibility dividend in its mature form.

Intelligence determines what an actor can do.

Standards determine what many actors can do together.

Sven Hardy Benson
2026
