# Expansion pass 03 — synthesis notes

This pass adds two synthesis chapters rather than another sequence of historical case studies.

## Chapter 19 — Where to Draw the Seam

Purpose: turn the book's accumulated cases into a practical theory of seam selection without inventing a pseudo-quantitative formula.

Claims are deliberately derived from cases and sources already represented in the main source ledger and the first two expansion notes: Baltimore hose interoperability, screw threads, containerization, TCP/IP, Unicode, IPv6 migration, TLS retirement, Rail Baltica, QUIC interoperability testing, authorization standards, verifiable credentials, and standards governance.

Key analytical tests introduced in prose:

- cost of disagreement at the interface
- number of bilateral relationships replaced by one shared seam
- variation worth preserving above and below the seam
- practical substitutability of implementations
- common-mode risk introduced by shared semantics
- migration and deprecation paths
- observable conformance
- irreversibility of consequences
- minimum necessary disclosure
- governance power created around definition, certification, registries, and distribution

Caution: these are questions for judgment, not a claimed mathematical model. The chapter explicitly avoids presenting a compatibility equation or pretending the variables have universal weights.

## Chapter 20 — The Compatibility Trap

Purpose: collect the strongest counter-thesis into one chapter so the book does not imply that interoperability and standardization are automatically welfare-improving.

Failure modes developed:

- monoculture and correlated failure
- premature convergence and legacy lock-in
- lowest-common-denominator design
- compliance theater and target gaming
- certification capture
- proprietary capture through extensions and adjacent infrastructure
- semantic drift beneath syntactic compatibility
- orphaned standards and maintenance funding
- coercive interoperability and the legitimate need for refusal/access control
- political choices laundered as technical necessities
- success creating constituencies that make migration difficult

The chapter relies on mechanisms already evidenced elsewhere in the manuscript rather than adding unsupported historical claims. Where it invokes agriculture as an analogy for monoculture, the analogy is qualitative and does not claim a specific crop-loss statistic.

## Falsification posture

The synthesis would be weakened if adaptive translation becomes so reliable that consequential interfaces can remain ambiguous without increasing disputes, fraud, safety failures, or verification costs. It would also be weakened if vertically integrated proprietary systems consistently deliver portability, contestability, and migration at lower social cost than shared seams.

Those possibilities remain open. The book's narrower claim is that high-consequence coordination among independently governed actors benefits from explicit, inspectable interfaces, and that those interfaces should be judged partly by the freedom and failure modes they create around themselves.
