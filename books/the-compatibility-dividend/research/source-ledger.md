# Source Ledger

This is the starting research trail for *The Compatibility Dividend*. Before a Shelf release, major historical and contemporary claims should be mapped to exact sources and dates in chapter notes or a release-ready citations pass.

## Research cautions

- The manuscript uses historical cases to extract structural lessons. Do not let the structural lesson turn a multicausal event into a monocausal story.
- Baltimore's incompatible hose couplings mattered; do not claim they alone caused the fire's destruction.
- Do not repeat the Roman-chariot origin myth for standard railway gauge as fact.
- Do not describe Malcolm McLean as inventing the idea of putting freight in boxes; his role is in commercial intermodal containerization and ecosystem formation.
- Do not treat QWERTY as settled proof that inferior standards always win; the economics literature disputes the canonical story.
- “Open standard” needs a defined sense: document access, implementability, licensing, governance, conformity testing, identifiers, distribution and switching can each differ.
- Standards-essential patent law is jurisdiction-specific and contested. Avoid universal legal conclusions.
- A W3C Verifiable Credential can make a claim machine-verifiable; it does not make every issuer trustworthy or every claim legally authoritative.
- OAuth authorizes access within a technical framework; it is not itself a complete legal theory of agency.
- MCP and A2A are rapidly evolving. Record exact versions whenever factual claims depend on their current behavior.
- The proposed “constitution of the machine economy” is a design synthesis, not a claim that the described stack has already been standardized or universally accepted.
