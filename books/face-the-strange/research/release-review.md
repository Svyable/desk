# Release Review — Face The Strange

**Review date:** 2026-09-03  
**Decision:** Release-cleared for first Shelf edition  
**Desk status:** Complete draft  
**Edition target:** First edition

## Scope reviewed

The canonical Desk publication packet was reviewed as a complete 25-chapter manuscript with front matter, back matter, book README/TOC, All Rights Reserved notice, machine-readable rights metadata, research brief, falsification notes, and source ledger.

The review keeps capability, consciousness, personhood, social response, autonomy, reliability, embodiment, adoption, and institutional change as distinct claim classes. The book is explicitly framed around human experience and institutional behavior rather than a claim that current systems are conscious or persons.

## Evidence and current-fact checks

The research packet is unusually explicit about uncertainty and counterevidence. It preserves the strongest failure modes for the thesis: capability progress can slow or fail to generalize; agents may remain too unreliable for deep delegation; persistent memory can create privacy and security costs; affective use can remain niche or harmful; embodied AI can lag digital systems; organizations can deliberately constrain autonomy; social responses can be shallow; cultures may adopt different norms; and advanced AI can disappear into infrastructure rather than becoming recognized as a counterpart.

Primary/authoritative anchors in the source ledger were checked for release suitability and are used with bounded claims rather than as universal proof:

- METR task-horizon work is treated as a bounded measure of software-task difficulty, not literal autonomous runtime or a proxy for all cognitive work.
- Stanford HAI's 2026 AI Index is used as a synthesis for adoption, performance, investment, education, policy, public opinion and economic context while retaining the methodological limits of underlying datasets.
- NIST AI RMF / Generative AI Profile is used as a practical governance framework rather than proof that safety or compliance is solved; the packet records that the framework is still evolving.
- EU AI Act material is used to show practical risk-, documentation- and accountability-based governance without treating EU law as globally representative or philosophically dispositive.
- OpenAI/MIT affective-use work and Common Sense Media youth studies are explicitly bounded by population, product, self-report and causal-inference limits and are not presented as evidence of consciousness, therapeutic efficacy, or universal attachment.
- Google DeepMind robotics material is identified as developer evidence and paired with independent counter-reporting on cost, precision, usefulness and deployment robustness.
- ILO exposure estimates are kept distinct from realized job displacement.
- ELIZA/CASA evidence is used only to establish human social-response tendencies, not machine understanding.

## Residual limitations for readers and future editions

The manuscript concerns a fast-moving field. Agent reliability, task horizons, enterprise deployment, youth use, robotics capability, AI regulation, labor effects and public attitudes can change quickly. Later editions should refresh those evidence lanes. The first edition's claims are written with those limitations and vintages visible, so those future-refresh needs are not unresolved first-edition blockers.

## Decision

**Release-cleared.** No factual, research, rights, structural, or status blocker identified in the reviewed first-edition packet prevents a deliberate Shelf release.
