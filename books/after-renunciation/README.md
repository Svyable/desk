# AFTER RENUNCIATION

## What happens to a secret capability after the country says it no longer wants it

**Sven Hardy Benson**

Status: working draft

## Reader promise

In November 1969 Richard Nixon publicly renounced biological warfare. In February 1970 the policy widened to toxins. Destruction plans followed. This sounds like an ending. It is a much harder epistemic event than that.

AFTER RENUNCIATION asks what a public prohibition does—and does not—let us infer about the machinery, knowledge, inventories, intelligence requirements and institutional habits built before it. The book is not an allegation that a prohibited offensive capability secretly continued. It is about the evidentiary problem created when a state changes policy faster than laboratories, archives, expertise and threat perceptions can disappear.

The governing distinction is: **policy can end an authorized mission without erasing the technical world that mission created.** That persistence is not proof of violation. It is the reason compliance has to be demonstrated rather than inferred from either suspicion or announcement.

2. **Destroy the Stockpile** — why policy requires inventories, custody and disposal.
3. **Toxins Do Not Fit the Box** — the 1969–70 argument over whether toxins were chemical or biological.
4. **Fort Detrick After the Weapon** — facilities outlive missions.
5. **Knowledge Cannot Be Burned** — tacit expertise, publications and personnel after program termination.
6. **Defensive Is a Verb** — how legitimate defensive work can overlap technically with prohibited capability.
7. **The Intelligence Exception** — watching adversary programs after renouncing one’s own.
8. **The Archive Problem** — what surviving paperwork can and cannot prove.
9. **Destruction Has Logistics** — environmental review, transport, neutralization and receipts.
10. **Verification Without Omniscience** — what arms control can actually observe.
11. **The Dual-Use Laboratory** — equipment does not announce intent.
12. **Soviet Mirror** — why adversary uncertainty keeps expertise alive.
13. **The Capability Residue** — distinguish retained knowledge from retained weapon.
14. **Secret Continuation Claims** — how to evaluate allegations without converting suspicion into fact.
15. **Intent Has a Timestamp** — the same apparatus can change legal and institutional meaning over time.
16. **What Compliance Owes** — positive receipts, not merely absence of exposure.
17. **After Renunciation** — a framework for policy discontinuity and technical persistence.
18. **The Last Vial** — what an ending looks like when knowledge survives it.

## Evidence posture

Primary-policy/declassified-document-heavy. The opening spine uses Nixon’s 25 November 1969 statement, NSDM 35, the toxin-policy review, the February 1970 White House toxin decision, NSDM 44, destruction planning, and later Biological Weapons Convention records. Claims of unauthorized continuation are held as disputed unless supported by case-specific evidence.
