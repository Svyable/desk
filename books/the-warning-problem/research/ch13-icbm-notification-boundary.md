# Chapter 13 research audit — 2024 ICBM notification boundary

**Scope:** one bounded claim audit for the opening of Chapter 13, “China’s Warning System.” This pass does not change manuscript prose.

## Why this passage needs a boundary

The chapter’s opening uses China’s September 25, 2024 Pacific ICBM launch to make an important argument: advance notification can add a different kind of evidence to a strategic warning picture and reduce the risk that a test is interpreted as an attack. That argument survives checking, but the current prose flattens facts established by different sources into one narrator-level account.

China’s own Ministry of National Defense statement establishes that the PLA Rocket Force launched an intercontinental ballistic missile carrying a dummy warhead at 08:44 on September 25, 2024, that it fell into an expected sea area in the Pacific, and that China characterized the launch as routine annual training not directed at a country or target. The public Chinese statement does **not** establish the launch point in Hainan, an approximately 11,000-kilometer flight, an impact area near French Polynesia, or which governments received advance notice.

Those additional details are supplied by the U.S. Department of Defense’s 2025 report to Congress. The report assesses that a DF-31B was launched from northern Hainan, flew roughly 11,000 kilometers, and landed in the ocean near French Polynesia. It also says China notified the United States, France, New Zealand, the United Kingdom, and Australia before the launch, while Japan, the Philippines, and other Pacific island countries were not notified. Those are useful and consequential facts, but where the chapter depends on them it should preserve their provenance as a U.S. external assessment rather than silently turning them into Chinese disclosure.

The Pentagon’s contemporaneous public briefing provides stronger support for the chapter’s central warning-system point than a generic claim about transparency. On September 26, 2024, Deputy Pentagon Press Secretary Sabrina Singh confirmed that the United States had received some advance notice, called that notification a step in the right direction, and said it reduced the risk of misperception and miscalculation. She also said the Department wanted a more regularized notification arrangement for ballistic-missile and space launches. That last point matters: the notification was useful precisely because it was unusual enough that Washington wanted to institutionalize it.

## Strongest counterargument

The launch is not a clean example of comprehensive regional transparency. According to the 2025 DoD report, several governments received advance notice while Japan, the Philippines, and other Pacific island countries did not. The same physical event therefore entered different national warning systems with different contextual information.

That asymmetry strengthens rather than weakens the book’s larger argument if it remains visible. Advance notice can reduce one recipient’s uncertainty without making the event intrinsically unambiguous. A message also cannot replace independent physical warning: states can deceive, misunderstand one another, or fail to notify relevant parties. Its value is that it is another channel with a different failure mode from infrared detection or radar tracking.

## Claim boundary for the eventual prose pass

A defensible formulation is:

> The September 2024 launch shows how advance notification can change the interpretation of an otherwise alarming sensor event for governments that receive it. China publicly confirmed the ICBM test and dummy warhead; the United States later assessed additional trajectory details and reported that notification was selective rather than universal. The episode therefore supports communication as an independent input to warning, not notification as a substitute for warning or proof of a settled regional transparency regime.

Avoid implying that:

- China publicly disclosed the Hainan launch point, approximately 11,000-kilometer trajectory, or impact near French Polynesia;
- every relevant regional government received advance notice;
- the notification by itself established that the launch was harmless or demonstrated how China would behave in a nuclear crisis;
- a Pentagon assessment of trajectory, system type, or recipients is the same evidentiary object as a Chinese official statement.

## Why this improves the book

Chapter 13 later tells the reader to keep the nouns attached to their owners: “China says. The Pentagon assesses. A satellite was launched. An analyst infers.” The opening should obey the same standard. Preserving source provenance makes the scene more credible and gives the chapter a sharper detail: the same missile launch looked different depending on whether a government had received the extra message before its sensors saw the plume.
