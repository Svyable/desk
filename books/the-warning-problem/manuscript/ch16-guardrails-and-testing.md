# Guardrails and Testing

Guardrails without testing are wishes. Testing without guardrails is theater. Nuclear warning AI needs both: rules that constrain behavior, and evaluations that try to break the rules' assumptions. The Nuclear Threat Initiative recommended research on fail-safe protocols and open publication where security allows. The Federation of American Scientists proposed joint risk-assessment frameworks across defense stakeholders for AI in NC3. Those proposals point in the right direction: treat AI near nuclear warning as a high-consequence engineered system, not as a consumer app with a security patch cycle.

Guardrails include provenance requirements, provisional labeling, confidence decay, semantic firewalls, shedding playbooks, human authority to slow, prohibition on generative numeric confidence, and independent review of dependency classes. Write them as testable requirements. If a requirement cannot be tested, it will not survive contact with a program manager's schedule.

Testing must include domain shift, deception, sensor loss, correlated faults, exercise-data leakage, spoofed multi-source confirmation, and automation-bias human-in-the-loop trials. Peacetime precision-recall on clean launches is a homework problem. Adversarial evaluation is the exam. Fund red teams that do not report to the shipping program's success metrics.

Continuous monitoring after deployment matters because models and environments drift. Distribution-shift alarms, canary inputs, and rollback drills belong in operations. A model that cannot be rolled back overnight is a hostage. Hostages make bad safety cases.

International technical cooperation on testing methods—without sharing sensitive detectors—can raise baselines. Shared taxonomies of false-alarm modes, shared lessons from non-nuclear high-consequence AI, and shared norms against automated nuclear launch are achievable even among rivals. Perfect verification of AI internals is not required to agree that certain uses are out of bounds.

Documentation is a guardrail. Training-data manifests, configuration pins, known failure cases, and decision-support limitation statements should travel with systems. Operators deserve manuals that admit brittleness. Marketing brochures are not manuals.

Governance should connect the Nuclear Weapons Council and digital AI authorities so that AI risks are evaluated beside traditional nuclear modernization decisions—as FAS suggested in spirit. Orphaned AI projects attached to NC3 without nuclear governance are how gaps form. Close the gaps with org charts that look bureaucratic because bureaucracy is how states remember danger.

Testing culture fails when failure is unpublishable inside the institution. Protect messengers who find flaws. Punish concealment. The false-alarm record exists because some people wrote things down. Continue the habit in the AI era.
