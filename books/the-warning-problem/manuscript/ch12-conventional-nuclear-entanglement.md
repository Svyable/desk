# Conventional-Nuclear Entanglement

Entanglement names a family of hazards in which conventional military operations interact with nuclear forces, command systems, or dual-capable weapons in ways that raise the risk of nuclear misinterpretation. Academic deterrence literature, including work associated with James Acton and Carnegie colleagues, has mapped pathways: attacks on dual-use warning and command nodes; dual-capable missiles whose launch signature looks nuclear-capable; co-location that makes conventional targeting resemble nuclear counterforce; and doctrinal statements that treat critical infrastructure attacks as potential nuclear thresholds. The map is not a prediction that entanglement must cause nuclear use. It is a warning that conventional war can generate nuclear-relevant pictures faster than political understanding can keep up—especially when AI accelerates the picturing.

Artificial intelligence enters entanglement as interpreter and accelerator. A fusion engine that freely mixes theater battle-damage assessment with strategic warning can move entanglement from geography into software. A model that predicts escalation risk can illuminate branches leaders might miss, or it can present a falsely precise ladder that becomes a script under stress. Escalation models are hypotheses about politics. They should be labeled as such. Software that hides the labeling will smuggle political theories into displays that look like sensor facts.

China's dual-capable systems, notably the DF-26 and discussions around related systems, have been central to Western warnings. Open analysis in venues such as the Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists has noted that Chinese nuclear experts have become more aware of entanglement risks even while disentanglement remains costly. Dual capability can be valued precisely because it complicates adversary targeting calculus. Stability advice that ignores that incentive will sound naive in Beijing and merely frustrated in Washington. AI that improves tracking of dual-capable units may sharpen the dilemma: better knowledge for one side, higher fear of preemption for the other.

Early-warning satellites and large phased-array radars are often dual-use by physics and by mission. They support nuclear warning and also conventional missile defense or air and maritime surveillance. In a conventional war, attacking those nodes can look like preparation to blind strategic warning before a nuclear strike. AI that rapidly classifies such attacks as "NC3 degradation indicative of nuclear preparation" can compress the path from conventional fight to nuclear fear. That classification may sometimes be right. It must not be automatic. Semantic firewalls and human review gates belong on that path.

United States discussions of interfacing nuclear command, control, and communications with joint all-domain command and control express an operational desire for connectivity. Connectivity improves awareness and can import conventional fog into nuclear assessment. Design documents should specify which conventional data classes may influence nuclear warning, under what human review, with what labels, and with what logging. Seamless should be treated as a hazard word until firebreaks are specified. Firebreaks are not sabotage of joint warfighting; they are how joint warfighting avoids becoming an accidental nuclear briefing.

Russia's signaling about critical infrastructure and escalation has kept entanglement salient in European scenarios. Conventional attacks on radars, satellite ground stations, or leadership networks could be read through a nuclear lens even when intended as conventional coercion. AI decision-support that war-games escalation may help leaders see off-ramps—or may normalize nuclear branching as just another menu. Treat menus that include nuclear branches with procedural solemnity: extra authentication, extra dissent, extra time where physics allows. Solemnity is a control.

Entanglement also lives in perception before hardware completes it. If one side believes the other can conventionally blind warning satellites or cyber-degrade NC3, it may adopt higher peacetime alert or launch-under-attack postures. AI-enabled concepts for finding and striking dual-use nodes worsen that fear even prior to reliable capability. Perceptions of entanglement can destabilize on timelines shorter than procurement. Crisis communication and restraint declarations about dedicated warning assets are therefore part of the military balance, not soft add-ons.

Mitigations are partial and still worth pursuing. Geographic and electronic disentanglement where feasible. Declaratory policies about non-targeting of dedicated early-warning satellites. Templates for explaining battle damage to dual-use nodes without sounding like nuclear preparation. AI tools that flag entanglement pathways for commanders rather than only proposing strikes on dual-use targets. Flagging pathways is a stabilizing use of machine assistance. Targeting recommendations that ignore pathway flags should be blocked from nuclear-adjacent displays.

Exercises should include conventional campaigns that accidentally stress nuclear warning networks, and should grade leaders on whether they leap to nuclear explanations too quickly. Speed toward nuclear framing is often failure of imagination about conventional explanations, sensor faults, and spoilers. Grade imagination. Promote it. Overseers who only ask about latency will receive latency. Oversters who ask about doubt management will receive doubt management. The reporting template is a weapon of cultural change. Rewrite the template. Then audit the numbers for gaming, because any metric will be gamed, and gaming detection is part of the control system rather than a reason to abandon measurement.

Treat flag preservation as interoperability. Fund the translators who keep meaning intact across classification seams. Confidence objects should expire when predicted corroboration fails to arrive. Display the expiry. Make expiry loud. A number that does not weaken under absent confirmation is not updating on evidence; it is defending its prior. In nuclear warning, defending a prior against the world is how priors become tragedies. Build decay into the estimator and into the interface so that operators cannot miss it.

Semantic firewalls between joint all-domain pictures and strategic nuclear assessment are not inefficiencies. They are firebreaks. Plant high-quality machine falsehoods in exercises. Reward detection. Publish sanitized lessons. If the only exercise stories are successful detections of planted attacks, crews learn that the machine is a hero. Heroes create deference. Deference is automation bias with a medal. Prefer stories in which the hero was wrong and the human was right—and the human was thanked.

Score vendors on auditability, ablation tooling, provisional labeling, and degraded-mode behavior. If the bake-off only measures peacetime precision-recall, the product will hide uncertainty because uncertainty looks like inaccuracy on naive scorecards. Rewrite the scorecard before the next contract option. Contracts are doctrine by other means.

Crisis communication templates for exercises, anomalous indications, and dual-use node damage should exist before they are needed. Templates are not scripts that eliminate politics; they are scaffolding that prevents the worst improvisations. Pair templates with authentication procedures so that messages can be trusted as coming from the claimed authority. A hotline that cannot authenticate under stress is a rumor pipe.

Integrity shedding should be as practiced as fire drills. First shed commercial enrichments, then secondary models, then advanced fusion, while retaining dual phenomenology and human authentication.
