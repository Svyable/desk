# Crisis Communication

Crisis communication is part of the warning system. Sensors measure. Fusion interprets. Communication between adversaries supplies meanings sensors cannot: intent, explanation, reassurance, threat, request for time. Hotlines, military-to-military contacts, and carefully authenticated messages are technologies of doubt management across borders. When they fail, each side is left alone with its screens.

Artificial intelligence changes crisis communication in ambivalent ways. It can draft clearer messages faster, translate, and check consistency with prior statements. It can also flood channels with generated content, deepfake audio of leaders, and rapid narrative warfare that makes authentication harder. The warning problem meets the authenticity problem. A center sure of its missile tracks may still be unsure whether the voice on the line is the claimed official.

Authentication procedures for leadership communications are therefore warning-adjacent infrastructure. They must work under stress, at night, across damaged networks, and without requiring a seminar. Prepositioned codes, challenge-response, and multi-path confirmation are old tools that remain essential as synthetic media improves. AI can help detect fakes; adversaries will improve fakes. Procedural authentication that does not depend on perfect detectors remains necessary.

Messages about exercises and tests reduce false warning risk if sent early and believed. Notification regimes in arms control historically served this function. Their erosion increases load on unilateral warning. Rebuilding notification habits—even informally—buys stability. AI scheduling assistants can help states remember to notify; they cannot create the political will to notify.

Explaining dual-use node damage in a conventional fight is a special communication problem. Silence may look like preparation for strategic blinding. Over-explaining may reveal vulnerabilities. Templates negotiated in peacetime can set expectations: what will be said, through which channel, with which authentication, when a radar site is struck. Without templates, improvisation under rage will default to worst-case narratives.

Allies need crisis communication among themselves as much as with adversaries. Divergent fusion pictures can split an alliance during a scare. Procedures to reconcile allied warning assessments quickly—and to elevate unresolved disagreement rather than paper over it—prevent synchronized false confidence. Disagreement among friends is information.

Public communication interacts with private warning. Leaks of high-confidence assessments can trap leaders. Guidance that keeps public statements conditional when private evidence is incomplete protects diplomatic off-ramps. AI tools that draft press lines from classified fusion tiles risk laundering confidence into public fact. Keep a human gate with authority to soften.
