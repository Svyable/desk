# Contradiction, Incentive and Claim Audit

Prime-gap mathematics has little of the classified-program disinformation problem present in some Desk books, but it has its own distortion incentives.

## Distortion risks

**Headline compression.** “Bounded gaps” is routinely paraphrased as “almost proved twin primes.” Reject that wording.

**Record worship.** A smaller explicit `H_1` upper bound can be newsworthy without representing proportional conceptual progress.

**Conditional laundering.** Bounds under Elliott–Halberstam, generalized Elliott–Halberstam or RH must never be quoted as unconditional.

**Computation laundering.** Enormous finite verification does not establish infinitude.

**Randomness language.** Cramér’s model and related probabilistic heuristics do not imply that primality is generated randomly.

**Priority simplification.** Zhang, Maynard, Tao and Polymath occupy different roles; do not compress the story into one lone-genius or crowd-genius myth.

## Contradictions to preserve

- Global prime density is smooth while local short-scale behavior can fluctuate more than naive models predict.
- Primes become sparse on average while bounded clusters recur infinitely often.
- Prime deserts can be engineered arithmetically while random models still provide useful extreme-value intuition.
- A numerically weak unconditional theorem can be epistemically stronger than a sharp conditional theorem.
- More computation can strengthen evidence without reducing the logical distance to an infinite statement.

These are productive tensions, not inconsistencies to edit away.
