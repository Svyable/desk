# Few and Far Between

## The hunt for the patterns hiding in prime gaps

**Sven Hardy Benson**

Prime numbers thin as the number line expands, yet they refuse to thin smoothly. Some crowd into bounded neighborhoods. Others abandon intervals far larger than the average spacing. Between those two extremes sits one of mathematics’ most revealing unfinished arguments: how much of the apparent disorder of the primes is governed by laws we can state precisely but still cannot prove?

This book follows the hunt from Hardy–Littlewood and Cramér through sieve theory, GPY, Yitang Zhang’s bounded-gap breakthrough, Maynard–Tao, Polymath, and the modern theory of large prime deserts.

The evidence classes used here are intentionally stricter than a normal popular-mathematics narrative:

1. **proved mathematics** — peer-reviewed theorems or standard established results;
2. **rigorous conditional mathematics** — results proved from explicitly named unproved hypotheses such as Elliott–Halberstam;
3. **computational evidence and serious heuristics** — finite verification, numerical experiments, probabilistic models, and conjectural asymptotics;
4. **speculative synthesis** — interpretations about what future methods may reveal, kept separate from mathematical claims.

A theorem is not promoted into a conjecture. A conjecture is not demoted into “probably basically proved.” A record computation is finite. A model is a model.

The book’s central claim is simpler: prime gaps are one of the cleanest places to watch mathematics separate appearance from proof.
