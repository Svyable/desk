# The Headcount Illusion — 2026 capability counterevidence

Research note for Chapter 4 of *Austerity Measures*. Checked 9 September 2026.

## Current UK policy response

### Cabinet Office, 21 January 2026

https://www.gov.uk/government/news/move-fast-fix-things-darren-jones-sets-out-plan-to-rewire-whitehall-and-incentivise-innovation-in-the-civil-service

The government announced a National School of Government and Public Services intended to bring more training in-house, align civil-service learning with priorities including AI, and reduce external training spending over time. The same announcement says government plans to halve spending on external consultants and reduce departmental administration costs by 16% over five years, with stated savings of more than £2 billion a year by 2030.

Use as counterevidence to any static claim that government is simply substituting contractors for staff. The current policy direction explicitly attempts to reduce external dependence while strengthening internal capability. Whether the targets are achieved is an empirical question; the announcement itself is evidence of institutional recognition of the problem, not proof of success.

### Government Skills, updated 31 March 2026

https://www.gov.uk/guidance/digital-and-data-learning

https://www.gov.uk/guidance/artificial-intelligence-skills-for-all

Government Skills now provides a cross-government digital, data, innovation and AI learning offer. The AI curriculum ranges from general awareness through practitioner material including machine learning, computer vision, NLP and working with large language models.

This supports the narrower manuscript claim that an AI-capable state requires distributed user competence as well as a smaller cadre of specialists. Training is not equivalent to operational capability, and course availability does not establish uptake or proficiency.

### Government Digital and Data Profession Capability Framework, current 2026

https://ddat-capability-framework.service.gov.uk/

The framework remains an active workforce-planning instrument for digital and data roles. It was updated in August 2026, including expanded treatment of the data and AI ethicist role. Its September 2026 roadmap explicitly links the framework to recruitment, development, capability assessment and workforce planning.

This is useful because it makes "capability" administratively concrete: roles, skills, levels, assessment and workforce planning rather than a vague plea to retain more people.

### Cloud Challenge Book 2026, 7 July 2026

https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/cloud-challenge-book-2026/cloud-challenge-book-2026

The Government Digital Service and Government Commercial Agency describe cloud, AI expertise, resilient infrastructure and commercial capability as linked national-scale challenges. The publication repeats the objective for one in ten civil servants to be digital, data and cyber professionals.

Again, treat the document as a statement of policy and procurement intent, not evidence that the capability objective has already been achieved.

## Strongest counterargument

The chapter should not imply that internal capability is synonymous with permanent headcount. A government can legitimately rely on external specialists, shared platforms, standardized commodity services and industry partnerships while improving its own ability to specify, evaluate and switch suppliers. Current UK policy itself mixes external partnership with internal upskilling.

The sharper test is functional: after outsourcing and automation, can the public institution still specify the service, evaluate claims about it, inspect outcomes, contest failures, preserve evidence, switch suppliers and recover during disruption? The right denominator is not employees retained. It is operational independence retained per unit of public cost.
