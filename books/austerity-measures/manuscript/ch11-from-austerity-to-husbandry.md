# From Austerity to Husbandry

There is an older word for the discipline we need.

Husbandry.

To husband a resource is to use it carefully while preserving its productive capacity.

You prune. You repair. You replace. You invest. You remove what no longer works. You do not preserve every branch because it once grew there, and you do not confuse eating the seed corn with reducing agricultural overhead.

Public institutions should be managed with the same distinction.

Routine cognitive work should be automated aggressively where the evidence supports it and the risks are manageable. Savings should be visible. So should the condition of the institution that produced them.

Measure the backlog.

Measure service quality.

Measure repeat contacts and appeals.

Measure whether poor citizens experience the same improvement as rich ones.

Measure dependence on single suppliers.

Measure whether data can leave a vendor’s platform in a usable form.

Measure how long it takes to change a policy and propagate the change through software.

Measure whether staff can explain the system they are accountable for.

Measure whether the agency can detect when its own AI is wrong.

Measure maintenance, not just launch.

The exact metrics will differ by institution. The principle is that the productivity account and the capability account must be read together.

A government that saves ten million pounds and loses the ability to renegotiate a hundred-million-pound contract did not become efficient.

A government that removes five hundred clerical roles while reducing errors, improving access, shortening waits and preserving meaningful appeal may have become much more efficient.

Headcount does not settle the question.

Neither does spending.

Capability is the object being managed.
