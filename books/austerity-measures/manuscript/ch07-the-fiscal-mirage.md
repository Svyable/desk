# The Fiscal Mirage

There is a recurring trick in public finance: move a cost somewhere else and call it a saving. Close a preventive service and a later crisis appears in another department. Delay maintenance and a replacement project arrives under a capital budget years later. Reduce legal support and cases reach court less prepared. Cut planning capacity and developments take longer while private applicants pay consultants to navigate the delay. The spreadsheet has borders. Reality does not.

AI will create new opportunities for this kind of accounting illusion because the immediate gains can be unusually legible. Headcount, processing time and cost per transaction can all fall while appeals, repeat contacts, abandoned claims and unusual cases become more expensive elsewhere. The institution may also lose the ability to change the system later, while vendor costs rise after an introductory contract expires.

A chatbot can reduce telephone calls and still increase the total amount of citizen time required to resolve a problem. An automated eligibility system can reduce processing cost while increasing the burden on appeal tribunals. A predictive maintenance system can make inspection cheaper while creating an expensive blind spot if the model does not recognize a new failure mode. A platform can reduce internal technical staffing and later make migration so difficult that the government effectively pays rent on its own dependence.

The accounting unit is the problem, not automation itself. A department can optimize its own budget while making the wider state more expensive. Government can optimize its administrative cost while transferring the cost to citizens. A minister can collect a saving this year while a successor receives the failure later.

The danger is not only bad AI. Excellent AI can produce the same result when the accounting stops too soon.
