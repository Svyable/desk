# Contradictions and incentive audit

## Central contradictions to preserve

- **Electromagnetic earthquake physics is real in some contexts; predictive electromagnetism is not established.** Parkfield recorded coseismic electric / magnetic effects without a detected short-term precursor.
- **A famous positive can weaken without the entire domain becoming impossible.** Corralitos is strongly challenged by instrument-history reanalysis; that constrains later single-station claims but does not prove every future feature is instrumental.
- **A statistical association can survive and remain useless as a warning.** The 2022 Google/QuakeFinder result is the cleanest example in this manuscript.
- **A plausible mechanism is not a field validation.** Positive-hole, electrokinetic, piezomagnetic, and atmospheric-coupling models each have physical ingredients without establishing prospective earthquake prediction.
- **A government mission demonstrates interest, not efficacy.** DEMETER and CSES are documented programs; their existence cannot be converted into institutional proof that the precursor hypothesis is true.
- **A negative operational conclusion does not imply physical silence.** USGS skepticism toward prediction is compatible with continued research into crustal electric / magnetic processes.

## Incentives and failure modes

### Precursor researchers
- publication and program continuity reward positive anomalies;
- mission / network identity can become tied to the phenomenon being real;
- flexible event windows and conditionality can unintentionally protect a hypothesis from failure;
- public-safety potential creates pressure to overinterpret modest associations.

### Skeptical institutions
- false alarms carry reputational and economic costs;
- agencies can prefer strong categorical language that protects the public from unsupported prediction claims;
- a hardened negative consensus can make small but serious anomalous results easier to dismiss than to replicate.

### Commercial / privately funded networks
- a successful prediction technology would have enormous value;
- long-term private funding can support experiments mainstream programs abandoned;
- the same ambition creates a reason to insist on independent scoring and open raw data.

### Governments
- earthquake monitoring has direct civil-defense value even if short-term prediction fails;
- electromagnetic / ionospheric sensors also support space-weather, navigation, communications, and Earth-science objectives;
- national investment therefore cannot be interpreted as secret confirmation of efficacy.

### Public and media
- successful-case stories such as Haicheng are memorable;
- null years and false alarms are not;
- phrases like '10× increased risk' can obscure a tiny absolute probability;
- 'scientists cannot predict earthquakes' can be misheard as 'nothing changes before earthquakes.'

## Disinformation / secrecy posture

This book found no need to invoke hidden classified precursor capability to explain the public record. State interest, space missions, and civil-defense research are documented openly enough to evaluate the basic scientific question. Any allegation of a secret operational earthquake-prediction capability would therefore require evidence beyond the existence of government monitoring programs or classified geophysical work.
