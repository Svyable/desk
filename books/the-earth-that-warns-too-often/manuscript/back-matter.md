# Back Matter

## What would change the conclusion

The book's central claim is deliberately vulnerable.

A strong prospective failure would move it toward skepticism: long-running networks in multiple tectonic settings, sensitive enough to detect known coseismic electric or magnetic effects, could apply frozen precursor algorithms to hidden future data and find no reproducible probability gain beyond standard seismicity forecasts. Repeated results of that kind would turn much of the older anomaly literature into historical evidence about false positives rather than evidence about earthquake preparation.

A strong prospective success would move it the other way: a predeclared feature, developed on one interval or region, could reproduce on independent future earthquakes with comparable effect size, calibrated probabilities, manageable false-alarm burden, and physically coherent scaling with distance, magnitude, geology, or frequency. Replication across two independently operated networks would matter much more than another famous case.

A mechanism would need still more. A positive-hole model should predict material and spatial dependencies. An electrokinetic model should predict relationships to fluids and pressure. A lithosphere-atmosphere-ionosphere model should predict intermediate ground or atmospheric changes rather than only a satellite anomaly. A theory that can accommodate every sign, window, channel, and tectonic setting after the fact has not explained enough to be useful.

## A note on prediction

The word is used narrowly here. The U.S. Geological Survey's practical distinction is a good one: a true earthquake prediction specifies time, place, and magnitude. Earthquake early warning is different. It detects rupture after it begins and uses the finite travel time of seismic waves to warn locations farther away. Probabilistic forecasts are different again.

Much of the literature discussed in this book concerns candidate precursors, not validated predictions.

## Source practice

The source trail in `research/` favors peer-reviewed primary papers, USGS and space-agency records, mission papers, contemporaneous scientific disputes, and later reanalyses. Popular summaries are used sparingly and never to upgrade an uncertain claim.

Historical cases are separated from later interpretations. The fact that VAN telegrams existed does not establish VAN's predictive skill. The fact that the Corralitos magnetometer recorded an unusual trace does not establish that the trace came from Loma Prieta preparation. The fact that France launched DEMETER and China launched CSES establishes institutional scientific interest, not efficacy. The fact that the 2022 Google/QuakeFinder case-control study found a held-out association establishes a result that needs replication, not an earthquake warning system.

## The narrow claim left standing

After the famous failures are removed, one question remains worth the instruments:

**Do defined electromagnetic or allied measurements provide reproducible prospective information about near-term earthquake probability beyond conventional seismic and geodetic inputs?**

The answer is not yet known.

That is a better place to end than pretending it is.
