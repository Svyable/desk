# The Earth That Warns Too Often

## Earthquake Precursors, Electric Signals, and the Prediction War

**Sven Hardy Benson**

Earthquakes do not arrive from nowhere. Faults load, fluids move, rocks fracture, electrical properties change, small earthquakes cluster, and the atmosphere above an active region is never perfectly isolated from the ground below. For more than a century, scientists and governments have tried to turn those physical changes into warnings.

Most attempts have failed.

Some failed spectacularly. Some dissolved into sensor faults or retrospective selection. Some remain disputed after decades. A few modern statistical results refuse to disappear cleanly, yet remain far too weak to tell a city that the ground will move tomorrow.

That uncomfortable middle is the subject of this book.

This is not a book claiming that earthquakes can presently be predicted in the strong sense of specifying time, location, and magnitude. They cannot. It is also not a book claiming that the crust must therefore be physically silent before rupture. Those propositions are different, and confusing them has distorted both the enthusiasts and the skeptics.

The working thesis is narrower: **some earthquakes may be preceded by measurable electromagnetic or allied changes, but a physical antecedent, a statistical association, a calibrated forecast, and a public warning are four different achievements.** A signal can survive as physics and fail as technology.

The evidence is handled in four classes throughout:

1. **Verified / declassified / documented facts** — instrument records, official mission histories, published experiments, contemporaneous prediction records, and established earthquake observations.
2. **Credible but incomplete evidence** — serious peer-reviewed associations, physically plausible interpretations, and historical reports whose record is real but not decisive.
3. **Disputed or unconfirmed claims** — operational prediction claims, retrospective anomaly interpretations, and proposed precursor classes that have not survived independent prospective validation.
4. **Speculative synthesis / open conjecture** — ideas used to generate tests, including the possibility that faults produce many genuine preparation-like signals while only a small fraction of preparations commit to damaging rupture.

No testimony, institutional affiliation, satellite mission, patent, government program, or published anomaly is treated as proof of predictive efficacy merely because it exists.

The standard is harder than that.
