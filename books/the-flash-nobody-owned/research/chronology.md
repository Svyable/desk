# Chronology

- **5 Aug 1963** — Limited Test Ban Treaty signed.
- **10 Oct 1963** — treaty enters into force.
- **17 Oct 1963** — first Vela pair launched.
- **2 Jul 1967** — earliest event later recognized as a gamma-ray burst recorded by Vela spacecraft.
- **1969–1972** — Vela 5/6 record the 16 events used in the discovery paper.
- **16 Mar 1973** — Klebesadel, Strong and Olson submit GRB discovery paper.
- **1 Jun 1973** — paper published in *The Astrophysical Journal*.
- **1973** — IMP-6 and other observations independently confirm key features of cosmic gamma-ray bursts.
- **1977** — U.S./Soviet intelligence attention to apparent South African Kalahari nuclear-test preparations; international pressure follows.
- **19 Jun 1979** — Vela 5B science archive nominally ends; old Vela assets remain part of the broader sensor history.
- **22 Sep 1979, 00:53 UTC** — Vela 6911 records the disputed double flash.
- **Oct 1979** — U.S. searches for corroborating evidence; internal assessments consider a low-yield nuclear event.
- **1–2 Nov 1979** — Ruina panel begins formal review.
- **9 Nov 1979** — White House report records panel skepticism and a possible meteoroid explanation; Arecibo disturbance noted as a lead.
- **Dec 1979** — interagency intelligence memorandum analyzes possible perpetrators conditionally.
- **9 Jan 1980** — White House mini-SCC consensus recorded as “we don't know what happened.”
- **Feb 1980** — State Department guidance reflects provisional non-confirmation; Carter privately records growing belief in Israeli involvement.
- **May 1980** — Ruina panel completes report, judging the signal probably not nuclear.
- **6 Jun 1980** — Frank Press summarizes panel reasoning while acknowledging dissent from DIA, CIA nuclear specialists and DOE laboratories.
- **17 Jun 1980** — State/INR memorandum records NRL hydroacoustic interpretation and DIA dissent.
- **2017** — Wright & De Geer publish modern optical reanalysis challenging the meteoroid hypothesis.
- **2018** — companion work argues hydroacoustic and radionuclide evidence strengthens the nuclear interpretation.
- **2023** — fiftieth anniversary of the GRB discovery highlights Vela’s accidental scientific legacy.

## Chronology warning

Later evidence must not be back-projected into what officials knew in September–November 1979. The manuscript should preserve the information set available at each decision point.
