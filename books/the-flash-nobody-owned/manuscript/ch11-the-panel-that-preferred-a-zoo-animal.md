# 11 — The Panel That Preferred a Zoo Animal

Frank Press, President Carter’s science adviser, convened an expert panel chaired by MIT professor Jack Ruina. Its job was explicitly technical: assess whether the September 22 signal was a nuclear explosion, a natural phenomenon or an instrumental false alarm, and review corroborating evidence.

The panel became skeptical of the nuclear interpretation.

A November 1979 White House report said the group had found non-nuclear bhangmeter events with some similarities to nuclear signatures and considered it possible that a meteoroid event could have produced the September signal. The report also emphasized differences between the September trace and previous nuclear signals and the absence, at that stage, of decisive corroboration.

By 1980 the panel’s bottom line was that the event was probably not a nuclear explosion and more likely belonged to the family of unexplained “zoo events.”

The phrase is revealing. A zoo is where anomalies go after they have been given a fence but not an explanation.

The panel did not prove a meteoroid struck Vela. It elevated that class of natural/instrumental explanation as more likely than a nuclear event.

That distinction matters because later polemics often convert the panel’s probabilistic judgment into a solved mechanism. The historical record shows something weaker: the panel believed the optical discrepancies plus lack of contemporaneous corroboration reduced the nuclear hypothesis enough that a natural explanation became preferable.

Yet other U.S. technical and intelligence organizations disagreed. Declassified memoranda show officials worried the panel’s conclusion would be perceived as a political whitewash. Frank Press himself acknowledged in June 1980 that key officials at DIA, CIA’s Nuclear Intelligence Panel and the DOE weapons laboratories continued to think the signal indicated a nuclear event, largely because of its close resemblance to known nuclear signatures.

This is not proof that the panel was politically manipulated.

It is proof that expert consensus did not exist.

The distinction is everything.
