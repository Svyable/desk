# Back Matter

## Evidence note

*Calendar Scars* uses “Miyake event” as a convenient observational term, not as proof that every abrupt annual radiocarbon excursion belongs to one physical class. The manuscript gives the greatest weight to globally replicated events supported by independent cosmogenic radionuclides, and treats weaker or incompletely replicated excursions as candidates unless the literature supports stronger language.

An exact tree-ring year should also be read narrowly. A terminal growth year can date felling when the outer edge survives. Archaeological context is still required to relate felling to construction, occupation, destruction, repair, voyage, or cultural change.

## Acknowledgment of uncertainty

The field is moving quickly. Event catalogs, particle-fluence reconstructions, annual calibration datasets, and the dating of candidate excursions will change. Future editions should prefer updated primary measurements over inherited event lists, and should retain published corrections rather than silently harmonizing them.
