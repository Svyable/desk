# Calendar Scars

## Miyake Events, Cosmic Storms, and the Years Trees Refuse to Forget

**Sven Hardy Benson**

Some dates survive because somebody wrote them down. Others survive because the sky altered the chemistry of a tree.

This book is about the second kind.

The term *Miyake event* is used here for abrupt, anomalous increases in cosmogenic radiocarbon recorded at annual resolution in tree rings, especially the verified extreme events that can be cross-checked against other cosmogenic radionuclides. It is not a claim that every reported radiocarbon excursion shares one physical cause. Where the literature distinguishes a verified extreme solar particle event from a candidate radiocarbon production event, so does the manuscript.

Dates before the Common Era are written BCE. Because papers sometimes label the same radiocarbon transition by the year before the increase, the year of the increase, or a two-year pair, the text preserves the convention most useful to the specific evidence and explains apparent one-year discrepancies when they matter. That nuisance turns out to be part of the story.
