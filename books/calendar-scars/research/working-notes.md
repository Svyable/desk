# Working Notes

## Reader promise

Show how rare cosmic-radiation events became two things at once: a proxy record of solar extremes outside modern instrumentation and an exact-year synchronization tool for archaeology. The book should keep returning to the collision between physical uncertainty at the Sun and chronological precision in wood.

## Distinct contribution

This is not a generic space-weather book, a radiocarbon-dating primer, or a Viking chronology book. Its object is the **calendar scar**: a globally distributed cosmogenic production event whose chemistry can synchronize otherwise disconnected annual archives and whose existence simultaneously extends the empirical tail of solar energetic-particle behavior.

## Strongest competing explanation

The method may remain niche. Suitable annual wood is uncommon, known events are sparse, destructive AMS measurements are expensive, candidate-event classification is difficult at smaller amplitudes, and archaeological association can dominate the final uncertainty. Conventional dendrochronology, Bayesian radiocarbon models, stratigraphy, and historical texts may remain more important for most sites.

For the hazard half, the strongest counterargument is that cosmogenic fluence is not a direct proxy for geomagnetic-storm severity. Modern grid-collapse rhetoric can therefore exceed what the ancient isotope record supports.

## Four evidence classes

Use `evidence-matrix.md` as the controlling ledger. In prose:

1. **Verified/documented:** annual 14C excursions, global replication, multi-radionuclide support, published archaeological exact-date demonstrations.
2. **Credible but incomplete:** inferred particle spectra and fluence, recurrence distribution, source physics, historical auroral associations.
3. **Disputed/not established:** weak candidate events, precise flare/CME reconstruction, direct event-to-historical-catastrophe claims, deterministic future damage.
4. **Speculative synthesis:** a global annual 14C atlas as chronological infrastructure; archive-wide synchronization; long-horizon engineering implications.

## Claims requiring continued audit

- Do not call 774 “ten times Carrington” without naming the measured quantity and source.
- Do not state a fixed recurrence interval as if events were periodic.
- Keep 774/775 and 993/994 naming conventions explicit.
- Preserve the 2013 one-year corrigendum.
- For Por-Bajin, separate isotope marker → felling year/season → construction inference → Manichaean historical interpretation.
- For L’Anse aux Meadows, say Norse wood cutting occurred in 1021; do not imply the entire occupation began or ended that year.
- For Dispilio, the final master-sequence ring is 5140 BCE; do not equate that automatically with settlement abandonment.
- Treat historical aurora reports as independent contextual evidence, not confirmation required for the cosmogenic event.

## Expansion priorities before publication gate

1. Deep-read the original 2012 and 2013 Miyake papers and reproduce the measurement logic, sample chronology, and initial source hypotheses without relying on later summaries.
2. Build an event-by-event table with year convention, raw Δ14C, independent replication, 10Be/36Cl status, inferred >30 MeV and >200 MeV fluence where reputable estimates exist, and confidence class.
3. Recover full supplementary methods for COSMIC 2018 to deepen ring-boundary, sample preparation, hemispheric transport, and event-season reconstruction.
4. Deep-read Por-Bajin supplementary material and archaeological site history; distinguish exactly what was known before the 2020 date.
5. Deep-read the L’Anse aux Meadows supplementary data: sample IDs, tool-mark criteria, waney-edge evidence, per-tree counts, laboratory replication, and occupation-duration counterevidence.
6. Reconstruct Ribe’s stratigraphic and trade chronology from the full annual-dating model rather than using it as a generic “trade came earlier” example.
7. Deep-read Dispilio’s 115-measurement dataset, master chronology construction, wiggle-match prior, and sampling strategy.
8. Add a serious Eastern Mediterranean chronology chapter case only where an actual known/candidate cosmogenic marker intersects a relevant timber sequence; do not imply Thera is solved by Miyake dating.
9. Build a historical-aurora source table with original-language/source provenance and control years before retaining any specific 774/993 chronicle examples.
10. Build a modern hazard matrix separating SEP radiation, solar radio, ionospheric disturbance, satellite effects, geomagnetic induction, aviation, astronauts, and grid exposure.
11. Add engineering sources from NOAA/SWPC, NASA, ESA, power-grid standards, radiation transport, and satellite anomaly literature.
12. Audit the 2026 event catalog against the newest peer-reviewed literature before final copy; the field is moving too quickly to freeze inherited lists.

## Prose audit

The draft deliberately uses recurring concrete objects—ring, beam, bark edge, accelerator—but must avoid turning “the tree did not know the year” into a repeated slogan. During revision, cut any recurrence that does not advance the argument. Reduce standalone aphoristic sentences where they begin to accumulate. Keep event cases deep rather than adding a rapid catalog of every candidate spike.

## Defeat conditions

The book’s central synthesis should be softened substantially if strong interlaboratory work finds canonical event timing is not globally coherent at annual scale; if major archaeological demonstrations fail replication; if background annual 14C structure makes event identification materially less reliable than current showcase cases suggest; or if event-source modelling shows the proxy-inferred particle tail is not meaningfully outside direct modern experience after consistent geomagnetic and production corrections.
