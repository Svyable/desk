# Source Log

## Source policy

The manuscript treats annually resolved isotope measurements and independently dated archaeological sequences as the hard center. Historical chronicles are corroborating context, not detectors. Modern hazard estimates are scenario-dependent extrapolations, not records of what a future event will do.

## Research cautions

- Do not describe the 774/775 event as “the Carrington event times X.” Solar energetic-particle fluence, flare radiative output, coronal-mass-ejection properties, and geomagnetic-storm severity are related but not interchangeable observables.
- Do not use a historical aurora report as proof that a specific isotope excursion came from the Sun. The isotope stack carries the causal weight.
- Do not call every abrupt annual radiocarbon variation a verified Miyake event. Candidate events require replication and, ideally, independent radionuclide support.
- Do not imply that exact-year dating works on arbitrary archaeological charcoal. It requires appropriate sequential material, usually tree rings, a recoverable event signature, and enough context to connect the dated growth to the human event of interest.
- Preserve the 2013 corrigendum as an instructive example: a missing ring shifted the second event by a year. This is evidence for why replication and cross-dating matter, not evidence that the method is useless.
