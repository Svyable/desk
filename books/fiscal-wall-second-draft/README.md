*When Cheap Money Ends*

This second full draft of *Fiscal Wall* begins away from the auction room. It starts with the ordinary human meaning of refinancing: the payment changes, the budget tightens, and choices that once appeared independent suddenly compete. From there it moves into the federal balance sheet and asks what a nation experiences when debt accumulated across a long low-rate era is repeatedly repriced into a more expensive world.

The argument is not that the federal government is a household. It is that duration, refinancing, and interest costs create tradeoffs in every balance sheet, even when the issuer is a sovereign with its own currency. This version follows those tradeoffs through retirement portfolios, banks, insurers, cities, inflation, taxes, growth, fiscal politics, and the dollar's role as a global safe asset. It is a civic-economic narrative rather than a market-plumbing narrative, designed to make the consequences legible without turning sovereign finance into household-budget metaphor.
