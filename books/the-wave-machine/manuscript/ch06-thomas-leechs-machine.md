# Thomas Leech's Machine

Thomas Leech, an engineering professor at Auckland, is the human hinge of Project Seal. Wartime secrecy transformed an academic engineer into the custodian of a problem whose solution, if it existed, would have been grotesquely consequential.

There is a tendency in secret-history writing to make the scientist either Faust or fool. Leech needs neither costume. The surviving history is more interesting. He appears to have treated the problem as an engineering program: instrumentation, experimental series, analysis, and postwar technical work.

The later final report matters because it separates Seal from rumor-driven weapons lore. A report called *The Generation of Wave Systems* is described in archival and historical accounts as tabulating and analyzing the project data. That document deserves line-by-line treatment in a later manuscript pass. Until then, this draft refuses to manufacture precision from secondhand summaries.

That restraint is not timidity. It is the only way to keep the real project visible underneath the mythology that accumulated around it.
