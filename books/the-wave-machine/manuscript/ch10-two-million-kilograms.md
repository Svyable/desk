# Two Million Kilograms

The most repeated Project Seal engineering claim is that an array totaling roughly two million kilograms of explosive, divided among multiple charges and placed several miles offshore, might generate a destructive wave on the order of ten meters at a suitable coast.

In this draft that number is evidence class two: historically reported, technically consequential, and awaiting direct verification against the original report before it is promoted into class one.

Even taken provisionally, it reveals the concept's operational problem. Two million kilograms is not a magic bomb. It is a maritime construction project conducted under hostile observation. Charges must be transported, emplaced, held at useful depths, spaced according to a target geometry, and detonated with the required synchronization. If the source is sensitive to depth, the sea state and bottom become engineering tolerances.

A strategic weapon can be physically possible and militarily absurd.

That category is underused in discussions of black technology. People ask whether something can work, as though the first laboratory yes carries it directly into an arsenal. Between possibility and capability sits manufacturing, deployment, reliability, countermeasures, signatures, and opportunity cost.

Seal may be most useful as a study of that middle territory.
