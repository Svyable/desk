# The War Ends First

Project Seal lost urgency as the Pacific war changed. This mundane fact is more explanatory than the mythology built around abandoned secret projects.

Military R&D is not a tournament in which every physically interesting concept proceeds until nature gives a final verdict. Wars end. Sponsors move. Better weapons arrive. Targets disappear. Logistics become intolerable. A program can stop with unanswered scientific questions because the operational question has already been answered by history.

The atomic bomb also changed the comparison. A device that could be delivered by aircraft and release extraordinary energy in a compact package made a coastline-specific array of underwater charges look less attractive even if the wave concept retained physical interest.

Abandonment therefore does not prove failure in every technical sense. Nor does it imply hidden success. It means the public record needs a more careful verb: the program ended.

Conspiracy narratives dislike this ambiguity because it is not dramatic enough. Engineering lives in it.
