# Bikini in the Background

After the war, the nuclear age put vastly larger underwater explosions within reach. Bikini Atoll became an accidental laboratory for questions adjacent to Seal: what does an enormous explosion in or near water actually do?

Historical reporting on Seal describes American interest in connecting Leech's work with observations from the Bikini tests and records contact involving Karl Compton. That does not mean nuclear testing validated a practical tsunami weapon. It means the scientific question remained interesting.

Underwater nuclear tests produced spectacular water columns, base surges, shock, contamination, and complex wave fields. Spectacle again tempts the eye to overestimate far-field tsunami efficiency.

A mushrooming column of water is not a continental-scale long wave.

The distinction is almost the whole book in miniature: enormous local violence can coexist with disappointing coupling into the mode a strategic concept requires.
