## Chapter 13 — The Small-State Advantage

**Material claim reviewed:** cheaper analysis, translation, retrieval, drafting, monitoring, and institutional memory can lower the minimum staffing threshold for some diplomatic functions, potentially giving small foreign services disproportionate gains while leaving frontier capability, classified data, cyber defense, talent, and vendor dependence as scale advantages.

**Current evidence:**

- Singapore Foreign Minister Vivian Balakrishnan gave unusually direct practitioner evidence on May 16, 2026: he described personal use of LLMs and an agent for analysis, abstraction, brief and speech drafting, question preparation, and managing the cognitive load of diplomatic travel. He also explicitly framed security and open-source boundaries as constraints. Source: https://www.mfa.gov.sg/newsroom/press-statements-transcripts-and-photos/minister-for-foreign-affairs-dr-vivian-balakrishnan-s-speech-at-ai-engineer-singapore--16-may-2026/
- In March 2026 Balakrishnan described AI as a way for Singapore to transcend constraints as a small city-state while stressing trusted ecosystems and national coordination. Source: https://www.mfa.gov.sg/newsroom/press-statements-transcripts-and-photos/remarks-by-minister-for-foreign-affairs-dr-vivian-balakrishnan-at-the-korea-singapore-ai-connect-summit--2-march-2026/
- Estonia’s 2026 Eesti.ai initiative provides a second small-state adoption case at the national level, but it is not evidence of foreign-ministry or diplomatic performance. Source: https://estonia.ee/stories/estonia-launches-ai-initiative-to-double-productivity

**Boundary / counterevidence:** these cases do not prove a general small-state diplomatic advantage, a staffing multiplier, or improved foreign-policy outcomes. Large states obtain the same tools with additional advantages in data, compute, classified access, cyber defense, procurement leverage, and talent. The manuscript says *possible* and explicitly preserves those counterforces.

**Release judgment:** no prose correction required. The chapter presents a conditional organizational mechanism, not a measured cross-country law.

## Chapter 14 — The Authoritarian Edge?

**Material claim reviewed:** centralized systems may gain real adoption-speed advantages through fewer veto points, concentrated procurement, data access, and mandated standards, while information distortion, common-mode errors, weak upward dissent, and political incentives can reduce the quality of correction.

**Evidence boundary:** the current chapter deliberately avoids claiming that China, Russia, democracies, or any other regime type has been empirically proven superior at AI-enabled statecraft. It treats the authoritarian-edge thesis as a mechanism to test and gives the strongest case for speed before giving the strongest case against epistemic quality. Current national AI programs and surveillance capacity show that centralized adoption is feasible; they do not isolate regime type as the causal variable for strategic performance.

**Counterevidence:** democratic pluralism does not guarantee truth or speed, and centralization does not guarantee distortion. The chapter expressly records pathologies on both sides and makes error correction, not regime branding, the key criterion.

**Release judgment:** no prose correction required. A stronger comparative empirical claim would require evidence the manuscript does not make.

## Chapter 15 — The Protocols of Trust

**Material claim reviewed:** rivals can create narrow procedural confidence without broad political trust through authenticated channels, incident rules, notifications, rehearsed failure behavior, and redundant communication paths.

**Historical and institutional evidence:**

- Existing source `ch01-002` documents the 1963 Washington–Moscow direct communications link after the Cuban Missile Crisis.
- The 1972 U.S.–Soviet Incidents at Sea Agreement required collision-avoidance practices, advance notice for some hazardous activity, incident reporting through naval attachés, and recurring implementation meetings. The agreement is a direct precedent for narrow procedural risk reduction between rivals without broader trust. Source: https://2009-2017.state.gov/t/isn/4791.htm
- Existing C2PA sources `ch03-004` and `ch03-005` support the narrower digital-provenance point: signatures and provenance can authenticate origin/history without proving truth, authority, or intent.

**Boundary / counterevidence:** historical hotlines and maritime agreements do not prove that proposed AI-era notification rules will work. Protocols can fail under compromise, political incentives, ambiguous authority, stale contact information, or deliberate exploitation. The chapter makes those failure modes central rather than incidental.

**Release judgment:** no prose correction required. The chapter uses established confidence-building mechanisms as analogues and clearly labels AI-era protocols as design proposals.

## Chapter 16 — AI Arms Control Without AI Arms Control

**Material claim reviewed:** comprehensive control of a general-purpose, rapidly changing, dual-use technology may be difficult, but states can still pursue narrower behavioral and procedural risk-reduction measures around military AI, authentication, human responsibility, testing, incidents, and crisis communication.

**Current authoritative evidence:**

- The U.S.-initiated Political Declaration on Responsible Military Use of AI and Autonomy establishes non-binding practices covering legal review, senior oversight, human responsibility, auditing, training, testing, safeguards, and deactivation of systems displaying unintended behavior. Source: https://2021-2025.state.gov/political-declaration-on-responsible-military-use-of-artificial-intelligence-and-autonomy/
- The UN General Assembly adopted resolution A/RES/80/58 in December 2025 on AI in the military domain and international peace and security, demonstrating broad multilateral engagement while not creating a comprehensive arms-control regime. Source: https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/4094455
- The CCW Group of Governmental Experts on lethal autonomous weapon systems held both March and August–September 2026 sessions to continue work on elements of an instrument and other measures. Source: https://transcripts.un.org/asset/k17/k17gugmjrx
- UN discussions in June 2026 also continued specifically on AI in the military domain and use of force. Source: https://indico.un.org/event/1023736/sessions/156128/

**Boundary / counterevidence:** these processes establish active norm-building and negotiation, not successful verification, reciprocal restraint, or a settled definition of military AI. The Political Declaration is non-binding; the CCW process remains under negotiation. The chapter’s “risk-reduction islands” are proposals, not claims that those agreements already exist.

**Release judgment:** no prose correction required. The manuscript is explicit about verification difficulty and treats declarations as reference points rather than enforcement mechanisms.

## Chapter 17 — The New Diplomat

**Material claim reviewed:** as routine cognitive production becomes cheaper, professional diplomatic value can shift toward judgment, relationships, language depth, verification, negotiation, field interpretation, and responsibility, provided institutions deliberately reassign saved time rather than simply producing more output.

**Current practitioner evidence:** Balakrishnan’s May 2026 remarks are direct evidence that a serving foreign minister already uses LLMs and an agent for analysis, abstraction, briefing, drafting, and preparation while stressing security boundaries and the continuing importance of human context. This supports the chapter’s task-audit premise without proving a universal future-of-work pattern. Source: https://www.mfa.gov.sg/newsroom/press-statements-transcripts-and-photos/minister-for-foreign-affairs-dr-vivian-balakrishnan-s-speech-at-ai-engineer-singapore--16-may-2026/

**Boundary / counterevidence:** the chapter does not claim all traditional diplomatic skills should survive. It explicitly rejects nostalgia and acknowledges that diplomats can misread rooms, relationships can bias judgment, and “feel for the country” can become an excuse against evidence. AI may make some traditional skills genuinely less scarce.

**Release judgment:** no prose correction required. The chapter is a professional-design argument grounded by current practitioner evidence rather than a measured labor-market forecast.

## Chapter 18 — The Last Human Channel

**Material claim reviewed:** the book’s final distinction is between capability and political authority: machines can support analysis, drafting, monitoring, simulation, and option generation, but consequential state intent still requires an accountable authorization path unless institutions explicitly and lawfully redesign that boundary.

**Evidence base:** this is a synthesis chapter. Its factual anchors are the already reviewed communications history, authentication evidence, crisis-communication mechanisms, alliance implementation evidence, commercial intelligence capability, current diplomatic AI practice, and military-AI governance work. It introduces no new quantitative or current empirical centerpiece requiring a separate source lane.

**Boundary / counterevidence:** the chapter expressly says the human boundary should not be preserved everywhere and may move as technical systems improve. It does not claim human judgment is inherently superior to machine analysis; it claims political responsibility and authorization should remain legible where sovereignty, escalation, irreversibility, or trusted state intent are implicated.

**Release judgment:** no prose correction required.
