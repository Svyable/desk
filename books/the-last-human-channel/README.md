*Diplomacy at Machine Speed.*

The central thesis is that artificial intelligence makes many supporting functions of statecraft abundant — analysis, translation, surveillance, drafting, simulation, persuasion, and first-pass policy work — while making trusted human judgment, authentication, attention, and authorized intent more strategically valuable.

The book follows diplomacy through earlier communications revolutions and into an AI environment where governments can see more, generate more options, and fabricate more convincingly than any foreign ministry in history. Its central question is not whether machines can perform diplomatic tasks. They can. It is which parts of statecraft should accelerate, which parts require deliberate friction, and what institutions let adversaries understand one another before machine-speed action outruns political control.

The complete Desk draft is designed as trade nonfiction with a classroom afterlife: readable as narrative statecraft and structured for graduate discussion of diplomacy, alliances, China, crisis management, and emerging technology.
