# Back Matter

## Evidence note

This manuscript treats Project Pluto as a documented engineering program, not as a legend. Claims are ranked in the accompanying evidence ledger. Where later popular accounts disagree with official or period technical records, the manuscript preserves the disagreement rather than silently selecting the most cinematic version.

## Chronology

- **1954–1955** — NACA/Lewis researchers and others investigate nuclear-heated ramjet concepts in the broader nuclear-flight environment.
- **1957** — The U.S. Air Force and Atomic Energy Commission place nuclear-ramjet development at Lawrence Radiation Laboratory; Project Pluto begins.
- **October 1960** — Tory II-A reaches criticality during development work at Livermore.
- **May 14, 1961** — Tory II-A performs its landmark high-power Nevada test, reported by Livermore as a 45-second full-power run.
- **1961** — Additional Tory II-A testing continues.
- **1962** — Tory II-C design documentation describes a realistic flight-engine prototype and its reactor structure.
- **May 1964** — Tory II-C undergoes successful high-power testing at the Nevada Test Site, reaching roughly half a gigawatt of thermal output in institutional summaries.
- **July 1, 1964** — Project Pluto is canceled after loss of firm military commitment.
- **1970s onward** — Pluto hardware and facilities enter storage, fuel-handling, reuse and eventual environmental-restoration histories.
- **2000s–2010s** — Nevada cleanup documentation preserves detailed physical evidence of the old Pluto facilities and their disposition.

## What would defeat the book's thesis

The core thesis is not that Pluto was nearly operational. It is that its nuclear ramjet crossed a meaningful technical demonstration threshold while the parent weapon lost strategic justification. That thesis would need revision if primary records showed the Tory tests failed their stated central objectives; that II-C was never intended as a representative engine prototype; or that cancellation was primarily caused by an unrecoverable reactor-physics failure rather than changing military requirement and remaining system costs.

The broader methodological claim—that real secret hardware programs leave convergent institutional and physical residue—would need narrowing if comparable national-laboratory-scale programs could be shown to run for years, build major test infrastructure, and leave essentially no procurement, facility, personnel, environmental, archival or later declassification trace.
