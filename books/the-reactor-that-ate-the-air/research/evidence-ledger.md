# Evidence Ledger

## Class 1 — Verified / declassified / documented

- Project Pluto was a U.S. Air Force / Atomic Energy Commission nuclear-ramjet development program centered at Lawrence Radiation Laboratory.
- Tory II-A was successfully tested at the Nevada Test Site in 1961.
- Tory II-C was built as a realistic flight-engine prototype and successfully tested at high power in 1964.
- Livermore institutional history reports roughly 500 MW thermal power for Tory II-C and states all six tests of the two Tory reactors were conducted without failure.
- The program was canceled July 1, 1964 after loss of firm military commitment.
- Major specialized ground infrastructure existed at the Nevada Test Site for airflow, remote handling, assembly/disassembly and radiological operations.
- Pluto facilities and radioactive hardware persisted into later fuel-handling and environmental-restoration programs.

## Class 2 — Credible but incomplete / model-dependent

- Exact SLAM operational altitude, speed and route profiles varied by design study and mission assumption.
- Detailed terrain-comparison guidance architecture and its maturity need contractor-level documentation.
- Quantitative routine radioactive release from a representative operational reactor depends on fuel erosion, fission-product retention, activation, power history and flight profile.
- The exact causal weighting of safety, testability, cost and competing strategic systems in cancellation requires deeper budget and policy reconstruction.

## Class 3 — Disputed / often repeated without sufficient primary confirmation

- Claims that sonic boom alone was deliberately intended as a mass-casualty weapon.
- Claims that the reactor exhaust trail was formally intended as a primary radiological attack mode rather than an accepted consequence of the propulsion architecture.
- Exact anecdotes about proposed terminal loitering, intentional post-payload contamination, or specific flight-test disposal schemes unless tied to dated design records.
- Colorful quotations attributed to project personnel in later popular histories without contemporaneous sourcing.

## Class 4 — Speculative synthesis / open conjecture

- Project Pluto as a calibration case for evaluating modern claims about secret exotic hardware programs: genuine programs at this scale should produce convergent technical, facilities, procurement and lifecycle residue, even if details remain classified.
- The proposition that removal of a dominant constraint—chemical-fuel endurance—reveals command, testability and mission control as the next scarce resources.
- The broader thesis that technological obsolescence can happen during technical success, making successful prototypes especially useful for studying system-level failure.
