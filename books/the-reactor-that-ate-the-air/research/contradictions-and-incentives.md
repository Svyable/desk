# Contradictions, Incentives, and Failure Modes

## Institutional self-celebration

Livermore histories have an understandable incentive to preserve Pluto as an example of laboratory daring and technical achievement. Their statements about successful tests are strong evidence for event chronology and institutional claims, but technical details should be cross-checked against period reports rather than relying only on retrospective summaries.

## Popular-history amplification

Project Pluto attracts lurid retelling. The real program already combines nuclear propulsion, low-altitude supersonic flight and strategic nuclear payloads, so writers have little need to invent. Yet exactly because the baseline is extreme, secondary accounts often promote possible effects into intended doctrine. The manuscript requires separate sourcing for sonic-boom lethality, routine radioactive exhaust magnitude, deliberate contamination missions, and terminal post-strike behavior.

## Participant memory

Engineers and program participants can supply details absent from formal records but may compress dates, variants or institutional disagreements after decades. Oral history is treated as testimony unless independently anchored.

## Anti-nuclear simplification

Critiques can also flatten the record by describing Pluto as technically absurd. That loses the central historical fact that the Tory reactor program achieved significant engineering goals. Moral rejection and technical assessment are separate questions.

## Pro-technology nostalgia

The opposite failure is to treat successful reactor tests as proof that SLAM was nearly deployable. It was not. Full airframe integration, guidance, launch/boost, representative flight testing, operational basing, command and lifecycle problems remained.

## Known contradiction to preserve

Retrospective sources differ in shorthand descriptions of Tory II-C output and test duration. The book uses 'roughly half a gigawatt' unless a chapter is tied to a specific test report and treats institutional summaries as rounded values rather than exact metrology.
