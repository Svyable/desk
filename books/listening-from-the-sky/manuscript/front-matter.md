# LISTENING FROM THE SKY

## The Secret Nuclear Sensor That Science Took Seventy Years to Verify

Sven Hardy Benson

This is a book about a scientific idea that spent most of its life in an awkward category: serious enough to classify, useful enough to fund, difficult enough to abandon, and for decades too poorly measured to know whether its most interesting premise was actually true.

Project MOGUL belongs to UFO history because debris recovered near Roswell became entangled with it. That is not this book. Here MOGUL is restored to its stranger original problem: whether microphones carried into the upper atmosphere could exploit long-range low-frequency sound to hear explosions and missiles at distances unavailable to ordinary ground stations.

The evidence will be kept in four bins even when doing so ruins a cleaner story. **Documented** means primary records, official histories, hardware records, or peer-reviewed measurements establish the claim. **Credible but incomplete** means the source is serious but an important denominator, protocol, provenance link, or independent replication remains missing. **Disputed** means a claim has been made but lacks adequate independent confirmation or collides with substantial counterevidence. **Speculative** means this book is making a synthesis or asking a question that the sources themselves do not establish.

The central claim is narrow. A sensor is not merely an instrument. It is an instrument plus a place in the world. Change the place and the observable world can change with it. That proposition is now experimentally tractable in stratospheric infrasound. It does not license the reverse move of treating every unexplained signal as evidence of a hidden source. A new listening position creates new artifacts as readily as new discoveries.
