# Evidence matrix

The manuscript uses four evidence classes. Classification is claim-specific: the same document can be Class 1 evidence that an official considered a hypothesis and only Class 2 or 3 evidence that the hypothesis itself was true.

| Claim | Class | Basis | Boundary / what it does not prove |
|---|---|---|---|
| Soviet-origin microwave/RF energy was directed at the U.S. Embassy in Moscow for years beginning in the 1950s. | 1 — verified/documented | U.S. technical monitoring; declassified White House/ARPA/CIA/State records; Senate review. | Does not by itself establish biological purpose or harmful dose. |
| U.S. officials did not initially know the signal's purpose. | 1 | TUMS/MUTS naming; Wiesner report; diplomatic memoranda; competing hypotheses in declassified records. | Later analysts may favor one explanation, but uncertainty was real at the time. |
| The U.S. established PANDORA/BIZARRE to investigate possible biological/behavioral effects. | 1 | Cesaro justification memo; ARPA/CIA records; later DARPA congressional response. | Program existence establishes concern, not efficacy. |
| Early U.S. estimates of Moscow power density were substantially revised downward. | 1 | Johnston and Cesaro 1967 memoranda summarizing improved on-site measurements. | Precise personal absorbed dose for each employee remains unavailable. |
| An early primate experiment reported behavioral degradation under a stronger exposure assumption. | 1 for the reported observation; 2 for causal interpretation | Johnston summary of BIZARRE. | Does not establish relevance to corrected Moscow exposure; later lower-level work did not show overt degradation. |
| Corrected-dose primate work failed to reproduce overt performance degradation. | 1 | Cesaro 1967 report; Johnston summary. | Does not prove all nonthermal RF effects impossible. |
| RAND reviewers judged the PANDORA animal evidence scientifically inadequate for the claimed behavioral effect. | 1 | Koslov 1969 review; 1971 RAND-led Navy review. | A methodological critique does not establish that every measurement was false. |
| The State Department used a cover story while collecting blood relevant to microwave-exposure research. | 1 | State medical cable and GWU cytogenetics contract in declassified archive. | Does not establish that an RF-caused cytogenetic injury was found. |
| PANDORA officials discussed unwitting controlled human exposure, including six-month protocols and shielding. | 1 | April-May 1969 PANDORA meeting minutes. | Available public evidence reviewed here does not establish that the proposed experiment was performed. |
| BIG BOY compared RF-exposed Saratoga personnel with less-exposed/new personnel and reported early negative psychological/genetic/physical comparisons. | 1 | Pollack memo and PANDORA minutes. | Exposure classification and statistical power require deeper reconstruction before strong inference. |
| Embassy personnel were kept uninformed about the signal until 1976. | 1 | State/White House record; Senate 1979 review. | Does not imply the government knew the signal was harmful. |
| The 1978 Foreign Service Health Status Study found no persuasive overall mortality/health catastrophe attributable to the exposure. | 1 for study result; interpretation supported by later review | Lilienfeld report as summarized by Elwood/IARC; Senate review. | Rare/subgroup/long-latency effects are less strongly constrained; personal dosimetry was poor. |
| Specific nonfatal symptoms or small-number disease categories differed in some analyses. | 2 — credible but incomplete | Lilienfeld tables and later reanalyses/reviews. | Multiple comparisons, wide confidence intervals, and exposure misclassification prevent treating isolated differences as proof of causation. |
| The Soviet signal was used to activate/interrogate intelligence devices. | 2 | Known Soviet passive RF bug precedent; U.S. memoranda explicitly considering device activation/interrogation. | No publicly recovered Soviet operator record in this source set proves this was the purpose of every Moscow transmission. |
| The Soviet signal was intended as a behavioral or health weapon. | 3 — disputed/not established | U.S. concern; Soviet bioeffects literature; later claims. | No public operator directive + no validated behavioral effect at measured exposure. |
| Soviet services possessed an effective microwave mind-control capability. | 3 | Allegations and inference from research interest. | Research interest, restrictive standards, and PANDORA do not establish capability. |
| Moscow exposure caused a defined “microwave sickness” syndrome in embassy personnel. | 3 | Some later interpretations of symptom data. | Cohort evidence did not demonstrate a coherent causal syndrome; exposure and outcome reconstruction remain weak. |
| Moscow proves the mechanism of Havana/AHI. | 3 | Historical analogy. | Moscow has measured source but uncertain health effect; many AHIs have reported health effect but no measured source. Analogy cannot close either chain. |
| Directed pulsed RF energy is physically/biologically plausible for some distinctive AHI features. | 2 | National Academies 2020; IC Experts Panel 2022. | Plausibility is not evidence that an adversary deployed such a device in a specific incident. |
| A foreign adversary caused the AHI population as a whole. | 3 under current official record | Most IC elements assess this very unlikely (2023; updated Jan. 2025). | Some components retain uncertainty around subsets/capabilities; intelligence judgments can change with new collection. |
| Adversarial ambiguity itself imposed costs on the United States. | 1 for costs; 4 for deliberate use of ambiguity as strategy | PANDORA spending, investigations, diplomacy, health studies, secrecy controversy. | No evidence that imposing epistemic cost was the Soviet purpose. |
| Secrecy can create a false evidentiary ecology in which real hidden programs are used to support stronger unverified claims. | 4 — speculative synthesis grounded in documented pattern | PANDORA existence + negative reviews + later cultural interpretation. | This is an interpretive model, not a discovered Soviet or U.S. doctrine. |

## Defeat conditions for the book's synthesis

The book's central synthesis should be revised or abandoned if strong primary evidence establishes that the Soviet operators' purpose was unambiguously biomedical and that the actual Moscow waveform/dose produced a reproducible relevant effect. Examples would include authenticated Soviet operational directives plus technical specifications matching the embassy signal, independently replicated exposure studies at realistic parameters, and a human dose-response pattern tied to measured exposure.

The synthesis should also narrow if better archival reconstruction shows that U.S. officials had a stable and accurate explanation for the signal much earlier than the current record suggests; that would weaken the claim that uncertainty itself drove the institutional response.
