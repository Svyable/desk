# Source log

Working source trail for **Clog the Sky**. Public, lawfully available material only.

## Primary / official

- CIA, **Report of the Scientific Panel on Unidentified Flying Objects**, 17 January 1953. Declassified copy: https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/CIA-RDP67-00059A000100030001-6.pdf
  - Establishes the Robertson Panel’s own language: no direct physical national-security threat in the evidence presented; concern instead about clogged communications, false alarms, public reaction, and possible hostile exploitation.
- CIA, alternate released copy of the Robertson Panel report: https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/DOC_0005517565.pdf
- CIA, Gerald K. Haines, **CIA’s Role in the Study of UFOs, 1947–90**, *Studies in Intelligence*, Vol. 40 No. 5: https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/studies-in-intelligence/archives/vol-40-no-5/the-cias-role-in-the-study-of-ufos-1947-90/
  - Official historical synthesis with important caveats; useful for CIA institutional chronology, 1952 Washington incidents, Robertson Panel consequences, and later reconnaissance-aircraft overlap.
- CIA, **How To Investigate a Flying Saucer** (2016): https://www.cia.gov/stories/story/how-to-investigate-a-flying-saucer/
  - Public Agency retrospective summarizing BLUE BOOK investigative categories, Robertson Panel concerns, Tremonton review, and false-positive logic.
- National Archives, **Project BLUE BOOK – Unidentified Flying Objects**: https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos
  - Archival inventory; BLUE BOOK total 12,618 reports, 701 categorized as unidentified; official Air Force closure conclusions reproduced.
- National Archives, **Records Related to UFOs and UAPs: Textual and Microfilm**: https://www.archives.gov/research/topics/uaps/textual-and-microfilm
  - Current archival map across record groups.
- National Archives, **Record Group 615: Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena Records Collection**: https://www.archives.gov/research/topics/uaps/rg-615
  - Current UAP records collection created pursuant to the FY2024 NDAA.
- National Archives, **Bulk Downloads for UAP Records**: https://www.archives.gov/research/catalog/catalog-bulk-downloads/uap-bulk-download
- National Archives, 2019/2020 Blue Book anniversary materials: https://www.archives.gov/press/press-releases/2020/nr20-19
- U.S. Air Force OSI, **Report of Investigation**, 28 March 1952, unidentified aerial-object photograph: https://prologue.blogs.archives.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/9/2018/04/report-of-investigation-march-28-1952.pdf
  - Concrete example of the investigative machinery around a case.

## Important official retrospective claims to audit against primary records

- Haines reports that the 19–20 and 27 July 1952 Washington radar events produced interceptor scrambles, headline pressure, and White House interest, and that Civil Aeronautics Administration investigation supported anomalous-propagation / temperature-inversion explanations for radar blips.
- Haines reports that later U-2 and OXCART flights generated a substantial share of UFO reports during relevant periods. This should be kept narrower than the common internet paraphrase that “half of all UFO reports” across the whole BLUE BOOK era were spy planes.
- CIA’s 2016 retrospective says the Robertson Panel feared Soviet use of UFO reporting to overload warning systems. This is evidence of the concern, not evidence that Soviet services actually executed such a campaign.

## Source discipline

A government document can establish that an official feared Soviet exploitation without establishing that exploitation occurred. An “unidentified” classification can establish that a case remained unresolved under the project’s available data and methods; it does not establish extraterrestrial origin or advanced technology. A later official historical essay is useful evidence of institutional memory but must not silently replace contemporaneous records where those records are available.
