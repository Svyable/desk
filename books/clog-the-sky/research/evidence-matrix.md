# Evidence matrix

## Class 1 — verified / declassified / documented

| Claim | Evidence | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| The U.S. Air Force operated SIGN, GRUDGE, and BLUE BOOK to investigate UFO reports. | NARA BLUE BOOK holdings; CIA institutional histories. | Program existence does not validate extraordinary interpretations of cases. |
| Washington-area radar/visual reports in July 1952 triggered military attention and interceptor scrambles. | CIA Haines history based on official records. | The case history does not establish one cause for every return or visual report. |
| CIA created a scientific review process after the 1952 sighting wave. | Declassified CIA records and Robertson Panel report. | CIA interest proves concern, not exotic origin. |
| The Robertson Panel concluded the evidence presented showed no direct physical national-security threat. | 17 Jan. 1953 report. | A panel conclusion is an assessment bounded by evidence, time, selection, and method. |
| The Panel separately warned of clogged communications, false alarms, public reaction, and possible hostile exploitation. | 17 Jan. 1953 report. | These were vulnerability judgments and threat scenarios, not evidence of a Soviet campaign. |
| The Panel recommended public education/debunking and monitoring certain civilian groups. | Panel report; CIA Haines history. | Recommendation does not prove all later public messaging followed one coordinated plan. |
| BLUE BOOK closed in 1969; 12,618 reports were recorded and 701 remained categorized as unidentified. | NARA Air Force fact sheet. | “Unidentified” is a residual category, not a physical-origin category. |

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete

- Some secret U.S. reconnaissance flights were reported by observers as UFOs, and secrecy constrained public explanation. The exact fraction varies by period and must be reconstructed carefully.
- The 1952 reporting wave probably imposed real analytic and communications costs, but a complete quantitative ledger of message volume, staffing burden, warning-channel congestion, and delayed handling of other traffic has not yet been reconstructed.
- Public mistrust was plausibly amplified when official explanations were necessarily incomplete or later shown to omit classified programs. The causal contribution of secrecy versus cultural fascination, press incentives, bureaucratic inconsistency, and genuine unresolved observations remains difficult to partition.
- Some BLUE BOOK cases remain scientifically interesting as records of unresolved observations. Their evidentiary value varies radically with sensor quality, witness conditions, timing, documentation, and later access to classified context.

## Class 3 — disputed / not independently established

- Soviet intelligence deliberately manufactured the 1952 U.S. UFO wave.
- The Robertson Panel secretly confirmed extraterrestrial craft and disguised that conclusion.
- Every unidentified BLUE BOOK case represents one common technology or phenomenon.
- The CIA/Air Force centrally fabricated the UFO phenomenon to conceal reconnaissance programs.
- Civilian UFO organizations were in fact controlled by hostile intelligence merely because the Panel recommended watching them.

## Class 4 — speculative synthesis / open conjecture

The strategically important object may sometimes be the **report ecology** rather than the object that generated the first report. Once a reporting network becomes culturally salient, an adversary can potentially exploit it without inventing the underlying phenomenon: add false reports, time ambiguous stimuli, imitate expected signatures, seed explanations, or simply wait for institutions to overload themselves. This is a general deception hypothesis suggested by the Panel’s logic, not a claim about a documented Soviet operation.

## Defeat conditions

The book’s central synthesis should narrow or fail if the primary record shows that the Panel’s “clogging,” false-alarm, and hostile-exploitation language was incidental rather than operationally meaningful; if no actual warning/communications burden can be demonstrated anywhere in the period; or if later records show that officials themselves did not treat the reporting ecology as a real security concern after January 1953.

A stronger version — that UFO culture was actively weaponized by a foreign intelligence service in this period — requires event-specific evidence of tasking, assets, dissemination, or operational effects. The current public record used here does not supply that evidence.
