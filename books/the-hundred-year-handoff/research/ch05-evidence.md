# Chapter 5 evidence notes — The Artifact

Research checked September 3, 2026.

## Core case: Ise Jingu and Shikinen Sengu

Ise Jingu's official English materials state that every twenty years a new divine palace with the same dimensions as the current one is built on the adjacent alternate site. Sacred apparel, furnishings, and divine treasures are remade, and the sacred symbol is transferred to the new sanctuary. The process includes about thirty rituals and ceremonies and takes about eight years.

The first recorded Shikinen Sengu at Naiku was conducted in 690. The sixty-second was conducted in 2013. Official Japanese materials for the sixty-third cycle state that related ceremonies began in 2025 and culminate in 2033.

Authoritative sources:

- Ise Jingu, English ritual page: https://www.isejingu.or.jp/en/ritual/index.html
- Ise Jingu, About Ise Jingu: https://www.isejingu.or.jp/en/about/index.html
- Ise Jingu, 63rd Shikinen Sengu: https://www.isejingu.or.jp/sengu/the63rd/about.html

## Claim to earn

The Ise case supports a narrow but important proposition: physical continuity of original material is not the only possible carrier of institutional or cultural continuity. Rebuilding can itself preserve a living capability by requiring materials, dimensions, rituals, craft techniques, and organizational coordination to be reproduced.

Do not claim that the twenty-year cycle exists solely or primarily as a vocational training system. The official religious meaning is much broader. The manuscript may infer that periodic remaking has a knowledge-transfer effect, but should mark that as a mechanism visible in the practice rather than an official single-cause explanation.

## Counterweight: authenticity is plural

The 1994 Nara Document on Authenticity rejects a single universal criterion for authenticity. It says judgments may draw on sources including form and design, materials and substance, use and function, traditions and techniques, location and setting, spirit and feeling, and other factors, while recognizing cultural diversity.

Source:

- ICOMOS, The Nara Document on Authenticity: https://landscapes.icomos.org/1994-the-nara-document-on-authenticity/

This is the chapter's main counterweight to a simplistic "replacement preserves identity" argument. In many artifacts, original material is itself evidence and value. The right handoff question is therefore which properties are invariant, which must remain original, which must remain functionally equivalent, and which may be replaced.

## Boundary claims

Avoid:

- saying Ise has been rebuilt every twenty years without interruption for 1,300 years;
- treating Ise as a generic engineering maintenance practice divorced from religious meaning;
- claiming a replica is always equivalent to an original;
- using the Ship of Theseus as proof rather than analogy;
- implying that long-lived systems should literally rebuild themselves on a fixed interval.

## Strongest safe formulation

A long-lived artifact can persist through controlled replacement when the handoff makes the system's important invariants legible and keeps the capability to reproduce them alive. Material continuity is one kind of continuity, not the definition of continuity itself.
