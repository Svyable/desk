# Chapter 3 evidence notes — The Missing Builder

Research checked September 3, 2026.

## NASA knowledge capture and transition

NASA's current knowledge-management program explicitly treats employee transition and retirement as risks to organizational knowledge continuity. The agency provides knowledge-capture and transfer guidance, continuity-book templates, after-action methods, task documentation tools, and transition guides.

Source:

- NASA APPEL Knowledge Management: https://www.nasa.gov/learning-resources/for-professionals/appel-knowledge-management/

NASA's supervisor guide states that experience and knowledge held by transitioning employees cannot be easily replaced and recommends ongoing capture and transfer rather than waiting until the end of a long career.

Source:

- NASA, *Knowledge Capture and Transfer: A Guide for Supervisors*: https://appel.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/kct-supervisors-guide.pdf

## Space Shuttle operational knowledge

In a 2022 NASA APPEL interview, Aeronautics Research Mission Directorate Chief Engineer Steve Hirshorn said that some Shuttle operational experience had been captured in documentation and policy, while a substantial amount left as personnel retired, moved on, or contractor staff were laid off. Hirshorn had supported 55 Shuttle missions as a flight controller and argued that experience and judgment extend beyond what can be reduced to formal policy statements.

Source:

- NASA, *Engineering Best Practices, Part 2*: https://www.nasa.gov/podcasts/small-steps-giant-leaps/small-steps-giant-leaps-episode-79-engineering-best-practices-part-2/

NASA also created a Shuttle Guidance, Navigation, and Rendezvous knowledge-capture catalog specifically to make lessons, technical history, and experience easier for future generations of engineers to locate.

Source:

- NASA Technical Reports Server, *Space Shuttle Guidance, Navigation, and Rendezvous Knowledge Capture Reports*: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20110023477

NASA's oral-history program separately identifies a Space Shuttle Tacit Knowledge Capture project based on interviews with former key program members.

Source:

- NASA Oral History Project List: https://www.nasa.gov/history/history-publications-and-resources/oral-histories/nasa-list/

## Nuclear tacit-knowledge transfer

IAEA knowledge-management work on the Finnish nuclear workforce reported that senior workers held tacit knowledge related to commissioning, early operation, maintenance, and relationships. It found that systematic methods for transferring this knowledge were still incomplete, that tacit knowledge could not be fully transferred merely by verbalizing it, and that some older practices were not worth preserving because younger workers had developed better ones.

Source:

- IAEA, *Managing Nuclear Knowledge*: https://www-pub.iaea.org/MTCD/Publications/PDF/Pub1235_web.pdf

IAEA guidance also states that no information-management system can replace face-to-face interactions for tacit-knowledge transfer and recommends combining personal transfer with job aids, structured on-the-job training, communities of practice, and explicit documentation.

Source:

- IAEA, *The Nuclear Power Industry's Ageing Workforce: Transfer of Knowledge to the Next Generation*: https://www-pub.iaea.org/MTCD/Publications/PDF/te_1399_web.pdf

## Strongest formulation Chapter 3 can safely earn

Long-lived technical organizations routinely discover that critical capability exists partly as tacit and relational knowledge, not only as formal documentation. Effective succession therefore combines records with supervised practice, mentoring, communities of practice, and deliberate transition. At the same time, preserving every veteran practice would be a mistake; the successor must be able to improve inherited methods.

## Claims to avoid

- Do not imply NASA "lost all Shuttle knowledge." The agency preserved extensive documentation and created dedicated capture efforts.
- Do not claim tacit knowledge is inherently superior to explicit procedures.
- Do not treat intuition as automatically trustworthy; expert judgment can encode obsolete habits or bias.
- Do not say knowledge can never be documented. The evidence says some tacit knowledge cannot be fully transferred by verbalization alone.
- Do not turn the chapter into an argument for retaining every veteran indefinitely. The handoff goal is organizational learnability, not permanent dependence on specific people.
