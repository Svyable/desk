# Chapter 6 evidence notes — The Memory

Research checked September 3, 2026.

## Core case: USS Thresher and SUBSAFE

USS Thresher (SSN-593) was lost on April 10, 1963 during deep-diving trials, killing all 129 people aboard. The U.S. Navy established the Submarine Safety Program (SUBSAFE) in June 1963 in response to the loss.

NAVSEA describes SUBSAFE as providing maximum reasonable assurance that submarine hulls maintain watertight integrity and that submarines can recover from unanticipated flooding. Certification spans design, material, fabrication, and testing. NAVSEA states that each step requires records collection, review, and approval; records are maintained for the entire life of the submarine; and material traceability runs from original manufacture through installation and final certification.

Authoritative sources:

- NAVSEA, USS Thresher: A loss. A legacy.: https://www.navsea.navy.mil/Media/News/Article/3354927/uss-thresher-a-loss-a-legacy/
- NAVSEA, NAVSEA Remembers Crew of USS Thresher (2025): https://www.navsea.navy.mil/Media/News/Article/4151815/navsea-remembers-crew-of-uss-thresher/
- NAVSEA, Thresher 59th Remembrance, SUBSAFE Program: https://www.navsea.navy.mil/Media/News/Article-View/Article/2998364/thresher-59th-remembrance-subsafe-program/

## Claim to earn

SUBSAFE demonstrates that memory in a high-consequence system can be operational evidence rather than a passive archive. The safety claim attaches to traceable pedigree, configuration, qualification, testing, review, and verification across the object's life.

Do not write that documentation alone caused SUBSAFE's safety record. NAVSEA emphasizes personnel, questioning attitude, audits, material control, training, compliance, and verification as well as records.

## Counterweight: archives must be selective

NARA explicitly says it cannot preserve all federal records and must be selective. Its appraisal role is both to identify important records for preservation and to avoid unnecessary maintenance of too much documentation. NARA records schedules distinguish permanent records from temporary records that are eventually destroyed.

Authoritative sources:

- NARA, Appraisal and Approval: https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/scheduling/nara-review
- NARA, Agency Permanent Records: https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/scheduling/permanent-records
- NARA, Records Schedule: https://www.archives.gov/about/records-schedule
- NARA, Preparing Disposition Instructions: https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/scheduling/instructions

This supports the chapter's distinction between memory architecture and storage volume. Retention has cost; too much undifferentiated documentation can reduce usability.

## Boundary claims

Avoid:

- claiming the cause of Thresher was conclusively one failed joint; official accounts describe the likely flooding casualty within a broader set of deficiencies;
- claiming no U.S. submarine has ever been lost after SUBSAFE; USS Scorpion was lost in 1968 but was not SUBSAFE-certified;
- claiming a record proves physical reality without verification;
- implying NARA destroys records merely to save storage costs;
- treating all tacit knowledge as reducible to records.

## Strongest safe formulation

A useful institutional memory lets a successor distinguish current state from historical state, trace claims to evidence, understand material changes, and identify which records carry continuing value. It must be selective enough to remain navigable and coupled tightly enough to verification that paperwork does not become a substitute for reality.
