# Cooking queue — The Hundred-Year Handoff

This is the next-draft queue, not a claim that research is complete.

## Next cluster: Chapters 2–4

### Chapter 2 — The Cathedral Problem

Research one medieval or early-modern project whose construction genuinely crossed generations. Separate documented succession mechanisms from romantic myth. Look for workshop records, master-builder transitions, templates/drawings, financing, and evidence that designs changed while continuity remained.

Pair it with one modern project explicitly governed across multiple administrations or engineering generations.

### Chapter 3 — The Missing Builder

Find documented cases of tacit-knowledge loss after retirement, turnover, vendor disappearance, or program shutdown. Favor NASA, nuclear operations, national labs, industrial maintenance, or other domains with formal knowledge-retention practices and post-incident evidence.

The chapter needs a countercase where heavy documentation failed because skill required apprenticeship or practice.

### Chapter 4 — The Half-Life of Instructions

Research digital preservation, obsolete formats, dependency decay, calibration records, language drift, and configuration management. Build a taxonomy of instruction half-lives without pretending there is one universal curve.

## Handoff-stack cluster: Chapters 5–9

### Artifact

Find cases where replaceable parts made a long-lived whole possible. Distinguish "original material" from continuity of function.

### Memory

Research archives and configuration-management systems that preserve rationale and provenance. Include one case of record abundance becoming unusable noise.

### Authority

Find explicit succession mechanisms in a standards body, trust/foundation, public institution, and constitutional system. Ask not only who has power but why later people accept that power as legitimate.

### Money

Build cases around endowments, infrastructure reserves, pensions, nuclear/decommissioning funds, and dedicated maintenance mechanisms. Find at least one fund that proved insufficient because assumptions changed.

### Repairability

Research infrastructure access, parts obsolescence, open specifications, right-to-repair, military sustainment, and software reproducibility. Preserve security/safety counterarguments.

## Institutional cluster: Chapters 10–15

### Constitution

Choose a constitutional mechanism with visible succession and adaptation over time. Avoid triumphalist longevity stories; include amendments, interpretive changes, institutional conflict, and legitimacy questions.

### Archive

Use NARA/Library of Congress or equivalent primary guidance. Focus on provenance, description, migration, access, and unknown future questions.

### Bridge

Choose a century-scale bridge with accessible design, inspection, repair, and ownership history. Prefer a case where the structure survived because major components or operating assumptions changed.

### Seed vault

Use Crop Trust, NordGen, Norwegian government, and FAO sources. Explain Svalbard as a backup node in a living distributed genebank system, not a self-sufficient apocalypse freezer.

### Long-running science

Select one longitudinal study or time series that crossed multiple principal investigators. Trace data governance, methodology changes, funding, and succession.

### Standard

Use one protocol or standard whose meaning survived generations of products because governance and reference systems remained active. Keep focus on temporal continuity to avoid duplicating *The Compatibility Dividend*.

## Failure cluster: Chapters 16–19

Find one strong case for each:

- useful system becomes ownerless;
- rationale disappears and a successor makes the wrong inference;
- maintenance is postponed across political/organizational generations;
- successor improves the outcome by rejecting or replacing the inherited design.

The final case is mandatory. Without it the book will drift into preservation ideology.

## Final synthesis: Chapters 20–21

Do not draft the final Handoff Test until at least fifteen evidence chapters exist.

Candidate test:

1. What must persist?
2. What must a stranger know?
3. Who inherits legitimate authority?
4. What resources cross the transfer?
5. Can the successor inspect and repair critical elements?
6. Which dependencies expire sooner than the system?
7. Can the successor change or retire the inheritance intelligently?

The final chapter should return to the WIPP problem, then shorten the horizon: the practical hundred-year handoff begins with the next maintainer and compounds through every successor after them.
