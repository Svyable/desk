# Chapter 1 evidence notes — A Message for the Year 12,000

Research checked September 3, 2026.

## Core factual anchor

The Waste Isolation Pilot Plant (WIPP) in southeastern New Mexico is the U.S. Department of Energy's operating deep geologic repository for defense-related transuranic radioactive waste. DOE describes waste emplacement in salt formations approximately 2,150 feet underground and long-term performance over the EPA regulatory period of 10,000 years after closure.

Authoritative sources:

- DOE WIPP, Closure & Institutional Control: https://www.wipp.energy.gov/closure-institutional-control.asp
- Sandia National Laboratories, *Looking 10,000 years into the future*: https://www.sandia.gov/labnews/2022/08/25/looking-10000-years-into-the-future/
- Sandia National Laboratories, *Expert Judgment on Markers to Deter Inadvertent Human Intrusion into the Waste Isolation Pilot Plant*: https://www.sandia.gov/research/publications/details/expert-judgment-on-markers-to-deter-inadvertent-human-intrusion-into-the-wa-1993-11-01/

## Passive institutional controls

DOE says passive institutional controls (PICs) are markers and methods intended to warn and inform future generations and civilizations about WIPP's location and purpose. The conceptual system is designed to communicate the location, design, contents, and danger of the disposal system during the 10,000-year regulatory time frame.

DOE describes a "defense in depth" concept involving multiple components and multiple forms of information, including surface and subsurface markers, an information center, buried rooms, records, maps, atlases, dictionaries, encyclopedias, and educational references.

Source:

- DOE WIPP, Closure & Institutional Control: https://www.wipp.energy.gov/closure-institutional-control.asp
- DOE WIPP fact sheet, *How Will Future Generations Be Warned?*: https://www.wipp.energy.gov/fctshts/pics.pdf

## Sandia marker panel

The 1993 Sandia report records principles recommended by the expert panel, including:

- the site should be marked;
- messages should be truthful and informative;
- the system should have multiple components;
- multiple means of communication should be used, including language, pictographs, and scientific diagrams;
- individual messages should provide multiple levels of complexity;
- materials with little recycle value should be preferred;
- international efforts should help maintain knowledge of repository locations and contents.

The report explicitly recognizes that marker efficacy and interpretability are expected to decline with time and may depend on the future society's technology, purposes, and cultural conditions.

Source:

- Sandia National Laboratories / OSTI, SAND92-1382: https://www.osti.gov/biblio/10117359

## Regulatory nuance

Do **not** write that EPA requires or guarantees that the physical warning message will remain effective for the full 10,000 years.

40 CFR §194.43 requires DOE to describe passive institutional controls designed to preserve knowledge of the disposal system, including markers as permanent as practicable, records in archives and land-record systems, and other practicable controls. EPA's response-to-comments material states that neither the disposal regulations nor compliance criteria require PICs to be definitively effective for 10,000 years; the standard is the most permanent practicable measures, and performance-assessment credit for controls is limited.

Sources:

- EPA final WIPP certification discussion of §194.43: https://wipp.energy.gov/library/CRA/BaselineTool/Documents/Regulatory%20Tools/04%2040%20CFR%20194%20Certification%20Decision%20Final%20Rule%20-%20Part%203.pdf

## Active institutional control boundary

WIPP materials reproduce §194.41, under which performance assessments may not credit active institutional controls for more than 100 years after disposal. This is useful for the book because it exposes the difference between an institution actively guarding a system and a handoff architecture that must remain meaningful when active control can no longer be assumed.

Do not imply the regulation predicts the government will disappear after 100 years. The rule limits performance-assessment credit; it is not a forecast of institutional collapse.

## Future-society work

Sandia's expert-judgment work explicitly considered possible societies across the next 10,000 years because experimentation and observation cannot resolve such uncertainty. Factors included technological development, population, economics, conservation of information, persistence of government control, and mitigation of nuclear-waste danger.

Source:

- Sandia, *Expert judgement on inadvertent human intrusion into the Waste Isolation Pilot Plant*: https://www.sandia.gov/research/publications/details/expert-judgement-on-inadvertent-human-intrusion-into-the-waste-isolation-pi-1991-12-01/

## Strongest formulation Chapter 1 can safely earn

WIPP is an extreme demonstration that long-term safety can depend on **succession of meaning**, not only persistence of material. DOE, EPA, and Sandia all treat knowledge preservation, records, markers, interpretability, and changing future human behavior as part of the repository's institutional-control problem.

The generalization should stay modest: ordinary systems do not need a 10,000-year marker. But systems expected to outlive their builders do need to ask which carrier, context, authority, and capability will survive the transfer.

## Claims to avoid

- Do not say WIPP's final permanent marker design has already been built; DOE describes a conceptual design and says final design selection occurs before closure.
- Do not say EPA guarantees a message will be understood for 10,000 years.
- Do not say active institutional control legally ends after 100 years; performance assessment cannot credit it beyond that limit.
- Do not present speculative future-society scenarios as forecasts.
- Do not use the famous phrase "This place is not a place of honor" as though it were the final official inscription without explaining its status in design studies.
- Do not claim physical markers are the sole control; DOE describes layered records, archives, maps, and other measures.
