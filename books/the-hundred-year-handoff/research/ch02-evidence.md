# Chapter 2 evidence notes — The Cathedral Problem

Research checked September 3, 2026.

## Cologne Cathedral chronology

Cologne Cathedral's present Gothic building began in 1248. The choir was consecrated in 1322. Construction later slowed and came to a halt in the early sixteenth century. Work resumed in 1842 and the cathedral was completed in 1880.

Authoritative sources:

- Cologne Cathedral official history: https://www.koelner-dom.de/erleben/geschichte
- Cologne Cathedral archaeological / construction history: https://www.koelner-dom.de/erleben/domgrabung
- UNESCO World Heritage listing: https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/292

## Riss F / medieval west-façade plan

The Cologne Cathedral workshop's official history says much of the old medieval workshop archive was removed in 1794 by French revolutionary forces and is considered lost. A small number of medieval plans reappeared in the early nineteenth century. The most important is the more-than-four-meter-high Riss F, produced around 1280, depicting the west façade ultimately completed roughly six centuries later.

Source:

- Cologne Cathedral, *Geschichte der Kölner Dombauhütte*: https://www.koelner-dom.de/downloads/geschichte-der-koelner-dombauhuette.pdf

Editorial boundary: do not imply one drawing contained every instruction required for nineteenth-century completion. The chapter's point is the opposite: the plan was one surviving component inside a reconstructed handoff.

## Nineteenth-century completion and modern technique

Cologne Cathedral's official history states that the nineteenth-century workshop used contemporary construction technology, including rail-mounted lifting equipment and a steam engine, even while following the medieval façade plan closely. More than 500 workers were employed at times.

Sources:

- https://www.koelner-dom.de/erleben/geschichte
- https://www.koelner-dom.de/downloads/geschichte-der-koelner-dombauhuette.pdf

This supports the chapter's distinction between continuity of design intent and continuity of method.

## Financing and the Zentral-Dombau-Verein

The Zentral-Dombau-Verein was founded in 1842 to help finance completion. Cologne Cathedral materials say it supplied roughly 60 percent of completion funds and today contributes more than 60 percent of the cathedral workshop's annual construction/preservation budget.

Sources:

- https://www.koelner-dom.de/unterstuetzen/zentral-dombau-verein
- https://www.koelner-dom.de/erleben/geschichte

Boundary: exact percentages depend on the period described. In prose, keep the distinction between completion-era financing and current annual contribution.

## Milan as the continuity countercase

Construction of Milan Cathedral began around 1386. The Veneranda Fabbrica del Duomo was established in 1387 for design and construction and remains the institution responsible for conservation and development more than 630 years later.

Sources:

- Milan Cathedral history: https://www.duomomilano.it/en/art-and-culture/the-cathedral/
- Veneranda Fabbrica: https://www.duomomilano.it/en/about-us/veneranda-fabbrica-duomo/

The current construction/restoration site describes craft skills accumulated and handed down across generations while being updated through new technology and scientific research.

Source:

- https://www.duomomilano.it/en/about-us/the-duomo-construction-site/

The Marble Yard continues to replace architectural and sculptural elements that can no longer safely remain on the cathedral.

Source:

- https://www.duomomilano.it/en/support/adopt-a-statue/

## Strongest formulation Chapter 2 can safely earn

Multigenerational construction does not require an unbroken chain of identical methods. Cologne demonstrates recoverability after a long interruption; Milan demonstrates continuity through a long-lived steward. In both cases, physical persistence depends on repeated transfers of knowledge, authority, money, skill, and permission to replace parts.

## Claims to avoid

- Do not portray Cologne as seven centuries of continuous construction; there was a multi-century halt.
- Do not say the original medieval archive survived intact; official workshop history says it was removed and remains lost.
- Do not say nineteenth-century builders used only medieval methods.
- Do not imply Milan Cathedral consists entirely of original medieval material; active replacement and restoration are part of continuity.
- Do not claim cathedral longevity proves long-lived institutions are inherently good. Survival is the fact being explained, not a moral verdict.
