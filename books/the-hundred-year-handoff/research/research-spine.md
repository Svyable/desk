# Research and falsification spine — The Hundred-Year Handoff

Research initiated September 3, 2026.

This document defines the evidence lanes the manuscript must earn. It is not evidence that all planned chapters have already been verified.

## Central hypothesis

Long-lived systems fail less often when succession is designed across multiple layers: artifact persistence, durable and interpretable memory, legitimate authority, resourced stewardship, practical repairability, and successor agency.

The hypothesis is deliberately narrower than "think long term" and stronger than "document your work."

## Counterclaims the book must survive

1. **Many long-lived systems were not designed for long life.** Survival can result from accident, wealth, cultural attachment, path dependence, or repeated rescue rather than superior handoff design.
2. **Overdesigning for distant successors can waste resources today.** Most products and institutions should not be engineered for centuries.
3. **Documentation can become a liability.** Excess records can create noise, security risk, privacy harm, legal burden, or false confidence.
4. **Future people may possess better solutions.** Preserving today's mechanism can crowd out replacement.
5. **Institutions cannot reliably bind distant generations.** Laws, funds, trusts, constitutions, and standards all depend on later legitimacy and enforcement.
6. **Repairability has tradeoffs.** Modularity, open interfaces, spare capacity, and long support can increase present cost, complexity, attack surface, or performance penalties.
7. **Some tacit knowledge is irreducible.** Apprenticeship and practice may transfer capability that documents cannot.
8. **The successor may rationally refuse the inheritance.** A handoff framework that treats refusal as failure is paternalistic and incomplete.

## Chapter 1 — WIPP / deep-time communication

### Claim to earn

WIPP demonstrates that long-term stewardship is not only a material durability problem. DOE and Sandia explicitly treat preservation of knowledge, interpretability, records, markers, and changing future societies as part of the safety problem.

### Primary / authoritative lane

- U.S. Department of Energy WIPP Closure & Institutional Control page.
- Sandia National Laboratories, *Expert Judgment on Markers to Deter Inadvertent Human Intrusion into the Waste Isolation Pilot Plant* (SAND92-1382, 1993).
- EPA 40 CFR Part 194 certification criteria, especially §194.41 and §194.43.
- WIPP compliance/recertification materials describing active and passive institutional controls.

### Important nuance

Do not write that EPA guarantees a marker message will remain understandable for 10,000 years. EPA's response-to-comments material is more careful: the regulations require the most permanent practicable measures, and credit for passive controls in performance assessment is limited. DOE's conceptual design uses the 10,000-year regulatory frame as its communication ambition, but absolute effectiveness is not guaranteed.

### Boundary

WIPP is an extreme case. It earns vocabulary about communication across succession distance; it does not prove that ordinary infrastructure needs millennial design.

## Chapter 2 — multigenerational construction

### Questions

- Which famous cathedrals or civic works were intentionally built across generations versus simply delayed?
- How did medieval workshops transfer geometry, craft, drawings, templates, authority, and financing?
- Did changing masters materially change the design?
- What modern megaprojects have explicit multigenerational or multi-administration governance?

### Evidence lanes

Use architectural histories, building archives, archaeology, primary records where available, and modern scholarship. Avoid popular myths about medieval builders knowingly sacrificing their entire lives for one fixed plan unless evidence supports it.

## Chapters 3–4 — tacit knowledge and instruction decay

### Questions

- What knowledge is systematically lost at retirement or turnover?
- Which industries use apprenticeship, qualification, watchstanding, or knowledge-capture systems because documentation alone is insufficient?
- How do file formats, dependencies, terminology, measurement systems, and metadata become unreadable?

### Evidence lanes

- NASA and national-laboratory knowledge-retention programs;
- nuclear operations and aviation qualification practices;
- digital preservation standards from Library of Congress, NARA, NIST, or equivalent institutions;
- software reproducibility research;
- organizational knowledge-transfer literature.

### Falsifier

Look for organizations that sustain complex systems across high turnover with minimal explicit knowledge-transfer architecture. If common, the book may overstate founder shadow.

## Chapters 5–9 — the handoff stack

### Artifact

Use lifecycle engineering, museum conservation, infrastructure, and long-lived hardware. Separate durability from maintainability.

### Memory

Use archival science, configuration management, scientific provenance, and maintenance records. Research cases in which enormous record volumes reduced rather than improved usability.

### Authority

Use succession law, constitutional practice, standards bodies, trusts/foundations, and asset governance. Ask how a later actor gains legitimate power to alter something created under a previous authority.

### Resources

Use endowments, decommissioning trusts, infrastructure reserves, pensions, dedicated fees, and lifecycle-costing frameworks. Examine failures where nominal funding existed but assumptions proved wrong.

### Repairability

Use right-to-repair law/policy, military sustainment, infrastructure inspection, open standards, parts obsolescence, and maintainable software. Preserve tradeoffs around security, safety, certification, and performance.

## Chapter 10 — constitutions

Do not claim constitutional longevity is simply a design-success story. Continuity may involve conflict, exclusion, reinterpretation, amendment, informal practice, institutional development, and power. Compare systems with different amendment structures and histories.

Primary lane: constitutional texts, amendment history, court records, Federalist-era primary sources where relevant, and high-quality constitutional scholarship.

## Chapter 11 — archives

Key distinction: storing is not preserving; preserving is not making discoverable; discoverability is not trustworthy provenance; access is not always desirable.

Primary lane: NARA, Library of Congress, National Archives UK or equivalent, OAIS/digital-preservation standards, major archive policies.

Counterevidence: privacy, security, indigenous data sovereignty, expungement, and the value of deliberate forgetting.

## Chapter 12 — bridges / infrastructure

Choose one infrastructure asset with a century-scale history and good inspection/maintenance records. Do not equate design life with service life.

Primary lane: FHWA, state DOT reports, NTSB where failures occurred, asset-management standards, engineering society publications.

Key question: what crosses the handoff besides the structure itself?

## Chapter 13 — seed vaults

Use Svalbard carefully. It is a backup facility within a global network, not a magical self-sustaining ark. Research deposit ownership, duplicate samples, viability, regeneration, withdrawals, and the relationship with national/international genebanks.

Primary lane: Crop Trust, Nordic Genetic Resource Center (NordGen), Norwegian government, FAO treaty materials.

## Chapter 14 — long-running science

Candidate cases:

- Framingham Heart Study;
- Harvard Study of Adult Development;
- astronomical plate archives and sky surveys;
- long ecological time series;
- specimen collections;
- long-baseline climate observations.

Research governance and data continuity, not only scientific findings. What had to be handed over for the time series to retain meaning?

## Chapter 15 — standards

Possible cases: TCP/IP, Unicode, measurement standards, engineering codes, file formats. The chapter should study how communities, specifications, reference implementations, governance, and compatibility keep meaning stable while versions change.

Boundary with *The Compatibility Dividend*: that book studies standards as compressed coordination. This chapter studies standards as intergenerational carriers of meaning and authority. Keep it narrow.

## Chapters 16–19 — failure modes

### Orphaned system

Find cases where a system remained useful but the original owner/vendor disappeared. Study the successor mechanism, not merely the shutdown.

### Lost rationale

Find well-documented cases where successors removed a safeguard because its reason was forgotten, and countercases where obsolete rationale was preserved too long.

### Deferred maintenance

Use audited infrastructure data. Avoid generic "America is crumbling" rhetoric. Connect present benefit to future obligation with actual lifecycle evidence.

### Successor disagreement

Essential chapter. Cases should show that adaptation, replacement, reinterpretation, restitution, or retirement can be successful handoffs. Continuity without agency is not the book's goal.

## Final synthesis falsifier

The Handoff Test fails if it cannot distinguish:

- a system worth preserving from one that should be replaced;
- useful memory from documentation hoarding;
- legitimate continuity from dead-hand control;
- funded stewardship from merely having a fund;
- genuine repairability from unsafe or impractical openness;
- adaptive succession from mission drift.

If the framework rewards longevity for its own sake, rewrite it.

## Novelty / collision discipline

A September 2026 web and Desk scan found substantial work on nuclear semiotics, future generations, long-term stewardship, maintainability, archives, infrastructure lifecycle management, constitutional continuity, and intergenerational justice. No claim of conceptual virgin territory is warranted.

The manuscript's proposed novelty is the cross-domain **handoff stack** and the competent-stranger test: treating century-scale continuity as repeated succession across artifact, memory, authority, resources, repairability, and successor agency.
