# Evidence map — Chapters 8–21

Current-source refresh: **2026-09-06**

This note maps the completion cluster to authoritative evidence lanes and the counterclaims each chapter must preserve in revision. It supplements the source ledger and research spine; it does not substitute for line-level fact checking before a released Shelf snapshot.

## Chapter 8 — The Money

**Claim lane:** Long-lived obligations need financing mechanisms that survive the revenue and actors that created them; a fund is only as good as its assumptions, governance, and adjustment process.

**Authoritative sources:**

- U.S. Nuclear Regulatory Commission — Financial Assurance for Decommissioning: https://www.nrc.gov/waste/decommissioning/finan-assur
- U.S. Nuclear Regulatory Commission — Reactor decommissioning background and current cost-estimate guidance: https://www.nrc.gov/waste/decommissioning/process

**Boundary:** Financial assurance is not a guarantee. Inflation, investment performance, changing scope, legal changes, and underestimated costs can make an apparently funded obligation inadequate.

## Chapter 9 — The Right to Repair

**Claim lane:** Repairability is a form of temporal sovereignty because successors need practical ways to inspect, maintain, replace, or migrate inherited systems when original suppliers disappear.

**Authoritative source:**

- Federal Trade Commission — *Nixing the Fix: An FTC Report to Congress on Repair Restrictions* (2021): https://www.ftc.gov/reports/nixing-fix-ftc-report-congress-repair-restrictions

**Boundary:** Maximum openness is not always safe. Safety, cybersecurity, certification, intellectual property, and quality-control constraints can justify restricted repair pathways. The stronger claim is continued legitimate intervention, not unrestricted modification.

## Chapter 10 — The Constitution You Inherit

**Claim lane:** Constitutional continuity depends on repeated legitimate succession and interpretable change mechanisms, not mere persistence of the original text.

**Primary lane:**

- U.S. Constitution, Article V and amendment history: https://constitution.congress.gov/constitution/article-5/

**Boundary:** Longevity does not prove justice or good design. Constitutional persistence can coexist with exclusion, conflict, capture, and dead-hand control. Replacement can be legitimate when an order loses legitimacy.

## Chapter 11 — The Archive That Waits

**Claim lane:** Digital preservation is active stewardship of usable, authentic, interpretable records; storage alone is insufficient, and selection/access rules are part of the handoff.

**Authoritative sources:**

- National Archives and Records Administration — Digital Preservation: https://www.archives.gov/preservation/digital-preservation
- NARA — Digital Preservation Framework for Risk Assessment and Preservation Planning: https://www.archives.gov/preservation/digital-preservation/risk
- NARA — Records scheduling and appraisal guidance: https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/scheduling

**Boundary:** Preserve-everything logic can create noise, privacy harm, security risk, legal burden, or illegitimate surveillance. Deliberate forgetting can be a legitimate stewardship choice.

## Chapter 12 — The Hundred-Year Bridge

**Claim lane:** Long service life is produced by repeated inspection, maintenance, repair, replacement, funding, and adaptation rather than original material simply persisting.

**Authoritative source:**

- New York City Department of Transportation — Brooklyn Bridge rehabilitation and maintenance history: https://www.nyc.gov/html/dot/html/infrastructure/brooklyn-bridge.shtml

**Boundary:** A historic structure can become unsafe, uneconomic, or functionally obsolete. Preservation of identity and preservation of service are related but not identical goals.

## Chapter 13 — The Seed Vault

**Claim lane:** Svalbard is a backup node in a distributed living genebank system; custody, depositor ownership, metadata, regeneration, and functioning source institutions all matter.

**Authoritative sources:**

- Crop Trust — Svalbard Global Seed Vault: https://www.croptrust.org/what-we-do/programs/svalbard-global-seed-vault/
- NordGen — Svalbard Global Seed Vault: https://www.nordgen.org/our-work/svalbard-global-seed-vault/

**Boundary:** The Seed Vault is not a self-sufficient agricultural ark. Not all crop diversity is conserved as orthodox seed, and agriculture depends on institutions, knowledge, land, water, breeding, and living cultivation.

## Chapter 14 — Science After the Scientist

**Claim lane:** Long-running scientific value depends on methodological lineage, provenance, governance, funding, and visible transitions across instruments and investigators.

**Authoritative source:**

- National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute — Framingham Heart Study: https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/science/framingham-heart-study-fhs

**Boundary:** Continuation is not automatically valuable. A study can become ethically, scientifically, or financially obsolete. The handoff must support informed continuation or closure.

## Chapter 15 — The Standard Nobody Owns

**Claim lane:** Standards act as intergenerational interfaces when governance, specifications, stability promises, independent implementations, and migration rules allow meaning to survive product generations.

**Authoritative source:**

- Unicode Consortium — Unicode Stability Policy, updated 2026-05-26: https://www.unicode.org/policies/stability_policy.html

**Boundary:** Backward compatibility can preserve bad decisions, complexity, and security liabilities. Open governance can still be captured, expensive to participate in, or practically dependent on dominant implementations.

## Chapter 16 — The Orphaned System

**Claim lane:** Systems can outlive solvent owners and active maintainers; anti-orphaning design needs successor authority, transferable records/rights, resources, and safe retirement pathways.

**Authoritative source:**

- U.S. Department of the Interior — Orphaned Wells Program Office: https://www.doi.gov/orphanedwells

**Boundary:** Public backstops can create moral hazard. Financial assurance and liability-transfer mechanisms should reduce incentives to externalize end-of-life costs.

## Chapter 17 — The Lost Rationale

**Claim lane:** Successors need enough evidence about why important constraints exist to distinguish safeguards from residue and to revise mechanisms without accidentally discarding purpose.

**Evidence lane:**

- Existing book sources on NASA knowledge capture, NARA preservation, SUBSAFE record discipline, and institutional decision continuity.

**Boundary:** Rationale can itself be wrong, retrospective, or obsolete. The chapter must reject both blind removal and superstitious preservation.

## Chapter 18 — Deferred Maintenance

**Claim lane:** Deferred maintenance can transfer present service costs into future deterioration, uncertainty, and constrained choice; good deferral requires visible condition, rationale, monitoring, triggers, and a credible repayment path.

**Current authoritative sources:**

- U.S. Government Accountability Office — Federal Buildings: Agencies Need Better Data and Planning to Address Repair Backlogs, GAO-25-108400: https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-25-108400
- U.S. Government Accountability Office — DOD Facilities: Actions Needed to Address Deferred Maintenance, GAO-26-107255, published 2026-08-21: https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-26-107255

**Boundary:** Not every deferred item is neglect. Risk-based prioritization, planned replacement, fiscal emergencies, and improved condition evidence can make delay rational.

## Chapter 19 — The Successor Who Disagrees

**Claim lane:** A successful handoff can end in legitimate retirement or replacement when successors conclude the inherited mechanism no longer deserves continuity.

**Authoritative sources:**

- National Park Service — Elwha River Restoration / Dam Removal: https://www.nps.gov/olym/learn/nature/dam-removal.htm
- National Park Service — Elwha Ecosystem Restoration: https://www.nps.gov/olym/learn/nature/elwha-ecosystem-restoration.htm

**Boundary:** “Successor agency” does not make every later preference wise. Retirement should account for dependencies, transition costs, records, rights, remediation, and affected communities.

## Chapter 20 — The Handoff Test

**Synthesis claim:** The competent-stranger test should expose failures across artifact, memory, authority, resources, repairability, and agency.

**Falsifier:** If the test rewards preservation regardless of value, treats documentation volume as memory quality, or cannot distinguish legitimate adaptation from drift, rewrite it.

## Chapter 21 — The Hundred-Year Handoff

**Synthesis claim:** Century-scale continuity is repeated ordinary succession rather than one founder’s design surviving intact.

**Anchor evidence:** Existing WIPP source set in the source ledger and Chapter 1 evidence note.

**Final boundary:** Long-term framing must remain proportional. It should not justify excessive present cost for systems whose plausible useful life is short, nor treat speculative future benefit as automatically overriding present rights and needs.
