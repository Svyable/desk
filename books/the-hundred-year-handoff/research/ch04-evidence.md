# Chapter 4 evidence notes — The Half-Life of Instructions

Research checked September 3, 2026.

## BBC Domesday as digital-obsolescence case

The 1986 BBC Domesday Project marked the 900th anniversary of the original Domesday Book with an interactive multimedia survey stored on 30 cm laserdiscs and accessed through BBC microcomputer hardware. It included maps, photographs, statistics, video, and community-contributed material.

An IAEA technical report later used it as a preservation-planning case study. The report contrasts the 1086 vellum Domesday Book, still readable after more than 900 years, with the 1986 system, whose laserdisc medium and required hardware had become obsolete in less than 20 years. Rescue efforts by The National Archives and Leeds University depended on finding surviving playback hardware and required extensive specialist work.

Source:

- IAEA Technical Reports Series No. 467, case study II-14, *Preservation Planning to Avoid the Problem of Digital Obsolescence*: https://www-pub.iaea.org/MTCD/Publications/PDF/trs467_web.pdf

The Science Museum Group likewise describes the BBC Domesday system as a prominent example of media obsolescence and notes later data-retrieval work by The National Archives.

Source:

- Science Museum Group collection, Philips LaserVision player / BBC Domesday system: https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/objects/co8095649

A National Archives rescue account describes preservation and emulation work after a surviving 1986 Domesday system was found still operational.

Source:

- *Domesday Redux: The Rescue of the BBC Domesday Project Videodiscs*: https://www.ariadne.ac.uk/issue/36/tna/

## Library of Congress format sustainability

The Library of Congress identifies seven factors relevant to long-term digital-format sustainability: disclosure, adoption, transparency, self-documentation, external dependencies, impact of patents, and technical protection mechanisms. The Library notes that preservation strategies may involve migration, emulation, or hybrid approaches.

Sources:

- Library of Congress, Sustainability of Digital Formats: https://www.loc.gov/preservation/digital/formats/
- Sustainability factors: https://www.loc.gov/preservation/digital/formats/sustain/sustain.shtml
- Format evaluation and relationships: https://www.loc.gov/preservation/digital/formats/intro/format_eval_rel.shtml

Editorial use: these factors support the manuscript's broader idea of dependency depth and recoverability. Do not pretend the Library uses the chapter's "instruction half-life" terminology.

## National Archives preservation planning

The U.S. National Archives' Digital Preservation Framework includes file-format risk analysis, preservation plans for categories of records, and action plans for hundreds of formats. The plans identify significant properties that should be retained where possible during migration.

Source:

- NARA Digital Preservation Framework: https://www.archives.gov/preservation/digital-preservation/risk

NARA guidance on future usability recommends sustainable formats and storage, multiple copies, regular refresh, migration as formats evolve, organization/metadata, and asset-management systems that permit export.

Sources:

- NARA video future-usability guidance: https://www.archives.gov/preservation/formats/video-future-usability.html
- NARA audio future-usability guidance: https://www.archives.gov/preservation/formats/audio-future-usability.html

## Strongest formulation Chapter 4 can safely earn

The preservation problem is not merely survival of bits or physical media. Long-term usability depends on an interpretive dependency chain: format knowledge, tools, environments, semantics, metadata, provenance, and the ability to migrate or emulate. Digital information can be copied with extraordinary fidelity and still become practically inaccessible if that chain decays.

## Claims to avoid

- Do not claim parchment is generally superior to digital storage.
- Do not claim the 1986 Domesday content was permanently lost; significant portions were rescued.
- Do not imply bit preservation is unimportant. It is necessary but insufficient.
- Do not say every dependency must be preserved unchanged. Migration and emulation are established strategies precisely because environments change.
- Do not present "half-life" as a measured universal quantity; it is the chapter's metaphor for dependency decay.
