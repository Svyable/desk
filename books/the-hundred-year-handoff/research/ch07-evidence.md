# Chapter 7 evidence notes — The Authority

Research checked September 3, 2026.

## Core case: Panama Canal authority transfer

The Torrijos-Carter treaty process began in 1977 and provided for transfer of the Panama Canal to Panama by December 31, 1999. Panama Canal Authority materials describe the legal transition and state that the Panama Canal Authority (ACP) is an autonomous public-law entity created under Panama's constitutional framework.

ACP's legal materials assign the Authority exclusive responsibility for administration, operation, conservation, maintenance, and modernization of the Canal and describe financial autonomy and its own assets. Panama approved the ACP's Organic Law on June 11, 1997, before final transfer.

Authoritative sources:

- Panama Canal Authority, Legal Foundations: https://pancanal.com/en/legal-foundations/
- Panama Canal Authority, Fundamentos Legales: https://pancanal.com/fundamentos-legales/
- Panama Canal Authority, Lo pactado: https://pancanal.com/tratados-torrijos-carter/lo-pactado/
- Panama Canal Authority, Frequently Asked Questions: https://pancanal.com/preguntas-frecuentes/

## Claim to earn

The Canal case shows that a durable handoff includes a receiving authority with a recognized legal basis, scope, resources, and succession path. The physical asset can remain largely continuous while the institution entitled to make binding decisions changes.

Do not imply that the transfer was frictionless, apolitical, or reducible to administrative planning. The Canal's sovereignty history was contested and politically consequential. The chapter uses the case to illuminate authority architecture, not to summarize the full geopolitical history.

## Countercase: Barnes Foundation and dead-hand control

The Barnes Foundation litigation is a useful countercase because the governing documents carried strong founder restrictions into later decades. In 2004 the Montgomery County Orphans' Court approved administrative changes including relocation of the collection and expansion of the board after litigation over the foundation's financial and operational circumstances. Later Pennsylvania appellate decisions summarize that procedural history.

Sources:

- In re The Barnes Foundation, 69 Pa. D. & C.4th 129 (Montgomery County Court of Common Pleas, Dec. 13, 2004): https://www.leagle.com/decision/69255918dc4th12911
- Pennsylvania Superior Court, In Re: The Barnes Foundation Appeal of Feudale (2013): https://law.justia.com/cases/pennsylvania/superior-court/2013/810-eda-2012.html

Important doctrinal nuance: the 2004 proceedings involved the doctrine of administrative deviation; do not casually label the entire result "cy pres." The chapter need not teach trust doctrine in depth. Its purpose is to show the structural tension between preserving founder constraints and preserving a mission under changed conditions.

## Boundary claims

Avoid:

- saying the Canal's transfer occurred without operational continuity work before 1999;
- equating possession or credentials with legitimate authority;
- saying the Barnes court simply ignored Barnes's intent;
- presenting court-approved deviation as automatically wise or neutral;
- implying every long-lived institution should be insulated from democratic control;
- treating formal authority documents as sufficient if practical decision power remains elsewhere.

## Strongest safe formulation

A durable authority handoff gives successors a recognized source of power, a defined scope, a succession rule, and a legitimate revision path. It protects a mission from casual capture without making the founder's administrative choices impossible to revisit.
