A book about a deceptively simple geometric stack — square, crescent, downward triangle, interpenetrating triangles, circle, Ajna/Om — and the possibility that its real usefulness is neither religious proof nor occult code, but a compact grammar for thinking about emergence.

The manuscript treats the historical chakra/tantric material, modern cognitive science, philosophy of mind, and metaphysical speculation as separate evidentiary layers. It does **not** claim that ancient practitioners predicted neuroscience. Its central proposition is more disciplined and more interesting: consciousness may be less usefully conceived as an extra substance or final layer of reality than as the point at which a physical system becomes recursively capable of representing the world, representing itself representing the world, and acting back on the world.

The geometric shorthand is:

**boundary → cycle → direction → integration → field → recursion**

Or:

**form → change → action → relation → model → self-model → action upon form**

The loop matters more than the ladder.

## Working thesis

A square does not know it is a square.

Somewhere between inert matter and reflective mind, physical systems acquire boundaries, dynamics, directed regulation, integrated models, self-models, and recursive access to their own representations. The Formula investigates whether that progression gives us a better way to frame consciousness without pretending the hard problem has disappeared.

## Research posture

Keep four layers visibly distinct throughout:

1. **Historical:** what tantric, chakra, yogic, and related traditions actually say, using primary texts and qualified scholarship.
2. **Scientific:** what neuroscience, cognitive science, complex systems, predictive processing, metacognition, control theory, and embodied cognition can support empirically.
3. **Philosophical:** what follows as an argument about selfhood, representation, recursion, subjectivity, and causation.
4. **Speculative:** what might be true if the pattern generalizes farther than the evidence currently permits.
