# The Museum Problem

The world's mineral collections contain millions of decisions made by humans who liked unusual rocks.

That makes museums simultaneously priceless and biased.

Collectors preferentially preserve beauty, rarity, locality and novelty. Mining districts are oversampled. Inconspicuous phases may be discarded. Locality labels can be wrong. Old specimens may have been treated, glued or contaminated. Yet collections also preserve material from mines that no longer exist and landscapes since destroyed.

For the mineral-shadow project, museums are latent observatories.

One could reanalyze historical specimens with modern microprobes, synchrotron methods and isotope systems, looking not for aliens but for the boundaries of natural mineral diversity. Which supposedly exotic industrial-like phases already occur naturally? Which associations never do? How often do database absences reflect sampling rather than chemistry?

This work is less glamorous than announcing a technosignature. It is also what makes a future announcement believable.

The denominator is stored in drawers.
