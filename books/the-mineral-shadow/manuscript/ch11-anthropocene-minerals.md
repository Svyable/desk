# Anthropocene Minerals

Humans have become mineral-forming agents without needing to qualify as a geological epoch.

Mines expose reactive surfaces. Smelters create slags. Fires produce new phases. Concrete carbonates and alters. Waste heaps develop mineral ecologies. Industrial sites concentrate elements into combinations rarely encountered naturally. Researchers have cataloged numerous mineral-like compounds associated with human activity, while carefully distinguishing formally recognized minerals from synthetic substances excluded by mineralogical definitions.

The nomenclature matters. A synthetic crystal is not automatically a mineral under standard definitions. Yet for planetary forensics, the process distinction is more important than the label. A future investigator would care that a phase required a furnace, refinery or controlled atmosphere whether a committee called it a mineral.

This is the bridge from biosignature to technosignature. Biology expands accessible chemical pathways. Technology expands them again by imposing temperatures, pressures, purification steps and reaction sequences that ordinary surface geology may rarely achieve.

The scientific question is whether any resulting material assemblage is sufficiently improbable under natural processes to function as evidence of technology.

Our own planet gives us the training set.
