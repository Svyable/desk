# The Shadow in Stone

The most interesting thing about a crystal may be the absent process it implies.

Mineral evolution gives us a disciplined way to think about planetary history as expanding and contracting chemical possibility. Biology altered Earth's mineral repertoire. Human technology is altering it again. Neither observation grants us an easy extraterrestrial diagnostic. It gives us something better: a program of comparative tests.

The four evidence classes remain separate at the end.

Verified: Earth mineral diversity changed through planetary history; oxygenation and biological activity opened mineral-forming pathways; humans create novel crystalline compounds and unusual assemblages; extraterrestrial materials already demonstrate that natural chemistry outside Earth can surprise us.

Credible but incomplete: mineral-diversity statistics may eventually constrain planetary biological history; industrial assemblages may contain durable process signatures useful in deep-time archaeology.

Disputed or unconfirmed: no known extraterrestrial mineral specimen establishes alien technology, and unusual composition alone is not evidence of manufacture.

Speculative: a calibrated mineral-technosignature pipeline might one day identify material whose best explanation is technological processing.

The discipline is to make that last category difficult to enter.

Stone remembers only what survives. We supply the inference. The entire scientific burden lies in not confusing the two.
