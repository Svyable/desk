# Life as a Geological Reagent

A lichen on a rock looks like decoration until you remember that it is doing chemistry.

Organisms acidify, chelate, oxidize, reduce, precipitate and concentrate. Microbes alter sulfur and iron. Shell builders move carbonate into structures. Plants change soils and water flow. Fungi tunnel into minerals. Biology is not simply sitting on geology. It is one of geology's reagents.

This complicates the usual hierarchy in which fossils are direct evidence and minerals are background. Biominerals can be direct products of organisms, but many more minerals are indirect consequences of biological alteration of an environment. The causal distance varies.

That distance should be recorded, not blurred. A shell mineralized by an organism is not epistemically identical to an oxidized uranium phase made possible by an atmosphere whose oxygen ultimately came largely from photosynthesis. Both are biological in a broad planetary sense. Only one has a short causal chain.

This matters off Earth. Remote spectroscopy will often give us mineral classes rather than organisms. Returned samples will be tiny and precious. A sensible astrobiology of minerals must rank causal proximity: direct biomineralization, biologically mediated precipitation, environmentally enabled mineralization, and merely compatible mineralogy.

The word compatible is doing useful work. Most of the universe will not confess.
