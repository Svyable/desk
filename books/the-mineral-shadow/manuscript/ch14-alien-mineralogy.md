# Alien Mineralogy

Alien mineralogy begins with a humiliating possibility: the strange rock is ordinary where it came from.

Meteorites taught this repeatedly. Phases rare at Earth's surface can be normal products of reducing nebular chemistry, parent-body alteration or shock. High-pressure minerals can arrive from violent histories rather than factories.

Any extraterrestrial technosignature claim therefore needs a local geological model. What elements are available? What oxidation states? What pressure-temperature trajectories? What impact regimes? What aqueous alteration? What photochemistry?

Only after those questions does artificial synthesis deserve a seat at the table.

This is especially important for returned asteroid and lunar samples. Modern analytical instruments can detect astonishingly small inclusions. Sensitivity creates anomalies faster than theory can contextualize them. Contamination adds another source: spacecraft materials, curation hardware, terrestrial dust and laboratory reagents.

The alien-mineral problem is thus also a custody problem. Extraordinary composition without extraordinary provenance is weak evidence.

The first alien technology we find, if we ever do, may look less like a saucer than a grain nobody can explain. That makes chain of custody part of astrobiology.
