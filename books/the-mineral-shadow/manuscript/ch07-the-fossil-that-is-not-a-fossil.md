# The Fossil That Is Not a Fossil

A fossil is persuasive because morphology can preserve a once-living structure. Mineralogical biosignatures are more treacherous. They often preserve environmental consequence rather than anatomy.

Magnetite is the classic warning. Particular magnetite crystals have been discussed as possible biosignatures because magnetotactic bacteria make highly controlled magnetic particles. But magnetite also forms through abiotic processes. Morphology, purity, size and chain arrangement can strengthen or weaken a biological interpretation; none should be detached from geological context.

The lesson is not that mineral biosignatures fail. It is that single-feature arguments fail.

A robust claim should combine mineral phase, texture, isotopes, organic chemistry, spatial context and plausible formation pathway. The more independent constraints converge, the less room remains for a mundane route. Independence matters: five measurements from the same altered grain are not necessarily five independent witnesses.

This is the same epistemic problem that appears in UAP sensor fusion, nuclear-test attribution and cryptid evidence, but here the witnesses are instruments interrogating a rock.

Stone is patient. Interpretation is not.
