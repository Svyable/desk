# Mineral Evolution

Mineral evolution is powerful partly because it is easy to misunderstand.

It does not say minerals are alive. It says mineral diversity is historically contingent. New planetary processes create new mineral-forming environments, and once those environments exist, new species can appear and persist.

The framework divides Earth's history into broad stages: presolar and planetary materials, differentiation and crust formation, aqueous alteration, tectonic cycling, oxygenation, biological innovation and eventually human activity. The exact stage boundaries are less important than the methodological shift. Mineralogy becomes temporal.

This temporal view creates testable questions. Which mineral species require free oxygen? Which require biological concentration of an element? Which depend on plate tectonics? Which phases survive long enough to remain diagnostic? How quickly does a planet's mineral diversity saturate under a given set of processes?

There is also a warning. Species counts are not pure natural facts. Mineral nomenclature is a human classification system. Sampling is wildly uneven. Earth is mined, drilled, blasted, traded and curated with an intensity no other planet receives. A rare Earth mineral might appear absent on Mars simply because no rover has driven over it.

The research program therefore needs occurrence probabilities and formation pathways, not just lists. A mineral shadow should be modeled as a network of processes and products.
