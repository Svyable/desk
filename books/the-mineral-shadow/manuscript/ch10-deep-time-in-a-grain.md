# Deep Time in a Grain

Zircon is a small object with an indecent amount of time inside it.

Its crystal lattice can incorporate uranium while largely excluding lead during formation, enabling uranium-lead geochronology. Ancient zircons preserve information about crustal processes and early Earth conditions across billions of years.

The relevance here is durability. Technosignature discussions often privilege radio because radio travels. Minerals offer the opposite strategy: do not travel; persist.

A manufactured phase buried in a stable geological environment might outlast languages, institutions and surface structures. But persistence is selective. Radiation damages lattices. Fluids alter phases. Metamorphism recrystallizes. Erosion moves grains. Subduction destroys crust. A durable signature requires both a resistant material and a favorable archive.

Deep time therefore converts the search for old technology into taphonomy. What survives? Where? In what altered form? Could natural processes imitate the residue?

The answer will differ radically between Earth, Moon and Mars. Earth is chemically and tectonically aggressive. The Moon is comparatively quiet but heavily gardened by impacts. Mars sits somewhere stranger.

There is no universal archive. There are only survival functions.
