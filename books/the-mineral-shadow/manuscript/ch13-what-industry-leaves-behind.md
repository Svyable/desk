# What Industry Leaves Behind

Civilizations do not leave technologies evenly. They leave dumps.

Archaeology has always known this. Middens are valuable because ordinary disposal preserves distributions of activity better than monuments preserve daily life. Industrial archaeology extends the principle to slag, tailings, ash, refractory brick, mine spoil and contaminated sediment.

A mineral technosignature search should begin there conceptually. Not with a pristine machine waiting under dust, but with process waste.

Smelting fractionates elements. Nuclear technology can alter isotopic inventories. Chemical manufacture produces catalysts and residues. High-temperature processes create phases that later weather into secondary minerals. The signature may therefore be a chain: primary industrial product, discarded waste, alteration rind, sedimentary incorporation.

Each step erases information while creating new information.

This chapter's conjecture is that waste mineralogy may outlive recognizable artifacts because waste is volumetrically large and distributed. The conjecture needs quantitative work: production masses, weathering rates, sediment dilution, tectonic survival and natural background distributions.

A technosignature that cannot survive those calculations is a story, not a search target.
