# Before Biology

Strip Earth of biology and the planet does not become mineralogically empty. It becomes simpler in a specific way.

The early Solar System already supplied a remarkable chemistry. Meteorites contain minerals made under reducing conditions, high temperatures, aqueous alteration and shock. Planetary differentiation added another engine: cores, mantles and crusts separated; magmas cooled; water circulated; impacts remade local pressure and temperature. None of this requires a cell.

This prebiotic baseline is indispensable because astrobiology has a bad habit of discovering a complicated thing and quietly promoting complication into life. Nature is perfectly capable of baroque chemistry without biology. Hydrothermal systems manufacture gradients. Ultraviolet light drives reactions. Serpentinization produces hydrogen. Impact events create phases that would look exotic in a hand specimen.

A useful mineral biosignature therefore cannot merely be rare. It has to be improbable under a well-specified abiotic model.

That is harder than it sounds. We do not possess a census of all minerals that every plausible rocky planet can make. We possess one extraordinarily well-sampled inhabited planet, a handful of meteorites, remote spectroscopy of other bodies, and a growing but still thin archive of returned extraterrestrial material. The denominator is weak.

The book will keep returning to this denominator problem. If we call a mineral anomalous because Earth laboratories seldom see it, we may only be measuring our sampling habits. If we call a phase technological because industry makes it, we need to show why a natural furnace, impact plume, fumarole or lightning channel could not.

Before biology, then, is not a chapter to get through. It is the control group.
