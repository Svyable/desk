# Spinach nanobionics audit — the plant is not the receptor

## Scope

This is one bounded source audit for **THE LISTENING FIELD**, stacked on the active manuscript branch rather than revising Chapter 1 concurrently. It checks the 2016/2017 spinach-nanobionics experiment because the planned Chapter 7, “The Spinach Experiment,” could otherwise become an attractive but misleading bridge between ordinary plant physiology, DARPA Advanced Plant Technologies, and eX Virentia.

## Primary experiment checked

Min Hao Wong et al., “Nitroaromatic detection and infrared communication from wild-type plants using plant nanobionics,” *Nature Materials* 16 (2017), 264–272. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/nmat4771

The experiment used living spinach as a transport, pre-concentration, support, and communication substrate for **engineered single-walled carbon-nanotube nanosensors** placed in leaf mesophyll. One nanotube sensor chemistry responded to nitroaromatic compounds; another supplied a reference signal. Nitroaromatics taken up through the roots were transported into the leaf, where changes in near-infrared fluorescence could be read externally.

The paper reports a combined root/stem residence time of about **8.3 minutes** and a leaf residence-time estimate of **1.9 min/mm**. The important object is therefore a chain: chemical in water → root uptake/transpiration → transport through living plant → encounter with an engineered nanosensor → altered near-infrared fluorescence → optical readout.

MIT's contemporaneous institutional account says the laboratory readout was demonstrated at about **one metre** using an infrared camera/computer arrangement and describes roughly ten minutes for contaminated groundwater to be drawn into the leaves under the demonstrated setup. That is useful engineering context, not evidence of landscape-scale remote sensing.

Authoritative institutional account: https://news.mit.edu/2016/nanobionic-spinach-plants-detect-explosives-1031

## The boundary the manuscript needs

The headline “spinach detects explosives” compresses two different inventions into one sentence.

The plant contributes capabilities that are genuinely valuable: roots sample water; transpiration moves analytes without an external pump; living tissue supports the embedded sensor; the organism is self-powered in the ordinary biological sense. But the **molecular recognition function used in this experiment was engineered into the plant with carbon nanotube sensors**. The paper is explicitly about giving wild-type plants a non-native function through plant nanobionics.

That makes this experiment a strong case for **plant as platform**, not evidence that unmodified spinach naturally identifies explosives. Chapter 7 should preserve that distinction because the later eX Virentia story asks almost the inverse question: can naturally occurring, unmodified vegetation carry a useful signature of a man-made chemical exposure without inserting a purpose-built molecular receptor?

DARPA's current eX Virentia program description is explicit on that point: its goal is to use **existing unmodified vegetation** as a sensor for activities of Department of War interest by relating natural internal biochemical responses to externally observable characteristics.

DARPA source: https://www.darpa.mil/research/programs/ex-virentia

The contrast is better than a smooth historical progression:

- nanobionic spinach: keep the plant, add a purpose-built molecular detector;
- APT: engineer plants so desired exposures produce remotely observable responses;
- eX Virentia: ask whether the unmodified plant's own response already contains enough specific information to read.

These approaches share the organism but move the sensing burden to different places in the chain.

## Strongest counterargument to the book

The spinach result weakens any claim that the strategic novelty lies primarily in vegetation's *native* ability to preserve informative environmental traces. A substantial part of the engineering advantage may come from treating a plant as cheap plumbing, power, deployment geometry, and analyte pre-concentration while the actual specificity comes from an engineered receptor. If that architecture wins on accuracy and cost, the grander “landscape as archive” thesis may be less important than ordinary hybrid sensor engineering.

The converse possibility also matters. The experiment's approximately metre-scale optical readout and controlled analyte delivery are far upstream of wide-area surveillance. If useful performance collapses with realistic soil transport, mixed chemicals, weather, genotype, sensor lifetime, optical clutter, and long standoff distance, the living platform may add complexity without solving the hard sensing problem.

The manuscript should allow either result to narrow the book.

## Quantitative burden before Chapter 7

Before prose makes operational claims, recover from the full paper and supplement:

- number and condition of plants;
- analyte identities and concentrations;
- false-positive/interferent panel and selectivity definition;
- uptake geometry and water/soil conditions;
- sensor loading and between-leaf / between-plant variance;
- response and recovery times;
- optical excitation and camera requirements;
- demonstrated standoff distance and signal-to-noise;
- sensor persistence in living tissue;
- whether repeated exposures were tested;
- what part of the signal came from native physiology versus nanotube chemistry.

Do not turn the paper's residence-time estimates into a generic detection time for field explosives.

## Editorial payoff

The best eventual scene is not “scientists taught spinach to smell bombs.” It is stranger and more precise. The spinach did several jobs for free—sampling water, moving it uphill, maintaining living tissue—and the engineers inserted the part evolution had not supplied: a fluorescent molecular recognition system tuned to the target chemistry. A camera then had to interrogate that engineered signal.

That gives the book a concrete question to carry into APT and eX Virentia: **which part of a living sensor are we actually borrowing, and which part are we secretly rebuilding?**

The question should remain prose, not become another named framework.

## Publication holds

- Do not say unmodified spinach naturally detects explosives from this experiment.
- Do not say the plant itself emitted an email or radio alert; external optics/electronics performed the readout and communication.
- Do not generalize the demonstrated roughly one-metre optical readout into aerial or satellite sensing.
- Do not turn nitroaromatic recognition into universal explosive identification; preserve the tested analytes and interferents.
- Do not conflate the nanobionic experiment with DARPA APT or eX Virentia unless a source establishes a programmatic lineage.
- Do not use the experiment as evidence for plant cognition, intention, or consciousness.
