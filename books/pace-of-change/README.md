# Pace of Change

*Why the Future Breaks When Our Clocks Fall Out of Sync*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Author** | Sven Hardy Benson |
| **Status** | Complete first draft |
| **Chapters** | 20 of 20 drafted |

*Pace of Change* argues that change itself is not the hidden variable that decides whether progress feels liberating or destabilizing. Pace is. Every system has a rate at which it can notice, interpret, absorb, learn from, maintain, and legitimize change. Technology, capital, law, organizations, infrastructure, skills, families, trust, and biology do not run on the same clock. When those clocks separate, the gap becomes consequential: a software change can propagate in minutes while recovery takes days; a product can reach millions before a regulator has vocabulary for it; a skill can lose market value faster than a worker can acquire a replacement; a company can change priorities faster than its people can learn whether the last change worked.

The book does not argue that faster is bad or slower is wise. It argues for **temporal fit**: matching the rate of change to the rate at which the surrounding system can produce reliable feedback and recover from error. Its practical claim is that leaders, institutions, workers, and designers should treat pace as an explicit design variable rather than as an accidental by-product of capability.
