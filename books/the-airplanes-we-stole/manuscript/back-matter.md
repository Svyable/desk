# Back Matter

## A note on what this book does not establish

Nothing in this manuscript establishes that the United States or any private contractor possesses technology of non-human origin. Nothing establishes that David Grusch's 2023 retrieval allegations are false merely because publicly documented foreign-materiel programs look different. Nothing in AARO's 2024 historical report should be treated as omniscient simply because it is official. Nothing in a whistleblower's clearance history should be treated as proof merely because the person worked close to classified systems.

The narrower claim is methodological. Real physical exploitation programs create constrained kinds of residue. Some residue can remain classified for decades. Some can be hidden by cover stories. Some can be transferred to contractors or partner states. But the larger and longer the program, the harder it becomes to avoid all of the following simultaneously: acquisition history, custody changes, transport problems, specialized facilities, unusual maintenance, safety events, test instrumentation, people with narrow technical jobs, procurement anomalies, downstream doctrine, successor organizations, foreign counterintelligence interest, and eventually documentary or material traces.

The book's proposed retrieval-signature method is therefore falsifiable. It would be weakened if well-documented historical black hardware programs routinely left no discoverable physical, organizational, archival, doctrinal, personnel, or adversary trace even many decades after termination. It would be strengthened if alleged UAP exploitation programs begin producing independently authenticated evidence across several such lanes, especially evidence generated before public controversy fixed the expected story.

## Evidence classes used throughout

**Class 1 — verified/declassified/documented:** official records, declassified reports, authenticated archival documents, acknowledged programs, surviving hardware, primary scientific measurements, and public records with checkable provenance.

**Class 2 — credible but incomplete:** serious reporting, testimony from plausibly placed witnesses, participant recollection, or technically coherent reconstruction that lacks enough independent evidence for Class 1.

**Class 3 — disputed/unconfirmed:** claims without independent confirmation, contested provenance, alleged program names or events supported principally by repetition or secondhand testimony, and extraordinary origin claims not established by material evidence.

**Class 4 — speculative synthesis/open conjecture:** the author's analytical extensions, proposed signatures, counterfactuals, and experiments.
