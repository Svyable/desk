# AARO and the Argument from Absence

AARO's February 2024 Historical Record Report Volume I states that it found no empirical evidence that the U.S. government or private companies have been reverse-engineering extraterrestrial technology. It says alleged programs described by interviewees resolved into nonexistent efforts, misidentified sensitive programs, or proposals and compartments that did not possess the claimed material.

That is an important official finding.

It is not the same thing as proof that no hidden program exists.

Negative historical investigations depend on access, search strategy, record survival, witness selection, program naming, compartment boundaries, and the possibility that relevant activity was deliberately omitted from ordinary channels. AARO says it obtained broad access. Critics dispute the adequacy of its methods and trustworthiness. Those disputes can be evaluated only with records, not posture.

The foreign-materiel cases help calibrate the negative claim. HAVE DOUGHNUT and CONSTANT PEG show that significant programs can remain publicly hidden for decades. They also show that once properly investigated with archival access, real programs generate converging evidence.

The right use of AARO is therefore Bayesian rather than theological. Its failure to find empirical evidence lowers confidence in the retrieval narrative to the extent its access and search were competent. It does not lower confidence to zero.

Likewise, the persistence of allegations raises the priority of testing them when the sources are plausibly placed. It does not raise their truth probability to one.

A good investigation should make itself vulnerable. It should publish enough method that a later archive can reveal where it missed.

A good extraordinary claim should do the same.
