# The Retrieval Signature

The retrieval signature is not a score that spits out aliens or no aliens.

It is a way to stop asking one piece of evidence to do every job.

**Custody:** Is there a recoverable chain from event to storage?

**Material:** Is there independently characterized hardware or sample evidence with controlled provenance?

**Operations:** Was the object merely stored, or tested and operated?

**Sustainment:** What recurring maintenance, consumables, tooling, or environmental controls were required?

**People:** What specialties and career patterns would the work need?

**Facilities:** What physical spaces, ranges, labs, shielding, power, isolation, or instrumentation would be expected?

**Risk:** What safety events or occupational protections would follow?

**Translation:** What doctrine, requirements, designs, or research programs changed because of the work?

**Adversary response:** Did foreign intelligence services behave as if the capability or captured object mattered?

**Archive:** Do contemporaneous records exist that predate the later public story?

HAVE DOUGHNUT scores strongly across many of these lanes because the program is documented. A fictional black program invented after the fact may score strongly on narrative specificity but weakly on independent lanes.

An authentic UAP exploitation program would not have to reproduce the MiG pattern exactly. It should create its own coherent pattern.

The strongest future evidence would be cross-lane evidence: a document naming a specimen that matches a facility record, which matches a material sample, which matches a witness whose role existed before the story became public.

Corroboration is not several people saying the same sentence.

It is different systems leaving the same shape.
