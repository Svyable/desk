# The Program That Could Lose

A theory becomes useful when it can be embarrassed.

The retrieval-signature approach can fail in several ways. A future declassification could reveal a genuine non-human exploitation program whose public residue was far thinner than this book predicts. Authenticated material could arrive with a custody chain that forces the extraordinary origin question open. AARO's access claims could collapse under documentary evidence. Or the historical control cases could prove less comparable than I have assumed because the physics, security, and institutional architecture of genuinely anomalous technology are qualitatively different from captured aircraft.

Those possibilities belong in the book now, before the evidence arrives.

The opposite failure is equally important. Years may pass with more testimony but no new independent evidence lanes. Program names may multiply while provenance remains absent. Alleged samples may continue to lose their chain of custody before reaching instruments. Claims of revolutionary exploitation may remain technologically sterile. If that happens, confidence should fall even if the witnesses remain sincere.

This is not cynicism. It is what we already do with HAVE DOUGHNUT.

Nobody needs faith that the United States tested the MiG-21. The program survived the transition from secret history to ordinary history because multiple forms of evidence converged. The airplane became a documented object inside a documented process.

The unresolved UAP question deserves the same standard precisely because the claim is larger.

If a non-human vehicle exists in a hangar somewhere, it is not protected from physics by the security badge on the door. It has mass. It occupies volume. It has surfaces. It responds to fields or does not. It can be sampled or there is a reason it cannot. People can measure it badly and then better. Machines can fail to understand machines.

The first extraordinary thing about the secret MiGs was that they were really there.

The second was more useful: eventually, we learned what the people around them actually did all day.

That is the kind of revelation worth waiting for.

Not another code name.

A world.
