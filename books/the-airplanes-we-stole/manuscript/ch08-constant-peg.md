# CONSTANT PEG

By 1977, the United States had moved beyond occasional exploitation flights. CONSTANT PEG turned the insight into an institution.

The 4477th Test and Evaluation Squadron, the Red Eagles, operated Soviet-designed MiG-17s, MiG-21s, and later MiG-23s from the Nevada ranges. The Air Force says the program ran through 1988 and exposed almost 6,000 American aircrew to real MiGs in more than 15,000 sorties.

The scale changes the evidentiary problem.

A hundred-flight test campaign can be compartmented as an exceptional project. Fifteen thousand sorties require an ecosystem. Pilots must be selected and checked out. Maintenance must become repeatable. Airspace must be reserved. Students must be brought into the secret at the right moment. Aircraft must be hidden from casual observation. Fuel and parts must arrive. Flight records must exist. Mishaps must be investigated. Commanders must argue for money.

The existence of CONSTANT PEG was not publicly acknowledged until 2006, almost two decades after operations ended. That delay demonstrates how long meaningful secrecy can persist. It also demonstrates its limit. When declassification finally came, the program was not represented by one ambiguous memo. There was an organizational history.

This is the distinction I want to preserve when comparing acknowledged exploitation with alleged UAP retrieval programs. A true program may be invisible to the public during operation. Mature physical activity should nonetheless have internal depth. If later evidence appears, it should reveal a world rather than a slogan.

CONSTANT PEG has names, places, aircraft, deaths, sorties, commanders, students, facilities, and downstream training effects. Its reality thickens as sources accumulate.

A false program often behaves differently. Its name may become more famous than its mechanics. Each new account points sideways to another account. Specific technical claims drift. Access is always one compartment away. Documentation is perpetually about the existence of the secret rather than the work performed inside it.

That difference is not proof. It is a research heuristic.

Real programs have chores.
