# Compartmentation: What It Can Hide

Compartmentation works because most people do not need the whole truth to perform a narrow job.

A maintainer needs the procedure. A security officer needs the access list. A pilot needs the envelope. A contracting officer needs a legal vehicle and funding authority. A scientist may need only a sample code and experimental requirement. The origin story can be withheld from all of them.

This architecture can hide purpose remarkably well.

It can also produce honest witnesses who misunderstand what they touched. A person may know with certainty that the material was unusual, the security exceptional, the program unacknowledged, and the customer strange while knowing very little about provenance.

That is why “firsthand” must be specified. Firsthand knowledge of a compartment is not necessarily firsthand knowledge of the object's origin. Firsthand handling of a material is not necessarily firsthand observation of its recovery. Firsthand knowledge that a program name exists is not firsthand knowledge that the folklore attached to the name is correct.

Historical black programs used exactly these boundaries.

The lesson for UAP research is not to distrust witnesses. It is to map what each witness could directly know.

Compartmentation can hide integration by splitting it across people.

It cannot turn secondhand origin into firsthand origin.
