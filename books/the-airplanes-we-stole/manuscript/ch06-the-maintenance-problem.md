# The Maintenance Problem

A secret airplane is most revealing when it breaks.

The glamorous part of foreign-materiel exploitation is acquisition. The durable part is maintenance. CONSTANT PEG's later MiG fleet arrived without a normal American supply chain, and official Air Force museum history describes aircraft reaching the unit in poor condition and requiring extensive repair and reconditioning. Maintainers had to keep a small, irreplaceable collection flying safely enough to train thousands of aircrew.

This is where the mythology of reverse engineering meets the tyranny of consumables. Tires age. Seals leak. hydraulic fluid contaminates. fasteners strip. bearings wear. wiring cracks. engines ingest debris. Instruments drift. A machine that can fly still has to fly tomorrow.

Maintenance is therefore a better discriminator than the first retrieval story because it repeats. A one-time recovery can be hidden by a small group. A decade of operation requires routines. Routines require tools, parts, inspection standards, documentation, and people who become unusually good at solving the same obscure failures.

If a program really possessed exotic vehicles and attempted to operate them, this burden would become extreme. Unknown materials may not tolerate ordinary machining. Unknown energy systems may require novel shielding. Unknown fields may interfere with instruments. Even a passive object needs environmental control if corrosion, contamination, temperature, radiation, or mechanical stress can alter it.

The stronger claim—successful reproduction—requires more. Manufacturing a copied component means metrology, precursor materials, fabrication equipment, quality assurance, failed batches, waste streams, and people who can tell a good part from a bad one. Revolutionary technology does not remove industrial process. It creates new industrial process.

That is why the retrieval-signature matrix in this book gives repeated logistics its own lane. Maintenance is difficult to romanticize, which is precisely why it is useful. It produces invoices, tool marks, safety rules, training, staffing patterns, and expertise that may survive the secrecy of the original purpose.

CONSTANT PEG's maintainers are historically important because they prove a simple point. Even when the government truly owns the secret airplane, somebody still has to find the leak.
