## Premise

Most of the world does not happen inside things. It happens where things touch: where a tire meets pavement, a bearing race meets a rolling element, a connector pin meets its mate, blood meets an implant, bone meets titanium, electrolyte meets electrode, metal meets semiconductor, and a robot finger meets an object it is trying not to drop. The working thesis of this book is that contact is a hidden primitive of civilization — a small boundary that often determines the behavior, lifetime, safety, and meaning of the much larger systems attached to it.

## Review status

This is a complete first Desk draft. It deliberately crosses mechanical engineering, tribology, heat transfer, electrical reliability, sensory biology, cell adhesion, biomaterials, microbiology, electrochemistry, semiconductor physics, and robotics. The research trail is designed to keep the synthesis honest: “contact” is the organizing lens, not a claim that all interfaces obey one equation or that surface effects always dominate bulk properties.
