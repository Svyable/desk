# Back Matter

## A note on the synthesis

*Where Things Touch* is intentionally cross-disciplinary. It does not propose a universal theory in which tires, cell adhesions, battery interphases, and semiconductor contacts are governed by one mechanism. They are not. The book uses contact as an organizing question: what must cross the boundary, what prevents it, how does the boundary evolve, and when does the interface become the system-level bottleneck?

The research trail in `research/` preserves the distinctions that the narrative necessarily compresses. Each chapter note identifies the major evidence used and a falsification or limits section describing where the chapter's framing could become misleading.

## On evidence

The manuscript favors primary sources, government technical reports, peer-reviewed reviews, and institutional research summaries that link to underlying publications. Historical numbers about economic losses from friction and wear are treated as period estimates rather than timeless constants. Clinical statements are framed as mechanisms and research findings, not individualized medical advice. Emerging battery, semiconductor, and robotics results are described as frontier evidence rather than settled commercial outcomes.

## Acknowledgments

This manuscript stands on disciplines whose practitioners have spent careers studying the small regions most people never see: tribologists reading wear scars, thermal engineers measuring joint conductance, connector engineers counting milliohms after vibration, cell biologists watching adhesions assemble, biomaterials researchers following protein adsorption, electrochemists trying to preserve nanometer-scale interphases, semiconductor engineers fighting contact resistance, and roboticists teaching machines how not to drop things.

Their work makes the large world function at small boundaries.

## Author

Sven Hardy Benson writes about software, intelligence, institutions, technology, and the strange places where the future becomes practical. *Where Things Touch* is a departure in subject and a continuation in method: start with the hidden mechanism, follow it across systems, and keep asking what happens when the abstraction meets reality.
