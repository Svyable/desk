## Claims ledger

### Class 1 — verified / documented

- MOGUL grew from the postwar requirement to detect Soviet nuclear activity at long range.
- Maurice Ewing’s ocean-acoustics work helped motivate the hypothesis that the atmosphere might contain a comparable low-loss acoustic region.
- The program combined military secrecy with work by civilian scientific institutions including NYU, Columbia/Woods Hole-linked expertise, UCLA and others documented in the Air Force history.
- Balloon trains carried acoustic and tracking hardware; altitude control and reliable station-keeping were practical problems, not decorative details.
- The 1994 Air Force investigation concluded that debris recovered near Roswell was consistent with a balloon device and most likely associated with MOGUL.
- Modern peer-reviewed balloon experiments have measured stratospheric infrasound environments, recorded a buried chemical explosion at 56 km from a balloon when a ground sensor at 46 km did not see the same signal, and in 2023 reported direct observation of an elevated acoustic duct using a known airborne source.

### Class 2 — credible but incomplete

- The exact reconstruction of which June 1947 balloon train produced the ranch debris depends on flight logs, terminology, launch cancellations, weather reconstruction, personnel recollections and how “Flight 4” is defined in surviving records. The manuscript should not convert “most likely” into “proved.”
- Retrospective testimony by military personnel can illuminate what the debris looked like and how secrecy was handled, but memory decades later is vulnerable to narrative contamination.
- Contemporary press behavior is strong evidence for what officials said, not necessarily for what every officer privately knew.

### Class 3 — disputed allegations

- Recovered alien bodies, non-human craft, indestructible memory metal, secret autopsy films and a hidden 1947 extraterrestrial exploitation program are not independently established by the public evidence assembled here.
- Later stories involving anthropomorphic test dummies should not be backdated into July 1947. The Air Force’s later report used dummy drops to address later body-related recollections, not as the physical contents of a 1947 MOGUL train.
- Patents, later exotic-material claims and modern UAP retrieval allegations are outside the evidentiary chain unless a primary provenance link is produced.

### Class 4 — speculative synthesis

- A state can create conspiracy fuel without inventing a conspiracy: classify a mundane-but-strange program, issue a cover explanation that is detectably incomplete, let records fragment, then disclose the underlying program decades after witness memory and popular culture have fused.
- The most interesting Roswell lesson may therefore be epistemic rather than extraterrestrial. Discovering one real secret beneath an official denial does not tell us how many additional secrets sit beneath it.
- Modern AtmoSOFAR work creates a historical reversal: the weirdest part of MOGUL—the idea that a balloon could exploit a high atmospheric acoustic duct—is now among the better-supported pieces of the story.

## Contradiction and incentive audit

**Air Force incentive:** close a politically costly historical controversy and protect institutional credibility. This does not invalidate documents, but it means conclusions should be decomposed into document-supported steps rather than accepted because they are official.

**UFO-investigator incentive:** preserve anomalous residue after a conventional explanation is offered. This can improve scrutiny but also encourage moving goalposts, privileging late testimony, and treating every missing record as positive evidence.

**Witness incentive:** usually mixed. Attention, identity, memory consolidation, interviewer cues, loyalty and genuine recollection can coexist. Do not moralize where source criticism is sufficient.

**Press incentive:** novelty and conflict. A spectacular first military release followed by retraction is narratively stronger than an experimental balloon program and naturally becomes the durable headline.

**Scientific incentive:** modern infrasound researchers have no need to vindicate Roswell. Their work is useful precisely because it independently tests the atmospheric physics without depending on the folklore.

## Chronology skeleton

- 1945–46: postwar U.S. intelligence confronts the problem of detecting a Soviet atomic test without access to Soviet territory.
- 1946–47: acoustic-duct concept and balloon experiments mature into MOGUL-related work.
- June 1947: New Mexico balloon operations later become central to the Air Force reconstruction.
- July 1947: debris recovered on the Brazel ranch; Roswell Army Air Field issues a “flying disc” release; higher headquarters quickly substitutes a weather-balloon explanation.
- August 1949: Soviet Union detonates its first atomic device. U.S. detection succeeds through airborne radionuclide sampling, demonstrating that strategic nuclear detection will become a multi-sensor problem rather than a single exotic instrument.
- Late 1940s onward: seismic, radionuclide and infrasound monitoring mature; MOGUL’s balloon architecture does not become the dominant nuclear-test detection system.
- Late 1970s–1990s: Roswell returns as a major UFO narrative; testimony and body stories accumulate long after the original event.
- 1994–97: Air Force reports identify MOGUL as the likely debris source and separately address later body narratives.
- 2015: free-flying stratospheric acoustic array measures an unfamiliar infrasound environment and explicitly notes MOGUL as a rare predecessor.
- 2021: balloon sensor records a buried chemical explosion at long range with a waveform closely matching the near-source ground record.
- 2023: peer-reviewed experiment reports the first direct observation of an elevated AtmoSOFAR acoustic duct using a ground-truth airborne source.
