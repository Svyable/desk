# ALIEN ENOUGH

## The Life We Might Be Missing on Earth

### Sven Hardy Benson

There is an awkward fact hiding inside the search for alien life.

We have one confirmed example.

Not one planet with life. One *kind* of life. Everything alive that we can confidently point to on Earth appears to participate in the same deep biochemical settlement: nucleic acids, proteins assembled from a familiar amino-acid repertoire, ATP-centered energy currencies, lipid membranes, ribosomes, a genetic code with common ancestry written all through it. The diversity is obscene. The architecture is conservative.

Then we build instruments to find life elsewhere.

You can see the problem before the spacecraft leaves the clean room.

This book uses four evidence classes throughout.

**1 — Verified / documented.** Results established by primary experiments, peer-reviewed measurements, official mission records, or other sources strong enough to carry the sentence being written. The common biochemical architecture of known terrestrial life belongs here. The 2010 GFAJ-1 arsenic claim belongs here only as a published historical claim; the later experiments finding no covalently bound arsenic in its DNA belong here as results. NASA's Life Detection Ladder belongs here as a documented framework, not as proof that any particular biosignature is sufficient.

**2 — Credible but incomplete.** Serious hypotheses, suggestive observations, model-dependent inferences, or results whose scope is narrower than the story commonly built around them. Claims about how much biological diversity standard cultivation, sequencing, spectroscopy, or metabolomics systematically miss often live here until the denominator is explicit.

**3 — Disputed / unconfirmed.** A naturally occurring terrestrial shadow biosphere; a surviving second genesis; organisms with a fundamentally independent biochemistry living unnoticed beside known life; claims that a particular unexplained environmental signal already represents such organisms. None is treated as established in this manuscript.

**4 — Speculative synthesis / open conjecture.** The proposition that life detection has a built-in observer problem: instruments can be exquisitely sensitive to the ontology encoded in their assays while remaining blind to viable alternatives. This is a research claim, not a revelation. It has to be cashed out in molecules, thresholds, false negatives, controls and experiments.

One additional rule matters: **possibility is not prevalence**. Showing that a detection method could miss an alternative chemistry does not show that such chemistry exists. Showing that synthetic biologists can imagine mirror organisms does not show that a mirror biosphere ever arose naturally. Showing that known life occupies stranger environments than expected does not make every strange environment inhabited.

The point is not to make the unknown larger by lowering the bar.

The point is to make the bar visible.
