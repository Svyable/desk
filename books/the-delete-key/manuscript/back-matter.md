# Back Matter

## A note on the argument

*The Delete Key* uses examples from software, regulation, records management, privacy, identity, infrastructure, and biology to make one cross-domain argument: safe removal is a capability, not an instinct.

The analogies are intentionally bounded. A brain is not an archive. A cell is not a citizen. Dead code is not a social class. Biological forgetting does not justify destroying institutional records, and low observed usage does not prove that a capability has no value. Rights, evidence, resilience, legal obligations, rare-event systems, and historical memory can all justify continued retention.

Throughout the book, “delete” is therefore shorthand for a family of different operations:

**Retire** stops operational use. **Revoke** ends authority or access. **Archive** removes something from active operation while preserving evidence or history. **Destroy** makes recovery infeasible where destruction is justified.

The difference matters.

## Selected sources

The research ledger for this edition prioritizes primary or authoritative material. Core sources include:

- U.S. Government Accountability Office, *Information Technology: Agencies Need to Plan for Modernizing Critical Decades-Old Legacy Systems* (GAO-25-107795, 2025), on legacy-system modernization and disposition planning.
- OECD, *Reviewing the Stock of Regulation* (2020), on programmed review, sunset mechanisms, and retrospective regulatory review.
- Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (GDPR), Article 5, and European Commission guidance on data minimisation and storage limitation.
- European Data Protection Board, 2026 Coordinated Enforcement Framework report on implementation of the right to erasure.
- NIST Special Publication 800-88 Rev. 2, *Guidelines for Media Sanitization* (2025), on making access to target data infeasible for a given level of effort.
- Tomás J. Ryan and Paul W. Frankland, “Forgetting as a form of adaptive engram cell plasticity,” *Nature Reviews Neuroscience* 23 (2022), 173–186.

The machine-readable source ledger and fuller research notes remain with the publication for readers who want to inspect the evidence trail.
