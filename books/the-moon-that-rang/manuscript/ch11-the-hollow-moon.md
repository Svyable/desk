# The Hollow Moon

The hollow-Moon theory has a problem more basic than evidence. It has no stable meaning.

In its mildest form, “hollow” sometimes means that the Moon contains large voids such as lava tubes. That is plausible and in places likely true. In a stronger form it means the lunar interior is substantially cavernous. In its most extravagant form, the Moon is an artificial shell, perhaps constructed or placed by non-human intelligence. These claims are routinely allowed to borrow evidence from one another even though they predict radically different worlds.

Apollo's ringing is the bridge. The argument usually runs in four steps: NASA struck the Moon; the Moon rang like a bell; bells are hollow; therefore the Moon is hollow. Each step feels intuitive enough to survive casual repetition. Only the first two are observations.

The third step is analogy masquerading as mechanics. Solid objects ring. The Earth has normal modes. A steel rail rings. A granite block rings. A wine glass rings partly because of its shape, but “ringing” is not a diagnostic category that uniquely identifies an empty interior.

The fourth step is worse because it discards everything else measured about the Moon. Mass and moment of inertia constrain how material is distributed. Lunar laser ranging constrains tidal deformation and rotational behavior. Seismic velocities constrain layers. Gravity mapping constrains density variations. Sample geochemistry constrains differentiation. Any artificial-shell model has to match those observations too.

A claim that explains one metaphor and ignores a planet's worth of measurements is not an alternative model.

Large lunar cavities, on the other hand, deserve serious attention. Lava tubes can form when the surface of a lava flow solidifies while molten material continues beneath it, leaving a void after drainage. Lunar gravity and imagery have identified pits and candidate subsurface voids that could someday shelter equipment from radiation and impacts. A Moon with caves is not a hollow Moon any more than Carlsbad Caverns make Earth a shell.

The distinction illustrates how anomalous claims survive by changing scale. Evidence for a kilometer-scale void can be rhetorically promoted into evidence for a planetary cavity. A long seismic coda can be promoted into evidence for artificial construction. Each promotion removes quantitative obligations.

A serious artificial-Moon hypothesis would need them back. What shell thickness? What density? What elastic properties? How does it reproduce the measured gravity field and moment of inertia? What are its predicted seismic modes? Why do Apollo waves map onto crust, mantle and core phases? What formation or emplacement process is being proposed, and what independent trace should it leave?

Once stated this way, the theory becomes testable enough to fail. That is not hostility. It is respect.

The interesting cultural question is why the hollow-Moon idea remains attractive when the genuine Moon is stranger. Deep tidally triggered quake nests are strange. A battered megaregolith that stores seismic energy in long codas is strange. A tiny iron-rich core detected by stacking half-century-old moonquakes is strange. A world still shrinking enough to fault may be strange. None requires an architect.

Perhaps the artificial explanation feels larger because it contains intention. Geology is indifferent. A constructed Moon implies a builder, purpose and hidden history. The same psychological move appears throughout anomaly culture: ambiguous structure becomes agency.

Agency is not impossible in the universe. It is merely expensive as an inference. An engineered object should usually become easier to identify as measurements improve because design imposes regularities. The hollow-Moon claim has moved in the opposite direction. Better measurements have produced a more detailed natural lunar interior while the artificial version remains elastic.

That asymmetry is evidence.

The correct scientific response to folklore is not ridicule. Ridicule cannot measure anything. The right response is to force the claim to choose a scale, mechanism and prediction. If what remains after that process is “the Moon has subsurface voids,” good: go map them. If what remains is “the Moon is a manufactured shell,” then give the shell dimensions and let the seismometers try to kill it.

A theory that cannot survive being made specific was never protected by mystery. It was protected by vagueness.
