# Back Matter

*The Rehearsal Economy* is a working Desk draft. Its central distinction is between prediction and preparation: rehearsals do not need to forecast the exact future to improve response, but they become dangerous when a representation is treated as more authoritative than the world it represents.

The historical, scientific, financial, organizational, and technical cases in the manuscript were chosen to test that argument across domains rather than to imply that all simulations are equivalent. The research trail records sources, counterevidence, and places where the manuscript deliberately uses cautious language because findings remain context-dependent or disputed.

No quotation in the manuscript is presented as a verbatim historical quotation unless it is sourced as such. Several familiar concepts—deliberate practice, implementation intentions, planning fallacy, psychological safety, scenario planning, stress testing, red teaming, digital twins, and predictive processing—have broad literatures with disagreements about scope and effect size. The manuscript uses them as bounded lenses rather than universal laws.

The book's strongest falsification condition is practical: if organizations that increase rehearsal quality, adversarial variety, reality contact, and recovery practice do not improve transfer to real decisions—or if additional rehearsal reliably produces more rigidity than preparedness across the domains discussed—the thesis would require substantial narrowing. A second challenge is distributional: rehearsal capacity may simply amplify the power of already resource-rich institutions. Chapter 24 treats that objection directly rather than assuming simulation is democratizing by default.

The working draft remains **Drafting** on the Svyable Desk and has not been released to Shelf.
