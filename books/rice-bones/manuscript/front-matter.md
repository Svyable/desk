# Rice Bones

## Cadmium, Women, and the River Japan Put on Trial

Sven Hardy Benson

A river can carry an argument for decades before anyone recognizes it as one.

In Toyama, the argument ran downhill from a mining complex in the mountains, entered irrigation works, spread across rice paddies, accumulated in food, crossed the gut, lodged in bodies, damaged kidneys, weakened bones, and finally arrived in court. No single link was exotic. The difficulty was that each belonged to a different profession, institution, and vocabulary. Mining engineers saw ore and drainage. Farmers saw failed crops and muddy water. Physicians saw renal dysfunction and bone disease. Public-health officials saw cases. Families saw women whose bodies hurt so badly that the sound of pain became the name of the disease.

This book is about what happened when those fragments were forced into one causal chain.

It is also about what happened after causation was established. That part is less famous outside Japan. The victims did not treat damages as the end of the problem. The pollution source still existed upstream. Contaminated soil still existed downstream. So the settlement architecture became a form of instrumentation. Residents and people they designated—scientists and lawyers among them—obtained powers to enter the mine and smelting facilities, inspect processes, request information, and collect environmental samples. The courtroom produced, in effect, a long-lived community audit system.

That is the hidden inheritance of Itai-itai disease. Environmental justice did not stop at proving yesterday’s injury. It acquired a method for checking tomorrow.

The evidence classes used throughout are simple. **Class 1** is verified or documented: official records, court findings, reproducible measurements, peer-reviewed medical and environmental findings, and directly documented agreements. **Class 2** is credible but incomplete: testimony, retrospective recollection, plausible mechanisms or local histories for which important records remain missing. **Class 3** is disputed or not established: claims that outrun available dose, chronology, pathology, or documentary support. **Class 4** is speculative synthesis: the book’s own attempt to connect systems without disguising interpretation as fact.

The point is not to make uncertainty disappear. It is to put it in the right place.
