# Back Matter

## Author’s note

*Rice Bones* is an investigative first draft, not a substitute for the Japanese archival and scientific record on which a publication-ready edition must rest. Japanese names, institutional translations, dates, patient counts, land-restoration figures, and technical descriptions should be rechecked against the original sources during expansion.

## Evidence note

The manuscript uses four evidence classes:

1. **Verified / documented:** official records, court findings, published scientific evidence, documented agreements, and measurements with clear provenance.
2. **Credible but incomplete:** testimony, retrospective accounts, incomplete dose histories, or claims supported in broad outline but lacking a complete underlying record.
3. **Disputed / not established:** claims that lack adequate exposure, pathology, chronology, provenance, or independent confirmation.
4. **Speculative synthesis:** the author’s interpretation connecting established facts into a larger institutional argument.

The central Class 4 synthesis is that Toyama’s post-1972 community inspection regime should be understood as a form of counter-monitoring: affected residents institutionalized an independent capacity to test corporate environmental claims at the source rather than relying exclusively on downstream disclosure.

## Acknowledgment of limits

This draft has not yet exhaustively reconstructed the district and appellate judgments; annual Kamioka inspection reports; mine process changes; year-by-year cadmium discharge; full paddy restoration engineering; or the complete Japanese medical literature on renal tubular dysfunction outside certified Itai-itai disease. Those are publication-gate tasks, not details to be filled by inference.
