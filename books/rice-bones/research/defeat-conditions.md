# Defeat Conditions

The manuscript’s central synthesis should be weakened or abandoned if any of the following are established:

1. The pollution-control agreement did not in practice provide the broad resident/designated-expert source access described in the Japanese scholarship.
2. Annual/recurring inspections were principally ceremonial and cannot be linked to meaningful independent sampling, information access, or engineering review.
3. Source-control improvements demonstrably occurred independently of the inspection regime, with resident participation adding no material monitoring or accountability function.
4. The claimed river → irrigation → paddy soil → rice → chronic intake pathway is materially contradicted by better exposure reconstruction showing another dominant source.
5. The established medical sequence does not support renal tubular dysfunction as central to classic Itai-itai osteomalacia.
6. Current cumulative patient/observer counts or the reported 2024–2026 living-status milestones prove incorrect.

Findings that would narrow but not defeat the thesis:

- Demonstrating that drinking/cooking water contributed more exposure than this draft currently emphasizes.
- Showing that particular inspection years had minimal value.
- Showing that company/state monitoring, rather than citizen sampling, supplied most quantitative evidence after 1972.
- Revising land-restoration area/cost totals after distinguishing project categories.

The book is strongest if the inspection thesis survives quantitative reconstruction, not if it survives only as a compelling institutional metaphor.
