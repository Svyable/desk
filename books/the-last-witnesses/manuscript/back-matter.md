# Back Matter

## A note on the case

The central physical scenario in this book is not original to the book. Lawrence M. Krauss and Robert J. Scherrer made the classic modern argument that a universe asymptotically dominated by a cosmological constant can become observationally misleading to very late observers: external galaxies disappear from view, the cosmic microwave background becomes inaccessible, and primordial nucleosynthetic evidence is increasingly compromised. Their paper, “The Return of a Static Universe and the End of Cosmology,” is the obvious starting point for the argument developed here.

The broader far-future setting owes much to Fred C. Adams and Gregory Laughlin’s work on the long-term astrophysical evolution of the universe. The modern observational case for accelerated expansion rests on late-1990s Type Ia supernova measurements by the High-Z Supernova Search Team and the Supernova Cosmology Project, followed by decades of independent cosmological probes. Planck provides a major reference point for the precision ΛCDM baseline. DESI matters to this book because its recent results keep the nature of dark energy empirically live rather than allowing the far-future projection to harden into a prophecy.

The research folder preserves a source ledger and falsification notes. The manuscript deliberately does not convert every paragraph into citation apparatus; it is trade nonfiction, not a review article. Concrete scientific claims should nevertheless be traceable to primary papers, collaboration publications, or authoritative technical sources.

## What would change the argument

The strongest version of the book’s future scenario weakens if dark energy is shown to evolve in a way that eliminates persistent accelerated expansion and its event horizon. It weakens if new durable observables are demonstrated to let remote future observers reconstruct the same cosmological history independently after the familiar evidence disappears. It fails entirely if the cited horizon and redshifting consequences are shown to follow from a mistaken physical premise.

Those outcomes would not erase the more general thesis that scientific evidence is historically situated. Unique transients, changing chemical environments, finite baselines, extinct biological systems, and ordinary archival decay already establish that access to evidence changes with time. The practical proposal—preserve uniquely time-dependent observations with provenance and enough independent structure to permit future disagreement—does not require the most dramatic cosmological ending to occur.

## Working status

This is a Desk draft. The source trail is intended to remain open to correction as DESI, supernova surveys, CMB experiments, gravitational-wave cosmology, and other observations improve the case. The book should be revised when the physics moves rather than defended against the movement.
