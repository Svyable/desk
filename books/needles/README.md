## Thesis

Project West Ford was not merely a bizarre Cold War stunt. It was an early experiment in planetary-scale infrastructure: the United States deliberately altered near-Earth space to solve a military communications problem, discovered that technically reversible interventions can leave persistent physical and political residue, and helped force a question we still have not answered cleanly—who gets to engineer a shared environment before anyone has agreed that it is shared?

## Evidence classes

1. **Verified / documented:** official program history, launches, dipole design, communications experiments, astronomy objections, surviving hardware and orbital-debris record.
2. **Credible but incomplete:** contemporary accounts of military requirements, internal expectations, diplomatic concern and institutional motives where primary files remain to be recovered.
3. **Disputed / unconfirmed:** claims that West Ford was intended as a permanent belt, caused specific harms not established by measurement, or concealed capabilities beyond the documented communications experiment.
4. **Speculative synthesis:** West Ford as an early governance prototype for deliberate planetary engineering, orbital commons and today's arguments over megaconstellations, debris and geoengineering.

## Defeat condition

The book's central interpretation weakens if the primary record shows that West Ford's international/scientific governance effects were negligible, that the intervention was understood contemporaneously as ordinary disposable hardware rather than environmental modification, or that later space-governance changes cannot be traced to the dispute except rhetorically.
