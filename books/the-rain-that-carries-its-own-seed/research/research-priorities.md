# Research Priorities and Defeat Conditions

## Highest-value next work

1. Quantify real atmospheric concentrations of warm-active biological INPs across cloud regimes, with source-resolved molecular identification.
2. Run matched ice-plus/ice-minus *P. syringae* cloud-chamber experiments across field-realistic concentrations and mineral-dust competition.
3. Measure genotype-specific phase partitioning into cloud water, initial ice, residual aerosol and fallout.
4. Test whether ice-active strains gain a net viable-deposition or colonization advantage across complete aerosol–cloud–precipitation cycles.
5. Measure persistence of intact cells, proteins, fragments and secreted particles separately under UV, desiccation, oxidation and freeze-thaw exposure.
6. Build landscape-scale flux budgets linking canopy/litter/water sources to boundary-layer bio-INPs, cloud residuals and precipitation.
7. Reproduce the 2026 microplastic-biofilm result using atmospherically realistic particle sizes, thin environmental biofilms, weathering histories and aerosolization.
8. Determine field-relevant PFAS and other pollutant effects on bacterial ice-nucleation spectra.
9. Separate warm-active biological INPs from unknown submicron nuclei in rain using proteomics, metagenomics and functional fractionation.
10. Develop cloud-model parameterizations constrained by measured biological source/activation distributions rather than tuned constants.

## Strong defeat conditions

The strong bioprecipitation feedback should be substantially weakened if:

- measured biological INPs are almost always too rare to affect initial ice once mineral/fungal/other competitors are included;
- matched active and inactive bacteria show no meaningful difference in cloud phase partitioning or deposition at realistic concentrations;
- biological effects demonstrated in chambers fail to reproduce in field cloud residuals;
- apparent precipitation enrichment is explained by below-cloud scavenging or source-population differences;
- no viable fitness advantage accrues to ice-active lineages across atmospheric transport cycles;
- regional models require source strengths far above observations to generate meaningful precipitation effects.

These negative results would not falsify bacterial ice nucleation itself.
