# Chronology

- 1960s — cloud physicists investigating hail and atmospheric ice nuclei encounter exceptionally active material associated with biological-rich sources.
- 1970s — bacterial sources of warm-temperature ice nucleation are identified; plant-pathology and atmospheric lines converge.
- 1982 — Lindow, Arny & Upper publish direct evidence linking ice-nucleation-active bacteria to frost injury in *Plant Physiology*.
- 1983–1985 — Advanced Genetic Sciences seeks U.S. approval for environmental tests of engineered ice-minus bacteria.
- 14 Nov 1985 — EPA approves experimental use permits for small-scale tests of two genetically altered ice-minus strains.
- 1987 — authorized field releases of ice-minus bacteria occur in California amid protests, vandalism and intense regulatory attention; Lindow publishes competitive-exclusion frost-protection work.
- 1990 — field/laboratory work examines persistence of ice-active *P. syringae* used in synthetic snow.
- 2000 — Hirano & Upper review *P. syringae* as pathogen, epiphyte and ice nucleus.
- 2008 — Morris et al. publish evidence tying *P. syringae* populations to rain, snow, freshwater, wild plants and crops, proposing a water-cycle life history.
- 2013–2014 — bioprecipitation synthesis argues for plausible landscape–microbe–precipitation feedbacks while emphasizing unresolved magnitude.
- 2017 — Failor et al. screen >33,000 precipitation isolates and reveal diverse bacterial ice nucleators including *Lysinibacillus*.
- 2022 — noncanonical Gram-positive ice nucleation is linked to PKS/NRPS biosynthetic machinery and secreted particles.
- 2023 — abundant biomolecules including RuBisCO are shown to have ice-nucleating capacity under laboratory conditions, broadening the biological source class.
- 2024 — Vali & Schnell publish detailed retrospective histories of the bacterial ice-nucleation discovery; high-speed cryomicroscopy visualizes *P. syringae* protein-driven freezing at interfaces; microplastic weathering is shown to alter ice nucleation.
- 2025 — experiments report emission of ice-nucleating particles by raindrop impact.
- 2026 — Carpenter et al. report that *P. syringae* biofilm colonization substantially warms median freezing temperatures of tested microplastics; separate work reports PFAS interference with bacterial ice-nucleation machinery.
