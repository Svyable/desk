# Document Appendix — The Bioprecipitation Test

## A. Historical control documents

1. EPA 1985 experimental-use approval for ice-minus bacteria. Establishes what was authorized, what phenotype was modified and what risk review was claimed.
2. 1987 Lindow competitive-exclusion paper. Establishes that manipulating ice-nucleating bacterial populations can alter frost injury under plant conditions.
3. 2008 Morris et al. water-cycle paper. Establishes the multi-habitat distribution and snow enrichment that motivated stronger atmospheric-life-cycle interpretations.
4. 2017 Failor et al. precipitation survey. Establishes diversity and concentration ranges across precipitation events.
5. 2024 Vali/Schnell retrospective. Useful for reconstructing discovery sequence and identifying primary mid-century sources.

## B. Prospective preregistration skeleton

Primary hypothesis: at field-realistic concentrations in warm mixed-phase conditions, an ice-nucleation-active *P. syringae* strain will be enriched in initial ice and fallout relative to a matched ice-deficient strain.

Primary outcomes: ice-onset temperature; ice-crystal number; genotype fraction in ice, liquid, residual aerosol and fallout; viable recovery; post-deposition colonization success.

Controls: inactive near-isogenic strain; killed active cells; purified/isolated ice-nucleating material; mineral dust competition; no-biological control.

Predefined defeat criterion: no reproducible active/inactive difference in phase partitioning or fallout across the concentration range measured in relevant field environments.

Secondary atmospheric hypothesis: measured source landscapes produce sufficient warm-active biological INP flux to explain observed enrichment in cloud ice without source terms exceeding independent aerosol observations.

Predefined defeat criterion: model requires biological source or activation rates outside observed bounds to produce field-scale effects.

## C. Language rules

Use “can nucleate ice” for laboratory mechanism.
Use “is present in precipitation” for isolation/detection.
Use “consistent with atmospheric selection” for enrichment unless source and scavenging alternatives are excluded.
Use “may contribute to precipitation microphysics” for cloud-scale synthesis.
Reserve “caused precipitation” for interventions or observations that exclude plausible counterfactuals.
Never use “controls weather” on present evidence.
