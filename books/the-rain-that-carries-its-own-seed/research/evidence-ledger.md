# Evidence Ledger

## Class 1 — verified / documented

- Ice-nucleation-active *Pseudomonas syringae* can catalyze freezing at warm subzero temperatures and contribute to frost injury on plants. Lindow, Arny & Upper 1982, DOI 10.1104/pp.70.4.1084.
- *P. syringae* occurs in rain, snow, alpine streams/lakes, wild plants and agricultural habitats; ice-active strains occur among these populations. Morris et al. 2008, DOI 10.1038/ismej.2007.113.
- Precipitation contains genetically diverse ice-nucleation-active bacteria, including previously unrecognized mechanisms. Failor et al. 2017, DOI 10.1038/ismej.2017.124.
- *Lysinibacillus parviboronicapiens* ice nucleation depends on noncanonical biosynthetic machinery associated with secreted particles. Failor/Monteil/Vinatzer-lineage work, ISME Journal 2022, article s41396-021-01140-4.
- Bacterial ice-nucleation protein activity has been visualized at interfaces using high-speed cryomicroscopy. Bieber et al. 2024, DOI 10.1126/sciadv.adn6606.
- EPA approved small-scale field tests of genetically altered ice-minus bacteria in 1985; authorized outdoor tests followed in 1987. EPA archived press release, 14 Nov 1985.
- Naturally occurring bacterial ice-nucleating preparations have been used in snowmaking.
- Weathering can alter microplastic ice-nucleation activity. Brahana et al. 2024, DOI 10.1038/s41467-024-53987-8.
- Colonization of microplastics by *P. syringae* can increase measured ice-nucleation temperatures in laboratory assays. Carpenter et al. 2026, DOI 10.1021/acs.est.5c18769.
- Raindrop impact can emit ice-nucleating particles from surfaces. JGR Atmospheres 2025, DOI 10.1029/2024JD042584.

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete

- Ice-active biological particles may materially influence initial ice formation in a subset of warm mixed-phase clouds.
- Enrichment of highly ice-active *P. syringae* in snow is consistent with atmospheric selection or phase partitioning, but alternative source and survival explanations remain.
- Biological INPs may be regionally more important over vegetated or low-dust environments than global means imply.
- Living cells, dead cells, fragments and secreted macromolecules probably contribute differently to atmospheric ice activity; field apportionment remains incomplete.
- Surface-to-cloud-to-precipitation transport can connect microbial populations over hydrological networks.

## Class 3 — disputed / overextended

- Specific rain or snow events being attributed primarily to bacterial seeding without controlled counterfactual evidence.
- Claims that forests reliably 'call' rain through bacterial aerosols as a dominant mechanism.
- Claims that biological cloud seeding can reliably control precipitation in operational settings without robust randomized evidence.
- Treating all warm-temperature atmospheric IN activity as *Pseudomonas* or as biological.
- Treating the presence of microbes in precipitation as proof that they initiated that precipitation.
- Treating laboratory microplastic/biofilm freezing shifts as evidence that atmospheric microplastics currently alter regional weather.

## Class 4 — speculative synthesis / open conjecture

- Ice nucleation may provide some microbial lineages with an evolved atmospheric deposition advantage by accelerating incorporation into ice and precipitation.
- Biologically active landscapes may participate in a precipitation feedback in which emission of warm-active biological INPs modifies cloud phase and returns water/organisms to source or downstream ecosystems.
- Anthropogenic surfaces and chemicals may be creating novel composite biological INPs with cloud properties different from preindustrial aerosol.
- Land-use and climate change may alter not only biological INP abundance but the ecological feedback gain of bioprecipitation.

## Non-negotiable claim boundary

The book may say that biology can influence ice nucleation and may participate in precipitation microphysics. It may not say that bacteria generally make rain, regulate climate, or intentionally engineer weather without new causal field evidence.
