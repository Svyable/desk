# Field Evidence Matrix

This matrix separates evidence that biological ice-nucleating particles exist in the atmosphere from evidence that they materially change cloud or precipitation outcomes.

| Evidence stream | What is directly established | What remains open |
|---|---|---|
| Louisiana precipitation, 2019 AEM | Heat-sensitive biological INPs were deposited year-round across 65 events; warm-active classes and bacterial-sensitive fractions varied with storm/source conditions. | Deposition does not by itself establish that the particles initiated the precipitation that collected them. |
| Virginia precipitation, 2017 ISME | Hundreds of confirmed ice-active cultured bacterial isolates from 23 precipitation events; taxonomic and mechanistic diversity. | Culturing misses nonculturable and cell-free activity; causal cloud role not observed directly. |
| British Isles aircraft sampling, 2021 | Airborne INPs aloft could not be explained by mineral dust alone; biological material was inferred as an important additional source. | Functional attribution to particular organisms/proteins and cloud-effect magnitude remain incomplete. |
| Arctic mountain observations, 2023 Nature Communications | Warm-season primary biological aerosol concentrations correlated with terrestrial biosphere indicators, supporting regional biological sources of high-temperature INPs. | Translation from INP abundance to precipitation response requires cloud-regime analysis. |
| Coastal precipitation/aerosol, 2021 ACP | Cultivable halotolerant ice-nucleating bacteria and fungi recovered from coastal air/precipitation; some froze at very warm temperatures. | Marine source attribution is stronger for some isolates than others; abundance relative to total INP pool remains uncertain. |
| Himalayan transect, 2022 JGR | Bioaerosol diversity and temperature-resolved INP concentrations measured across elevation/environment gradients. | Presence of putative ice-nucleating genera did not simply correlate with INP concentration, warning against taxonomic inference alone. |
| Rain-impact emission, 2025 JGR | Raindrop impact can generate ice-nucleating particles from surfaces. | Fraction biological, vertical transport efficiency, and subsequent cloud impact vary by surface and meteorology. |
| Convective-storm bioaerosol study, 2026 | Precipitation and cold pools measurably alter bioaerosol populations, with storm-dependent changes in fungi and other fluorescent particles. | Linking these altered populations to subsequent cloud glaciation needs sequential storm or chamber experiments. |

## Claim boundary

These studies make it increasingly difficult to treat biological INPs as a laboratory curiosity. They do not yet demonstrate a general precipitation feedback. The missing bridge is intervention or phase-resolved observation showing that the measured biological fraction changes cloud evolution relative to an otherwise matched atmosphere.
