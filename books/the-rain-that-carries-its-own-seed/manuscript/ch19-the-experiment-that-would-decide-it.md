# The Experiment That Would Decide It

There is no single experiment that can prove a planetary feedback.

There can be an experiment that kills the easiest escape routes.

The decisive program would begin with matched organisms. Use an ice-nucleation-active *Pseudomonas syringae* strain and a near-isogenic ice-deficient derivative. Confirm that they have comparable aerosol size, viability and growth under the chosen conditions except for the nucleation phenotype. Label them genetically in a way that does not alter fitness.

Then put them through weather.

Not simulated weather as a metaphor. A controlled cloud facility capable of producing supercooled droplets, turbulent mixing and measurable precipitation or ice fallout.

Run active and inactive strains separately and together. Randomize chamber order. Blind downstream sample analysis. Use concentrations spanning field observations rather than only doses guaranteed to produce an effect. Add mineral dust at realistic backgrounds. Repeat temperature profiles from warm mixed-phase conditions down into regimes where mineral particles activate abundantly.

Measure five outcomes.

First, ice initiation. Does the active strain shift first-freezing temperature and ice number as expected?

Second, partitioning. Are active cells enriched in ice crystals relative to liquid droplets and residual aerosol?

Third, fallout. Do active cells leave the chamber faster in precipitating or settling ice?

Fourth, viability. Are cells recovered from fallout capable of colonizing a relevant plant or water habitat?

Fifth, fitness. Across repeated transport cycles, does the active genotype gain a net dispersal advantage relative to the inactive genotype?

The fifth outcome is the one most atmospheric experiments omit.

Without it, we can prove microphysics but not adaptation.

A parallel experiment should use killed cells and purified ice-nucleating material. If dead preparations reproduce cloud effects but not lineage advantage, the atmospheric and evolutionary hypotheses separate cleanly.

A third arm should test alternative biological nuclei such as fungal macromolecules and *Lysinibacillus* secretions. This determines whether any observed cloud effect is a special feature of *Pseudomonas* or a broader consequence of warm-active biological particles.

The chamber work establishes mechanism and dose.

The field campaign then asks whether the atmosphere supplies the dose.

Sample a landscape with strong biological aerosol sources over a full season. Characterize surface populations, emitted aerosols, vertical profiles, cloud water, ice residuals and precipitation. Use aircraft when possible to collect particles inside mixed-phase clouds. Record temperature-resolved INP spectra rather than total particle counts. Sequence organisms and assay ice-nucleation genes or proteins. Measure mineral and soot competitors.

Follow air masses with trajectories but treat trajectories as probability fields, not exact paths.

The key analysis is enrichment across phases. If active biological nuclei are disproportionately represented in initial cloud ice compared with incoming aerosol and liquid droplets, that supports selective incorporation. If the enrichment predicts earlier glaciation or precipitation after controlling for meteorology, the cloud-level case strengthens.

A regional model comes last.

Feed it observed source fluxes and activation spectra rather than convenient literature constants. Run ensembles with biological activity intact, removed and shifted in temperature. Ask for predictions not used in calibration: cloud phase, radar signatures, precipitation timing and spatial distribution.

If the model only reproduces rainfall after tuning unknown biological emissions, it has not tested the hypothesis.

The program should be preregistered where practical because flexible analysis is a serious risk. Cloud data are high-dimensional. One can discover a suggestive subset after the fact almost indefinitely.

Specify effect sizes that would count as meaningful.

Specify null conditions.

For the evolutionary claim, predefine what fitness advantage across transport cycles would be required to justify adaptive language. For the cloud claim, define the shift in glaciation or precipitation that must be reproduced across independent chamber runs. For the field claim, define enrichment metrics and meteorological covariates before looking at outcomes.

There is also a clean defeat condition.

If active and inactive strains show identical phase partitioning and atmospheric residence under realistic concentrations, while biological nuclei contribute negligibly once mineral competition is included, then the strong bioprecipitation feedback should be abandoned.

The proteins would remain interesting.

The crop frost mechanism would remain real.

Snowmaking would still work.

Bacteria would still occur in rain and snow.

A beautiful planetary synthesis could fail without taking the underlying science with it.

That is what makes the experiment worth doing.

A hypothesis that cannot survive losing its grandest claim is not a research program. It is a brand.
