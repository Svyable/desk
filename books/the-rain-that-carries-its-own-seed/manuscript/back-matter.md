# Back Matter

The strongest claim in this book is deliberately smaller than its title.

Biological ice nucleation is real. *Pseudomonas syringae* and other organisms or biological materials can catalyze freezing at temperatures unusually warm for atmospheric ice nucleation. The open question is not whether the mechanism exists. It is how often it matters to cloud evolution and whether any organisms gain enough from precipitation-mediated transport for the trait to participate in an ecological feedback.

That distinction should survive every future revision.

Three classes of result would materially change the book. First, cloud-chamber or field work showing that realistic biological INP concentrations produce reproducible changes in mixed-phase glaciation and precipitation under mineral competition. Second, genotype-resolved experiments showing an atmospheric dispersal or deposition advantage for active versus matched inactive strains. Third, regional observational studies linking source landscapes to cloud ice and precipitation through molecularly identified biological nuclei rather than generic warm-freezing activity.

A strong negative result would matter just as much. If biological activity becomes negligible at measured atmospheric concentrations once competing INPs are included, then the weather-scale feedback should be cut back sharply. The molecular biology, frost pathology, precipitation microbiology and industrial snowmaking would remain.

The research files accompanying this manuscript maintain a four-class evidence ledger, chronology, contradiction audit and source log so that future editing does not turn an attractive feedback into retrospective certainty.
