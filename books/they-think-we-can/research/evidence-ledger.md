# Evidence Ledger — They Think We Can

## Governing rule

**Government interest is evidence of government interest. It is not, without an additional chain, evidence that the investigated capability worked.**

The central empirical task is to reconstruct whether U.S. and Soviet anomalous-research programs were genuinely independent evidence streams or partially coupled through publications, visitors, intelligence collection, rumors, threat estimates, and observation of each other's programs.

---

## Class 1 — verified / declassified / documented

### U.S. intelligence concern about Soviet anomalous research

1. **DIA, John D. LaMothe, _Controlled Offensive Behavior — USSR_, ST-CS-01-169-72, July 1972.**
   - Declassified copy in CIA STARGATE collection.
   - Documents a U.S. defense-intelligence effort to survey Soviet research relevant to influencing human behavior.
   - Explicitly includes psychokinesis, ESP, astral projection, clairvoyance, and precognition among subjects surveyed.
   - Repeats reporting of 20+ Soviet centers and approximately $20 million annual paranormal-research spending in 1967.
   - **Does not establish that the center count or budget estimate was correct, or that the reported research produced an operational capability.**
   - Source: https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/CIA-RDP96-00787R000100120001-9.pdf

2. **DIA / Army reference chain preserved in declassified psychoenergetics records.**
   - Includes _Soviet and Czechoslovakian Parapsychology Research_ (DIA, Sept. 1975), _Soviet and East European Parapsychology Research_ (CIA, Apr. 1977), and _Paraphysics R&D — Warsaw Pact_ (DIA, Mar. 1978).
   - Establishes sustained U.S. collection/assessment interest.
   - Source: https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/CIA-RDP96-00788R001300040001-4.pdf

3. **902d Military Intelligence Group memo, 15 May 1978.**
   - Requests briefing on Soviet parapsychology research and intelligence applications the Soviets might exploit, explicitly in support of hostile-intelligence-collection threat assessment.
   - This is strong evidence for the book's distinction between *threat consideration* and *validated capability*.
   - Source: https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/CIA-RDP96-00788R002000160008-6.pdf

4. **DIA, _Psychoenergetics Research_, 7 Aug. 1984.**
   - Describes Soviet review, military support, and expected future intelligence/warfare applications in predictive language.
   - Must be analyzed as an intelligence forecast, not retroactively converted into proof of achieved applications.
   - Source: https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/CIA-RDP96-00788R001800210001-0.pdf

5. **CIA/SRI parapsychology work existed.**
   - CIA records document 1973 SRI parapsychology studies and subsequent U.S. program history.
   - Source: https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp96-00787r000100070001-5
   - CIA retrospective: https://www.cia.gov/stories/story/ask-molly-did-cia-really-study-psychic-powers/

### Cross-border idea circulation

6. **Declassified U.S.-held translation, _Soviet Research on ESP and Thought Transmission_.**
   - Soviet scientific discussion explicitly references the Western _Nautilus_ submarine telepathy story while warning that the report might not be true and calling for further experiments/theory.
   - This is a concrete example of a Western paranormal claim entering Soviet discussion.
   - Source: https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/NSA-RDP96X00790R000100020026-8.pdf

7. **American fact-finding on Soviet “psychoenergetic devices.”**
   - A technical memorandum by William A. Tiller describes a fact-finding trip to Russia covering Kirlian photography, acupuncture-related instrumentation, telekinesis experiments, and field detectors.
   - Establishes direct scientific/technical observation and exchange as one possible coupling channel; it does not validate the claimed effects.
   - Source: https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp96-00787r000100200002-9

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete

1. **Magnitude of Soviet state funding.** The recurring $20 million figure and center counts require provenance reconstruction. Do not inflation-adjust or narratively amplify until the original estimate basis is found.

2. **Institutional authority of Soviet research.** “Soviet scientists,” “the USSR,” military support, Academy interest, Party sanction, and named laboratories are not interchangeable. Map institution, sponsor, investigator, date, and authority separately.

3. **Causal role of foreign-threat reporting in U.S. program starts/continuation.** Documents establish concern, but each funding decision needs its own record. Avoid claiming “the Soviet program caused STARGATE” without budget/tasking evidence.

4. **Soviet observation of U.S. programs.** Published Western work, scientific visits, and press coverage make observation plausible and sometimes documented, but the degree to which it drove Soviet funding remains open.

5. **Operational denominators.** Both sides' archives disproportionately preserve striking claims and program summaries. Full task counts, failed experiments, abandoned devices, and negative evaluations are required.

## Class 3 — disputed / unconfirmed

- Reliable operational telepathy to submarines.
- Soviet or U.S. operational psychokinetic weapons.
- Long-range mental incapacitation or behavior-control systems based on paranormal mechanisms.
- Claims that either superpower achieved robust clairvoyant penetration of protected facilities.
- Large secret Soviet breakthroughs inferred solely from U.S. threat assessments.
- Any claim that Kirlian photography imaged a paranormal biological field.
- The proposition that classified status implies hidden validating evidence.

These may be discussed as claims with provenance. They may not be narrated as capabilities.

## Class 4 — speculative synthesis / open conjecture

### Mirror-race model

A low-probability/high-consequence claim can generate a self-reinforcing national-security loop:

1. Country A produces a claim, experiment, rumor, or research program.
2. Country B collects evidence that A is interested.
3. B treats A's interest as a reason to investigate or defend.
4. A observes B's investigation.
5. A's estimate of B's seriousness rises.
6. Parallel programs are later mistaken for independent convergence on the phenomenon.

This model is plausible. It is **not yet demonstrated as the dominant causal history** of Cold War psychoenergetics.

### Classification-weight hypothesis

Secret circulation can increase the perceived authority of weak claims because readers reasonably infer that classified reports may rest on inaccessible sources. Test by comparing claims across public source → classified assessment → later briefing and tracking whether confidence increases without new evidence.

### Threat-threshold hypothesis

Research can be rational at probabilities far below the threshold for scientific belief when the hypothesized adversary capability has extreme consequences and investigation is cheap. This predicts continued low-cost exploration even under weak evidence without requiring institutional credulity.

## Strongest countermodels

### Countermodel A — genuine independent convergence

Soviet and U.S. researchers independently encountered repeatable anomalous effects. Intelligence attention followed the data, and cross-national interest is partly evidence of a real phenomenon.

**What would strengthen it:** primary Soviet protocols predating relevant Western influence; complete datasets; independent replications; prospective effects surviving modern controls; technical applications whose performance can be demonstrated.

### Countermodel B — ordinary scientific fashion

No intelligence feedback loop is required. Parapsychology was internationally fashionable enough that researchers in multiple countries pursued it for domestic scientific/cultural reasons.

**What would strengthen it:** funding/tasking records with little foreign-threat language; similar research trajectories in nonaligned countries without superpower threat dynamics.

### Countermodel C — bureaucratic opportunism

Investigators used Soviet-threat claims instrumentally to win funding for research they already wanted to do. The loop existed rhetorically but did not substantially alter national threat beliefs.

**What would strengthen it:** private correspondence distinguishing public justification from actual motivation; proposal language that escalates foreign threat relative to internal assessments.

### Countermodel D — real Soviet program, inflated American estimate

The USSR genuinely funded anomalous research, but U.S. intelligence exaggerated its scale, coherence, or progress. No symmetric feedback is needed.

## Quantitative extraction schema

For every foreign-capability claim, record:

| Field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Claim | Exact technical proposition |
| Earliest located source | Date, author, institution |
| Source type | experiment / press / HUMINT / translation / estimate / testimony |
| Original data visible? | yes/no/partial |
| Country of origin | where claim first appears |
| Known cross-border exposure | publications, visits, press, intelligence collection |
| Confidence language | exact contemporaneous wording |
| Budget/capability number | amount/unit and provenance |
| Repetition count | how many later docs repeat it |
| Independent source count | sources not downstream of same origin |
| Domestic action | contract, briefing, defense, experiment |
| Foreign observation of action | documented/inferred/unknown |
| Operational result | success/failure/unknown |
| Post-program audit | what became visible later |

The crucial quantity is not number of documents. It is number of **independent evidentiary roots**.

## Publication holds

Do not publish the following sentences without stronger evidence:

- “The Soviets spent $20 million a year on psychic warfare.”
- “The Soviet military had twenty psychic research centers.”
- “The U.S. started remote viewing because the Soviets were ahead.”
- “Both superpowers independently discovered that psi worked.”
- “Soviet psychotronic weapons existed.”
- “The _Nautilus_ telepathy experiment happened as popularly described.”
- “Party sanction” as shorthand for Politburo-level operational endorsement without a primary Soviet record.

---

## Defeat conditions

The book's central mirror-race thesis should be materially weakened or abandoned if primary-source reconstruction shows that:

1. major Soviet anomalous programs predated relevant Western claims and were motivated by independent experimental results;
2. those results replicated prospectively across independent Soviet institutions under improving controls;
3. U.S. program authorizations relied mainly on strong domestic experimental performance rather than foreign-threat uncertainty;
4. cross-border citation/provenance analysis shows little coupling between the supposedly parallel evidence streams; and
5. post-Soviet archives reveal operational capabilities commensurate with the stronger Cold War U.S. forecasts.

If those conditions are met, the more surprising book is not about mirrors. It is about what everyone missed.

## Primary research queue

1. Recover the original source behind the 1967 $20 million / 20+ centers claim in _Controlled Offensive Behavior_.
2. Obtain full _Soviet and Czechoslovakian Parapsychology Research_ (1975) and map every substantive capability claim to its cited source.
3. Obtain _Soviet and East European Parapsychology Research_ (CIA, 1977) and compare its source roots to the 1975 DIA report.
4. Reconstruct _Paraphysics R&D — Warsaw Pact_ (1978), especially budget/capability changes and uncertainty language.
5. Build a date graph of SRI publications, Soviet citations/visits, U.S. foreign-threat reports, and U.S. program authorizations.
6. Search Russian-language post-Soviet memoirs and archives for sponsor, budget, institutional structure, and assessments of efficacy; treat memoirs as testimony until corroborated.
7. Separate electromagnetic bioeffects, hypnosis, pharmacology, and other physically ordinary behavior-influence research from paranormal mechanisms. The 1972 umbrella category is analytically dangerous.
8. Compare the psychoenergetics case with documented threat-estimate failures such as bomber/missile-gap and Team B debates, while avoiding false equivalence.
9. Find a positive control: a foreign technology warning where low-confidence intelligence correctly anticipated a real capability. The framework must explain successful warning as well as inflation.
10. Carry the method forward to contemporary UAP, quantum, neurotechnology, and Chinese/Russian “disruptive technology” claims only after the Cold War case is established.
