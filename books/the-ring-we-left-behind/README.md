## Thesis

Project West Ford was a technically serious Cold War attempt to manufacture a dependable communications medium by releasing hundreds of millions of resonant copper dipoles into orbit. Its deeper importance is not that the United States once tried something strange in space. It is that engineers, astronomers, diplomats and military planners were forced—before the modern space age had acquired most of its rules—to confront a question we still have not settled: when an intervention works locally but alters a shared environment globally, what counts as success?

West Ford is unusually useful because the evidence is concrete. Hardware survives in the Smithsonian. Lincoln Laboratory acknowledges the program. Contemporary technical papers describe the belt and its orbital behavior. Astronomers publicly objected. The first deployment failed to disperse properly; the second produced a functioning passive scattering medium. Most dipoles decayed as intended, while clumped remnants persisted far longer. Active communications satellites rapidly made the concept obsolete.

The book does not treat West Ford as secret geoengineering, a weapons belt, proof of deliberate space pollution, or a hidden precursor to contemporary atmospheric manipulation. It treats those later interpretations as claims requiring their own evidence.

## Evidence classes

1. **Verified/declassified/documented:** Air Force sponsorship; MIT Lincoln Laboratory development; 1961 and 1963 deployments; copper-dipole design; communications experiments; astronomical controversy; surviving museum hardware; orbital persistence of failed-to-disperse clumps.
2. **Credible but incomplete:** exact internal military risk calculations, the full policy chain behind authorization, Soviet technical assessment, and the relative weight of scientific protest versus superior satellite technology in ending the concept.
3. **Disputed/unconfirmed:** claims that West Ford was principally a weapon, climate intervention, or covert environmental experiment rather than the documented passive-communications experiment.
4. **Speculative synthesis/open conjecture:** West Ford as an early prototype of the governance problem now posed by megaconstellations, orbital debris, planetary-scale experimentation and interventions whose externalities cross borders without asking permission.

## Defeat condition

The central argument weakens substantially if the historical record shows either that West Ford never functioned as a communications medium at all, or that its designers and authorizers had already solved the externality/governance problem in a durable international framework before deployment. Neither proposition is assumed.
