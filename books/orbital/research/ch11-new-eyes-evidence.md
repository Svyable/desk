# Chapter 11 evidence notes — The New Eyes

This packet supports the first full draft of live Chapter 11. The chapter argues that commercial Earth observation created an **evidence market**: governments retain unique classified collection and fusion capabilities, but some forms of strategic visibility can now be purchased, shared, archived and analyzed outside the traditional intelligence chain.

## Evidence classes used here

- **Official historical/technical fact:** U.S. government or regulator source.
- **Company capability statement:** useful for what a provider says its system can do; retain qualifiers such as “under optimal conditions” and do not convert marketing into independent validation.
- **SEC filing:** preferred for current public-company capabilities/business model because statements are made in regulated disclosures.
- **Reported visual evidence:** journalism using third-party commercial imagery; distinguish provider interpretation from independent verification.
- **Interpretation:** the manuscript's systems argument, not an official finding.

## 1. Opening — Bucha and the orbital archive as a timestamp

**Reuters video/reporting, April 5, 2022 — satellite images appear to show bodies on Bucha street**  
Reuters syndication: https://www.yahoo.com/news/satellite-images-appear-show-bodies-132036447.html

Use for:
- Maxar imagery collected during the period Russian forces controlled Bucha;
- comparison between dark objects in the satellite imagery and bodies later documented at street level in corresponding positions;
- Maxar's statement that high-resolution imagery corroborated ground photographs/video showing bodies left in the streets;
- independent expert explanation that matching objects relative to buildings/cars was comparatively straightforward.

Evidence class: **reported visual evidence with provider data**.

Required nuance:
- Russian officials denied responsibility and claimed the scene was staged after withdrawal;
- the imagery/time sequence contradicted that specific timing claim;
- do not imply a satellite image by itself establishes every element of legal responsibility for every death.

**Associated public analysis reported by the New York Times and other outlets, April 4–5, 2022.**

The manuscript uses the episode for a narrow institutional point: a private archive created time-stamped evidence that could test a public claim while the conflict was ongoing.

## 16. Core interpretive claims

### “Orbit became a timestamp”
Meaning: archives allow investigators to test later claims against collections made earlier, sometimes before the question existed.

Would be weakened if archives were too sparse or inaccessible for meaningful retrospective comparison. The Bucha case and large commercial archives demonstrate at least some high-value counterexamples.

### “Evidence market”
Meaning: purchasable remote-sensing observations can be acquired/shared outside one state's classified intelligence chain.

It does **not** mean:
- truth is automatically commoditized;
- every customer can access every image;
- classified intelligence is obsolete;
- analysis becomes trivial.

### “The intelligence monopoly did not disappear. A piece of it became a market.”
This is the chapter's central boundary.

Government advantages retained include:
- classified sensors and specifications;
- protected tasking priorities;
- SIGINT and other non-commercial collection;
- clandestine/human sources;
- military sensors and operational reporting;
- fusion, authorities and classified analytic context.

Commercial advantages include:
- shareability;
- additional collection capacity;
- market-funded innovation;
- diverse providers;
- archival depth;
- easier partner/public distribution.

### “One system scans the world. Another watches the door.”
Interpretive shorthand contrasting Planet-style broad daily monitoring with BlackSky-style high-revisit monitoring of selected strategic targets. It is not an exhaustive provider taxonomy.

### “A pixel is not an intelligence assessment.”
Critical guardrail. Imagery has ambiguity, collection gaps, weather/geometry limits and interpretation error. The chapter should never treat public access as equivalent to professional analytical competence.

## 17. Drafting guardrails

- Do not state that commercial imagery proved Russian *intent* before Feb. 24, 2022. It documented physical preparation and movement.
- For Bucha, keep the claim narrow: commercial archival imagery contradicted the specific assertion that visible bodies had appeared only after Russian withdrawal; do not claim imagery alone legally adjudicated every death.
- Distinguish commercial imagery from classified national technical means; avoid speculative claims about current classified resolution/capabilities.
- Ground sample distance is not guaranteed identification performance.
- “Daily Earth scan” does not mean cloud-free usable imagery everywhere every day.
- Retain “under optimal conditions” around BlackSky peak revisit claims.
- Treat provider capability pages as provider statements; use SEC filings or government procurement where available.
- Do not imply Ukraine owned/operated the ICEYE spacecraft after the 2022 agreement; ICEYE said it continued to operate the satellite while providing full capabilities/access.
- Avoid publishing sensitive current tasking patterns or tactics. The chapter is about institutional structure and public-source historical examples.
- Commercial does not mean public: imagery/tasking can be proprietary, expensive, exclusive or contractually restricted.
- NOAA/Office of Space Commerce licensing remains a meaningful terrestrial control layer.
- Carry the chapter into “Launch on Demand” through replenishment and capacity, not through claims that satellites are disposable.
