## 4. Fleet reliability, manufacturing correction, and software correction

**FCC 21-48, April 27, 2021**  
https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-21-48A1.pdf

Use for:
- Starlink’s stated nominal satellite mission life of 5–7 years at the time.
- record of early disposal / maneuverability failures.
- SpaceX representation that it identified root causes, improved its manufacturing process, and updated software on satellites already in orbit.
- SpaceX representation that 720 of the last 723 satellites launched were maneuverable above injection altitude as of mid-February 2021.

Important nuance: these are statements in the FCC record, including company representations; do not silently upgrade them into independent FCC engineering findings.

## 5. Traditional satellite-life contrast

**Maxar Technologies 2020 Form 10-K**  
https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1121142/000155837021001635/maxr-20201231x10k.htm

Use for:

This supports the chapter’s contrast between long-lived capital assets and faster constellation refresh without claiming that every traditional satellite was designed for exactly fifteen years.

## 6. 2026 Starlink manufacturing scale and vertical integration

**SpaceX public securities material filed with the SEC, June 2026**  
https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1181412/000162828026041013/japanfwp_06042026.htm

Use for:
- Redmond manufacturing average of approximately 70 satellites per week from December 2025 through April 2026.
- stated production scope including bus structures, phased-array antennas, propulsion, solar arrays, and inter-satellite lasers.
- Bastrop as the major Starlink ground-product manufacturing site and the company’s continuing push to insource more production.

Do not compare seven satellites/day in 2019 with 70/week in 2026 as though they represent the same spacecraft or a clean productivity time series. Generations, mass, capability, and production mix changed.

**SpaceX SEC correspondence, May 7, 2026**  
https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1181412/000162827926000584/filename1.htm

Use for:
- SpaceX explanation of a five-year useful life for Starlink Broadband satellites.
- statement that satellites may be capable of producing revenue beyond five years but can be retired earlier for degradation-risk management and generational transition.
- three-year useful-life discussion for a separate mobile-satellite class; do not blur that class with broadband spacecraft.

## Drafting guardrails

- Do not frame traditional spacecraft engineering as irrational or merely bureaucratic. Long replacement cycles and scarce launch opportunities made high individual-unit reliability economically sensible.
- Do not equate fleet-level redundancy with disposable or low-quality spacecraft.
- Do not imply SpaceX invented satellite mass production. OneWeb/Airbus is deliberately placed in the opening to establish the broader industrial transition.
- Do not claim complete SpaceX vertical integration. The relevant claim is control of many critical interfaces and major in-house subsystems, not ownership of every upstream component.
- Treat shortened replacement cycles as both a competitive advantage and a source of orbital/environmental obligations.
- Carry OneWeb’s bankruptcy into the next chapter as a business-model warning, not a punch line.
