# Chapter 8 evidence notes — The Customer Everywhere

This packet supports the first full draft of live Chapter 8. The chapter's job is to show orbital connectivity becoming ordinary infrastructure across households, mobility, enterprise and emergency use—while preserving the limits of affordability, regulation, capacity and substitution.

## 1. Opening scene — United Flight 2940

**United Airlines, October 14, 2025 — first Starlink-equipped mainline flight**  
https://united.mediaroom.com/2025-10-14-United-Schedules-First-Starlink-Equipped-Mainline-Flight-for-Take-Off

Use for:
- United Flight 2940 departing Newark/New York for Houston at 8:00 a.m. ET on October 15, 2025.
- Boeing 737-800 and more than 170 customers onboard.
- two Starlink antennas per 737-800.
- first major U.S. airline mainline flight offering Starlink across personal devices and seatback entertainment screens, according to United.
- airline-promoted uses: live streaming, real-time work collaboration, gaming, shopping and multiple devices.
- more than half of United's regional fleet already equipped at the time.

**Houston Airports, October 17, 2025**  
https://www.fly2houston.com/airport-business/newsroom/press-releases/item/first-united-airlines-flight-equipped-with-starlink-lands-at-iah/

## 5. Rural / interim infrastructure — Alaska

**The Kuskokwim Corporation — Connect TKC**  
https://kuskokwim.com/connect-tkc/

Use for:
- March 2023 program intended as an immediate connectivity bridge while longer-term broadband infrastructure remained under development.
- Starlink units and installations provided at no cost to participating TKC households; households responsible for ongoing service/maintenance.
- 450 households served, reaching 49% of TKC households across 46 communities and five states, according to TKC.

**NTIA, Tribal Broadband Connectivity Program awards, 2023**  
https://www.ntia.gov/press-release/2023/biden-harris-administration-announces-over-744-million-internet-all-grants-tribal-lands

Use for:
- Yukon-Kuskokwim Delta project planning Starlink installations for 1,410 homes and nine community anchor institutions across nine Alaska Native Villages.
- NTIA explicitly describing Starlink in that project as an “interim broadband solution.”

This is primary federal evidence for the chapter's bridge-not-binary framing.

**FCC 24-116 — Alaska Connect Fund**  
https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-24-116A1.pdf

Use for broader policy context:

## 8. Mobility remains legally fragmented

**Starlink support — in-motion availability**  
https://starlink.com/to/support/article/9eb841b3-2e43-a6fb-ecc7-ea58fb5600b5

Use for company disclosure that:

- international-waters availability differs from territorial permissions.

The chapter's formulation should remain: physical coverage is not the same as lawful/commercial service.

## 9. Customer-everywhere synthesis

The chapter's core argument is interpretive:

1. the network initially wins where terrestrial alternatives are weakest;
2. customers then integrate connectivity into ordinary workflows;
3. enterprise uses diversify across mobility, fixed sites and backup links;
4. the network becomes less visible during normal operation and more consequential during failure;
5. many local adoption decisions accumulate into infrastructure concentration.

The phrase **infrastructure becomes boring** is the chapter's organizing concept. Boring means the service is judged by ordinary expectations rather than admired as a space achievement.

## Drafting guardrails

- Do not imply Starlink is the best broadband technology everywhere; explicitly preserve fiber, terrestrial mobile, GEO/MEO and other LEO alternatives.
- Do not equate technical coverage with legal availability, affordability or capacity.
- Keep rural Starlink framed as one useful option and sometimes an interim bridge, not a substitute for every long-lived terrestrial build.
- Treat SpaceX's enterprise descriptions and customer-retention claims as company statements.
- Do not fabricate individual passenger, sailor, student or disaster-responder experiences.
- Keep Ukraine / wartime dependence out of this chapter; that is reserved for the later strategic-power chapter.
- The next chapter should move from customers using orbital services to customers physically entering privately operated crew transportation / orbital destinations.
