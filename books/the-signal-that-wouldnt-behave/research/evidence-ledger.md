## Known disinformation / distortion incentives

**Program advocates:** career, funding, identity, retrospective prestige, selection of best cases.

**Skeptics:** reputational reward for closure, temptation to reuse criticisms that later protocols addressed, underweighting residual statistical structure.

**Intelligence organizations:** conceal sources/methods, overstate foreign threats to secure attention, compartment operational failures, or release records with context missing.

**Foreign states:** exaggerate unconventional capabilities for deterrence or prestige; conceal genuine weak programs; seed misleading narratives.

**Popular media / memoir markets:** select spectacle, compress code-name history, omit denominators and null sessions.

**FOIA readers:** treat classification markings and CIA hosting as endorsement rather than provenance.

## Publication rule

No Class 2–4 claim should be promoted into declarative factual prose without attribution or explicit uncertainty. Where a chapter deliberately speculates, the speculative move must be identifiable from context and linked to a possible disconfirming observation.
