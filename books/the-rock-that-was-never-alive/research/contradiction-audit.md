# Contradiction and incentive audit

## Contradictions to keep visible

- **'Pristine' vs 'uncontaminated':** returned samples have controlled contamination histories, not zero contact with all terrestrial materials.
- **Ingredients vs life:** amino acids, bases, phosphate and sugars are biologically relevant but not evidence of a living system by themselves.
- **Delivery vs origin:** extraterrestrial delivery can contribute chemistry without implying that life originated in space.
- **Habitable vs inhabited:** water, brines and energy gradients establish possibility, not organisms.
- **ALH84001 then vs now:** the 1996 paper's hypothesis was scientifically legitimate to test; later abiotic explanations weaken necessity without proving every observation meaningless.
- **Chirality:** racemic mixtures support abiotic synthesis in returned samples, but enantiomeric excess alone is not automatically biological.

## Incentives

- Mission teams benefit from public excitement but also from conservative claim boundaries that protect long-term scientific credibility.
- News coverage systematically compresses 'ingredient used by life' into 'sign of life.'
- Origin-of-life communities have theoretical incentives around favored pathways; exogenous-delivery claims should therefore be quantitative.
- Panspermia advocates can borrow credibility from precursor delivery even though transfer of living systems is a separate hypothesis.
- Skeptics can overcorrect by treating abiotic explanations as proof that life is absent; failure of one biosignature is not proof of sterility.

## Disinformation / secrecy posture

No classified or alleged classified evidence is needed for this book. Historical government space programs are used only through public mission and curation records. The primary epistemic hazards are contamination, semantic inflation, publication incentives and underconstrained causal inference rather than intelligence deception.
