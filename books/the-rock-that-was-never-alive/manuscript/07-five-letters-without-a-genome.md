# 7. Five Letters Without a Genome

Adenine. Guanine. Cytosine. Thymine. Uracil.

In terrestrial biology these names belong inside a system. Bases are arranged in polymers. Sequences are copied. Errors occur. Selection acts on consequences. Information has a material inheritance mechanism.

In Bennu, the five bases are molecules in a rock.

The difference between those sentences is the difference between alphabet and literature.

All five canonical nucleobases have now been reported in the returned material. The result is exciting because it strengthens the case that key molecular precursors were available beyond Earth and could have been delivered to young planets. It is also a perfect trap. DNA and RNA use nucleobases, therefore nucleobases are not DNA and RNA.

A printing shop can contain every letter in Shakespeare without containing Hamlet.

The analogy is imperfect because prebiotic chemistry is not typesetting. Molecules interact, react, degrade, concentrate and sometimes organize. But the inferential gap is real. A base is not a sequence. A sequence is not self-replication. Self-replication is not open-ended evolution. Open-ended evolution is not necessarily a cell. The ladder has many rungs and the first few may be common.

One reason astrobiology repeatedly gets into trouble is that familiar biological molecules arrive with semantic baggage. We know what adenine does in us, so we feel we know what adenine means in a sample. The molecule has no obligation to carry its terrestrial job description across contexts.

This becomes especially important for remote sensing. Spectroscopy tends to collapse context. We detect absorption features, atmospheric gases, surface pigments, plume constituents. A sample-return mission can examine mineral associations, isotopes, spatial distributions and contamination witnesses. A telescope cannot put a grain under ten instruments.

That does not make remote biosignatures useless. It makes combinations essential.

Bennu's five letters are therefore both discovery and warning. The universe can apparently print an alphabet before it learns to write.
