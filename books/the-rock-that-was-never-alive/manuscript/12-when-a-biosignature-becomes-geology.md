# 12. When a Biosignature Becomes Geology

A failed biosignature does not become useless evidence. It becomes geology.

This is an underrated scientific upgrade.

The ALH84001 organics now help illuminate Martian water-rock reactions. Bennu's amino acids illuminate parent-body chemistry. Ryugu's molecule distributions help reconstruct alteration and transport inside an asteroid. Material once interesting because it looked close to biology becomes interesting because non-biological systems can produce it.

The category shift is psychologically difficult because the word *abiotic* sounds like subtraction. It is actually mechanism.

Serpentinization, for example, is not 'nothing happened.' Water reacts with rock, generating new minerals and chemical gradients and sometimes hydrogen. On Earth, such environments can feed ecosystems. On another world, the same reaction can generate compounds that imitate parts of an ecosystem's chemical output while no organism is present.

This creates a hierarchy of explanations that cannot be managed by slogans. A biological pathway may be possible. An abiotic pathway may be possible. The evidentiary task is to compare their predictions: isotope distributions, associated minerals, reaction products, spatial gradients, temperatures, kinetics, and the total assemblage rather than the celebrity molecule.

One reason alleged alien artifacts and biological samples attract similar mistakes is that both invite a jump from anomaly to agency. A material is unusual, therefore manufactured. A molecule is familiar, therefore biological. The missing step is generative competition: what else makes this thing?

Good anomaly science is not the art of explaining anomalies away. It is the art of making rival generators explicit enough to lose.

Bennu improves that competition by giving us a richly measured dead generator.

The asteroid is not the boring control. It is the control that keeps getting more capable.
