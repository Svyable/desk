# 11. Allan Hills, 1996

In August 1996, a Martian meteorite became the most famous rock in the world.

The team studying ALH84001 reported a suite of observations they argued were consistent with possible relic biological activity: carbonate globules, organic compounds including PAHs, fine mineral phases including magnetite, and microscopic structures that could be read as microfossil-like. The paper was careful enough to include alternatives, but public culture is bad at carrying conditional verbs.

Mars life had entered the room.

What followed is more valuable than the announcement.

Researchers challenged the size and morphology of the proposed fossils. Abiotic formation pathways were investigated for magnetite and organics. Contamination arguments were tested. The meteorite became a long-running workshop on how biosignatures fail to be singular.

By 2022, work highlighted by NASA reported that the meteorite's organic material could be explained by non-biological water-rock reactions, including serpentinization and carbonation. That does not retroactively make the 1996 work foolish. It makes ALH84001 a case study in what happens when multiple suggestive clues share a sample but not necessarily a cause.

The episode is often told as a cautionary tale about hype. That is too easy. The deeper problem was underdeveloped abiotic context. If a mineral shape resembles bacterial magnetite, how many inorganic routes have been searched? If organics occur near carbonate, how many geochemical reactions can create the association? If a structure is the size of a microbe, what nonliving structures occupy the same scale?

A biosignature can weaken without any measurement being fraudulent.

That point matters enormously for UAP-like epistemologies of anomaly, but here the domain is unusually clean. The rock exists. The instruments exist. The data can be revisited. People can disagree about formation pathways without disagreeing that the sample is Martian.

ALH84001 is therefore not a story of science being embarrassed by aliens that weren't there.

It is science learning how creative geology can be when biology is the hypothesis everyone wants to test.
