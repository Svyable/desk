## Reader promise

The U.S. government really did fund remote-viewing research and operational tasking. The interesting question is not whether a classified stamp proves psychic powers. It is why a program could accumulate statistically contested laboratory anomalies, striking individual sessions, methodological criticism, real intelligence customers, and decades of institutional life without becoming a dependable collection discipline.

The book follows the gap between **effect** and **service**: target pools, cue leakage, transcript scoring, Soviet-threat mirroring, operational tasking, customer value, the 1995 Utts/Hyman disagreement, and the intelligence problem created when correct-looking fragments cannot be distinguished prospectively from error.

## Strongest competing explanation

There was no anomalous channel to translate. Early SRI work was vulnerable to sensory cues and flexible judging; later experiments remained methodologically related and may have reproduced shared artifacts; operational anecdotes selected rare correspondences from a large, noisy archive; and Cold War threat perception plus low program cost explain the program’s longevity without requiring psi.

The manuscript treats that as a serious live explanation rather than a ritual skeptical paragraph.

## Evidence classes

1. **Verified/declassified/documented:** U.S. government sponsorship and organizational history; named SRI/Army/DIA/CIA programs; declassified session reports including both positive and poor-correlation assessments; the 1974 Nature publication; National Research Council review; the 1995 AIR evaluation; CIA’s public account of program termination and declassification.
2. **Credible but incomplete:** case-specific official claims that impressions were confirmed by HUMINT or technical sources; participant testimony about exceptional operational sessions; exact customer impact and local program-performance judgments where complete consecutive records are unavailable.
3. **Disputed/not established:** that remote viewing was a dependable operational intelligence source; that Soviet/Russian programs possessed proven psychic weapons; that famous individual cases establish a paranormal mechanism; that a validated successor program secretly continued after the public 1995 termination.
4. **Speculative synthesis/open conjecture:** adversarial uncertainty as a persistence mechanism; secrecy as selection pressure on evidence; remote viewing as a weak statistical effect that, if real, could still be operationally untaskable; and strategic mirroring as an explanation for parallel state interest.
