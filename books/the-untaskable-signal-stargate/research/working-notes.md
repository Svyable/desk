# Working notes

## Book thesis

**The Untaskable Signal** argues that the most durable lesson of the U.S. remote-viewing program is the gap between an experimental anomaly and an intelligence capability. The historical record permits a serious dispute about some controlled statistical results while simultaneously supporting a much firmer conclusion that the program did not become a dependable, calibrated intelligence source. The book treats that divergence as the subject, not as an inconvenience to be resolved by choosing believer or skeptic.

## Reader

A curious but evidence-demanding reader interested in intelligence history, anomalous cognition, classified research, experimental design, and the epistemology of disputed phenomena. The reader should not need prior familiarity with parapsychology.

## Distinct contribution

Nearby popular treatments tend to do one of four things: celebrate famous psychic-spy anecdotes; debunk the field through early methodological failures; narrate the program as Cold War weirdness; or argue the laboratory statistics in isolation. This book instead follows **translation pressure** across layers: experimental protocol → claimed anomaly → trained human source → raw intelligence report → analyst interpretation → customer decision → program evaluation.

The distinctive empirical collision is between (a) increasingly formal controlled research, (b) declassified operational transcripts including explicit failures, (c) Cold War Soviet-threat mirroring, and (d) the 1995 split between scientific and operational evaluation.

## Strongest competing explanation

There was never a paranormal or otherwise anomalous information channel. Early successes were aided by cue leakage and flexible judging; later positive results reproduced shared methodological artifacts inside a small research lineage; operational successes are selected anecdotes; and the program persisted because foreign-threat uncertainty, low costs, classification, and internal champions made continuation easier than a clean termination decision.

The manuscript should become *more* skeptical if consecutive-case reconstruction supports this explanation. Do not preserve an “unexplained residue” for atmosphere.

## Four evidence classes

1. **Verified/declassified/documented facts** — program existence, published papers, session reports, official evaluations, explicit documented failures, program names and transfers once page-indexed.
2. **Credible but incomplete reporting/testimony** — case-specific confirmation claims, participant recollections, local customer judgments, some foreign-program characterizations.
3. **Disputed allegations** — operational psychic capability, hidden successor programs, proven Soviet psychic weapons, spectacular claims lacking frozen contemporaneous records.
4. **Speculative synthesis/open conjecture** — adversarial uncertainty as a persistence mechanism; secrecy as experimental selection pressure; weak anomaly as potentially real but operationally untaskable.

## Chapter jobs

1. **The Coordinates** — establishes the four evidence classes and the gap between anomaly and service.
2. **Nature, 1974** — shows how the claim became test-shaped and entered serious public science.
3. **The Cue Problem** — establishes information leakage and target-pool design as the core experimental problem.
4. **The Enemy Might Know Something** — explains rational early state interest without converting interest into validation.
5. **The Program With Too Many Names** — breaks the myth of one seamless secret machine and examines bureaucratic persistence.
6. **Raw Intelligence** — follows the institutional handoff from viewer fragments to analyst interpretation.
7. **Tehran** — puts the method under real operational stakes and time pressure.
8. **The Search Problem** — distinguishes target matching from finding an unknown object/location in a vast hypothesis space.
9. **Hits Travel Better** — restores denominators and explains archival/anecdotal selection.
10. **The Transcript Knows the Date** — develops Marks/Kammann cue criticism without laundering it onto every later protocol.
11. **The Laboratory Gets Cleaner** — gives later protocol improvements and statistical work their strongest fair hearing.
12. **The Statistician and the Skeptic** — reconstructs the Utts/Hyman inference dispute.
13. **The Useful Lie Detector** — formalizes operational utility, calibration, and marginal information gain.
14. **The Customer** — treats customer behavior as evidence about usefulness rather than ontology.
15. **Soviet Mirrors** — separates parallel state interest from independent empirical convergence.
16. **Secrecy Is an Experimental Condition** — treats classification and compartmentation as causal variables in the evidence ecology.
17. **What Would Count** — states modern adversarial tests and explicit defeat conditions.
18. **The Untaskable Signal** — returns to the opening distinction and transfers the lessons to other classified anomalous research without claiming equivalence.

## Contradiction / incentive ledger

### Proponent incentives and vulnerabilities

- Program champions had budget and reputational incentives to foreground impressive cases.
- Viewers and researchers could sincerely remember successes more vividly than failures.
- Later memoirs are vulnerable to feedback contamination and narrative compression.
- Statistical researchers may reasonably privilege aggregate effects while underweighting operational single-case usefulness.
- “Later protocols fixed early flaws” is a claim requiring study-specific support; it cannot be used generically.

### Skeptical incentives and vulnerabilities

- Early cue failures are tempting universal explanations even when later protocols differ.
- Operational failure is tempting evidence against every laboratory result, which is a category error.
- Prior disbelief can become an unfalsifiable demand for an unspecified hidden flaw.
- Cultural ridicule can substitute for mechanism-specific criticism.

### Intelligence-community incentives

- Foreign threat can justify exploratory funding independent of domestic evidence.
- Small program cost lowers termination pressure.
- Classification protects both useful frontier exploration and weak research.
- Program transfers can preserve an unresolved question without implying covert success.
- Customers who found no value may silently stop tasking, leaving testimonial samples enriched for satisfied users.

### Archival incentives

- FOIA releases are not a randomized sample of historical attention.
- Search tools privilege memorable target words and famous names.
- Classification stamps create false epistemic authority for modern readers.
- A primary source can authentically preserve a mistaken judgment.

## Claims to avoid unless upgraded by evidence

- “CIA proved psychic powers.”
- “CIA concluded there was no effect whatsoever.”
- “The Soviets had psychic weapons.”
- “Remote viewers found X” when the actual record is an official post hoc assessment or participant recollection.
- “STARGATE ran continuously under one name/agency for twenty years.”
- “The program continued secretly after 1995.”
- “Quantum physics explains remote viewing.”
- “A patent proves a psychotronic device works.”
- “The 1974 cue problem invalidates every later experiment.”
- “The 1995 statistics prove operational utility.”

## Priority case reconstructions

1. Select one early SRI geographical-target series and rebuild target selection, transcript handling, judging, cue criticism, and reply literature.
2. Select a bounded sequence of Tehran-hostage sessions and score complete outputs prospectively against known ground truth, not famous excerpts.
3. Recover an officially described “confirmed by HUMINT/national technical sources” case and locate the independent confirming records if public.
4. Reconstruct one celebrated Soviet-facility case from original tasking through later retelling.
5. Build a consecutive sample of explicit low-correlation training/operational sessions to restore denominator context.

## Modern falsification design

A strong contemporary trial should be preregistered and jointly designed by proponents and skeptical experimentalists. Use automated random target selection; cryptographic commitment before sessions; viewer/interviewer/judge blinding; full transcript retention; no discretionary trial deletion; predeclared primary endpoint; independent replication at unaffiliated labs; and a separately registered operational benchmark with a broad target space.

For the operational benchmark, actual-target transcripts should be mixed with decoy transcripts so analysts do not know which kind they receive. If both improve analyst decisions equally, a creativity/prompting explanation gains weight. If actual-target transcripts uniquely improve information gain and decisions, the anomalous-information claim strengthens.

## Voice guardrails

The subject supplies enough weirdness. Do not decorate it. Avoid spooky scene-setting, invented rooms, invented reactions, or omniscient descriptions of participants’ thoughts. Use the bureaucracy, protocol, and documents as the concrete objects. Let the dry collision between SECRET paperwork and uncertain information carry the tone.

Do not write “not X but Y” in every chapter. Do not end every chapter with a slogan. Preserve the occasional hard line only after the evidence earns it.

## Publication state

Drafting. The argument is complete as a first coherent pass, but the book is not a release candidate. It requires major source-specific expansion, page-indexed citations, chapter-length validation, and a whole-book anti-repetition/anti-slop pass.
