# Chronology

This chronology is intentionally conservative. Program naming and sponsorship shifted, and “STARGATE” is often used retrospectively for work conducted under earlier names. Exact dates and organizational transitions should be page-indexed from official program histories in the next research pass.

- **Early 1970s** — U.S. intelligence interest grows in claims of Soviet/East European parapsychology and unconventional human-performance research. CIA support for SRI investigations begins in this period.
- **1972–1973** — SRI researchers Russell Targ and Harold Puthoff conduct high-profile experiments involving claimed extrasensory information acquisition, including work with Uri Geller and remote geographical targets.
- **October 18, 1974** — *Nature* publishes Targ and Puthoff, “Information transmission under conditions of sensory shielding,” 251:602–607.
- **Mid-1970s** — CIA and DIA assessments discuss Soviet/Czechoslovak parapsychology, “paraphysics,” psychokinesis and related research. U.S. agencies continue exploratory contracting and threat assessment.
- **1977** — CIA’s initial direct research role winds down according to the agency’s later public historical summary; Defense Department organizations continue related work.
- **1977–1978** — Army and defense organizations establish or develop operational/research efforts associated with names including GONDOLA WISH and GRILL FLAME. Exact naming boundaries require document-level reconciliation.
- **1978** — David Marks and Richard Kammann publish a *Nature* critique of remote-viewing judging, arguing that sensory cues in transcripts can account for apparent matching success in the examined material.
- **Late 1970s–early 1980s** — GRILL FLAME and related Army efforts produce training and operational session reports. The public archive contains explicit failures as well as positive evaluations.
- **1979–1981** — Iran hostage crisis becomes a real operational target. Multiple declassified GRILL FLAME sessions are tasked against hostage locations and related questions.
- **1981** — David Marks publishes “Sensory cues invalidate remote viewing experiments” in *Nature*, continuing the methodological critique.
- **Early 1980s** — Army program structures evolve; CENTER LANE is associated with later Army/INSCOM activity. Viewer training and operational methods continue.
- **1986** — Marks and Christopher Scott publish “Remote viewing exposed” in *Nature*, part of the skeptical methodological record.
- **Mid-to-late 1980s** — Program sponsorship and naming continue to shift, including SUN STREAK. SRI research leadership also evolves; Edwin May becomes a central research figure.
- **1988** — National Research Council publishes *Enhancing Human Performance: Issues, Theories, and Techniques*. Its review finds little or no scientific evidence supporting paranormal techniques such as remote viewing for Army purposes.
- **Late 1980s–early 1990s** — Government-supported anomalous-cognition research continues, including work at SRI and later SAIC under Edwin May and collaborators. Later protocols emphasize more formal experimental controls and statistical analysis than early demonstrations.
- **Early 1990s** — STAR GATE becomes the name associated with the DIA program in its final phase; retrospective accounts often apply STARGATE/STAR GATE to the broader historical lineage.
- **1994** — Funding and institutional support weaken substantially; program future becomes uncertain.
- **1995** — DIA transfers the program to CIA for review. CIA commissions the American Institutes for Research to evaluate the research and operational applications.
- **1995** — Jessica Utts concludes the controlled research provides persuasive statistical evidence for anomalous cognition; Ray Hyman rejects the inference that paranormal functioning has been established and emphasizes methodological dependence and lack of sufficiently independent replication.
- **1995** — Operational review does not find remote viewing sufficiently reliable and useful for intelligence. CIA does not restore the program.
- **Mid-to-late 1990s** — At congressional urging, CIA works with DIA and Army to gather and declassify a large historical collection of records.
- **1998–2000s** — Many STARGATE/GRILL FLAME records are released through FOIA/CREST, including session reports from the late 1970s and 1980s.
- **2016 onward** — CIA’s Electronic Reading Room makes much of the declassified collection searchable online, greatly increasing public access while also encouraging document-by-document discovery detached from original sequence.
- **2020s** — Remote viewing remains culturally and experimentally contested. The historical program is frequently cited in public discussions of anomalous cognition, UAPs, consciousness, and government secrecy, often with insufficient separation between documented program existence and demonstrated efficacy.

## Chronology cautions

1. Do not infer a single uninterrupted organizational chain merely from the succession of code names.
2. Do not assume every document now filed in the STARGATE collection was created under a program formally named STARGATE.
3. Distinguish dates of document creation, original classification, declassification decision, and web publication.
4. For every famous operational case, build a micro-chronology from tasking through transcript, feedback, evaluation, and later retelling before using it as a narrative centerpiece.
