# 9. The Sterile Temptation

Sterile neutrinos are theoretically legal in a way that makes them dangerous to the imagination. The Standard Model counts three active flavors coupled to the Z boson, but gauge-singlet neutral fermions are not forbidden. Add one light state and oscillation phenomenology acquires another mass splitting and mixing angles.

A gallium deficit can be fit by νe disappearance. The required parameter region tends toward large electron-flavor mixing at eV-scale Δm². Historically this connected gallium to the reactor antineutrino anomaly and, more loosely, to accelerator appearance anomalies such as LSND and MiniBooNE.

The temptation is synthesis by proximity: three anomalies, one particle.

But the channels disagree in important ways. Electron-neutrino disappearance, electron-antineutrino disappearance, and muon-to-electron appearance constrain different combinations of mixing matrix elements. Global fits often exhibit appearance-disappearance tension. A model flexible enough to touch each anomaly can still fail to make them mutually consistent.

The sterile hypothesis deserves to live or die by those cross-channel debts, not by its narrative elegance.
