# Back Matter

## Chronology

- 1990s — GALLEX conducts 51Cr source calibrations of its gallium solar-neutrino detector; measured production is lower than conventional expectation.
- 1990s–2000s — SAGE performs 51Cr and 37Ar source tests, again obtaining low ratios.
- 2010 — combined reanalysis helps establish the modern “gallium anomaly” framing.
- 2011–2020 — reactor and accelerator sterile-neutrino programs expand; appearance/disappearance tension grows.
- 2018–2020 — very-short-baseline reactor experiments, including STEREO, increasingly constrain simple sterile explanations of the reactor anomaly.
- 9 June 2022 — BEST results published in Physical Review Letters: both concentric gallium zones show deficits.
- 30 May 2024 — Norman et al. publish a precise 71Ge half-life measurement, ruling out a sufficiently large half-life shift as an explanation.
- 23 February 2026 — Cadeddu et al. publish a self-consistent electron-wave-function reassessment in Physical Review D.
- 31 July 2026 — Physical Review Letters accepts Cadeddu et al.'s beyond-factorization cross-section calculation, which can potentially resolve the anomaly without sterile neutrinos.
- 10 August 2026 — Iwata, Ejiri and Sarkar publish a new low-energy 71Ga response study using electron-capture rates, corrected charge-exchange information and shell-model calculations.

## Defeat conditions

The book's central synthesis would be weakened if independent calculations show the 2026 factorization correction to be negligible under realistic 71Ge transition densities; if a future spatially resolved source experiment observes an unambiguous oscillatory L/E pattern independent of absolute cross section; or if a direct cross-section measurement validates the older prediction at few-percent precision while the deficit persists.

It would be strengthened if revised cross sections independently converge on a roughly 15–20% reduction and simultaneously reproduce nuclear observables not used in tuning.
