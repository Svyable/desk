# 16. What Five Sigma Means When the Model Moves

Five sigma is one of physics' most abused pieces of punctuation.

The gallium deficit exceeded five sigma under widely used capture-cross-section predictions. The statistical statement is real. If those prediction distributions and experimental uncertainties are right, the observed rates are extraordinarily low.

But sigma counts distance inside a model. Change a theoretically inferred normalization or broaden a nuclear uncertainty and the standardized distance changes even though not one germanium atom moves.

This does not mean significance is fake. It means significance is conditional.

Particle physics learned to demand five sigma because large data sets produce fluctuations and because trials factors can manufacture excitement. Gallium teaches a different lesson: theoretical systematics can be structurally wrong rather than merely Gaussianly uncertain. If the approximation that defines the central value fails, adding a small nuisance parameter around that value does not protect the inference.

The correct response is not nihilism. It is modularity. Publish raw production rates. Publish source activities and covariance. Publish zone geometry. Publish extraction efficiencies. Publish multiple cross-section predictions with their ingredients exposed. Then new nuclear theory can update interpretation without rewriting the experimental record.

An anomaly should be able to survive the death of its favorite denominator.
