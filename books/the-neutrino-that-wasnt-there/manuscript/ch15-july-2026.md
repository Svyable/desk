# 15. July 2026

On July 31, 2026, Physical Review Letters accepted a paper by Cadeddu, Cargioli, Dordei, Ferro, Giunti and Pitzalis with a title unusually explicit for a mature anomaly: “Possible solution to the gallium anomaly moving beyond the leptonic wave-function factorization.”

The authors revisited the 71Ga neutrino-capture cross section without the standard factorized treatment. They used phenomenologically constrained Gamow-Teller transition densities chosen to reproduce the precisely measured 71Ge half-life. The resulting source cross section can be substantially smaller than the conventional value—small enough, in their analysis, potentially to account for the roughly twenty-percent deficit without invoking sterile neutrinos.

This is evidence class one about the paper's existence and calculation. It is not evidence class one that the anomaly is solved.

The result must survive independent nuclear-theory reproduction, scrutiny of transition-density assumptions, consistency with charge-exchange data, and comparison against other observables. A calculation can move a denominator; nature must still agree.

But the timing changes the book. We are no longer writing a retrospective about an unexplained anomaly. We are watching the explanatory center of gravity move in real time from beyond-Standard-Model phenomenology toward low-energy nuclear electroweak structure.

That is worth physicists' attention because this kind of migration will happen again.
