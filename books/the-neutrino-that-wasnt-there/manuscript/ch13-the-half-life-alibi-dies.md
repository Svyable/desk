# 13. The Half-Life Alibi Dies

One escape route was pleasantly concrete: perhaps the accepted 71Ge half-life was wrong enough to affect the inferred neutrino capture cross section and event counting.

In 2024, E. B. Norman and collaborators remeasured it with three approaches. They obtained 11.468 ± 0.008 days, consistent with the accepted value but much more precise.

That result matters because null work rarely gets the drama it deserves. A mundane explanation was asked to identify itself and failed.

The half-life cannot carry a twenty-percent deficit.

This does not push all probability mass into sterile neutrinos. It pushes it into the remaining boxes: source strength, chemical/counting systematics, nuclear transition strengths, electron wave functions, or new physics. Good anomaly science is often subtraction.

The 2024 measurement is also a warning against the phrase “systematics could explain it.” Which systematic? How large? Does an independent measurement permit it? By 2024 one candidate had been turned from a gesture into a corpse.
