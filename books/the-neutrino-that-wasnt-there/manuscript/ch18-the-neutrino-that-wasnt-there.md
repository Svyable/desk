# 18. The Neutrino That Wasn't There

Perhaps there is a sterile neutrino. Perhaps gallium will help find it. The title of this book is not a verdict.

It is a warning about ontology.

For three decades a missing fraction of expected neutrinos slowly acquired the social shape of a missing particle. The move was rational: oscillations had solved the solar-neutrino problem; eV-scale sterile states were allowed; reactor and accelerator anomalies seemed nearby. BEST then drove the gallium deficit above five sigma under conventional predictions.

And still the two-zone geometry did not deliver the clean oscillatory signature one might have hoped for. Reactor searches tightened. Cosmology complained. The 71Ge half-life was remeasured and refused blame. Nuclear theorists returned to the capture calculation. By 2026, an accepted PRL argued that the conventional leptonic factorization itself may have put too much interaction strength in the denominator.

If that survives, the gallium anomaly will not become boring. It will become exemplary.

A calibration experiment will have discovered that the calibration theory was less settled than the field believed. A five-sigma particle-physics anomaly will have exposed a low-energy nuclear/electronic-structure approximation. The detectors will have been right to report the deficit. The phenomenologists will have been right to explore sterile oscillations. The nuclear theorists will have been right to reopen a supposedly mature cross section.

Science is at its strongest when those statements can all be true without anyone pretending the final explanation was obvious in advance.

The missing neutrino may yet appear. But the deeper discovery is already here: in precision physics, the denominator is part of the apparatus.
