# 5. The First Missing Fifth

The early source results were not individually a five-sigma revolution. They accumulated into an irritation.

When reanalyses combined GALLEX and SAGE calibrations using updated 71Ga capture cross sections, the average ratio landed below unity enough to become a named discrepancy. Depending on cross-section choices and statistical treatment, the significance shifted. This is important. The anomaly was never just a pile of counts; it was counts divided by a model.

That denominator contained excited-state contributions to 71Ge. The source neutrino energies are high enough to reach low-lying excited states unavailable to the Sun's lowest-energy pp neutrinos. A misestimate there could selectively affect calibrations.

The sterile interpretation, meanwhile, had a convenient L/E. Meter baselines and sub-MeV neutrinos make sensitivity to eV-scale mass splittings plausible. The survival probability in a 3+1 model can be written with an effective amplitude sin²2θee and Δm²41. For sufficiently rapid oscillations across a broad target, the pattern can average into an apparent normalization deficit.

Notice the problem. Exactly the regime that makes sterile oscillations plausible can also erase the spatial wiggle that would distinguish them from a cross-section normalization error.

This was the ambiguity BEST was designed to attack.
