# 10. The Reactor That Disagreed

Reactors once seemed like gallium's natural ally. Short-baseline antineutrino experiments observed fewer events than flux models predicted, creating the reactor antineutrino anomaly. An eV-scale sterile state could reduce both reactor ν̄e and gallium νe survival.

Then the reactor story became more complicated.

Very-short-baseline experiments such as STEREO were built to look for relative spectral distortions across baselines, a test less dependent on absolute reactor flux predictions. STEREO found data compatible with no sterile oscillation and excluded important parts of the parameter space associated with the original reactor-anomaly best fit.

Meanwhile reactor-flux modeling itself acquired known problems, including the famous 5 MeV spectral bump. This made “reactor deficit equals oscillation” less secure.

Gallium therefore lost the comfort of a clean companion anomaly. The sterile region preferred by BEST can run into disappearance constraints elsewhere, depending on model assumptions and data combinations.

A new particle should improve coherence across experiments. If it fixes one denominator by making several others worse, the bookkeeper should become suspicious.
