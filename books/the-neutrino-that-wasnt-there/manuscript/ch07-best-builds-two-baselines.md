# 7. BEST Builds Two Baselines

The chromium source at Baksan was strong enough to make an old solar-neutrino detector behave like a small neutrino laboratory. Its measured activity was 3.414 megacuries. BEST put it at the common center of two gallium targets and asked a question the earlier source calibrations could not ask cleanly: does the deficit change with distance?

That geometry mattered because the two leading stories make different promises. A wrong overall gallium capture cross section can lower both zones together. Meter-scale oscillation into a sterile state can, for some mass splittings, imprint a spatial dependence as the neutrino beam crosses the inner and outer volumes. BEST was therefore not simply another measurement of whether gallium came up short. It was an attempt to make two explanations disagree inside the same apparatus.

The collaboration repeatedly exposed the targets, chemically extracted the few atoms of radioactive germanium that had been made, and counted them. The inner target produced 54.9 atoms of 71Ge per day, with statistical uncertainty of about 2.5 atoms per day and a 1.4-atom systematic uncertainty. The outer target produced 55.6 per day with comparable errors. Converted into measured-to-predicted ratios, the inner zone was 0.79 ± 0.05 and the outer zone about 0.77 ± 0.05. Both were roughly a fifth low.

The most revealing number was less dramatic: outer divided by inner was 0.97 ± 0.07. Within the experiment's precision, the two zones agreed.

That is awkward evidence for anyone hoping BEST would draw a neat oscillation wave across the tanks. It does not rule out sterile-neutrino oscillations. At sufficiently large mass splitting, oscillations can average over the finite source and target geometry and leave both zones suppressed by nearly the same amount; the BEST collaboration explicitly reported allowed sterile-neutrino regions of that kind. But the near-equality means the experiment did not obtain the spatial signature that would have made an oscillation interpretation unusually hard to imitate with a common normalization error.

Nor does equal suppression prove that the gallium cross section is wrong. The predicted rate also depends on source activity, extraction and counting efficiencies, geometry, and the nuclear calculation connecting 51Cr neutrino lines to 71Ga capture. BEST's value is that many of those quantities are shared or tightly related between the zones, while baseline is deliberately changed. A ratio between zones cancels much of what an absolute rate cannot.

So the experiment succeeded in an inconvenient way. It reproduced the deficit in a purpose-built apparatus strongly enough that the old gallium anomaly became harder to dismiss as a historical accident. At the same time, its internal comparison declined to tell physicists whether the missing events had gone into a new neutrino state or had first been created on paper by an imperfect prediction.

Two tanks were supposed to turn one anomaly into a shape. They gave almost the same number instead. That nearly flat geometry is not the absence of information. It is the reason the denominator became interesting.
