# 7. BEST Builds Two Baselines

BEST was an experiment designed by people who understood exactly why a single low ratio was not enough.

A powerful 51Cr source, measured at about 3.4 megacuries, was placed at the center of two concentric gallium volumes. The inner zone surrounded the source closely; the outer zone extended the baseline. If electron neutrinos oscillated into a sterile state on the meter scale, the production rate might differ between zones in a way a common cross-section error could not easily mimic.

This is experimental geometry as argument.

The source was exposed repeatedly. Germanium was extracted independently from the zones. The collaboration then compared measured 71Ge production with predictions.

The result was not the clean sinusoid sterile-neutrino enthusiasts might have ordered. Both zones were low.

That sounds disappointing only if one mistakes experiments for confirmation machines. The two-zone result sharpened the anomaly by showing that the deficit survived a purpose-built test, while simultaneously weakening the most naive hope that the spatial structure would identify a unique oscillation length.

Nature, or the denominator, had refused to make the plot pretty.
