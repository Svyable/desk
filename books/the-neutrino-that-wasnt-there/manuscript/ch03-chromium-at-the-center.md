# 3. Chromium at the Center

A 51Cr neutrino source is a lovely object conceptually: radioactive chromium decays by electron capture and produces nearly monoenergetic electron neutrinos. There is no reactor fuel evolution, no kilometer baseline, no atmosphere, no accelerator beam simulation. Put the source near gallium, know the activity, integrate the geometry, and count germanium.

Reality adds edges. Source activity is inferred calorimetrically and by nuclear data. Different neutrino lines carry different energies and branching fractions. The capture cross section changes with energy. Shielding and source construction matter to handling even when neutrinos themselves leave almost untouched. Geometry must be integrated over a large target rather than replaced by one cartoon distance.

Still, compared with many neutrino measurements, the experiment is brutally local.

GALLEX's chromium calibrations and SAGE's source exposures were therefore unsettling. Their combined low rate could be read as an efficiency problem, but the collaborations had reasons to believe the extraction and counting machinery. It could be read as nuclear cross-section trouble, but the conventional calculations had pedigrees. Or it could be the short-baseline oscillation signature of an extra neutrino state.

The seductive move is to ask which explanation is exotic. That is the wrong scale. Sterile neutrinos are exotic at the particle level. A 10–20 percent error in a mature weak-interaction cross section is exotic at the metrology level. Either would be a serious discovery.

An anomaly is interesting when every exit is expensive.
