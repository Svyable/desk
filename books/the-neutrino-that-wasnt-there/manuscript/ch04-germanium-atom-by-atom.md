# 4. Germanium, Atom by Atom

The gallium experiment ends not with a neutrino but with germanium.

After exposure, germanium carrier and newly produced 71Ge are chemically extracted, synthesized into a counting gas, and introduced into counters designed to recognize the low-energy signatures of 71Ge electron capture. The number of neutrino interactions is inferred from a decay-time distribution laid over backgrounds.

This is where vague accusations about “detector error” become useless. A serious alternative must name a mechanism and pay its quantitative bill. Was germanium extraction inefficient under source-running conditions but not under routine solar operation? Was there an unmodeled loss in synthesis? Were counter efficiencies wrong? Did background modeling steal events? Did carrier recovery fail to trace radioactive 71Ge?

Decades of gallium work developed internal checks precisely because a radiochemical detector gives no event-by-event directional or energy information. BEST inherited that culture.

The important lesson is not that chemistry cannot be wrong. Chemistry is perfectly capable of being wrong. It is that an explanation earns weight by reproducing the structure of the residual. A common extraction bias could depress both zones of BEST similarly. Oscillation predicts a baseline pattern. A cross-section shift changes normalization everywhere. These hypotheses are not interchangeable once the apparatus has more than one spatial region.

The book will keep returning to that geometry because it is the closest thing the anomaly has to a fingerprint.
