# Evidence Ledger

## Class 1 — Verified / documented facts

- GALLEX and SAGE performed intense radioactive-source calibrations and reported measured-to-predicted 71Ge production ratios below unity.
- BEST used a 3.414-MCi 51Cr source at the center of two concentric gallium targets and published low production ratios in both zones in PRL 128, 232501 (2022).
- The 71Ge half-life was remeasured as 11.468 ± 0.008 d in PRC 109, 055501 (2024), consistent with the established value.
- STEREO's relative-baseline reactor measurements found no evidence for the simple light-sterile explanation of the reactor antineutrino anomaly in its tested region.
- A February 2026 PRD calculation reassessed 71Ga capture with self-consistent electron wave functions.
- On 31 July 2026 PRL accepted a calculation moving beyond leptonic wave-function factorization that finds substantially reduced source capture cross sections are possible.
- A separate August 2026 PRC paper evaluated low-energy 71Ga weak responses using electron-capture rates, corrected charge-exchange responses and shell-model calculations.

## Class 2 — Credible but incomplete

- Some or all of the gallium deficit may originate in shortcomings of the conventional 71Ga source-neutrino cross-section calculation.
- Meter-scale νe disappearance into a light sterile state remains a mathematically viable description of gallium rates in parts of parameter space.
- The similarity of BEST inner/outer deficits is more naturally compatible with a common normalization shift than with some oscillation patterns, but rapid or averaged oscillations can mimic normalization.

## Class 3 — Disputed / unconfirmed

- That BEST constitutes detection of a sterile neutrino.
- That one 2026 cross-section calculation has definitively solved the gallium anomaly.
- That reactor, gallium, LSND and MiniBooNE anomalies all arise from the same sterile state.
- That cosmology model-independently excludes every eV-scale sterile-neutrino construction.

## Class 4 — Speculative synthesis / open conjecture

- The gallium anomaly may become a canonical example of a statistically decisive discrepancy caused by a structurally incomplete theoretical denominator rather than an experimental error or new particle.
- Future neutrino anomalies should be analyzed with prediction models treated as calibrated instruments, including approximation failure modes rather than only parametric uncertainties.
