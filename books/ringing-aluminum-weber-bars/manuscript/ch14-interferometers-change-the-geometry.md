# Interferometers Change the Geometry

A resonant bar asks spacetime to ring a mass. An interferometer asks spacetime to change the relative lengths of two paths.

The conceptual shift is enormous.

Instead of waiting for a narrow mechanical resonance in a single massive cylinder, laser interferometers compare optical path lengths along perpendicular arms. Suspended mirrors serve as test masses. A passing gravitational wave changes the differential arm length, altering the interference pattern of the light.

The attraction is obvious only after decades of engineering have made it look obvious. Interferometers promised broader bandwidth and exquisite strain sensitivity, but they also created a detector that was almost offensively vulnerable to the world. Seismic motion, thermal motion, laser frequency noise, radiation pressure, scattered light, control-system couplings and countless instrumental states could move the readout.

The solution was not to build one perfect instrument.

It was to build an observatory whose internal behavior could be measured almost as obsessively as the universe.

This is where the Weber comparison becomes most useful if kept modest. Weber had already discovered the basic epistemic problem: a detector claiming an invisible cosmic signal must explain why its own body did not make the event. Interferometers multiplied the number of ways the body could speak.

The answer became an ecology of witness sensors and models. Seismometers. Magnetometers. microphones. control channels. calibration systems. veto studies. environmental injections. hardware injections. independent sites.

The detector ceased to be a single sensor and became a monitored organism.

Laser interferometry also changed how source theory and data analysis interacted. Compact binary inspirals produce waveforms that general relativity can predict with enough accuracy to support matched filtering. The signal is not just an excess energy pulse. It has structure: a chirp whose frequency and amplitude evolve in a constrained way.

That structure gives the analyst more leverage but also introduces model dependence. A template bank can find weak signals buried in noise because it knows what to look for. It can also miss signals outside its assumptions. Modern searches therefore use multiple pipelines, including less-modeled burst analyses, and compare results.

The contrast with Weber's era is not primitive versus sophisticated. Weber's bars were sophisticated for their time. The deeper contrast is evidentiary density.

By 2015, a gravitational-wave candidate would have to survive far more than coincidence.

It would have to survive the detector.
