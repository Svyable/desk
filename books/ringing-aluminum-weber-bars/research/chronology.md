# Chronology

- **1916–1918** — General relativity's gravitational-wave solutions and early radiation calculations enter the literature.
- **1950s** — Physical status and detectability of gravitational waves become clearer through theoretical work and relativity meetings.
- **1959–1960** — Weber publishes foundational detector concepts and work on detection/generation of gravitational waves.
- **1965–1967** — Weber's resonant-mass apparatus operates; early detector papers appear.
- **June 16, 1969** — *Physical Review Letters* publishes “Evidence for Discovery of Gravitational Radiation.”
- **1970** — Weber publishes sidereal anisotropy claim; Peter Kafka and others emphasize severe astrophysical consequences of the reported flux.
- **1972–1974** — Independent groups publish increasingly sensitive replication/null results; IBM and Bell Labs results become central to the dispute.
- **Mid-to-late 1970s** — Weber's claimed detections lose broad acceptance, while resonant-mass detector research continues.
- **1980s–2000s** — Cryogenic resonant bars and bar networks improve sensitivity; interferometer programs mature.
- **September 14, 2015** — Advanced LIGO records GW150914 at Livingston and Hanford.
- **February 11, 2016** — LIGO/Virgo publicly announce the first confirmed direct gravitational-wave detection.
