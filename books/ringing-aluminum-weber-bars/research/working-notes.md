Repository search on default branch returned no substantive Weber/resonant-bar manuscript.

## Core question

What changes in a field after a plausible instrument reports a phenomenon that later turns out to be real, but the claimed events do not survive independent replication?

## Distinct contribution

Most popular histories use Weber as a prelude to LIGO. This book makes the failed claim itself the center: not as scandal, but as a stress test of coincidence, calibration, negative evidence, source plausibility and instrument self-knowledge.

## Strongest counterargument

Nothing deep is required. Weber's analysis was wrong; other groups demonstrated that; LIGO later used a different technology. The book must therefore earn any broader thesis by documenting specific methodological tensions rather than claiming a romantic direct lineage.

## Prohibited overclaims

- Do not say Weber fabricated data.
- Do not say one proven bug explains all events unless a primary-source reanalysis establishes it.
- Do not imply LIGO's later discovery vindicates Weber.
- Do not imply modern blind injections or data-quality systems were created specifically because of Weber unless documentary evidence supports that genealogy.
- Do not treat participant memoirs as neutral adjudication.

## Voice note

Keep the machinery concrete. Aluminum, resonance, piezoelectric readout, calibration impulses, event windows, thermal motion. Avoid turning the book into a generic philosophy-of-science lecture. Let each abstraction be paid for by an experimental detail.
