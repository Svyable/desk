# Evidence Matrix

## Class 1 — verified / documented

- Weber proposed and built resonant-mass gravitational-wave detectors.
- He published claims of coincident detector events and argued in 1969 that they constituted good evidence for discovery of gravitational radiation.
- He later reported a sidereal anisotropy in the event intensity.
- Multiple independent groups built resonant-mass detectors and reported null results inconsistent with a persistent Weber-strength event population.
- Garwin and Levine explicitly calibrated detection efficiency in their negative experiments.
- Resonant-mass detector development continued after Weber's claims lost acceptance.
- Advanced LIGO detected GW150914 on September 14, 2015; the collaboration announced the result on February 11, 2016.

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete / interpretation-dependent

- Weber's excess likely involved analysis and event-definition choices that other groups did not share.
- His use of a quadratic detector and hands-on analysis may have contributed materially to the discrepancy, as argued retrospectively by Levine.
- Personality, institutional trust and funding dynamics affected how rapidly the dispute hardened.
- The Weber controversy influenced the experimental culture of later gravitational-wave searches in ways that are plausible but difficult to isolate from broader developments in experimental physics.

## Class 3 — disputed / not established

- Weber detected genuine astrophysical gravitational waves in 1969–70.
- A single uniquely identified software, arithmetic or hardware error explains the entire event population.
- Independent null experiments were collectively incapable of observing the class of signals Weber reported.
- LIGO's later detection validates Weber's events.

## Class 4 — speculative synthesis / open conjecture

- False discovery claims can accelerate methodological specification by making hidden degrees of analytical freedom visible.
- A field becomes robust when it can preserve a measurement principle while discarding a founding detection claim.
- Gravitational-wave astronomy's most important technological progress was accompanied by an equally important increase in evidentiary density around each candidate event.

## Contradiction ledger

The manuscript must preserve these tensions rather than resolving them rhetorically:

- Weber was both a genuine pioneer and, by the later community judgment, wrong about his claimed detections.
- Coincidence is a powerful control and an insufficient causal proof.
- Negative replications can be decisive while still differing technically from the original apparatus.
- Astrophysical implausibility is evidence against an interpretation but not a logical veto on surprising nature.
- Later detector practice resembles solutions to problems exposed by the Weber episode, but resemblance is not proof of direct historical causation.
