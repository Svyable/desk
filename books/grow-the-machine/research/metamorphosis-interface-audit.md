# Grow the Machine — metamorphosis-interface audit

## Why this pass

Chapter 1 has the right conceptual object: the unusual HI-MEMS move was not merely attaching a controller to an insect but intervening during development. The current evidence ledger, however, leaves the Cornell/Boyce Thompson pupal-interface work in Class 2 pending recovery. That primary work is recoverable and materially changes the evidence boundary.

This note makes one bounded correction: reconstruct what early-metamorphosis insertion actually demonstrated, separate that result from the later beetle free-flight demonstrations, and preserve the strongest counterargument to calling development a manufacturing process.

## Primary program record: what DARPA asked for

The March 9, 2006 federal solicitation notice for **BAA06-22, Hybrid Insect MEMS (HI-MEMS)** says DARPA sought proposals to create insect-cyborgs, possibly by intimately integrating microsystems within insects during early stages of metamorphosis.

Archived federal solicitation notice: https://www.fbodaily.com/archive/2006/03-March/11-Mar-2006/FBO-01003518.htm

The 2008 DARPA Fact File already in the book's evidence ledger adds the later program-level mission objective and describes exploiting developmental entry points to establish interfaces.

**Boundary:** solicitation language establishes the proposed engineering strategy, not successful integration, flight, sensing, navigation, energy harvesting, or operational use.

## Primary performer evidence: development really did alter the interface

Bozkurt, Gilmour, Sinha, Stern & Lal, **“Insect–Machine Interface Based Neurocybernetics,” IEEE Transactions on Biomedical Engineering 56(6), 2009**, is the key paper the current ledger was waiting for.

Accessible author-hosted copy: https://ibionics.ece.ncsu.edu/assets/Publications/insect_machine_interface_based_neurocybernetics.pdf

The paper reports microfabricated probes inserted into *Manduca sexta* during metamorphic development. Its central result is stronger than “the implant survived.” The authors report tissue development around the inserted electronics and characterize the resulting interface as mechanically stable and electrically reliable. The paper explicitly compares developmental-stage insertion with adult-stage insertion and reports substantially less adverse reaction to pupal-stage electronics.

That supports moving a **narrow** proposition into Class 1:

> In *Manduca sexta* under the tested laboratory conditions, inserting probes during metamorphic development could yield adult bioelectronic interfaces whose mechanical attachment and electrical coupling benefited from tissue development around the probes.

It does **not** support the broader propositions that metamorphosis automatically integrates arbitrary electronics, that the interface is permanently stable, that it beats every adult implantation method, or that a useful mission payload can be fabricated this way.

The paper's own framing matters. It treats metamorphic growth as a way to form the interface. That makes the book's “development as fabrication” interpretation less metaphorical than the current ledger suggests. But the demonstrated fabrication operation is specifically **interface formation/anchoring and coupling**, not construction of a whole machine.

## A second 2009 paper closes part of the actuation bridge

Bozkurt, Gilmour & Lal, **“Balloon-Assisted Flight of Radio-Controlled Insect Biobots,” IEEE Transactions on Biomedical Engineering 56(9), 2009**, reports radio-controlled *Manduca sexta* flight using probes implanted through the authors' Early Metamorphosis Insertion Technology.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1109/TBME.2009.2022551

This is important because it links developmentally formed interfaces to subsequent neuromuscular actuation in the adult moth. It still does not establish the full HI-MEMS mission architecture. The balloon itself is a useful warning against romanticizing biological miniaturization: payload and lift remained engineering constraints.

For narrative purposes, the moth and beetle lines should not be silently fused. The Cornell/Boyce Thompson *Manduca* work is the cleaner evidence for **metamorphosis-built interface formation**. The Berkeley/Michigan beetle work is the cleaner evidence for impressive untethered flight control. Combining their strongest properties into one imaginary insect would recreate exactly the proposal → subsystem → remembered capability compression the chapter warns against.

## Fresh 2026 counterexample: implantation can help for ordinary geometry reasons

Kai, Long, Lin & Sato, **“Fully Implanted Miniature Radio Controller Boosts Cyborg Insect Mobility in Challenging Terrains,” Cyborg and Bionic Systems (2026)**, provides a useful modern control on the thesis.

Publisher page: https://spj.science.org/doi/10.34133/cbsystems.0589

The authors report a 10 × 10 × 3 mm fully implanted wireless controller in cockroaches. Implanted animals retained gap-negotiation behavior closer to intact insects than animals carrying electronics on the back, and an automatic stimulation algorithm navigated implanted cockroaches to a target with a reported 90.9% success rate under the study's conditions.

This is **not** a metamorphosis result. That is precisely why it belongs in this book. Some advantages attributed rhetorically to “growing the machine” may actually come from the simpler fact that putting electronics inside the body preserves external geometry, reduces snagging, or changes mass distribution. A mature-stage implant can therefore capture part of the apparent advantage without recruiting development as a fabrication process.

The book should force developmental integration to beat this control rather than treating every internal biohybrid as evidence for morphogenesis-as-manufacturing.

## Strongest counterargument

The broad thesis fails if development is doing no uniquely valuable engineering work.

A foreign object surrounded by tissue after metamorphosis is not automatically “manufactured by the organism.” Tissue may merely tolerate or encapsulate it. Mechanical anchoring may improve while electrical selectivity, long-term stability, emergence yield, animal health, calibration, or mission reliability worsens. And if an adult-stage internal implant delivers the same functional advantage with better yield and controllability, invoking metamorphosis adds biological complexity rather than removing manufacturing complexity.

The comparison therefore needs at least three arms wherever evidence permits:

1. external adult-mounted electronics;
2. internal adult-stage implantation;
3. developmental-stage implantation followed through emergence.

The developmental-manufacturing claim earns its larger language only when arm 3 produces a measurable advantage attributable to development itself: stronger attachment, lower interface impedance/drift, better tissue coupling, lower mass/volume penalty, higher functional lifetime, less adverse response, simpler assembly, or another specified metric after survival and yield are counted.

## Revision consequences for Chapter 1

The opening should eventually make four distinctions explicit without turning them into a checklist on the page:

- DARPA proposed developmental integration in 2006.
- The Cornell/Boyce Thompson moth work later demonstrated that metamorphic tissue growth could form mechanically/electrically useful probe interfaces.
- The spectacular untethered beetle demonstrations are a related but technically distinct evidence line and should not inherit the moth work's developmental provenance automatically.
- Modern internal implants show that “inside the animal” and “built by development” are different engineering propositions.

This sharpens rather than weakens the chapter's best idea. The remarkable result is no longer merely that DARPA imagined letting metamorphosis do assembly work. Under bounded laboratory conditions, researchers actually recruited development to form part of a functional bioelectronic interface. The remaining question is how much work development did that ordinary implantation could not.

## Publication holds

Before later chapters generalize this result, recover from the full Bozkurt 2009 methods/results and figures:

- developmental stage and exact insertion geometry;
- sample counts and survival/emergence denominators;
- mechanical pull/attachment measurements, if quantified;
- impedance/electrical-coupling measurements and time course;
- adult-stage control details;
- histological evidence for tissue response;
- duration of functional interface after emergence.

Do not use “the insects did not react adversely” as a general animal-welfare or long-term biocompatibility conclusion. Treat it only within the paper's measured endpoints and observation window.
