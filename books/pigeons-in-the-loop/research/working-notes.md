# Working Notes

## Core distinction

Do not ask `did pigeon guidance work?` as one question. Maintain five layers:

1. animal visual discrimination;
2. moving-target pursuit;
3. disturbance robustness;
4. peck-to-servo transduction;
5. integrated weapon guidance.

Most popular accounts slide from layer 2 to layer 5.

## Naming discipline

- **Project Pigeon**: wartime Skinner-centered biological guidance work.
- **Pelican**: related guided-weapon/airframe context; do not lazily use as synonym for Pigeon without source support.
- **ORCON**: postwar Naval Research Laboratory `organic control` continuation.
- **Bat**: electronic radar-homing glide bomb comparator, not simply `the same bomb with radar` unless specific airframe genealogy is documented.

## Voice

The bird is already strange enough. Do not decorate.

Avoid:
- `nature's computer` as an unqualified claim;
- generic `innovation is often mocked` morals;
- pretending Skinner invented machine vision;
- retroactive AI language except as explicitly labeled functional analogy;
- turning military animal use into whimsy.

Prefer the physical loop: lens → image → peck → pickup → servo → airframe.

## Epistemic pressure points

- Skinner is indispensable and interested.
- Museum artifact is strong on existence, weak on performance.
- NRL report should outrank later popular retellings for ORCON.
- NDRC records should outrank remembered cancellation stories.
- Final Bat success cannot substitute for Bat maturity at the earlier Pigeon decision date.

## Potential book-defining calculation

If source data permit, reconstruct a simple closed-loop model using measured peck spatial variance and latency, transducer gain and airframe response. The goal is not to simulate a fictional combat shot; it is to identify which parameter most strongly constrains stability. This could test whether the real bottleneck was the bird, the pickup/servo, or the vehicle.

## Underwritten angle

The strongest book is not `we almost had pigeon missiles`.

It is `for a brief period, an evolved visual system may have been a rational engineering substitute for electronics, and the archival task is to find exactly where that substitution stopped being competitive`.
