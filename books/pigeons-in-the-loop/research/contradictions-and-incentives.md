# Contradictions and Incentives Audit

## Cancellation date

**Conflict:** public accounts variously point to late 1943, January 1944, March 1944 or October 1944.

**Likely explanation:** different sources are labeling different events: demonstration, committee vote, appeal decision, residual project work or formal administrative termination.

**Rule:** do not choose one date merely because it appears most often. Recover NDRC minutes/letters and create an event-level sequence.

## Why Project Pigeon stopped

**Skinner-side incentive:** Skinner had reason to emphasize unjustified distrust and the difficulty of getting an unconventional behavioral system taken seriously; the episode supported his later account of behavioral engineering.

**Acquisition-side incentive:** committees had reason to describe cancellation in technical/opportunity-cost language rather than ridicule, especially when defending allocation of wartime research resources.

**Current posture:** interface/servo difficulty, competitive projects and social distrust can all coexist. Do not collapse to one motive without documentary allocation.

## `The pigeons worked`

**Conflict:** demonstrations and later recollections suggest impressive tracking; later summaries also describe variability and the eventual preference for electronics.

**Rule:** separate target-discrimination capability, disturbance robustness, transducer performance, closed-loop weapon guidance and mission reliability. Success at one layer cannot stand for all five.

## Three-pigeon redundancy

**Risk:** popular explanation treats majority voting as automatic reliability improvement.

**Counterpoint:** shared image, weather, optics, vibration and training regimes create correlated failure modes. Reconstruct actual combining logic before applying modern ensemble language.

## Bat as proof of rational cancellation

**Risk:** because Bat later reached combat, historians can project its mature 1945 status backward into earlier decisions.

**Rule:** compare programs at the actual decision dates. Eventual winner's success is not evidence that the earlier portfolio choice was obvious.

## Pigeon-as-computer metaphor

**Value:** helps modern readers understand that the bird performed sensing/classification/tracking functions later implemented electronically.

**Risk:** imports digital-computing concepts and suggests algorithmic transparency the animal did not possess.

**Rule:** use functional comparison, not identity.

## Animal welfare

**Fact:** the proposed terminal weapon would not return its pigeon guidance component.

**Risk:** either sentimentalizing the birds until engineering disappears or sanitizing the animal as a neutral component.

**Rule:** state the moral fact plainly and keep efficacy analysis separate.
