# Source Log

## Primary / first-person / artifact-level

### B. F. Skinner, `Pigeons in a Pelican`, *American Psychologist* 15(1), 1960, 28–37

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1037/h0045345
- B. F. Skinner Foundation project page and film pointer: https://www.bfskinner.org/project-pigeon/
- Use: Skinner's own declassified retrospective on Project Pigeon and ORCON; conceptual and apparatus history; his account of institutional reception.
- Boundary: retrospective advocate account published many years after the wartime work. Do not treat recollection as a complete contemporaneous test log.

### Smithsonian National Museum of American History, B. F. Skinner's Nose Cone of a Pigeon-Guided Missile

- https://americanhistory.si.edu/collections/object/nmah_690069
- Smithsonian portal mirror: https://www.si.edu/object/nmah_690069
- Object ID: nmah_690069; accession 1981.0997.
- Use: physical existence, maker attribution, three-compartment concept, dimensions/materials, provenance to Skinner gift.
- Boundary: museum description is an artifact summary, not a complete technical specification or proof of operational performance.

### U.S. Naval Research Laboratory, `Project ORCON: The Use of Pigeons to Guide Missiles`, *Report of NRL Progress*, August 1959, pp. 1–8

- Citation confirmed in later scholarly bibliographies; full report remains a high-priority recovery target.
- Use once recovered: postwar replication/test architecture, quantitative performance, stated reason for termination, instrumentation, bird counts and environmental tests.
- Boundary: do not quote or invent results before full text is in hand.

## Secondary material used only as leads

Later public histories frequently repeat a $25,000 General Mills/NDRC contract, 64-bird cohort, exceptional peck-rate claims, stress tests, long-term retention tests and competing cancellation dates. These are useful search leads, not frozen Class 1 claims. Recover the underlying contract, notebooks, films, NDRC summaries and ORCON report before using exact values as settled fact.
