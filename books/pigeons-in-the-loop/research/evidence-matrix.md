# Evidence Matrix

## Class 1 — Verified / documented

- B. F. Skinner pursued a wartime program known as Project Pigeon to use trained pigeons as part of a missile/bomb guidance system.
- A physical Skinner-associated three-pigeon nose-cone apparatus survives in the Smithsonian collection.
- The intended architecture used trained target-directed pecking as guidance information rather than using the birds merely as passengers or message carriers.
- Skinner published a declassified retrospective in 1960 describing Project Pigeon and the later Naval Research Laboratory continuation known as ORCON (`organic control`).
- ORCON existed as a postwar NRL research effort; a 1959 NRL progress report is cited in the scholarly record and is a priority for full-text recovery.
- Bat was a U.S. Navy radar-homing glide bomb developed through the National Bureau of Standards and partner laboratories and used in combat in 1945.
- Radar/electronic guidance therefore became a real contemporary competitor, not a hypothetical technology invented after Project Pigeon disappeared.
- James H. Capshew published a peer-reviewed history-of-technology reconstruction of Project Pigeon in 1993.

## Class 2 — Credible but incomplete

- The repeatedly reported ~$25,000 wartime development contract through General Mills/NDRC. Strongly repeated, but primary contract not yet recovered in this branch.
- Exact dates and sequence of late-1943/1944 funding votes, appeals, residual work and administrative termination.
- Exact flock size and how many birds reached each qualification stage.
- Claims that trained pigeons maintained useful tracking under loud noise, pressure variation, acceleration, temperature and other stressors; plausible and repeated, but protocol-level records remain to be audited.
- Exact peck rates and endurance records.
- Exact quantitative performance of ORCON and the extent to which the Navy replicated Skinner's results independently.
- Skinner's precise consulting role in ORCON.
- The relative weight of social distrust of pigeons versus servo/integration problems versus competing guidance programs in termination decisions.

## Class 3 — Disputed / not established

- That Project Pigeon was combat-ready when canceled.
- That the program was canceled solely because military officials laughed at the birds.
- That the pigeons were categorically unreliable.
- That the wartime system would have outperformed Bat or other electronic guidance in operational use.
- That three-pigeon voting eliminated meaningful biological failure modes.
- That every colorful stress-test anecdote circulated in later media is accurately specified.
- That one canonical cancellation date can be stated without defining whether it refers to vote, appeal, work stoppage or formal closure.
- That a particular number of birds, pecks or dollars should be treated as exact before the underlying record is recovered.

## Class 4 — Speculative synthesis / open conjecture

- Project Pigeon as a temporary `biological compute window` in which evolved visual processing may have been competitive before electronic seekers matured.
- The project as an early engineering case of accepting an opaque component because its input-output behavior can be characterized.
- The three-bird design as a precursor in spirit to ensemble redundancy, with the important warning that shared environmental failure creates correlated errors.
- The project's institutional difficulty as an `eccentricity tax`: unfamiliar components may require stronger demonstrations than familiar components with comparable uncertainty.
- ORCON's cancellation as evidence that electronics won not only on raw tracking performance but on manufacturing, storage, maintenance, documentation and an industrial learning curve.
- Project Pigeon as a useful calibration case for evaluating contemporary fringe technology: force strange claims rapidly toward measurable interfaces rather than accepting or rejecting them aesthetically.
