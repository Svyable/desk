## Boundary cases with primary enforcement records

### Theranos / Elizabeth Holmes

- U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission, **“Theranos, CEO Holmes, and Former President Balwani Charged With Massive Fraud,”** March 14, 2018.  
  https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2018-41
- SEC complaint, **Securities and Exchange Commission v. Elizabeth Holmes, et al.**, filed March 14, 2018.  
  https://www.sec.gov/files/litigation/complaints/2018/comp-pr2018-41-theranos-holmes.pdf

Use these sources for claims about what the SEC alleged or what Holmes agreed to in the SEC resolution. Do not generalize from press coverage when the primary record is available. Keep civil allegations, settlements, and later criminal proceedings legally distinct.

The SEC said Theranos and Holmes raised more than $700 million while making false or misleading statements about technology, business, and financial performance. The complaint is particularly useful for the book because it distinguishes public narrative from claims about what the proprietary analyzer could do at the time.

## Falsification note

The manuscript should change if the evidence shows that aggressive founder promises are usually well calibrated, that sophisticated investors reliably discount them without organizational side effects, or that successful companies do not exhibit meaningful internal costs from narrative lock-in.

The argument is not that founders should communicate timidly. It is that organizations need a mechanism for maintaining a private, evidence-sensitive model of reality even when public coordination requires a simpler and more confident story.
