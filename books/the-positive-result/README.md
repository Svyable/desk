# THE POSITIVE RESULT

## What Viking found, what Mars chemistry erased, and why science still cannot close the case

**Sven Hardy Benson**

Status: active draft

## Reader promise

In 1976 two laboratories landed on Mars carrying experiments built to detect living metabolism. One of them returned the kind of signal its designers had defined as positive. Science did not announce life. This book reconstructs why—not to reverse the verdict by rhetoric, but to ask a harder question: what happens when an experiment succeeds according to its protocol while the surrounding evidence makes the interpretation unacceptable?

The argument follows the evidence forward through Viking, Phoenix perchlorate, the later recovery of Martian organics, Curiosity methane, modern abiotic chemistry, planetary protection, and the design of the next decisive life-detection experiment.

## Governing rule

**A positive assay is not an organism. A chemical alternative is not a demonstrated cause. An unresolved experiment is not evidence that every explanation is equally good.**

## Evidence posture

Primary posture: NASA/NTRS mission records + peer-reviewed planetary chemistry/astrobiology. The Viking LR response is documented. Its interpretation as extant Martian biology is disputed. Perchlorate and related oxidizing chemistry materially strengthen abiotic explanations but do not retroactively constitute a direct measurement of the 1976 reaction mechanism. Later Martian organics and methane establish neither Viking biology nor extant life.
