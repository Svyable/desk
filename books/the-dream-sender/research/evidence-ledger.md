# Evidence ledger

## Class 1 — verified / published / documented

- Montague Ullman published a 1966 methodology and preliminary-findings paper on dreams and telepathy in *Archives of General Psychiatry*: https://doi.org/10.1001/archpsyc.1966.01730120045006
- Ullman and Stanley Krippner published a 1970 report of three studies in *American Journal of Psychiatry*: https://doi.org/10.1176/ajp.126.9.1282
- Edward Belvedere and David Foulkes published a 1971 failure to replicate a prior dream-telepathy result: https://doi.org/10.2466/pms.1971.33.3.783
- Irvin L. Child published a 1985 methodological critique of how mainstream psychology reviews had represented the Maimonides work: https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.40.11.1219
- Storm et al. published a 2017 meta-analysis of 50 dream-ESP studies reporting a positive aggregate effect: https://doi.org/10.11588/ijodr.2017.2.34888
- Matthew C. Howard published a 2018 reanalysis using inverse-variance weighting, reporting a much smaller overall effect and an association between effect size and sample size: https://doi.org/10.11588/ijodr.2018.2.52040
- Storm et al. published a 2019 response disputing parts of Howard's analysis: https://doi.org/10.11588/ijodr.2019.1.59265

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete

- Retrospective descriptions of individual Maimonides nights, participant selection, sender experience, laboratory culture, and unpublished procedural choices. These may be historically useful but require triangulation against contemporaneous records.
- Claims about the complete number of formal versus pilot sessions vary with inclusion rules and source. Use the original papers and a session-level reconstruction before publication.

## Class 3 — disputed / unconfirmed

- The inference that above-chance target matching demonstrates telepathy, clairvoyance, precognition, or another extrasensory channel.
- Claims that failed replications are explained by hostile experimenters, psi-missing, decline effects, or psychological conditions unless those moderators were prospectively specified and independently reproduced.
- Claims that mainstream neglect itself proves suppression or confirms the phenomenon.

## Class 4 — speculative synthesis

- Dream reports may be an unusually sensitive test bed for weak-information claims because they combine internally generated imagery with high-dimensional semantic scoring.
- If a genuine anomaly exists, its failure to become an engineering discipline after decades is itself a mechanistic constraint.
- A modern adversarial multicenter design could potentially resolve a much larger fraction of the dispute than another small, investigator-controlled study.

## Contradictions to preserve

- Positive Maimonides series coexist with a published early failure to replicate.
- Child's argument that critics misrepresented procedures can be correct without telepathy being real.
- A positive meta-analytic mean can coexist with a small-study gradient and a much smaller inverse-variance weighted estimate.
- Methodological improvement over time does not automatically imply declining or stable effect; analyses disagree on how to model that relation.

## Incentives and distortions

Believers benefit reputationally from a surviving anomaly; skeptics benefit from decisive debunking; journals historically favored interesting outcomes; small specialist literatures can develop citation loops; retrospective memoirs reward vivid nights over boring null sessions; and textbook simplification can harden an uncertain record into a verdict. None of these incentives establishes which interpretation is correct.
