# Back Matter

The source trail for this draft is maintained in `research/source-log.md` and the evidence classes in `research/evidence-ledger.md`.

Core chronology: 1950s — REM sleep becomes an experimental handle for dream collection. 1962 — Montague Ullman establishes the Maimonides Dream Laboratory in Brooklyn. 1964 onward — formal dream-telepathy work develops with Stanley Krippner and later Charles Honorton. 1966 — Ullman publishes methodology and preliminary findings in *Archives of General Psychiatry*. 1970 — Ullman and Krippner publish three studies in *American Journal of Psychiatry*. 1971 — Edward Belvedere and David Foulkes publish a failure to replicate. 1985 — Irvin L. Child publishes a methodological critique of how psychological reviews had represented the dream-ESP work. 2017 — Storm and colleagues publish a meta-analysis covering dream-ESP studies from 1966–2016. 2018 — Matthew Howard publishes an inverse-variance weighted reanalysis with a substantially smaller estimated effect and a small-study concern. 2019 — Storm and colleagues reply.

The book’s falsification standard is intentionally stronger than “another significant small study.” A decisive test requires preregistration or registered-report review, complete session reporting, secure target handling, blind transcript production, independent judging, multicenter replication, a sample powered for small effects, and prospectively specified outcomes. A negative result with tight enough uncertainty to exclude the effect sizes claimed by the modern literature would substantially weaken the experimental dream-ESP tradition. A positive result should first be called a protocol-level anomaly, not telepathy, and then subjected to mechanism-discriminating replications.

The unresolved research backlog includes a complete session-level reconstruction of the Maimonides program; full annotation of every formal and pilot series; original target pools and judging sheets where preserved; archival correspondence showing planned versus published sessions; every independent replication and null; modern dream-content reliability work; target-library norming; inter-rater reliability; publication-bias simulations; hierarchical reanalysis clustered by investigator and laboratory; and a preregistered multicenter design with an explicit error budget.

No claim in this manuscript establishes telepathy, clairvoyance, precognition, nonlocal consciousness, or any other paranormal mechanism. The documented anomaly is narrower: some published experiments and some pooled analyses report above-chance correspondence under their stated methods. The scientific question is whether that correspondence survives a modern apparatus designed to make ordinary information pathways and analytic flexibility too small to carry it.
