# Back Matter

## Evidence note

This manuscript uses four evidence classes. Class 1 is verified, declassified, official, or directly documented scientific observation. Class 2 is credible but incomplete evidence whose interpretation remains open. Class 3 is disputed reporting, exploratory statistical association, or a claim lacking independent confirmation. Class 4 is speculative synthesis used to generate tests rather than establish facts.

The book deliberately treats `unexplained` as a temporary classification. It never promotes a VASCO transient, an infrared-excess candidate, a UAP report, or a statistical association into proof of extraterrestrial technology.

## Chronology

- **1949–1958** — POSS-I photographs much of the northern sky on red- and blue-sensitive plates.
- **April 12, 1950** — the plate later analyzed for nine simultaneous apparent transients is exposed.
- **July 19, 1952** — the red/blue exposure pair later analyzed as the bright triple transient is taken.
- **October 4, 1957** — Sputnik 1 begins the artificial-satellite era.
- **1980s–2000s** — Palomar and UK Schmidt plate material is digitized through the DSS effort.
- **2009** — N6946-BH1 undergoes its weak outburst and later optical disappearance.
- **2016** — Villarroel, Imaz and Bergstedt publish the pilot disappearing-star SETI search.
- **2017** — Adams et al. publish Hubble confirmation that N6946-BH1 is dramatically fainter, consistent with a failed-supernova interpretation.
- **2018** — NASA convenes its technosignatures workshop in Houston.
- **2020** — VASCO's first large USNO-to-Pan-STARRS search reports roughly one hundred one-epoch red point sources after large-scale filtering and visual inspection.
- **2021** — the nine-transient April 1950 event is published in Scientific Reports.
- **2024** — the 1952 bright triple transient and Project Hephaistos seven Dyson-sphere-model-compatible candidates are published in MNRAS.
- **October 20, 2025** — Bruehl and Villarroel publish exploratory associations between POSS-I transients, nuclear-test timing and UAP-report counts in Scientific Reports.
- **February 24–25, 2026** — Rubin Observatory issues its first scientific alert stream, roughly 800,000 alerts on the first night, with a system designed to scale toward millions nightly.

## What would defeat the book's thesis

The book's core thesis is methodological rather than extraterrestrial: old and modern survey comparisons can produce scientifically useful searches for extreme disappearances, but extraordinary interpretation becomes credible only after instrument, catalogue, kinematic, astrophysical and statistical failure modes are aggressively eliminated.

That thesis would need revision if high-confidence disappearing-source searches proved impossible to calibrate across survey generations; if old plate morphology could not be distinguished from defects even probabilistically; or if candidate attrition failed to respond to better astrometry and imaging in the expected ways.

The more exotic synthesis would be defeated more simply: every surviving candidate could acquire a conventional explanation. That outcome would still leave useful event-rate limits and transient astrophysics.
