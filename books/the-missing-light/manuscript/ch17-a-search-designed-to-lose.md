# A Search Designed to Lose

A credible technosignature search should be designed to embarrass its favorite hypothesis.

That principle sounds hostile only if the purpose of the experiment is advocacy.

For disappearing stars, the favored exotic hypothesis might be stellar-scale engineering. The search should therefore begin by constructing the strongest possible machinery for proving that candidates are not engineered.

Start with persistence. Require at least two independent historical detections for the highest-confidence vanishing-star class. Single-plate detections remain a separate fast-transient sample.

Then motion. Propagate Gaia astrometry backward and forward where available. Search infrared catalogues for high-proper-motion counterparts. Fit trajectories rather than nearest-neighbor matches.

Then depth. Establish local limiting magnitude for every nondetection rather than relying on nominal survey depth. Mask chip gaps, bright-star halos and bad regions.

Then wavelength. Search optical, near-infrared, mid-infrared, ultraviolet, radio and X-ray archives where relevant. A missing optical star with strong infrared emission is not missing in the thermodynamic sense.

Then astrophysics. Compare candidates with variable-star classes, flare stars, supernovae, tidal disruption events, changing-look nuclei, stellar mergers and obscuration events. Use spectroscopy whenever a counterpart exists.

Then instrument forensics. Reinspect original plates. Compare independent scans. inspect plate defects and nearby stellar PSFs. Recover emulsion and development metadata where possible.

Then statistics. Freeze candidate criteria before testing correlations with external events. Use held-out plates. Correct for multiple testing. Publish nulls and closure reasons.

Finally, replication. A candidate that exists only in one processing pipeline should die there.

This is not a formal seven-step framework intended for a slide. It is simply the order in which expensive claims should encounter cheaper explanations.

The search also needs explicit defeat conditions for the technosignature interpretation. If every high-confidence historical source acquires a natural counterpart under deeper imaging, the event-rate limit becomes the result. If transient clusters track plate batches or scanners, the archive is explaining itself. If the nuclear-test association fails in independent observatory material, it should be retired rather than endlessly reparameterized. If Hephaistos-like infrared candidates resolve into dusty astrophysical systems, that narrows the Dyson-sphere parameter space.

A field earns credibility by killing its own best stories.

There is a second reason to design for loss. Technosignature science sits near a cultural ecosystem that rewards ambiguity. Any unresolved dot can accumulate narratives faster than data. Public candidate lists may be useful for reproducibility but can also turn coordinates into mythology.

Researchers should therefore publish closure aggressively. Candidate recovered as high-proper-motion M dwarf. Candidate traced to plate flaw. Candidate identified as background galaxy. Candidate remains unresolved pending spectrum.

The unresolved category must be allowed to shrink.

None of this requires hostility to the possibility of nonhuman technology. On the contrary, the possibility is too important for weak evidence.

If a source survives all of these gates, the next move should not be declaration. It should be more measurement.

The strongest technosignature would be one that becomes more specific as instruments improve.
