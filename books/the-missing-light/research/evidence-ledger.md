# Evidence Ledger

## Class 1 — Verified / documented

- POSS-I created red- and blue-sensitive photographic sky plates during the 1950s; the plate archive was later digitized and remains accessible through DSS/MAST.
- VASCO is a published astronomical research program using historical-modern survey comparisons to search for vanishing and appearing sources and possible technosignatures.
- The 2020 VASCO paper compared ~600 million USNO-B1.0 objects with Pan-STARRS, found ~150,000 preliminary unmatched sources, visually inspected a subset of ~24,000, and reported ~100 one-epoch red point-source candidates.
- Nine apparent point sources were reported on an April 12, 1950 Palomar plate and absent on a subsequent exposure within roughly an hour.
- Three ~16th-magnitude point-like sources on a July 19, 1952 Palomar red exposure were absent on the following blue exposure and remained absent in much deeper 2023 GTC imaging, as reported in MNRAS 2024.
- N6946-BH1 underwent a weak 2009 outburst and later became dramatically fainter in optical imaging; published multiwavelength analysis identifies it as a strong failed-supernova candidate.
- NASA convened a dedicated technosignatures workshop in 2018.
- Project Hephaistos published seven M-dwarf infrared-excess candidates compatible with partial Dyson-sphere models while explicitly warning that follow-up is required and technological origin is premature.
- Rubin Observatory issued roughly 800,000 scientific alerts on February 24, 2026, with expected mature alert rates in the millions per night.

## Class 2 — Credible but incomplete

- Some VASCO plate transients may represent genuine astronomical fast-transient phenomena rather than plate artifacts; individual physical identifications remain incomplete.
- N6946-BH1 is consistent with failed supernova/direct black-hole formation, but long-term interpretation remains model-dependent and benefits from continued infrared/X-ray follow-up.
- The 1952 triple transient's morphology, timing and modern nondetections make simple moving-object and ordinary-variable explanations difficult, but not impossible.
- Pre-Sputnik plates reduce one important contaminant class—ordinary artificial satellite glints—without removing aircraft, balloons, rockets, meteors or plate defects.

## Class 3 — Disputed / exploratory / unconfirmed

- The 2025 reported temporal associations between POSS-I transient detections and nuclear-test dates require independent replication and stronger control of observational and statistical confounds before causal interpretation.
- The reported association between transient counts and historical UAP-report counts does not establish that the two datasets share a physical cause.
- Claims that the 1950 or 1952 transient clusters represent nonhuman technology are unsupported by independent confirming evidence.
- Claims that a missing modern counterpart is equivalent to a physically vanished star are false without motion-aware, depth-aware, multiwavelength validation.

## Class 4 — Speculative synthesis / open conjecture

- A mature technosignature program could combine persistent-then-vanished stellar sources with infrared energy accounting to search for large-scale engineering while producing useful natural transient science regardless of outcome.
- Independent pre-Sputnik plate archives could serve as a quasi-control epoch for satellite-glint contamination and test whether VASCO-like transient clusters recur across observatories.
- A replicated correlation between plate transient rates and nuclear tests could reveal either an unexpected physical contamination mechanism or another shared cause before any aerospace interpretation is entertained.
- Future Rubin-era multi-site, high-cadence searches could define and prospectively test the optical morphology of historical transient clusters.
