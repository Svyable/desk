# Source Log — The Missing Light

2. Villarroel et al. (2020), **The Vanishing and Appearing Sources during a Century of Observations Project. I. USNO Objects Missing in Modern Sky Surveys and Follow-up Observations of a Missing Star**, *Astronomical Journal* 159:8. DOI 10.3847/1538-3881/ab570f. https://www.iac.es/en/science-and-technology/publications/vanishing-and-appearing-sources-during-century-observations-project-i-usno-objects

3. Villarroel et al. (2021), **Exploring nine simultaneously occurring transients on April 12th 1950**, *Scientific Reports* 11:12794. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-021-92162-7

4. Solano et al. (2024), **A bright triple transient that vanished within 50 min**, *Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society* 527:6312–6320. https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/527/3/6312/7457759

5. Adams et al. (2017), **The search for failed supernovae with the Large Binocular Telescope: confirmation of a disappearing star**, *MNRAS* 468:4968–4981. https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/468/4/4968/3098190

6. NASA Hubble, **Collapsing Star Gives Birth to a Black Hole**, May 25, 2017; useful official summary and imagery for N6946-BH1. https://science.nasa.gov/missions/hubble/collapsing-star-gives-birth-to-a-black-hole/

7. STScI/MAST, **Digitized Sky Survey / Photographic Sky Surveys** documentation. https://archive.stsci.edu/missions-and-data/dss--gsc and https://outerspace.stsci.edu/spaces/MASTDATA/pages/176435492/Photographic%2BSky%2BSurveys

8. NASA Astrobiology, **NASA Technosignatures Workshop**, September 26–28, 2018. https://astrobiology.nasa.gov/events/nasa-technosignatures-workshop/

9. NASA Technosignatures Workshop Participants (2018), **NASA and the Search for Technosignatures: A Report from the NASA Technosignatures Workshop**. arXiv:1812.08681. https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.08681

10. Suazo et al. (2024), **Project Hephaistos II. Dyson sphere candidates from Gaia DR3, 2MASS, and WISE**, *MNRAS* 531:695–707. DOI 10.1093/mnras/stae1186. https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/531/1/695/7665761

11. NSF, **NSF-DOE Vera C. Rubin Observatory launches real-time discovery machine for monitoring the night sky**, February 25, 2026. Reports ~800,000 first-night alerts and expected scaling to ~7 million/night. https://www.nsf.gov/news/nsf-doe-vera-c-rubin-observatory-launches-real-time

12. Bruehl & Villarroel (2025), **Transients in the Palomar Observatory Sky Survey (POSS-I) may be associated with nuclear testing and reports of unidentified anomalous phenomena**, *Scientific Reports* 15:34125, October 20, 2025. Reports a 45% higher probability of at least one transient inside a nuclear-test ±1-day window (RR 1.45, 95% CI 1.10–1.90, p=.008) plus small UAP-report associations. The authors explicitly do not establish causation. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-21620-3

13. Watters, Dominé, Little, Pratt & Knuth (2026), **Critical Evaluation of Studies Alleging Evidence for Technosignatures in the POSS1-E Photographic Plates**, arXiv:2601.21946, January 29, 2026. Major counteranalysis: argues the nuclear-test correlation becomes insignificant after normalization by observation days, does not recover the claimed Earth-shadow deficit in the most vetted dataset, and identifies catalogue/plate-systematics concerns. Preprint, not treated as peer-reviewed resolution. https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.21946

14. Doherty (2026), **Independent Replication of Nuclear Test-Transient Correlations and Earth Shadow Deficit in POSS-I Photographic Plates**, arXiv:2604.00056, March 31, 2026. Reanalyzes the released data; reports reproduction of the nuclear-window association and persistence under negative-binomial weather/lunar controls plus permutation testing. Independent analyst but not an independent plate archive; preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00056

15. Cann (2026), **Geomagnetic storm suppression of photographic plate transient detections in the POSS-I archive: an independent physical variable strengthening the nuclear test correlation**, arXiv:2604.04950, April 2, 2026. Reports persistence of the nuclear-window association after Kp and lunar controls and a separate transient/Kp relationship. Preprint; uses the same underlying archive/catalogue family and therefore does not settle candidate-validity objections. https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04950

16. Bruehl, Doherty, Streblyanska & Villarroel (2026), **Use of machine learning to enhance detection of transient astronomical phenomena in historical observatory images**, *Scientific Reports*, published August 10, 2026. Classifier trained on 250 expert-reviewed image pairs; out-of-fold AUC 0.81, sensitivity/specificity 0.71; reports the nuclear-window elevation surviving artifact-probability filtering. This improves artifact control but does not constitute independent raw-archive replication. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-66148-2

17. Nevada National Security Site, **Nuclear Testing Archive**. Authoritative archive containing more than 386,000 historical documents on U.S. nuclear testing, fallout detection/measurement, and related technical studies; a primary-document route for test/fallout reconstruction rather than reliance on secondary date lists. https://nnss.gov/nuclear-testing-archive/

18. Philippe et al. (2026), **Reconstruction of fallout deposition from U.S. atmospheric nuclear tests conducted in New Mexico and Nevada**, *Scientific Reports* 16:23550, August 24, 2026. Modern fallout-transport/deposition reconstruction useful for testing whether a proposed photographic-contamination mechanism predicts geography and timing rather than merely calendar proximity. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-62206-x

## Context and follow-up priorities

- Original POSS-I observing logs and plate metadata from Caltech/Palomar archives: https://galex.caltech.edu/palomar/media/archival.html
- Gaia DR3 astrometric documentation for historical position propagation.
- Pan-STARRS DR1 survey papers for exact completeness, bandpasses and depth.
- WISE confusion and artifact documentation for Dyson-sphere candidate vetting.
- LBT failed-supernova survey continuation papers after Adams et al. 2017.
- Independent plate archives: Harvard DASCH, Sonneberg, UK Schmidt, Bamberg and other digitized historical collections.
- Historical satellite, balloon, sounding-rocket and aircraft test records for candidate dates before Sputnik.
- Reconstruct the nuclear-test analysis against actual Palomar observing opportunities rather than calendar days, preserving the Watters critique as a live alternative model.
- Reproduce fallout hypotheses plate by plate using official test times/locations, meteorology, exposure/development metadata, emulsion batches and matched controls; require transport physics to predict delay and geography.
- Replicate the nuclear-window result on independently scanned historical plates with the candidate pipeline, test windows and exclusions frozen before dates are revealed.
- Original UAP source catalogue and inclusion/exclusion rules used by Bruehl & Villarroel, with duplicate-report audit.
