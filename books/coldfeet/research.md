# COLDFEET research ledger

## Working thesis

Project COLDFEET is a compact documented case of **intelligence after abandonment**: a secret or strategically relevant technical system can become unusually legible when evacuation separates its operators from its material traces. The thesis must be tested against what NP-8 actually yielded, not inferred from the drama of the collection method.

## Evidence classes

### 1. Verified / declassified / documented

- CIA publicly documents Project COLDFEET as a 1962 operation against abandoned Soviet drift station NP-8.
- CIA identifies Maj. James F. Smith, USAF, and Lt. (jg) Leonard A. LeSchack, USNR, as the collectors inserted on 28 May 1962.
- CIA identifies Intermountain Aviation and pilots Connie Seigrist and Douglas Price in the operation.
- CIA documents use of a modified B-17 and Fulton surface-to-air recovery system.
- CIA says the men photographed the station and recovered more than 150 pounds of documents, equipment samples/material.
- William M. Leary's CIA *Studies in Intelligence* history cites John Cadwalader, “Operation Coldfeet: An Investigation of the Abandoned Soviet Arctic Drift Station NP 8,” *ONI Review* 17 (August 1962), 344–55, as a declassified after-action source.

### 2. Credible but incomplete

- CIA's retrospective statement that the take provided valuable intelligence on Soviet advanced acoustic detection of under-ice U.S. submarines and Arctic ASW. Treat as credible institutional reporting but reconstruct the exact analytical findings from Cadwalader/ONR/ONI material before making technical claims.
- Participant recollections about the pickup, training, equipment and intelligence value. Useful for chronology and physical experience; memory is not a substitute for contemporary records.
- Published historical accounts that characterize NP-8 as more modern or valuable than the initially considered NP-9. Verify against planning records.

### 3. Disputed / not yet independently confirmed

- Any precise claim that COLDFEET caused a specific U.S. ASW capability, procurement decision or program change.
- Any detailed specification of Soviet acoustic equipment not tied to the declassified technical report or recovered-document analysis.
- Any assertion that Soviet abandonment itself represented a security failure by Soviet standards; emergency evacuation may have been rational even if it created exploitation opportunity.
- Any claim that COLDFEET discovered a capability unknown to U.S. intelligence rather than refining estimates of known Soviet work.

### 4. Speculative synthesis / open conjecture

- “Abandonment” can be treated as a distinct intelligence state between defended operation and archaeological ruin.
- Security models systematically underweight transition states: evacuation, crash, disposal, maintenance, capture, decommissioning and salvage.
- The most valuable intelligence at NP-8 may have been relational—how ordinary instruments, logs and practices fit together—rather than a single exotic device. This remains synthesis until the take is reconstructed.

## Contradictions to track

- CIA popular-history pages emphasize success and valuable intelligence; determine whether contemporary after-action assessments are equally strong.
- Public accounts differ on whether the mission lasted roughly four days on the ice or is described institutionally as a seven-day mission; distinguish total mission span, planned ground stay and actual ground stay.
- Some summaries blur NP-9 and NP-8. Preserve target chronology.
- Some secondary accounts say recovery occurred June 1; CIA's current account says pickups began after a fog delay and identifies June 2 in its broader summary. Resolve from flight logs/after-action report.

## Incentive audit

CIA has an institutional incentive to showcase ingenuity and successful clandestine tradecraft. Participants have understandable incentives to remember a dangerous mission as consequential. Later popular accounts privilege the Skyhook because it is visual. Soviet/Russian retrospective sources may have incentives to minimize intelligence loss or emphasize ordinary scientific character. None of these incentives establish falsity; they determine what needs independent corroboration.

## Research queue

1. Obtain/page-index Cadwalader ONI report and separate recovered-object inventory from later summary.
2. Reconstruct NP-8/NP-9 Soviet station histories from Russian/Soviet scientific sources rather than U.S. intelligence histories alone.
3. Build exact planning chronology: 1961 sighting → NP-9 concept → funding/approval delay → NP-8 abandonment → CIA takeover → May 1962 execution.
4. Establish Smith and LeSchack's technical qualifications without embellishing biography.
5. Reconstruct Fulton development from ONR technical sources; distinguish tested capability from later cinematic mythology.
6. Determine what was in the 150-pound take and what analysts concluded item by item where records permit.
7. Find contemporaneous U.S. Arctic acoustic/ASW programs to establish baseline knowledge before COLDFEET.
8. Look for Soviet accounts of NP-8 evacuation and what equipment was deliberately removed versus abandoned.
9. Separate collection success from intelligence success. The first is documented. The second requires a before/after analytical ledger.
10. Build comparator appendix: abandoned/captured technical sites versus deliberate foreign-materiel recovery, without turning the book into another AZORIAN manuscript.

## Defeat condition

The book's distinct thesis weakens substantially if the primary record shows that NP-8's recovered material contributed little new knowledge and that the operation's enduring significance was principally demonstration of Fulton recovery. If so, the manuscript should become a narrower history of technological improvisation rather than inflate abandonment into a general intelligence principle.
