# Back Matter

## A note on sources

*Hallucinations: How AI Leads Us Astray* is written about a fast-moving technology and an even faster-moving set of products. Concrete claims in the manuscript are anchored, where possible, to primary sources, official records, peer-reviewed research, standards documents, and first-party technical publications. The research directory for this book keeps the source trail separate from the reading experience so the prose can remain prose.

Dates matter. Model behavior changes. Product interfaces change. Benchmarks change. Policies change. A benchmark score reported for a model in one configuration should not be treated as a permanent property of every later model bearing a related name. A court decision establishes what that court decided in that case; it does not automatically establish a universal rule in every jurisdiction. A study establishes what its design supports; it should not be stretched into a law of human behavior.

The manuscript uses *hallucination* because it is the public term, while noting the terminological dispute. NIST's Generative AI Profile uses *confabulation* for confidently presented false or internally inconsistent generated content. The book's use of *shared hallucination* is broader and explicitly social: an unsupported representation gains stability through human-machine interaction, interface authority, repetition, memory, or institutional action. It is not a clinical diagnosis and should not be read as one.

## The practical doctrine

The argument of the book can be carried into ordinary work without treating every sentence as a forensic exhibit.

Find the hinge. Identify the few claims on which the consequence actually turns. Ask what kind of act produced each one: retrieval, measurement, calculation, inference, user statement, model generation, or memory. Keep the evidence close to the hinge. Make important uncertainty survive summarization. Let a missing fact remain missing when invention would be worse. Separate proposal from authorization. Prefer reversible actions when evidence is incomplete. Make corrections travel at least as far as the original error did.

Above all, do not treat agreement among generated artifacts as independent confirmation unless genuinely independent evidence entered the process.

## Research maintenance

The source ledger records publication and access dates, the claim supported, and limits on interpretation. Chapter briefs include a falsification note: evidence that would weaken the chapter's central claim or force the argument to narrow. That practice matters in a book about synthetic certainty. A thesis should expose how it could be wrong.

Future revisions should update time-sensitive sources rather than silently treating a 2026 snapshot as permanent. Where a later primary source contradicts an earlier account, the later edition should preserve enough of the change to show that the record evolved.

Sven Hardy Benson writes about intelligence, institutions, technology, incentives, and the systems beneath familiar outcomes. His work asks what changes when a tool becomes infrastructure and when an apparently individual decision turns out to be a property of a larger system.
