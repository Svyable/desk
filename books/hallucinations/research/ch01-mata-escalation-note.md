# Chapter 1 research note — *Mata v. Avianca* as an escalation failure

Accessed 2026-09-07.

## Editorial purpose

Chapter 1 already uses *Mata v. Avianca* well as an example of fabricated professional form. The primary sanctions order supports an even sharper version of the book's systems argument: the consequential failure was not simply that ChatGPT invented cases. The failure escalated after the invented authorities encountered ordinary verification signals and human actors had multiple chances to stop the chain.

That makes *Mata* more useful as a case about **failed interruption** than as a morality tale about a lawyer naively asking a chatbot a question.

## Primary record

The controlling source is Judge P. Kevin Castel's June 22, 2023 sanctions opinion and order in *Mata v. Avianca, Inc.*, No. 22-cv-1461 (PKC), 678 F. Supp. 3d 443 (S.D.N.Y. 2023).

Primary-order text: https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/new-york/nysdce/1%3A2022cv01461/575368/54/

Docket mirror: https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/63107798/mata-v-avianca-inc/

### Sequence worth preserving

1. The March 1, 2023 opposition brief cited authorities later shown to be nonexistent or materially fabricated.
2. Avianca's lawyers told the court they could not locate several cited cases.
3. The court then ordered plaintiff's counsel to provide copies of the decisions.
4. By Schwartz's later account, the court's order caused him to have doubts about at least *Varghese*. He said he asked ChatGPT whether the case was real despite already highly suspecting that it was not; ChatGPT again assured him that it existed and was available on Westlaw and LexisNexis.
5. On April 25, counsel nevertheless filed an affidavit attaching purported copies or excerpts of the cases. The sanctions order found that LoDuca signed the affidavit without meaningful inquiry into whether its statements were true and that Schwartz had prepared the affidavit and exhibits.
6. The order's sanctions analysis focused heavily on conduct after warning signs appeared. The court expressly distinguished the use of a technological tool from the obligation to verify a filing and found bad faith in the response to the emerging evidence that the cited authorities were false.

The vivid point is not merely that the system hallucinated twice. It is that **the second hallucination was treated as a check on the first after an independent human source—the opposing party and then the court—had already supplied contrary evidence.**

## Strongest counterargument / limiting evidence

Do not turn *Mata* into evidence that generative AI use by lawyers is itself sanctionable or uniquely irresponsible. The order does not establish that proposition. It is a single incident, and Judge Castel explicitly treated technological assistance as compatible with competent lawyering when lawyers fulfill their Rule 11 duties.

Nor should the chapter imply that the attorneys were sanctioned simply for believing the first generated answer. The stronger and more accurate account is that responsibility intensified as contrary evidence accumulated and the lawyers failed to perform independent verification before submitting or reaffirming claims to the court.

That distinction matters for the book's larger thesis. A hallucination becomes institutionally dangerous not at the instant of generation but when ordinary stopping points—opposing evidence, source checks, provenance requirements, accountable sign-off—fail to interrupt it.

## Suggested manuscript use

A later prose pass could deepen the existing paragraph beginning "In the *Mata* episode, one of the most revealing details..." with one additional beat: by the time Schwartz asked ChatGPT whether *Varghese* was real, the court's request for the actual opinions had already created a reason to doubt it. The model's reassurance therefore did not merely repeat an error; it displaced stronger external evidence.

That lets the chapter earn its later principle—"repetition is not verification when the repetitions share a source"—from a concrete sequence rather than from abstraction alone.
