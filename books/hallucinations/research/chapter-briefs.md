# Chapter briefs and falsification notes

Each chapter has a purpose, evidence posture, and a condition that would force the argument to narrow. These notes are editorial controls, not claims that every chapter has an independent empirical literature.

## Chapter 1 — The Sentence That Never Happened
**Purpose:** Open with *Mata v. Avianca* and show why fabricated professional form is more dangerous than obvious nonsense.  
**Falsification note:** Narrow if the primary order does not support the described sequence or later records materially alter the account.

## Chapter 2 — Plausibility Is Not Truth
**Purpose:** Separate fluency, factuality, calibration, retrieval, and verification without reducing modern LLMs to old autocomplete.  
**Falsification note:** Narrow incentive claims if later architectures or evaluations make the described guessing pressure irrelevant.

## Chapter 3 — The Guessing Machine
**Purpose:** Show why evaluations and products can reward answers over abstention, and why missingness can be information.  
**Falsification note:** Revise if aggressive answering consistently dominates uncertainty-preserving systems on both usefulness and costly-error measures.

## Chapter 4 — The Human Ear for Confidence
**Purpose:** Explain why fluent, coherent, professional language can receive credibility before explicit checking.  
**Falsification note:** Do not claim a universal fluency effect; incorporate replication limits and AI-specific evidence as it matures.

## Chapter 5 — The Reward for Being Wrong
**Purpose:** Connect model incentives to organizational metrics and introduce epistemic externalities.  
**Falsification note:** Narrow if organizations reliably internalize downstream correction cost without structural controls.

## Chapter 6 — The User in the Model
**Purpose:** Show how user belief and prompt framing change the epistemic environment; distinguish preference alignment from factual alignment.  
**Falsification note:** Tie model-specific claims to named benchmarks and dates; do not generalize to every model or prompt.

## Chapter 7 — The Agreement Machine
**Purpose:** Explain why machine agreement is not independent evidence when agents or users share information lineage.  
**Falsification note:** Narrow if same-model multi-agent systems are shown to provide independence comparable to genuinely independent evidence sources.

## Chapter 8 — Shared Hallucination
**Purpose:** State the central synthesis: unsupported representations can gain stability through human-machine interaction, memory, interfaces, workflow, and action.  
**Falsification note:** Narrow if downstream design and interaction contribute little to persistence or consequence relative to model-level factuality alone.

## Chapter 9 — The Citation Laundromat
**Purpose:** Show how retrieval, summarization, synthesis, and citation placement can strip uncertainty from a claim.  
**Falsification note:** Do not imply every cited AI answer launders provenance; the claim concerns systems that fail to preserve source-to-claim support.

## Chapter 10 — The Memory We Rewrite Together
**Purpose:** Treat persistent AI as a narrator of personal and institutional history; emphasize source status, modal drift, and editable memory.  
**Falsification note:** Do not claim AI summaries necessarily alter autobiographical memory without direct evidence.

## Chapter 11 — The Interface That Knows
**Purpose:** Show how placement, branding, voice, and process verbs lend authority.  
**Falsification note:** Do not overstate *Moffatt v. Air Canada* as a universal rule for AI liability.

## Chapter 12 — When Workflows Believe
**Purpose:** Explain the transition from generated language to structured state; introduce epistemic least privilege and fail-closed evidence behavior.  
**Falsification note:** Revise controls if field evidence shows a proposed gate increases total harm through delay or overload.

## Chapter 13 — The Courtroom Test
**Purpose:** Use law as a mature provenance system and show that new tools do not erase duties of competence, candor, supervision, and verification.  
**Falsification note:** Distinguish ABA guidance from binding jurisdiction-specific law.

## Chapter 14 — The Clinic Test
**Purpose:** Show why medicine cannot solve uncertainty through abstention alone; preserve reported, observed, measured, inferred, and confirmed states.  
**Falsification note:** Do not infer patient-outcome harm directly from task-level hallucination studies.

## Chapter 15 — Search Becomes an Answer
**Purpose:** Explain the shift from source ranking to synthesis and treat hidden query generation and source selection as editorial acts.  
**Falsification note:** Revise if answer systems reliably preserve disagreement and claim-level provenance in ordinary use.

## Chapter 16 — The Agent Chain
**Purpose:** Show how error changes when systems can act, compress context, and hand intermediate representations to other agents.  
**Falsification note:** Distinguish hypothetical failure chains from documented incidents unless incident-level evidence exists.

## Chapter 17 — Correction Is Harder Than Creation
**Purpose:** Introduce correction debt and explain why generation can be cheap while tracing and repair remain expensive.  
**Falsification note:** Narrow if lineage and automated correction make marginal repair cost comparable to generation in realistic deployments.

## Chapter 18 — The Price of Epistemic Convenience
**Purpose:** Ask which friction protected discovery or verification before AI removed it; introduce hinge verification.  
**Falsification note:** Do not treat friction as inherently good; incorporate evidence of safe friction removal.

## Chapter 19 — The Unanswerable Question
**Purpose:** Define hallucinated decidability: a settled answer to a structurally open, future-dependent, value-laden, inaccessible, or ambiguous question.  
**Falsification note:** Avoid calling questions permanently unknowable when they are merely unanswered today.

## Chapter 20 — The Evidence Supply Chain
**Purpose:** Move from grounding to lineage and separate authority, freshness, independence, relevance, completeness, and modality.  
**Falsification note:** Do not present provenance standards as universal truth-verification protocols.

## Chapter 21 — Designing for Doubt
**Purpose:** Turn the diagnosis into product principles: source proximity, alternatives, explicit missingness, reversible autonomy, lineage, time, and correction.  
**Falsification note:** Treat each design principle as a testable hypothesis, not a universal mandate.

## Chapter 22 — The Right to Uncertainty
**Purpose:** Argue that consequential inference about people should remain contestable and proportionate to evidence.  
**Falsification note:** Keep the proposed right normative unless a jurisdiction explicitly establishes such a legal right.

## Chapter 23 — The Human Job
**Purpose:** Replace token-by-token review with hinge detection, value choice, authorization, apprenticeship, evidence stewardship, and ownership of consequence.  
**Falsification note:** Keep apprenticeship-debt claims provisional and incorporate evidence that AI can improve skill acquisition.

## Chapter 24 — The Synthetic Witness
**Purpose:** Show how generated images, audio, video, text, and process claims can imitate artifacts whose authority historically came from contact with events. Separate provenance from truth.  
**Evidence anchors:** C2PA's open standard and published provenance principles.  
**Falsification note:** Narrow if provenance signals prove unusable in practice or if the chapter overreads provenance as proof of factual truth.

## Chapter 25 — The Database That Learned the Rumor
**Purpose:** Show how a generated error can become durable data and later appear grounded to downstream systems; distinguish technical model collapse from the book's organizational analogy.  
**Evidence anchors:** Shumailov et al., *Nature* 2024, on recursive training with model-generated data and preservation of original data.  
**Falsification note:** Do not claim synthetic data inevitably causes collapse; the result depends on training regime and retained original data.

## Chapter 26 — When the Model Is Right
**Purpose:** Supply the book's strongest counterweight: humans can distrust algorithms too much after salient errors, and human review can itself reduce accuracy. The goal is calibration, not skepticism.  
**Evidence anchors:** Renier, Schmid Mast & Bekbergenova 2021; classic human-automation research.  
**Falsification note:** Do not generalize one vignette study into a universal algorithm-aversion law; narrow claims by task and deployment.

## Chapter 27 — The Verification Dividend
**Purpose:** Recast verification as reusable productivity infrastructure rather than repeated manual overhead.  
**Evidence anchors:** legal citation verification, deterministic calculation, provenance, observability, and high-reliability workflow patterns.  
**Falsification note:** Narrow if field evidence shows verification infrastructure consistently costs more than the correction and trust benefits it creates.

## Chapter 28 — The Confidence Market
**Purpose:** Locate the commercial and organizational incentives that reward decisiveness, smoothness, and early productivity while correction costs arrive later.  
**Evidence anchors:** human-automation literature, algorithm-aversion research, and organizational measurement principles.  
**Falsification note:** Revise if markets reliably reward uncertainty-preserving systems without regulatory, contractual, reputational, procurement, or institutional pressure.

## Chapter 29 — The Last Hallucination
**Purpose:** Synthesize the book: hallucination is a property of an epistemic architecture, not the speaker alone. End with calibration and methods rather than fatalism.  
**Falsification note:** Avoid claiming systems design can eliminate model-level hallucination or all downstream misinformation; the conclusion remains conditional on the evidence above.
