## Thesis

The popular story says AI hallucination is a defect inside a model: a machine makes something up, a human catches it, engineers reduce the error rate, and progress resumes. This book argues that the consequential failure is usually larger. A falsehood becomes dangerous when a person accepts it, an interface strips away uncertainty, a summary drops provenance, a workflow repeats it, or an institution converts fluent text into action. The most important hallucinations are therefore shared events. Machines generate plausibility; humans and institutions can finish the job by granting it authority.

The practical question is not whether generative systems can be made perfectly truthful. It is how to build a culture and an infrastructure in which uncertainty survives contact with speed, convenience, and confidence.
