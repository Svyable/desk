## Author's Note

This is a work of narrative nonfiction about a specific class of drugs — GLP-1 receptor agonists and the dual- and triple-hormone agonists that followed them — and the decade, roughly 2017 to 2027, in which they moved from a niche diabetes therapy to one of the most consequential commercial and medical stories of the twenty-first century so far.

Every trial result, approval date, dollar figure, and dated event in this book is drawn from a real, checkable source: peer-reviewed clinical trials, FDA approval records, company disclosures, federal research-institute publications, and named, dated journalism. Where a number is an estimate rather than a settled fact — a manufacturing-cost projection, a mortality forecast, a decade-out sales impact — the text says so. Where evidence is incomplete, contested, or still accumulating, the text says that too. This book does not invent quotations, does not invent statistics, and does not construct composite scenes involving real, identifiable people. A small number of named public figures are quoted from their own public statements, cited to the original source.

This book takes obesity seriously as a biological condition and a public-health emergency, not as a referendum on any individual's character. It also takes seriously the industries, workers, and patients whose lives have been rearranged by a molecule that most of them had never heard of in 2020. Readers looking for medical advice should talk to a clinician; this is a work of reporting and argument, not a prescribing guide.

The word "obesity" appears throughout in its clinical sense, tied to the disease definitions used by the trials and agencies this book cites. Nothing in this book is intended to reduce a reader's own relationship with food, weight, or their body to a single molecule's verdict on it.
