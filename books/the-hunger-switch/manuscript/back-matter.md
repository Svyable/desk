## A note on sources and method

Every dated fact, trial result, dollar figure, and direct quotation attributed to a named person in this book is drawn from a real, checkable source, listed with its chapter-by-chapter use in this book's research trail. Sources lean toward primary material where it exists publicly — FDA approval records and safety alerts, company clinical-trial disclosures, peer-reviewed trial publications in venues including the New England Journal of Medicine, Nature Medicine, and Nature Communications, federal research-institute releases, and congressional hearing records — and toward named, dated journalism from established outlets where primary material was not directly available, such as an executive's remarks on an earnings call or a market reaction to a specific piece of news.

This book does not invent quotations, does not invent statistics, and does not construct composite scenes involving real, identifiable people. Where evidence is preliminary, contested, or still accumulating as of this book's drafting in September 2026 — long-run cardiovascular and mortality data beyond the several years current trials have covered, manufacturing-cost estimates built on academic modeling rather than disclosed company accounting, the durability of the addiction-related findings, or the eventual scale of generic competition following India's early 2026 patent expiration — the text says so directly rather than presenting a projection as a settled fact. Readers wanting to verify a specific claim, or to see what a chapter's sourcing does and does not establish, should consult the source ledger accompanying this manuscript.

## Glossary

**GLP-1 (glucagon-like peptide-1).** A hormone released by the gut within minutes of eating, which stimulates glucose-dependent insulin release, slows gastric emptying, and reduces appetite. The physiological signal this entire book's drug class is built to amplify and extend.

**Incretin.** The general category of gut hormones, including GLP-1 and GIP, that stimulate additional insulin release beyond what rising blood glucose alone would trigger — the "incretin effect" first proposed in 1932 and proven experimentally in the 1960s.

**GLP-1 receptor agonist.** A drug that binds and activates the GLP-1 receptor, mimicking and extending the natural hormone's effects. Exenatide (Byetta), liraglutide (Victoza, Saxenda), and semaglutide (Ozempic, Wegovy, Rybelsus) are all GLP-1 receptor agonists.

**Dual agonist.** A drug engineered to activate two separate hormone receptors simultaneously. Tirzepatide (Mounjaro, Zepbound), activating both the GLP-1 and GIP receptors, is the primary example this book examines.

**Triple agonist.** A drug activating three hormone receptors simultaneously — in retatrutide's case, GLP-1, GIP, and glucagon receptors together.

**Fatty-acid acylation.** The chemical modification strategy, central to liraglutide's and semaglutide's development, that attaches a fatty-acid side chain to a peptide so it binds reversibly to albumin in blood, dramatically extending the peptide's functional half-life.

**Food noise.** Patient-originated shorthand for persistent, intrusive thoughts about food and eating; by the mid-2020s, the subject of its own emerging clinical research literature.

**503A / 503B.** The two federal categories of pharmacy compounding, distinguished by scale and regulatory oversight, central to this book's account of the compounded-GLP-1 gray market and its 2024-2026 wind-down.

**Patent cliff.** The point at which a drug's core patent protection expires and generic or biosimilar competition becomes legally possible — projected for semaglutide's core U.S. patent in December 2031, with considerably earlier expiration in India, China, Brazil, Canada, and Turkey.

## About the author

Sven Hardy Benson writes narrative nonfiction about the collision between new science and the institutions, markets, and cultural assumptions built before that science arrived. *The Hunger Switch* is his account of the decade in which a hormone discovered in a desert lizard's venom became one of the most consequential medical, commercial, and cultural stories of the current era.
