*How One Molecule Took Over Appetite, Industry, and the Global Body*

Narrative nonfiction about the GLP-1 obesity-drug era: how a peptide isolated from Gila monster venom in a Bronx VA laboratory, and a parallel three-decade research program inside a century-old, foundation-controlled Danish insulin company, produced semaglutide and tirzepatide — and how those two molecules, in barely five years, reshaped appetite science, a small country's GDP, drug manufacturing, insurance policy, addiction medicine, and the balance sheets of industries from snack food to life insurance.

The book's working thesis is that this story only makes sense held two ways at once: as a genuine, evidence-backed medical breakthrough, and as a case study in how a scarcity-driven, celebrity-accelerated, unevenly priced commercial explosion around that breakthrough produced real, avoidable harm alongside its real benefit. Neither truth cancels the other.

## What this is

A complete first draft, written in one continuous drafting pass: roughly 66,000 words across twenty chapters plus front matter, a prologue, an epilogue, and back matter. Every dated fact, trial result, dollar figure, and quotation attributed to a named person is sourced to a real, checkable record in this book's research trail. No invented statistics, no invented quotations, no composite scenes involving real, identifiable people.

## Fact boundary

This is narrative nonfiction reporting and argument, not medical advice. Long-term (10+ year) cardiovascular, cancer, and mortality data for this drug class are still accumulating; several figures in this book — manufacturing-cost estimates, mortality-reduction projections, decade-out sales impacts — are academic or industry modeling exercises rather than confirmed, disclosed accounting, and the text flags them as such throughout. Some late-chapter developments (the oral Wegovy launch price, the FDA's April 2026 503B Bulks List proposal, India's March 2026 generic semaglutide launch) were current as of this draft's September 2026 completion and may have moved by the time of any later editorial pass.
