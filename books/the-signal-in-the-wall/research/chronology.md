# Chronology

- **1953** — U.S. personnel occupy the Moscow chancery; records later place awareness of unusual radio/microwave signals in this era.
- **1960s** — U.S. technical monitoring and concern about the Moscow signal intensify; TUMS terminology appears; security compromises and bugging shape interpretation.
- **1964** — U.S. officials discuss shielding and countermeasures; numerous hidden microphones are found in embassy spaces during the broader security crisis.
- **1965–1969** — Projects PANDORA/BIZARRE and related work investigate possible biological/behavioral effects; exposure estimates are corrected downward, changing interpretation of some experiments.
- **June 1967** — Secretary Rusk raises the electromagnetic signal with Soviet Foreign Minister Gromyko.
- **1975–1976** — stronger/additional signals sharpen U.S. concern; Kissinger and Ford raise the issue; Ambassador Stoessel pushes for staff briefing.
- **February 1976** — embassy staff are briefed amid leaks and controversy.
- **1970s onward** — microwave auditory mechanism receives continued experimental/theoretical treatment.
- **Late 2016** — U.S. personnel in Havana begin reporting unusual acute sensory and health events.
- **2018** — JAMA publishes early clinical cohort report; State Department Accountability Review Board reports on institutional response.
- **2019** — JAMA publishes advanced neuroimaging group analysis and accompanying debate.
- **2020** — National Academies judges directed pulsed RF the most plausible mechanism among those considered for distinctive acute cases, while emphasizing uncertainty and multiple possible contributors.
- **2 Feb 2022** — ODNI/CIA release IC Experts Panel summary finding pulsed electromagnetic energy plausible for a subset of core characteristics.
- **1 Mar 2023** — IC updated assessment says most agencies judge a foreign adversary responsible for reported AHIs to be very unlikely overall.
- **18 Mar 2024** — NIH reports no significant MRI-detectable brain injury or biological abnormality distinguishing studied AHI participants from controls, while emphasizing symptoms are real and potentially disabling.
- **29 Jul 2024** — GAO reports on DOD care coordination and treatment access for affected personnel.
- **10 Jan 2025** — ODNI publishes updated December 2024 AHI assessment; overall foreign-adversary judgment remains very unlikely for most of the IC, with nuance among components and continuing uncertainty around limited possibilities.
