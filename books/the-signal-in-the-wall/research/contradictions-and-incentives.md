# Contradictions and Incentives Audit

## Contradictions to preserve

1. **A real historical beam does not establish a modern weapon.** Moscow increases plausibility that states conduct covert RF operations; it does not identify the purpose or provenance of Havana-era events.
2. **A real sensory bioeffect does not establish injury.** Microwave hearing is mechanistically accepted; broad chronic AHI symptom causation remains unresolved.
3. **Mechanism plausibility and actor attribution diverge.** NASEM 2020 and the IC Experts Panel 2022 gave RF serious weight; 2023 and 2025 IC assessments judged foreign adversary responsibility very unlikely overall.
4. **Symptoms can be real while persistent structural imaging is negative.** NIH 2024 explicitly supports both propositions.
5. **Ordinary-source evidence can resolve a recording without resolving an illness.** A cricket-like audio sample is evidence about that sample, not every event.
6. **Classification can legitimately hide evidence and also create myth-making space.** Neither effect should be treated as proof of the preferred theory.

## Incentives

### U.S. government
- protect personnel;
- avoid falsely accusing another state;
- avoid appearing to minimize employee reports;
- protect sources and methods;
- demonstrate institutional competence;
- contain diplomatic and workforce consequences.

### Foreign adversaries
- conceal real collection or harassment operations;
- exploit U.S. uncertainty;
- encourage narratives that waste resources or damage trust;
- deny activities regardless of purpose.

### Affected personnel
- obtain care, recognition, benefits and explanation;
- preserve professional credibility;
- make sense of abrupt disabling experiences.

These incentives do not imply fabrication.

### Researchers and clinicians
- solve an unusual scientific problem;
- protect patients;
- publish novel findings;
- defend disciplinary interpretations.

### Media and political actors
- prefer a coherent villain or coherent debunking;
- reward certainty, leaked fragments and memorable mechanisms;
- compress confidence language.

## Disinformation risk

The topic is unusually vulnerable to information operations because both overbelief and over-dismissal can harm U.S. institutions. A foreign service need not possess an exotic weapon to amplify belief that it does. Conversely, evidence of propaganda does not establish that all underlying incidents are invented. Treat influence activity as a separate evidentiary layer.
