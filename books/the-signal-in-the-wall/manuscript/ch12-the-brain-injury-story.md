# 12. The Brain-Injury Story

Images are seductive because they look like facts before interpretation arrives.

Early clinical work on U.S. personnel from Havana described neurological symptoms and later reported group-level neuroimaging differences. The 2019 JAMA paper on advanced neuroimaging was careful about causation, but public discussion often became less careful. Brain differences slid toward brain damage; brain damage slid toward external attack.

The inferential ladder was taller than the data.

Group imaging studies face familiar problems: multiple comparisons, small or selected cohorts, uncertain pre-event baselines, heterogeneous timing, and the fact that statistical differences do not automatically identify cause. Critiques in the medical literature emphasized these issues. None required accusing patients of fabrication.

Then the NIH performed a larger, controlled set of evaluations over several years. In March 2024, NIH reported that researchers found [no significant evidence of MRI-detectable brain injury or biological abnormalities distinguishing the AHI group from matched controls](https://www.nih.gov/news-events/news-releases/nih-studies-find-severe-symptoms-havana-syndrome-no-evidence-mri-detectable-brain-injury-or-biological-abnormalities). Participants' symptoms were nevertheless described as real, disruptive, and in some cases prolonged.

This is exactly the kind of result anomaly debates handle badly.

Negative MRI findings do not prove nothing happened. Some transient insults leave no persistent MRI signature. Functional neurological disorders and persistent postural-perceptual dizziness are real disorders of function, not euphemisms for malingering. Stress can be consequence, contributor, or both.

But the NIH result does weaken claims that a characteristic persistent structural brain injury has been demonstrated across the AHI cohort.

That sentence is not as emotionally satisfying as 'attack confirmed' or 'mass hysteria.' It is more useful.

A mature book has to let evidence downgrade its favorite story.
