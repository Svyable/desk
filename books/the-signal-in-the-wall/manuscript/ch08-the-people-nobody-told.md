# 8. The People Nobody Told

The Moscow Signal story is not only about what entered the embassy. It is about what did not leave Washington.

Information.

For years, people lived and worked inside a building exposed to a signal the U.S. government was studying without being fully informed about the concern. Declassified records show officials wrestling with whether and how to brief staff. Spouses were at points treated differently from employees even when they shared the same building. Medical sampling was conducted under a cover explanation.

The institutional logic is recognizable. The health significance was uncertain. The diplomatic cost of disclosure was real. Publicity could trigger fear. The Soviets denied responsibility. Officials worried about classified counterintelligence activities. There was no clean message to deliver.

None of that erases the ethical problem.

When uncertainty concerns a person's own body, classification becomes morally asymmetrical. The institution can tolerate ambiguity because it owns the files. The exposed person has to decide whether a headache matters without knowing the exposure exists.

This history shadows modern AHI response. The U.S. government now emphasizes care, reporting, and respect for personnel who come forward. That posture is not merely compassionate; it is an institutional correction. The HAVANA Act and later care structures reflect a recognition that attribution can remain unresolved while obligations to affected employees remain concrete.

Yet openness creates a different risk. Once an organization warns a global workforce about a poorly bounded incident phenotype, reporting may expand. That does not make reports false. It changes the denominator. Symptoms common in ordinary life enter a surveillance system designed around an extraordinary hypothesis. The more heterogeneous the case pool becomes, the harder it is to infer a common cause.

The policy problem is therefore nasty: under-communicate and you repeat Moscow; overgeneralize and you may destroy the analytic specificity needed to discover what happened.

The solution cannot be disbelief.

It has to be measurement paired with care.

Treat the person. Preserve the event scene. Capture environmental data. Record onset features before narrative convergence. Maintain controls. Separate eligibility for medical support from certainty about hostile attribution.

A victim should not have to win an intelligence argument to receive care.

An intelligence argument should not be won because a victim deserves care.

Those two sentences belong together.
