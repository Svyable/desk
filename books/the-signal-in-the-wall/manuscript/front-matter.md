# Front Matter

## The Signal in the Wall
### Moscow, Microwave Hearing, and the Weapon We Still Cannot Prove

There is a class of historical fact that becomes less believable when you describe it accurately.

For years, the Soviet Union directed microwave energy at the United States Embassy in Moscow. American officials knew. They complained privately. They worried about health effects. They ran classified experiments. They did not promptly tell everyone living and working in the building. Some of the experiments were stranger than the rumor that later grew around them.

That happened.

A second fact sounds equally theatrical. Short pulses of microwave energy can make a person perceive a click, buzz, or knocking sound without an acoustic speaker in the room. The effect is not telepathy. It is not a radio receiver hidden in the brain. Absorbed energy produces a tiny, rapid thermal expansion; the resulting pressure wave travels through tissue and bone to the cochlea. The auditory system does the rest.

That happens too.

Now add a third fact. Beginning in Havana in 2016, American personnel reported abrupt, sometimes directional sensory phenomena followed in some cases by dizziness, headache, tinnitus, cognitive difficulty, disequilibrium, and prolonged disability. The episodes eventually acquired an administrative name—anomalous health incidents—and an irresistible public one: Havana Syndrome.

Here is where disciplined thinking usually collapses.

Because the Moscow beam was real, some people treat a modern microwave weapon as established. Because later intelligence assessments judged a foreign campaign unlikely, other people treat the reported suffering as unreal. Because microwave hearing exists, it gets promoted from mechanism to culprit. Because MRI studies disagree or evolve, images become verdicts. Because intelligence remains classified, absence of public proof becomes proof of hidden proof.

Every move is tempting. None is adequate.

This book keeps four questions separate: Did an event occur? Can a proposed mechanism physically produce some reported features? Is there evidence a device existed in the relevant place and time? Is there evidence tying an actor to its use? Those are four different burdens. The Moscow Signal closes the first burden for one historical case. Microwave hearing closes a narrow mechanism question. The AHI record does not thereby close the device or attribution questions.

The oddity is not that governments investigate strange possibilities. The oddity is how quickly the investigation itself can become part of the evidence ecology. Secret programs create real documents and missing documents. Real victims generate urgent policy. Real laboratory effects make an attack hypothesis less absurd. Political incentives reward decisive language. Classification prevents outsiders from seeing the denominator. Medical categories shift as cohorts widen. Retrospective storytelling then irons all of it into a single line.

I am interested in the wrinkles.

The beam that existed. The monkey experiment run at the wrong assumed exposure level. The embassy families who were not told. The audio recordings that may contain crickets without explaining an illness. The 2020 National Academies report that put directed pulsed RF at the front of its mechanism list. The 2022 Intelligence Community Experts Panel that found pulsed electromagnetic energy plausible for a subset. The 2023 and 2025 assessments that nevertheless judged foreign adversary responsibility for the reported incidents very unlikely overall. The 2024 NIH work that found severe symptoms but no consistent MRI-detectable brain injury or biological abnormality distinguishing the group from controls.

Contradiction is not contamination here. Contradiction is the subject.

The best ending would be boring: a detector with a timestamp.
