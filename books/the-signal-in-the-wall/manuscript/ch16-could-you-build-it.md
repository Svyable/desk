# 16. Could You Build It?

'Could a device do this?' is not one question.

Could laboratory equipment create microwave auditory perception? Yes.

Could an engineered RF source direct energy toward a person through some real-world geometry? Yes, in principle, subject to distance, aperture, frequency, power, obstructions, and pointing.

Could a portable or concealable system reproduce a specific reported acute phenotype without obvious collateral signatures? That requires calculation.

Could it do so through walls at useful standoff distance, repeatedly, safely for the operator, with enough targeting precision to affect one person but not another? Harder.

Could a foreign service deploy such systems across multiple countries without producing exploitable intelligence residue? Harder again.

The device question therefore belongs on a ladder.

The public debate often jumps from rung one to rung five. Because pulsed RF can create an auditory effect, a suitcase weapon is assumed. Because military laboratories have high-power microwave systems, covert anti-personnel deployment is assumed. Because patents exist, efficacy is assumed.

Patents are particularly weak evidence. A patent demonstrates a filing and a claim structure, not operational performance.

An honest feasibility chapter would build link budgets across plausible frequencies and distances; estimate antenna apertures and gains; model attenuation through common construction materials; compare peak and average power requirements; examine collateral RF detection; and ask what a clandestine operator would need to know about the target's exact position.

The question should be adversarially engineered.

For every configuration that looks feasible, ask what signature it creates.

For every signature that seems avoidable, ask what engineering penalty avoidance imposes.

A secret weapon is still a machine.

Machines have mass, heat, power supplies, apertures, tolerances, failure modes, operators, and logistics. Classification can hide documents. It cannot repeal thermodynamics.
