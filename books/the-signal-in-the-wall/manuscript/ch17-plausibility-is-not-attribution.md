# 17. Plausibility Is Not Attribution

This entire book can be reduced to one sentence that modern discourse appears structurally incapable of retaining:

**Plausibility is not attribution.**

The Moscow Signal is verified exposure without settled purpose.

Microwave hearing is verified mechanism without an AHI event.

The National Academies report is mechanism ranking without actor identification.

The 2022 IC panel is classified-access plausibility judgment without public device proof.

The 2023 and 2025 assessments are attribution judgments that do not erase physics.

The NIH studies are cohort-level medical findings that do not retroactively instrument an event.

Every piece answers a different question.

Conspiracy narratives are often built by stacking true statements until the unsupported bridge between them becomes hard to see. The Soviet Union irradiated an embassy. True. The U.S. secretly researched effects. True. People can hear pulsed microwaves. True. Diplomats later reported strange directional sensations. True. Therefore Russia attacked them with a microwave weapon. Unsupported bridge.

Debunking narratives can perform the mirror image. A famous recording resembled crickets. True. NIH did not find a characteristic persistent MRI injury. True. Most intelligence agencies assess foreign responsibility as very unlikely. True. Therefore no externally caused event occurred in any case. Also a bridge.

The correct answer may be asymmetrical and ugly: most reports may have ordinary or diverse causes while a small number remain unresolved; or all may eventually resolve without a hostile device; or a bounded external mechanism may be demonstrated for a subset without validating the larger mythology that grew around it.

Science does not owe us narrative uniformity.

Intelligence does not owe us a villain.

Victims do not owe us a mechanism before they deserve care.

The burden is to keep the boxes labeled until evidence earns the arrows between them.
