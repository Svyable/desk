# 18. Instrument the Room

The ending is embarrassingly practical.

Stop trying to solve a transient physical event years after it happened with better adjectives.

Instrument the room.

The National Academies said this in 2020 in more technical language. A suspected RF event can be characterized in real time with modern electronics: power, frequency, pulse width, repetition rate, angle of arrival. Add synchronized microphones, infrasound and ultrasound sensors, magnetic-field channels, power-line monitoring, environmental controls, GPS-disciplined timestamps, and tamper-evident local storage. Add wearable vestibular and physiological markers if ethical and useful. Establish matched control sites so that ordinary electromagnetic and acoustic clutter has a denominator.

Do not build a detector that flashes 'attack.' Build an instrument that records physics.

The experimental design should be hostile to the favored hypothesis. If RF is suspected, include shielding and dummy sensors. If ultrasound is suspected, instrument near-field and room-resonance behavior. If ordinary acoustics are suspected, maintain a reference library of insects, appliances, HVAC transients, motors, switching supplies, and urban sounds. If psychosocial amplification is a concern, keep exposure classification blinded during symptom scoring when possible.

Most important: preregister what would count as evidence.

A synchronized, directional, non-ambient RF transient coincident with an acute sensory event would be important.

Replication across independent sensors would be better.

A field that changes predictably with movement or shielding would be better still.

A captured waveform that can reproduce a narrow sensory effect in a safe laboratory analog would transform the debate.

An identified emitter and custody chain would transform it again.

Until then, the historical record supports a position that feels unsatisfying because it is not tribal enough.

States have aimed microwave energy at diplomatic facilities.

The United States has secretly studied biological effects of such exposure.

Pulsed RF can produce auditory perception.

Some expert panels have found an RF mechanism plausible for a subset of AHI reports.

Most of the U.S. intelligence community nevertheless judges foreign adversary responsibility for the reported incidents very unlikely overall.

NIH has not found a consistent persistent MRI-detectable brain injury signature across studied participants.

Those sentences coexist.

The mystery survives not because we lack stories.

We lack the waveform.
