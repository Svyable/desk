# 10. Havana, 2016

In late 2016, U.S. personnel in Cuba began reporting unusual health events. Some described sudden sounds or sensory phenomena with apparent directionality, followed by symptoms including head pain, tinnitus, dizziness, imbalance, visual disturbance, cognitive difficulty, or fatigue. The reports eventually spread beyond Cuba to other countries and to the United States.

The public story expanded faster than the evidence base.

'Sonic attack' was an early phrase, vivid and perhaps misleading. If an acoustic weapon had injured people, ordinary microphones should matter. If a non-acoustic mechanism produced an auditory sensation, microphones might record nothing useful. If an ordinary environmental sound accompanied an unrelated medical event, the recording could be real and causally irrelevant.

The event class also changed as reporting widened. Some cases had a sudden, spatially specific onset. Others were dominated by chronic symptoms. Some occurred in residences, some in workplaces, some in transit. The administrative category AHI became broader than the narrow phenotype that initially drove exotic-mechanism speculation.

This is not evidence that the later reports were fake. It is evidence that grouping is a causal decision.

The State Department's 2018 Accountability Review Board focused on institutional response rather than deciding mechanism. The [State Department summary](https://2017-2021.state.gov/cuba-accountability-review-board/) records thirty recommendations concerning coordination, security, medical issues, communication, and risk management. That alone tells us something about how the event landed inside government: as a cross-domain crisis before it was an established diagnosis.

Medical urgency and intelligence uncertainty arrived at the same time.

That combination is structurally dangerous. Clinicians are trained to act under uncertainty for the benefit of the patient. Intelligence analysts are trained to withhold confidence when evidence is incomplete. Security managers are trained to reduce exposure to possible threats before attribution closes. Diplomats are trained to consider bilateral consequences. Each profession can behave rationally and still create a collective narrative that outruns any one evidence stream.

Havana is not one mystery. It is several institutions sharing a word.
