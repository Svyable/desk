# Back Matter

## A note on language

This manuscript uses **anomalous health incident (AHI)** for the administrative category used by the U.S. government and **Havana Syndrome** only when discussing the public/media label or historical literature that uses it. Neither term is treated as proof of a single etiology.

## A note on patents

Patents, where referenced in later expansion, are evidence that an inventor filed claims. They are not evidence that the described system works as claimed, was built, was deployed, or caused any reported event.

## A note on classified evidence

The manuscript works only from lawfully public material. When an official assessment states that analysts reviewed classified intelligence, the manuscript reports the assessment and its confidence language; it does not imply access to the underlying material.

## A note on people who reported AHIs

Disagreement about cause is not a judgment about sincerity. Symptoms can be severe and disabling even when a mechanism is unknown or when objective testing does not reveal a persistent structural lesion. The manuscript separates medical care from attribution because collapsing them harms both.

## Working conclusion

The strongest claim currently supportable from the public record is not that a microwave weapon caused AHIs, nor that the hypothesis has been disproved in every case. It is that the historical, biophysical, medical, and intelligence records create a sharply testable problem whose decisive evidence must come from synchronized event capture.
