# 9. The Epidemiology That Refused to Behave

If the Moscow Signal had been an effective chronic health weapon, the embassy population should have carried a readable scar.

That was the hope behind epidemiological work: compare exposed personnel, look for excess disease, identify patterns, turn a frightening but ambiguous exposure into a population-level answer.

The answer did not become clean.

Historical reviews of the Moscow embassy cohort have not established a convincing disease pattern attributable to the microwave exposure. That negative evidence matters. It pushes against popular narratives in which the beam is casually described as a proven health weapon.

But epidemiology has limits here too. Exposure reconstruction was imperfect. Personnel rotated. Signal levels changed. Health endpoints differ in latency and detectability. Cohort sizes were not enormous. A mechanism producing acute sensory effects would not necessarily produce a cancer signal decades later. Failure to observe one category of harm does not eliminate another.

This is where bad reasoning often performs a substitution.

Claim A: Moscow microwave exposure did not produce a demonstrated excess of a certain chronic disease.

Claim B: microwave energy cannot produce any relevant human effect.

Those are not the same claim.

The inverse substitution is equally bad.

Claim A: pulsed RF can produce auditory perception.

Claim B: Moscow exposure injured embassy personnel.

Also not the same.

The correct posture is endpoint discipline. Each proposed effect needs its own expected signature and study design.

That becomes crucial in Havana-era research. If a case definition contains a heterogeneous mix of acute and chronic symptoms, if exposure is unknown, if enrollment happens after publicity, and if controls are imperfectly matched, even sophisticated imaging can produce findings that are hard to interpret. The study may be statistically careful and still be answering a fuzzy etiologic question.

Epidemiology cannot rescue a badly specified exposure.

It can tell us about groups. It cannot manufacture the missing waveform.
