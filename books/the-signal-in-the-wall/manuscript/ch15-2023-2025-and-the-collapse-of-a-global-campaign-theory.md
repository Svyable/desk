# 15. 2023, 2025, and the Collapse of a Global-Campaign Theory

If the 2022 experts panel made directed RF seem more plausible, the 2023 Intelligence Community Assessment made a coordinated foreign campaign seem less plausible.

Most intelligence agencies concluded it was very unlikely that a foreign adversary was responsible for the reported AHIs overall. Analysts cited targeting and collection work that failed to produce the expected pattern of adversary involvement, questions about device feasibility and availability, and medical uncertainty.

Then came the [January 10, 2025 updated assessment](https://www.dni.gov/files/ODNI/documents/assessments/NIC-Unclassified-ICA-Updated-Assessment-AHI-December2024.pdf). The overall judgment remained: most of the IC continued to assess foreign adversary responsibility as very unlikely. But the update did not convert the subject into absolute closure. Some components adjusted confidence or allowed limited possibilities around a small subset and foreign research.

This is not contradiction in the trivial sense. It is two different propositions.

Proposition one: certain physical mechanisms could plausibly generate some reported characteristics.

Proposition two: intelligence does not support a foreign actor conducting the reported global campaign.

Both can be true.

A gun can exist without having fired the bullet under investigation. A laboratory can study a technology without fielding it. A state can possess relevant scientific literature without operating a worldwide attack program. A symptom cluster can include a few externally caused events and many unrelated cases. Or none of the cases may involve the proposed device.

The global-campaign theory has a heavy logistical burden. Repeated attacks across countries should generate travel, procurement, training, tasking, surveillance, communications, accidents, captures, defectors, technical emissions, or intelligence intercepts. The more events attributed to one adversary program, the larger the expected residue.

That is why negative intelligence collection matters even when it does not prove impossibility.

Secrecy can hide operations.

Scale makes hiding expensive.
