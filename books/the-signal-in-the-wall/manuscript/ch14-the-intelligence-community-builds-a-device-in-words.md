# 14. The Intelligence Community Builds a Device in Words

In February 2022, the U.S. Intelligence Community released the executive summary of an experts panel convened by ODNI and CIA. The panel had access to more than a thousand classified documents and multidisciplinary expertise. Its public conclusions were striking.

A subset of anomalous health incidents, it said, could not be easily explained by known environmental or medical conditions. [Pulsed electromagnetic energy, particularly in the radio-frequency range, was judged a plausible explanation for the core characteristics](https://archive.dni.gov/index.php/newsroom/reports-publications/reports-publications-2022/3591-complementary-efforts-on-anomalous-health-incidents). Ultrasound was also considered plausible in close-access scenarios. Psychosocial factors alone were judged insufficient for the panel's core subset.

The panel went further than a generic statement that microwaves can affect people. It reasoned about a class of possible devices and exposure geometries.

This is where the public record becomes epistemically asymmetric. The panel saw classified incident and intelligence material outsiders cannot inspect. Its conclusion therefore deserves weight but cannot be independently reproduced from the public summary.

A dangerous rhetorical move follows: 'they saw the secret evidence, therefore the weapon exists.'

The opposite move is equally lazy: 'if the evidence were real they would release it.' Intelligence sources and methods are often legitimately protected.

The only stable position is to separate **expert judgment under classified access** from **publicly reproducible proof**.

The panel's conclusion raises the probability of a physically feasible external mechanism for some cases. It does not by itself establish an operational adversary program.

The next intelligence assessments would make that distinction painfully visible.
