# Back Matter

## About the Book

*Predictable* is a book about a distinction that technology booms make unusually easy to forget: a technology can matter enormously without making every investment built around it a good one.

Artificial intelligence provides the live case. Prediction markets provide a second lens. Both convert uncertain futures into numbers that look more settled than the assumptions underneath them often are.

The book does not argue that AI is fake, that prediction markets are useless, or that a crash must occur on a particular schedule. It asks a narrower question: when a future becomes widely believed, what has already been paid for?

## Notes and Sources

The manuscript was researched chapter by chapter using a source ledger maintained alongside the book.

The sourcing hierarchy is deliberate. Company filings, earnings releases, official market rules, government statistics, regulatory materials, and original academic research are preferred for claims they can answer directly. Contemporaneous reporting is used where the event itself is the subject, such as a same-day market reaction. Historical analogies are supported by historical records and modern scholarship rather than by retrospective anecdotes alone.

Current-company figures and market prices are snapshots, not permanent facts. The source ledger records dates so that a reader can distinguish an observed result from guidance about the future. Company forecasts are treated as forecasts. Backlogs are not treated as revenue. Announced projects are not treated as completed capacity. Market prices are not treated as objective probabilities without considering the contract and market structure that produced them.

Where evidence cuts both ways, the manuscript keeps the counterevidence. The goal is not to win an argument by removing inconvenient facts. It is to make the argument strong enough to survive them.

The research files accompanying this manuscript contain the chapter-level source ledger with source titles, dates, URLs, claim summaries, and caveats. Those materials are the canonical source record for the draft and can be converted into publication-format endnotes during final production.

## Acknowledgments

This book depends on work done by people who make uncertainty legible: researchers who publish methods as well as results, statisticians who keep uncomfortable denominators in the table, regulators and public agencies that preserve records, archivists who keep old financial documents searchable, engineers who explain what the machinery can and cannot do, and companies willing to disclose enough about their economics to be examined.

The historical record is particularly humbling. Every generation leaves behind confident forecasts and incomplete evidence. The useful part is that it also leaves documents.

Any remaining errors of interpretation belong to the author.
