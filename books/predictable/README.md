AI, Prediction Markets, and the Next Great Bubble.

The working thesis is that societies repeatedly make the same mistake around transformative technologies: they correctly recognize that something important has arrived, then misprice how quickly its economics will mature, how much capital the transition can absorb, and who will ultimately capture the returns. AI can therefore become as consequential as its strongest advocates believe and still produce enormous financial destruction along the way.

Prediction markets give the book its second lens. They turn uncertain claims about politics, economics, science, and artificial intelligence into visible prices, making collective belief easier to observe than ever before. The book asks whether those prices make us wiser about the future or merely give speculation a more scientific-looking interface.

The aim is not to predict the date of an AI crash. It is to investigate the recurring gap between technological truth and financial value, trace the physical and financial machinery of the AI buildout, and show how a society surrounded by forecasts can still become dangerously certain.
