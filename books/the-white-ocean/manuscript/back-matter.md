# Back Matter

This manuscript treats milky seas as an observationally established but mechanistically incomplete class of marine luminous events. The source trail deliberately separates what has been directly sampled from what has been inferred through remote sensing, historical testimony, bacterial physiology, and ecological analogy.

The core empirical sequence is unusually compact. Historical mariners repeatedly described large, steady, whitish luminous seas. In 1985 the USS *Wilkes* encountered and sampled such an event near Socotra, with investigators reporting luminous bacteria identified as *Vibrio harveyi* associated with algal material. In 2005–2006 researchers demonstrated that a 1995 event could be recovered in archived low-light satellite observations. In 2021 VIIRS analysis identified twelve candidate events from 2012–2021, including the enormous 2019 Java event. In 2022 the published track, photographs and recollections of the yacht *Ganesha* independently tied the Java satellite feature to a surface encounter.

Those facts do not establish one universal biological mechanism. Species identity, host association, the role of phytoplankton, event-initiation physics, vertical structure, ecological function, and event-to-event heterogeneity remain open.

The research directory contains the working evidence classes, chronology, source log, contradiction audit, freshness note, and the expansion gate required before this draft can be treated as a full-length manuscript.
