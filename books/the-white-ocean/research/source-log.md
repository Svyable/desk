# Source Log

## Milky-sea observation and remote sensing

- Miller, S. D. et al. (2021), “Honing in on bioluminescent milky seas from space,” *Scientific Reports* 11, 15443. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-021-94823-z
  - Systematic VIIRS Day/Night Band search; twelve candidate events during 2012–2021; 2019 Java event roughly 100,000 km² and persistent across multiple moon-free observing windows.
  - Use: event geometry, detection method, candidate denominator, estimated bacterial-scale implications.

- Miller, S. D. (2022), “Boat encounter with the 2019 Java bioluminescent milky sea: Views from on-deck confirm satellite detection,” *PNAS* 119(29), e2207612119. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2207612119
  - NOAA repository copy: https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/54100
  - Use: Ganesha track, photographs, ship log, retrospective crew accounts, cross-validation with VIIRS.

- Miller, S. D., Haddock, S. H. D., Elvidge, C. D. & Lee, T. F. (2006/2007), “Twenty thousand leagues over the seas: the first satellite perspective on bioluminescent ‘milky seas’,” *International Journal of Remote Sensing* 27(23), 5131–5143. https://doi.org/10.1080/01431160600554298
  - Use: DMSP recovery of 1995 event and early satellite-forensics method.

- NASA Earth Observatory (2021), “Hunting Milky Seas by Satellite.” https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/hunting-milky-seas-by-satellite-149017/
  - Authoritative explanatory source for DNB confounds, spectral limitations, and event examples. Secondary to the peer-reviewed paper.

## Direct biological field evidence

- Lapota, D., Galt, C., Losee, J. R., Huddell, H. D., Orzech, J. K. & Nealson, K. H. (1988), “Observations and measurements of planktonic bioluminescence in and around a milky sea,” *Journal of Experimental Marine Biology and Ecology* 119, 55–81.
  - Primary field study of the 1985 USS Wilkes encounter near Socotra. Use with care: one directly sampled event does not establish a universal species or host.

- Nealson, K. H. & Hastings, J. W. (2006), “Quorum Sensing on a Global Scale: Massive Numbers of Bioluminescent Bacteria Make Milky Seas,” *Applied and Environmental Microbiology* 72(4), 2295–2297. https://doi.org/10.1128/AEM.72.4.2295-2297.2006
  - Mechanistic/ecological commentary connecting satellite observations, 1985 sampling, luminous bacteria, possible phytoplankton scaffolds and global-scale quorum logic. Explicitly notes uncertainty about *Phaeocystis* and *V. harveyi* generality.

## Quorum sensing and luminous Vibrio biology

- Henke, J. M. & Bassler, B. L. (2004), “Three Parallel Quorum-Sensing Systems Regulate Gene Expression in Vibrio harveyi,” *Journal of Bacteriology* 186(20), 6902–6914. https://doi.org/10.1128/JB.186.20.6902-6914.2004
  - Primary molecular biology for multi-input quorum regulation of bioluminescence and other genes.

- Henares, B. M., Higgins, K. E. & Boon, E. M. (2012), “Discovery of a Nitric Oxide Responsive Quorum Sensing Circuit in Vibrio harveyi,” *ACS Chemical Biology* 7(8), 1331–1336. https://doi.org/10.1021/cb300215t
  - Demonstrates additional environmental-signal integration into quorum circuitry. Included to prevent an oversimplified “cell density switch” account.

## Evidence cautions

- Historical witness compilations are useful for phenomenology and geography but require source-by-source provenance before quotation.
- *Vibrio harveyi* nomenclature and the *V. campbellii* clade require taxonomic checking in any expanded molecular chapter.
- Satellite-detected candidates are radiometric anomalies consistent with milky seas; only the 2019 Java event currently has strong same-event surface photographic corroboration in this research spine.
- Patents are not used as evidence of biological mechanism or efficacy.
