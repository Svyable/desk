# Evidentiary Matrix

## Class 1 — verified / documented

- Large, steady luminous-sea events have been reported repeatedly in maritime records.
- The USS *Wilkes* encountered and sampled a milky sea near Socotra in 1985.
- That sampled event contained luminous bacteria identified in the published work as *Vibrio harveyi*, associated with algal/particulate material.
- Quorum sensing regulates bioluminescence in *V. harveyi* and related luminous vibrios.
- Archived low-light satellite data recovered a large luminous ocean feature corresponding to a 1995 milky-sea report.
- VIIRS Day/Night Band analysis identified multiple candidate milky-sea events from 2012–2021.
- The 2019 Java event covered roughly 100,000 km² and persisted through multiple nighttime observing windows.
- The yacht *Ganesha* independently crossed, logged and photographed luminous water within the 2019 Java satellite feature.

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete

- Historical reports that distinguish steady glow from mechanically stimulated dinoflagellate flashing.
- Crew estimates of apparent luminous depth and horizon-scale extent.
- The inference that attachment to phytoplankton, particles or organic films creates local densities sufficient for quorum-regulated light across broad regions.
- The inference that surface convergence, bloom senescence or mesoscale circulation helps maintain events.
- Event durations extending through cloudy or moonlit intervals when the satellite cannot recover the weak signal.

## Class 3 — disputed / not established

- *Vibrio harveyi* as the universal milky-sea organism.
- *Phaeocystis* as a necessary or universal host/scaffold.
- A single quorum threshold or single ecological trigger shared by all events.
- Any adaptive account in which milky-sea light definitely recruits grazers or larger predators.
- Every historical “white sea” account as the same biological phenomenon.
- Any simple climate-change trend in milky-sea frequency based on current records.

## Class 4 — speculative synthesis / open conjecture

- Milky seas are emergent ecological states produced when physical concentration, organic substrate, microbial community and quorum regulation align over large surface-water regions.
- Different events may converge visually while using different luminous taxa or substrates.
- Event persistence may resemble a flame: stable spatial structure with continuous biological turnover rather than the same cells remaining luminous for weeks.
- A satellite-triggered rapid-response network can transform milky-sea research from opportunistic encounter to prospective event science.

## Defeat conditions

The central synthesis should narrow or fail if repeated targeted expeditions show that satellite-confirmed events lack luminous bacterial communities, lack locally concentrated microbial structures, or derive their radiance from a different mechanism. The single-organism version fails if independently sampled events repeatedly recover different luminous taxa. The quorum-centered version weakens if direct in-situ measurements show light production unrelated to the relevant signaling state. The “one phenomenon” framing should be abandoned if visually similar events resolve into multiple distinct mechanisms.
