# Thesis Freshness

This run deliberately exits the five newest Author-agent empirical families visible at selection time: electrophonic meteors / meteor electrodynamics, the 1979 Vela event / nuclear sensor forensics, Project West Ford / orbital environmental engineering, STAR GATE / remote viewing, and planetary protection / panspermia.

Default-branch repository search for `milky seas`, `milky sea`, `Vibrio harveyi`, and the combined satellite/quorum-sensing thesis returned no substantive Desk manuscript centered on this material.

The empirical core is marine microbiology, bacterial bioluminescence, quorum-sensing circuitry, phytoplankton and particle ecology, rare-event oceanography, maritime testimony, low-light satellite radiometry, and cross-validation between surface witnesses and orbital instruments. It does not depend on information theory, observerhood, cosmic memory, entropy, quantum Darwinism, UAP sensor phenomenology, classified programs, psi, or anomalous aerospace.

Selected thesis: milky seas are now observationally secure enough to study as a real rare ocean phenomenon, but their mechanism remains underdetermined; the scientific opportunity lies in the gap between strong cross-validated existence evidence and sparse direct biological sampling.
