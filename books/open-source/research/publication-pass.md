# Publication Pass Notes

The final Desk publication pass adds three synthesis pieces without introducing a new set of 2026 factual claims:

- **Interlude — What If the Moat Is Real?** attacks the book's thesis from the strongest opposing case: persistent frontier leads, proprietary deployment loops, post-training advantage, enterprise liability transfer, safety centralization, capital intensity, quality heterogeneity, behavioral lock-in, platform concentration, and the economic value of temporary scarcity.
- **Interlude — The Copy Machine** extends the value-migration argument into model reproduction: teacher models, distillation, synthetic curricula, evaluation generation, maintenance assistance, specialization, provenance, and the way cheap generation can increase the scarcity of selection and real-world corrective data.
- **Coda — The 2027 Dashboard** converts the forecasts into observable signals rather than adding predictions that can be reinterpreted later.

## Falsification tests added in this pass

The adversarial interlude should count against the book if the following patterns dominate through 2027:

1. Open models fail to create credible substitutes for ordinary commercial workloads even when their raw benchmark capability looks close.
2. The open–closed capability gap widens enough that premium providers do not need to justify price through reliability, tooling, support, or integration; superior capability alone does the work.
3. Enterprise switching remains mostly theoretical because model-specific behavioral integration makes portability too costly.
4. Safety, insurance, procurement, or regulation creates a durable two-tier market in which consequential capability remains centralized and open systems remain structurally below the economically important frontier.
5. Open-model creators depend on strategic subsidy without developing durable complementary revenue, while closed laboratories sustain the research treadmill from model access alone.

The copy-machine argument should count against the book if synthetic and teacher-driven learning turns out to be economically marginal outside a small number of laboratories, if provenance constraints prevent useful downstream reproduction at scale, or if generated training material fails to reduce the labor and data cost of building competent descendants.

## Dashboard discipline

The dashboard is deliberately multi-dimensional. Do not collapse it into one "open won / closed won" score. Record at least these separately:

- open–closed capability gap and the time lag to a commercially substitutable open alternative;
- cost per accepted outcome rather than posted token price;
- share of workloads using active routing across models/providers/locations;
- derivative depth and teacher use;
- license friction and time-to-lawyer;
- maintenance concentration and multi-hardware support lag;
- local/private execution as a share of useful production work;
- neutrality disputes that produce concrete governance or product responses;
- Chinese-family normalization in ordinary non-Chinese infrastructure;
- enterprise ownership of private evaluations and recovery drills;
- monetization captured around open weights;
- scarcity half-life at the new bottlenecks.

These additions are analytical extensions of the existing research corpus. If future editions attach new empirical examples to them, those examples should receive dated source records before publication.
