# Evidence ledger — FLASH ON THE MOON

## Governing discipline

No evidence may move upward by adjacency. A documented study of a lunar nuclear detonation is not evidence of an operational lunar nuclear mission. A participant recollection about prestige motive is not automatically the Air Force's formal requirement. A destroyed volume is evidence of an archival gap, not evidence of the most dramatic content compatible with that gap.

## Class 1 — verified / documented

- *A Study of Lunar Research Flights, Volume I* survives publicly, dated 19 June 1959 and prepared by Armour Research Foundation under Leonard Reiffel.
- The surviving report explicitly considers nuclear detonations in the lunar environment and scientific information potentially obtainable from them.
- Its documented research topics include lunar structure/origin, optical studies, seismic observations, lunar surface/magnetic fields, plasma/magnetic effects and organic matter.
- Reiffel later publicly confirmed the project and his role.
- Project Horizon provides a contemporaneous U.S. Army control for serious military lunar planning that did not become the proposed lunar base.
- U.S. high-altitude nuclear tests provide a control class for nuclear-space concepts that **did** cross into execution and left abundant physical/instrumental traces.
- The 1963 Limited Test Ban Treaty prohibited nuclear explosions in outer space for parties; the later Outer Space Treaty further constrained WMD activity and military use of celestial bodies.

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete

- Prestige/public-demonstration value was important to A119. Reiffel's later account is significant, but exact contemporaneous Air Force requirement language must be recovered before assigning a single dominant motive.
- Carl Sagan participated in analytical work. Exact tasking, calculations and surviving document authorship require primary-document reconstruction.
- Companion study volumes existed and were later destroyed. Need archival disposition records and inventories before making precise claims about number, contents or destruction rationale.
- Soviet lunar-nuclear concepts are reported in secondary histories. Require Russian primary documentation before using a detailed mirror-program narrative.

## Class 3 — disputed / unconfirmed

- A119 reached hardware integration, assigned launch vehicle, operational launch planning or warhead mating.
- A lunar nuclear device was secretly launched or detonated.
- Missing A119 records document execution or a hidden continuation.
- A119 was cancelled specifically because Apollo was judged a better prestige demonstration. Plausible retrospective account; needs decision-chain documentation.
- Exact Soviet E-series lunar nuclear mission details where sourced only to later recollection or secondary retelling.

## Class 4 — speculative synthesis

### Paper capability
A documented institution can possess a real classified **paper capability**: enough expertise and sponsorship to model an extraordinary operation without possessing an executable system.

### Execution distance
Claims should be mapped across interest → study → concept → engineering → hardware → integrated test → operational authorization → execution → measured effect → useful capability. Evidence at one rung cannot silently establish the next.

### Archive inflation
Classification and missing records may increase later perceived capability because absent evidence can be mentally populated with whatever would complete the story. Prediction: claims should grow stronger with retelling when verb-level provenance is not enforced.

## Quantitative extraction burden

For the proposed mission, recover or bound:

- contemplated device type, mass and yield;
- launch-vehicle candidates and translunar payload capacity at the relevant date;
- guidance/miss-distance requirement;
- target geometry and desired illumination relative to Earth;
- detonation altitude/contact/fuzing assumptions;
- ejecta mass/velocity estimates;
- optical brightness calculation and atmospheric/phase-angle assumptions;
- seismic coupling assumptions;
- plasma/magnetic observables;
- observing network and instrument requirements;
- launch-failure and Earth-return risk;
- schedule and decision milestones;
- evidence of hardware procurement, if any.

## Contradiction ledger

1. **Scientific experiment vs propaganda spectacle.** Do not choose one prematurely; reconstruct sponsor requirements and participant roles.
2. **'Plan' vs 'study.'** Popular accounts routinely strengthen the verb. Use source-specific language.
3. **Sagan involvement vs Sagan mythology.** Do not let later fame enlarge his documented role.
4. **Destroyed records vs hidden execution.** Archival loss is real; content inference remains bounded.
5. **Technically conceivable vs operationally feasible.** A nuclear device existed; a reliable lunar delivery/detonation/observation mission was a separate system.
6. **Later treaty prohibition vs 1959 decision.** Do not project 1963/1967 legal regimes backward as causes of a 1959 termination.

## Incentive ledger

- Air Force: prestige, strategic signaling, weapons/environment knowledge, institutional position in emerging space mission.
- Armour Research Foundation: sponsored technical research and scientific opportunity.
- Scientists: genuine lunar/plasma/seismic questions, career/research incentives, secrecy obligations, personal ethical judgments.
- Later journalists/authors: unforgettable headline pressure can collapse motive and maturity distinctions.
- Government/archive: classification and retention rules can obscure motive and maturity even without a continuing conspiracy.

## Publication holds

Do not publish as fact until primary support is recovered:

- 'The main purpose was to frighten/impress the Soviets.' Attribute to Reiffel where used.
- 'A hydrogen bomb was rejected because it was too heavy.' Verify against surviving report and launch-mass assumptions.
- 'The flash would have been visible to the naked eye.' Reconstruct the optical calculation and viewing geometry.
- exact count/content/destruction dates of missing volumes;
- exact Soviet E-4 mission architecture;
- claim that A119 was cancelled in favor of Apollo;
- claim that Sagan calculated dust-cloud visibility unless document-level attribution is recovered.

## Defeat-capable comparative test

Build a blinded historical sample of classified programs across three bins: study-only, prototype/test, operational. Extract document types without labels: contracts, technical reports, hardware drawings, procurement, safety plans, test ranges, logistics, training, telemetry, accident planning, operational orders, post-test reports and downstream effects. If study-only programs routinely exhibit the same trace stack as operational programs, `execution distance` is weak. If trace classes separate strongly, the framework earns use on modern classified-adjacent claims.
