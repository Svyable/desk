# Source trace — FLASH ON THE MOON

Checked 2026-09-19. Public/lawful sources only.

## Primary / near-primary anchors

1. **Leonard Reiffel / Armour Research Foundation, _A Study of Lunar Research Flights, Volume I_ (19 June 1959).** Surviving A119 report. National Security Archive preservation copy: https://nsarchive2.gwu.edu/NSAEBB/NSAEBB479/docs/EBB-Moon02.pdf
   - Use for what the surviving study actually considered.
   - Do not use the report's existence as proof of mission execution.

2. **National Security Archive, _The U.S. Military's Plans for a Moon Base_ / lunar planning document set.** https://nsarchive2.gwu.edu/NSAEBB/NSAEBB479/
   - Useful provenance hub for A119 and Project Horizon.
   - Recover each underlying document directly before making fine-grained claims.

3. **U.S. Army, _Project Horizon, Volume I: Summary and Supporting Considerations_ (1959).** https://nsarchive2.gwu.edu/NSAEBB/NSAEBB479/docs/EBB-Moon01_sm.pdf
   - Contemporaneous control: serious military lunar planning that did not become the proposed base.

4. **U.S. Army, _Project Horizon, Volume II: Technical Considerations and Plans_ (1959).** https://nsarchive2.gwu.edu/NSAEBB/NSAEBB479/docs/EBB-Moon01A_sm.pdf
   - Compare maturity signals: logistics, construction, transport, schedule, staffing.

5. **U.S. Department of State, Limited Test Ban Treaty history.** https://history.state.gov/milestones/1961-1968/limited-ban
   - Later legal boundary; do not project backward into the 1959 cancellation without evidence.

## Research note

The strongest current source is the surviving report itself. Popular summaries often make the story cleaner than the archive: one motive, one bomb, one plan, one cancellation reason. The manuscript should become less clean as research improves. That is a feature. A119's value is precisely that a real classified study can be both extraordinary and far from operational execution.
