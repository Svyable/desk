# NEITHER CONFIRM NOR DENY

*The Ship That Hid a Submarine Recovery in Plain Sight*

Sven Hardy Benson

This is a book about retrieval.

Not the retrieval people usually mean when they use that word now.

In 1974 the United States put a six-hundred-foot ship over a dead Soviet submarine more than three miles down in the Pacific and tried to pick up a large section of it with a mechanical claw. The ship was real. The submarine was real. The secrecy was real. So was the cover story. So was the failure during the lift. So was the partial recovery.

That combination makes Project AZORIAN unusually valuable now. We have spent years arguing about alleged hidden retrieval programs for objects whose provenance is disputed. Here is a retrieval program whose target, ship, contractors, engineering problem, concealment architecture, political risk, partial failure, press exposure and institutional afterlife can be studied in public.

The comparison does not prove or disprove any modern crash-retrieval allegation. It gives us something more useful than another opinion: a control.

Throughout this book I separate four kinds of evidence. **Documented** means supported by declassified records, official histories, archival material, physical artifacts or similarly checkable primary evidence. **Credible but incomplete** means reporting, testimony or reconstruction that has meaningful provenance but does not close the chain. **Disputed** means allegations that remain materially unverified or contradicted. **Speculative** means my synthesis: an interpretation offered as an interpretation, with conditions under which it should be abandoned.

The distinction matters because secrecy is not magic. A classified program can hide purpose while remaining unable to hide displacement, steel, money, contractors, ships, cranes, failures, funerals, lawsuits, reporters, Soviet trawlers, stolen papers, and the human appetite for telling somebody what happened.

AZORIAN did not leave no evidence. It left the wrong evidence.

That is the problem this book is about.
