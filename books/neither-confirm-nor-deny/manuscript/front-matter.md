# NEITHER CONFIRM NOR DENY

## How a secret becomes a physical object

Sven Hardy Benson

## A note about what this book is allowed to know

This is a book about secrecy, which means it has to refuse the most flattering temptation available to a writer about secrecy: pretending that the missing pages belong to him.

They do not.

Project AZORIAN is useful because enough of the machinery eventually came into daylight to make secrecy measurable. A ship existed. A capture vehicle existed. Contractors existed. A Soviet submarine existed on the Pacific floor. The cover story existed too, and parts of it were made materially true. A recovery was attempted in 1974. It was only partly successful. The operation was exposed. The phrase that became the `Glomar response` entered the legal and bureaucratic vocabulary of American secrecy.

Those are not rumors about a hidden program. They are the exposed anatomy of one.

The book uses four evidence classes throughout.

**Class 1 — verified / declassified / documented.** Official records, declassified material, contemporaneous government documents, court records, physical artifacts, and claims the responsible institutions now acknowledge.

**Class 2 — credible but incomplete.** Serious reporting, participant recollection, technically plausible reconstruction, or documentary fragments that matter but do not independently establish the larger claim attached to them.

**Class 3 — disputed allegation.** Claims of hidden programs, capabilities, motives, recoveries, materials, access, or results that lack sufficient independent confirmation.

**Class 4 — speculative synthesis.** My models. These are not discoveries hiding behind footnotes. They are propositions the evidence is being asked to injure.

The central one is simple: **a large secret technical program has two bodies. One is classified information. The other is physical dependency.**

You can classify the mission. You cannot classify gravity.

A 600-foot ship still needs steel. A heavy-lift system still needs engineering. Contractors still have payrolls. A cover identity still has to survive contact with harbors, journalists, insurers, suppliers, satellites, curious adversaries and the weather. If the hidden thing is ambitious enough, secrecy does not make its physical requirements disappear. Secrecy has to route around them.

That is the subject of this book.

It is also why the book eventually returns to modern allegations of deeply compartmented aerospace, retrieval and reverse-engineering programs. Not to use AZORIAN as proof that such programs exist. That move is intellectually cheap. Governments have hidden extraordinary projects before; therefore any extraordinary hidden project is possible. True in the useless sense.

The harder question is the reverse one.

If the alleged program were real, what would it have to become in the world?

What would it need?

What would it build?

What would it borrow?

What would it fake?

What would it accidentally make true?

And after fifty years, what shape would the hole have?
