# FIVE DAYS BEFORE

## The satellite that found an earthquake signal nobody can use

**Sven Hardy Benson**

A book about DEMETER, the French microsatellite built to look for electromagnetic effects associated with earthquakes, and the uncomfortable gap between a statistically detectable anomaly and a prediction anyone should trust with a city.

## Governing thesis

Some large statistical studies of DEMETER data report small ionospheric changes before earthquakes. Other careful analyses show how easily apparent precursors can be manufactured by background selection, cultural noise, geomagnetic variation, post-event fitting, and the enormous search space of a restless ionosphere. The interesting question is therefore narrower than “can earthquakes be predicted?” and stranger than “all precursor research is pseudoscience”: what evidentiary transformation would be required to turn a retrospective geophysical correlation into prospective information with decision value?

The book follows that transformation one rung at a time: rock physics → atmospheric coupling → ionospheric measurement → anomaly definition → statistical association → out-of-sample replication → prospective alarm → useful forecast. A result may be real at one rung and fail completely at the next.

## Evidence posture

1. **Verified/documented:** CNES's DEMETER mission operated from 2004 to 2010 to study ionospheric electromagnetic phenomena including possible earthquake-related effects. Peer-reviewed DEMETER analyses report small statistically significant VLF and ion-density associations around earthquakes. USGS reviews conclude that convincing, reliable electromagnetic earthquake precursors have not been demonstrated and that major earthquakes cannot presently be predicted.
2. **Credible but incomplete:** several DEMETER analyses report effects that survive specified statistical controls, including nighttime ion-density anomalies days before M>5 events and small VLF attenuation hours before main shocks. These are associations in noisy observational data, not validated operational predictors.
3. **Disputed/unconfirmed:** proposed lithosphere-atmosphere-ionosphere coupling mechanisms, earthquake-light mechanisms as general precursor channels, TEC precursor claims, and specific electromagnetic alarm schemes remain contested or method-dependent. Some celebrated signals have been reinterpreted as cultural noise or analysis artifacts.
4. **Speculative synthesis:** earthquake-precursor science may be a particularly clean laboratory for studying the difference between anomaly discovery and forecastability. The book will test whether the field's recurring failures arise primarily from nonexistent signals, weak/nonunique signals, analysis flexibility, inadequate physical mechanisms, base-rate problems, or some combination.

## Publication target

65,000–80,000 words. Eighteen developed chapters. The book must be legible to a reader fascinated by anomalous geophysics without rewarding credulity, and fair to precursor researchers without converting statistical association into prediction. Its strongest skeptical case must remain capable of winning.
