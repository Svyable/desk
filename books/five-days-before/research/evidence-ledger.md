# Five Days Before — evidence ledger

Working rule: an anomaly is not a precursor; a precursor is not a predictor; a predictor is not automatically useful. Every chapter should identify which rung its evidence actually reaches.

## Class 1 — verified / documented

### DEMETER existed to investigate this problem

- DEMETER operated from 2004 to 2010 as a low-Earth-orbit microsatellite measuring electromagnetic waves and plasma parameters, with earthquake-related ionospheric effects among its explicit scientific targets.
- Mission-level fact establishes institutional scientific interest and a measurement archive. It does **not** establish the reality of preseismic electromagnetic effects.

### Peer-reviewed statistical associations exist in the DEMETER archive

- Němec et al. (2009), *Journal of Geophysical Research: Space Physics*, DOI 10.1029/2008JA013972. More than 3.5 years of DEMETER data; reported a very small statistically significant nighttime VLF intensity decrease around 1.7 kHz during 0–4 hours before main shocks.
  - https://doi.org/10.1029/2008JA013972
- Píša et al. (2013), *Journal of Geophysical Research: Space Physics*, DOI 10.1002/jgra.50469. Almost 6.5 years and roughly 8,400 earthquakes; reported ~2 dB nighttime attenuation around 1.7 kHz before main shocks, with dependencies on season, latitude and ocean/continent setting.
  - https://doi.org/10.1002/jgra.50469
- Yan, Parrot & Pinçon (2017), *Journal of Geophysical Research: Space Physics*, DOI 10.1002/2017JA024623. Superposed-epoch analysis of ion density; reported nighttime perturbations for M>5 earthquakes, strongest about five days before and within ~200 km. Authors explicitly state ion density alone cannot be used for earthquake forecasting.
  - https://doi.org/10.1002/2017JA024623
- Li & Parrot (2012), *Natural Hazards and Earth System Sciences*, DOI 10.5194/nhess-12-2957-2012. Automated ion-density anomaly search produced substantial false alarms and missed detections; detection fraction increased with earthquake magnitude.
  - https://doi.org/10.5194/nhess-12-2957-2012

### The mainstream operational conclusion remains negative

- USGS Earthquake Hazards: major earthquakes are not presently predictable in the required time/location/magnitude sense.
  - https://www.usgs.gov/programs/earthquake-hazards/science
- USGS Geomagnetism: despite decades of investigation, there is no convincing evidence of electromagnetic earthquake precursors; reported signals have often been attributable to bad data or normal/global field variation.
  - https://www.usgs.gov/programs/geomagnetism/science
- Johnston (2002), USGS/International Geophysics review: clear demonstration of precursory EM signals had not been achieved, while coseismic magnetic changes are a different and established category.
  - https://www.usgs.gov/publications/38-electromagnetic-fields-generated-earthquakes

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete

### DEMETER's reported associations may encode a real geophysical relationship

The VLF and ion-density studies are peer-reviewed analyses of large observational datasets and deserve experiment-level reconstruction rather than dismissal by label. Their reported effects, however, remain observational associations. Before manuscript promotion they require:

- exact event-selection criteria;
- background construction;
- multiple-comparison treatment;
- effect-size and uncertainty extraction;
- dependence on local time, latitude, season, depth and magnitude;
- prospective or held-out replication where available;
- independence between analyses using overlapping DEMETER data.

### Stress-activated charge carriers are a candidate mechanism, not established earthquake forecasting physics

- Freund (2007), *Natural Hazards and Earth System Sciences*, proposes stress activation of electronic charge carriers in rocks and a path toward surface electrical effects.
  - https://doi.org/10.5194/nhess-7-535-2007
- St-Laurent, Derr & Freund (2006) propose stress-activated positive holes as a mechanism for earthquake lights.
  - https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pce.2006.02.003

These papers support a physically specified hypothesis worth testing. They do not establish that crustal stress routinely produces the ionospheric signals attributed to impending earthquakes, or that those signals are diagnostic.

## Class 3 — disputed allegations / unconfirmed interpretations

### Specific TEC precursor claims

Pre-earthquake total-electron-content enhancements have been reported for major earthquakes, including Tohoku. Their status is contested.

- Eisenbeis & Occhipinti (2021), *JGR Space Physics*, DOI 10.1029/2020JA028733, argues that the reported TEC enhancement can be produced as an artifact of reference-curve selection and postseismic TEC behavior. Synthetic examples show the method can generate apparent pre-event signals from data without a true precursor.
  - https://doi.org/10.1029/2020JA028733

The manuscript must not say “TEC precursors are disproven” globally from this one controversy. It may say a prominent analysis pathway can manufacture a precursor-like feature and therefore background-model choice is part of the causal claim.

### Alum Rock ULF pulse precursor

- Wang et al. (2021/2022), *Geophysical Journal International*, DOI 10.1093/gji/ggab530, reexamined claimed ULF pulses before the 2007 and 2010 Alum Rock earthquakes. The pulse-count increase before the 2007 event could be reproduced at the QuakeFinder station, but counts depended on arbitrary thresholds; the nearby Stanford-USGS station did not show the same pattern, and temporal structure implicated local cultural noise.
  - https://www.usgs.gov/publications/assessment-a-claimed-ultra-low-frequency-electromagnetic-ulfem-earthquake-precursor

This is a key book case because observation and interpretation separate cleanly.

## Class 4 — speculative synthesis / open conjecture

1. Some preseismic electromagnetic or ionospheric effects may be physically real yet too weak or nonspecific for useful prediction.
2. The main epistemic failure in precursor research may occur at transitions between rungs: mechanism → observable, observable → anomaly, anomaly → association, association → prospective prediction, prediction → decision value.
3. Large retrospective archives may preferentially produce interesting associations because the event fixes the spatial and temporal search target after the fact. Prospective evaluation removes this privilege.
4. A future multi-channel system might gain predictive information even if no single channel is useful alone, but this remains conjecture until tested out-of-sample with a frozen alarm rule.

## Counterevidence that must remain alive

### Parkfield

Johnston et al. (2006), *Bulletin of the Seismological Society of America*, examined magnetic data around the long-awaited 28 September 2004 M6.0 Parkfield earthquake and reported no unusual pre-event ULF noise of the type proposed for Loma Prieta, nor the suggested electric-field changes. The authors concluded useful prediction of damaging earthquakes appeared unlikely using those electromagnetic data.

- https://www.usgs.gov/publications/seismomagnetic-effects-long-awaited-28-september-2004-m-60-parkfield-earthquake

Parkfield is not universal disproof. It is unusually valuable negative evidence because instrumentation was in place before a known high-interest event.

### The noisy-ionosphere control

Geomagnetic storms themselves produce strong DEMETER electromagnetic phenomena. Any earthquake association must therefore be conditioned against space weather rather than merely described as an ionospheric anomaly.

- Parrot et al. (2006), JGR, DOI 10.1029/2005JA011583.
  - https://doi.org/10.1029/2005JA011583

### Volcanoes

Zlotnicki et al. (2013) report DEMETER ionospheric disturbances around eruptive volcanoes. Even if both earthquake and volcano associations are physically real, this creates a specificity problem for a generic “geophysical disturbance” observable.

- https://doi.org/10.1155/2013/530865

## Contradiction and incentive ledger

- **Mission naming bias:** DEMETER's name embeds the target hypothesis. That can sharpen experimental purpose while also increasing risk of target-oriented interpretation. Do not infer investigator bias without evidence; do treat hypothesis-conditioned analysis choices as a methodological variable.
- **Retrospective privilege:** many studies know earthquake time/location before defining the local comparison. Prospective forecasting does not.
- **Publication incentive:** positive anomalies are more publishable than null searches. Search explicitly for null DEMETER, ground magnetometer, TEC and replication studies.
- **Institutional skepticism:** USGS has an operational responsibility for hazard communication, giving it reason to demand prospective reliability. That does not make every skeptical methodological claim automatically correct; reconstruct the data arguments.
- **Commercial incentive:** private precursor systems may benefit from publicity around apparent successes. Evaluate complete alarm histories, not selected hits.
- **State-program incentive:** continued Chinese/French/other national investment demonstrates perceived scientific or strategic value of measurement, not successful prediction.

## Defeat conditions for the book's synthesis

The central anomaly-versus-forecastability thesis weakens materially if a preregistered, prospective, independent system using electromagnetic/ionospheric measurements demonstrates stable incremental forecast skill for damaging earthquakes over accepted seismological baselines, with explicit time/location/magnitude windows, tolerable false alarms, and replication in held-out regions.

The thesis also needs revision in the opposite direction if careful reanalysis shows the major DEMETER pre-event effects disappear under appropriate controls. In that case the book becomes less about a real-but-useless signal and more about how retrospective statistics generated one. Do not protect the preferred middle position.
