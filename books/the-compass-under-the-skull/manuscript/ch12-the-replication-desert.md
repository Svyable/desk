# The Replication Desert

A scientific result can become famous long before it becomes common.

Human magnetoreception is full of papers that are discussed more often than they are repeated. The reasons are partly technical. A serious experiment needs controlled Earth-strength fields, magnetic shielding or at least careful field mapping, low-noise physiological recording, appropriate sham conditions and researchers comfortable with both sensory neuroscience and magnetics. None of that is impossible. It is simply specialized enough that replication does not happen automatically.

Specialization creates a dangerous ecology. One laboratory builds an apparatus over years, becomes the world's expert in its peculiar artifacts, and publishes a surprising effect. Another laboratory that might challenge it lacks the hardware. The first group therefore performs most follow-up work, which improves technical continuity but weakens independence.

The solution is not to discount specialized experiments by default. Particle physics, astronomy and neuroimaging all rely on specialized infrastructure. The solution is to make transfer part of the claim. Publish coil geometry, field maps, switching waveforms, analysis code, raw or minimally processed data, exclusion rules and calibration routines. Then help another group build a deliberately nonidentical apparatus that produces the same biological result.

Replication should also be prospective. Reanalyzing an old dataset is valuable for checking statistics, but a sensory effect has to survive new participants and new noise. The strongest design would register the exact primary outcome before enrollment: which stimulus contrast, which EEG frequency band or behavioral measure, which time window, which sample size, which exclusions, which stopping rule.

A replication desert makes certainty inflate strangely. Supporters say an effect has stood for years, as though elapsed time were replication. Skeptics say nobody has reproduced it, sometimes when nobody has seriously tried. Both claims mistake sociology for data.

The field needs boring abundance. Several laboratories should be able to run the same core paradigm. Null results should be publishable. Apparatus failures should be documented. A result that depends on a hidden tuning parameter should be forced to reveal it.

Human magnetoreception does not need another spectacular first. It needs a second, a third and a fourth.
