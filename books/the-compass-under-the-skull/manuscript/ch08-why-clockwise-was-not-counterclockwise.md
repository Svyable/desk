# Why Clockwise Was Not Counterclockwise

If a magnetic-field experiment reports that clockwise and counterclockwise rotations produce different neural responses, the first instinct should be suspicion. Nature may distinguish them, but apparatus does too.

A coil system has wires, current directions, amplifiers, switching sequences and spatial asymmetries. A shielded room has seams. EEG cables leave the scalp in particular directions. The participant's head is not a sphere. Even if two stimulus sequences are mathematically mirror images, the laboratory may not be.

This is why the directional asymmetry in the 2019 study is both the most interesting and the most vulnerable part of the result.

The authors argued that the asymmetry followed geomagnetic coordinates rather than arbitrary equipment coordinates. Certain counterclockwise horizontal rotations under a downward-directed field produced strong alpha event-related desynchronization, while corresponding clockwise rotations did not. The vertical polarity mattered too. Such selectivity resembles biological tuning: a receptor and its downstream circuitry need not respond equally to all trajectories through field space.

A magnetite-based sensor can, in principle, be directionally asymmetric because a ferromagnetic particle has a magnetic moment. Its mechanical effect depends on how that moment is oriented relative to the external field and to whatever cellular structure anchors it. A receptor population distributed nonuniformly around the head could therefore respond differently to opposite rotations.

But before invoking anatomy, the experiment has to earn the asymmetry. Current reversal can alter induced electric fields. Mechanical forces on the coil can reverse. Tiny acoustic differences can appear. An EEG amplifier may interact with switching transients. The proper control is not merely “no field”; it is a current-matched sham in which the electronics work while the net magnetic stimulus is canceled or rearranged.

The 2019 team used double-wrapped coils for exactly this reason. Yet no control is metaphysical. A sham can match one artifact and miss another. Independent construction matters because a second apparatus will have different mechanical and electrical imperfections. If the same geomagnetic asymmetry reappears in a different room, the biological explanation strengthens sharply.

There is also an ecological hypothesis hidden in the asymmetry. The subjects were largely from the Northern Hemisphere, where the Earth's magnetic field points downward into the ground. The absence of comparable responses to upward-directed configurations was interpreted as evidence that the system is tuned to fields encountered during life. That raises an obvious test: recruit participants raised in the Southern Hemisphere, where inclination points upward relative to the local surface, and predict a mirrored sensitivity.

That experiment is more valuable than another hundred Northern Hemisphere participants because it converts a post hoc ecological story into a prospective geographic prediction. If developmental calibration matters, migration history may matter too. Someone raised in Sydney and living in California might carry one tuning profile, adapt to another, or show neither. The time scale itself becomes measurable.

The clockwise/counterclockwise asymmetry also forces the field to confront coordinate systems. Does the receptor care about head-centered rotation, body-centered rotation, gravitational vertical, retinal coordinates, or geographic north? Rotate the participant physically while holding the laboratory field sequence fixed and those frames come apart. Tilt the head. Change the initial azimuth. Use the same vector trajectory while reversing the relation to gravity. Each manipulation removes one family of explanations.

This is how a weak effect becomes a scientific object. Not by collecting more examples of the original pattern, but by asking what transformations preserve it.

A genuine magnetic sense should have invariances and dependencies. An equipment artifact will have its own. The art of the experiment is to make those symmetries disagree.
