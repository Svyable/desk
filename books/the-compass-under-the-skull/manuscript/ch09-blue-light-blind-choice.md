# Blue Light, Blind Choice

The 2022 Scientific Reports paper by Kwon-Seok Chae and colleagues is difficult to fit neatly beside the 2019 EEG result, which is exactly why it matters.

The researchers used a behavioral orientation paradigm rather than passive EEG. Participants were exposed to controlled magnetic conditions and asked to make directional choices. The reported effect depended on blue light reaching the eyes. Alter the spectral condition and the magnetic orientation weakened or disappeared. Add weak radiofrequency magnetic fields near the relevant resonance frequency and the response changed again. Those are not generic “magnetic fields affect people” observations. They are mechanism-shaped observations.

A radical-pair compass should care about photochemistry. If the receptor depends on photoexcited cryptochrome, darkness is not just an inconvenience; it removes the initiating energy source. Wavelength matters because flavin-based photochemistry has an action spectrum. Weak RF fields can perturb spin dynamics at specific frequencies and orientations. The 2022 study therefore tried to interrogate the mechanism through the peculiar sensitivities of radical-pair chemistry.

This approach has pedigree in bird research. Weak radiofrequency noise has disrupted magnetic orientation in migratory birds under certain conditions, a result difficult to reconcile with a simple mechanical magnetite compass. The human study borrowed that logic and pushed it into a forced-choice task.

The danger is equally familiar. Behavioral effects are vulnerable to expectation, subtle cues, flexible scoring and participant heterogeneity. A magnetic study with light changes introduces additional variables: visual adaptation, arousal, circadian effects, pupil size and possible awareness of illumination condition. RF equipment can introduce sound or heat. A resonance-looking effect is impressive only if neighboring frequencies, sham RF and apparatus controls behave as predicted.

The study reported food-context-related group differences in orientation tendency, another intriguing and potentially fragile detail. If motivational state genuinely gates access to magnetic information, it may tell us something about how an ancestral system was used. It may also reflect post hoc subdivision in a noisy behavioral dataset. The next experiment should not admire the pattern. It should preregister it.

The strongest result would be transfer. Classify an individual under one condition, then predict that person's behavior under a second mechanistically linked condition. If blue-light dependence and RF sensitivity travel together within the same participants, the radical-pair interpretation gains coherence. If only group-level significance appears after different exclusions and subdivisions, the mechanism story weakens.

There is another reason to care about the blue-light result. It supplies a direct conflict with the magnetite interpretation of the EEG data. The 2019 response was reported under conditions the authors considered inconsistent with a light-dependent radical-pair compass. The 2022 behavior appears to require light and show RF resonance effects. If both are real, either the experiments are measuring different magnetic pathways or one mechanism inference is wrong.

This is where scientific storytelling usually becomes dishonest. A review eager to establish human magnetoreception can cite both papers as independent confirmation and glide past the fact that they disagree about how the supposed sense works. A skeptical review can cite the disagreement as proof that the entire field is incoherent. Both waste information.

Mechanism disagreement is useful when it yields a cross-test. Run the 2019 EEG stimulus in blue light and darkness while applying the 2022 RF perturbation. Run the 2022 behavioral task while measuring alpha-band EEG. Reverse magnetic polarity while holding inclination constant. Separate retinal illumination from general blue-light exposure. Predefine all exclusions, responder rules and primary outcomes.

Then let the mechanisms fail publicly.

If blue light and resonance perturbations alter both EEG and behavior, the radical-pair model advances. If EEG remains light-independent and polarity-sensitive while behavior remains light- and RF-dependent, the multiple-pathway hypothesis becomes concrete. If one effect disappears under preregistered replication, the field becomes simpler. If both disappear, that result should be published with the same seriousness as the originals.

The 2022 study is therefore not valuable because it settles human magnetoreception. It is valuable because it makes a different physical bet.
