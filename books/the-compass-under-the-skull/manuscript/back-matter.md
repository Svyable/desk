# Back Matter

The evidentiary rule used in this book is intentionally asymmetric. A surprising mechanism claim has to earn each bridge separately. Magnetite in human tissue does not establish a magnetite receptor. Human CRY2 supporting magnetosensitivity in a transgenic fly does not establish a human cryptochrome compass. An EEG response does not establish conscious perception. Above-chance directional behavior does not identify the receptor. A molecular mechanism in a bird does not transfer automatically to a human.

The reverse errors are equally important. Failure to identify the human receptor does not make animal magnetoreception fictional. A disputed behavioral paper does not erase open EEG data. A pollution source for some brain magnetite does not prove that all human magnetite is exogenous. Mechanism disagreement does not by itself falsify the underlying phenomenon.

The research files accompanying this manuscript separate four classes of evidence and preserve links to the strongest primary material used in the first pass. The manuscript should not be treated as publication-ready until those source traces are page-indexed where possible, the human studies are reconstructed at protocol level, independent replication and criticism are exhaustively searched, and each chapter reaches Desk's substantive length gate without padding.

The single most important open task is not another literature review. It is a preregistered cross-mechanism replication in which magnetite-like and radical-pair-like predictions are tested in the same participants and the same apparatus across more than one laboratory.

If that experiment fails cleanly, the book should contract around why the earlier evidence looked persuasive. If it succeeds, the field becomes much stranger and much easier to study.
