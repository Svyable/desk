# Evidence Matrix

## Class 1 — verified / documented

- Magnetoreception is experimentally established in multiple animal taxa, though mechanisms differ by species and task.
- Human brain tissue contains magnetite; the 1992 PNAS paper documented ferromagnetic magnetite in postmortem tissue.
- Some human brain magnetite is morphologically consistent with combustion-derived pollution; Maher et al. 2016 documented an exogenous particle population.
- Human CRY2 can support light-dependent magnetosensitivity when expressed in a Drosophila assay; this establishes molecular capability in that heterologous system, not a human sensory organ.
- Purified CRY4 from migratory European robins shows magnetically sensitive photochemistry in vitro, strengthening radical-pair plausibility in birds.
- Wang et al. 2019 reported direction- and geometry-selective alpha-band EEG responses to Earth-strength magnetic-field rotations and made data/scripts public.
- Chae et al. 2022 reported light-dependent human magnetic orientation with RF-field dependencies in their behavioral paradigm.

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete

- The 2019 EEG effect reflects a genuine human sensory transduction pathway rather than an apparatus/analysis artifact.
- The 2022 behavioral effect reflects genuine geomagnetic orientation rather than subtle cueing, analytic flexibility or unstable participant effects.
- Polarity sensitivity in the 2019 result favors ferromagnetic transduction.
- Blue-light and RF dependencies in the 2022 result favor radical-pair chemistry.
- Individual “responder” phenotypes are stable within people and biologically meaningful.

## Class 3 — disputed / unconfirmed

- Humans possess a consciously accessible magnetic compass.
- A specific magnetite-containing human receptor organ has been anatomically identified.
- Human CRY2 or another human cryptochrome is the in vivo geomagnetic receptor.
- The 2019 and 2022 effects have been independently replicated across multiple non-originating laboratories under preregistration.
- Historical human homing claims establish reliable magnetic navigation.

## Class 4 — speculative synthesis / open conjecture

- Humans may retain two separable magnetic pathways: a polarity-sensitive ferromagnetic pathway and a light-dependent radical-pair pathway.
- Human magnetic sensitivity may be developmentally calibrated to local geomagnetic inclination.
- A magnetic pathway may survive below awareness after losing most adaptive navigational value.
- Modern magnetic/RF noise or exogenous magnetite pollution may alter a weak ancestral system.

## Defeat conditions

The manuscript's central thesis should materially weaken if adequately powered, preregistered, independent studies fail to reproduce both the 2019 EEG signature and the 2022 light/RF behavioral signature under faithful protocols. The multi-pathway synthesis should be abandoned unless a within-subject factorial design demonstrates reproducible dissociation between mechanism-specific manipulations. The magnetite interpretation should narrow if the EEG effect proves light/RF dependent in the way radical-pair chemistry predicts. The radical-pair interpretation should narrow if the behavioral effect survives darkness and lacks frequency-specific RF sensitivity in independent tests.
