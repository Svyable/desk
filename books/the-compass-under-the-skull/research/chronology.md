# Chronology

- **1970s–1980s** — Human magnetic-orientation and homing experiments, including work by Robin Baker and others, attract attention and controversy; replication is uneven and methodological disputes persist.
- **1978 onward** — Radical-pair ideas for biological magnetoreception are developed in physical and chemical models, later linked to cryptochrome photochemistry.
- **1992** — Kirschvink, Kobayashi-Kirschvink and Woodford report magnetite biomineralization in human brain tissue in *PNAS*.
- **2000** — Ritz, Adem and Schulten publish a photoreceptor-based radical-pair model for avian magnetoreception.
- **2004** — Ritz and colleagues report radiofrequency disruption of magnetic compass orientation in European robins, strengthening radical-pair mechanism arguments.
- **2011** — Foley, Gegear and Reppert report that human CRY2 restores light-dependent magnetosensitivity in cryptochrome-deficient Drosophila.
- **2016** — Maher and colleagues report abundant combustion-derived magnetite nanoparticles in human brain tissue, complicating provenance of brain magnetite.
- **2019** — Wang, Hilburn, Shimojo, Kirschvink and colleagues report direction-selective alpha-band EEG responses to Earth-strength magnetic-field rotations in humans; they argue the response pattern is more consistent with ferromagnetic transduction than a radical-pair inclination compass.
- **2021** — Xu and colleagues report magnetically sensitive photochemistry in CRY4 from migratory European robins, with stronger sensitivity than homologues from non-migratory chicken and pigeon in their in-vitro system.
- **2022** — Chae and colleagues report human magnetic orientation dependent on blue light and weak radiofrequency manipulations, arguing for a light- and magnetic-resonance-dependent mechanism.
- **2026 publication-state question** — The central unresolved issue remains whether the modern human physiological and behavioral findings reproduce independently and whether their apparently conflicting mechanism signatures can be resolved in one preregistered experimental framework.
