# Meister 2016 arithmetic audit

## Why this audit exists

Chapter 1 currently says the ferritin particle was physically too weak, but deliberately holds the numerical gap until the original calculation is checked. This note closes that narrow source burden without turning one critique into a verdict on every later ferritin/RF system.

## Primary source checked

Markus Meister, “Physical limits to magnetogenetics,” *eLife* 5:e17210 (Version of Record, 8 September 2016): https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.17210 and https://elifesciences.org/articles/17210

The paper analyzes several then-prominent magnetogenetic proposals, including the TRPV4–ferritin construct reported by Wheeler et al. and the ferritin/heating proposal associated with Stanley et al. Its abstract gives the overall discrepancy as roughly **5–10 orders of magnitude**, depending on the proposed mechanism.

## The cleanest number for Chapter 1

For the field-gradient mechanism applied to the Wheeler et al. construct, Meister uses approximately **0.05 T** field strength and **6.6–7 T/m** gradient. His Equation 10 gives a force on one ferritin complex of about **7 × 10^-23 N**.

He compares that with a directly measured force scale of about **2 × 10^-13 N** for force-sensitive channels in auditory hair cells. On those assumptions, the ferritin-gradient force is roughly **3 billion times smaller** than that comparator, i.e. about **9.5 orders of magnitude** below it. Meister summarizes the mechanism as “at least 9 log units too weak.”

This is the strongest concrete arithmetic object for the opening because the assumptions and comparator are visible. The manuscript should not flatten the whole paper into “ferritin is nine orders too weak” without naming the mechanism: the paper evaluates several force, torque, thermal and molecular-compass proposals and the gap changes with the route.

## Heating boundary

Meister separately argues that native ferritin is a poor nanoscale heater. The iron core is only about **5–6 nm** across, below the size range normally useful for magnetic hyperthermia, and ferritin’s ferric-hydroxide core has much lower magnetic susceptibility than magnetite. His broader conclusion is that the then-proposed ferritin pulling/heating routes could not account for the reported observations under the published conditions.

Do not convert this into a universal statement that RF can never perturb a ferritin-linked biological system. Meister explicitly leaves the logical possibility that an observed phenomenon, if real, could have a different mechanism. That sentence is unusually important for *IRON RADIO* because it is the hinge between the failed direct force/heating story and the later FeRIC biochemical proposal.

## Strongest counterargument / scope limit

The hair-cell gating force is a comparator, not a measurement of the exact TRPV4–ferritin construct’s gating threshold. Later theory also explored more favorable magnetomechanical/magnetothermal scenarios under changed assumptions. Those points can matter to mechanism design, but they do not erase the specific 2016 calculation under the Wheeler experimental parameters.

More importantly, Meister 2016 cannot validate or invalidate the later FeRIC iron/ROS/oxidized-lipid mechanism by itself. A biochemical amplification route changes the causal chain being tested. It therefore acquires a new burden: direct evidence for field-dependent iron handling, ROS/lipid changes, channel dependence, electrophysiological effect, thermal/artifact controls, and independent replication. A replacement mechanism is not a retroactive rescue of an old dataset.

## Manuscript-safe claim

A precise version suitable for Chapter 1 after editorial integration is:

> In Meister’s 2016 calculation using the field and gradient reported for the Wheeler construct, the force on a ferritin complex was about 7 × 10^-23 newtons. His comparison force for mechanically gating a sensory ion channel was about 2 × 10^-13 newtons: a gap of more than nine orders of magnitude. That calculation attacks the proposed tugging mechanism under those conditions; it does not by itself prove that every reported biological response was imaginary or that every later ferritin-linked mechanism is impossible.

The exact wording can be tightened to match the chapter, but the mechanism qualifier should survive.

## What still needs checking before broader numerical claims

- Meister’s separate dipole-interaction, torque and heating calculations if Chapter 4 gives a mechanism-by-mechanism quantitative table.
- Wheeler et al.’s original supplementary field/gradient measurements rather than relying only on Meister’s extraction.
- Stanley et al.’s exact RF parameters and thermal assumptions before assigning its claim a numerical gap.
- Later theoretical replies, including Barbic 2019, when the manuscript compares whether changed particle properties or field regimes can make a ferritin mechanism physically plausible.
- The 2020 and 2024 FeRIC papers at methods/results level before the book states the magnitude, latency, pathway specificity or generality of the biochemical/electrophysiological effect.

## Editorial consequence

The useful narrative is sharper than “physics said no, biology said yes.” Physics said that particular direct-energy stories were off by enormous margins. If a later biological effect survives, it has to earn a different causal route experimentally. That keeps the arithmetic devastating without letting it do work outside its assumptions.
