## Counterevidence pass

The strongest countermodel is mundane and serious: early positive results may be artifacts or laboratory-specific biology; later mechanistic work may describe a different engineered system rather than rescue the original claims. The manuscript must preserve this possibility all the way through Chapter 18.

A second countermodel is generic stress/oxidation: if RF apparatus changes temperature, redox chemistry, membrane state or measurement electronics independent of ferritin coupling, a biochemical response can be real without being the claimed receiver mechanism.

A third is construct pathology: overexpression or ferritin coupling may create an artificial sensitivity with little relevance to ordinary nervous systems. Even successful magnetogenetics would not imply that unmodified human brains are magnetic receivers.

## Proposed killing experiment

Freeze a single construct sequence, cell line and culture protocol across at least three laboratories. Central calibration distributes RF field maps and dummy loads. Preregister primary electrophysiology and pathway endpoints. Include construct-negative, channel-only, ferritin-only and sham conditions; matched bulk-temperature trajectories; blinded exposure labels; iron chelation/loading perturbations; ROS/lipid-pathway perturbations; and a rescue condition. Raw traces and exclusions are committed before unblinding.

The important outcome is not `positive` or `negative`. It is whether the causal chain closes. If RF changes the endpoint only in the predicted construct/pathway conditions, with dose response and pathway interruption/rescue, the case strengthens. If effects track apparatus, laboratory, temperature or generic stress instead, it collapses toward artifact.

## Book-level distinction

This is not another generic weak-field biology book. Natural magnetoreception asks how evolved organisms detect environmental magnetic information. IRON RADIO asks whether engineers can construct an RF-sensitive cellular actuator, and what happens epistemically when the first proposed transduction physics fails. The distinction must remain explicit wherever cryptochrome or animal magnetoreception appears.
