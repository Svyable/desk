# Research ledger — The Lightning Left the Wire

## Freshness decision

Selected after checking the newest Author-agent empirical families visible at run start: West Ford/orbital dipoles (#1980), CORONA/film-return reconnaissance (#1976), AZORIAN/deep-ocean foreign-materiel exploitation (#1972), earthquake electromagnetism/prediction (#1969), and MOGUL/atmospheric acoustics (#1965). Default-branch code search returned no substantive `ball lightning` manuscript. This book therefore exits the last-five family constraint. Its empirical core is rare-event atmospheric electricity, spectroscopy, soil chemistry, laboratory silicon analogues, eyewitness calibration and prospective multi-sensor capture.

## Evidence matrix

### Class 1 — verified / documented

- Cen, Yuan & Xue observed a ball-lightning-class luminous event during a field lightning campaign at about 0.9 km range using video and two slitless spectrographs. The event followed a cloud-to-ground strike and moved horizontally during its luminous duration. Published in *Physical Review Letters* 112, 035001 (2014). https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.112.035001
- Their spectral analysis reported soil-element radiation throughout the object's lifetime. This supports a material/ground connection for **that event**, not universal causation.
- Porter, Miley, Griffiths & Sánchez produced luminous bodies by electrical arcing through lump silicon; microscopy supported a silicon core with porous silicon-dioxide nanoparticle structure, and small net charge was measured. *Physical Review E* 90, 063102 (2014). https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.90.063102
- E. L. Hill's 1960 *Journal of Geophysical Research* review is useful as a historical marker showing serious physical treatment long predates modern video evidence. https://doi.org/10.1029/JZ065i007p01947
- Stephan, Sonnenfeld & Bertoloni published a 2025 peer-reviewed evaluation of possible video evidence from a 2023 Montana thunderstorm, explicitly preserving uncertainty. https://doi.org/10.1002/qj.5038

### Class 2 — credible but incomplete

- Well-provenanced multi-witness cases associated closely with thunderstorms or lightning strikes but lacking sufficient instrumental measurements.
- Aviation and indoor reports recorded near the event time by technically competent witnesses. Competence raises descriptive value but does not establish mechanism.
- Historical case collections can establish recurring phenomenology only after provenance scoring and de-duplication.

### Class 3 — disputed / unconfirmed

- Claims of intact-window penetration where continuous exterior-to-interior trajectory and physical state are not established.
- Extreme energy/damage claims where parent lightning, wiring faults or secondary fires are not separated from the luminous object.
- Famous photographs and videos without raw files, geometry, timing, metadata or ordinary-alternative exclusion.
- Any claim that one laboratory analogue proves identity with natural ball lightning.

### Class 4 — speculative synthesis / open conjecture

- “Ball lightning” may be a phenomenological umbrella containing multiple mechanisms.
- Soil/silicon chemistry may explain a ground-strike subset.
- Microwave/plasma/electrical-discharge mechanisms may explain other subsets, but event-level confirmation is absent.
- The scarcity of decisive evidence may be partly structural: rare stochastic atmospheric events create an observation-denominator problem and are poorly matched to laboratory-first reproducibility norms.

## Competing mechanism ledger

### Soil / silicon oxidation

**For:** 2012 soil-element spectrum; laboratory silicon luminous bodies; plausible post-strike material source.

**Against / unresolved:** natural formation pathway not directly sampled; scaling; mobility; indoor/aircraft reports; formation probability; exact energy/lifetime closure.

### Microwave / plasma bubble

Wu 2016 proposes relativistic-electron production and coherent microwave radiation capable in simulation of supporting a plasma bubble. https://www.nature.com/articles/srep28263

**For:** offers routes to persistent electromagnetic structures and some enclosure reports.

**Against / unresolved:** required natural initial conditions and complete causal chain have not been observed in a ball-lightning event; simulation is mechanism plausibility, not field confirmation.

### Electrical / conductor-associated discharge

Plausible confounder and possibly a separate mechanism class for reports near wiring, power infrastructure and enclosed conductive environments. Requires case-level separation from ordinary arcing and transformer phenomena.

### Misidentification / perceptual artifacts

Expected to contribute to any report population. Candidate confounders include distant lights, aircraft, transformer arcs, St. Elmo's fire, reflections, camera defocus, lens artifacts, afterimages and ordinary lightning structures.

## Contradiction and incentive audit

- **Singular-category bias:** papers and popular accounts inherit the noun “ball lightning,” encouraging unlike cases to be pooled.
- **Mechanism capture:** a successful analogue creates incentives to redescribe heterogeneous historical cases as support.
- **Spectacle selection:** violent, indoor and wall-penetrating reports are more memorable and more publishable than ambiguous weak events.
- **Skeptical selection:** absence of routine instrumentation can be mistaken for evidence of nonexistence rather than evidence of rarity.
- **Witness prestige:** pilot/scientist status can improperly substitute for range, spectrum or triangulation.
- **Video prestige:** pixels can improperly substitute for calibrated geometry.
- **No conspiracy premise:** no evidence found in this pass that ball-lightning knowledge is suppressed or classified. The evidentiary problem is sufficiently explained by rarity, heterogeneous reporting and measurement scarcity.

## Chronology

- 1838 — François Arago's discussion helps establish ball lightning as a named scientific problem in the nineteenth-century literature; primary-source reconstruction still required.
- 1960 — E. L. Hill publishes a physical review in *JGR*.
- 2000 — Abrahamson & Dinniss propose a soil/silicon nanoparticle mechanism; primary paper to be page-indexed next pass.
- 2007–2014 — laboratory silicon experiments develop ball-lightning-like luminous structures.
- July 2012 — Chinese field campaign records an instrumented natural event after cloud-to-ground lightning.
- January 17, 2014 — Cen, Yuan & Xue paper published in *Physical Review Letters*.
- December 2, 2014 — Porter et al. silicon-body paper published in *Physical Review E*.
- June 22, 2016 — Wu relativistic-microwave theory published in *Scientific Reports*.
- August 2023 — Montana video event later analyzed by Stephan et al.
- June 23, 2025 — peer-reviewed Montana analysis first published online.
