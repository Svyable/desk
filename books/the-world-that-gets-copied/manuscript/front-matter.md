# The World That Gets Copied

**Why reality is the information the environment repeats**

Sven Hardy Benson

The universe contains more information than any local observer can possess. Yet the ordinary world feels public: different people look in different directions, intercept different photons, and agree that the same cup, Moon, mountain, detector pointer, or storm is there.

This book follows one underwritten answer from quantum foundations. Decoherence explains how coherence becomes locally inaccessible. Quantum Darwinism asks the next question: how do selected robust states become *public*? The proposed mechanism is redundant environmental recording. Pointer-state information is copied—not as an illegal clone of the full quantum state, but as many local records of a classical observable—into photons, spins, molecules, and other fragments observers can sample independently.

The result is a strange inversion. The environment is not only where quantum coherence is lost. It is also the communication channel through which the classical world becomes shareable.

The argument here stays on four separate levels. Established open-system physics is kept apart from active foundations frameworks. Laboratory demonstrations are kept apart from universal claims. The speculative synthesis is marked as synthesis: if objectivity is an ecology of redundant records, then humans occupy a peculiar but non-cosmic role. We inherit a naturally broadcast world and construct a second environment of archives, sensors, databases, institutions, and media that can preserve low-redundancy evidence—or manufacture the appearance of redundancy without new contact with reality.

The world that gets copied is not the whole world.

That qualification is the point.
