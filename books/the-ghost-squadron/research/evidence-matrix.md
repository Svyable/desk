# Evidence Matrix

## Class 1 — verified, declassified, documented

- CIA-published declassified account by Gene Poteat states that Project PALLADIUM generated calibrated false targets in Soviet radars by receiving, delaying, and retransmitting radar signals.
- The same account states that PALLADIUM teams used NSA/SIGINT feedback and military support, and describes operations from ground bases, ships, and submarines.
- The account describes a Cuban missile crisis operation using a destroyer-mounted system and submarine-released metallized spheres for calibration.
- Official U.S. histories document WWII radar deception, Vietnam-era jamming/EW, Wild Weasel tactics, and contemporary electromagnetic deception doctrine.
- Public GAO records document MALD's intended role as an aircraft-simulating decoy designed to provoke and occupy air defenses.
- Public DAU guidance describes DRFM as a radar spoofing technique based on coherent capture and retransmission.

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete

- Participant recollections and retrospective histories about exact performance, success rates, and operational reach where the full underlying mission record is not public.
- General inferences about how particular Soviet/Cuban operator reactions mapped onto exact receiver thresholds without the full raw SIGINT record.

## Class 3 — disputed or unsupported allegations

- Claims that a specific modern UAP event was a PALLADIUM-like classified deception without event-specific technical or documentary evidence.
- Claims that current classified systems can inject arbitrary objects into all modern multi-sensor networks.
- Claims of uninterrupted program lineage from PALLADIUM to any named contemporary system without public documentation.

## Class 4 — speculative synthesis

- The proposal that some military anomaly events may be deliberate probes intended to elicit defensive network reactions.
- The idea that future anomaly observatories should be red-teamed with calibrated electronic ghosts, physical decoys, and corrupted metadata as a standard validation method.
- The broader framing of adversary sensor networks as remotely interrogable experimental instruments.

## Contradictions and disinformation incentives

Electronic-warfare organizations benefit from ambiguity about capability. Deterrence can reward adversaries for overestimating spoofing reach; secrecy can also conceal mundane limitations. Participants may remember successful operations more vividly than failed ones. UAP communities may over-apply a real historical program to unrelated events, while institutional skeptics may invoke generic spoofability without showing a compatible mechanism. Both moves collapse evidence classes.

The manuscript therefore treats PALLADIUM as an existence proof of a specific historical capability and method, not as a universal explanation.
