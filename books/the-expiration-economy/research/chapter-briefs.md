# Chapter Research Briefs

These briefs are working research controls for *The Expiration Economy*. They are not chapter outlines. Their purpose is to keep each chapter anchored to a distinct empirical job, preserve counterevidence, and prevent a broad metaphor about endings from becoming a universal explanation.

## Chapter 1 — Everything Outlives Its Welcome

**Job:** Establish the asymmetry between creating and ending without pretending it is new in every domain.

**Evidence to deepen:** Global e-waste growth; long-tail maintenance; examples where obsolete assets remain costly after usefulness falls.

**Counterevidence / falsification:** Durable assets can become more valuable with age. If most apparent "legacy" stock is economically rational to retain, the opening must narrow its claim from persistence in general to unmanaged persistence.

**Avoid:** A montage of dead apps, abandoned gadgets, and old laws with no measured consequence.

**Handoff:** Move from visible clutter to the less visible production systems that cannot simply be switched off.

## Chapter 2 — The Graveyard in Production

**Job:** Show why legacy software survives inside active organizations: dependencies, undocumented interfaces, customer expectations, data formats, regulatory obligations, and migration risk.

**Evidence to deepen:** Protocol and software deprecation cases; enterprise migration costs; examples where old systems remain because the replacement risk is larger than the maintenance burden.

**Counterevidence / falsification:** Old systems are not automatically bad systems. A boring stable system may be cheaper and safer than a rewrite. If migration failures dominate legacy failures, the chapter should emphasize selective retirement rather than modernization ideology.

**Avoid:** Treating age as technical debt.

**Handoff:** Once software persists, so does the data produced by it.

## Chapter 3 — Permanent by Default

**Job:** Distinguish deliberate retention from accumulation caused by cheap storage and weak disposition rules.

**Evidence to deepen:** GDPR Article 17; NARA retention schedules; NIST media sanitization; litigation and accountability reasons to retain records.

**Counterevidence / falsification:** Deletion can destroy legal rights, institutional memory, scientific reproducibility, safety evidence, or history. If the chapter cannot specify who has authority to delete and what must survive, it is not ready.

**Avoid:** Equating "right to erasure" with a universal right to delete all records.

**Handoff:** Data reveals a governance problem that law has long faced: rules are easy to enact and politically difficult to unwind.

## Chapter 4 — Laws Without Funerals

**Job:** Examine repeal, sunset provisions, periodic reauthorization, and the institutional interests that form around rules.

**Evidence to deepen:** Congressional Research Service description of sunset provisions; GAO work on sunset review; examples where sunsets force review and examples where they are routinely extended.

**Counterevidence / falsification:** Frequent reauthorization may reflect continuing value rather than inertia. Sunset mechanisms can create uncertainty and lobbying cycles without improving policy.

**Avoid:** Claiming that every law should expire automatically.

**Handoff:** The same power asymmetry appears in commercial products when a producer's desire to leave conflicts with a user's dependence.

## Chapter 5 — The Product That Will Not Leave

**Job:** Put product end-of-life inside a customer-rights and lifecycle-cost frame.

**Evidence to deepen:** Device and network shutdowns; software support periods; e-waste; repair, export, interoperability, and data portability.

**Counterevidence / falsification:** Indefinite support can be unsafe, economically impossible, or block security improvements. The chapter should not imply that a vendor owes eternal service.

**Avoid:** Treating all obsolescence as planned obsolescence.

**Handoff:** Human systems struggle with endings partly because we often think survival is the only healthy state. Biology complicates that intuition.

## Chapter 6 — Cells Know When to Die

**Job:** Explain apoptosis accurately enough to show that regulated removal is part of organismal maintenance.

**Evidence to deepen:** Susan Elmore's review of apoptosis; regulated cell death literature; role in development, immune function, and tissue homeostasis.

**Counterevidence / falsification:** Cellular death is not institutional policy. Dysregulated cell death can itself cause disease. The analogy fails wherever it is used to justify human destruction without an equivalent regulatory mechanism.

**Avoid:** "Nature proves companies must die."

**Handoff:** Move from microscopic turnover to ecological processes in which removal, decomposition, and disturbance alter the whole system.

## Chapter 7 — Fire, Rot, and Renewal

**Job:** Show that ecosystems sometimes depend on disturbance and decomposition while preserving ecological context.

**Evidence to deepen:** U.S. Forest Service prescribed-fire guidance; decomposition and nutrient cycling; regimes where fire suppression increases fuel loads.

**Counterevidence / falsification:** Fire is not universally beneficial; prescribed burns carry risk; ecosystems differ radically. If the argument requires "destruction is good," it has become too crude.

**Avoid:** Romanticizing wildfire or converting ecology into a business slogan.

**Handoff:** Biological and ecological removal lead to a subtler case: the mind itself does not retain everything it encounters.

## Chapter 8 — Forgetting Is a Function

**Job:** Use memory research to show that forgetting can be adaptive, then sharply separate human memory from institutional deletion.

**Evidence to deepen:** Nørby's review of adaptive forgetting; Ryan and Frankland on adaptive engram plasticity; Anderson and Hulbert on active forgetting.

**Counterevidence / falsification:** Forgetting can also be harmful. Institutional memory is not a brain, and automated deletion can eliminate evidence rather than improve cognition.

**Avoid:** "The brain deletes data to stay efficient" as a literal technical claim.

**Handoff:** The legal system offers a more explicit engineered form of release: discharge after financial failure.

## Chapter 9 — The Fresh Start

**Job:** Examine bankruptcy as a formal mechanism for ending unpayable obligations while preserving rules about what cannot be escaped.

**Evidence to deepen:** U.S. Courts Bankruptcy Basics; discharge rules; reorganization versus liquidation.

**Counterevidence / falsification:** A discharge is not universal and many debts or liens can survive. Bankruptcy can impose losses on creditors, employees, communities, and suppliers.

**Avoid:** Using bankruptcy as a moral metaphor detached from legal procedure.

**Handoff:** Finance is full of instruments whose duration is specified in advance; that makes time itself part of the contract.

## Chapter 10 — Markets With Clocks

**Job:** Show how maturity, expiration, renewal, and time-bounded claims constrain open-ended obligation.

**Evidence to deepen:** Bond maturity, options expiration, leases, certificates, time-limited rights, and renewal pricing.

**Counterevidence / falsification:** Expiration can create rollover risk, cliff effects, and short-termism. Perpetual instruments also have legitimate uses.

**Avoid:** Pretending every market works better with a hard stop.

**Handoff:** The useful question is not whether everything should expire, but what indefinite survival costs when nobody prices the tail.

## Chapter 11 — The Forever Tax

**Job:** Name the recurring cost categories created by persistence: maintenance, compatibility, security, monitoring, staffing, compliance, cognitive load, and opportunity cost.

**Evidence to deepen:** RFC 8996 maintenance and attack-surface rationale; legacy support cases; credential and account sprawl.

**Counterevidence / falsification:** Replacement also has costs. If a legacy system's marginal support cost is low and migration risk is high, the "forever tax" can be smaller than the exit tax.

**Avoid:** Unsupported percentages for technical debt or maintenance spending.

**Handoff:** In physical systems the tail becomes visible because steel, concrete, fuel, contamination, and land cannot be hidden in a backlog.

## Chapter 12 — Ghost Infrastructure

**Job:** Make end-of-life liability physical through decommissioning.

**Evidence to deepen:** NRC reactor decommissioning process; funding and license termination; e-waste; other regulated infrastructure where closure has procedural stages.

**Counterevidence / falsification:** Infrastructure can be repowered, repurposed, mothballed, or preserved. Decommissioning is not always immediate or optimal.

**Avoid:** Treating abandoned infrastructure and regulated decommissioning as the same thing.

**Handoff:** Institutions can become ghost infrastructure too: still staffed, funded, and governed after the original mission weakens.

## Chapter 13 — Zombie Organizations

**Job:** Examine organizational persistence without using "zombie" as an insult for every mature institution.

**Evidence to deepen:** Temporary commissions; program review; corporate dissolution; mission drift; institutional succession.

**Counterevidence / falsification:** Continuity protects expertise, rights, relationships, and public capacity. A mission can evolve legitimately rather than expire.

**Avoid:** Assuming age or bureaucracy proves uselessness.

**Handoff:** Organizations reveal a records problem: ending a function does not mean destroying all evidence of it.

## Chapter 14 — Archive, Delete, Destroy

**Job:** Establish lifecycle states that are often collapsed into the word "delete."

**Evidence to deepen:** NARA records schedules; NIST media sanitization; permanent versus temporary records; transfer to archives; certificates of sanitization.

**Counterevidence / falsification:** Destruction that is too early can violate law or erase accountability. Archival retention can itself create privacy and security costs.

**Avoid:** Technical claims that logical deletion equals physical erasure.

**Handoff:** Protocol standards show how a community can publicly announce that a once-valid thing should stop being supported.

## Chapter 15 — Deprecation Is a Public Service

**Job:** Treat deprecation as an active governance function, not neglect.

**Evidence to deepen:** RFC 8996 deprecation of TLS 1.0/1.1; security and maintenance rationale; acknowledged interoperability consequences.

**Counterevidence / falsification:** Deprecation can strand users and force costly upgrades. If compatibility burdens fall mostly on weak users while benefits accrue to vendors, the public-service framing needs qualification.

**Avoid:** "Old equals insecure."

**Handoff:** AI changes the scale of this problem because it makes creation dramatically easier than lifecycle governance.

## Chapter 16 — Creation Without Friction

**Job:** Connect generative AI to a measurable rise in artifact creation and therefore potential lifecycle load.

**Evidence to deepen:** AI adoption; code generation; agent creation; declining marginal creation cost; enterprise governance responses.

**Counterevidence / falsification:** AI can also reduce cleanup costs by documenting, testing, migrating, classifying, and deleting old artifacts. If automation retires things as quickly as it creates them, the scarcity thesis weakens.

**Avoid:** Assuming every generated artifact enters production.

**Handoff:** The sharpest case is not generated text but software that can retain authority after the human who launched it stops paying attention.

## Chapter 17 — Agents That Never Go Home

**Job:** Define the lifecycle problem of persistent agents: identity, credentials, schedules, memory, delegations, downstream effects, and ownership changes.

**Evidence to deepen:** AI lifecycle standards; service-account governance; credential rotation and revocation; task schedulers and delegated access.

**Counterevidence / falsification:** Many agents are ephemeral and sandboxed. Do not extrapolate persistent-agent risk to every model call or chatbot interaction.

**Avoid:** Science-fiction scenarios unsupported by deployed system architecture.

**Handoff:** If an agent can outlive a session, the retirement of the model or intelligence behind it becomes a genuine systems problem.

## Chapter 18 — How to Retire an Intelligence

**Job:** Turn AI decommissioning into concrete lifecycle work.

**Evidence to deepen:** NIST AI 600-1 decommissioning guidance; retention/security; dependencies; open-source components; user attachment; audit evidence.

**Counterevidence / falsification:** Retirement can reduce accountability if model versions, evaluations, logs, or incident evidence are destroyed. Safe decommissioning may require preservation as well as shutdown.

**Avoid:** Conflating model retirement with deleting weights.

**Handoff:** Once ending requires specialized procedures across domains, ending capacity itself may become scarce.

## Chapter 19 — The Scarcity of Ending

**Job:** State the economic thesis in falsifiable terms.

**Evidence to deepen:** Rising stocks of software, data, devices, AI artifacts, and infrastructure; labor and market functions devoted to lifecycle management.

**Counterevidence / falsification:** Cleanup may remain a routine maintenance function rather than a distinct economic scarcity. Lower AI costs could also automate retirement faster than accumulation.

**Avoid:** Unsupported claims that cleanup is already a trillion-dollar market.

**Handoff:** Scarcity becomes personal when ownership changes and a system's next steward inherits obligations they never chose.

## Chapter 20 — The Inheritance Problem

**Job:** Examine what survives a steward: accounts, keys, domains, digital assets, subscriptions, models, agents, records, and obligations.

**Evidence to deepen:** Revised Uniform Fiduciary Access to Digital Assets Act; digital estate practice; organizational offboarding and succession.

**Counterevidence / falsification:** Privacy does not vanish at death or incapacity, and fiduciary access is deliberately bounded. Inheritance mechanisms can overreach as easily as they can fail.

**Avoid:** Treating digital property, private communications, and delegated authority as one legal category.

**Handoff:** Inheritance exposes the value of reversibility: a successor needs the ability to change course without destroying everything first.

## Chapter 21 — Reversible by Design

**Job:** Examine design choices that preserve optionality: modularity, staged commitment, rollback, export, migration, revocation, and bounded pilots.

**Evidence to deepen:** Real-options logic; rollback and versioning practices; staged infrastructure or policy commitments.

**Counterevidence / falsification:** Reversibility can cost money, reduce performance, encourage indecision, and be impossible in domains with irreversible physical or social effects.

**Avoid:** Presenting reversibility as a universal virtue.

**Handoff:** A system can be technically reversible while a person remains permanently exposed through retained information.

## Chapter 22 — The Right to Disappear

**Job:** Test the moral and legal limits of persistent digital memory.

**Evidence to deepen:** GDPR Article 17 and its exceptions; de-indexing versus deletion; retention needed for legal claims, public interest, research, or records duties.

**Counterevidence / falsification:** Other people may have rights in the same records. Public accountability and history can outweigh an individual's preference for erasure.

**Avoid:** Claiming an absolute legal right to disappear.

**Handoff:** The same conflict appears in commerce when a producer wants to disappear but a remaining customer still depends on the product.

## Chapter 23 — The Last Customer

**Job:** Put the final user at the center of product shutdown economics.

**Evidence to deepen:** Network sunsets; device support; data export; migration; notice periods; security support and interoperability.

**Counterevidence / falsification:** A vendor cannot rationally support every product forever. Customers also bear some responsibility for migration when clear, reasonable exit paths exist.

**Avoid:** Moralizing every shutdown as betrayal.

**Handoff:** The friction in these endings creates demand for people and firms whose product is the exit itself.

## Chapter 24 — The Business of Cleanup

**Job:** Identify real economic functions created by end-of-life complexity.

**Evidence to deepen:** Secure media sanitization; records disposition; e-waste processing; cloud/software migration; infrastructure decommissioning; digital-estate services; AI lifecycle assurance.

**Counterevidence / falsification:** Some of these are established maintenance categories with new branding. The chapter should prove common economics before calling them one industry.

**Avoid:** Market-size theater and speculative TAM arithmetic.

**Handoff:** A market can sell cleanup after the fact, but the book's final question is whether the exit can be designed before the cleanup becomes a crisis.

## Chapter 25 — Design the Exit

**Job:** Synthesize recurring design questions without inventing a rigid framework.

**Evidence to deepen:** Ownership, review triggers, portability, revocation, retention/disposition, migration, proof of ending, succession, and tail funding across prior cases.

**Counterevidence / falsification:** Domain differences matter. A nuclear plant, a human memory, a TLS version, a statute, and an AI agent should not be forced into identical lifecycle rules.

**Avoid:** Ending with a numbered management system that flattens the book's complexity.

**Final note:** The conclusion should leave the reader with a changed default question: not only "How do we start this?" but "What will it take for someone else to end, transfer, or preserve this safely when its first purpose is gone?"
