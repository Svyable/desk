# Chapter 2 research audit — FMO coherence and functional use

Scope: one bounded evidence audit for Chapter 2, **“The Leaf That Started an Argument.”** The current chapter has the right instinct—do not turn photosynthetic spectroscopy into a claim that plants run quantum computers—but its historical sequence can be made more exact. In particular, the manuscript says follow-up experiments pushed the discussion toward physiological temperatures, then treats the identity and function of the observed oscillations as an open interpretive dispute. A later polarization-controlled FMO experiment supplies a much sharper empirical boundary.

## Primary evidence to carry into the eventual prose pass

### The famous 2007 result was a strong interpretation, not merely an observation

Engel et al., *Nature* 446 (2007), reported long-lived beating signals in two-dimensional electronic spectra of the *Chlorobium tepidum* FMO complex at 77 K and interpreted them as electronic quantum coherence participating in energy transfer. The paper went further than simply reporting oscillations: it argued that the wavelike behavior could help explain efficient transfer by allowing the complex to explore energy-transfer pathways.

Source: Engel et al., “Evidence for wavelike energy transfer through quantum coherence in photosynthetic systems,” *Nature* 446, 782–786 (2007): https://doi.org/10.1038/nature05678

That historical fact matters because Chapter 2 should not imply that the later popular extrapolation appeared from nowhere. Some of the ambitious functional language was present in the scientific interpretation itself, even though popular accounts pushed it much farther toward “quantum computing.”

### A later FMO experiment separated the long-lived beats from long-lived electronic coherence

Thyrhaug et al., *Nature Chemistry* 10 (2018), revisited FMO using polarization-controlled two-dimensional electronic spectroscopy plus theoretical modeling. Their key result is unusually useful for this chapter: they assigned the **long-lived quantum beats exclusively to vibrational origin** and found that electronic coherences had dephased within **240 fs even at 77 K**. They also found evidence for vibronically coupled excited states, so the result did not reduce the system to classical hopping or make vibrations irrelevant. It narrowed what the long-lived spectral signal could establish.

Source: Thyrhaug et al., “Identification and characterization of diverse coherences in the Fenna–Matthews–Olson complex,” *Nature Chemistry* 10, 780–786 (2018): https://doi.org/10.1038/s41557-018-0060-5

This is a better documentary hinge than a generic statement that “the argument shifted.” The reader can watch a specific celebrated inference become more discriminating: oscillation → type of coherence → lifetime → possible functional role.

### Sunlight and femtosecond excitation remain different experimental objects

The chapter is right to distinguish ultrafast coherent excitation from natural illumination. A useful modern quantitative source is Dodin & Brumer, *Scientific Reports* 12 (2022), which analyzes sunlight coherence time in the context of natural and artificial light harvesting and emphasizes the mismatch between coherent femtosecond pulse experiments and continuous broadband solar illumination.

Source: Dodin & Brumer, “The coherence time of sunlight in the context of natural and artificial light-harvesting,” *Scientific Reports* 12 (2022): https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-022-08693-0

Use this as a scope condition, not as proof that coherent dynamics are biologically irrelevant. The experimental question is whether the relevant coherences are generated, sustained, or dynamically regenerated under physiological excitation and whether changing them changes energy-transfer performance.

## Strongest counterargument

Do not overcorrect the 2007 story into “the coherence hypothesis was simply debunked.” The 2018 FMO result still identifies vibronic coupling as relevant to the spectroscopy, and subsequent work continues to investigate exciton–vibrational coupling, synchronization, and short-lived coherent dynamics in photosynthetic antennas. More broadly, electronic energy transfer in molecular aggregates is quantum mechanical even when long-lived electronic superpositions are absent.

A recent example is Kim et al., *Nature Communications* 15 (2024), which models and experimentally studies exciton–vibrational energy dissipation and long-lived coherence in photosynthetic antennas. Its existence is useful counterevidence against writing the history as a clean funeral for coherence. It does **not** reverse the narrower Thyrhaug result for FMO’s long-lived beats or establish that long-lived electronic coherence is required for photosynthetic function.

Source: “Quantum phase synchronization via exciton-vibrational energy dissipation sustains long-lived coherence in photosynthetic antennas,” *Nature Communications* 15 (2024): https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-47560-6

## Claim boundary for Chapter 2

The strongest defensible version is not:

> Photosynthesis is efficient because it uses long-lived electronic quantum coherence.

Nor is it:

> Later work showed that coherence has nothing to do with photosynthetic transport.

A better formulation is:

> Early FMO spectroscopy made long-lived electronic coherence a plausible explanation for striking oscillatory signals and invited a functional interpretation. Better-discriminating experiments later assigned FMO’s long-lived beats to vibrations and bounded the electronic coherence to much shorter times, while leaving vibronic coupling and short-time open-system quantum dynamics scientifically relevant. The engineering lesson is therefore not to preserve a celebrated spectral signature; it is to identify which dynamics causally improve transfer under the operating conditions that matter.

That formulation supports the book’s larger argument without requiring the biological controversy to resolve in favor of the project.

## What would weaken this formulation

The eventual prose should narrow again if newer primary experiments establish any of the following with convincing controls:

- long-lived electronic coherence in FMO under physiological conditions after electronic and vibrational contributions are cleanly separated;
- a causal manipulation showing that the relevant coherence materially changes biological energy-transfer yield or useful photochemistry under realistic excitation;
- evidence that the 2018 polarization-controlled assignment is not robust to improved models or independent replication;
- a natural-illumination preparation mechanism that makes the laboratory coherence directly representative of in-vivo dynamics.

Conversely, if controlled perturbations repeatedly show that removing or changing the relevant coherent contribution leaves functional transfer essentially unchanged, Chapter 2 should become still less coherence-centered.

## Editorial opportunity

The current chapter is strongest when it distinguishes **capability from functional use** and when engineered ENAQT experiments “spin away the biological story.” It is weaker where it narrates the FMO controversy through several abstract paragraphs about interpretation. A later prose pass can replace some of that exposition with the 2007 → 2018 experimental sequence: first the famous beat, then the polarization experiment that asks what was actually beating. That gives the chapter a concrete scientific reversal without turning it into a takedown.

Keep the chapter’s existing warning against the “plant quantum computer” analogy, but make clear that the original scientific paper itself used strong functional language. That is both fairer and more interesting than placing all exaggeration outside the laboratory.
