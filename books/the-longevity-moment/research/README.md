# Research Brief — The Longevity Moment

## Thesis under test

*The Longevity Moment* is not primarily a book about whether biomedical science will radically extend human lifespan.

It tests a different proposition:

> The first consequential effect of a longer expected life can arrive before any individual receives extra biological years. Once people and institutions treat a longer usable future as plausible, the value of flexibility, re-entry, recurring education, career mobility, adaptable housing, staged retirement, early family transfers, and capacity-based rather than age-based decisions can rise in the present.

The strongest version of that claim would be too broad. Subjective longevity beliefs do not dominate behavior. Money, defaults, health, family, institutions, and constraints often matter more. Chronological age remains a useful predictor in many contexts. Linear education-work-retirement structures can create coordination, security, commitment, and efficient specialization. Reversibility has costs.

The version worth testing is narrower:

> Modern adult life is already long enough that one early configuration can stop fitting while decades remain. Institutions perform better across that duration when ordinary transitions do not erase accumulated state, when age is not asked to proxy for more than it can predict, and when the ability to change course is not reserved only for wealthy households.

## Distinction from *The Mortality Discount*

*The Mortality Discount* studies how institutions that price lifespan—pensions, insurers, retirement systems, governments, and markets—can be wrong about mortality, fertility, and the pace of longevity science.

*The Longevity Moment* studies the behavioral and institutional consequence of believing there may be more future. It is about anticipatory longevity, optionality, re-entry, age rules, care, work, housing, relationships, and the design of a multistage life. Biomedical aging science appears only as uncertainty around the time horizon, not as the book's main object.

## Empirical lanes

1. **Subjective time horizons and behavior.** Socioemotional selectivity theory, subjective survival probabilities, subjective age, and evidence on retirement/claiming behavior. The book must not imply that beliefs determine behavior strongly when evidence finds modest effects.
2. **Existing longevity without radical life extension.** Social Security life tables, U.S. centenarian data, and Stanford's New Map of Life work establish that long later lives and three-stage-life mismatch are already observable.
3. **Older-worker mobility and discrimination.** BLS tenure data, OECD career-mobility research, EEOC law, and field experiments on age discrimination constrain claims about hiring, training, and expected tenure.
4. **Housing and place.** Harvard JCHS work on older-adult housing and WHO age-friendly-city guidance anchor claims about accessibility, affordability, transportation, and environmental determinants of independence.
5. **Family overlap and care.** Pew multigenerational-household research, AARP/National Alliance for Caregiving data, and peer-reviewed later-life divorce research anchor the long-middle chapters.
6. **Money and wealth.** Federal Reserve Survey of Consumer Finances data, Social Security life tables, and retirement research anchor claims about wealth, housing equity, subjective survival, and late-life planning.
7. **Retirement ages as institutional choices.** OECD *Pensions at a Glance 2025* and U.S. Social Security rules anchor the argument that pension ages vary, change, and coordinate behavior without constituting biological truths.
8. **Healthspan versus lifespan.** WHO healthy-life-expectancy measures and the aging-science literature support the separation between survival and usable health while preserving the uncertainty of biological intervention.
9. **Inequality in longevity.** The National Academies report on life expectancy by income anchors the argument that average longevity gains can hide unequal lifetime benefit and capacity.
10. **Aging biology as uncertainty, not promise.** The 2023 updated hallmarks-of-aging review is used to describe the field's mechanistic richness, not to imply that any retail intervention extends healthy human lifespan.

## Falsification spine

The book should weaken or revise its thesis if any of the following dominate the evidence:

- Subjective survival or time-horizon beliefs turn out to have little behavioral relevance compared with defaults and immediate constraints.
- Healthy life expectancy stagnates or falls in ways that make care, rather than optionality, the dominant later-life institution.
- Linear career and retirement structures continue to outperform multistage arrangements for most people because transition costs, wage losses, and uncertainty are too high.
- Re-entry systems materially weaken standards, contracts, pensions, or organizational commitment more than they improve resilience.
- Chronological age remains the least harmful administrative proxy because direct capacity testing is too costly, biased, invasive, or unreliable.
- Longer-life flexibility remains primarily a luxury good, widening rather than narrowing inequality.
- Technological change eliminates job demand faster than recurring education and mobility can restore useful employment.
- The demographic response to aging becomes primarily compulsory later retirement, making the lived effect of longevity less optional rather than more.

## Source discipline

- Prefer primary government, intergovernmental, peer-reviewed, or first-party institutional sources.
- Record the source vintage. Pension rules, labor conditions, caregiving prevalence, health measures, household wealth, and demographic counts can change.
- Do not treat a period life table as a personal forecast.
- Do not treat subjective age as a biological-age measure.
- Do not infer causality from associations between subjective views of aging and later outcomes unless the underlying study supports it.
- Do not turn average longevity into a claim about any occupation or income group without preserving distributional differences.
- Do not treat a molecular hallmark, biomarker, animal result, venture round, or aging-company announcement as evidence of human lifespan extension.
- Do not use later-life divorce trends as proof that longevity causes divorce; the manuscript explicitly preserves cohort, remarriage, economic, and cultural explanations.
- For every chapter, keep at least one note about evidence that would complicate the clean version of the argument.

## Manuscript convention

The prose does not carry academic footnotes after every sentence. Claims are supported through this research trail. `source-ledger.csv` contains the stable source records and `chapter-briefs.md` maps each chapter to source IDs, claim boundaries, and falsification notes.
