# Chapter Briefs and Falsification Notes

## Ch 1 — The Future Gets Longer First

**Evidence:** `stanford-new-map`, `carstensen-sst`, `hurd-subjective-survival`, `ssa-life-table`, `census-centenarians`, `hallmarks-aging`, `who-hale`.

**Claim boundary:** The chapter argues that perceived future time can affect motivation and planning before an individual receives extra biological years. It does not claim subjective longevity dominates financial, institutional, health, or family constraints.

**What would make this incomplete:** Evidence that subjective survival/time-horizon beliefs have negligible behavioral relevance after accounting for defaults and liquidity would reduce the anticipatory component of the thesis.

## Ch 2 — The Forty-Year-Old Problem

**Evidence:** `stanford-new-map`, `bls-tenure`, `oecd-career-mobility`, `carstensen-sst`.

**Claim boundary:** Midlife is used as a diagnostic point where accumulated commitments and remaining horizon can both be large. Forty is illustrative, not a biological threshold.

**What would make this incomplete:** If voluntary midcareer mobility generally worsens long-term work attachment or well-being, the case for building more transition paths would need to narrow.

## Ch 3 — The Age We Think We Are

**Evidence:** `pinquart-subjective-age`, `who-ageism`, `neumark-hiring`, `burn-ageist-ads`, `census-centenarians`.

**Claim boundary:** Subjective age is treated as a self-perception construct, not a biological measure. Older-worker discrimination evidence is occupation- and study-specific, not proof about every employer.

**What would make this incomplete:** If chronological age continues to outperform richer measures for high-stakes decisions at far lower cost and bias, age-based rules may remain more defensible than the chapter's critique suggests.

## Ch 4 — A Longer Life Has a Price Today

**Evidence:** `hurd-subjective-survival`, `ssa-life-table`, `ssa-period-tables`, `fed-scf`.

**Claim boundary:** The chapter uses longevity risk to show why duration changes saving and retirement decisions. It does not recommend an annuity, claiming age, or savings rate.

**What would make this incomplete:** Strong evidence that subjective longevity beliefs have little influence on saving or claiming relative to liquidity and defaults would shift emphasis toward institutional design.

## Ch 5 — The One-Way Door

**Evidence:** `stanford-new-map`, `oecd-career-mobility`, `harvard-older-housing`.

**Claim boundary:** Reversibility is presented as valuable under uncertainty, not as a universal moral good. Some commitments derive value from being difficult to reverse.

**What would make this incomplete:** If added flexibility routinely reduces investment, trust, specialization, or institutional durability more than it improves adaptation, the framework should favor stronger commitment.

## Ch 6 — Education Has an Expiration Date

**Evidence:** `stanford-new-map`, `oecd-career-mobility`.

**Claim boundary:** The degree itself does not literally expire. The claim concerns the shelf life of job-specific knowledge and the need for recurring access to serious learning.

**What would make this incomplete:** If most occupational skill change can be absorbed informally or inside firms without formal re-entry to education, universities may matter less than the chapter implies.

## Ch 7 — The Career That Needs a Second Beginning

**Evidence:** `bls-tenure`, `oecd-career-mobility`, `neumark-hiring`.

**Claim boundary:** Multiple career chapters are an option, not an expectation. Long tenure can create valuable expertise and continuity.

**What would make this incomplete:** Evidence that older-worker career changes usually lead to persistent earnings or employment losses even when voluntary would make mobility a less general prescription.

## Ch 8 — Marriage, Again and Again

**Evidence:** `brown-gray-divorce`.

**Claim boundary:** Later-life divorce is not attributed to longevity alone. Cohort history, remarriage, economic independence, social norms, prior divorce, and relationship quality all matter.

**What would make this incomplete:** If later-life relationship change remains a small cohort-specific phenomenon rather than a durable feature of longer lives, the chapter should be read as a relationship-duration argument rather than a demographic forecast.

## Ch 9 — The House That Becomes a Trap

**Evidence:** `harvard-older-housing`, `fed-scf`, `who-age-friendly-framework`.

**Claim boundary:** Homeownership can be both valuable and constraining. Aging in place is not criticized when the home and community continue to fit.

**What would make this incomplete:** If accessible replacement housing and community-preserving moves are already broadly available at acceptable cost, the claimed lock-in would be overstated.

## Ch 10 — Parenthood in the Long Middle

**Evidence:** `pew-multigenerational`, `aarp-caregiving`.

**Claim boundary:** Multigenerational living and caregiving have many causes besides longevity. The chapter focuses on overlap of obligations, not a single demographic cause.

**What would make this incomplete:** If added lifespan is accompanied by much shorter periods of dependency and caregiving, the projected care overlap would be less important.

## Ch 11 — Inheritance Arrives Too Late

**Evidence:** `fed-scf`, `ssa-life-table`, `ssa-period-tables`.

**Claim boundary:** The timing argument concerns the marginal usefulness of resources across life stages; it does not advise donors to transfer assets they may need for their own long futures.

**What would make this incomplete:** If early transfers substantially increase donor insecurity, family conflict, or recipient dependency in typical households, later transfer may remain the more robust default.

## Ch 12 — Sixty-Five Is Still a Number

**Evidence:** `oecd-pensions`, `ssa-life-table`, `nas-income-longevity`, `who-hale`.

**Claim boundary:** Retirement ages are administrative choices, but the fiscal pressures behind them are real. The chapter does not claim age is irrelevant to health or work capacity.

**What would make this incomplete:** If simple universal thresholds produce better equity and administrative outcomes than more flexible systems, moving the cliff may be superior to adding complexity.

## Ch 13 — The Employer's Short Memory

**Evidence:** `oecd-career-mobility`, `bls-tenure`, `neumark-hiring`, `neumark-state-laws`, `burn-ageist-ads`, `eeoc-adea`.

**Claim boundary:** Experience is not assumed to be valuable automatically. Incumbent knowledge can be obsolete, and outside hiring can be the correct response.

**What would make this incomplete:** If employer-funded retraining of older workers has low realized payback because workers exit sooner than expected or skills fail to transfer, the investment case should be narrower and more role-specific.

## Ch 14 — The City with a Longer Day

**Evidence:** `who-age-friendly-framework`, `who-age-friendly-guide`, `harvard-older-housing`.

**Claim boundary:** Environmental design can enable or constrain capacity; it cannot eliminate biological decline or guarantee independence.

**What would make this incomplete:** If specialized services prove more cost-effective and acceptable than universal age-friendly redesign in many low-density settings, the city argument should become more plural.

## Ch 15 — The Consumer After Senior

**Evidence:** `fed-scf`, `census-centenarians`, `who-ageism`, `hallmarks-aging`.

**Claim boundary:** Older consumers are heterogeneous in wealth and health. The chapter does not equate wealth held by older households with universal affluence.

**What would make this incomplete:** If chronological-age marketing remains highly predictive after controlling for function, household, and behavior, the commercial disappearance of “senior” would be overstated.

## Ch 16 — The Optionality Divide

**Evidence:** `nas-income-longevity`, `fed-scf`, `oecd-career-mobility`, `harvard-older-housing`, `who-hale`.

**Claim boundary:** Inequality affects both years lived and the ability to adapt. The chapter does not claim public infrastructure can equalize all life-course differences.

**What would make this incomplete:** If transition-support policies have weak effects on mobility, health, or resilience relative to their fiscal cost, private and family solutions may remain more dominant than the book prefers.

## Ch 17 — Healthspan Is the Real Budget

**Evidence:** `who-hale`, `hallmarks-aging`, `nas-income-longevity`.

**Claim boundary:** HALE is a population measure, not an individual forecast. The chapter avoids treating disability as absence of meaningful life and distinguishes environment from biology.

**What would make this incomplete:** If future interventions reliably compress morbidity across populations, care and adaptation may become less central than the chapter assumes.

## Ch 18 — The Age Rule

**Evidence:** `oecd-pensions`, `who-ageism`, `eeoc-adea`, `neumark-hiring`, `burn-ageist-ads`.

**Claim boundary:** Age rules are often administratively useful and sometimes necessary. The chapter argues for fit and review, not abolition.

**What would make this incomplete:** If capacity testing or individualized rules introduce more bias, gaming, privacy loss, and administrative cost than age thresholds, simple age rules should remain in more domains.

## Ch 19 — The People Who Cannot Reinvent

**Evidence:** `nas-income-longevity`, `aarp-caregiving`, `oecd-career-mobility`, `harvard-older-housing`.

**Claim boundary:** Reinvention is a right to possibility, not an obligation. Physical work, disability, place, care, debt, and exhaustion can make continuity rational or change infeasible.

**What would make this incomplete:** If lower-income and physically demanding occupations gain unusually large benefits from automation and job redesign, the constraint profile could improve faster than expected.

## Ch 20 — Reversible Institutions

**Evidence:** `stanford-new-map`, `oecd-career-mobility`, `who-age-friendly-guide`, `harvard-older-housing`.

**Claim boundary:** Re-entry and ramps have carrying costs. The chapter does not assume every system should maintain unlimited slack.

**What would make this incomplete:** Evidence that re-entry paths materially erode standards, contracts, pension sustainability, or organizational investment would require stronger boundaries around reversibility.

## Ch 21 — What Would Make This Wrong

**Evidence:** all ledger sources, with particular reliance on `hurd-subjective-survival`, `who-hale`, `nas-income-longevity`, `oecd-career-mobility`, `oecd-pensions`, `hallmarks-aging`.

**Claim boundary:** This chapter is the explicit adversarial test. It preserves scenarios in which longevity gains stall, healthspan disappoints, age rules remain efficient, flexibility becomes precarious, or technology reduces labor demand faster than people can adapt.

**What would make this incomplete:** New evidence that contradicts the book's core assumptions should be added here before publication rather than harmonized away elsewhere.

## Ch 22 — The Longevity Moment

**Evidence:** synthesis of all lanes; no new empirical claim should be introduced here without adding a ledger source.

**Claim boundary:** The “moment” is literary shorthand for a distributed change in planning horizons, not a dated scientific threshold.

**What would make this incomplete:** If the source record suggests no common mechanism beyond general social change, the conclusion should narrow to a set of adjacent reforms rather than claim a unified longevity lens.
