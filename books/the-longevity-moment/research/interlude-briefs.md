# Interlude Briefs and Falsification Notes

## Interlude — The Stranger Who Gets Your Pension

**Evidence:** `hershfield-future-self`, `hurd-subjective-survival`, `carstensen-sst`.

**Claim boundary:** Future-self continuity is used as one psychological mechanism that can affect intertemporal choice. It is not treated as a complete theory of retirement saving, identity, or long-horizon behavior; liquidity, defaults, income, institutions, health, and family constraints remain material.

**What would make this incomplete:** If future-self continuity has weak or non-replicating behavioral effects outside experimental settings, the identity lens should be treated as a literary aid rather than a major causal mechanism.

## Interlude — The Long Contract

**Evidence:** `fed-scf`, `stanford-new-map`, `oecd-career-mobility`, `oecd-pensions`.

**Claim boundary:** “Duration risk” is used analogically outside finance. The interlude does not claim careers, marriages, housing, or family obligations obey bond-duration equations; it argues only that longer time horizons magnify both compounding value and persistent mismatch.

**What would make this incomplete:** If long-duration arrangements generally remain easy to renegotiate at low cost or if the social value of durable commitment consistently dominates adaptation costs, the case for formal review points should narrow.

## Interlude — The Succession Bottleneck

**Evidence:** `bls-tenure`, `oecd-career-mobility`, `who-ageism`.

**Claim boundary:** Longer tenure is not assumed to be harmful and chronological age is not treated as grounds for displacement. The interlude separates contribution from control and asks whether advancement depends unnecessarily on an incumbent’s departure.

**What would make this incomplete:** If longer senior tenure generally expands mentorship, organizational capacity, and opportunity without materially slowing advancement or concentrating authority, the bottleneck claim would apply only to scarce-role institutions.
