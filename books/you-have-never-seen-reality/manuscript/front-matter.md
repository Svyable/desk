# You Have Never Seen Reality

*Why Perception Shows You What Works, Not What Is*

**Sven Hardy Benson**

---

There is no red in an apple.

There is light.

There are reflectance properties, illumination, a retina, photopigments, opponent channels, cortex, context, adaptation, memory, expectation and a nervous system trying to make a useful decision before something eats it.

Then there is red.

Red is real as experience.

That is different from saying red is sitting on the apple waiting for an eye to collect it.

The distinction sounds small until you notice how often perception works this way.

The world in front of you feels continuous even though each eye contains a blind spot. It feels stable while your eyes make rapid jumps several times a second. A person can change during a brief interruption and you may not notice. A rubber hand can begin to feel like part of your body. Time can seem to slow in danger and vanish in absorption. A blind person can learn to extract spatial information from patterns delivered through skin or sound.

None of this means reality is fake.

It means experience is an achievement.

The brain and body do not merely receive a world. They construct a usable relationship with one.

For most of this book, that claim will remain completely terrestrial. We will stay with eyes, hands, attention, animals, sensory substitutions and the wonderfully unreliable feeling that what we see is simply what is there.

Only then will we ask the question that makes the title dangerous.

If perception is an interface all the way down to color, objecthood, body and duration, why are we so sure that space and time themselves belong to the world exactly as they appear?

Maybe they do.

That answer has to remain available.

Otherwise this would not be an investigation.

It would be a conversion.
