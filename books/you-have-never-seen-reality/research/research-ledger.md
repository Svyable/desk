# Research ledger — You Have Never Seen Reality

## Governing question

How much objective structure can an organism infer from perception when perception is demonstrably constructed, selective, action-oriented and species-specific—and what evidence would justify extending “interface” language from color and objects to spacetime itself?

## Distinction from Consciousness First

*Consciousness First* is ontological: it asks whether consciousness can be the fundamental substrate from which physical reality emerges.

*You Have Never Seen Reality* is epistemic and perceptual: it does not require fundamental consciousness at all. A physicalist can accept most of the book. The core problem is that an organism never encounters the external world without sensory transduction, neural processing, attention, memory and action. The book asks what survives those transformations and how much metaphysics can legitimately be inferred from experience.

## Evidence classes

### Class 1 — established perceptual facts

1. Human color experience depends on spectral input, retinal cone responses, opponent processing, adaptation, illumination and context; color experience is not a literal copy of a wavelength label embedded in objects.
2. Each eye contains a physiological blind spot at the optic disc; ordinary vision generally does not present it as a hole.
3. Saccades repeatedly shift retinal input while visual experience remains comparatively stable.
4. Change blindness demonstrates that large scene changes can escape awareness under disrupted attention.
5. Body ownership can be experimentally manipulated, including the rubber-hand illusion.
6. Human duration judgments vary systematically with attention, arousal, memory and task.

### Class 2 — established mechanisms / active models

1. Perceptual systems integrate uncertain cues rather than merely copy sensory input.
2. Predictive-processing and Bayesian models explain many perceptual phenomena but should not be treated as one settled brain theory.
3. Sensory substitution demonstrates that useful spatial or object information can be extracted from nontraditional sensory channels after training.
4. Animal sensory systems sample variables humans cannot directly experience, constraining anthropocentric claims about the privileged character of human perceptual space.

### Class 3 — Interface Theory and evolutionary claims

1. Hoffman, Singh and Prakash's Interface Theory of Perception argues that evolutionary fitness need not favor veridical depiction and uses formal evolutionary games to motivate interface-like perception.
2. Published critics dispute whether the theory defines veridicality appropriately and whether the formal games justify broad claims about real perceptual systems.
3. The existence of adaptive compression does not establish total non-veridicality; a representation can omit or distort some properties while preserving real topology, order, invariants or causal structure.

### Class 4 — physics bridge

1. Relativity undermines a universal observer-independent present in the naive sense.
2. Some quantum-gravity research treats spacetime geometry as emergent from deeper degrees of freedom.
3. These developments weaken naive identification of experienced spacetime with ultimate ontology but do not establish that biological perception constructs physical spacetime.

### Class 5 — speculative synthesis

1. Spacetime as experienced may function as an interface encoding deeper relational structure.
2. Object boundaries, locality and temporal passage may be representation-level features rather than fundamental categories.
3. Scientific objectivity may arise not from direct perception but from invariants preserved across multiple observers, instruments and transformations.

## Contradiction ledger

### Constructed vs false

All perception is processed, but processing does not entail fabrication. The book must distinguish compression, transformation, uncertainty reduction and genuine error.

### Non-resemblance vs non-veridicality

A representation can fail to resemble its target while preserving useful objective structure. The desktop metaphor is strongest when it describes abstraction, not when it becomes proof that nothing perceived corresponds to reality.

### Illusions vs ordinary success

Illusions reveal mechanisms under particular conditions. They cannot be used to erase the overwhelming everyday success of perception-guided action.

### Evolutionary fitness vs truth

Natural selection directly rewards reproductive success, not philosophical truth. But accurate tracking can itself be fitness-enhancing. Any “fitness beats truth” claim must state assumptions and model scope.

### Private perception vs public science

Different observers have species-specific and individual interfaces, yet science converges on stable measurements. Any radical interface theory must explain intersubjective invariants rather than treating them as an embarrassment.

### Perceptual spacetime vs physical spacetime

Depth perception is constructed from cues. That does not establish that relativistic spacetime or quantum-gravity geometry is a neural construction. The bridge requires an explicit theory.

## Strongest realist account

Perception is an imperfect but genuinely world-tracking biological measurement system. Evolution selects organisms that detect behaviorally relevant real structure. Perceptual qualities are observer-dependent encodings, but objects, causal relations and spatial structure are sufficiently veridical to support successful action and cumulative science. Illusions expose edge cases and inference mechanisms, not a universal veil.

## Strongest interface account

Evolution constructs species-specific action interfaces optimized for fitness rather than resemblance. The experienced world contains icons and compressed relational variables that guide behavior while concealing most properties of the substrate. Scientific instruments expand and stabilize the interface, but even they deliver representations rather than unmediated reality.

## Defeat conditions

The strongest “interface” thesis must narrow if evolutionary models show robust pressure toward structural veridicality under realistic environments, if perceptual representations can be shown to preserve the very structures the radical account says they hide, or if extending interface language to spacetime generates no testable difference from ordinary scientific realism.

The spacetime-desktop extension fails if it remains a metaphor with no mapping between deeper variables and recovered physical geometry.

## Publication holds

- Do not say the apple “is not really red” without distinguishing reflectance, illumination and experience.
- Do not use the blind spot as evidence that the brain invents arbitrary content.
- Do not imply change blindness means observers see almost nothing.
- Do not say predictive processing is a settled unified theory of perception.
- Do not say Hoffman's evolutionary games prove truth is selected against in nature generally.
- Do not equate “constructed” with “illusory.”
- Do not use animal sensory diversity to deny an observer-independent world.
- Do not claim emergent spacetime physics proves spacetime is a perceptual interface.
- Preserve the strongest structural-realist answer throughout: interfaces can be indirect and still latch onto reality.

2. Reconstruct Simons–Levin and later change-blindness replication/attention work rather than relying on demonstration lore.
3. Build the color chapter from current color-science reviews, separating reflectance, spectra, photoreceptor coding, constancy and qualia.
4. Reconstruct visual stability around saccades from primary psychophysics and neuroscience.
5. Audit predictive-processing claims against competing cue-integration and recurrent-processing accounts.
6. Reconstruct sensory-substitution evidence, including what becomes perceptual versus cognitively decoded after training.
7. Build comparative sensory case studies from ultraviolet vision, echolocation, infrared sensing, polarization and electroreception.
8. Reproduce the assumptions of Hoffman's evolutionary games and the strongest published criticisms.
9. Define types of veridicality: resemblance, structural, relational, causal, task-relative and ecological.
10. Connect the final physics chapters only after establishing what “interface” would have to mean mathematically rather than metaphorically.
