# Reader experiments — You Have Never Seen Reality

These are manuscript tools, not proofs of metaphysics. Each demonstration should be used to expose a specific perceptual assumption, followed immediately by the strongest ordinary explanation.

## 1. Find the blind spot

**Claim tested:** visual continuity exceeds raw monocular retinal sampling.

**Procedure:** close one eye, fixate a mark, move a second mark laterally until it disappears near the optic-disc location.

**Do not infer:** that the brain arbitrarily invents the entire scene.

**Chapter:** 2.

## 2. Troxler fading

**Claim tested:** stable peripheral stimuli can fade under fixation as neural responses adapt and attention is constrained.

**Do not infer:** that unattended objects cease to exist.

**Chapter:** 2 or 4.

## 3. Saccade awareness

**Claim tested:** rapid eye movements are frequent while visual experience remains comparatively stable.

**Procedure:** alternate gaze between your two eyes in a mirror. You will not normally see your own eye movement the way an observer does.

**Do not infer:** that vision is “off” during every saccade.

**Chapter:** 3.

## 4. Blink continuity

**Claim tested:** brief loss of retinal input is usually not experienced as a disruptive blackout.

**Do not infer:** that the brain stores a full-resolution scene during the blink.

**Chapter:** 3.

## 5. Change blindness

Use published demonstrations or purpose-built original stimuli with proper attribution.

**Claim tested:** apparent scene richness is compatible with missed changes when transient cues are disrupted.

**Do not infer:** that observers retain no scene information.

**Chapter:** 4.

## 6. Rubber-hand / body-ownership demonstrations

Do not instruct unsafe self-experimentation. Describe laboratory paradigms and use videos/figures only with rights cleared.

**Claim tested:** multisensory timing and spatial congruence can alter reported body ownership.

**Do not infer:** that the biological body is unreal.

**Chapter:** 6.

## 7. Chronostasis / stopped-clock effect

**Claim tested:** timing around saccadic shifts can produce distortions in subjective duration.

**Do not infer:** that physical time stops or is created by attention.

**Chapter:** 7.

## 8. Sensory substitution

Prefer published participant reports and controlled studies rather than simplified “seeing with the tongue/skin” headlines.

**Claim tested:** useful spatial structure can sometimes be learned through a sensory channel different from ordinary vision.

**Do not infer:** that all modalities become phenomenologically identical.

**Chapter:** 12.

## Book-wide experimental rule

Every demonstration gets four labels:

1. **What physically changes**
2. **What the participant reports**
3. **What mechanism is supported**
4. **What metaphysical conclusion does *not* follow**

This format keeps the book experiential without turning illusions into ontology by accumulation.
