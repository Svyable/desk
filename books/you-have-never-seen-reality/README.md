## Thesis

You have never seen the world.

You have seen a biological solution to the problem of surviving in it.

Color is constructed from spectral information rather than copied from objects. The visual field feels continuous despite blind spots, saccades, limited acuity, and severe attentional bottlenecks. Body ownership can be manipulated. Duration can stretch and collapse. Different animals sample different variables from the same environment. Perception is therefore not a passive photograph of reality.

That much is mainstream.

The controversial step begins afterward. If evolution builds useful representations rather than transparent ones, how much objective structure does perception preserve? Donald Hoffman's Interface Theory of Perception argues that fitness can favor representations that conceal underlying reality. Critics answer that usefulness and veridicality are not opposites, and that organisms may track real relational structure without resembling the substrate they track.

*You Have Never Seen Reality* lives inside that argument.

It begins with things a reader can test on their own eyes and body, moves through attention, predictive perception, sensory substitution and animal worlds, then reaches the dangerous question only after the empirical ground is solid: if color, objects, body and time are already constructions, is spacetime itself the one perceptual category we should exempt from construction?

The book does not answer that question by assertion. It asks what would have to be preserved for science to work if the world we experience is an interface.

## Reader method

The early chapters are deliberately experiential. Whenever possible, the reader first encounters a perceptual fact in their own visual or bodily experience, then gets the mechanism, then the strongest ordinary explanation, and only then the philosophical consequence. Every demonstration is constrained by four questions: **what physically changed, what was reported, what mechanism is supported, and what metaphysical conclusion does not follow?**

See the [reader experiment framework](research/reader-experiments.md).

## Evidence rule

Constructed perception is not the same as false perception. An illusion demonstrates a mismatch under particular conditions, not that perception is globally unreliable. Evolutionary usefulness does not prove non-veridicality. A perceptual interface can preserve real relational structure. Animal differences do not imply that there is no observer-independent world. Emergent-spacetime physics does not establish that spacetime is a biological display. The book earns every escalation separately.
