## Contradictions and incentives

**Wartime program advocates.** Incentive to preserve an apparently useful line of research under conditions in which even low-probability weapons could matter. Their optimism is evidence of institutional interest, not proof of physical scalability.

**Postwar technical investigators.** Incentive to salvage general wave science from a weapons project, potentially making the program look more scientifically fruitful than militarily useful. This is not misconduct; it changes the question being answered.

**Later journalists and popular historians.** Incentive toward the phrase “tsunami bomb,” which compresses an ambiguous experimental program into a vivid finished object. Count claims and “atomic-bomb rival” language need primary verification.

**Debunkers.** Incentive to treat failure of weaponization as evidence the entire program was foolish. That can erase real experimental findings about explosive coupling, depth, and geometry.

**Conspiracy communities.** Incentive to treat declassification as proof that later unexplained natural disasters could be covert continuations. Existence of the historical program does not establish continuity, capability, or event-specific causation.

**Modern hazard science.** Incentive is not principally historical; current papers optimize explanation of natural events. Their value here is as a physical control, but they should not be made to answer military-history questions they were not designed to address.
