# Research Trail

## Evidence classes

**Class 1 — Verified / documented.** Primary government or institutional records, contemporary technical reports, later official scientific publications, and directly catalogued archival material.

**Class 2 — Credible but incomplete.** Serious historical reporting based on declassified records, later participant recollection, and technical inference that is plausible but dependent on incomplete geometry, scale, or source reconstruction.

**Class 3 — Disputed / not established.** Claims of an operationally ready tsunami bomb, specific unverified wartime target plans, and later allegations that natural tsunami disasters were secretly weapon-induced without direct evidence.

**Class 4 — Author synthesis / open conjecture.** The “scale trap” thesis; the idea that secrecy can preserve a weapons concept culturally after engineering practicality has collapsed; and comparisons between Project Seal, later nuclear-wave work, impact-tsunami debates, and Hunga Tonga.

## Claim boundaries

- The existence of Project Seal is not evidence that its proposed weapon was operationally practical.
- A small underwater explosion producing a measurable or locally large wave does not establish a scalable far-field tsunami weapon.
- A model or basin experiment can establish relationships among charge geometry, depth, wave amplitude, and bathymetry without establishing military feasibility at full scale.
- A patent, memorandum, or weapons requirement establishes institutional interest, not efficacy.
- Hunga Tonga demonstrates that explosive events can generate damaging tsunami through several coupled mechanisms. It does not validate Project Seal’s conventional-charge design.
- Later nuclear underwater tests can constrain the physics of explosion-generated waves but should not be reverse-engineered into claims that Project Seal possessed nuclear capability or intended a nuclear device.
- No natural tsunami should be attributed to secret weapons without direct event-specific evidence.

## Falsification notes

The book’s stronger thesis weakens if the primary record shows that senior investigators clearly recognized the scale/logistics problem early, treated the project mainly as basic wave research, and never maintained serious expectations of an operational weapon. The secrecy-afterlife thesis also weakens if public misunderstanding is better explained simply by sensational later journalism than by the structure or duration of classification. The manuscript should change accordingly if those records demand it.
