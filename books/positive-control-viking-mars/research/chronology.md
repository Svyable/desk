# Chronology — Positive Control

## Before Viking

- **1960s–early 1970s:** NASA develops increasingly explicit Mars biology concepts in the context of exobiology, planetary protection and the Mariner-era picture of Mars.
- **1972:** Gilbert Levin publishes the conceptual basis for detecting metabolically produced labeled gas with a Mars lander experiment.
- **1970s:** Viking biology team develops three nonidentical life-detection experiments rather than relying on one universal biosignature.
- **1976, prelanding:** Levin and Straat publish the LR radiorespirometry design, including the logic of radioactive substrates and heat-treated controls. NASA TR R-460 records laboratory work relevant to interpretation and known complexities in label release, substrate behavior and sterilization.

## Viking on Mars

- **July 20, 1976:** Viking 1 lands in Chryse Planitia.
- **July 28, 1976:** Viking 1 biology operations begin, according to JPL's later mission completion notice.
- **Summer 1976:** LR nutrient addition produces substantial radioactive-gas evolution. A high-temperature heat-treated control eliminates the response. Gas Exchange releases substantial oxygen after humidification; Pyrolytic Release produces a small carbon-fixation signal that is altered by heat treatment.
- **September 3, 1976:** Viking 2 lands in Utopia Planitia.
- **September 11, 1976:** Viking 2 begins biology operations.
- **October 1976:** Klein et al. publish preliminary combined biology results in *Science*, explicitly preserving both chemical and biological interpretations.
- **December 1976:** Levin and Straat publish LR interim results: similar responses at both landing sites, stability to 18°C, substantial inactivation after approximately 50°C treatment, complete inactivation after 160°C, and unresolved nonbiological alternatives.
- **1977:** Gas Exchange team publishes oxygen/CO2 behavior and a Martian surface-chemistry model. Levin and Straat report an under-rock sample with a similar LR response, a complicated second nutrient response, and substantial response reduction after 46°C treatment.
- **May 28–30, 1977:** Viking biology instruments are switched off after completing planned and additional experiments. JPL states that laboratory studies continue to interpret the results.
- **Late 1970s:** Laboratory LR simulations show that the abrupt gas decrease after second nutrient injection can be reproduced through physicochemical CO2/water/soil equilibrium, demonstrating that even the LR advocates do not treat every feature of the trace as biological.

## The long interval

- **1980s–1990s:** Mars surface oxidant models evolve; Viking's lack of a convincing GCMS organic inventory remains a major reason the life interpretation loses mainstream support.
- **2000s:** New Mars missions establish extensive water-related mineralogy and environmental complexity but do not repeat Viking's direct metabolism experiments.

## Phoenix and the chemical reopening

- **May 25, 2008:** Phoenix lands in the Martian northern plains.
- **2008:** Phoenix wet chemistry identifies perchlorate-bearing soil; the result is published in *Science* in 2009. The analyzed solutions imply roughly 0.4–0.6% perchlorate by mass in those samples.
- **2010:** Navarro-González et al. report laboratory pyrolysis of organic-bearing Atacama soils with magnesium perchlorate and argue that Viking's trace chlorinated compounds may be consistent with Martian organics transformed during heating.
- **2011:** The authors issue a correction to compound concentration estimates without withdrawing the central interpretation. Klaus Biemann publishes a critical comment disputing the inference from the experiments to Viking's GCMS results.
- **2012:** Curiosity lands at Gale Crater with the SAM instrument suite, beginning a new generation of evolved-gas and GCMS measurements.
- **2013:** Quinn et al. show that irradiation of calcium perchlorate under Mars-relevant conditions can generate hypochlorite, trapped oxygen and chlorine dioxide; they demonstrate mechanisms capable of accounting for important Viking biology observations without requiring hydrogen peroxide or superoxide.
- **2015:** Freissinet et al. report definitive chlorobenzene and dichloroalkane detections in Sheepbed mudstone analyses and conclude the products involve Martian chlorine and reduced organic carbon from Martian or exogenous sources.
- **2016:** Levin and Straat publish a mature case for interpreting LR as extant life. It is a participant argument, not a consensus statement.
- **2017:** Georgiou et al. expand experimental work on radiation-driven reactive oxygen species in oxychlorine-bearing Mars analogues and thermal behavior relevant to Viking soil reactivity.
- **2018:** Guzman et al. reanalyze Viking GCMS datasets and report an identification consistent with chlorobenzene, reopening aspects of the old organic-carbon record while remaining dependent on retrospective data interpretation.

## Current status used by this manuscript

The sequence does not converge on a retroactive yes/no. It produces a more precise uncertainty. Viking measured real chemical reactivity. LR's response met several features its designers regarded as biologically meaningful, including heat sensitivity. Later work supplies concrete abiotic chemistry capable of reproducing important pieces of the record, while Phoenix and Curiosity undermine the old picture of a simply organic-free Martian surface. No subsequent mission has independently repeated Viking's metabolism assay with modern orthogonal biosignature instrumentation at either Viking site.
