# Contradictions and Incentives

Magnetoreception is vulnerable to two opposite prestige economies. Quantum language can inflate ordinary spin chemistry into metaphysical claims, rewarding overstatement. At the same time, weak-field biological effects can be dismissed by researchers unfamiliar with nonequilibrium sensory amplification, rewarding premature skepticism.

Mechanism camps also create incentives. Cryptochrome and magnetite programs compete for limited attention even though dual-receptor architectures are biologically plausible. Negative anatomical findings against one proposed magnetite structure should not be generalized into evidence against all ferromagnetic sensing. In-vitro CRY4 magnetic sensitivity should not be generalized into proven in-vivo receptor identity.

Bioelectromagnetic health controversies create additional contamination. Bird RF-disruption experiments can be recruited into claims about human wireless exposure that the animal data do not establish. This book explicitly blocks that inference.

The 2019 human EEG study is high-risk for narrative distortion because 'human magnetic sense' is a more attractive headline than 'direction-selective alpha-band response under one controlled paradigm.' Treat the latter as the claim until independent replication closes the gap.
