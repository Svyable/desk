# Source Log

## Primary and high-value sources

- Xu et al., "Magnetic sensitivity of cryptochrome 4 from a migratory songbird," Nature 594 (2021), 535–540. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-03618-9 — Direct in-vitro magnetic sensitivity of European-robin CRY4; does not establish in-vivo receptor identity.
- Engels et al., "Anthropogenic electromagnetic noise disrupts magnetic compass orientation in a migratory bird," Nature 509 (2014), 353–356. https://doi.org/10.1038/nature13290 — Double-blind behavioral disruption/restoration under shielding; grounded aluminum attenuated roughly 50 kHz–5 MHz noise by about two orders of magnitude, and low-noise rural tests restored unscreened orientation. Supports a local sensory-disruption claim, not a population-level migration or human-health claim.
- Schwarze et al., "Weak Broadband Electromagnetic Fields are More Disruptive to Magnetic Compass Orientation in a Night-Migratory Songbird (Erithacus rubecula) than Strong Narrow-Band Fields," Frontiers in Behavioral Neuroscience 10:55 (2016). https://doi.org/10.3389/fnbeh.2016.00055 — Purpose-built electromagnetically quiet laboratory: weak broadband ~2 kHz–9 MHz noise disrupted orientation, while stronger narrow-band fields at the free-electron Larmor frequency, twice it, 1.315 MHz, and 50 Hz did not under the tested conditions. Important counterevidence to an overly simple narrow-resonance account; does not rule out radical-pair magnetoreception.
- Hore & Mouritsen, "The Radical-Pair Mechanism of Magnetoreception," Annual Review of Biophysics 45 (2016), 299–344. https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-biophys-032116-094545 — Mechanistic review and constraints.
- Mouritsen, "Long-distance navigation and magnetoreception in migratory animals," Nature 558 (2018), 50–59. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-018-0176-1 — Comparative navigation review.
- Wang et al., "Transduction of the Geomagnetic Field as Evidenced from alpha-Band Activity in the Human Brain," eNeuro 6 (2019). https://doi.org/10.1523/ENEURO.0483-18.2019 — Human EEG result; replication status and conscious perception unresolved. Data links provided by authors.
- Deviers et al., "Avian cryptochrome 4 binds superoxide," Computational and Structural Biotechnology Journal 26 (2024). https://doi.org/10.1016/j.csbj.2023.12.009 — Computational/biophysical contribution to candidate radical chemistry, not direct animal behavior.
- Wiltschko/Wiltschko literature on avian magnetic compass behavior — use chapter expansion to reconstruct primary experiments rather than relying only on reviews.
- Lohmann laboratory sea-turtle magnetic-map literature — expansion should use individual primary field-coil experiments with exact species and field parameters.

## Claim discipline

Cryptochrome magnetic sensitivity in vitro is not equivalent to receptor identity in vivo. RF disruption of bird orientation does not imply generalized human health effects. Broadband RF disruption is a mechanistic constraint, not by itself proof of one radical-pair model; narrow-band nulls and apparatus/light dependence must be carried with the positive effect. Magnetite occurrence is not receptor proof. The 2019 human EEG finding is treated as credible but incomplete pending robust independent replication.
