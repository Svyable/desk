# Source Log

## Primary and high-value sources

- Xu et al., "Magnetic sensitivity of cryptochrome 4 from a migratory songbird," Nature 594 (2021), 535–540. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-03618-9 — Direct in-vitro magnetic sensitivity of European-robin CRY4; does not establish in-vivo receptor identity.
- Engels et al., "Anthropogenic electromagnetic noise disrupts magnetic compass orientation in a migratory bird," Nature 509 (2014), 353–356. https://doi.org/10.1038/nature13290 — Double-blind behavioral disruption/restoration under shielding; mechanistic interpretation remains contested at detail level.
- Hore & Mouritsen, "The Radical-Pair Mechanism of Magnetoreception," Annual Review of Biophysics 45 (2016), 299–344. https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-biophys-032116-094545 — Mechanistic review and constraints.
- Mouritsen, "Long-distance navigation and magnetoreception in migratory animals," Nature 558 (2018), 50–59. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-018-0176-1 — Comparative navigation review.
- Wang et al., "Transduction of the Geomagnetic Field as Evidenced from alpha-Band Activity in the Human Brain," eNeuro 6 (2019). https://doi.org/10.1523/ENEURO.0483-18.2019 — Human EEG result; alpha-band response to specific Earth-strength field rotations, polarity/static-field dependence; no conscious perception established. Data and analysis resources were made public.
- Sastre et al., "Human EEG responses to controlled alterations of the Earth's magnetic field," Clinical Neurophysiology (2002). https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1388245702001864 — Blinded fifty-participant study reporting overwhelmingly negative EEG and subjective results across several geomagnetic manipulations; essential null background for later human claims.
- Chae et al., "Human magnetic sense is mediated by a light and magnetic field resonance-dependent mechanism," Scientific Reports 12 (2022), 8997. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-022-12460-6 — Reports light-dependent forced-choice magnetic orientation and RF-resonance effects in men; supports a different putative mechanism from the polarity-sensitive 2019 EEG study and should not be described as a direct replication.
- Deviers et al., "Avian cryptochrome 4 binds superoxide," Computational and Structural Biotechnology Journal 26 (2024). https://doi.org/10.1016/j.csbj.2023.12.009 — Computational/biophysical contribution to candidate radical chemistry, not direct animal behavior.
- Heyers et al., "A visual pathway links brain structures active during magnetic compass orientation in migratory birds," PLoS ONE 2 (2007), e937. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0000937 — Tracing plus ZENK activity links retina, visual thalamus and Cluster N; supports visual-pathway processing but does not identify the receptor molecule.
- Zapka et al., "Visual but not trigeminal mediation of magnetic compass information in a migratory bird," Nature 461 (2009), 1274–1277. https://doi.org/10.1038/nature08528 — Bilateral Cluster N lesions abolish tested magnetic-compass orientation while sun/star compass behavior remains; ophthalmic trigeminal section did not abolish the tested compass behavior.
- Heyers et al./Mouritsen group, "Magnetic field changes activate the trigeminal brainstem complex in a migratory bird," PNAS 107 (2010). https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0907068107 — Magnetic-field changes activate trigeminal brainstem nuclei; V1 section reduces activation, consistent with a separate magnetic-information channel.
- Kobylkov et al., "Magnetoreceptory Function of European Robin Retina: Electrophysiological and Morphological Non-Homogeneity," Cells 11 (2022), 3056. https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4409/11/19/3056 — Reports magnetic-field-dependent retinal electrophysiological effects and spatial non-homogeneity; useful physiological bridge but not cell-specific receptor proof.
- Wiltschko/Wiltschko literature on avian magnetic compass behavior — use chapter expansion to reconstruct primary experiments rather than relying only on reviews.
- Lohmann laboratory sea-turtle magnetic-map literature — expansion should use individual primary field-coil experiments with exact species and field parameters.

## Claim discipline

Cryptochrome magnetic sensitivity in vitro is not equivalent to receptor identity in vivo. Retinal expression is not equivalent to transduction. Cluster N necessity for tested compass behavior does not make Cluster N the complete navigation system. Trigeminal magnetic activation can coexist with a visually mediated compass and may represent a different magnetic variable or function. RF disruption of bird orientation does not imply generalized human health effects. Magnetite occurrence is not receptor proof. The 2019 human EEG finding is treated as credible but incomplete pending robust independent replication. The 2022 behavioral result is not a direct replication of the 2019 EEG result and points toward a different putative mechanism; disagreement between those mechanistic signatures is evidence to resolve, not something to smooth away.
