# Source Log

## Primary and high-value sources

- Xu et al., "Magnetic sensitivity of cryptochrome 4 from a migratory songbird," Nature 594 (2021), 535–540. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-03618-9 — Direct in-vitro magnetic sensitivity of European-robin CRY4; does not establish in-vivo receptor identity.
- Engels et al., "Anthropogenic electromagnetic noise disrupts magnetic compass orientation in a migratory bird," Nature 509 (2014), 353–356. https://doi.org/10.1038/nature13290 — Double-blind behavioral disruption/restoration under shielding; mechanistic interpretation remains contested at detail level.
- Hore & Mouritsen, "The Radical-Pair Mechanism of Magnetoreception," Annual Review of Biophysics 45 (2016), 299–344. https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-biophys-032116-094545 — Mechanistic review and constraints.
- Mouritsen, "Long-distance navigation and magnetoreception in migratory animals," Nature 558 (2018), 50–59. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-018-0176-1 — Comparative navigation review.
- Wang et al., "Transduction of the Geomagnetic Field as Evidenced from alpha-Band Activity in the Human Brain," eNeuro 6 (2019). https://doi.org/10.1523/ENEURO.0483-18.2019 — Human EEG result; replication status and conscious perception unresolved. Data links provided by authors.
- Deviers et al., "Avian cryptochrome 4 binds superoxide," Computational and Structural Biotechnology Journal 26 (2024). https://doi.org/10.1016/j.csbj.2023.12.009 — Computational/biophysical contribution to candidate radical chemistry, not direct animal behavior.
- Wiltschko/Wiltschko literature on avian magnetic compass behavior — use chapter expansion to reconstruct primary experiments rather than relying only on reviews.
- Lohmann laboratory sea-turtle magnetic-map literature — expansion should use individual primary field-coil experiments with exact species and field parameters.

## Claim discipline

Cryptochrome magnetic sensitivity in vitro is not equivalent to receptor identity in vivo. RF disruption of bird orientation does not imply generalized human health effects. Magnetite occurrence is not receptor proof. The 2019 human EEG finding is treated as credible but incomplete pending robust independent replication.
