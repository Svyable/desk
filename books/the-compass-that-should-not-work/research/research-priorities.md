# Research Priorities

1. Multicenter replication of avian RF-disruption experiments with shared calibrated hardware and complete environmental spectra.
2. Direct CRY4 causal tests linking molecular perturbation to neural signal and orientation behavior while preserving ordinary vision.
3. Comparative CRY4 biophysics across migratory and non-migratory lineages with phylogenetic controls.
4. Anatomically rigorous search for ferromagnetic receptor structures using contamination-resistant mineral mapping and neural tracing.
5. Partition compass, map, polarity, inclination and intensity behaviors before assigning receptor mechanisms.
6. Independent preregistered replication of the 2019 human EEG paradigm.
7. Field ecology measuring whether anthropogenic RF environments alter migration in free-living animals.
8. A shared quantitative model connecting radical-pair kinetics, receptor orientation, neural gain and behavioral thresholds.

## Defeat conditions

The CRY4 receptor hypothesis should lose substantial probability if specific perturbations that abolish its magnetic photochemistry leave well-powered magnetic orientation intact. The RF-radical-pair argument should weaken if standardized multicenter experiments fail to reproduce frequency-dependent disruption. The human neural-sensitivity claim should be treated as non-robust if independent preregistered laboratories cannot reproduce the EEG signature. Magnetite receptor claims require identified sensory anatomy, not magnetic particles alone.
