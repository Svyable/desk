# The Experiment That Could Lose

A field with several plausible mechanisms needs an experiment designed to humiliate at least one of them.

Imagine a coordinated program using a migratory bird species with established orientation behavior. First, sequence and quantify retinal cryptochrome expression across migratory state. Second, introduce targeted mutations or reversible molecular perturbations predicted to alter radical-pair chemistry while preserving ordinary vision. Third, test magnetic orientation under calibrated static fields and preregistered radiofrequency perturbations. Fourth, record retinal and downstream neural activity. In parallel, characterize magnetic particles and trigeminal pathways without assuming they are irrelevant.

The radical-pair hypothesis should predict a linked signature: molecular magnetic sensitivity, field-angle dependence, spectral dependence, RF susceptibility and neural/behavioral consequence. If the protein remains magnetically sensitive in vitro but its targeted disruption leaves behavior untouched across properly powered experiments, the case for its receptor role should weaken sharply.

The magnetite family should face equally direct tests. Candidate particles must be biologically produced, sit in defined sensory cells, mechanically couple to transduction machinery, drive field-dependent neural activity and matter to behavior. Mere presence of magnetic iron is insufficient.

A multicenter design is essential because environmental electromagnetic differences between laboratories may be part of the phenomenon. Each site should measure its full magnetic noise spectrum and run identical portable coil systems. Data analysis should be preregistered and blinded.

Human magnetoreception deserves a parallel but separate protocol: replicate the EEG paradigm with independent laboratories and hardware teams, then test conscious discrimination only if the neural effect survives. Negative behavioral results should not erase a neural effect; negative neural replication should sharply reduce confidence in the original claim.

The point is not to produce one heroic experiment. It is to force molecular, neural and behavioral evidence to close on the same mechanism.
