# A Bird in a Funnel

Long before the molecule, there was the bird.

Migration researchers developed simple orientation cages in which a restless migratory bird leaves directional marks while attempting to depart. The setup looks almost insulting compared with the claim it supports. Change the magnetic field around the cage with coils, remove celestial cues, and the preferred direction can rotate. Under the right conditions, orientation follows the manipulated magnetic field.

This is important because magnetoreception did not begin as a theory looking for a phenomenon. It began as behavior that demanded mechanism. The behavior also turned out to be fussy. In many songbirds the magnetic compass is light dependent. It can behave as an inclination compass rather than a polarity compass, distinguishing the angle field lines make with gravity rather than simply magnetic north from south. Light wavelength matters. So does experience and calibration against other cues.

The fussiness is a gift. Every dependency is a possible fingerprint. A mechanism that predicts sensitivity to field inclination, spectral conditions, field strength and radiofrequency perturbation can be compared against one that predicts a different pattern. The animal becomes an instrument whose quirks are data.

It is also dangerous. Behavioral assays are vulnerable to subtle bias, uncontrolled noise, seasonality, stress and experimenter effects. Magnetoreception has repeatedly produced findings that looked cleaner in one laboratory than another. The mature response is neither dismissal nor credulity. It is double blinding, sham conditions, replication across sites, and enough physical monitoring to know what magnetic environment actually reached the animal.

The best bird experiments therefore resemble precision physics more than folk naturalism. Coils must be calibrated. Background radio noise matters. Field gradients matter. Metal in the room matters. The birds themselves must be tested at the correct migratory state.

This is one reason the subject is underwritten. It is expensive in an unfashionable way: not because one machine costs a billion dollars, but because several disciplines must all be competent at once. An ornithologist can unknowingly build a bad electromagnetic laboratory. A physicist can unknowingly build a good electromagnetic laboratory in which a stressed bird no longer performs the behavior of interest.

The funnel is not crude. It is a boundary object between worlds.
