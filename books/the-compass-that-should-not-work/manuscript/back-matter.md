# Back Matter

## Chronology

1950s–1960s — Controlled orientation experiments establish magnetic compass behavior in migratory birds.

1970s — Radical-pair ideas emerge as a possible route for weak magnetic fields to alter chemistry.

Late twentieth century — Magnetite-based receptor hypotheses expand alongside behavioral work in birds, fish, turtles and other animals.

2000 — A cryptochrome-based radical-pair compass model gives the molecular hypothesis a concrete photochemical architecture.

2014 — Double-blind work reports disruption of European robin magnetic orientation by anthropogenic electromagnetic noise, with orientation restored under shielding.

2019 — Wang and colleagues report direction-selective alpha-band EEG responses to Earth-strength magnetic-field rotations in humans; conscious magnetoreception remains unestablished.

2021 — Xu and colleagues demonstrate magnetic sensitivity of purified cryptochrome 4 from European robin and identify radical-pair-relevant tryptophan pathways.

2020s — Structural, computational and comparative work continues to test CRY4 and alternative radical pairs while receptor anatomy remains incomplete.

## Closing note

The field is unusually close to a satisfying answer and unusually capable of embarrassing anyone who announces that answer too early. That is a healthy combination.
