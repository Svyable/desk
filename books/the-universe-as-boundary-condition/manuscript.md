# The Universe as Boundary Condition

## Inertia, Clocks, Galaxies, and a Machian Framework Humanity Has Never Quite Finished Testing

Sven Hardy Benson

### Working thesis

Modern physics usually treats local inertia as local fact: a kilogram resists acceleration here because that is what inertial mass does. Cosmology then enters later, as large-scale initial conditions and expansion history. This book proposes a deliberately conservative but genuinely universal conjecture—**Boundary-Condition Inertia (BCI)**—in which local inertial behavior is allowed to depend very weakly on global gravitational boundary conditions: ambient curvature, large-scale mass distribution, cosmological expansion state, and rotational frame structure. General relativity remains the baseline theory. The new claim is not that Mach was right in some mystical sense. It is that humanity has not exhausted the experimentally allowed question of whether inertia has a tiny environment-dependent component tied to the universe that defines the inertial frame.

BCI is designed to be killed. It must reduce to ordinary local Lorentz physics where existing tests demand it, recover standard gravity in the solar system, survive pulsars and cosmology, and explain any galaxy-scale benefit without becoming a shapeless substitute for dark matter. Its value is not that it solves everything. Its value is that it forces laboratory clocks, rotating masses, planetary systems, galaxies, lensing and cosmology into one falsification program.

# 1. The Room With No Outside

Newton's bucket remains irritating because the water knows it is rotating. Relative to what? General relativity answered part of the question by making spacetime dynamical, and frame dragging made the answer experimentally physical. Yet the philosophical residue never disappeared: what fixes an inertial frame when the universe itself contains the matter that defines geometry?

BCI begins there, not with flying saucers. If inertia has any environmental dependence at all, it should first be visible as a precision anomaly in clocks, interferometers, gyroscopes or dynamics.

# 2. Mach Without Incense

Mach's name has accumulated too much metaphysics. The usable content is narrower: perhaps the distribution and motion of cosmic matter contribute to the definition of local inertial structure.

General relativity embodies some Machian features and rejects others. Rotating matter drags frames, but empty solutions exist. BCI treats Mach's principle as an experimental prompt rather than a doctrine.

# 3. A Boundary Variable

The framework introduces an environment functional, call it B, constructed from covariant quantities describing the surrounding gravitational state. B cannot be an arbitrary cosmic scalar pasted onto Newton's second law. It must be built so local coordinate choices cannot create physics.

The simplest phenomenology is that inertial response receives a tiny correction depending on B and perhaps its gradients or time evolution. In high-acceleration, weak-curvature laboratory conditions the correction must be negligible. The interesting territory is weak acceleration, long coherence time, large gravitational baselines, rotation and cosmological environment.

# 4. The Equivalence Principle Is the Fence

Any theory that lets inertial response vary risks violating the equivalence principle. That makes precision gravity experiments the fence around the entire framework.

MICROSCOPE, atom interferometers, lunar laser ranging, gravitational redshift tests, torsion balances and clock networks restrict composition dependence and local position dependence. BCI survives only if the environment term is universal enough—or small enough—to evade those tests while producing a distinct signature elsewhere.

# 5. Clocks Know the Potential

Gravitational redshift is established physics. A clock higher in a gravitational potential runs differently from one lower down. That does not prove environmental inertia, but it demonstrates the operational point: gravity already appears in local phase accumulation.

BCI therefore treats clocks as primary dynamical instruments. If a cosmic boundary variable changes inertia, it may also change phase evolution. The framework must predict whether those effects are locked together or separable.

# 6. Rotation Is the First Global Hint

Frame dragging is the cleanest reminder that rotating matter changes local inertial structure. Gravity Probe B and satellite laser-ranging measurements do not show Machian inertia in the strong sense. They show something more disciplined: angular momentum in one place modifies inertial-frame behavior elsewhere in precisely calculable ways.

BCI asks whether there are additional, much smaller environment-dependent rotational terms beyond standard GR. The answer may simply be no.

# 7. The Laboratory Test Nobody Should Skip

Before invoking galaxies, test rotation locally. Compare atom interferometers, optical clocks, superconducting gyroscopes and precision accelerometers under controlled changes in orientation, latitude, sidereal time, source-mass configuration and rotor state.

A universal boundary effect must show geometry and time dependence that ordinary thermal, magnetic and vibration backgrounds cannot imitate.

# 8. Why MOND Matters Even If It Is Wrong

MOND identified an empirical embarrassment: galaxy dynamics become unusually regular around a characteristic low-acceleration scale. Its successes in rotation curves and scaling relations are real empirical facts even if the underlying explanation is dark matter, modified gravity, modified inertia, feedback, or some combination.

BCI takes the acceleration regularity seriously without adopting MOND wholesale. The question is whether the apparent threshold is really acceleration, or a proxy for an environmental boundary state.

# 9. Modified Inertia, Not Just Modified Gravity

Changing the gravitational field equation and changing inertial response are not equivalent moves. A modified-inertia theory can be history-dependent or nonlocal in ways a simple modified-force law is not.

BCI belongs closer to the modified-inertia family, but it imposes a cosmological boundary interpretation: the correction should depend on the relationship between a local trajectory and the surrounding spacetime state.

# 10. The Galaxy as a Precision Experiment

A spiral galaxy offers billions of test particles across a common gravitational environment. Rotation curves, vertical motions, bars, warps, satellite orbits and the radial acceleration relation become a high-dimensional dataset.

BCI must predict more than flat curves. It must predict residuals. If two galaxies have the same baryonic acceleration but different external environments, the framework should say whether their dynamics differ and by how much.

# 11. The External-Field Problem

MOND's external-field effect is one precedent for environment-sensitive dynamics. BCI would need its own version, derived from the boundary functional rather than inserted phenomenologically.

Wide binaries, open clusters, dwarf satellites and galaxies in clusters are natural tests because they occupy different external gravitational conditions while sharing overlapping internal accelerations.

# 12. Lensing Is Where Easy Theories Die

Any framework that changes galaxy dynamics must face gravitational lensing. It is not enough to move stars correctly while light sees the wrong geometry.

BCI therefore cannot remain a Newtonian modification. A viable version requires a covariant completion specifying how the boundary variable affects—or does not affect—the metric seen by light.

# 13. Pulsars Are the Executioners

Binary pulsars combine strong gravity, precise clocks, orbital dynamics and gravitational radiation. Any universal modification of inertia risks altering periastron advance, orbital decay, Shapiro delay or strong-equivalence behavior.

The pulsar record is therefore less a source of inspiration than a machine for destroying parameter space.

# 14. Cosmology Cannot Be the Wastebasket

A universal theory cannot explain galaxies by saying “cosmology” and then ignore the cosmic microwave background, nucleosynthesis, growth of structure, lensing, supernova distances and gravitational-wave propagation.

BCI's boundary variable must emerge from a cosmological solution, not from a number chosen to fit rotation curves.

# 15. The Coincidence of Acceleration Scales

The MOND acceleration scale is numerically suggestive relative to cosmological acceleration scales involving H0 and the cosmological constant. Suggestive is not explanatory.

BCI treats this numerical proximity as a target for derivation. Either a covariant theory produces the scale naturally, or the coincidence remains a coincidence. Numerology is not allowed to do the work.

# 16. A Craft, If One Ever Appeared

Only after surviving laboratory and astronomical tests does the framework touch UAP propulsion. If inertia contains an environment-dependent term, one can ask whether technology could modulate it. There is currently no evidence that this is possible.

The engineering burden would be severe: a device would need to alter the relevant boundary-sensitive variable locally without producing forbidden gravitational, radiative, energetic or equivalence-principle signatures.

# 17. The Five Experiments

BCI can be made concrete through five coordinated tests: long-baseline clock comparisons across changing gravitational environments; atom-interferometer tests synchronized to source-mass and rotational modulation; wide-binary and cluster dynamics; galaxy residuals conditioned on external environment; and pulsar/cosmological consistency fits using one parameterization.

The framework succeeds only if the same parameters survive all five.

# 18. A Theory Worth Considering Is a Theory That Can Lose

Humanity does not need another universal story immune to contradiction. It needs frameworks that connect scales tightly enough that nature can say no.

BCI is worth considering because it links an old philosophical wound—the origin of inertia—to modern precision tools. It may die quickly. That would be useful. Or a residual might survive, first in a clock, then in a galaxy, then somewhere nobody expected. The point is to make those outcomes commensurable.

## Status

This is an 18-chapter conceptual first pass, not yet a 54,000+ word publication manuscript. The next pass needs an explicit covariant toy model, parameter definitions, equivalence-principle bounds, clock and atom-interferometer signal estimates, galaxy likelihood tests, external-field predictions, pulsar constraints, lensing equations, and a cosmological solution that either closes the theory or kills it.
