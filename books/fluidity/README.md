*How Anthropic Solves Navier–Stokes*

The three-dimensional incompressible Navier–Stokes equations are among the most useful equations in science and engineering, yet the global existence-and-smoothness question remains one of mathematics’ Millennium Prize Problems. **Fluidity** asks a deliberately premature question: if a frontier AI laboratory eventually helps resolve it, what would the actual path look like?

Anthropic is the book’s protagonist because its 2026 work makes a concrete architecture visible: long-running scientific agents, multi-agent theorem graphs, Lean formalization, adversarial review, auditable artifacts, and machine-generated research mathematics. The manuscript combines that emerging stack with the parallel frontier in AI-assisted fluid dynamics, rigorous numerics, formal PDE infrastructure, independent proof checking, and the growing competition among Anthropic, OpenAI, Google DeepMind, academic groups, and open formal-mathematics communities.

The book does **not** claim that Anthropic has solved Navier–Stokes. As of the September 6, 2026 research cutoff, the Clay Mathematics Institute still lists the problem as unsolved. The book’s central wager is instead that a credible AI-assisted resolution is more likely to come from an **AI mathematical institution**—discovery systems, numerical laboratories, theorem libraries, adversarial agents, multiple independent checkers, and human experts coordinated around one exact target—than from a language model simply producing a brilliant proof in prose.

The discovery machinery can be reckless because the certification machinery cannot be.

## Factual boundary

1. **Numerical Navier–Stokes is not the Millennium problem.** Engineers solve discretized forms constantly; Clay asks a rigorous global existence-and-smoothness or admissible-breakdown question for the three-dimensional incompressible equations.
2. **A candidate singularity is not a singularity theorem.** Numerical discovery can identify an object worth proving; validated mathematics must close the gap.
3. **A machine-checked proof can still target the wrong theorem.** High-stakes verification must audit the formal statement, definitions, axioms, trusted computing base, and correspondence with the accepted Clay target.
4. **Formalization of a known theorem is different from discovering new mathematics.** Anthropic’s Fermat formalization is strong evidence about machine-scale reconstruction and coordination, not direct evidence that Navier–Stokes will yield.
5. **A preprint claim is not an accepted Millennium resolution.** The manuscript records live claims for falsification and verification purposes without upgrading them to settled results.

## Editorial principles

1. **Keep the title provocative and the text exact.** The book can ask how Anthropic solves Navier–Stokes while explicitly preserving that, at the research cutoff, Anthropic has not done so.
2. **Separate discovery from certification.** Models, numerical methods and agents may search aggressively; the path to a final claim becomes progressively narrower, more reproducible and more independent.
3. **Treat failure as research output.** Counterexamples, dead branches, false inequalities and broken proof strategies should become reusable negative knowledge.
4. **Make the target immovable.** Strategies may change; the formalized mathematical obligation must not silently weaken.
5. **Prefer independent verification to brand trust.** A final proof should survive outside the lab, under multiple checkers and hostile expert scrutiny.
6. **Let the institution outlive the model.** Model versions will age quickly; formal libraries, proofs, counterexamples, numerical certificates and provenance should remain usable.
