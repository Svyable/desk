# Operational-denominator audit

This is a bounded research pass for *The Small Signal*. It tests Chapter 1's strongest historical move: using the Tehran session as a bridge from laboratory anomaly to intelligence utility. The surviving file proves tasking. It does not yet prove usefulness, and it contains enough protocol detail to show exactly what the book must recover before treating the case as an operational hit or miss.

## Primary-record findings

### The January 23, 1980 file really is Tehran tasking

The declassified GRILL FLAME report for session CC70 says the request came from SOD, J3, OJCS at the Pentagon and that its purpose was to provide information relevant to the hostage situation in the U.S. Embassy compound in Tehran. It explicitly labels the viewer's impressions "raw intelligence data," says they had not undergone intermediate analysis, evaluation, or collation, and assigns interpretation and use to the requester.

Source: CIA FOIA, *Summary Remote Viewing Session CC70*, 23 January 1980, CIA-RDP96-00788R000800580001-1:
https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp96-00788r000800580001-1

**Use:** Chapter 1 is on solid ground using the document as evidence that remote viewing entered an operational intelligence workflow. Keep the narrower verb: *tasked*. The file does not establish that the product improved a decision.

### CC70 was not an isolated Tehran attempt

The CIA collection contains other reports explicitly described as attempts to provide information relevant to the Iran/Tehran hostage situation. Examples include session CC11 and session D-8. This matters because a vivid single transcript cannot supply its own denominator.

Sources:
- CIA FOIA, *GRILL FLAME Remote Viewing (RV) Session CC11 Report*, CIA-RDP96-00788R000800060001-8:
  https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp96-00788r000800060001-8
- CIA FOIA, *Summary Analysis — Remote Viewing (RV) Session D-8*, 26 August 1980, CIA-RDP96-00788R001000090001-2:
  https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp96-00788r001000090001-2

**Publication hold:** Do not call CC70 a successful or unsuccessful Tehran operation until the manuscript has reconstructed the relevant task series, not merely the memorable session.

### The archive preserves explicit low-correlation sessions

A 22 March 1982 training report for session 876 says post-session analysis found "very little correlation" with the target and points readers to another session on the same target. It is a training case, not an operational Tehran control, so it cannot be inserted into the Tehran denominator. But it demonstrates something methodologically important: the archive contains contemporaneous negative evaluations as well as transcripts later readers can mine for correspondences.

Source: CIA FOIA, *Remote Viewing Session #876*, 22 March 1982, CIA-RDP96-00788R001400250001-0:
https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp96-00788r001400250001-0

**Use:** Resist the easy skeptical story that every miss disappeared from the record. Some misses were recorded plainly. The harder question is whether the operational evaluation system captured *all* taskings and outcomes in a way that permits an unbiased denominator.

### The later CIA summary collapses two different claims

CIA's current public history says the 1995 independent evaluation found enough accurate remote-viewing experiences to defy randomness while also finding the phenomenon too unreliable, inconsistent, and sporadic for intelligence use; CIA did not restore the program.

Source: CIA, *Ask Molly: Did CIA Really Study Psychic Powers?*:
https://www.cia.gov/stories/story/ask-molly-did-cia-really-study-psychic-powers/

That summary is useful orientation, but Chapter 1 should not treat it as the final evidentiary layer. "Defy randomness" concerns experimental/statistical evidence; "useful for intelligence" concerns an operational record, consumer needs, and decision consequences. The manuscript's thesis depends on keeping those ledgers separate, so Chapters 10–11 must recover the AIR report and the Utts/Hyman positions directly rather than letting CIA's retrospective synthesis stand in for them.

## The denominator the Tehran chapter needs

For the Iran-hostage tasking family, build a session-level table before publication with:

1. session date and report identifier;
2. requester and exact target/task known before the session;
3. cueing given to the viewer before and during the session;
4. whether the viewer was asked to locate, describe, identify, or forecast;
5. every substantive claim in the transcript, not selected correspondences;
6. target facts independently knowable at the time;
7. scoring or evaluation recorded before feedback, if any;
8. whether a consumer judged the product useful, misleading, or irrelevant;
9. whether it changed collection, rescue planning, targeting, or another decision;
10. conventional intelligence available to the same consumer;
11. repeat sessions on the same target and their outcomes;
12. failed, ambiguous, cancelled, or unevaluated taskings.

The denominator is not simply "number of remote-viewing sessions." A session can contain dozens of claims at different levels of specificity, and several sessions can concern the same target. The unit of operational value is ultimately the decision opportunity: did the anomalous product improve a choice relative to the information already available?

## Strongest counterargument to the book's small-signal framing

The book risks granting too much by beginning with the phrase "statistically real and practically useless." CIA's public summary uses language sympathetic to a non-random laboratory residue, but the conventional model does not have to concede a real anomalous signal at all. A small aggregate can be produced by methodological leakage, publication selection, dependence, flexible analysis, or other biases whose scale is comparable to the reported effect.

Operational failure therefore cannot rescue a disputed laboratory anomaly by saying the effect was merely too weak for intelligence. The manuscript should maintain three live possibilities until the AIR and modern experimental records are reconstructed:

- a real anomalous signal exists but is operationally weak;
- the apparent laboratory signal is methodological artifact and operations add no independent support;
- heterogeneous methods produce a mixture that cannot responsibly be summarized by one latent effect.

The title is a hypothesis under test, not a premise.

## Disconfirming evidence that must survive the narrative

The 1982 session 876 record is useful precisely because it frustrates a neat archival-selection argument: at least some poor correlations were documented. If later research finds systematic contemporaneous scoring, complete task logs, and negative sessions preserved at rates comparable to positive ones, the book should narrow or abandon any claim that secrecy itself created the apparent operational signal by hiding misses.

Likewise, if the AIR operational review shows that remote-viewing products prospectively changed decisions with measurable advantage over conventional baselines, "practically useless" becomes too strong. Utility has to be measured, not inferred from program cancellation.

## Narrative opportunity

Chapter 1 already has the right object: the phrase "raw intelligence." The stronger next move is not another abstract warning about noisy information. Follow the administrative trail. CC70 was one report in a task family. Other Tehran reports exist. The archive even contains sessions elsewhere that evaluators called poor. The reader can then feel the denominator problem arrive historically rather than being told that denominators matter.

A useful sequence for later revision is:

- open on CC70 and its "raw intelligence" label;
- reveal that Tehran generated multiple sessions rather than one cinematic attempt;
- show that the archive was capable of recording low-correlation outcomes;
- ask what the complete operational ledger contains;
- only then move from transcript fascination to the distinction between statistical existence and decision utility.

That progression makes the missing denominator the live question carrying the chapter instead of a methodological lecture inserted after the anecdote.

- Extract Jessica Utts's and Ray Hyman's conclusions separately, including what each says about laboratory evidence versus operational utility.
- Enumerate all Iran-hostage-related GRILL FLAME/STARGATE sessions recoverable in the declassified collection before selecting any as representative.
- Recover contemporaneous requester evaluations and determine whether they were written before target feedback.
- Identify the decision opportunities attached to Tehran tasking and whether any remote-viewing product changed them.

Until those steps are complete, the safest reader-facing claim is the strongest one the primary file actually proves: remote viewing was operationally tasked and its output entered the system as unevaluated raw intelligence.
