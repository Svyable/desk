# Chapter Briefs and Falsification Notes

This file is the editorial control surface for *Solar Century*. Each chapter has one job and one failure test. The book should weaken its claim where evidence does not support the stronger version.

## 1 — The Fuel That Arrives Free
**Job:** Establish the architectural consequence of a generator with no recurring fuel purchase.
**Falsification:** Do not call the system free energy; capital, land, labor, finance, maintenance, and network costs remain substantial.

## 2 — The Panel Was the Trojan Horse
**Job:** Show how commoditized generation moves value into balance-of-system hardware, interconnection, finance, and grid controls.
**Falsification:** If system costs routinely overwhelm generator savings, module commoditization is less transformational than argued.

## 3 — Noon Is an Infrastructure Problem
**Job:** Make electricity's time stamp central and introduce storage, transmission, flexible demand, and curtailment as temporal responses.
**Falsification:** Avoid implying daily shifting solves multiday or seasonal scarcity.

## 4 — The Grid Learns to Breathe
**Job:** Recast the grid from one-way delivery network into active coordination system.
**Falsification:** Complexity is not automatically resilience; software and distributed controls can add common-mode and cyber risks.

## 5 — The Battery Makes Time Tradable
**Job:** Treat storage as a market in timing rather than a magical cure for variability.
**Falsification:** Duration, degradation, safety, mineral, financing, and utilization constraints can make other flexibility resources cheaper.

## 6 — Millions of Power Plants
**Job:** Explain the statistical and institutional consequences of distributed generation and aggregation.
**Falsification:** Distributed ownership does not eliminate the need for shared networks or coordinated standards.

## 7 — The Roof Joins the Grid
**Job:** Show why behind-the-meter economics, split incentives, retail tariffs, and distribution engineering make rooftop solar a distinct infrastructure lane.
**Falsification:** Rooftops cannot be assumed to replace all utility-scale generation or network investment.

## 8 — The Factory in a Box
**Job:** Explain how manufacturing repetition allows generator cost to learn globally while projects remain local.
**Falsification:** Manufacturing scale can create oversupply, quality problems, concentration risk, and stranded factories.

## 9 — China Builds the Learning Curve
**Job:** Treat China's manufacturing and deployment scale as a major industrial engine in a cumulative international solar history.
**Falsification:** Avoid monocausal national history; research, policy, finance, and demand were globally distributed.

## 10 — The Price of a Watt
**Job:** Separate module price, installed cost, financing, LCOE, capture price, and delivered-system cost.
**Falsification:** Never use LCOE alone to claim system superiority.

## 11 — The Copper Century
**Job:** Expose the material body beneath apparently weightless electrification.
**Falsification:** Copper scarcity can be mitigated by new supply, aluminum substitution, efficiency, design changes, and recycling; forecasts are not destiny.

## 12 — The Transformer Bottleneck
**Job:** Use grid-equipment lead times to demonstrate bottleneck migration from fast generation into slower network supply chains.
**Falsification:** Do not imply every region or transformer class shares the same lead time.

## 13 — Interconnection Is the New Drilling
**Job:** Make grid access the scarce development right and explain queues, upgrade costs, and flexible interconnection.
**Falsification:** Queue capacity is not equivalent to committed future projects; attrition and speculative applications matter.

## 14 — When Power Goes Negative
**Job:** Reinterpret negative prices and curtailment as signals for storage, flexible load, transmission, and improved market design.
**Falsification:** Negative prices have multiple causes and are not a pure solar statistic.

## 15 — Demand Learns the Weather
**Job:** Show how forecasting and automation can move non-urgent work toward abundant electricity without degrading the service customers want.
**Falsification:** Many loads are genuinely inflexible; coordination can create rebound peaks, privacy concerns, and reliability risk.

## 16 — The Electric Household
**Job:** Show how electrified transport, heat, hot water, storage, and solar turn the home into a coordinated energy portfolio.
**Falsification:** High upfront costs, rental housing, service-panel limits, and local-grid constraints can exclude households from benefits.

## 17 — The Industrial Sun
**Job:** Follow cheap electricity into heat, materials, hydrogen, computation, and industrial siting.
**Falsification:** Not every industrial process can or should follow hourly solar prices; throughput, quality, chemistry, and capital utilization can dominate.

## 18 — The Solar South
**Job:** Make financing cost, affordability, off-grid access, utility solvency, and productive demand central to solar deployment in developing economies.
**Falsification:** Do not treat emerging and developing economies as one market or assume distributed solar substitutes for strong grids at industrial scale.

## 19 — Energy Sovereignty Without a Pipeline
**Job:** Distinguish continuous fuel dependence from episodic equipment and supply-chain dependence.
**Falsification:** Domestic sunlight does not equal industrial autarky; modules, finance, software, networks, materials, and replacement capability still create dependencies.

## 20 — The Land Under the Light
**Job:** Treat land, siting, host communities, agrivoltaics, brownfields, transmission corridors, and reversibility as infrastructure design.
**Falsification:** Land comparisons must use consistent system boundaries and should not erase local ecological or cultural value.

## 21 — The Recycling Century
**Job:** Reframe retired modules and grid equipment as future material inventories and make end-of-life design part of present infrastructure.
**Falsification:** Technical recyclability does not guarantee economic recycling; collection, purity, commodity value, and policy matter.

## 22 — The Dark Week Problem
**Job:** Stress-test the thesis against sustained low-output periods, winter peaks, long-duration balancing, and rare reliability events.
**Falsification:** If total reliability costs at high solar penetration systematically dominate plausible firm low-carbon alternatives, the organizing claim should be weakened.

## 23 — The Politics of Cheap Noon
**Job:** Show that falling generation cost redistributes rents and forces renegotiation of tariffs, ownership, permitting, incumbent assets, and transition costs.
**Falsification:** Aggregate savings do not guarantee politically durable distribution; household bills and local losses matter.

## 24 — Infrastructure Under a Star
**Job:** Synthesize the book without claiming solar monopoly: the century is solar when enough complementary infrastructure reorganizes around abundant daylight electricity.
**Falsification:** The title is too strong if solar remains a peripheral resource that does not materially shape storage, grid, demand, industrial, or market design.
