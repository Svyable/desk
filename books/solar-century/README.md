The twentieth century built energy around fuel: extract it, refine it, transport it, burn it, and move the resulting power outward from large machines. *Solar Century* argues that photovoltaics change the architecture rather than merely substituting one generator for another. Once generation becomes a mass-manufactured, zero-fuel-cost surface that can be deployed from rooftops to gigawatt fields, scarcity migrates into storage, transmission, distribution, transformers, interconnection, materials, financing, land, flexible demand, software, and political permission.

The central argument is:

> Fossil systems move fuel through space. Solar systems must move electricity through space **and time**.

And the governing law of the manuscript is:

> **Cheap generation does not eliminate scarcity. It relocates scarcity.**

## The book's lane

This is not a book arguing that every electron should come from solar, that batteries solve every duration, or that declining module prices automatically produce a cheap and reliable electricity system. It treats firm generation, transmission, storage, demand flexibility, grid stability, supply-chain concentration, financing, land conflict, mining, recycling, winter scarcity, and political resistance as first-order parts of the story.

The novelty target is the synthesis: the panel is the visible object, while the revolution is everything the panel forces the rest of the infrastructure to become. The book follows the migration of scarcity after the generator becomes cheap.

*Solar Century* also remains distinct from Sven Hardy Benson's *Energy, Value & Intelligence*. That book asks how energy, intelligence, and value interact across technological civilization. This manuscript stays inside one transformation and follows its physical and institutional consequences from the cell to the continent.
