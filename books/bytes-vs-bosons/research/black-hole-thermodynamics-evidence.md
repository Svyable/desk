# BYTES VS BOSONS — black-hole thermodynamics evidence note

Scope: Chapters 5–7. This note locks the historical and epistemic sequence before the manuscript moves into quantum information.

## 1. The order matters

The manuscript should preserve this progression:

1. classical black-hole area theorem;
2. Bekenstein’s entropy proposal and generalized second law;
3. four laws of black-hole mechanics;
4. Hawking radiation and a physical temperature;
5. Bekenstein-Hawking normalization;
6. evaporation and the pure-to-mixed information problem.

Do not narrate later holographic ideas backward into Bekenstein’s original papers.

## 2. Bekenstein 1973: what was actually claimed

Primary source:
- Jacob D. Bekenstein, “Black Holes and Entropy,” *Physical Review D* 7, 2333 (1973).
- https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.7.2333

Supported:
- black-hole area and entropy share an irreversible-growth analogy;
- assigning black-hole entropy helps preserve a generalized second law when ordinary entropy crosses the horizon;
- Bekenstein used information-theoretic language concerning inaccessible information about the interior;
- the entropy should scale as horizon area in Planck units times a dimensionless constant.

Not yet fixed in this paper:
- the final factor of 1/4;
- Hawking temperature;
- a unique microscopic theory of what the entropy counts;
- the later holographic principle.

Editorial hold:
- never say Bekenstein “proved information is stored on the horizon.”

## 3. Classical area theorem is conditional

Relevant sources:
- Stephen Hawking, “Gravitational Radiation from Colliding Black Holes,” *Physical Review Letters* 26, 1344 (1971).
- James Bardeen, Brandon Carter and Stephen Hawking, “The Four Laws of Black Hole Mechanics,” *Communications in Mathematical Physics* 31, 161 (1973).
- https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01645742

The classical nondecrease of horizon area is not an unconditional law of all quantum gravity.

It relies on the classical general-relativistic framework and energy/regularity assumptions. Hawking radiation later allows the horizon area to decrease because semiclassical quantum effects invalidate the classical conditions used by the theorem.

Editorial rule:
- say “classically, under the theorem’s assumptions” rather than “black-hole area can never decrease.”

## 4. Four laws: analogy before Hawking temperature

Bardeen-Carter-Hawking (1973) establishes the structural laws of stationary black-hole mechanics.

Safe summary:
- surface gravity is constant over a stationary horizon under the relevant conditions;
- the first law relates changes in mass, area, angular momentum and charge;
- the second law is the classical area nondecrease theorem;
- the third law concerns unattainability of zero surface gravity in finite physical operations.

Historical discipline:
- in 1973 the formal analogy did not yet establish a physical black-hole temperature;
- Hawking’s subsequent quantum calculation is what turns the temperature analogy into a real radiative temperature.

## 5. Hawking radiation: strong theory, no direct astrophysical detection

Primary sources:
- Stephen Hawking, “Black hole explosions?,” *Nature* 248, 30–31 (1974). https://doi.org/10.1038/248030a0
- Stephen Hawking, “Particle Creation by Black Holes,” *Communications in Mathematical Physics* 43, 199–220 (1975). https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02345020
- Stephen Hawking, “Black holes and thermodynamics,” *Physical Review D* 13, 191 (1976). https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.13.191

Supported:
- quantum fields in a black-hole spacetime yield outgoing radiation with a thermal temperature related to surface gravity;
- for Schwarzschild holes, temperature is inversely proportional to mass;
- combining temperature with the first law fixes the entropy normalization at one quarter of horizon area in Planck units.

Important qualification:
- direct Hawking radiation from an astrophysical black hole has not been observed;
- analogue-gravity experiments are not direct observations of gravitational black-hole evaporation.

## 6. Do not teach the pair-creation cartoon as the mechanism

The “virtual pair near the horizon, one falls in, one escapes” story can be used only as a labeled heuristic.

It is not Hawking’s derivation.

The derivation is quantum field theory in curved spacetime, comparing mode structure across a collapsing geometry. Particle definitions and vacuum structure are subtle in curved spacetime.

Publication preference:
- omit the pair cartoon unless a later edit can make its limitations unmistakable.

## 7. Greybody factors

“Hawking radiation is thermal” is an idealized statement that needs one sentence of refinement.

The radiation measured at infinity is filtered by the curved-spacetime potential outside the horizon. Frequency-dependent transmission probabilities (“greybody factors”) modify the emitted spectrum and flux.

This does not remove the thermodynamic temperature.

It prevents the reader from imagining a perfect laboratory blackbody spectrum at infinity with no propagation effects.

## 8. The 1976 information-loss claim is historical, not current consensus wording

Primary source:
- Stephen Hawking, “Breakdown of Predictability in Gravitational Collapse,” *Physical Review D* 14, 2460 (1976).
- https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.14.2460

Hawking’s claim:
- semiclassical evaporation produces a mixed final state from a pure initial state;
- the process therefore cannot be represented by an ordinary unitary S-matrix;
- information loss is fundamental in that proposal.

Editorial rule:
- attribute the claim to Hawking’s 1976 position.
- do not write “black holes destroy information” as a settled modern fact.

## 9. Page curve and island results: major theoretical progress, model-dependent

Useful primary sources:
- Geoff Penington, Stephen H. Shenker, Douglas Stanford and Zhenbin Yang, “Replica wormholes and the black hole interior,” arXiv:1911.11977. https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.11977
- Ahmed Almheiri, Thomas Hartman, Juan Maldacena, Edgar Shaghoulian and Amirhossein Tajdini, “Replica Wormholes and the Entropy of Hawking Radiation,” arXiv:1911.12333. https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.12333

Supported:
- gravitational path-integral/island calculations can reproduce Page-curve behavior in controlled models;
- these results are important evidence that semiclassical gravity plus generalized entropy contains more information-recovery structure than Hawking’s original calculation captured.

Do not upgrade to:
- “the paradox is experimentally solved”;
- “we know exactly how information escapes a real astrophysical black hole”;
- “wormholes have been observed carrying the information out.”

Interpretations vary.

Safe:
- entropy scales with area;

- microscopic state counting is a central quantum-gravity problem.

Unsafe:
- each Planck area is literally one conventional computer bit;
- the event horizon is a material storage surface;

## 11. “No hair” language needs caveats

The manuscript may say that familiar stationary black-hole solutions are characterized macroscopically by a small set such as mass, angular momentum and charge.

Avoid:
- presenting “no hair” as a theorem covering every matter field, boundary condition or modified-gravity theory without exception.

The narrative point is exterior macroscopic compression, not a universal classification theorem.

## 12. Part II checkpoint before Chapter 8

Chapters 5–7 should leave the reader with four separate statements:

1. **Classical gravity:** horizon area has a nondecrease theorem under stated assumptions.
2. **Black-hole thermodynamics:** entropy is proportional to horizon area and temperature to surface gravity.
3. **Semiclassical QFT:** black holes radiate and can evaporate.
4. **Quantum-information problem:** Hawking’s original evaporation calculation appears incompatible with unitary pure-state evolution if taken to completion.

Do not merge those into the slogan “information is on the horizon.”

Chapter 8 can now introduce no-cloning because the reader has a concrete reason quantum information rules matter.
