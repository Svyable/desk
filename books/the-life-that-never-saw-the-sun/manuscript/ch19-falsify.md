# Chapter 19 — What Would Falsify the Story

A good deep-biosphere story needs ways to lose.

The strongest version of this book’s thesis is not that radiolysis matters somewhere. That is already documented. The stronger proposition is that radiolysis can be a quantitatively important primary energy source for long-lived subsurface ecosystems and can therefore expand the plausible habitable volume of rocky worlds.

Several observations could force that claim downward.

If careful site-level budgets showed that radiolytic hydrogen production is consistently too small to match measured microbial maintenance demand, then radiolysis would become background chemistry rather than ecological power. If isotopic and hydrological work showed that supposedly isolated communities are routinely sustained by recent surface organic carbon or electron acceptors, the phrase photosphere-independent would need major retreat. If improved contamination controls erased the strongest examples of indigenous deep communities, the empirical foundation would narrow.

For *Desulforudis* specifically, a decisive test is not whether the organism can be found again. It has been. The harder test is whether its in situ energy and nutrient budget closes without hidden imports. That requires simultaneous measurement of hydrogen production and consumption, sulfate generation and loss, carbon fixation, nitrogen source, phosphorus and trace-metal flux, cell abundance and maintenance power.

For the “single-species ecosystem” idea, mineral-attached metagenomes and viromes could defeat the clean version by revealing ecologically essential rare partners overlooked in fracture water. That would not erase *Desulforudis*; it would change the unit of autonomy from a genome to a community.

For the Mars extrapolation, the claim loses if realistic water availability is effectively zero in the crustal settings where radiolytic energy is sufficient, or if oxidant/reductant production does not coexist at usable scales. A favorable energy calculation without liquid water is a habitable spreadsheet, not a habitat.

For icy moons, the model weakens if useful radiolytic products cannot be transported to liquid environments faster than they are destroyed or sequestered.

The cleanest modern experiment would be a cross-site energy audit. Select several deep continental fracture systems spanning radionuclide abundance, rock type and hydrological isolation. Measure the complete redox budget prospectively. Quantify radiolysis from actual U-Th-K distributions and water geometry. Measure H2 flux rather than concentration alone. Isolate fractures with packers. Use metatranscriptomics and isotope tracers to determine active pathways. Run contamination blanks at every stage. Compare predicted supportable biomass with measured intact-cell abundance.

Then preregister the model before seeing the biology.

If radiolysis predicts where low-carbon deep autotrophy is strongest, the thesis gains real causal weight. If it does not, the book has mistaken an elegant mechanism for a dominant one.

That is an acceptable outcome.

A theory of hidden life should be easier to kill than hidden life itself.
