# Research Notes — The Life That Never Saw the Sun

## Central distinction

The manuscript separates **local energetic autonomy** from **global biogeochemical autonomy**. Radiolysis can generate H2 locally and, in some mineral systems, oxidants capable of generating sulfate. That does not establish that every nutrient and electron acceptor in a field site is locally regenerated or independent of surface history.

## Four-class working rule

- **Class 1:** directly measured, peer-reviewed, or otherwise documented.
- **Class 2:** serious interpretation with incomplete constraint or model dependence.
- **Class 3:** disputed claim, overextension, or unconfirmed inference.
- **Class 4:** speculative synthesis or proposed experiment.

## Contradictions / pressure points

- Mponeng 2008: >99.9% *D. audaxviator* in sampled fluid phase vs. broader possible mineral-attached/viral/rare community.
- “Independent of the Sun”: strong for immediate H2 energy supply in radiolysis-driven systems; weaker if sulfate or nutrients inherit surface-derived oxidation history.
- Genome potential vs. active metabolism: encoded pathways do not by themselves prove in-situ flux.
- Ancient groundwater ages vs. perfect isolation: noble-gas residence constraints permit mixing and do not date every molecule equally.
- Deep nematodes demonstrate persistence/colonization at depth, not subterranean origin of animal life.
- Mars radiolysis models demonstrate energetic habitability under assumed groundwater conditions, not extant life.
- Sweden 2024 strengthens recurrence of *Desulforudis* as a deep primary producer while weakening any universal single-species-ecosystem narrative.

## Incentive / distortion audit

Deep-life results attract extreme-language incentives: “life without the Sun,” “single-species ecosystem,” “worms from hell,” and “nuclear-powered microbes.” These phrases are useful entry points but can collapse mechanism and provenance. Astrobiology adds a second incentive: terrestrial results are more newsworthy when linked to alien life. The manuscript therefore preserves the terrestrial result even when the planetary analogy fails.
