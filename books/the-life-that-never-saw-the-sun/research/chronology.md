# Chronology

- **1990s** — Modern deep-biosphere work accelerates as drilling and mine access reveal microorganisms kilometers beneath the surface.
- **2002** — Chapelle et al. report a hydrogen-based subsurface microbial community dominated by methanogens in deep igneous-rock groundwater in Idaho.
- **2005** — Lin et al. publish quantitative evidence that radiolysis can account for exceptionally high H2 in deep Witwatersrand fracture waters and support lithoautotrophic communities.
- **2008** — Chivian et al. publish the complete genome of *Candidatus Desulforudis audaxviator* from ~2.8 km depth and describe the sampled fluid-phase community as >99.9% dominated by the organism.
- **2011** — Borgonie et al. report nematodes from 0.9–3.6 km deep South African fracture waters, including *Halicephalobus mephisto*.
- **2013** — Holland et al. report noble-gas evidence for fracture-fluid components isolated for roughly 1.5–2.64 billion years in Canadian Shield rock at ~2.4 km depth.
- **2016** — Bagnoud et al. reconstruct a hydrogen-driven microbial metabolic network in Opalinus Clay and explicitly discuss the distinction between local chemolithotrophy and ultimate sulfur provenance.
- **2018** — Magnabosco et al. estimate the continental subsurface hosts roughly 2–6 × 10^29 prokaryotic cells; Dzaugis et al. quantify radiolytic H2 production in martian environments.
- **2021** — Tarnas et al. model present-day martian subsurface regions where radiolysis could supply sufficient redox energy for Earth-like sulfate reducers if groundwater is present.
- **2024** — Westmeijer et al. report a 975-m Swedish groundwater community dominated by *Candidatus Desulforudis audaxviator*, with broader prokaryotic and eukaryotic diversity than the classic Mponeng sample.
- **2025** — New modeling extends ionizing-radiation/radiolysis habitability calculations across Mars, Europa and Enceladus, remaining explicitly theoretical with respect to life detection.
- **2026** — This manuscript treats the terrestrial record as a calibration set: demonstrated radiochemistry and biology on Earth, modeled habitability elsewhere, no extraterrestrial detection claimed.
