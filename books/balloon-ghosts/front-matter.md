**Sven Hardy Benson**

## Note on evidence

This book is about a category error that secrecy makes easy.

People see an object. The object is real. The explanation is withheld. A false explanation is supplied. Years later the false explanation is exposed. The exposure is then taken as proof that the most extraordinary surviving explanation must be true.

That final move does not follow.

The chapters therefore keep four evidence classes separate: documented/declassified facts; credible but incomplete reporting or testimony; disputed claims without independent confirmation; and speculative synthesis. A classified balloon program is evidence for a classified balloon program. A misleading government statement is evidence that officials misled the public. Neither fact, by itself, establishes extraterrestrial hardware.

The converse matters too. Showing that governments ran secret aircraft and balloon programs does not explain every unresolved sighting. `Unknown` is a statement about the state of an investigation. It is not a synonym for `alien`, `hoax`, or `classified program`.

The interesting subject is what happens to knowledge when the true answer cannot be said.
