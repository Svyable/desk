# Front Matter

## The Belt We Made

### Starfish Prime and the Day Humanity Changed Near-Earth Space

Sven Hardy Benson

---

There are technologies that alter the world because they are used at scale.

There are others that alter the world because the world is part of the machine.

Starfish Prime belonged to the second category before almost anyone had a vocabulary for it.

In July 1962 the United States exploded a thermonuclear device high above the Pacific. The test lasted an instant. One of its consequences did not. Energetic electrons became trapped by Earth's magnetic field and formed an artificial radiation population that remained measurable for years. Satellites encountered an environment their designers had not planned to exist because, until a government made it, it did not exist.

This book is about that category error.

It is also about how easily historical facts become folklore. Starfish is routinely invoked in arguments about EMP, grid collapse, satellite vulnerability, nuclear war, and even the militarization of space. Some of those arguments rest on real observations. Others enlarge an observation until it becomes a myth. The discipline here is to stay close to measurements, contemporary engineering reports, satellite failure analyses, treaty records, and the limits of what those records can prove.

The stranger truth does not need help. We made a radiation belt.
