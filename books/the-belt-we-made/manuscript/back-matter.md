# Back Matter

## A Note on Evidence

This manuscript uses four evidence classes throughout: documented facts, credible but incomplete evidence, disputed or unconfirmed claims, and speculative synthesis. These classes are not rankings of emotional importance. They are reminders of what kind of sentence the evidence can support.

The central historical claims are unusually well supported because NASA, AEC/DOE, military, treaty, and engineering records survive publicly. The weakest areas are the popular retrospective counts: exactly how many satellites should be described as "lost" because of Starfish, exactly which Hawaiian failures should be assigned to which EMP component, and exactly how individual decision-makers weighted the artificial-belt problem in treaty politics. Those questions require narrower language, not stronger adjectives.

## Research Note

A publication pass should privilege original 1962–1967 technical reporting wherever possible and use modern institutional summaries mainly for orientation. The primary scientific sequence—Hess, Explorer XV, Telstar failure analysis, artificial-belt decay papers—should carry the book's technical spine.
