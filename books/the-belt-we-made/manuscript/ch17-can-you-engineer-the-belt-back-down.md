# 17. Can You Engineer the Belt Back Down?

Once humans learned that they could create artificial trapped radiation, a symmetric question followed: could they remove it?

The idea appears in later discussions of radiation-belt remediation. If very-low-frequency waves or other controlled electromagnetic interactions can change particle pitch angles, perhaps trapped electrons can be encouraged into atmospheric loss cones and precipitated out of the belts.

This is a real research area. It is not a demonstrated planetary cleanup service.

The distinction is essential because remediation proposals are often described at the level of mechanism. A wave-particle interaction can be physically real without providing an efficient, controllable, safe, economical way to modify a large radiation population at scale.

Starfish is a warning against confusing mechanism with system capability in both directions.

We knew trapped particles could be created before we knew how persistent the population would become. We should not repeat the reasoning error by assuming that because a loss mechanism exists, large-scale remediation is therefore straightforward.

The asymmetry is familiar in environmental engineering. Pollution can be easy to produce and expensive to remove. Debris can be easy to create and hard to deorbit. A side effect can be an output of normal operation while cleanup requires a separate infrastructure.

The magnetosphere obeys no moral preference for reversibility.

A technology that can push a system away from baseline does not automatically contain its own undo button.
