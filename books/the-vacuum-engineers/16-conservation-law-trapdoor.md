# Chapter 16 — The Conservation-Law Trapdoor

Every exotic propulsion claim eventually approaches the same trapdoor: momentum conservation.

A device inside a closed system cannot produce net center-of-mass acceleration without exchanging momentum with something external or revealing that the system boundary was drawn incorrectly. This is not a bureaucratic preference. It is one of the most deeply tested organizing principles in physics.

Vacuum-propulsion proposals therefore have only a few intellectually honest options. The device exchanges momentum with real emitted radiation. It couples to an external field or medium. It changes the stress-energy distribution in a way already accounted for by general relativity. Or it reveals new physics that extends the conservation ledger without merely deleting it.

'Pushing on the quantum vacuum' is not yet a mechanism. The phrase must identify the field degrees of freedom receiving momentum and explain why the process does not reduce to a closed internal interaction.

This is why reactionless-thrust language often hides more than it explains. Rockets are not special because they carry propellant; photons propel too. Solar sails exchange momentum without expelling onboard mass. Electrodynamic tethers push against planetary magnetic fields. A vehicle can be propellantless without being reactionless.

Metric engineering is stranger because in general relativity matter and geometry participate in one coupled stress-energy system. But the same discipline applies: write the geometry, write the required stress-energy, specify how it is generated, and close the energy-momentum accounting.

If a theory proposes that the vacuum supplies the missing term, that term must be measurable somewhere.

There is no shame in failing this test. Most interesting propulsion ideas should fail. The value is in learning exactly where.

A trapdoor is useful when it is marked on the floor.
