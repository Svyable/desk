# Chapter 17 — What Would Change Physics

A fringe technology becomes less fringe when it predicts a result that the ordinary model says should not happen, then survives the experiment designed to kill it.

For vacuum propulsion, the decisive result would not be a spectacular video.

It would be a boring residual.

Imagine three independent laboratories receive geometrically identical devices, some active and some internally disabled. The active devices generate a force vector that reverses with electrical phase or geometry exactly as preregistered. The force persists as chamber pressure falls through the regime where ion wind and outgassing vanish. It survives cable rerouting, magnetic shielding, thermal matching, dummy loads and apparatus rotation. It scales with a theoretically specified vacuum-boundary parameter rather than dissipated heat. The integrated momentum exceeds the maximum possible photon recoil by orders of magnitude. Independent balances agree.

That would not prove zero-point propulsion.

It would prove that the conventional force inventory is incomplete.

That sentence should be engraved above every exotic-propulsion laboratory.

A residual comes before an explanation.

The history of fringe propulsion is full of effects that were promoted to mechanisms too quickly. A balance deflects and somebody announces reactionless thrust. A capacitor moves and somebody announces electrogravity. A microwave cavity drifts and somebody announces vacuum momentum. Once the mechanism enters the name of the effect, null tests become emotionally expensive.

The better program names the observable first.

Force versus command state.

Acceleration versus applied force.

Clock shift versus field configuration.

Neutral-mass displacement versus source state.

Interferometric phase versus geometry.

No ontology is required to collect the data.

The vacuum-engineering ladder should begin lower than propulsion.

Stage zero is calibration.

Build a force-measurement system capable of detecting known forces below and above the target anomaly. Inject photon recoil with a calibrated optical source. Apply electrostatic forces with a known electrode geometry. Introduce controlled magnetic coupling. Heat a dummy load. Drive a piezoelectric actuator. Move a cable deliberately. The team should demonstrate that its apparatus can identify the signatures of ordinary mechanisms before it looks for an extraordinary one.

This is not glamorous, which is why it is rarely the viral part.

It is the part that makes every later plot interpretable.

Stage one is the closed conventional force budget.

For a claimed vacuum thruster, every measured momentum path gets an upper bound. Residual gas. Outgassing. Photon radiation. Electromagnetic interaction with chamber walls. Magnetic interaction with Earth’s field and nearby materials. Thermal expansion. Radiometric effects. Mechanical vibration. Cable forces. Charge leakage. Ground currents. Data-analysis drift.

The word “negligible” should be rationed.

A nuisance force is negligible only relative to the claimed signal and after measurement or a defensible bound.

Suppose the device reports one micronewton. A thermal artifact bounded at ten nanonewtons can be neglected provisionally. A thermal model with an uncertainty of two micronewtons cannot. The scale of the anomaly determines the cruelty of the controls.

Stage two is the blind device family.

One active device is a story.

A family is physics.

Construct multiple units whose internal geometry varies along a theoretically meaningful axis. If the claim involves Casimir mode structure, vary separation, area, material response or cavity geometry in a way that produces a quantitative predicted change. If the claim involves a polarizable-vacuum effect, vary the parameter the theory says should control coupling. If the claim involves an inertia mechanism, vary acceleration frequency or environmental field state according to the model.

Include shams that reproduce heat, current and mass distribution without the active geometry.

The analyst does not know which is which.

The force law should emerge from the coded matrix.

This is much harder to fool than a hero device.

Stage three is laboratory independence.

The second lab should not clone the first lab’s weaknesses.

Different balance architecture. Different vacuum chamber. Different high-voltage or RF supply. Different software. Different building. Ideally different country and research culture.

Send the theoretical scaling law before sending the device.

If a signal exists only on the original torsion pendulum, the torsion pendulum is still a suspect. If it survives a flexure balance and an optical force sensor with different systematics, the suspect list shrinks.

Independence should include negative expertise.

Invite a precision metrologist who thinks the effect is nonsense. Invite an RF engineer who has spent a career finding leakage. Invite a vacuum scientist who knows what polymers do after six hours under pumpdown. Invite a gravitational experimentalist who distrusts every cable in the room.

Hostility is a laboratory resource.

Stage four is momentum accounting.

A device that appears to accelerate without propellant does not automatically violate momentum conservation because momentum can leave through fields, radiation or interaction with the environment. The entire system boundary must be drawn honestly.

Enclose the device electromagnetically where possible. Monitor radiated power and angular distribution. Measure forces on the chamber or nearby structures. Change the distance to boundaries. Suspend the source and enclosure as one system if the geometry allows. Look for equal-and-opposite momentum where conventional field theory predicts it.

A true reactionless claim needs more than thrust on the internal component.

It needs center-of-mass behavior of the closed apparatus inconsistent with ordinary momentum conservation.

That is a ferocious standard.

It should be.

Stage five asks whether the anomaly is vacuum-specific at all.

This is where many theories become vulnerable.

If the claimed mechanism is altered electromagnetic mode density, deliberately vary that mode density while holding dissipated power constant. If the effect is tied to a Casimir-like geometry, change the separation or boundary material in the regime where QED predicts a known change. If the force does not track the allegedly causal vacuum variable, stop calling it vacuum thrust.

The technology can survive under another name.

The explanation cannot.

If a force does track a vacuum-state parameter, the result becomes much more interesting. It still may be conventional electromagnetic physics. Boundary-condition changes alter capacitance, fields, forces, losses and thermal behavior too. The experiment must show that the scaling cannot be explained by those ordinary consequences.

Stage six is energy accounting.

This is where zero-point energy claims often evaporate.

Measure all input power over a complete cycle, including preparation, switching, cryogenics, pumps, reset operations and control electronics. Measure mechanical work and radiation leaving the system. If a claimed vacuum-energy device returns to its initial state after a cycle and produces net energy beyond all inputs, the result is extraordinary.

Do not extrapolate from a one-way release.

A compressed spring releases energy too.

The question is whether the system can repeat without paying the reset cost.

The dynamical Casimir effect is an excellent control lesson. Photons appear from a modulated boundary condition, but the modulation is driven. Any proposed vacuum-power device should identify where its analogue of the pump is hiding.

Stage seven separates inertia from thrust.

If a device allegedly manipulates inertia, thrust measurement is not enough. Apply a calibrated external force to a test mass under active and sham vacuum conditions. Measure acceleration. Reverse the active state. Swap masses. Check whether the inferred inertial mass changes.

Then measure weight or free fall separately.

If inertial response changes but gravitational response does not, equivalence is altered one way. If both change proportionally, it is altered another way. If neither changes and only the energized apparatus pushes, the effect belongs in propulsion or electromagnetism, not inertia control.

This is where a one-percent claim would be almost embarrassingly easy to test compared with the historical mystery around it.

Large new physics does not always require large apparatus.

Stage eight is the metric test.

Metric engineering demands an independently measurable spacetime effect: differential clock rate, interferometric phase consistent with curvature rather than refractive index, gravitational acceleration on a neutral test mass, or another observable that cannot be reproduced by ordinary electromagnetic stress and material deformation.

The sensor should ideally be outside the active material.

This matters for metamaterial and analog-gravity claims. A medium can make light propagate as though it inhabits an effective curved geometry. That is not the same as curving the spacetime experienced by a neutral atom next to the medium.

Put the atom outside.

Put a clock outside.

Put a neutral torsion balance outside.

If the “metric” disappears when the probe is no longer part of the engineered electromagnetic medium, the result remains interesting optics. It is not gravitational engineering.

Stage nine is scaling.

A real anomaly without a usable scaling law is new physics waiting for explanation, not a propulsion architecture.

Vary stored energy. Geometry. Field strength. Frequency. Material. Temperature. Acceleration history. Gravitational orientation. Device size.

The theory must predict which variables matter and how.

A force that grows linearly with dissipated power across every configuration has a different smell from a force that follows a boundary-dependent vacuum observable while heat is held fixed. A gravitational effect should be checked against the stress-energy actually introduced by the apparatus. If the claimed enhancement is a billion times larger than ordinary general relativity predicts, that enhancement factor is not a detail. It is the new law.

Stage ten is adversarial replication at useful scale.

Only now should aerospace performance enter the conversation.

Can the effect produce enough force per unit mass, input power, field strength and volume to matter? Can the device operate continuously? Does it survive materials limits? Does it dump heat? Do required voltages cause breakdown? Does the performance law improve or collapse with scale?

A scientifically real nanonewton anomaly can be worth a Nobel Prize and still be worthless for propulsion.

Do not insult the discovery by pretending usefulness is required.

This staged program also tells us what classified evidence would have to look like if it ever surfaced.

A memorandum saying “vacuum energy appears promising” is stage minus one.

A contractor report deriving a metric is theoretical work.

A patent is a proposed implementation.

A test video is an observation without controls.

A calibrated force curve with blind shams, environmental logs and independent replication is evidence of a physical effect.

A neutral-clock shift or external gravitational signal tied to the device state is evidence of something far stranger.

The hierarchy prevents government letterhead from performing laboratory work by association.

It also protects whistleblower testimony from being asked to do the impossible. A credible witness can establish that a program, briefing or claim existed. They cannot, by testimony alone, establish that an apparatus obeyed a new force law. That requires the measurement chain.

This is where UAP enters responsibly.

Suppose a military sensor record shows an object with apparently extreme acceleration and no visible exhaust. That can motivate interest in unusual propulsion. It does not specify the mechanism. Metric engineering, inertia reduction, plasma effects, sensor geometry, conventional classified technology and data error are competing hypotheses at that stage.

Reverse engineering from performance to vacuum physics skips too many arrows.

The better approach is to extract mechanism-independent constraints first: range, velocity, acceleration, thermal output, spectral signatures, acoustic behavior, interaction with air or water, electromagnetic emissions, inertial consequences. Then ask which propulsion families can fit the observations.

If vacuum engineering is one candidate, it should make additional predictions.

A metric mechanism might affect clocks or propagation near the object. An inertia-control mechanism might change structural loads relative to observed acceleration. A conventional plasma system might show ionization or RF signatures. A sensor artifact might fail independent geometry.

The UAP case becomes an experiment only when it generates discriminating measurements.

The same logic applies to secrecy.

A hidden propulsion program would still need materials, power systems, metrology, test infrastructure and people. If the underlying effect is strong, adjacent civilian experiments may constrain it even without access to the program. Precision equivalence tests, Casimir measurements, inertial metrology, quantum optics and gravitational sensors collectively reduce the space in which a giant new coupling can hide.

This is not proof that classified physics cannot exist.

It is a way to stop classification from becoming an infinite residual category.

The experiment ladder can also fail productively.

Suppose stage one finds every claimed thrust correlates with cable motion. Publish that.

Suppose stage three cannot replicate the original force. Publish the sensitivity and the mismatch.

Suppose stage five finds a real force but it scales with an ordinary dielectric property rather than vacuum mode structure. That may still be useful propulsion engineering.

Suppose stage eight measures no external metric effect to exquisite precision. That constrains the coupling implied by the model.

A good frontier program produces knowledge even when the starship dies.

This was one of the best features of NASA’s Breakthrough Propulsion Physics effort. The program’s existence did not mean NASA believed every candidate. It meant some extraordinary claims could be converted into experiments, literature assessments and constraints. Several died or narrowed. That is success in the research sense.

The next program should be even more explicit about the evidentiary ladder.

No press release at stage zero.

No propulsion language for a force that has not closed its momentum budget.

No inertia language for thrust alone.

No metric language for an optical effective medium alone.

No energy-source language without a complete cycle.

No classified-success inference from a funded theoretical report.

The romance can survive these rules.

In fact it becomes better.

Imagine the signal makes it through.

Three laboratories. Different balances. Blind devices. High vacuum. Thermal and magnetic controls. Force scaling with a preregistered vacuum-boundary variable. Closed radiation budget. Center-of-mass anomaly in the isolated system. Then an external neutral atom interferometer sees a correlated acceleration or phase shift beyond the electromagnetic model.

At that point nobody needs to say “Hal Puthoff was right.”

One would need to say something much more dangerous:

The apparatus is right.

The next experiment would ask whether the anomaly couples to local gravitational potential, Casimir geometry, material permittivity, acceleration history, electromagnetic mode density or some other variable. Competing theories would acquire distinct scaling laws. The phenomenon would move from anecdote to metrology.

If no anomaly survives, the limits themselves matter. They constrain entire families of models. If one survives, the world changes in a way that does not require anybody to believe a witness, a contractor, a classification story or a patent.

Physics changes when the residual stops caring who wants it to be true.

The residual also has to know what variable made it.
