# Chapter 7 — Robert Forward's Permission Slip

Long before 'metric engineering' became Pentagon-adjacent vocabulary, Robert L. Forward was already asking whether general relativity and quantum vacuum effects might be engineered rather than merely observed.

Forward is important because he was not a mascot for limitless energy. He was an aerospace physicist with a taste for outrageous devices and an equal taste for equations. His papers and books treated gravitational engineering, negative mass, Casimir forces and exotic propulsion as design problems whose absurdity had to be quantified.

That style matters.

The frontier often divides into two bad camps. One says an idea violates intuition and therefore should not be calculated. The other says an equation permits a configuration and therefore civilization is one materials breakthrough away from building it. Forward's better habit was to calculate the price.

The price of spacetime engineering is usually where the romance dies. Energy densities become astronomical. Negative-energy requirements collide with quantum inequalities. Structures require forms of matter not known to exist in usable bulk. Stability and causality problems appear after the first elegant metric.

Yet the calculation is not wasted. It converts impossible-sounding language into constraints.

Puthoff's later vacuum-engineering work belongs to this Forward tradition more than to the free-energy tradition when read in its strongest form. The claim is not that a garage device already extracts limitless power. The claim is that known physics contains vacuum and metric structures weird enough that an advanced engineering analysis should ask what controls them.

That is a much narrower proposition.

A good fringe book should preserve it. The fact that a technology is wildly impractical under known constraints is not equivalent to a theorem that no future physics can ever alter those constraints. But it is also not permission to treat the constraint as optional.

Forward's real gift was permission to ask the embarrassing question and then require the embarrassing number.
