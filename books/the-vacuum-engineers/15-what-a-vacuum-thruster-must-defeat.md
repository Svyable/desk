# Chapter 15 — What a Vacuum Thruster Must Defeat

A claimed vacuum thruster does not begin by defeating relativity. It begins by defeating the laboratory.

Millinewton and micronewton force measurements are fertile ground for artifacts: thermal expansion shifts the center of mass; cables apply torques; magnetic fields couple to current loops; outgassing produces momentum; vibration rectifies through suspension systems; electrostatic charge pulls on nearby structures; RF leakage interacts with the chamber; convection survives where experimenters thought the gas was irrelevant.

The history of propellantless-thrust claims is crowded with effects that weakened as metrology improved. That history should not make anomalous thrust untestable. It should dictate the test.

A serious vacuum-propulsion experiment needs at least three independent force sensors based on different physical principles, a pressure sweep into high vacuum, dummy loads matched for thermal and electromagnetic behavior, orientation reversals, blind configuration swaps, full RF characterization, magnetic mapping, thermal imaging, cable-force calibration, vibration logging and a momentum budget that includes emitted photons.

The strongest version would be run at multiple laboratories with hardware shipped in sealed, randomized configurations. The analysis code and defeat criteria would be fixed before unblinding.

Then comes scaling. A residual that tracks gas density is not vacuum propulsion. A residual that tracks dissipated power but changes with cable routing is not vacuum propulsion. A residual that survives vacuum but scales exactly with photon momentum deserves no new physics. A residual that reverses with apparatus orientation while environmental channels do not deserves attention.

Only after that do we ask whether the force couples to zero-point fields, spacetime geometry or something else.

The fringe often wants the grand theory first. Metrology should be crueler. First establish that something pushed.
