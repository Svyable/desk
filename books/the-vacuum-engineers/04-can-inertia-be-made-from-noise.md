# Chapter 4 — Can Inertia Be Made From Noise?

The most consequential Puthoff-adjacent proposal is not free energy. It is the attempt by Bernard Haisch, Alfonso Rueda and Puthoff to derive inertia from interaction between accelerating matter and the electromagnetic zero-point field.

If that mechanism were right in a technologically controllable way, propulsion would acquire a new lever. Change the coupling and one might imagine changing inertial response. This is the precise point at which an elegant explanatory proposal becomes an engineering temptation.

The 1994 Physical Review A paper by Haisch, Rueda and Puthoff argued that the reaction force conventionally called inertia could arise from electromagnetic zero-point interactions. The work attracted serious attention because it posed a concrete physical mechanism rather than merely announcing that inertia was mysterious.

It also attracted serious criticism. Subsequent papers challenged the derivation, relativistic treatment and magnitude of the proposed effect. This is not a side note to be handled after the exciting part. It is the exciting part. A frontier theory becomes science by surviving hostile mathematics.

Even if some component of inertial mass were emergent from vacuum-field interactions, a second problem remains: controllability. A mechanism can explain a constant without allowing a knob. Nuclear binding energy contributes to mass, but this does not make ordinary objects massless on command. A field-theoretic origin for inertia would not automatically imply inertial shielding.

So the evidence ladder here is unusually clean. Class one: inertia exists, quantum vacuum effects exist, and the cited papers exist. Class two: there are intellectually serious attempts to connect them. Class three: no accepted experiment has demonstrated tunable inertial mass through zero-point-field manipulation. Class four: propulsion systems based on deliberate inertia reduction remain conjectural.

This distinction matters because the phrase 'inertia engineering' appears technologically specific while concealing the absence of the key demonstrated operation: changing inertia itself.

The machine begins only when the knob moves.
