## Evidence classification at this stage

### 1. Verified/declassified/documented

Project COLDFEET existed. NP-8 was an abandoned Soviet drifting research station. Smith and LeSchack parachuted to the station in May 1962. They photographed it and recovered roughly 150 pounds of documents, samples and equipment. A B-17 equipped with Fulton Skyhook recovered the material and men without landing. CIA institutional histories characterize the resulting intelligence as useful for understanding Soviet under-ice acoustic detection and Arctic anti-submarine-warfare work.

### 2. Credible but incomplete

Participant-level details preserved in retrospective histories, including fine-grained planning conversations, subjective assessments and exact interpretations of individual recovered items, remain attributable rather than automatically promoted to independently verified fact.

### 3. Disputed / not independently established here

Exact operational performance of any Soviet under-ice detection capability; claims that NP-8 could reliably detect or target particular U.S. submarines at militarily decisive ranges; undisclosed recovered devices; or broad claims that Soviet polar science was merely a military cover.

### 4. Speculative synthesis

`Residue grammar`, `configuration premium`, `dual use is not a verdict`, and the comparison between abandoned-site exploitation and modern anomalous-material/site claims are Sven Hardy Benson's analytical constructs. They are not historical program terminology.

## Contradiction and incentive audit

CIA has an institutional incentive to narrate COLDFEET as an ingenious operational success and Skyhook as a technological triumph. That does not make the account false, but it makes independent reconstruction important. Later drafting should seek Office of Naval Research, DIA, Air Force, Navy, Soviet/Russian and Arctic-science records capable of checking the Agency narrative from outside it.

The strongest alternative reading is less glamorous: the United States exploited an abandoned research station and recovered interesting documents/equipment, but the public record may not yet justify a strong claim about how much novel operational capability the material revealed. The manuscript should preserve that possibility.

## Research queue

1. Locate contemporaneous ONR and DIA COLDFEET planning/after-action records and distinguish what was expected from what was actually learned.
2. Reconstruct NP-8's published Soviet scientific program from Soviet/Russian polar-science sources rather than CIA description alone.
3. Build a technical primer on Arctic underwater acoustics: ice scattering, ambient noise, ducts, seasonal effects and why under-ice ASW is a special sensing problem.
4. Identify exactly which recovered categories of equipment are named in public records and refuse to fill gaps with inference.
5. Compare abandoned-site exploitation with documented foreign-materiel exploitation without implying that every recovered object produces the same evidentiary wake.
6. Stress-test `residue grammar` against archaeological and intelligence cases where configuration produced a convincing but wrong functional inference.

## Governing prohibition

COLDFEET proves that intelligence services exploit abandoned scientific infrastructure and that scientific work can have direct military relevance. It does not prove that any unrelated anomalous site is a secret laboratory, that recovered UAP materials exist, or that classification validates extraordinary technology. Analogy may generate a test. It may not supply the result.
