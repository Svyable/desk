# Evidence Rules

This book is about a secret intelligence operation, but secrecy is not evidence of the extraordinary. It is a condition under which evidence becomes harder to inspect.

Every controversial claim is sorted before it is narrated.

**Class 1 — verified/declassified/documented.** Public records, released intelligence histories, official artifacts, contemporaneous documents, and technical literature whose provenance can be checked.

**Class 2 — credible but incomplete.** Participant recollection, serious reporting, or institutional retrospective material that is plausible and attributable but does not independently establish every detail.

**Class 3 — disputed allegation.** Claims without adequate independent confirmation, including claims that borrow credibility from a documented secret program without a case-specific bridge.

**Class 4 — speculative synthesis.** My interpretation: useful only if the reader can see where the documents end and the inference begins.

The book will repeatedly distinguish **site**, **purpose**, **capability**, and **interpretation**. Finding scientific instruments at a military-relevant site does not prove a weapon. Finding military relevance does not make the science fake. Finding a classified program does not prove its technical premise worked.

## Governing test

A recovered site should be treated as a system of relationships. What was powered? What was wired to what? What was calibrated? What was logged? Which components were consumed or replaced? What environmental constraints shaped the layout? Which documents describe routine work rather than aspirations? What would have to be present if the proposed function were true?

Call this **residue grammar**. A single exotic-looking component can mislead. A network of mundane components in the right relationship can reveal a mission.

## Publication holds

Until primary records justify them, the manuscript will not assert exact Soviet technical capabilities beyond what released sources support; that NP-8 possessed an operational ability to detect specific U.S. submarines at operationally decisive ranges; that every Soviet Arctic scientific program was military cover; that COLDFEET recovered a particular classified device not identified in public records; or that modern anomalous sites can be diagnosed by analogy to NP-8.

Patents, if later used, will be evidence of filing and technical proposal—not efficacy. Testimony will remain testimony. Institutional histories will be checked against contemporaneous records where available.

## Defeat condition

The book's central synthesis weakens if the recovered NP-8 material cannot be shown to have supported a meaningful functional inference beyond what U.S. analysts already knew, or if the official account substantially overstates the intelligence value of the recovered material. The story remains historically interesting in that case, but the stronger claim—that abandoned technical residue can disclose mission architecture—would need to contract.
