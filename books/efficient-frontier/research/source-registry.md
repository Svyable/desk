# Source Registry

This registry records the foundational sources used to keep established theory separate from the manuscript's synthesis. It is not a claim that every example in every chapter has already received publication-candidate fact checking; current examples should be reverified during that pass.

| ID | Source | Supports | Limits / cautions |
|---|---|---|---|
| S01 | Harry Markowitz, “Portfolio Selection,” *The Journal of Finance* 7(1), 1952, pp. 77–91. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1952.tb01525.x | Mean-variance portfolio selection; evaluating portfolios as combinations; efficient versus inferior portfolio choices. | Finance-specific model with expected-return/variance assumptions. Does not support the manuscript's broader coined vocabulary by itself. |
| S02 | Stephen Boyd and Lieven Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, Cambridge University Press, 2004. ISBN 978-0-521-83378-3. Author-hosted material: https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/ | Pareto optimality, dominance, optimal trade-off curves/surfaces, scalarization in convex multicriterion problems. | Convex mathematical setting; not a general moral or political decision theory. |
| S03 | Kaisa Miettinen, *Nonlinear Multiobjective Optimization*, Springer, 1998. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4615-5563-6 | Conflicting/incommensurable objectives, Pareto solutions, preference methods, aspiration levels, graphical treatment. | Technical multiobjective optimization; applications require problem-specific objectives and preference information. |
| S04 | Herbert A. Simon, “A Behavioral Model of Rational Choice,” *The Quarterly Journal of Economics* 69(1), 1955, pp. 99–118. DOI: https://doi.org/10.2307/1884852 | Bounded rationality and the limits of exhaustive optimization; foundation for satisficing/aspiration-level reasoning. | Does not contain or endorse the manuscript term “enough line.” |
| S05 | Frank H. Knight, *Risk, Uncertainty and Profit*, Houghton Mifflin, 1921. Library of Congress record: https://www.loc.gov/item/21012860/ | Historical distinction between calculable risk and deeper uncertainty. | Terminology has a large later literature; use as intellectual lineage, not a complete modern taxonomy of uncertainty. |
| S06 | Avinash K. Dixit and Robert S. Pindyck, *Investment under Uncertainty*, Princeton University Press, 1994. DOI/stable record: https://doi.org/10.2307/j.ctt7sncv | Irreversibility, investment timing, and option value of waiting for better information. | Formal real-options setting; manuscript extensions to careers, architecture, and policy are analogical unless separately modeled. |
| S07 | Donald Goldfarb and Garud Iyengar, “Robust Portfolio Selection Problems,” *Mathematics of Operations Research* 28(1), 2003, pp. 1–38. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1287/moor.28.1.1.14260 | Robust portfolio formulations designed to combat sensitivity to statistical and modeling errors; uncertainty structures. | Portfolio-specific robust formulations; does not imply every robust decision should optimize worst-case outcomes. |
| S08 | Aharon Ben-Tal, Laurent El Ghaoui, and Arkadi Nemirovski, *Robust Optimization*, Princeton University Press, 2009. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1515/9781400831050 | General robust-optimization treatment under uncertain problem parameters and uncertainty sets. | Robustness is conditional on the modeled uncertainty set and performance criterion. |
| S09 | T. P. Wright, “Factors Affecting the Cost of Airplanes,” *Journal of the Aeronautical Sciences* 3(4), 1936, pp. 122–128. DOI: https://doi.org/10.2514/8.155 | Historical empirical foundation for production learning/experience relationships. | One industry and historical setting; do not universalize a specific curve shape to all learning. |
| S10 | C. A. E. Goodhart, “Problems of Monetary Management: The U.K. Experience,” later collected in *Monetary Theory and Practice: The U.K. Experience*. Springer record: https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-349-17295-5 | Historical source associated with the target/measure problem later popularized as Goodhart's law. | Avoid presenting later slogan wording as a verbatim quotation from the original unless directly verified. |
| S11 | Ronald H. Coase, “The Problem of Social Cost,” *The Journal of Law and Economics* 3, 1960, pp. 1–44. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1086/466560 | Social cost, reciprocal harms, transaction costs, bargaining/institutional comparison, and the importance of legal allocation. | Do not reduce to “private bargaining always solves externalities”; transaction costs and institutional alternatives matter. |
| S12 | Kaisa Miettinen, “Some Methods for Nonlinear Multi-objective Optimization,” in *Evolutionary Multi-Criterion Optimization*, LNCS 1993, Springer, 2001. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/3-540-44719-9_1 | Preference articulation, Pareto-optimal solutions, aspiration levels, and methods for nonlinear multiobjective problems. | Method survey; not evidence for the manuscript's organizational or personal examples. |

## Research gaps for publication-candidate review

The current manuscript is intentionally mechanism-heavy and contains relatively few claims that depend on current company statistics. Before release, any concrete examples added during editing should get their own claim notes. Special attention should go to contemporary AI/product examples, infrastructure-policy examples, regulatory claims, and any numerical statement that could be mistaken for observed data rather than illustration.
