# Chronology

This chronology distinguishes events from later interpretation. Dates should be tightened against contemporaneous records during the next source pass.

- **1944** — Robert Thomas Bigelow is born in Nevada.
- **Childhood / adolescence** — Bigelow later says a story told by his grandparents about a close UFO encounter near Las Vegas helped shape his interest in the subject. This is retrospective family testimony, not a reconstructed incident record.
- **1960s–1980s** — Bigelow builds a real-estate career in Las Vegas, moving from apartment acquisition and construction into larger repeatable lodging concepts.
- **1988** — Budget Suites of America begins operations. The extended-stay business becomes the core fortune financing Bigelow's later private research and aerospace work.
- **1995** — Bigelow establishes/funds the National Institute for Discovery Science (NIDS), a privately financed organization devoted to unusual scientific and anomalous questions.
- **1996** — Bigelow purchases the Utah property later widely known as Skinwalker Ranch after reports of unusual events from the prior owners. NIDS begins extended field investigation.
- **April 9, 1997** — UNLV announces a $3.7 million gift from Robert and Diane Bigelow establishing the Bigelow Chair of Consciousness Studies.
- **July 3, 1997** — UNLV announces psychologist Charles T. Tart as the first Bigelow Chair of Consciousness Studies.
- **1999** — Bigelow Aerospace is founded.
- **2000** — NASA's TransHab development is cancelled by Congress; expandable-habitat intellectual property and expertise later continue through Bigelow Aerospace licensing and NASA-industry agreements.
- **2002** — NASA records describe Bigelow Aerospace beginning a series of Space Act Agreements with Johnson Space Center involving technical exchanges and licensing relationships.
- **2004** — NIDS is placed on hold/inactive. In a 2006 interview, Colm Kelleher attributes the change partly to a decline in strong physical-trace cases and reduced intensity at the ranch, along with staff departures.
- **2006-07-12** — Genesis I launches and deploys as Bigelow Aerospace's first orbital expandable-habitat technology demonstrator.
- **2007-06-28** — Genesis II launches as a second technology demonstrator with additional systems and sensors.
- **2008** — DIA solicits work under the Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications Program (AAWSAP). Bigelow Aerospace Advanced Space Studies (BAASS) becomes the contractor under HHM402-08-C-0072.
- **2009-06-30** — DIA status material records 26 detailed BAASS research reports delivered by this point, twice the minimum requirement, with reviews described as overwhelmingly positive.
- **2009-06-24** — Senator Harry Reid writes Deputy Secretary of Defense William Lynn III seeking restricted special-access protection for the advanced-aerospace effort.
- **2009-10-30** — DIA produces an information memorandum reviewing the first-year advanced aerospace contract deliverables.
- **2009-11-13** — DIA review declines to justify the requested restricted SAP for the overall program at that level.
- **2012** — AARO's 2024 historical report dates the end of AAWSAP to 2012. Public accounts dispute how later AATIP-labelled activity should be institutionally characterized.
- **2013-01-16** — NASA announces a $17.8 million contract with Bigelow Aerospace for the Bigelow Expandable Activity Module (BEAM) technology demonstration on the International Space Station.
- **2016-04-08** — BEAM launches aboard a SpaceX Dragon cargo mission.
- **2016-04-16** — BEAM is installed on the ISS Tranquility module.
- **2016-05-26 / 2016-05-28** — First expansion attempt is halted after less movement than expected; NASA and Bigelow teams review data, then successfully expand BEAM on May 28.
- **2016-06-06** — ISS crew first enters BEAM.
- **2016** — Bigelow sells the Utah ranch to an entity associated with Brandon Fugal; the property's public/media era changes substantially under later ownership.
- **2017-05-28** — CBS broadcasts Lara Logan's interview with Bigelow. He says he is 'absolutely convinced' and asserts an existing extraterrestrial presence. This establishes his public conviction, not the underlying proposition.
- **2017-12** — Major national reporting brings the Pentagon's UAP-related program history and Bigelow's contractor role into broad public view.
- **2020-03** — Bigelow Aerospace lays off its workforce amid the COVID-19 period and an already difficult commercial-station market.
- **2020** — Bigelow Institute for Consciousness Studies (BICS) is founded to support research into survival of consciousness after physical death and the nature of the afterlife.
- **2021** — BICS runs its global essay contest on evidence for survival after death: 1,300 applications, 204 final essays from 38 countries, 29 winners, and $1.8 million in prizes according to BICS.
- **2021-12** — BICS awards top essay prizes to Jeffrey Mishlove ($500,000), Pim van Lommel ($350,000), and Leo Ruickbie ($150,000).
- **2022** — NASA/ISS arrangements evolve after Bigelow Aerospace's shutdown; BEAM remains in use on station and NASA continues treating the module as a successful technology demonstration/storage volume.
- **2023** — BICS funds a nine-month challenge involving 67 mediums, six teams, three countries, and $390,000; BICS states that the challenge assumes the afterlife is real and mediums can obtain valid information from it.
- **2024-03-08** — Department of Defense releases AARO's Historical Record Report, Volume I, including a retrospective account of AAWSAP/AATIP, BAASS, Skinwalker-related work, the Reid SAP request, KONA BLUE, and alleged reverse-engineering claims.
- **2026** — AARO continues publishing UAP annual reports and historical/FOIA material. This manuscript treats later AARO claims as official institutional findings and continues to separate those findings from independent proof or disproof of every underlying allegation.
