# The Forbidden Sense

*Why governments kept testing psi after science thought the question should have died*

Sven Hardy Benson

There are two bad ways to write about psychic research.

The first is to decide that the classified stamp proves the miracle. The second is to decide that the subject sounds ridiculous and therefore the documents, experiments and people are beneath inspection.

This book uses neither shortcut.

The United States really ran classified and compartmented programs that tested remote viewing. Intelligence analysts really tracked Soviet and East European parapsychology and psychotronics. U.S. defense organizations really translated Chinese work on qigong and “extraordinary human function.” Laboratory literatures on ganzfeld telepathy, precognition, presentiment and random-event effects really exist. Skeptics have also identified serious problems involving controls, replication, publication bias, scoring, sensory leakage and operational usefulness. No accepted physical mechanism for psi exists, and no public record establishes a mature psychic intelligence capability.

The interesting territory is the distance between those statements.

I use four evidence classes throughout the research trail:

**Documented:** primary government records, program histories, published experiments, identifiable reviews and institutional actions.

**Credible but incomplete:** testimony or statistical findings with meaningful evidence but unresolved replication, access or methodological questions.

**Disputed:** claims whose proponents and critics materially disagree about evidentiary sufficiency.

**Speculative:** synthesis about what might explain the residue if a residue survives better testing.

These categories are intentionally conservative. A CIA Reading Room document proves the document existed and says what the document says. It does not independently prove every claim inside it. A peer-reviewed paper proves a study was published. It does not guarantee the effect is real. A skeptical review can expose weaknesses without proving the null in every later protocol.

The manuscript’s wager is simple: if psi is false, the history is still an unusually rich case study in how intelligence organizations and scientific communities behave around low-probability, high-consequence claims. If even a small anomalous information effect is real, the correct response is not instant metaphysics. It is metrology.

Build the experiment that makes the anomaly impossible to romanticize.
