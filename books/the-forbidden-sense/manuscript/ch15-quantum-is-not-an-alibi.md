# Quantum Is Not an Alibi

Quantum mechanics has become the most prestigious hiding place for ideas that do not yet have mechanisms.

This is unfair to quantum mechanics.

The theory is strange enough without being asked to explain telepathy, precognition, consciousness, healing, synchronicity and every unexplained correlation in human life.

The pattern is familiar. A psi experiment reports information without an obvious classical signal. Quantum entanglement involves correlations across separated systems. Therefore, perhaps entanglement explains psi.

The middle step is missing.

Entanglement is not a synonym for mysterious connectedness. It is a precise relationship among quantum states with precise mathematical predictions. Standard quantum mechanics does not permit people to use entanglement as an ordinary faster-than-light communications channel. Measurement outcomes are locally random. Correlations become visible when results are compared through ordinary causal channels.

A human telepathy model based on entanglement would need far more than the word “nonlocal.”

What degrees of freedom in two brains are entangled?

How is entanglement created and maintained in warm, noisy biological tissue?

How does the target information become encoded?

What measurement corresponds to the receiver’s report?

What decoherence times matter?

Why can the process carry semantically structured information when ordinary entanglement cannot be used for controllable superluminal signaling?

What quantitative effect should change when distance, shielding, temperature or timing changes?

Until these questions have answers, quantum language is analogy.

Analogies can inspire experiments. They cannot substitute for them.

The misuse of quantum mechanics in anomalous research creates a perverse skeptical reaction. Scientists hear loose entanglement claims and dismiss the entire subject. A bad theory can make an observation harder to investigate.

That is another reason to separate effect from mechanism.

If a preregistered remote-viewing study produces a reproducible above-chance effect, the result does not become less interesting because somebody proposed a terrible quantum explanation. The anomaly stands or falls on the protocol.

The correct theoretical response would be restraint.

Physics contains serious foundational questions about time, measurement, causality and the relationship between quantum descriptions and classical reality. There are time-symmetric formulations and retrocausal interpretations. There are delayed-choice experiments whose narrative order can be counterintuitive. There are indefinite causal-order frameworks in quantum information. There are quantum biological effects in systems such as photosynthetic complexes and radical-pair chemistry.

These are real research areas.

None currently demonstrates human precognition or psychokinesis.

The distinction is not pedantic. It is what protects frontier physics from becoming a permission slip.

A future theory could connect some of these domains. Science has surprised us before. But a connection must produce new predictions, not merely make an anomaly sound less embarrassing.

Consider retrocausality.

Some interpretations of quantum mechanics allow mathematical descriptions in which future boundary conditions play a role or influences are represented in time-symmetric ways. This does not automatically mean a person can know tomorrow’s lottery numbers. An interpretation can be empirically equivalent to standard quantum mechanics and provide no new signaling capacity.

To explain precognition, a model must connect macroscopic biological states to future randomly selected events in a way that creates measurable information before selection.

That is an enormous additional claim.

The same caution applies to the block universe.

Relativity makes simultaneity frame-dependent. In a four-dimensional spacetime picture, events at different times can be treated as parts of one geometric structure. Popular writing sometimes turns this into “the future already exists, so maybe we can access it.”

Geometry is not access.

A sealed archive across town already exists. You still need a channel to read it.

Psi theories often use ontology to smuggle in information flow.

Physics demands dynamics.

There is another seductive word: information.

Quantum information theory has transformed physics. Entropy, information bounds, error correction and quantum channels are deep technical concepts. But “information” in these contexts is not a free-floating substance that consciousness can retrieve from the universe.

Information is instantiated in physical correlations.

If a remote viewer obtains target information, we need to know what physical variables correlate with target and brain and how that correlation is established.

Saying “consciousness accesses nonlocal information” restates the observation.

It does not explain it.

This is particularly important because the information metaphor can become unfalsifiable. If no energy transfer is detected, proponents say the effect transfers information, not energy. If distance does not matter, they say information is nonlocal. If shielding does not matter, they say the channel is outside ordinary fields.

Each failed physical prediction can make the proposed process less physical.

Eventually nothing remains to measure except the statistical correlation itself.

A correlation can still be real. But the theory has stopped helping.

The correct move is to keep the phenomenon at the statistical level until a mechanism earns promotion.

There is precedent for this patience.

Mendel’s inheritance ratios were useful before DNA. Thermodynamics worked before statistical mechanics. Epidemiology identified risk factors before molecular pathways. A stable empirical law can precede mechanism.

But those empirical laws were stable.

Psi’s problem is not merely that mechanism is missing. The effect itself remains contested.

This should reorder research priorities.

Spend less time explaining psi with quantum mechanics.

Spend more time making the effect impossible to ignore.

A ten-laboratory preregistered replication is more valuable than ten speculative quantum-consciousness papers.

If the replication works, physicists will come.

That is how extraordinary interdisciplinary claims should enter mature science: through data strong enough to create a problem for the neighboring field.

Physics should not be recruited in advance to make the claim respectable.

There is one quantum-adjacent domain worth discussing because it provides a model of how strange biological physics can become respectable: magnetoreception.

The radical-pair hypothesis proposes that light-induced chemical reactions involving electron spins can be sensitive to weak magnetic fields, potentially contributing to avian compass behavior. The idea involves genuine quantum spin dynamics in a warm biological system.

This once might have sounded absurd.

What made it scientific was not the weirdness. It was the chain from molecules to fields to reactions to behavior, with experiments capable of perturbing pieces of the chain.

That is the standard for any future quantum psi model.

Identify the molecule.

Identify the state.

Identify the interaction.

Perturb it.

Make the behavioral effect change.

Until then, “quantum” belongs in the research-notes margin, not the conclusion.

Classical physics is also less exhausted than people assume.

Biological systems produce electric, magnetic, acoustic, thermal and chemical signals. The brain’s magnetic fields are tiny but measurable with sensitive instruments. The heart’s electromagnetic activity extends outside the body. Bodies emit infrared radiation. Mechanical vibrations travel through structures. Radiofrequency environments are dense.

Any claimed short-range anomalous interaction should first exclude these ordinary channels.

This is especially true for experiments where sender and receiver occupy nearby rooms. Shielding, distance and timing can test known interactions.

Long-distance remote-viewing claims are different. Ordinary biological emissions attenuate. There is no established channel carrying arbitrary semantic target information across continents from a photograph to a brain.

If such results became robust, the pressure on physics would be real.

But that pressure has to come from measurement.

There is a sociological reason quantum explanations proliferate before measurement: they reduce stigma.

“Telepathy” sounds occult.

“Nonlocal quantum information” sounds technical.

The underlying evidentiary state may not have changed at all.

Scientific language can function as camouflage.

The Soviet psychotronics literature did something similar with “bioinformation” and “paraphysics.” Chinese qigong research sometimes translated traditional concepts into electromagnetic or energetic language. Each culture uses its prestigious explanatory vocabulary to domesticate anomalies.

Our culture’s prestige vocabulary is quantum.

We should be suspicious precisely because the physics is real.

Real theories are easy to borrow.

Hard to obey.

A proper physical theory of psi would likely be restrictive. It would say when the phenomenon cannot occur. It would predict a scaling law. It would identify conservation relationships or explain why ordinary conservation statements are not violated. It would connect to existing experiments outside parapsychology.

Most importantly, it would risk death.

A model that survives only by becoming more abstract whenever a test fails is not frontier physics. It is metaphysics wearing a lab coat.

There is nothing wrong with metaphysics.

The mistake is confusing categories.

This book does have a metaphysical implication, but it comes later and conditionally.

If a reproducible anomalous information effect exists, then current accounts of how biological systems acquire information are incomplete. That statement is modest and enormous at the same time.

It does not tell us whether consciousness is fundamental.

It does not tell us whether the universe is simulated.

It does not tell us whether death is survivable.

It does not tell us whether quantum mechanics needs modification.

It tells us where the measurement boundary failed.

Science can proceed from there.

The human temptation is to leap from one crack in a wall to a map of the entire territory behind it.

Anomalies deserve better than that.

A real anomaly should be allowed to stay small until it grows.
