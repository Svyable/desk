# Research trail — The Forbidden Sense

This file records the evidentiary spine used in the first full argumentative draft. It is not a claim that every source below supports psi. Several of the most important sources are skeptical or operationally negative.

## Evidence classes

### 1. Verified / declassified / documented

- CIA public history, “Ask Molly: Did CIA Really Study Psychic Powers?” Confirms CIA research beginning in 1972, transfer of the work to Defense sponsorship, the 1995 independent evaluation, declassification, and CIA’s public characterization that reported accuracy was too unreliable for intelligence use. https://www.cia.gov/stories/story/ask-molly-did-cia-really-study-psychic-powers/
- CIA STARGATE/GRILL FLAME remote-viewing session reports, including operational tasking tied to the Iran hostage crisis and training sessions with explicitly poor correlation. Examples:
  - https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp96-00788r000800060001-8
  - https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp96-00788r000700070001-8
  - https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp96-00788r001400250001-0
  - https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp96-00788r000900390001-1
- Declassified government reference compilation naming U.S. intelligence studies of Soviet/East European parapsychology, paraphysics and controlled offensive behavior: https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/CIA-RDP96-00788R001300040001-4.pdf
- DIA translation of Chinese qigong research literature: https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp96-00792r000200650023-0
- Declassified material discussing Chinese somatic-science / extraordinary-function protocols and controls: https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp96-00792r000300410014-5

These sources establish government activity, document contents and historical attention. They do not independently prove anomalous mechanisms.

### 2. Credible but incomplete / active evidence

- Bem, Tressoldi, Rabeyron & Duggan, meta-analysis of 90 anomalous-anticipation experiments, F1000Research (2016). Reports a small positive aggregate effect and discusses independent replications. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4706048/
- Mossbridge, Tressoldi & Utts, “Predictive Physiological Anticipation Preceding Seemingly Unpredictable Stimuli: A Meta-Analysis,” Frontiers in Psychology (2012). Reports small pre-stimulus physiological differences across 26 reports. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23109927/
- Tressoldi et al., “Remote Viewing: A 1974–2022 Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis,” Journal of Scientific Exploration (2023). Reports a positive aggregate effect across selected remote-viewing studies. https://doi.org/10.31275/20232931

These are evidence that positive statistical literatures exist. Their interpretation remains disputed and depends on study quality, independence, publication processes, analytic choices and priors.

### 3. Skeptical / negative / boundary evidence

- National Research Council review summarized by Swets & Bjork, “Enhancing Human Performance: An Evaluation of ‘New Age’ Techniques Considered by the U.S. Army,” Psychological Science (1990). Reports little or no scientific evidence supporting paranormal techniques such as remote viewing and psychokinesis. https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1111/j.1467-9280.1990.tb00074.x
- Rouder & Morey, “A Bayes factor meta-analysis of Bem’s ESP claim,” Psychonomic Bulletin & Review (2011). Finds only limited-to-noteworthy evidence in subsets and argues it remains far below what is needed to overcome an appropriately skeptical prior. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21573926/
- The 1995 AIR operational evaluation, as summarized in CIA’s own later public account, judged the remote-viewing output too inconsistent and sporadic for intelligence use. https://www.cia.gov/stories/story/ask-molly-did-cia-really-study-psychic-powers/

### 4. Historical / sociological context

- Li Jianhui, “The Craziness for Extra-Sensory Perception: Qigong Fever and the Science–Pseudoscience Debate in China,” Zygon (2015). Historical analysis of China’s 1979–1999 qigong/ESP controversy. https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/zygo.12178

## What would change the book’s conclusion

The manuscript currently argues for serious uncertainty, not confirmation. The conclusion should move strongly toward artifact/null if large adversarial preregistered replications repeatedly exclude the effect sizes claimed by the strongest positive meta-analyses.

The conclusion should move strongly toward a genuine anomalous-information effect if a prospectively fixed protocol produces a reproducible nonzero result across multiple independent skeptical and proponent laboratories, especially if individual or physiological predictors generalize to held-out data.

No quantum, consciousness, spiritual or nonhuman mechanism should be inferred from a statistical effect alone.

## Claim hygiene for future passes

- Preserve the distinction between government interest and government knowledge.
- Treat patents as evidence of filing only, not efficacy, if added later.
- Treat witness testimony as testimony unless independently corroborated.
- Do not use classification markings as confidence ratings.
- When discussing Soviet, Russian, Chinese, Israeli or other foreign programs, prefer primary documents or scholarly histories over recursive web claims.
- Add null studies and failed replications alongside positive meta-analyses rather than building a one-sided source ledger.
- Avoid “quantum” explanations unless a source provides a quantitative biological mechanism and falsifiable prediction.
