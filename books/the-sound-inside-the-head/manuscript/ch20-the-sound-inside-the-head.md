# The Sound Inside the Head

The story began with a click.

That is worth remembering because everything that came later became larger than the original physical fact.

A pulsed radio-frequency field can deposit a small amount of energy in the head quickly enough to create thermoelastic expansion. Expansion creates a pressure wave. The cochlea hears it. The sensation can seem to come from inside the head because, in a literal mechanical sense, the sound is generated there.

No conspiracy is required.

No new physics is required.

No direct electromagnetic conversation with consciousness is required.

The verified phenomenon is strange enough.

Then institutions did what institutions do around a strange fact with national-security implications. Scientists argued about mechanism and dose. Defense organizations explored applications. Adversary research raised fears of technological surprise. Patents accumulated. Some weapon ideas remained speculative; some directed-energy systems became real in other regimes. Decades later, American personnel in Havana reported acute directional sensations followed by symptoms that in some cases became disabling.

The old effect became available as an explanation.

Availability is not causality.

The National Academies considered directed pulsed radio-frequency energy the most plausible mechanism among those it evaluated for distinctive early cases. The 2022 intelligence-community expert panel also judged pulsed electromagnetic energy plausible for a selected subset and said relevant sources could be concealable. The 2023 intelligence assessment judged a foreign campaign very unlikely. The 2024 NIH studies found no consistent persistent MRI or broad biomarker signature across the cohort they studied. The January 2025 assessment preserved the majority judgment while two intelligence components shifted, with low confidence, toward roughly even odds that a foreign actor had developed or deployed a relevant novel capability in a small subset. In 2026, congressional committees publicly pursued reports of acquired pulsed-energy hardware, investigative reporting described a secret Norwegian self-experiment, and the Director of National Intelligence reportedly recalled the earlier AHI intelligence assessments amid allegations of analytic failure.

That sequence does not form a straight line toward belief.

It does not form a straight line toward dismissal either.

It forms a case study in what happens when evidence streams answer different questions at different confidence levels.

The physics asks whether the body can convert pulses into sensory events.

The engineering asks whether a practical device can create more than a sensory event under covert field conditions.

The medicine asks what happened to the people who became ill.

The intelligence asks whether a foreign actor built, moved, and used a relevant system.

The bureaucracy asks who qualifies for care and compensation.

The politics asks whether institutions told the truth.

Those questions touch.

They are not interchangeable.

The four-class evidence ledger used throughout the research trail exists because this subject punishes imprecision.

Class 1 contains verified or documented facts: microwave hearing; the thermoelastic mechanism; published clinical studies; the 2020 National Academies report; the 2022 expert-panel findings; the text of the 2023 and 2025 intelligence assessments; NIH’s 2024 findings; GAO’s documentation of care and registry problems; official congressional actions in 2026.

Class 2 contains credible but incomplete material: serious investigative reporting about foreign technical programs, the acquired device, the Norwegian experiment, and operational links that rely on anonymous or classified sources.

Class 3 contains disputed allegations: claims that a particular Russian unit conducted particular attacks, claims that a specific technology caused individual events without event-level measurements, and claims that recalled intelligence assessments were deliberately manufactured unless and until the underlying record is released and independently evaluated.

Class 4 contains synthesis: the possibility that only a small subset involved external energy; the possibility that some exposure was related to surveillance rather than intended injury; the possibility that classification itself prevented the science from converging by keeping the most important exposure parameters away from independent investigators.

The categories are not permanent.

Evidence can move a claim.

That is the point.

The strongest lesson from Havana may eventually have nothing to do with which country did what. It may be about how institutions handle low-probability, high-consequence claims when the relevant science is real but the event evidence is incomplete.

The skeptic faces a genuine risk.

Technological surprise exists. States have missed adversary programs before. Intelligence collection is not omniscient. Dismissing an unconventional mechanism because it sounds like science fiction can be catastrophic when the underlying physics is sound and the engineering has moved faster than the analyst’s prior.

The believer faces a different risk.

Once a real effect is found, every gap can be filled with a secret capability. Classification can explain missing evidence. Intelligence failure can explain negative assessments. A small subset can preserve the hypothesis when the broad category collapses. A hidden program can explain the absence of public hardware. Used carelessly, secrecy becomes a universal solvent for falsification.

Both risks are real.

The answer is not centrism.

It is experimental pressure.

Make each hypothesis predict something the others do not.

Instrument the rooms.

Capture the pulses if they exist.

Measure the spatial field.

Collect acute biological samples before the signal decays.

Characterize any recovered hardware.

Expose phantoms and animal models under controlled conditions.

Use blinded, ethical human threshold studies where appropriate.

Let skeptical and sympathetic teams design the protocol together.

Publish enough of the methods that outsiders can attack the inference.

Keep mechanism separate from attribution until independent evidence joins them.

And care for people before the geopolitical argument is settled.

There is a temptation in books about anomalous phenomena to end by making the anomaly larger. The final page becomes a door to deeper mystery.

This case deserves the opposite treatment.

The extraordinary part is that part of the mystery is already ordinary physics.

The skull can make sound from a radio-frequency pulse.

That fact is not proof of a weapon.

It is not proof against one.

It is a piece of reality sturdy enough to stand on while the rest is tested.

The sound inside the head should teach us one discipline above all:

Do not confuse the existence of a mechanism with the history of an event.

The distance between those two things is where evidence lives.
