# Back Matter

The public record on anomalous health incidents changes unusually quickly because evidence is split between medicine, intelligence, journalism, congressional oversight, and classified technical work. Dates matter.

The manuscript’s evidence posture is deliberately asymmetric. A peer-reviewed laboratory result can establish a physical or biological effect without establishing a historical event. An intelligence assessment can change the probability of attribution without changing laboratory physics. Testimony can identify a line of inquiry without proving the underlying claim. A patent establishes a filing and disclosed design, not efficacy. A research contract establishes government interest, not operational success. A congressional allegation is evidence that an allegation was made through an official channel, not proof that the allegation is correct.

The research directory contains the working source log, chronology, four-class evidence ledger, contradiction and incentive audit, and defeat conditions. Those files are part of the book’s epistemic architecture rather than ornamental bibliography.

The strongest current public facts are these.

Pulsed radio-frequency energy can produce auditory perception through thermoelastic pressure generation in the head. The phenomenon has been reproduced and modeled for decades.

The 2020 National Academies committee judged directed pulsed RF the most plausible mechanism among those it considered for distinctive acute cases, while emphasizing heterogeneity and uncertainty.

The 2022 Intelligence Community Experts Panel judged pulsed electromagnetic energy plausible for a subset of AHIs and recommended better detectors, biomarkers, and experiments. It was not tasked with attribution.

The 2023 Intelligence Community Assessment judged foreign responsibility very unlikely for the reported AHI population. The January 2025 update retained that majority view while two components shifted, with low confidence, toward roughly even odds that a foreign actor had developed or deployed a relevant novel capability in a small subset.

NIH studies published in March 2024 found severe symptoms but no consistent MRI-detectable brain injury or broad biological abnormality distinguishing the studied AHI group from controls.

In 2026, congressional committees publicly investigated reports that the U.S. government had acquired and was testing a foreign-made pulsed-RF device. Serious investigative reporting described a separate Norwegian pulsed-energy self-experiment. The public technical record remains incomplete. In June 2026, House Intelligence Committee leadership announced that the Director of National Intelligence had recalled prior AHI intelligence assessments amid allegations of analytic failures. A full replacement public assessment has not yet resolved those disputes.

The manuscript therefore ends where a good investigation should begin: with specific measurements that can make belief move in either direction.
