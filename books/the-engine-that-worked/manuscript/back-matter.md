# Back Matter

## Evidence note

This manuscript deliberately distinguishes the demonstrated nuclear-ramjet reactor program from the proposed SLAM weapon system. Tory ground-test success is not described as a full vehicle flight. Proposed payload, altitude, speed and mission concepts are treated as design goals unless a cited source supports a stronger claim. Modern Russian performance figures are attributed to Russian officials unless independently corroborated.

## Working bibliography

The research source log contains live links and claim boundaries. Core primary records include Theodore Merkle's 1959 *The Nuclear Ramjet Propulsion System*; James Hadley's *Tory II-A: A Nuclear Ramjet Test Reactor* and Tory II-A final test report; Walter and Smiley's *Tory II-C Data Book*; and C. E. Walter's *Engineering Design of the Tory II-C Nuclear Ramjet Reactor*. Institutional histories from Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory and the Nevada National Security Site are used for program chronology and facility context. Contemporary 1964 Air Force coverage is used as a time-local record of the continuation debate.

Modern Burevestnik material is bounded to public official statements, U.S. government assessments, arms-control reporting and open-source analysis. No claim is made to possess classified information.
