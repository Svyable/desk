# 14. The Ghost Returns

In March 2018 Vladimir Putin publicly introduced a nuclear-powered cruise missile among a group of new strategic systems. Russia calls it Burevestnik. NATO uses the reporting name Skyfall.

The resemblance to Pluto is irresistible and therefore dangerous.

Both concepts use nuclear energy to pursue extraordinary cruise-missile range. Both collide immediately with questions of reactor size, radiation, testing and strategic utility. But resemblance is not identity. Public information about Burevestnik's propulsion architecture remains incomplete. A historian should not paste Tory II-C into a Russian diagram and declare the engineering solved the same way.

The evidence needs sorting.

**Documented:** Russia publicly acknowledges the program. U.S. government documents have discussed Burevestnik as a nuclear-powered, nuclear-armed cruise missile. The U.S. State Department associated the August 8, 2019 Nenoksa accident with a recovery operation involving the program, an assessment Russia did not fully detail publicly.

**Credible but incomplete:** open-source analysts have tracked candidate test activity and infrastructure. Arms-control researchers have reconstructed a history of attempts and failures from imagery, notices and official statements.

**Official claim requiring independent caution:** in October 2025, Russian officials said Burevestnik completed a flight of about 14,000 kilometers lasting about fifteen hours. Arms Control Today reported the claim and also noted Putin said forces still had to determine employment and prepare infrastructure. That last detail is almost painfully Pluto-like: even after an announced long-range test, there is still a system around the system.

One can believe the Russian test claim broadly and still remain uncertain about operational maturity, reliability, production, reactor lifetime, signature, accuracy and deployment doctrine.

That is not skepticism for sport. It is the ladder from Chapter Seven.

Physics success is not program success.

The modern echo also changes the historical reading of Pluto. Nuclear cruise propulsion was not a bizarre cul-de-sac that intelligent states could only contemplate before they knew better. The attraction is durable: route flexibility, enormous range, penetration options, strategic signaling. So are the costs.

The ghost returned because the trade space returned.
