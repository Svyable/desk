# Evidence ledger — THE SECOND FLASH

## Class 1 — verified / declassified / documented

- On 22 September 1979 Vela 6911 recorded an optical double-pulse signal that triggered investigation as a possible atmospheric nuclear explosion.
- Vela was a U.S. nuclear-detection satellite system; later Vela spacecraft carried Earth-facing bhangmeters designed to detect atmospheric nuclear-test optical signatures.
- A White House scientific panel chaired by Jack Ruina ultimately judged the signal probably was not from a nuclear explosion and considered non-nuclear explanations including a meteoroid-related event.
- Declassified records show significant disagreement inside the U.S. government; officials and technical organizations including CIA, DIA, DOE and others continued to assign substantial weight to the nuclear interpretation.
- Carter's contemporaneous diary records a growing belief among scientists advising him that Israel may have conducted a test near southern Africa. This establishes Carter's recorded belief, not attribution.
- Peer-reviewed papers by Christopher M. Wright and Lars-Erik De Geer (2017, 2018) reanalyzed optical, hydroacoustic and radionuclide evidence and argued that a low-yield nuclear test is consistent with the combined record.

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete

- The double flash was caused by a low-yield atmospheric nuclear explosion.
- Hydroacoustic signals described in later-declassified records can be tied to the same physical event and localized consistently with the optical event.
- Iodine-131 measured in Australian sheep thyroids was fallout from the 22 September event.
- The oft-repeated statement that Vela had correctly detected 41 previous atmospheric nuclear tests is an important calibration prior but needs event-level reconstruction, including false alarms and system changes.
- CIA's reported `90% plus` probability is useful evidence about an intelligence judgment; the underlying likelihood model and information set require reconstruction.

## Class 3 — disputed / unconfirmed

- Israel conducted the test.
- South Africa conducted the test.
- Israel and South Africa jointly conducted it.
- The Carter White House deliberately suppressed a known nuclear test for diplomatic reasons.
- The Ruina panel knowingly produced a false scientific conclusion.
- Any single hydroacoustic, radionuclide, ionospheric or optical datum independently proves the nuclear interpretation.

## Class 4 — speculative synthesis / open conjecture

- **Sensor veto:** one anomalous feature in a target-like record can acquire disproportionate power to veto the target interpretation when corroboration is incomplete.
- **Corroboration debt:** every claimed supporting sensor carries its own geometry, threshold, timing, calibration and false-positive burden; piling modalities together without paying those debts creates counterfeit certainty.
- **Institutional parallax:** agencies can rationally produce different posterior judgments from overlapping evidence because their missions impose different costs for false positives and false negatives.
- **Policy-shaped uncertainty:** political consequences can alter decision thresholds without necessarily altering the underlying scientific probability.

## Strongest countermodel

Vela 6911 was an aging satellite that generated an unusual optical record lacking the clean independent corroboration expected from an atmospheric nuclear test. The Ruina panel may have been substantially correct: an instrumental or environmental `zoo event` could have mimicked the signature, while later researchers assembled weak and partly post-selected signals into an apparently convergent nuclear case. Political controversy then made the unresolved event more memorable than ordinary false alarms.

The book loses if an event-level reconstruction shows that the optical signature was common among non-nuclear Vela anomalies, that the purported hydroacoustic timing/localization does not survive propagation analysis, and that the radionuclide evidence has no meaningful source discrimination.

## Publication holds

Do not publish precise yield, location, probability, isotope concentration, propagation time, detector ratio, or historical false-positive rate until the primary source, unit convention, uncertainty and denominator are extracted.

Do not write `41 previous detections, all correct` until the exact source universe is reconstructed.

Do not convert Carter's diary, CIA probability language, DIA criticism, later scholarship, or press reporting into state attribution.

Do not call the Ruina conclusion a cover-up without evidence of knowing falsification rather than scientific disagreement, policy thresholding, or motivated interpretation.
