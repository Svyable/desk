# Evidence ledger — The Year in the Wood

## Working thesis

Rare extreme solar-particle episodes leave abrupt cosmogenic isotope excursions in annual natural archives. Once independently confirmed and calendar-dated, those excursions can operate as global tie-points for archaeology and paleoclimate chronology even while the detailed astrophysical source remains partly model-dependent.

## Class 1 — verified / declassified / documented

- Miyake et al. 2012 measured a sharp annual carbon-14 increase at 774/775 CE in Japanese cedars: https://www.nature.com/articles/nature11123
- Miyake et al. 2013 measured another rapid event around 993/994 CE: https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms2783
- Independent tree-ring studies reproduced the 774 signal across geographically separated wood: https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/2014GL059874
- Multiradionuclide work found compatible carbon-14, beryllium-10 and chlorine-36 evidence for solar energetic particle origins of 774/5 and 993/4: https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms9611
- Strong events have also been documented at 7176 and 5259 BCE in multiple tree-ring chronologies: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-28804-9
- The 993 event was used to place Norse woodcutting at L'Anse aux Meadows in 1021 CE: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-03972-8
- The 775 event was used to date Por-Bajin construction to summer 777: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32513700/
- The 5259 BCE event was used to anchor the Neolithic Dispilio chronology: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-48402-1

## Class 2 — credible/model-dependent

- Proton fluence and spectral reconstruction from cosmogenic isotope production.
- Event duration shorter than annual ring resolution.
- Subannual timing inferred from earlywood/latewood chemistry and carbon-cycle transport.
- Recurrence estimates based on incomplete and partly targeted annual sampling.
- Late-glacial event magnitude comparisons after carbon-cycle and geomagnetic normalization.

## Class 3 — disputed / incomplete

- Specific medieval or ancient chronicles as direct eyewitness descriptions of canonical particle events.
- Any one candidate carbon-14 excursion without replication being promoted to a confirmed global event.
- Claims that “Miyake event” means a particular flare energy, CME configuration or geomagnetic index.
- Claims that a modern event would necessarily destroy the global power grid or modern civilization.

## Class 4 — synthesis / conjecture

- Maintaining a formal global event catalog optimized separately for chronology and solar physics.
- Coordinated annual radiocarbon surveys that combine unbiased recurrence estimation with targeted archaeological anchor searches.
- Treating Miyake markers as a distributed synchronization system linking tree rings, ice cores and archaeological wood.

## Contradictions and incentives

Discovery incentives favor positive spikes over clean annual sequences, biasing recurrence estimates unless survey selection is explicit. Archaeological incentives favor event matches that produce famous exact dates. Space-weather communication incentives favor catastrophic framing. Historical scholarship can overfit evocative sky descriptions to known isotope dates. All four incentives should be tracked in claims and peer review.

## Required nulls

- Annual sequences with no candidate event must be retained in recurrence datasets.
- Claimed new events should survive measurement in independent absolutely dated trees.
- Where possible, companion beryllium-10 or chlorine-36 evidence should be sought before confident solar-source classification.
- Exact archaeological dates should be challenged for ring-count errors, old wood, reuse, missing outer rings and circular event assignment.
