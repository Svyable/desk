# ATOMIC FRACKING — research and evidence ledger

## Working thesis

Project Plowshare's gas-stimulation experiments are a control case for a recurring frontier-technology error: **physical feasibility is only one layer of deployability**. A spectacular demonstration can succeed on its headline metric while exposing fatal constraints elsewhere in the system.

The manuscript must not flatten this into “nuclear fracking failed because radiation.” Gasbuggy, Rulison, and Rio Blanco had different device, geology, production, contamination and economic outcomes. The point is to preserve those differences.

## Evidence classes

### 1 — Verified / documented

- Project Plowshare was formally established by the AEC in 1957 to investigate peaceful applications of nuclear explosives. USGS: https://www.usgs.gov/publications/environments-us-and-ussr-nuclear-explosion-sites-petroleum-stimulation-projects
- Project Gasbuggy was a joint AEC / El Paso Natural Gas / Bureau of Mines experiment to test large-scale nuclear fracturing of a gas-bearing formation. USGS OFR 68-315: https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/1968/0315/
- Gasbuggy detonated December 10, 1967, at about 4,240 feet depth. The contemporary USGS report gives a 26 kt design yield; later public descriptions commonly give ~29 kt. Preserve the provenance rather than silently harmonizing the figures.
- Livermore's retrospective account says Gasbuggy increased gas production six to eight times over previous rates but produced undesirable tritium concentrations. LLNL: https://st.llnl.gov/news/look-back/project-gasbuggy-plowshare-program
- Rulison followed in September 1969; Livermore says gas quality problems resembled Gasbuggy.
- Rio Blanco followed in May 1973 using three nuclear explosives. Livermore says its device design was acceptably clean but only small amounts of gas were obtained and site problems made production testing impractical.
- USGS documented both U.S. and Soviet petroleum-stimulation interests by 1970. This establishes parallel investigation, not equivalence of results.
- Gasbuggy underwent later radiological cleanup/restoration. Public report record: https://digital.library.unt.edu/ark:/67531/metadc1109014/

### 2 — Credible but incomplete

- Retrospective economic estimates of total gas-stimulation investment and recoverable return. Before publication, recover the underlying DOE report and normalize nominal-year dollars.
- Participant and laboratory descriptions of why particular device designs reduced tritium and how much improvement was achieved. Require primary technical reports for quantitative claims.
- Public-acceptance history. Do not infer consumer behavior from institutional statements alone; seek contemporary hearings, polling, utility/regulatory records and proposed commercialization plans.

### 3 — Disputed / unconfirmed

- Claims that nuclear gas stimulation was secretly commercially successful.
- Claims of undisclosed operational continuation after Plowshare's public termination.
- Claims that contamination was either catastrophically greater than documented monitoring indicates or essentially irrelevant. Both require site- and pathway-specific evidence.
- Claims that Soviet peaceful nuclear explosions establish hidden U.S. applications by analogy.

### 4 — Speculative synthesis / open conjecture

**The feasibility trap:** programs can over-weight a successful physical demonstration because it converts the project from impossible to real, even when system-level viability remains poor.

**The spectacular-component bias:** observers judge the component with the greatest novelty—here the nuclear explosive—while decisive uncertainty migrates into less glamorous layers such as product quality, geology, economics and governance.

**Institutional half-life:** a technology's true cost includes monitoring, restrictions, cleanup, recordkeeping and liability after the program stops operating.

**Success-vector accounting:** replace binary “worked/failed” judgments with a vector: device performance / fracture creation / production uplift / product quality / unit economics / regulatory viability / public acceptance / long-tail burden.

These are authorial models, not historical findings. They should be tested against non-nuclear technologies and failure cases.

## Contradictions to keep alive

1. **Yield figures.** Contemporary USGS material gives Gasbuggy a 26 kt design yield; later sources often say 29 kt. Track design versus measured/reported yield before choosing a number in publication prose.
2. **“Technical feasibility.”** LLNL says the three gas-stimulation tests established technical feasibility. That phrase must not be converted into commercial success.
3. **Contamination versus exposure.** Presence of tritium in produced gas, modeled consumer dose, actual environmental release and surface/groundwater monitoring are different claims.
4. **Public acceptance versus economics.** The technology may have failed commercially even in a counterfactual world with greater public acceptance. Quantify both.
5. **Hydraulic fracturing comparison.** Modern shale development uses a package of horizontal drilling, high-volume hydraulic fracturing, proppants, seismic/geosteering and logistics. Do not write a cartoon in which one 1960s technique simply “beat the bomb.”

## Strongest countermodel

There may be no deep frontier-technology lesson here. Nuclear gas stimulation may simply have been a rational experiment conducted during a period of cheap federal nuclear infrastructure, then abandoned when ordinary engineering alternatives became superior and environmental/political costs became clearer. “Feasibility trap” could be retrospective narrative imposed on normal R&D portfolio behavior.

### What would defeat the book's stronger thesis

- Program records showing decision-makers explicitly modeled the full deployment stack early, updated rationally after each experiment, and terminated the program at roughly the economically optimal information point.
- Evidence that contamination/public acceptance played little causal role relative to straightforward unfavorable reservoir economics.
- Evidence that conventional alternatives had already clearly dominated before Gasbuggy, making the program less a frontier experiment than institutional inertia.

If those records win, narrow the book. Do not protect the metaphor.

## Quantitative publication burden

Before later chapters state precise performance or economic claims, build a table for Gasbuggy / Rulison / Rio Blanco containing:

- device count and reported/design yield;
- emplacement depth and target formation;
- pre-shot well production baseline;
- post-shot production rate and duration;
- recoverable gas estimate before/after;
- radionuclide species and concentration versus time;
- flaring/release quantities;
- modeled consumer dose assumptions;
- direct project cost and cost year;
- projected commercial well cost;
- cleanup/legacy-management cost where available;
- contemporary gas price and competing stimulation cost;
- site restrictions and monitoring duration.

No cross-project ratio without denominator and year.

## Anti-laundering rule

The existence of a documented government program does not validate adjacent extraordinary claims. Plowshare proves that governments have pursued startling, expensive and technically serious ideas in public and classified-adjacent nuclear ecosystems. It does not prove that any unrelated alleged secret technology exists.
