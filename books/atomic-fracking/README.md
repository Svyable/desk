## Reader promise

America once detonated nuclear explosives under natural-gas fields on purpose. Not in a weapons accident, not as a covert attack, and not in a thought experiment. Government laboratories and private energy companies were trying to fracture rock.

The surprise is not that the idea was insane. The surprise is that parts of it worked. Gasbuggy increased gas production. The engineering question then collided with contamination, economics, regulation, public acceptance, and a competing technology that did not require burying nuclear explosives under commercial gas fields.

ATOMIC FRACKING uses Project Plowshare's gas-stimulation experiments as a control case for frontier technology. A demonstration can establish physical feasibility while simultaneously destroying the case for deployment. The book asks what institutions mean by “success” when a machine performs its intended physical action but the resulting product cannot survive the rest of reality.

Four evidence classes remain explicit throughout: documented/declassified facts; credible but incomplete reporting or testimony; disputed allegations; and speculative synthesis/open conjecture. Later claims about hidden applications, secret continuations, or exotic nuclear engineering receive no evidentiary upgrade merely because Plowshare itself was real.
