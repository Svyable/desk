# Evidence and source log — The World Beneath Daylight

## Working thesis

Earth's deep biosphere demonstrates that life can persist for geological time in environments powered locally by geochemical disequilibria rather than contemporary sunlight. This does **not** establish extraterrestrial life. It changes the search prior in a narrower way: hostile surfaces cannot be used as whole-world sterility tests when Mars and several icy moons plausibly contain protected water-rock environments whose physics overlaps known terrestrial habitats.

The book's strongest synthesis is that astrobiology has an **access bias**. We disproportionately theorize around environments our instruments can see—surfaces and atmospheres—while some of the longest-lived plausible habitats are buried. This is a methodological claim, not a frequency estimate for alien biospheres.

## Four evidence classes

### Class 1 — verified / documented / directly measured

These claims can support manuscript declaratives without speculative phrasing, while preserving ordinary scientific uncertainty.

- Microbial life exists kilometers below Earth's surface in continental fracture waters and deep marine environments.
- The 2008 *Science* study of the Mponeng fracture-water community reconstructed a near-monoculture dominated by *Candidatus Desulforudis audaxviator* and inferred metabolic capabilities including sulfate reduction and autotrophic carbon/nitrogen acquisition.
- Water radiolysis produces hydrogen and oxidized products; terrestrial deep ecosystems can use radiolytically supported chemical energy.
- Serpentinization produces hydrogen-rich reducing conditions, and the Lost City Hydrothermal Field hosts microbial communities using methane- and sulfur-related metabolisms.
- Metabolically competent microorganisms were recovered from South Pacific Gyre sediments deposited as long as 101.5 million years ago.
- Cassini directly detected an Enceladus plume and sampled water vapor, ice grains, salts, organics and molecular hydrogen; later analysis detected phosphates in Enceladus-derived ice grains.
- Europa has strong geophysical evidence for a global subsurface salty ocean.
- Europa Clipper launched October 14, 2024, is currently an active mission, and is scheduled to reach Jupiter in April 2030; its formal objective is habitability characterization rather than direct life detection.

### Class 2 — credible but incomplete inference

- The global deep biosphere contains on the order of tens of petagrams of living carbon and occupies roughly billions of cubic kilometers. These are synthesis estimates from sparse sampling and modeling, not a direct census.
- *D. audaxviator* can operate substantially independently of contemporary photosynthetic input in its sampled fracture environment. The precise long-term material history of all substrates remains a geochemical inference.
- Radiolysis can become a principal accessible energy source in sufficiently old marine sediments after surface-derived organic inputs are depleted.
- Enceladus's molecular hydrogen and silica observations are best explained by ongoing warm water-rock/hydrothermal interaction under current models.
- Europa's Tara Regio carbon dioxide is likely derived from its interior/ocean rather than exogenic delivery, based on spatial association and current JWST analyses.
- Radiolysis in water-bearing portions of Martian crust could supply enough redox power for sulfate-reducing microbes at densities comparable with some terrestrial deep habitats. This is an energy calculation contingent on groundwater and geochemical assumptions.
- Surface-generated oxidants could increase Europa ocean habitability if geologic exchange transports them downward at sufficient rates.

### Class 3 — disputed / unresolved

- Present-day Martian atmospheric methane abundance, variability, source and sink mechanisms. Instruments have reported results that are difficult to reconcile at face value; biology is only one possible source.
- Specific interpretations of bright radar reflections under the Martian south polar cap as present liquid water; alternative mineral/ice explanations remain material.
- Exact upper temperature and lower power boundaries of life in nature. Laboratory growth near 122 °C is established, while theoretical boundaries above it are not direct demonstrations.
- Claims that any one organic molecule, isotopic fractionation, morphology or mineral texture constitutes extraterrestrial life without convergent evidence.
- Specific origin-of-life scenarios at alkaline hydrothermal vents, surface ponds, ice or other settings. None currently demonstrates the complete natural transition to autonomous evolving life.
- Europa plume claims have varied by dataset and method; the book does not rely on a confirmed persistent Europa plume.

### Class 4 — speculative synthesis / open conjecture

- Subsurface microbial biospheres could be common enough that atmospheric biosignature surveys systematically undercount inhabited worlds.
- The biologically habitable lifetime of a planet can substantially exceed its surface-habitable lifetime through deep refuge.
- Low-power subsurface ecologies may represent a common evolutionary endpoint that rarely produces complex or technological life.
- Independent life at Enceladus or Europa would demonstrate that permanent darkness is compatible not only with maintenance but potentially with life's origin.
- A well-characterized sterile ocean world could constrain the probability that life originates readily in favorable environments.

None of these class-4 claims is presented as established frequency, probability or census.

## Core primary / authoritative sources

### Mponeng / *Desulforudis audaxviator*

Chivian, D. et al. “Environmental genomics reveals a single-species ecosystem deep within Earth.” *Science* 322 (2008), 275–278. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1155495

PNNL publication record with abstract and bibliographic details: https://www.pnnl.gov/publications/environmental-genomics-reveals-single-species-ecosystem-deep-within-earth

PubMed record: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18845759/

NASA Astrobiology summary, useful for the radiolysis-independent-of-sunlight framing but secondary to the paper: https://astrobiology.nasa.gov/news/life-without-the-sun/

Boundary: the study found an extraordinary low-diversity fracture-water community. The manuscript avoids turning “>99.9% of recovered microbial sequences” into a universal claim that no other organism existed anywhere in the surrounding rock.

### Radiolysis

Sauvage, J. F. et al. “The contribution of water radiolysis to marine sedimentary life.” *Nature Communications* 12 (2021). DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-021-21218-z

USGS publication record and summary: https://www.usgs.gov/publications/contribution-water-radiolysis-marine-sedimentary-life

Key result used: common marine sediment types can catalytically increase radiolytic hydrogen yields, and modeled radiolytic power becomes especially important in old sediment where fresh organic carbon has been depleted.

Boundary: this does not imply that all deep sediment communities are radiolysis-dominated or that radiolysis alone supplies every nutrient and electron acceptor.

### Lost City / serpentinization

Brazelton, W. J., Schrenk, M. O., Kelley, D. S., Baross, J. A. “Methane- and Sulfur-Metabolizing Microbial Communities Dominate the Lost City Hydrothermal Field Ecosystem.” *Applied and Environmental Microbiology* 72 (2006). https://doi.org/10.1128/AEM.00574-06

The Lost City system is used as evidence that serpentinization-driven hydrogen-rich alkaline hydrothermal environments can support life without local sunlight.

Boundary: the existence of such an ecosystem does not establish that alkaline vents were the origin site of terrestrial life, nor that extraterrestrial serpentinite systems are inhabited.

### Very old marine sediment

Morono, Y. et al. “Aerobic microbial life persists in oxic marine sediment as old as 101.5 million years.” *Nature Communications* 11 (2020), 3626. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-17330-1

Key result used: most analyzed individual cells from the ancient sediments incorporated labeled substrates under incubation, demonstrating metabolic competence in communities from extremely old, low-energy sediment.

Boundary: sediment depositional age is not treated as demonstrated age of a single continuously surviving cell. The manuscript avoids the popular “100-million-year-old individual bacterium woke up” formulation.

### Mars subsurface radiolysis

Tarnas, J. D. et al. “Earth-like Habitable Environments in the Subsurface of Mars.” *Astrobiology* 21 (2021), 741–756. https://doi.org/10.1089/ast.2020.2386

Key result used: using Martian meteorite compositions, the authors calculated that radiolysis could provide sufficient redox resources for sulfate-reducing microbes in water-bearing crust, with potential cell densities comparable to terrestrial deep environments under modeled assumptions.

Boundary: modeled redox energy is not evidence for present groundwater, organisms or habitation.

### Enceladus hydrogen / hydrothermal activity

NASA/JPL explanation of Cassini hydrogen result: https://science.nasa.gov/photojournal/enceladus-hydrothermal-activity/

NASA/JPL 2017 ocean-world synthesis: https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/nasa-missions-provide-new-insights-into-ocean-worlds-in-our-solar-system/

Key observation used: Cassini's Ion and Neutral Mass Spectrometer found molecular hydrogen in plume gas during the October 28, 2015 flyby; the interpretation favors ongoing water-rock reactions and hydrothermal activity.

Boundary: hydrogen is an abiotic geochemical product and energy source. It is not evidence that methanogens exist.

### Enceladus phosphorus

NASA/JPL summary of 2023 phosphate result with publication trail: https://www.nasa.gov/missions/cassini/nasa-cassini-data-reveals-building-block-for-life-in-enceladus-ocean/

Key observation used: phosphate salts were detected in salt-rich ice grains derived from Enceladus's ocean, with geochemical modeling indicating substantial phosphate availability.

Boundary: phosphorus availability increases habitability. It is not a biosignature.

### Europa ocean / mission

NASA Europa Clipper mission overview: https://science.nasa.gov/mission/europa-clipper/mission-overview/

Current NASA mission page: https://science.nasa.gov/mission/europa-clipper/

NASA/JPL mission page and instrument list: https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/missions/europa-clipper/

NASA explanation of Europa's life-relevant ingredients and radiation chemistry: https://science.nasa.gov/mission/europa-clipper/why-europa-ingredients-for-life/

Current facts used: Clipper launched October 14, 2024; arrival at Jupiter is planned for April 2030; the mission investigates whether Europa has conditions suitable for life and is not itself a direct life-detection mission.

### Europa carbon dioxide

NASA Webb summary: https://www.nasa.gov/solar-system/nasas-webb-finds-carbon-source-on-surface-of-jupiters-moon-europa/

Key result used: CO2 concentration in Tara Regio is interpreted as likely originating from Europa's subsurface ocean and as geologically recent surface emplacement/exchange.

Boundary: carbon from an ocean is a habitability clue, not biological carbon.

### Europa surface processing

NASA/JPL summary of impact gardening and radiation-related biosignature degradation: https://europa.nasa.gov/news/34/surface-of-jupiters-moon-europa-churned-by-small-impacts/

Key use: potential surface biosignatures are exposed to radiation and impact mixing; shallow subsurface material may preserve chemistry better.

### Deep biosphere scale

Deep Carbon Observatory project archive: https://deepcarbon.science/

2018 DCO synthesis release mirrored by EurekAlert: https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/510185

Reported synthesis ranges used with explicit uncertainty: approximately 2–2.3 billion km³ deep-biosphere volume and roughly 15–23 Pg carbon in deep life. The manuscript treats these as extrapolative estimates rather than direct measurements of every subsurface habitat.

## Contradiction and confounder audit

### “Deep life is independent of sunlight”

Counterevidence / boundary: many deep ecosystems consume buried organic matter ultimately made by photosynthesis or electron acceptors generated through surface biology. The manuscript reserves strong independence language for specific systems where local geochemical energy budgets are documented or plausible, rather than assigning independence to the deep biosphere as a whole.

### “Liquid water means habitability”

Counterevidence / boundary: salinity, water activity, temperature, energy flux, nutrient availability, permeability and waste removal can render liquid environments biologically poor. High-pressure ice can potentially isolate an ocean from rock. The book treats water as solvent access, not sufficient condition.

### “Hydrogen and methane mean life”

Counterevidence / boundary: serpentinization, radiolysis and other abiotic reactions produce hydrogen; abiotic methane pathways exist. Geochemical source context is mandatory.

### “Europa/Enceladus resemble terrestrial vents, therefore life should exist”

Counterevidence / boundary: terrestrial vent organisms descend from an already evolved biosphere. Habitability does not solve origin. Ocean-world origin pathways remain unresolved.

### “Very old sediment contains individual 100-million-year-old cells”

Counterevidence / boundary: the secure age is depositional. Slow generational turnover and limited migration remain possible. Manuscript language is constrained accordingly.

### “Deep biosphere estimates show most life is underground”

Counterevidence / boundary: global cell and biomass estimates rely on sparse sampling and extrapolation through heterogeneous crust. The existence and broad extent of subsurface life are secure; exact fractions are not.

### “Mars methane is a biosignature”

Counterevidence / boundary: observational disagreement and abiotic source/sink possibilities remain substantial. No manuscript argument depends on Martian methane being biological.

### “Absence of surface life implies absence of life”

Counterevidence: this is exactly the inference deep-Earth controls reject. However, moving every null indefinitely deeper would make the hypothesis unfalsifiable. The manuscript instead requires habitat-specific predictions and appropriately scoped negative conclusions.

## Known incentive distortions

- **Astrobiology discovery incentive:** novel habitability or biosignature claims attract funding and attention; the manuscript separates ingredients from inhabitants.
- **Mission advocacy:** proposed missions benefit from framing destinations as potentially life-bearing; official objectives are quoted conservatively and not upgraded.
- **Popular-science compression:** “radiation-powered bacterium” and “100-million-year-old microbes revived” are memorable but mechanistically lossy; primary papers control wording.
- **Origin-of-life school competition:** vent, pond, ice and other origin models have active proponents. The book does not adopt one as established.
- **Negative-result weakness:** sparse sampling makes global sterile-world conclusions difficult. The book treats nulls at their actual sampling scale.

## Falsification posture

The central synthesis would be weakened if terrestrial sun-independent deep ecosystems proved much rarer or more surface-dependent than current evidence indicates, if plausible extraterrestrial subsurface environments lacked sustained free-energy flux once better geochemical data arrived, or if repeated targeted sampling showed that deeply protected environments are consistently sterile despite long-lived energy-rich conditions.

The book becomes stronger—not weaker—if such negative evidence is explicit. The subject is not “life always finds a way.” The subject is what Earth has demonstrated biology can do, and what that demonstration does and does not authorize us to infer elsewhere.
