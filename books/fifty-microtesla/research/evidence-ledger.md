# FIFTY MICROTESLA — evidence ledger

Working rule: **behavior is not receptor; receptor is not pathway; magnetic sensitivity is not a conscious magnetic percept.**

## 1 — Verified / documented

- Migratory birds including European robins show magnetic-compass orientation under controlled conditions. A substantial behavioral literature reports that the compass is light-dependent and inclination-based rather than a simple polarity compass.
- Ritz et al., *Nature* (2004), reported that weak oscillating RF fields disrupted magnetic orientation in European robins, including a 7 MHz condition. This is evidence consistent with a radical-pair process, not direct identification of the receptor. https://doi.org/10.1038/nature02534
- Zapka et al., *Nature* (2009), reported that lesions of visual forebrain cluster N abolished magnetic-compass-guided orientation while trigeminal section did not abolish the compass in their European-robin experiments. https://doi.org/10.1038/nature08528
- Xu et al., *Nature* (2021), showed purified European-robin cryptochrome 4 (ErCRY4) is magnetically sensitive in vitro and more sensitive in their assay than homologues from chicken and pigeon. Site-specific mutations implicated a chain of flavin–tryptophan radical pairs. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-03618-9
- Bassetto et al., *Nature* (2023), showed radical-pair magnetosensitivity in Drosophila and found that expression of European-robin CRY4 could support magnetic-field sensitivity in fly assays. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-05735-z
- Denton et al., *Nature Communications* (2024), modeled a route by which a tightly bound FAD-superoxide radical pair could remain responsive to Earth-strength fields under strongly asymmetric recombination, invoking the quantum Zeno effect. This is a mechanistic proposal/model, not demonstration that this pair is the avian compass receptor. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-55124-x

## 2 — Credible but incomplete

- The radical-pair/cryptochrome model is currently one of the leading explanations for the avian magnetic compass. The convergence of light dependence, RF disruption, visual-system involvement, retinal cryptochrome localization, in-vitro CRY4 sensitivity and cross-species molecular work is substantial. It is still not a completed receptor-to-neural-code causal chain.
- CRY4 is a strong candidate receptor. There is not yet conclusive experimental evidence that a particular cryptochrome is the primary magnetoreceptor in birds or other vertebrates; this limitation is stated explicitly in the 2023 RF literature.
- Magnetite-based sensing remains relevant to magnetic biology, especially as a candidate for map/intensity information in some taxa. Evidence against a trigeminal requirement for the robin compass should not be inflated into a universal elimination of magnetite-based magnetic sensing.

## 3 — Disputed / unconfirmed

- That humans possess a useful conscious magnetic compass comparable to migratory birds.
- That CRY4 has been proved to be *the* avian magnetoreceptor in vivo.
- That one identified radical pair completely explains avian navigation.
- That weak anthropogenic RF effects on bird orientation establish broad human health effects from everyday RF exposure.
- That quantum coherence in magnetoreception supports quantum consciousness, telepathy, psi, or other nonlocal cognition claims.

## 4 — Speculative synthesis / open conjecture

The book's wager is that magnetoreception is epistemically valuable beyond navigation. It is a case in which an animal behavior looked almost occult because the relevant stimulus is weak, invisible to us, and difficult to map onto a familiar receptor. The mechanism may require quantum spin chemistry without requiring macroscopic quantum mysticism. That combination lets the book ask a dangerous question cleanly: **how many apparently impossible biological observations are impossible only because we have guessed the wrong transducer?**

That question does not license unknown transducers on demand. The radical-pair story became serious because it accumulated restrictive predictions: light dependence, field geometry, frequency sensitivity, candidate molecules, mutations, localization, and behavior. An anomaly earns mechanism by becoming easier to kill.

## Contradictions and live problems

- Candidate radical chemistry remains contested. Short-wavelength photoreduction models face behavioral findings under wavelengths that complicate simple versions of the FAD/tryptophan story.
- RF-disruption results are powerful but experimentally delicate; shielding, local electromagnetic noise, coil geometry, field calibration and replication conditions matter.
- In-vitro molecular sensitivity is not in-vivo receptor proof. The missing chain includes native photochemistry, amplification, partner proteins, retinal organization, neural coding and behavioral necessity.
- Different magnetic tasks may use different receptors. Compass direction, polarity, inclination, intensity and geographic map information must not be collapsed.

## Strongest countermodel

There may be no single elegant magnetic sense. Animals could combine multiple ordinary-but-specialized mechanisms: radical-pair chemistry for a light-dependent compass, magnetic particles or other receptors for intensity/map cues, and nonmagnetic celestial/olfactory/landmark cues for calibration. The apparent mystery may come partly from researchers trying to force a distributed navigation stack into one receptor story.

## Defeat conditions

The central CRY4-forward narrative weakens materially if clean in-vivo loss-of-function experiments leave magnetic compass behavior intact under conditions where all relevant CRY4 function is demonstrably abolished; if the reported weak-RF behavioral signatures fail robust preregistered multi-lab replication under calibrated low-noise conditions; or if another receptor system supplies a complete stimulus-to-neural-code-to-behavior causal chain that explains the same restrictive observations better.

## Publication holds

Do not write “birds see magnetic fields” as established phenomenology. Do not write “quantum compass proved.” Do not infer consciousness from magnetosensitivity. Do not generalize robin results to all birds or all magnetic behaviors. Do not convert a modeled quantum-Zeno route into a biological finding. Do not use patents or popular accounts as efficacy evidence.
