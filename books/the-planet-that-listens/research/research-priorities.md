## Defeat conditions for book-level synthesis

### Claim: verification research became an accidental planetary observatory

Defeated or materially weakened if:
- scientific/civil uses are mostly promotional anecdotes rather than sustained operational or peer-reviewed applications;
- data access is too restricted for meaningful non-verification science at scale;
- major examples (GRBs, meteors, tsunami warning) prove isolated rather than structurally related to background characterization.

Evidence currently favors the synthesis, but publication pass should quantify breadth rather than rely on memorable examples.

### Claim: independent physical channels are more valuable than sensor count

Defeated or weakened if operational nuclear-test attribution is shown to depend overwhelmingly on one dominant technology with other channels contributing negligible information across realistic event classes.

Current evidence supports event-dependent complementarity: seismic dominates underground detection; radionuclides can establish nuclear character; infrasound/hydroacoustics add geometry/coupling in selected cases.

### Claim: Vela 1979 remains publicly unresolved

Defeated if newly released primary material provides a reproducible, high-confidence physical chain establishing either a nuclear event or a non-nuclear mechanism, with credible source attribution where claimed.

If such evidence exists, manuscript must update decisively rather than preserve ambiguity for narrative value.

### Claim: modern IMS would materially narrow a Vela-like ambiguity

Defeated or weakened if modeling shows a plausible 1979 source yield/location would likely evade modern infrasound, hydroacoustic, seismic and radionuclide stations just as thoroughly, leaving optical/national sensors dominant.

This should be tested quantitatively in the next pass.

### Claim: cavity decoupling is best treated as a costed evasion mechanism rather than magic invisibility

Defeated if primary experiments show practically achievable decoupling under realistic clandestine conditions can routinely suppress all relevant observables without major observable preparation.

Current public literature instead suggests strong but condition-dependent seismic decoupling with significant engineering constraints.

## Decisive analyses to add

- Forward simulation: hypothetical 1 kt, 0.1 kt and 10 t underground sources across granite, tuff, salt and cavity-decoupled cases; list expected detectability by regional/global seismic networks and likely cross-media channels.
- Counterfactual Vela 1979: place a range of hypothetical surface/maritime sources near proposed regions and estimate which current IMS stations would be geometrically useful.
- Background atlas: one year of representative IMS-like event classes by technology to demonstrate how sparse true nuclear events are relative to natural/industrial backgrounds.
- Source independence graph for Vela: every public claim node linked to underlying document/data source so repeated secondary citations cannot masquerade as corroboration.

## Prose risks for anti-slop pass

- Repeated “the planet does not care about our categories” construction.
- Overuse of short aphoristic closers around physics/politics.
- Repetition of “Class 1 is evidence of belief, not event” phrasing; retain the principle but let individual documents carry it.
- Excessive use of “not X but Y” around verification vs certainty.
- Abstract epistemology arriving before scene or instrument detail.

Final pass should deepen the odd physical objects—bhangmeter waveform, Tatum salt cavity, SOFAR hydrophone cable, xenon sampler, microbarometer array—rather than adding more framework language.
