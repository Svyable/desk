# Zoonosis: Nature’s Deadliest Diseases

## Sven Hardy Benson

A zoonosis is an infection that can pass between animals and humans. That definition is clean. The world it describes is not.

This book begins at the seam: bat and orchard, camel and market, rodent and kitchen, mosquito and blood, chicken and industrial shed, forest edge and road. It is a history of diseases, but more importantly a history of contact. Nature supplies astonishing biological possibility. Human beings rearrange the encounters.

The central argument is not that every epidemic is caused by environmental destruction, farming, trade, or poverty. That would be too easy and often false. Spillover is contingent. Many exposures go nowhere. Many animal pathogens cannot reproduce in us. Some cross once and die with their accidental host. Others acquire the ability to move among humans and alter history.

The useful question is therefore not simply, “What animal did it come from?” It is: what chain of ecology, behavior, infrastructure, biology, economics, and chance made this crossing possible—and which links could have been broken?

Throughout, four evidence classes are kept separate: established findings; credible but incomplete evidence; disputed claims; and explicit synthesis or speculation. Where origin evidence is incomplete, the uncertainty stays on the page.
