# Forgotten Zoonoses

Pandemic preparedness has a glamour problem.

Novel viruses attract simulations, headlines, venture capital, security briefings, and emergency appropriations. Meanwhile, endemic zoonoses keep disabling and killing people whose suffering is too geographically familiar to count as surprise.

Brucellosis moves through livestock and unpasteurized dairy products and can produce prolonged human illness. Leptospirosis circulates through animal urine and contaminated water, flourishing where flooding, sanitation failures, occupational exposure, and poverty overlap. Anthrax persists in environmental spores and livestock systems. Bovine tuberculosis complicates the boundary between veterinary and human disease control. Rabies remains the clearest indictment.

These diseases are not “emerging,” and that linguistic fact can reduce their political visibility. A new threat promises a future catastrophe. An old threat requires admitting that the catastrophe has already been tolerated.

The global burden is also hard to count. People may never reach clinics. Diagnostics may be absent. Symptoms resemble other febrile illnesses. Veterinary reporting and human surveillance may live in databases that do not communicate. Underdiagnosis then becomes underfunding, which produces more underdiagnosis.

Neglect is a feedback loop.

The most revealing map of zoonotic disease is therefore not the map of where pathogens exist. It is the map of where infection becomes visible enough to matter to institutions.
