# COVID-19 and the Unfinished Origin

Origin stories become political because origins distribute blame.

SARS-CoV-2 demands unusual discipline. As of the mid-2020s, substantial scientific evidence supports a zoonotic-spillover pathway associated with the early Wuhan outbreak and susceptible mammals in the wildlife trade. The deeper viral lineage is related to coronaviruses found in bats. Yet important underlying data have remained unavailable, and no intermediate animal carrying the direct progenitor has been established.

A laboratory-related accident has also been investigated and argued for. Publicly available evidence has not established it as fact. WHO’s 2025 scientific advisory assessment said the weight of available evidence suggested zoonotic spillover while also stating that necessary information had not been provided and that hypotheses could not be completely closed.

Those sentences belong together.

Certainty is tempting because uncertainty has been weaponized. But scientific integrity is not choosing the ambiguity most useful to one’s politics. It is preserving distinctions: evidence, inference, missing data, allegation, and speculation.

COVID-19 also demonstrates why origin and prevention are related but not identical. Even a perfectly reconstructed first infection would not explain the entire catastrophe. Delayed recognition, silent transmission, global mobility, indoor air, institutional preparedness, public communication, hospital capacity, vaccine development, inequality, and political decisions shaped what happened after emergence.

We should want the origin answer because truth matters and because mechanisms guide prevention. We should resist pretending to possess evidence we do not have for exactly the same reasons.

The lesson of COVID-19 is not that origin science failed. It is that origin science is forensic science conducted inside geopolitics.
