# Back Matter

The Cancer Key is a complete nonfiction Desk manuscript reported through September 4, 2026.

The field described here changes quickly. Trial status, regulatory status, company ownership, pricing and clinical guidance may change after the reporting cutoff. The manuscript therefore treats dates as part of the evidence rather than permanent labels.

The book uses “neoantigen” primarily to mean a tumor-specific antigen created by a cancer-associated alteration and presented to the immune system, while recognizing that the scientific literature uses related terminology across mutation-derived peptides, fusions and other tumor-specific antigen sources. “Personalized cancer vaccine” refers to a product whose selected antigen payload is tailored to an individual patient's tumor. It does not imply that every component of the vaccine is unique or that the treatment has been proven effective.

“FDA approved” refers to authorization by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration for a defined product and use. An investigational therapy in a registered clinical trial can be rigorously manufactured and legally administered without being approved. Expanded access, Right to Try and physician-led pathways in other jurisdictions are separate legal categories. The manuscript distinguishes these categories because collapsing them into the word “unapproved” obscures both legitimate research and genuine consumer-protection problems.

The companies discussed in the book are included because they illustrate parts of the neoantigen value stack or have publicly documented programs relevant to the reporting period. Inclusion is not an endorsement. Discussion of direct-pay or frontier providers describes public claims and institutional models and should not be read as a judgment that any named provider's treatment is clinically effective, ineffective, lawful or unlawful beyond what cited regulators or sources establish.

The September 4 update adds TG4050, an individualized Modified Vaccinia Ankara viral-vector vaccine developed by Transgene with NEC's neoantigen-prediction technology, as a new early randomized signal outside melanoma. The peer-reviewed Phase I report remains small and was primarily designed around safety; the later three-year disease-free follow-up is company-reported. Neither should be read as equivalent to a large confirmatory efficacy trial.

The research trail in the accompanying `research/` directory records the major sources used for concrete claims, chapter by chapter, along with counter-evidence and questions that could falsify the book's working thesis. Primary sources are preferred for trial status, regulatory policy, public funding and company announcements; peer-reviewed literature is preferred for biological and clinical interpretation. Company websites are used for company descriptions and advertised offerings, not as independent evidence of efficacy.

The release-candidate research record is frozen to September 4, 2026. Later scientific presentations, regulatory decisions, trial readouts, pricing changes or product-status changes belong to a later edition rather than being silently backfilled into this one.

No medical decision should be made from this book. People with cancer should seek advice from licensed clinicians who can evaluate pathology, stage, prior treatment, current evidence and trial eligibility for the individual patient.
