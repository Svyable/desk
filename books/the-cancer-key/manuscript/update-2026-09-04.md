# Update — September 4, 2026

Three days after the reporting cutoff for the first released edition, the map moved again.

Not enough to change the central argument of this book. Enough to sharpen it.

Transgene and NEC reported new long-term follow-up from TG4050, an individualized neoantigen therapeutic vaccine being studied after surgery and adjuvant treatment for HPV-negative head and neck squamous cell carcinoma. The platform is not mRNA. It uses a modified vaccinia Ankara viral vector to encode as many as thirty patient-specific predicted neoantigens selected from tumor sequencing.

That difference matters because the neoantigen idea has never belonged to one delivery technology.

The target is the private mutation.

The vehicle can change.

The peer-reviewed Phase I report, published in Nature Communications, describes a randomized study in which sixteen evaluable patients received TG4050 immediately after standard treatment and sixteen were assigned to observation with the option to receive the vaccine after recurrence. At the paper's primary data cutoff, after a median follow-up of roughly thirty months, none of the sixteen patients in the immediate-treatment group had relapsed, while three of sixteen in the observation group had relapsed. The vaccine was reported as well tolerated. Neoantigen-specific T-cell responses were detected in most treated patients and persisted beyond the end of dosing in some cases.

Those numbers are striking.

They are also small.

The trial's primary purpose was safety. Sixteen patients per group is not a substitute for a powered Phase III efficacy trial, and percentages become visually dramatic when the denominator is tiny. A result of zero relapses among sixteen treated patients is a reason to keep testing the approach. It is not evidence that an individualized vaccine prevents every recurrence in head and neck cancer.

The distinction is especially important here because the September follow-up arrived with a headline that naturally invites overstatement. Transgene and NEC said that all sixteen immediately treated patients remained disease-free at three years. That later update extends the favorable observation from the peer-reviewed paper, but the three-year figure is company-reported follow-up rather than a new randomized publication with a larger sample.

This book has argued from the beginning that personalized cancer medicine needs two disciplines at once.

One is the willingness to notice a signal before every uncertainty has disappeared.

The other is the willingness to keep the denominator in view.

TG4050 deserves both.

The result adds something the melanoma success could not add by itself. The strongest validation in the field still comes from Merck and Moderna's large Phase III melanoma program. TG4050 does not rival that evidentiary weight. What it does is broaden the plausible platform story beyond one company pair, one tumor type and one delivery format.

That matters for the meaning of the word platform.

If individualized neoantigen medicine were really a story about one proprietary mRNA process, then the science and the company would be difficult to separate. A favorable randomized signal from a viral-vector program makes the underlying target class look a little more independent of the vehicle used to deliver it.

The architecture is familiar even when the product is different.

Tumor tissue is sequenced. Mutations are identified. A prediction system ranks candidate neoantigens. A patient-specific set is selected. A manufacturing platform encodes that set. The product is administered after visible disease has been treated, when the remaining malignant population is smaller and the immune system may have a more realistic chance of erasing residual clones before they become clinically obvious.

The repeated pattern strengthens one of this book's more cautious claims: the adjuvant setting may be where personalization first becomes easiest to prove.

That does not mean it will remain there.

It means recurrence prevention gives the field a cleaner biological problem than trying to reverse a large, heterogeneous, immunosuppressive tumor under immediate time pressure.

TG4050 also sharpens the company chapter.

Transgene brings a viral-vector manufacturing platform. NEC brings computational target selection. The study acknowledgements identify funding from Bpifrance, Transgene and NEC Bio. The familiar public-private braid appears again: public financing, corporate technology, academic and clinical investigators, patient tissue, proprietary prediction and a product whose value will ultimately depend on larger trials.

The breakthrough still refuses to stay inside one institutional box.

This is why the ownership question cannot be reduced to whether taxpayers paid for a particular vial.

Public value can enter earlier through scientific infrastructure, research grants, clinical institutions, national investment agencies and the datasets that make prediction possible. Private value enters through capital at risk, manufacturing systems, software, trial execution and commercialization. A later product can be genuinely private and still rest on a public substrate.

TG4050 also gives the field another useful falsification opportunity.

Its favorable Phase I signal can fail in Phase II.

The zero-versus-three recurrence pattern can narrow as more patients accumulate. The immune responses can prove interesting without translating into a clinically meaningful difference. Manufacturing can work in a small trial and become harder at scale. A result in resected HPV-negative head and neck cancer can remain specific to that setting.

None of those outcomes would make the present data fraudulent or meaningless.

They would make the map more precise.

That is what mature medicine does with promising early evidence.

It does not freeze the headline.

It asks the next study to carry more weight.

For patients, the update changes almost nothing about the practical advice in this book. TG4050 remains investigational. It is not an FDA-approved personalized cancer vaccine. The existence of a favorable early randomized result does not make direct-pay products using different formulations, different manufacturing controls or different diseases equivalent to the studied product.

This point becomes more important as the category gains credibility.

A rising scientific tide can lift weak commercial claims along with strong clinical programs. Every legitimate success in personalized cancer vaccination makes it easier for an unrelated provider to say that "personalized cancer vaccines work" and allow the listener to supply the missing inference.

The inference is usually the problem.

A platform can be real while a particular product is unproven.

A product can be promising while a particular disease is unresponsive.

A clinical signal can be meaningful while a commercial claim built on it is misleading.

The more evidence the field accumulates, the more carefully those levels need to be separated.

The September TG4050 update therefore does not overturn the conclusion of The Cancer Key.

It makes the conclusion less dependent on melanoma.

Neoantigens increasingly look like a genuine therapeutic target class that can be operationalized through more than one technological stack. But the strongest proof remains unevenly distributed across diseases and trial phases. The field now contains a positive large Phase III melanoma topline result, promising randomized early-stage head and neck data, durable mechanistic pancreatic signals, early personalized T-cell responses in solid tumors and a terminated colorectal program.

That is not a solved disease.

It is a field beginning to separate the places where the key turns from the places where it does not.

The next decisive evidence will come from repetition.

More patients.

More tumor types.

More platforms.

More randomized comparisons.

More complete accounting of who was enrolled, who reached manufacturing, who received treatment and who did not recur.

The scientific achievement is becoming easier to see because it is becoming easier to test.

That is progress.
