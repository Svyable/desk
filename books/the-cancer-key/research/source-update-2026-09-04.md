## TG4050 — randomized Phase I evidence and later follow-up

48. **Ottensmeier, Christian, et al. “A viral-based individualized neoantigen vaccine as adjuvant treatment in resected head and neck squamous cell carcinoma: a randomized Phase I trial.” Nature Communications (2026).**  
    https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-76667-1  
    Use: peer-reviewed randomized Phase I evidence for TG4050 in resected, locally advanced HPV-negative head and neck squamous cell carcinoma. TG4050 encodes up to 30 patient-specific predicted neoantigens in a Modified Vaccinia Ankara viral vector. Among 16 evaluable patients assigned to immediate vaccination, no relapses were reported at a median follow-up of about 30 months, versus three relapses among 16 patients assigned to observation and vaccination after recurrence. Safety was the primary endpoint; feasibility and efficacy were secondary, and immunogenicity was exploratory. Neoantigen-specific T-cell responses were detected in treated patients. The small sample does not establish confirmatory efficacy. The paper's acknowledgements identify funding from Bpifrance, Transgene and NEC Bio.

49. **Transgene / NEC. “Transgene and NEC Report 100% Three-Year Disease-Free Survival with TG4050 and Publish Phase 1 Results in Nature Communications.” September 2–3, 2026.**  
    https://www.nec.com/en/press/202609/global_20260903_02.html  
    https://www.transgene.com/press-releases/  
    Use: later company-reported follow-up stating that all 16 immediately treated TG4050 patients remained disease-free at three years and noting continued development of the program. This is used only as a dated follow-up to the peer-reviewed Phase I report, not as independent confirmatory efficacy evidence.

## Interpretation rule

The TG4050 update modestly broadens the evidence base for individualized neoantigen vaccination beyond melanoma and beyond mRNA delivery. It does not change the hierarchy of evidence in the book: the large Phase III INTerpath-001 melanoma topline remains the strongest current clinical validation; TG4050 remains an investigational, small Phase I signal requiring larger randomized confirmation.

The update does not convert any personalized neoantigen vaccine into an FDA-approved product and does not imply that unrelated direct-pay personalized cancer-vaccine offerings share TG4050's manufacturing controls, clinical protocol or evidence.
