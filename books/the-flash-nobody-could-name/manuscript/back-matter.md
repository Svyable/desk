# Back Matter

This manuscript is a working Desk edition. It uses only publicly available, lawfully accessible sources: declassified U.S. government records, public archival collections, official laboratory histories, scholarly work, and credible secondary reporting used as a map back to primary material. It does not claim access to classified information.

The evidentiary posture is intentionally asymmetric toward caution. A document can establish that an agency or official made an assessment without establishing the event described by that assessment. A later admission that a state possessed nuclear weapons establishes capability, not responsibility for the September 22, 1979 event. A physically plausible natural explanation is not treated as a demonstrated cause. Actor attribution remains separate from event classification throughout.

The central public archive used for this pass is the National Security Archive's Nuclear Vault collection on the Vela incident, especially its 2016 and 2019 document sets. Those collections reproduce or summarize declassified White House, State Department, CIA, Defense, and Energy records and provide archival provenance. The source log in `research/source-log.md` records the links used in the working draft and identifies where underlying technical reports still need page-level reconstruction.

Important unresolved research targets include the full Ruina panel report and technical appendices; original Vela 6911 bhangmeter traces and calibration information sufficient for quantitative reconstruction; the Naval Research Laboratory hydroacoustic analysis and search-space denominator; original Arecibo event analysis; full atmospheric-sampling and meteorological records; laboratory documentation for the Australian sheep iodine-131 result; and authenticated primary records bearing directly on specific actor attribution.

The book should change if those records change the evidence. Its defeat conditions are explicit in the research matrix. A complete technical reconstruction showing that the September 22 optical pattern can be reproduced by a documented non-nuclear spacecraft-local mechanism, with the alleged corroborating channels explained independently, would materially weaken the nuclear-event thesis. Conversely, an authenticated contemporaneous operational record or uniquely diagnostic physical chain tying a nuclear detonation to the observed time and region would move the event out of the disputed category. A specific Israel–South Africa joint-test attribution requires additional actor-specific evidence beyond proof that some nuclear detonation occurred.
