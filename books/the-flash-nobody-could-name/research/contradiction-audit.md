# Contradiction and incentive audit

The Vela case is unusually vulnerable to narrative capture because almost every major actor had a legitimate institutional reason to weight the same evidence differently. This file records contradictions instead of smoothing them into a single story.

## Optical record

**Nuclear-favoring reading:** the bhangmeter system was designed around a distinctive atmospheric nuclear double pulse and had a strong record against known tests. A double-pulse event should therefore begin with a high nuclear prior.

**Counterevidence:** the two bhangmeters were less self-consistent than in typical known nuclear events, particularly around the second pulse. The satellite was old, and a spacecraft-local disturbance was physically conceivable.

**Do not resolve by rhetoric:** “designed to detect bombs” does not make every trigger a bomb; “aging satellite” does not make every anomalous trigger hardware failure.

## Meteoroid / local-spacecraft explanation

**Supporting reading:** a meteoroid impact or impact-generated/reflected light can potentially explain asymmetry between sensors and a transient local optical event.

**Counterevidence:** the mechanism must reproduce the timing and morphology of the observed double pulse, not merely generate light. Publicly available reconstruction remains insufficient here.

**Incentive risk:** a White House panel explicitly tasked to look for natural/technical alternatives may correctly expose an initial confirmation bias while acquiring a corrective bias of its own.

## Nuclear scientific review

**Supporting reading:** Agnew, Garwin, Lukasik, and other technically experienced officials treated a nuclear event as plausible/likely despite acknowledging anomalies.

**Counterevidence:** expertise in weapons phenomenology can increase sensitivity to real target signatures but does not eliminate false-positive risk; exact probabilistic calibration of expert judgment is unknown.

**Incentive risk:** agencies responsible for strategic warning and proliferation may rationally prefer false positives to false negatives at the collection stage.

## Ruina panel

**Supporting reading:** panel considered signal deviations, lack of decisive corroboration, archive comparisons, and natural mechanisms; its conclusion was probabilistic, not merely a public-relations sentence.

**Counterevidence:** significant technical dissent survived; later declassified records indicate some intelligence assessments remained strongly nuclear-favoring.

**Incentive risk:** a non-nuclear conclusion reduced immediate diplomatic costs. Political usefulness is not proof of manipulation, but it changes how the conclusion was operationalized.

## Hydroacoustics

**Nuclear-favoring reading:** later official accounts describe NRL analysis as locating nuclear-like maritime acoustic evidence near Prince Edward/Marion Islands.

**Counterevidence:** underlying public waveform, array geometry, search denominator, and alternative-event analysis are incomplete in the working record.

**Do not overclaim:** a memo about a technical report is primary evidence of the memo and secondary evidence of the underlying waveform unless the report is available.

## Arecibo ionosphere

**Nuclear-favoring reading:** a traveling ionospheric disturbance may be physically consistent with a large atmospheric source and was considered one of the better corroboration leads.

**Counterevidence:** ionospheric disturbances are non-unique; the White House record itself notes that the causal relationship was less well understood and the timescale differed.

**Search-bias risk:** retrospective archive search after a salient alarm can discover weak coincidences.

## Fallout and iodine-131

**Nuclear-favoring reading:** iodine-131 is a fission product, and abnormal levels were reportedly found in Australian sheep after rain in the post-event period.

**Counterevidence:** immediate sampling did not produce an unambiguous public plume; the NSC memo explicitly records investigation of industrial/pharmaceutical sources. Public working set lacks quantitative isotope suite and controls.

**Do not overclaim:** “iodine-131 existed” is not “Vela fallout reached Melbourne.”

## South Africa

**Nuclear-favoring attribution:** South Africa later acknowledged a real nuclear arsenal and possessed relevant technical/geographic capability.

**Counterevidence:** later capability disclosure does not provide an event-specific chain to September 22, 1979.

**Moral-bias risk:** the apartheid regime's documented secrecy and abuses can make technically unsupported allegations feel easier to accept. Keep moral judgment separate from event proof.

## Israel

**Nuclear-favoring attribution:** Israel had a sophisticated opaque nuclear posture and politically sensitive relations with the United States; cooperation with South Africa gives joint-test scenarios surface plausibility.

**Counterevidence:** current public source set contains no authenticated operational record establishing a joint September 22 test.

**Political-incentive risk:** Carter administration had real reasons to avoid a crisis involving Israeli nuclear testing. That is evidence about incentive, not evidence that the test occurred or was covered up.

## Cover-up thesis

**Supporting material:** strong internal disagreement, political stakes, selective/fragmentary declassification, and a semiofficial non-nuclear conclusion despite continuing dissent.

**Counterevidence:** adversarial scientific review is exactly what a responsible warning system should do after an anomalous alert. A politically convenient conclusion can still be technically correct.

**Required proof:** show that decision-makers possessed and accepted decisive contrary evidence and knowingly misrepresented it. Current working record does not clear that bar.

## Author incentives

This subject rewards certainty. A definitive secret test is more marketable than sensor ambiguity. A definitive debunking is also cleaner. The manuscript's risk is mistaking unresolvedness for sophistication and refusing to update. The defeat conditions in `evidence-matrix.md` are therefore binding: if stronger primary records decisively close the physical event, the book must say so even if that makes the current thesis less interesting.
