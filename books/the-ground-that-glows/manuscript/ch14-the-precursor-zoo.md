# The Precursor Zoo

Earthquake lights do not live alone.

They share the enclosure with radon anomalies, groundwater changes, electrical transients, magnetic fluctuations, ionospheric disturbances, thermal infrared anomalies, unusual animal behavior, cloud claims, changes in well levels, and assorted signals that appear in papers under the word *precursor*.

The list is so long that it creates two opposite intuitions.

One is optimistic. Fault preparation is a huge physical process. Rock deforms. Cracks open. Fluids move. Gas escapes. Stress changes conductivity. It would be surprising if absolutely nothing measurable happened before rupture.

The other intuition is statistical. If enough variables are monitored around enough earthquakes, some will look unusual before some events by chance alone.

Both intuitions can be true.

Radon is a good example. Radon emanation can change with cracking, fluid transport, pressure, temperature, and meteorological conditions. There are published pre-earthquake anomalies. There are also many non-seismic causes of variation and no universally reliable warning rule. The signal is physically connected to crustal processes but operationally promiscuous.

Animals are worse. There are credible observations that animals respond to weak vibrations, infrasound, electric fields, magnetic changes, chemical changes, and social cues humans may miss. That biological sensitivity makes unusual behavior plausible. It does not make an anecdote specific to an impending earthquake. A dog that behaves strangely is a sensor with unknown calibration, unknown threshold, no timestamp protocol, and a reporter who usually becomes interested only after the earthquake.

Multi-parameter approaches promise to fix this. Perhaps no one precursor is reliable, but a combination is. A radon rise plus a magnetic anomaly plus an ionospheric change plus clustered microseismicity might be far more specific than any component.

This is reasonable. It is also a perfect environment for overfitting.

With enough variables, an algorithm can discover a pattern before historical earthquakes that never repeats. Machine learning does not abolish the retrospective machine; it can automate it.

The only defense is untouched data.

Train the model on one period. Freeze it. Evaluate it on a later period in which the earthquakes have not yet been used to tune feature selection. Report full confusion matrices and alert durations. Compare against simple baselines such as recent seismicity and established probabilistic forecasts. Publish failures.

Some recent ionospheric studies have begun reporting true-positive and false-positive rates rather than only famous case studies. That is progress even when the performance is mediocre. A field becomes more credible when it starts measuring how often it is wrong.

The precursor zoo may eventually yield one useful animal.

For now, the cages need labels.
