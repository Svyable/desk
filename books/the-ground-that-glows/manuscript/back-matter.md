# Back Matter

## A note on evidence

This manuscript deliberately separates four evidence classes rather than averaging them into a single confidence tone.

**Verified/declassified/documented facts** include instrumented earthquakes, published laboratory measurements, mission histories, official geological assessments, and statements that can be checked against primary or authoritative records.

**Credible but incomplete evidence** includes well-documented luminous observations whose source is not uniquely identified, statistical precursor associations not yet demonstrated as reliable operational forecasts, and physical mechanisms that work under constrained conditions but have unresolved field-scale transport or energy questions.

**Disputed claims** include specific precursor interpretations without independent prospective confirmation, universal mechanism claims, and event attributions that do not rule out infrastructure, atmospheric, instrumental, or statistical alternatives.

**Speculative synthesis** is explicitly the author's. It is used to connect evidence streams and design tests, not to upgrade claims.

## What would change the conclusion

The manuscript's central distinction would need revision if an independently run, preregistered, prospective precursor system demonstrated reproducible earthquake forecast skill with useful lead time and a tolerable false-alarm burden across multiple tectonic regions. Conversely, the earthquake-light side of the argument should contract sharply if continuous multi-instrument field networks fail to find non-infrastructure luminous events under geological conditions the current mechanisms identify as favorable.

The book is intentionally unfinished at the level of those experiments. The physics is interesting enough without pretending the verdict arrived early.
