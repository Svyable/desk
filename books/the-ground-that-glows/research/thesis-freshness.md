## Selected thesis

**The Ground That Glows: Earthquake Lights, Electric Rocks, and the Prediction Problem** argues that a subset of earthquake-associated luminous/electromagnetic phenomena may be physically real while the stronger claim of useful short-term electromagnetic prediction remains unestablished. The empirical collision is between instrumented earthquake-light cases and rock-electrical mechanisms on one side, and the denominator/base-rate failures of ionospheric precursor research on the other.

## Diversity against previous five Author-agent empirical families

At selection time the five newest Author-agent books were:

1. **The Light We Leak** — ultraweak biological photon emission / mitogenetic-radiation history.
2. **The White Ocean** — milky seas / marine bacterial bioluminescence / satellite ocean radiometry.
3. **The Sound That Arrived Too Soon** — electrophonic meteors / atmospheric electrodynamics / local acoustic transduction.
4. **The Flash Nobody Could Name** — 1979 Vela event / nuclear-detection sensor forensics.
5. **The Sky We Built** — Project West Ford / orbital environmental engineering and governance.

The present book is intentionally outside those empirical cores. Its center of gravity is solid-Earth geophysics, fault mechanics, rock electrification, atmospheric electrical breakdown, precursor statistics, operational earthquake forecasting, and lithosphere-atmosphere-ionosphere coupling.

## Similarity risk deliberately controlled

The subject contains light and electromagnetism, which superficially overlap with recent books. The empirical core is nevertheless distinct: not biological photon emission, marine bioluminescence, meteor transduction, nuclear detection, or orbital engineering. The manuscript also avoids turning the topic into another generic “hidden signal” book by making forecast calibration, false-alarm denominators, fault geology, rock transport physics, and infrastructure attribution central.
