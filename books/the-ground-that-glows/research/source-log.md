# Source Log

Checked 2026-09-14. Public, lawfully available sources only.

## Authoritative boundaries

- U.S. Geological Survey, “What are earthquake lights?” Notes disagreement over whether individual reports establish EQL, acknowledges plausible physics-based hypotheses for some cases, and explicitly notes that some reports are power-line arcing. https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/what-are-earthquake-lights
- U.S. Geological Survey, “Are earthquakes associated with variations in the geomagnetic field?” States that electromagnetic variations have been observed after earthquakes but that decades of work have not produced convincing evidence of electromagnetic precursors. https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/are-earthquakes-associated-variations-geomagnetic-field

## Ionospheric / satellite precursor research

- Parrot, M. (2018), “Statistical Analysis of the Ionospheric Density Recorded by the DEMETER Satellite During Seismic Activity,” in AGU Geophysical Monograph *Pre-Earthquake Processes*. Summarizes reported statistical perturbations and limitations. https://doi.org/10.1002/9781119156949.ch18
- Masci, F. (2015), “On the reliability of the Spatial Scintillation Index to detect earthquake precursors in the ionosphere,” *Radio Science*. Critical examination of an ionospheric precursor index. https://doi.org/10.1002/2015RS005734
- Akyol et al. (2020), “A Machine Learning-Based Detection of Earthquake Precursors Using Ionospheric Data,” *Radio Science*. Reports test-period detections and false alarms, useful for denominator-aware analysis. https://doi.org/10.1029/2019RS006931
- “Detection of pre-seismic magnetic field anomalies using Swarm satellite data: a case study of the 2025 Mw7.7 Myanmar earthquake,” *Scientific Reports* (2025). Current positive case-study literature. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-20901-1
- “Ionospheric Scintillation Anomalies from COSMIC-2 GNSS-RO from 2019 and 2024 as Potential Earthquake Precursors,” *ISPRS International Journal of Geo-Information* 15 (2026): 128. ## Critical context / forecasting standard

- Science / AAAS report on atmospheric electrical precursor debate, including comments from Tom Jordan and Jeffrey Love emphasizing lack of diagnostic evidence and the need for prospective testing. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aae0148

## Source discipline

Patents, popular video compilations, social-media clips, and unsourced historical retellings are not used as efficacy evidence. A public video can identify a candidate event only if provenance is sufficient to establish location/time; source attribution requires independent grid, lightning, geometry, and instrument evidence. Program funding or the existence of a satellite mission is evidence of research interest, not proof that the investigated precursor exists.
