# Contradiction and Incentive Audit

## Observation versus attribution

**Positive evidence:** Pisco and other cases include documented light during earthquakes.

**Counterevidence:** urban earthquakes produce power-grid arcs; USGS explicitly identifies this explanation for some reported EQL. Video without grid/lightning provenance cannot uniquely identify a geological source.

**Manuscript rule:** never write “the earthquake produced the light” when the record only establishes “light was recorded during the earthquake.”

## Coseismic effect versus precursor

**Positive evidence:** timed Pisco flashes correlate with strong local ground motion; shallow-source modeling can make local electrical discharge energetically more plausible.

**Contradiction:** a signal generated by passing seismic waves is a consequence of rupture, not a warning before rupture.

**Manuscript rule:** coseismic observations cannot be smuggled into the precursor evidence pile.

## Historical rift association

**Positive evidence:** Thériault et al. identify strong rift/steep-fault enrichment in a selected historical case set.

**Counterevidence/incentive:** land-based populated rifts provide better observation conditions than offshore subduction zones; catalog inclusion is partly retrospective.

**Manuscript rule:** treat rift enrichment as a testable hypothesis, not established geology.

## Positive-hole mechanism

**Positive evidence:** laboratory program reports stress-activated charge and develops a physical carrier model.

**Counterevidence:** field-scale propagation through wet heterogeneous crust, required carrier densities, and source-to-atmosphere energy budgets remain disputed; Jánský et al. sharply constrain deep-source scenarios.

**Incentive issue:** a mechanism capable of explaining lights, air ions, infrared changes, ground potentials, and ionospheric anomalies can become narratively overextended.

**Manuscript rule:** require quantitative scaling separately for each claimed observable.

## Satellite precursors

**Positive evidence:** DEMETER/Swarm/GNSS studies report statistical or event-level anomalies before earthquakes.

**Counterevidence:** ionosphere is intrinsically variable; solar/geomagnetic forcing and analytic flexibility create false structure; USGS finds no convincing reliable electromagnetic precursor.

**Incentive issue:** post-event case studies are publishable and visually compelling; null periods are less salient.

**Manuscript rule:** demand prospective or held-out performance, full false-alarm denominators, and baseline comparisons.

## Prediction culture

**Positive incentive:** a reliable short-term earthquake precursor would save lives and transform geophysics.

**Distorting incentives:** enormous societal value encourages overinterpretation of weak signals; after a disaster, hindsight makes anomalies look diagnostic; public audiences compress probabilistic claims into yes/no warnings.

**Manuscript rule:** preserve the distinction among physical anomaly, causal mechanism, statistical association, and operational forecast.
