# Notes and Sources

This book separates four kinds of claims throughout: documented observations; credible but incomplete inference; disputed attribution; and speculative synthesis. A sonar-associated stranding is not treated as proof of one mechanism merely because the timing is close. A controlled behavioral response is not treated as injury. A government report is not treated as omniscient. A pathology paper is not asked to reconstruct behavior it did not observe.

The primary documentary anchor for the 2000 Bahamas event is the U.S. Navy / National Marine Fisheries Service, *Joint Interim Report: Bahamas Marine Mammal Stranding Event of 15–16 March 2000* (2001), preserved by NOAA: https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/16198/. The investigation concluded that tactical mid-range-frequency sonars aboard U.S. Navy ships were the most plausible sound source involved while emphasizing the interaction with propagation, bathymetry, constrained egress and beaked-whale sensitivity.

For the 2002 Canary Islands event and the gas/fat embolic pathology, see Antonio Fernández et al., “Gas and Fat Embolic Syndrome Involving a Mass Stranding of Beaked Whales (Family Ziphiidae) Exposed to Anthropogenic Sonar Signals,” *Veterinary Pathology* 42 (2005): https://doi.org/10.1354/vp.42-4-446. The paper reports acute gas-bubble-associated lesions and fat embolism and discusses both behaviorally mediated nitrogen supersaturation and possible acoustic effects on gas nuclei as candidate mechanisms.

For the post-moratorium Canary Islands observation, see Antonio Fernández, Manuel Arbelo and Vidal Martín, “No mass strandings since sonar ban,” *Nature* 497 (2013): https://doi.org/10.1038/497317d.

For direct behavioral-response evidence, see Peter L. Tyack et al., “Beaked whales respond to simulated and actual navy sonar,” *PLOS ONE* 6 (2011): https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0017009. See also Stacy L. DeRuiter et al., “First direct measurements of behavioural responses by Cuvier’s beaked whales to mid-frequency active sonar,” *Biology Letters* 9 (2013): https://doi.org/10.1098/rsbl.2013.0223.

For the larger 2026 controlled-exposure data set, see Brandon L. Southall et al., “Behavioral responses of goose-beaked whales (*Ziphius cavirostris*) to simulated military sonar,” *Ecosphere* (2026): https://doi.org/10.1002/ecs2.70501.

For long-term abundance and survival on the Southern California Navy range, see K. Alexandra Curtis et al., “Abundance, survival, and annual rate of change of Cuvier’s beaked whales (*Ziphius cavirostris*) on a Navy sonar range,” *Marine Mammal Science* (2021): https://doi.org/10.1111/mms.12747.

For an acoustic-source comparison that did not show the same clear response pattern as naval mid-frequency active sonar, see the 12 kHz multibeam echosounder study in *JASA*: https://doi.org/10.1121/10.0001385.

For NOAA’s synthesis of Mariana Archipelago beaked-whale strandings and sonar concern, including the hypothesis that infrequent sonar exposure and quiet ambient conditions may increase response severity, see: https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/feature-story/beaked-whale-strandings-mariana-archipelago-may-be-associated-sonar.

For current U.S. regulatory treatment, including the 2025–2032 Atlantic Fleet Training and Testing Marine Mammal Protection Act authorization, see NOAA Fisheries: https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/action/incidental-take-authorization-military-readiness-activities-atlantic-fleet-training-and.

For the continuing incompleteness of basic beaked-whale natural history, see the 2026 first at-sea genetic/acoustic identification of ginkgo-toothed beaked whales: https://doi.org/10.1111/mms.70052.

The manuscript does not claim that all sonar-associated strandings share one mechanism, that every beaked whale exposure produces dangerous supersaturation, that direct acoustic trauma never occurs, or that the persistence of whales on naval ranges proves absence of sublethal cost.

## Research priorities

The highest-value next work is a quantitative event matrix across the Bahamas 2000, Greece 1996, Canary Islands 2002/2004, Mariana events and matched negative naval exercises, with source characteristics, tracks, propagation, species, bathymetry, carcass freshness, pathology and confidence grades.

A second priority is a dive-profile gas-kinetics matrix that runs published controlled-exposure traces through multiple independent physiological models rather than one preferred parameterization.

A third is rapid postmortem imaging and gas composition for future strandings, matched against non-sonar controls at comparable postmortem intervals.

A fourth is individual-level habituation research on repeatedly identified whales, separating within-animal response change from population selection.

A fifth is cross-site population monitoring capable of detecting biologically meaningful annual trends rather than treating low statistical power as evidence of zero effect.

## Defeat conditions

The central behavioral-injury thesis should be substantially weakened if well-characterized sonar exposures repeatedly produce the dive alterations identified as risky while validated physiological models and direct proxies show no meaningful change in gas-related risk; if modern postmortem controls explain the characteristic gas/fat embolic lesions as ordinary stranding/decomposition artifact; and if severe strandings prove no more common around relevant sonar operations after correcting for search effort, geography and whale abundance.

The habituation component should weaken if individually identified whales show no systematic relationship between prior sonar history and response after controlling for exposure and context.

The geography component should weaken if matched open-ocean and constricted/island exposures yield comparable severe outcomes or if the apparent island association disappears under better denominator data.

The book’s claims are designed to lose if those observations arrive.
