# Chronology

- **1960s onward** — Mid-frequency tactical active sonar becomes increasingly important in antisubmarine warfare; later historical reviews note a shift in patterns of some beaked-whale mass strandings after modern naval sonar enters service, though older detection and operational records are incomplete.
- **1996** — Beaked whales strand in Greece in temporal association with a NATO exercise; the event becomes an important pre-Bahamas case in later sonar-stranding reviews.
- **15–16 March 2000** — Multiple cetaceans, including beaked whales, strand in the northern Bahamas during U.S. Navy antisubmarine operations.
- **December 2001** — U.S. Navy / NMFS publish the Bahamas joint interim report, concluding tactical mid-range-frequency sonar was the most plausible sound source involved while identifying environmental interactions.
- **24 September 2002** — Neo-Tapon naval exercise begins near the Canary Islands; fourteen beaked whales strand, with strandings beginning about four hours after mid-frequency sonar activity starts.
- **2003–2005** — Canary pathology is presented and published; gas bubble-associated lesions and fat embolism become central to the decompression-like injury hypothesis.
- **2004** — Another Canary Islands beaked-whale event follows naval activity. Spain imposes a moratorium on relevant naval sonar exercises around the islands.
- **2011** — Tyack et al. publish direct measurements showing beaked whales respond to simulated and actual Navy sonar with cessation of echolocation, avoidance and altered dive behavior.
- **2013** — DeRuiter et al. publish direct Cuvier's beaked-whale responses to controlled mid-frequency sonar at relatively low received levels. Fernández et al. report no recurrence of Canary Islands mass strandings after the sonar moratorium.
- **2010s** — Navy range studies expand passive acoustic monitoring, tagging and individual photo-identification of beaked whales, especially off Southern California.
- **2020** — NOAA summarizes research suggesting some Mariana Archipelago beaked-whale strandings may be associated with sonar and notes possible effects of infrequent exposure and quiet background conditions.
- **2021** — Curtis et al. publish abundance, survival and trend estimates for Cuvier's beaked whales on the Southern California Navy range, showing persistent occupancy but limited power to detect modest long-term change.
- **14 November 2025** — New Atlantic Fleet Training and Testing marine-mammal regulations take effect for a seven-year period through 13 November 2032.
- **2026** — Southall et al. publish controlled-exposure behavioral measurements from 53 Cuvier's beaked whales, substantially expanding the direct-response data set.
- **2026** — First definitive at-sea acoustic/genetic identification of the ginkgo-toothed beaked whale links the BW43 click type to *Mesoplodon ginkgodens*, underscoring how incomplete basic beaked-whale natural history remains.
