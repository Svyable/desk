# Research — The War Below Hearing

## Working thesis

The strongest reading of the beaked-whale/sonar record is a systems-level injury model. Mid-frequency active sonar can trigger strong, context-dependent behavioral responses in extreme divers. Those responses can alter dive timing, exertion, movement and recovery in ways that may destabilize pressure-management physiology, with gas/fat embolic injury and coastal stranding as rare severe outcomes under particular configurations of animal state, geography and exposure.

This is not equivalent to saying that every sonar exposure causes decompression sickness, that all strandings are sonar-caused, or that the exact sound-to-bubble mechanism has been demonstrated in vivo.

## Four evidence classes

### 1. Verified / documented

- U.S. Navy / NMFS investigated the March 2000 Bahamas stranding and concluded tactical mid-range-frequency sonar was the most plausible sound source involved, while identifying propagation, bathymetry, constrained egress and species sensitivity as interacting factors: https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/16198/.
- Fourteen beaked whales stranded in the Canary Islands during the 24 September 2002 Neo-Tapon naval exercise; strandings began about four hours after mid-frequency sonar activity started.
- Fernández et al. documented severe congestion/hemorrhage, gas-bubble-associated lesions and fat embolism in examined Canary Islands animals: https://doi.org/10.1354/vp.42-4-446.
- Controlled studies have directly measured cessation of echolocation, avoidance, altered movement and extended/non-foraging intervals in beaked whales exposed to simulated naval sonar: https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0017009 and https://doi.org/10.1098/rsbl.2013.0223.
- The 2026 Southall et al. study reports controlled exposure data from 53 Cuvier's beaked whales: https://doi.org/10.1002/ecs2.70501.
- A persistent Cuvier's beaked-whale population has been monitored on the Southern California Navy range: https://doi.org/10.1111/mms.12747.
- Spain imposed a 2004 moratorium on relevant naval sonar exercises around the Canary Islands; Fernández et al. reported no subsequent recurrence of the prior mass-stranding pattern: https://doi.org/10.1038/497317d.
- Current NOAA/Navy regulations explicitly model marine-mammal behavioral disturbance and auditory injury from sonar and other sources: https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/action/incidental-take-authorization-military-readiness-activities-atlantic-fleet-training-and.

### 2. Credible but incomplete

- Sonar-induced changes in dive behavior can materially alter nitrogen kinetics and contribute to gas embolic injury in susceptible animals.
- Prior exposure/habituation helps explain why whales on frequently used ranges sometimes respond differently from animals in quiet or infrequently exposed regions.
- Steep island bathymetry and constrained escape routes amplify the probability that avoidance becomes stranding.
- Some historical sonar-associated strandings with incomplete modern pathology/exposure records belong to the same causal family as Bahamas 2000 and Canaries 2002.
- Repeated sublethal foraging disruption may create demographic cost even where mass strandings do not occur.

### 3. Disputed / not independently closed

- One universal mechanism explains all sonar-associated beaked-whale strandings.
- Direct acoustic activation of gas nuclei is a demonstrated field mechanism at realistic operational exposures.
- The popular “panic → rapid ascent → bends” sequence accurately describes most severe cases.
- Any specific poorly documented historical stranding can be attributed to sonar solely from temporal proximity to naval activity.
- Persistence of a local population on a Navy range proves sonar has no fitness effects.

### 4. Speculative synthesis / open conjecture

- Military sonar can act as an acoustic evolutionary trap by recruiting anti-predator behavior in an acoustic geometry unlike the threats under which that behavior evolved.
- Exposure history may create population-level acoustic cultures of tolerance or avoidance.
- Sensory-mediated hazards in other extreme-diving taxa may show analogous behavior-to-physiology pathways.
- Environmental regulation should treat sensory interpretation as part of “dose” when organism behavior controls the physical hazard pathway.

## Contradictions to preserve

- Strong behavioral responses occur at relatively low received levels, yet some whales remain on heavily used sonar ranges.
- Gas/fat embolic pathology supports a pressure-management mechanism, yet direct field measurements of bubble formation during sonar response do not exist.
- The Canary Islands intervention is strongly suggestive, but it is not randomized and the pre-intervention severe-event count is small.
- Navy funding/access enabled much of the evidence documenting Navy effects, creating both conflict-of-interest concerns and uniquely valuable instrumentation.
- Strandings are conspicuous but rare; sublethal disturbance is common enough to measure but much harder to convert into population consequence.

## Incentive / disinformation audit

No evidence reviewed for this book requires a secret or exotic sonar capability. Operational classification can limit parameter access, but the central problem is explainable with publicly acknowledged mid-frequency active sonar and known diving physiology. Do not use missing tactical details as evidence for undisclosed biological mechanisms.

Navy incentives include preserving training capability, meeting environmental law, reducing exercise disruption and avoiding protected-species mortality. Environmental organizations have incentives to foreground severe effects. Researchers may depend on Navy funding and range access. These incentives are tracked but do not replace source-level evaluation.

## Decisive experiment sketch

A multicenter, preregistered controlled-exposure program would compare identified Cuvier's beaked whales across chronically exposed and low-exposure populations. High-resolution archival tags, satellite tags, passive acoustic arrays, precise source telemetry, bathymetry and individual history would be combined. Multiple independent gas-kinetics models would be frozen before analysis. Primary outcomes would be dive deviation, foraging interruption, recovery time and model-predicted change in tissue supersaturation. No-sonar and biologically relevant acoustic controls would be included. Severe exposure escalation would be prohibited; the study tests the intermediate causal links, not lethal injury.
