# Back Matter

## A Note on Evidence

This book rests on public reporting, court and regulatory records, peer-reviewed research, and primary technical documentation rather than on anonymous sources or private access. Financial fraud figures, court outcomes, and regulatory actions are drawn from contemporaneous news reporting, government press releases, and, where available, court filings; academic claims are drawn from the original papers rather than secondary summaries where the original was accessible. The detailed source trail is kept in `research/README.md` alongside the manuscript, separated from the narrative so the chapters can read cleanly while the claims underneath them remain checkable.

A few boundaries are worth stating directly. Market-sizing figures for the identity verification industry vary meaningfully across research firms and should be read as informed estimates, not precise facts. The causal role of any single deepfake in any single election or fraud outcome is difficult to isolate cleanly from the many other factors operating on the same event, and this book has tried to say so wherever the evidence is suggestive rather than conclusive. Where a company's own safety claims or adoption figures are the only available source for a fact about that company's own technology, that dependency is noted rather than hidden.
