# Release review — 2026-09-02

## Publication judgment

*Proof of Human* is ready for a first Shelf release as narrative nonfiction with an explicit research and rights apparatus. The manuscript is complete at 29 of 29 chapters with checked front and back matter. This review does not treat chapter completion as publication approval by itself; it records the factual and editorial checks that support freezing a first public edition.

The central thesis remains supportable: generative systems have reduced the cost of producing convincing synthetic media and impersonation, while provenance, personhood, authentication, and institutional verification have become more economically important. The manuscript also preserves the strongest counterpoint: verification infrastructure can create exclusion, centralization, surveillance, false confidence, and new failure modes of its own.

## Time-sensitive claims checked

- European Commission guidance confirms that Article 50 transparency obligations under the EU AI Act apply from August 2, 2026. The Commission's Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-Generated Content was published June 10, 2026 and is a voluntary compliance aid rather than the source of the legal obligation.
- The U.S. TAKE IT DOWN Act was signed into law on May 19, 2025. References to it should therefore describe enacted federal law, not a pending proposal.
- The manuscript's treatment of watermarking and content credentials remains appropriately qualified: provenance and marking mechanisms are presented as useful but incomplete infrastructure rather than proof that synthetic content can always be detected.
- Election and influence-operation examples remain framed with causal restraint. The manuscript does not equate documented deployment of synthetic media with proof that a specific election outcome was changed.
- The companion-AI and model-collapse discussions preserve counterevidence rather than presenting one tragic case or one synthetic-data result as universal proof.

## Source discipline

The existing research brief distinguishes primary legal/regulatory records, peer-reviewed research, official technical documentation, and journalism used for incident reporting. Market-size estimates remain identified as estimates. Named fraud, election, scientific-integrity, provenance, biometric, labor, and influence-operation examples have an explicit source trail in the research packet.

Future editions should revisit fast-moving legal and technical details, especially implementation of AI transparency rules, provenance adoption, biometric/personhood regulation, and new evidence about synthetic-media persuasion or detection. Those are revision triggers, not blockers to this first edition.

## Release conditions

The first release should preserve the committed Desk manuscript, research packet, rights files, and media as one frozen snapshot. Desk remains the canonical working edition and must stay `Complete draft`; only the Shelf snapshot becomes `Published`.

No unresolved factual issue identified in this review requires withholding the first edition.
