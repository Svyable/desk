# Evidence ledger — VIKING: Mars Said Maybe

## Class 1 — verified / documented

### Viking mission and Labeled Release
- NASA Viking mission history: https://science.nasa.gov/mission/viking/
- Levin, G. V. & Straat, P. A. (1976), “Viking labeled release biology experiment: Interim results,” *Science* 194. NASA NTRS record: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19770033065
  - Flight tests at both landing sites produced similar radioactive-gas evolution after labeled nutrient addition.
  - Heat treatment altered/inactivated the active response.
  - Authors explicitly said available facts did not yet permit a conclusion about life.
- Devincenzi & Deal (1976), NASA TR R-460, development work for LR: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/archive/nasa/casi.ntrs.nasa.gov/19760014825.pdf
  - Important because flight interpretation must be compared with preflight controls, detection thresholds and known abiotic responses.

### Viking molecular analysis
- Biemann et al. (1977), “The search for organic substances and inorganic volatile compounds in the surface of Mars,” *JGR* 82: https://doi.org/10.1029/JS082i028p04641
- Biemann & Lavoie (1979), “Some final conclusions and supporting experiments related to the search for organic compounds on the surface of Mars,” *JGR* 84: https://doi.org/10.1029/JB084iB14p08385
  - Treat the historical conclusion as an instrument-era conclusion: no organic substances detected within stated limits at the sampled Viking sites.

### Phoenix perchlorate
- Hecht et al. (2009), “Detection of perchlorate and the soluble chemistry of martian soil at the Phoenix lander site,” *Science* 325: https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1172466
  - Phoenix wet chemistry found 0.4–0.6% perchlorate by mass in analyzed samples.

### Later Martian organics
- Eigenbrode et al. (2018), “Organic matter preserved in 3-billion-year-old mudstones at Gale crater, Mars,” *Science* 360: https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aas9185
  - Curiosity detected organic matter in ancient Gale crater mudstones. This is not evidence that the organics were biological.

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete

### Perchlorate as an interpretive complication for Viking GCMS
- Navarro-González et al. (2010), “Reanalysis of the Viking results suggests perchlorate and organics at midlatitudes on Mars,” *JGR: Planets* 115: https://doi.org/10.1029/2010JE003599
- 2011 correction: https://doi.org/10.1029/2011JE003854
  - Mars-analog experiments with added magnesium perchlorate showed destruction/chlorination of organics during heating.
  - Authors reinterpret Viking chlorinated compounds as compatible with perchlorate plus organics.
  - **Boundary:** Phoenix did not sample the Viking sites; the analog experiment does not directly measure 1976 Viking soil chemistry.

### Abiotic LR mechanisms
Acquire and compare primary literature for hydrogen peroxide, superoxide, oxychlorine and other reactive-soil models. No single mechanism is to be called *the explanation* until quantitative fit across first injection, controls, reinjection, kinetics, temperature treatment and both landing sites is reconstructed.

## Class 3 — disputed / unconfirmed

- “Viking detected extant Martian life.” The LR data are compatible with a biological interpretation according to Levin/Straat, but compatibility is not unique identification.
- “The GCMS result proves no life was present.” The measurement constrained detectable organics under its sampling/preparation/detection chain; later perchlorate work complicates conversion of that result into a universal absence claim.
- “NASA covered up life.” No evidence currently in this project supports intentional suppression. Institutional consensus, conservative interpretation and disagreement among experiment teams must not be rewritten as conspiracy without documentary evidence.
- “Perchlorate vindicates Levin.” It does not. It changes one important chemical premise and potentially the interpretation of chlorinated compounds; it does not identify the LR active agent.

## Class 4 — synthesis / open conjecture

- Viking is a model for **instrument disagreement under model uncertainty**: sensors can measure different observables correctly while the mapping from observable to ontology remains unstable.
- Future life-detection missions may need intentionally orthogonal assays plus predeclared procedures for resolving disagreement, rather than a simple vote among instruments.
- A sample-preparation step should be treated as part of the measurement model. Heating is not passive observation if the matrix chemically transforms the target.

## Contradictions to preserve

1. LR metabolism-like response vs. GCMS organic non-detection.
2. Historical interpretation of chlorinated compounds as contamination vs. later perchlorate/chlorination experiments.
3. Later confirmation of Martian organics vs. absence of evidence that those organics are biological.
4. Biological compatibility of LR data vs. multiple plausible abiotic pathways.
5. Mission-level “no life detected” summary vs. unresolved experiment-level interpretation.

## Incentive / distortion audit

- **Mission institutions:** incentive toward claims that survive extraordinary scrutiny; conservatism is not evidence of deception.
- **Experiment investigators:** understandable attachment to instruments and interpretations developed over years; conflict of intellectual investment must be discussed without psychologizing results away.
- **Media:** incentive to turn ambiguous assay behavior into “life/no life” binary.
- **Later advocates and skeptics:** retrospective selection of Viking details can make either side look cleaner than the contemporary record.
- **Astrobiology funding/public narrative:** dramatic life claims attract attention; so do definitive debunkings. Neither incentive identifies the chemistry.

## Defeat test

The book's strongest version fails if later chemistry merely adds trivia while leaving the original inference structure untouched. It also fails if a well-established abiotic mechanism uniquely reproduces the complete LR record under Viking conditions. In either case, narrow the thesis rather than preserve drama.
