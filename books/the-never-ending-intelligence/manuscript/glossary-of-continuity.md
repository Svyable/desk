# Glossary of Continuity

The vocabulary of persistent intelligence is still unsettled. Some terms in this book come from established computing practice; others are adaptations; several are analytical language coined here to make recurring design problems easier to see. This glossary is a map, not a standards document.

**Attention constitution** — The policy governing what is allowed to wake a persistent agent, what can be ignored or batched, how much cognition an event may consume, and when human attention may be interrupted. The point is that attention becomes an allocatable institutional resource once agents can create and inherit future wakeups.

**Authority envelope** — The bounded set of powers available to a durable agent identity or to one temporary reasoning invocation acting on its behalf. An authority envelope should be scoped by task, consequence, time, and delegation rights rather than treated as a permanent memory of what the agent was once allowed to do.

**Behavioral compatibility envelope** — The properties a successor model or harness must preserve even when its internal reasoning, prose, or implementation changes. These may include policy obedience, evidence standards, commitment semantics, retry behavior, escalation rules, and authority boundaries. It is a way to migrate cognition without pretending all model changes are behaviorally neutral.

**Causal deletion** — Forgetting that follows the consequences of a memory rather than merely deleting the original record. If a memory created a profile, trigger, policy, embedding, evaluation case, or downstream summary, deleting the source may not remove its future influence. Causal deletion asks what derived state must also change for forgetting to become real.

**Cognitive liabilities** — Future burdens created by machine intelligence: unresolved commitments, sleeping triggers, old permissions, stale goals, evaluation maintenance, memory governance, delegated descendants, and other obligations that may continue consuming attention or creating risk. The phrase is meant to counter the habit of treating every persistent capability as an asset.

**Cognition time** — The periods during which expensive reasoning is actually instantiated. A persistent agent can possess long world and workflow histories while spending very little time in active inference. Separating cognition time from elapsed time is what makes always-on responsibility economically different from always-on thought.

**Computational estate** — The goals, memories, commitments, identities, credentials, artifacts, triggers, and unfinished responsibilities that require disposition when a human principal dies, leaves an organization, loses authority, or otherwise can no longer govern a persistent agent. The term is about succession and consent, not about creating a digital replica of a deceased person.

**Computational due process** — Procedures that make consequential machine-mediated decisions contestable: provenance, notice, explanation at the level relevant to the decision, correction, appeal, human authority, and the ability to challenge an interpretation rather than merely receive an output. The phrase is a design proposal rather than a claim about current law.

**Continuity capital** — Accumulated structure that makes future cognition locally competent: evaluated procedures, repositories, tools, memory schemas, failure cases, authority maps, relationship history, triggers, and artifacts. Generic model capability becomes more useful because it wakes into an environment improved by predecessors. Like other capital, continuity capital can appreciate, depreciate, or become stranded.

**Continuity decay** — The loss of usefulness or validity in durable agent state as the world changes. Old tests can encode obsolete assumptions. Memory can become stale. Triggers can watch conditions that no longer matter. Policies can outlive their rationale. Continuity must therefore be maintained, not merely stored.

**Continuity fire drill** — A deliberate exercise in suspending, migrating, degrading, or terminating a persistent agent to discover hidden dependencies before a real failure forces the issue. The test asks whether responsibilities, credentials, triggers, descendants, commitments, and records remain governable when the ordinary cognitive machinery disappears.

**Continuity ledger** — The compact durable structure that tells a future reasoning instance what responsibility still exists: mandate, authority, relevant world-state references, consequential events, commitments, wake conditions, provenance, and termination semantics. It is a handoff between temporary minds rather than a transcript of everything previous minds thought.

**Continuity manifest** — A transfer map created when a persistent responsibility moves to a successor. It identifies which mandates, permissions, commitments, triggers, memories, evaluations, descendants, external reliance relationships, and deletion obligations must migrate, expire, or remain historical. The manifest distinguishes task migration from responsibility migration.

**Credible cognitive exit** — The practical ability to replace a model provider, agent platform, memory service, or orchestration layer without abandoning the continuity that makes the system useful. Raw data export is insufficient if goals, provenance, commitments, triggers, evaluations, and identity relationships cannot be reconstructed elsewhere.

**Durable causal identity** — The book's central operational concept. A persistent agent can be treated as continuous when unfinished work, commitments, authority, history, and consequences survive the replacement of individual model invocations. The term deliberately avoids claiming one continuous conscious subject.

**Epistemic security** — Protection of a persistent system's durable understanding from manipulation. Traditional security asks whether data or actions were compromised. Epistemic security also asks whether poisoned evidence, corrupted provenance, repeated misinformation, or inherited false conclusions can alter what future invocations believe.

**Evaluation debt** — The gap between a system's evolving behavior and the evidence available to tell whether the evolution helped. Rapid changes to models, prompts, retrieval, tools, memory, and workflow can outrun the evaluation suite, leaving an organization with a more complicated agent but less knowledge about its reliability.

**Event-sourced mind** — An analogy for agents whose consequential history is reconstructed from durable events rather than one continuous internal memory stream. The useful extension of event sourcing is epistemic: preserve what happened separately from what a previous agent inferred about what happened.

**Goal half-life** — The time over which a goal should remain active without reaffirmation. Some goals expire naturally within hours or days. Others deserve periodic review. Constitutional constraints may intentionally persist much longer. Assigning temporal semantics prevents old intentions from becoming immortal merely because the system can preserve them.

**Governance diff** — A compact account of how a persistent agent's effective institution changed: goals, permissions, policies, model routes, memory influence, evaluations, commitments, and escalation rules. Humans supervising long-lived systems need to understand material deltas rather than reread every event the system has experienced.

**Institutional memory asymmetry** — A power difference created when one side of a relationship can reconstruct years or decades of evidence while the other side cannot. Persistent corporate, government, or platform agents may widen this asymmetry; portable personal agents could reduce it by giving individuals durable memory institutions of their own.

**Legible ending** — Termination in which an observer can show that future wakeups are disabled, credentials are revoked, descendants are accounted for, commitments are transferred or closed, relevant evidence is preserved lawfully, and the old identity can no longer exercise hidden authority. Killing the current model process is not a legible ending.

**Mandate** — A governed version of a goal. A mandate identifies the principal, purpose, success evidence, constraints, authority, review conditions, and termination rules. It is designed to survive time without allowing a short prompt to become an indefinite license.

**Memory debt** — The future cost created by retained information: privacy exposure, stale beliefs, retrieval noise, contradictory records, migration burden, persuasive historical profiles, and the difficulty of determining what can safely be deleted. More memory can reduce rediscovery while simultaneously increasing memory debt.

**Mindflow** — The movement of verified cognitive work through a machine organization. When temporary agents can be instantiated and dissolved cheaply, headcount becomes a poor measure of synthetic capacity. The more relevant questions become how work is decomposed, routed, checked, merged, and preserved.

**Outcome unknown** — A first-class workflow state used when an agent cannot determine whether an external action completed before an interruption. Instead of assuming failure and retrying, the successor reconciles with the external world. The concept comes from ordinary distributed-systems caution but becomes especially important when temporary reasoning instances perform consequential actions.

**Persistence** — Continuity of responsibility across time. It does not require uninterrupted inference, one model process, one provider, perfect memory, or a gigantic context window. A system may be dormant for months and still be persistent if the relevant obligation remains durable and can wake the correct future cognition.

**Presence** — The appearance of continuous intelligence to a user. A chat interface may feel continuously present while having little durable responsibility. Conversely, a silent agent may be absent from experience for a year while remaining genuinely persistent. Presence and persistence are different properties.

**Right to fresh inference** — The principle that a person, organization, hypothesis, or decision should sometimes be evaluated without allowing old machine interpretations to dominate the context automatically. It is the computational equivalent of fresh eyes and a defense against biography becoming destiny.

**Role capital** — Expertise embedded in a reusable machine role rather than one persistent worker: instructions, tools, memory scope, evidence standards, evaluation cases, authority boundaries, and learned procedures. Temporary model invocations can inherit role capital and then disappear while the role continues improving.

**Standing responsibility** — An obligation that remains active even while no model is thinking. This is the foundation of durable agents. The responsibility may be represented by a mandate, trigger, commitment, or monitored condition and can summon cognition only when the world makes further thought useful.

**Temporal blast radius** — The amount of future behavior a compromise or mistake can influence. An error in a disposable invocation may die quickly. The same error promoted into durable memory, policy, identity, or scheduled intent can shape months or years of future action. Persistent-agent security therefore has a time dimension.

**Temporal privilege** — Authority considered across time rather than only at the instant it was granted. A permission that was legitimate last year may be illegitimate now because the principal changed, the goal expired, the organization reorganized, or the environment became more consequential. Long-lived agents need privilege expiration and revalidation.

**Temporary mind** — A bounded reasoning invocation that reconstructs enough context to perform work on behalf of a durable identity and then disappears. Temporary minds make cognition replaceable and allow the system to allocate different models, contexts, permissions, and budgets to different episodes.

**Typed memory** — Durable information whose status is explicit: fact, observation, hypothesis, preference, procedure, policy, commitment, archive, constitutional constraint, or another defined class. Typing memory prevents a retrieved speculation from quietly acquiring the authority of a verified fact.

**Wake contract** — A durable specification of what future event or time condition deserves renewed cognition. A mature wake contract identifies the source, predicate, budget, owner, expiration, consequence, and recovery behavior if the expected wakeup is missed.

**World time** — Time in the external environment, which continues while the agent sleeps. Prices change, people reply, software deploys, evidence arrives, organizations reorganize, and commitments approach deadlines. Persistent systems must reconcile themselves with world time rather than assuming the state they remember is still current.

**Workflow time** — The time of unfinished responsibility: deadlines, waits, retries, review periods, expirations, and conditional next steps. Workflow time can continue while cognition time is zero. Much of persistent-agent architecture exists to preserve workflow time reliably across temporary thinkers.

The vocabulary can change. The architecture should remain testable.

If a future system makes several of these concepts obsolete, that will be evidence that the field found a better way to represent continuity.
