# Research — The Flash That Couldn’t Be Classified

This research package separates physical observations from institutional judgments and actor attribution. It is intentionally asymmetric: gamma-ray bursts function as a control case in which anomalous Vela triggers acquired independent reality through repetition and cross-platform observation; the September 22, 1979 Vela event remains a singular historical case whose public evidence did not force convergence.

The working rule is that testimony is evidence that a claim was made; an intelligence assessment is evidence of an agency judgment; a declassified memorandum is evidence of what the memorandum records; and none becomes direct proof of an underlying event merely through official provenance.

## Evidence classes

1. **Verified / documented / directly measured** — treaty dates; Vela launches and mission; published Vela gamma-ray burst detections; the September 22 optical trigger; existence and conclusions of U.S. panels and assessments; South Africa’s documented nuclear program and 1977 Kalahari preparations; later IAEA verification; current NNSA/CTBTO monitoring architecture.
2. **Credible but incomplete** — nuclear interpretation of the 1979 signal; later hydroacoustic and radionuclide reconstructions; detailed physical interpretations of particular ancillary signals; intelligence judgments based partly on classified context.
3. **Disputed allegation** — a specific Israeli, South African, or joint test; particular device type or purpose; a deliberate Carter administration cover-up; unverified claims of decisive hidden evidence.
4. **Speculative synthesis / open conjecture** — secrecy as an evidentiary ecology; deliberate ambiguity as a possible verification-evasion strategy; analogy to modern UAP and other anomaly programs.

## Files

## Remaining research priority

The highest-value future work is source-level rather than thematic: recover the fullest available Ruina-panel working record; identify raw or minimally processed Event 747 optical data and calibration history; reconstruct all operating corroborative sensors with geometry and detection probability; audit the Arecibo ionospheric claim from original observations; reproduce Wright/De Geer hydroacoustic and radionuclide calculations; and build an operational actor matrix from primary South African, Israeli, U.S., British, French, Soviet/Russian, and IAEA records.
