## Thesis

The Vela satellites were built to distinguish prohibited nuclear explosions from everything else the physical world could make a detector see. That adversarial sensing problem accidentally discovered gamma-ray bursts, which became real astronomy through repetition, independent platforms, triangulation, population statistics, counterparts, and redshifts. The same detector lineage later produced the disputed September 22, 1979 South Atlantic double flash, where a strong nuclear-looking optical signature, incomplete corroboration, secrecy, rare-event statistics, and political consequence prevented comparable closure. The collision reveals how anomalies earn—or fail to earn—classification.
