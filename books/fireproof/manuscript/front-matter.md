# Fireproof

## Asbestos, the Navy, and the Hazard Built Into the Fleet

Sven Hardy Benson

This is a book about an institutional knowledge problem disguised as an industrial material problem. Asbestos did not enter American warships because nobody understood that dust could hurt lungs. It entered because it solved several naval problems extremely well: heat, fire, steam, insulation, weight, cost, availability. The harder historical question is not whether somebody somewhere knew asbestos was dangerous. Somebody did. The question is how knowledge traveled—or failed to travel—from medical literature and industrial hygiene into specifications, procurement, ship design, maintenance practice, warnings, respiratory protection, medical surveillance, and compensation.

That distinction matters. “The Navy knew” is too crude to be useful unless we can put an office, date, document, distribution list, specification, physician, yard, command, or decision behind the verb. This manuscript therefore treats knowledge as something with an address.

The public documentary record is unusually rich. NAVSEA maintains an asbestos FOIA reading room containing military specifications and qualified-products records. GAO’s 1979 review examined Navy asbestos practices at Long Beach and Norfolk and described the Navy’s removal policy. Congressional records document the extraordinary range of asbestos uses aboard ships. Navy Medicine now operates what it describes as the world’s largest asbestos medical-surveillance program. Those facts do not by themselves prove a single continuous institutional cover-up. They do establish a long paper trail through which a more precise story can be told.

The evidence ledger uses four classes throughout: documented public records; credible but incomplete testimony or reporting; disputed allegations; and explicit synthesis. The book will not convert a later lawsuit allegation into an earlier Navy fact, nor a product specification into proof that every ship carried that product, nor a medical paper into proof that a particular procurement officer read it.
