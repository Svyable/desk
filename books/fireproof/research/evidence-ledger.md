# Evidence ledger and source trail

## Class 1 — verified / documented

- U.S. GAO, **HRD-80-2, Navy Efforts To Protect Workers From Asbestos Exposure**, published October 18, 1979. GAO reviewed Long Beach and Norfolk Naval Shipyards, Navy shipboard asbestos use, removal costs and compensation. GAO records a 1975 Navy policy of replacing asbestos insulation when damaged or when removal was necessary for repairs, modified in 1979 to selectively replace insulation in high-maintenance areas. https://www.gao.gov/products/hrd-80-2
- NAVSEA FOIA Reading Room, **Asbestos**. Public index of historical military specifications and qualified-products records, including asbestos-fiber thermal insulation and metallic-asbestos gaskets. https://www.navsea.navy.mil/Contact/FOIA/Reading-Room/Asbestos/
- U.S. Senate hearing record describing extensive Navy use of asbestos from the late 1930s through the late 1970s in engines, pipe covering, boilers, valves, gaskets, living spaces and other applications. https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CHRG-110shrg33911/pdf/CHRG-110shrg33911.pdf
- Navy Medicine, **Asbestos Medical Surveillance Program**. Navy describes AMSP as the largest program of its kind and enrolls Navy asbestos workers with occupational exposure histories. https://www.med.navy.mil/Navy-and-Marine-Corps-Force-Health-Protection-Command/Environmental-Health/Occupational-and-Environmental-Medicine/Occupational-and-Environmental-Medicine-Division/Surveillance-and-Certification-Exam/Process-and-Protocol/
- Navy Medicine institutional history records expansion of occupational-health responsibilities including asbestos hazard control in the 1970s. https://www.med.navy.mil/Navy-and-Marine-Corps-Force-Health-Protection-Command/About-Us/Our-History/

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete

- Worker testimony, retrospective occupational histories and litigation discovery can identify practices and local knowledge but require corroboration against contemporaneous yard and command records.
- Historical medical literature can establish what was publicly knowable at a date; it cannot establish which Navy decision-makers possessed or acted on that knowledge without routing/distribution evidence.

## Class 3 — disputed / allegation

- Claims that “the Navy knew” at a single early date and deliberately concealed a settled mesothelioma risk from all sailors and yard workers. This formulation collapses different diseases, latency periods, offices, eras and standards. It must be decomposed document by document.
- Claims derived solely from litigation summaries about particular memoranda or warnings until original documents and provenance are obtained.

## Class 4 — synthesis / open conjecture

- The central synthesis is that asbestos history is best reconstructed as a **knowledge-routing failure under engineering pressure**: hazard information, material specifications, industrial-hygiene practice, operational necessity and compensation evolved on different clocks.
- A second synthesis is that latency creates institutional moral hazard: the person approving a material can be retired before the disease it contributes to becomes diagnosable.

## Contradictions to preserve

The Navy’s later surveillance and hazard-control programs are evidence of institutional response, not automatic evidence of earlier concealment. Extensive historical use proves exposure opportunity, not dose for any individual. A military specification proves authorization/qualification, not installation on every hull. Conversely, absence of a modern exposure limit in an earlier era does not mean absence of medical warning.

## Research targets

Page-index HRD-80-2; obtain and index NAVSEA specification files; trace Bureau of Medicine industrial-hygiene bulletins; reconstruct Long Beach and Norfolk practices; distinguish asbestosis, lung cancer and mesothelioma knowledge chronologies; build a year-by-year warning/control/specification ledger; locate Navy procurement and ship-design correspondence; audit congressional testimony against primary records; build compensation and litigation chronology.
