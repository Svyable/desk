# Research trail — The Body in the Beam

This research trail separates four evidence classes throughout the manuscript:

1. **Documented / declassified / directly established** — primary government records, acknowledged programs and systems, reproducible physical effects, peer-reviewed studies, and official assessments as to what those assessments actually say.
2. **Credible but incomplete** — mechanism rankings from incomplete case data, intelligence judgments whose underlying collection is not public, technically relevant reporting or testimony lacking event-level physical corroboration.
3. **Disputed allegation** — claims of specific covert anti-personnel electromagnetic deployments, foreign attack programs, or causal attribution not independently established in the public record.
4. **Speculative synthesis** — the author’s proposals about institutional failure, hidden-program footprints, prospective metrology, and how the evidence classes may fit together.

The manuscript does not infer efficacy from classification, patents, program names, foreign interest, or funding. A declassified document establishes what the document records; it does not automatically validate scientific claims contained inside it.

## Primary and authoritative source ledger

### Moscow Signal / PANDORA

**National Security Archive — “The Moscow Signals Declassified: Microwave Mysteries, Projects PANDORA and BIZARRE” (2022).** Curated posting built around declassified U.S. records, including ARPA’s 1965 PANDORA justification, State Department and embassy communications, and later technical/diplomatic material. Used for the documented existence of the Moscow Signal investigation and PANDORA, not as proof that the signal caused illness.

https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/briefing-book/intelligence-russia-programs/2022-09-13/moscow-signals-declassified-microwave

**Jerome Wiesner report to Clark Clifford, “Bugging of the U.S. Embassy, Moscow,” June 24, 1964.** Declassified White House-era security review reproduced by the National Security Archive. Used for the fact that senior U.S. scientific/security officials were concerned about the unexplained microwave signal and wanted it better understood or stopped.

https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/document/28785-document-1-white-house-jerome-wiesner-report-clark-clifford-bugging-us-embassy

**Department of State memorandum of conversation, “Signals Against Embassy Moscow Chancery,” June 25, 1967.** Used for Secretary of State Dean Rusk’s documented raising of the electromagnetic signal with Soviet Foreign Minister Andrei Gromyko. This establishes diplomatic concern, not biological causation.

https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/document/28790-document-6-department-state-memorandum-conversation-signals-against-embassy-moscow

**State Department telegram, “Moscow Signal Press Guidance,” May 12, 1972.** Used for the government’s own contemporary description of PANDORA as an effort to simulate the beam and examine possible health effects.

https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/document/28813-document-5-state-department-telegram-088304-us-embassy-moscow-moscow-signal-press

**Embassy Moscow telegram, “Microwave RFI Detected at Amembassy Moscow,” June 26, 1975.** Used for continued monitoring, reference to earlier PANDORA work, comparison with exposure standards, and the recorded statement that earlier health-hazard investigations had been negative under the conditions considered.

https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/media/28814/ocr

### Microwave auditory effect and RF biophysics

**A. H. Frey, “Auditory system response to radio frequency energy,” Aerospace Medicine (1961).** Early peer-reviewed report documenting auditory perception associated with radio-frequency exposure.

https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/13895080/

**K. R. Foster and E. D. Finch, “Microwave hearing: evidence for thermoacoustic auditory stimulation by pulsed microwaves,” Science (1974).** Experimental evidence supporting thermally generated acoustic transients as the mechanism for microwave hearing.

https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/4833827/

**J. C. Lin, “Hearing of microwave pulses by humans and animals: effects, mechanism, and thresholds.”** Review describing the thermoelastic/bone-conduction account and relevant thresholds. Used to keep microwave hearing within its measured mechanism rather than as evidence for generalized neurological control.

https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17495664/

**K. R. Foster, D. C. Garrett, M. C. Ziskin, “Can the Microwave Auditory Effect Be ‘Weaponized’?” Frontiers in Public Health (2021).** Critical technical discussion of the engineering and exposure requirements around weaponization claims. Used as counterevidence against freely extrapolating from the Frey effect to arbitrary covert capabilities.

https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35004589/

### Exposure guidance / established health-effects framework

**ICNIRP, Guidelines for limiting exposure to electromagnetic fields (100 kHz to 300 GHz), Health Physics (2020).** Used for current international RF exposure guidance, dosimetric distinctions, and the evidence basis for established adverse-effect limits.

https://www.icnirp.org/en/publications/article/rf-guidelines-2020.html

**World Health Organization — Radiofrequency fields / electromagnetic-fields health information.** Used for the distinction between biological effects and health hazards and for the public-health position that broad adverse effects from ordinary low-level environmental RF exposure have not been established.

https://www.who.int/teams/environment-climate-change-and-health/radiation-and-health/non-ionizing/emf/radiofrequency-fields

https://www.who.int/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/radiation-electromagnetic-fields

### Acknowledged directed-energy systems

**U.S. Army, “ARDEC engineers develop Solid State Active Denial Technology for non-lethal crowd control” (2016).** Public Army description of solid-state Active Denial Technology using approximately 95 GHz RF energy to create a brief intolerable heating sensation at shallow skin depth. Used as an acknowledged anti-personnel directed-energy capability with a specified mechanism.

https://www.army.mil/article-amp/176579/ardec_engineers_develop_solid_state_active_denial_technology_for_non_lethal_crowd_control

**U.S. Army / JPEO Armaments & Ammunition Portfolio Book (2025), Solid State Active Denial Technology.** Current public program description of a 90–95 GHz prototype and intended nonlethal repel role. Used to show that acknowledged human-effects directed-energy engineering continues to exist without implying covert neurological effects.

https://jpeoaa.army.mil/Portals/94/Documents/JPEOAAPortfolioBook_2025.pdf

### Anomalous health incidents

**National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine, *An Assessment of Illness in U.S. Government Employees and Their Families at Overseas Embassies* (2020).** Used for the committee’s mechanism assessment. The manuscript preserves the report’s uncertainty: directed pulsed RF energy was judged the most plausible among considered mechanisms for distinctive early cases; the report did not identify a weapon, adversary, or captured waveform.

https://nap.nationalacademies.org/catalog/25889/an-assessment-of-illness-in-us-government-employees-and-their-families-at-overseas-embassies

**Office of the Director of National Intelligence, “Complementary Efforts on Anomalous Health Incidents” / IC Experts Panel (2022).** Used for the panel’s findings that a subset of reported cases could be due to external stimuli and that pulsed electromagnetic energy, particularly RF, was a plausible mechanism, while information gaps remained. The recommendations for detectors, biomarkers, clinical measurements, and further biological-effects research are central to Chapters 11 and 17.

https://archive.dni.gov/index.php/newsroom/reports-publications/reports-publications-2022/3591-complementary-efforts-on-anomalous-health-incidents

**ODNI, *Updated Assessment of Anomalous Health Incidents* (March 1, 2023).** Used for the coordinated intelligence judgment that most IC agencies assessed foreign-adversary responsibility for the reported AHI population as very unlikely and for the assessment’s discussion of alternative medical/environmental explanations and collection results.

https://www.dni.gov/files/ODNI/documents/assessments/NIC-Unclassified-ICA-Updated-Assessment-AHI-March2023.pdf

**ODNI, *Updated Assessment of Anomalous Health Incidents, as of December 2024* (released January 10, 2025).** Current core intelligence source for the book. Most IC components continued to assess foreign responsibility as very unlikely. New reporting caused two components to change aspects of their judgments about foreign capability and a possible role in a small number of events; the changed views carried low confidence and did not establish deployment of a weapon in reported incidents.

https://www.dni.gov/files/ODNI/documents/assessments/NIC-Unclassified-ICA-Updated-Assessment-AHI-December2024.pdf

**NIH, “NIH studies find severe symptoms of ‘Havana Syndrome,’ but no evidence of MRI-detectable brain injury or biological abnormalities” (March 18, 2024).** Authoritative summary of the two NIH/JAMA studies and their limitations.

https://www.nih.gov/news-events/news-releases/nih-studies-find-severe-symptoms-havana-syndrome-no-evidence-mri-detectable-brain-injury-or-biological-abnormalities

**Pierpaoli et al., “Neuroimaging Findings in US Government Personnel and Their Family Members Involved in Anomalous Health Incidents,” JAMA 331(13), 2024.** Used for the finding that the exploratory imaging study did not identify significant group differences in brain structure or function after multiple-comparison adjustment.

https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2816532

**Chan et al., “Clinical, Biomarker, and Research Tests Among US Government Personnel and Their Family Members Involved in Anomalous Health Incidents,” JAMA 331(13), 2024.** Used for the broader clinical/biomarker findings, including the absence of significant group differences in most measures and the specific areas where differences were observed.

https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2816533

**U.S. Government Accountability Office, *Havana Syndrome: Better Patient Communication and Monitoring of Key DOD Tasks Needed to Better Ensure Timely Treatment* (GAO-24-106593, 2024).** Used for institutional/clinical-care context, not mechanism attribution.

https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-24-106593

## Contradictions the manuscript preserves

- The Moscow microwave exposure is documented, while its intended purpose and any resulting health effect remain historically disputed.
- PANDORA existed because biological effects were considered worth testing; a later embassy cable described earlier PANDORA health-hazard investigations as negative under the conditions considered.
- Microwave auditory perception is established, while technical literature disputes easy extrapolation from that effect to a covert injurious weapon.
- Active Denial proves intentional remote RF pain is engineerable in one frequency/effect regime; it does not validate claims about deep neurological effects.
- The 2020 National Academies and 2022 IC experts work kept pulsed RF mechanisms in serious consideration for a subset; the 2023 and 2025 coordinated intelligence assessments weigh strongly against a broad foreign campaign.
- The 2025 update is not identical to 2023: two IC components shifted some capability/limited-event judgments because of new foreign technical reporting, but most components retained the overall very-unlikely assessment.
- NIH found severe and disabling symptoms in participants while failing to identify a consistent MRI-detectable injury or most other group-level biological abnormalities.

## Falsification / revision notes

The book’s central synthesis would need substantial revision if any of the following occurred:

- a publicly verifiable device, waveform, or technical record tied through strong chain of custody to one or more AHI events;
- replicated prospective detection of a distinctive energetic transient tightly time-linked to a consistent acute clinical pattern;
- declassified high-confidence intelligence establishing an operational foreign anti-personnel program whose technical parameters match reported incidents;
- strong prospective clinical evidence establishing a unique injury signature inconsistent with the manuscript’s current caution about specificity;
- strong experimental evidence showing that the key proposed RF mechanisms cannot produce relevant effects at remotely plausible exposures;
- high-quality prospective monitoring of strong incidents repeatedly showing no energetic signal within well-characterized detector coverage, which would weigh against external-energy hypotheses for those cases.

The manuscript should also change if future declassification materially alters the January 2025 intelligence picture. “No public evidence” is a time-bounded statement, not an ontological claim.

## Claims deliberately not made

This draft does not claim:

- that the Soviet Union intentionally used the Moscow Signal as a health weapon;
- that PANDORA proved low-level microwave injury or behavioral control;
- that ordinary Wi-Fi, mobile phones, base stations, or compliant civilian RF environments cause the AHI symptom complex;
- that the microwave auditory effect permits arbitrary voice-to-skull transmission at tactically useful range;
- that Active Denial Technology causes deep neurological injury;
- that Russia, China, Cuba, Israel, or another state has been publicly proven to have caused reported AHIs;
- that a patent, paper, funding line, security classification, or intelligence interest proves operational efficacy;
- that negative MRI or biomarker findings mean affected personnel fabricated or imagined symptoms;
- that classified science is inherently more advanced or more reliable than open science.
