# Front Matter

# The Body in the Beam

*The Secret History of Microwaves, Invisible Weapons, and the Biology Between Physics and Intelligence*

Sven Hardy Benson

There is a category of knowledge that becomes unreliable in two opposite directions at once.

Put it near a classification stamp and believers make it stronger than the evidence. Put it near a conspiracy theory and skeptics make it weaker.

Radio-frequency bioeffects have lived in that category for most of the modern national-security state.

The fixed points are strange enough. Beginning in the Cold War, the Soviet Union directed microwave energy at the U.S. Embassy in Moscow for years. American officials measured the signal, investigated its purpose, protested it, and funded biological research under PANDORA and related efforts. In 1961, Allan Frey reported that pulsed radio-frequency energy could produce auditory perception. Decades later, the U.S. military openly developed a 95-gigahertz directed-energy system designed to create an intense heating sensation in human skin. High-power microwave systems became an acknowledged military technology against electronics.

Then, in 2016, U.S. personnel in Havana began reporting abrupt, disturbing health events that eventually became known as Havana Syndrome and then anomalous health incidents. A National Academies committee found directed pulsed radio-frequency energy the most plausible mechanism among those it considered for distinctive early cases, while stressing limitations. An Intelligence Community experts panel later described pulsed electromagnetic energy as plausible for a subset. In 2023, most intelligence agencies judged foreign responsibility for the incident set very unlikely. In January 2025, most retained that judgment, while two components shifted aspects of their view after new reporting about foreign scientific and weapons progress. NIH studies in 2024 found severe reported symptoms but no consistent MRI-detectable brain injury or most other measured biological abnormalities compared with controls.

All of those statements can be true at once.

This book lives in the space their coexistence creates.

It does not assume a Russian microwave weapon caused Havana. The public evidence does not establish that. It does not assume every government denial is trustworthy. Intelligence agencies are not laboratories and classification is not peer review. It does not turn ordinary Wi-Fi, cellular networks, or civilian RF exposure into covert weapons by association. Exposure parameters matter. It does not treat the testimony of affected personnel as proof of mechanism, or negative imaging as proof that nobody experienced a disabling event.

The method is simple enough to be annoying.

Separate the claim.

A documented exposure is one thing. A laboratory bioeffect is another. A military capability is another. An adversary program is another. Deployment is another. Attribution to a particular illness is another. Secrecy can connect these layers in reality. It cannot connect them for us on paper.

The research notes use four evidence classes throughout: documented or declassified facts; credible but incomplete reporting or testimony; disputed allegations without independent confirmation; and speculative synthesis. A claim can move classes as evidence improves. Nothing is protected because it is exciting.

The title refers to a human problem more than a weapons class. We evolved inside fields we cannot directly see. Our nervous systems reveal only a narrow sample of the physical world. Civilization’s answer has been to build external senses: antennas, spectrometers, dosimeters, clocks, imaging systems, archives.

When those external senses are absent, the body becomes the detector.

The body is a poor forensic instrument.

This is the history of what happened when governments discovered that fact too late, and what would be required not to repeat it.
