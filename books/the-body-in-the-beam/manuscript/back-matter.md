# Back Matter

The evidentiary rule of this book is deliberately stricter than the subject’s folklore.

A government document proves that the government created or possessed the document. A declassified threat assessment proves the assessment was made. A program record proves the program existed. A laboratory paper proves that the reported experiment was conducted and reported; replication determines how much more it earns. A patent proves a claim was filed, not that a device worked. Testimony proves that a person made the statement and, depending on access and corroboration, may support what they directly observed. Classification proves sensitivity, not truth.

For controversial claims, the working categories are:

1. **Documented / declassified / directly established.** Examples in this book include the Moscow Signal, Project PANDORA’s existence, the microwave auditory literature, Active Denial Technology, official AHI assessments, and the 2024 NIH studies.
2. **Credible but incomplete evidence.** This includes mechanism assessments based on limited case data, intelligence judgments whose underlying collection remains classified, and technically informed testimony that lacks public physical corroboration.
3. **Disputed allegation.** Claims of a specific covert foreign anti-personnel directed-energy program causing reported AHIs belong here unless and until stronger public evidence emerges.
4. **Speculative synthesis.** The book’s proposals about how secrecy distorts error correction, what footprints a hidden program should leave, and how a prospective detector network should be organized belong here when they go beyond cited institutional findings.

This classification is not permanent. Evidence is allowed to move claims.

The January 2025 Intelligence Community assessment is a useful example. Most components continued to assess that foreign responsibility for reported anomalous health incidents was very unlikely. Two components shifted aspects of their judgments because of new reporting about foreign scientific and weapons development; their changed judgments carried low confidence and did not establish deployment in the incidents. If the underlying reporting is later declassified, the relevant claims should be revisited at the level that evidence supports.

Likewise, the 2024 NIH studies found no consistent MRI-detectable brain injury or most other measured biological differences in the studied cohort compared with controls. Future high-quality work could alter the medical picture. Until then, the negative result belongs in the center of the evidence, not in a footnote.

The source trail in `research/README.md` favors primary and official material: declassified diplomatic records, CIA/DoD material, ODNI assessments, NIH research summaries and papers, National Academies review, WHO/ICNIRP exposure guidance, and public military documentation of directed-energy systems. The research file also records where a source establishes interest rather than efficacy.

No non-public classified material was used in this draft. The book makes no claim of access to classified programs, Q-cleared reverse-engineering efforts, intelligence sources, or unreleased medical records.

The practical proposition at the end of the manuscript is intentionally modest: where external energetic stimuli remain plausible enough to affect personnel protection and intelligence collection, prospective instrumentation is cheaper than permanent retrospective ambiguity.

Build detectors. Preserve raw data. Separate clinical care from geopolitical attribution. Record nulls. Keep the clocks synchronized.

Then let the next event be less mysterious than the last.
