# Back Matter

## Chronology

**1994** — Fishman et al. report terrestrial gamma-ray flashes in BATSE data from the Compton Gamma Ray Observatory.

**2000s** — RHESSI and ground/air observations establish TGFs and gamma-ray glows as a recurring high-energy atmospheric phenomenon; runaway-electron models mature.

**2008** — Fermi launches, providing a large TGF and particle-beam data set.

**2009–2011** — Fermi detects electron/positron beams associated with TGFs; AGILE reports TGF photons extending to about 100 MeV.

**2012** — Dwyer publishes a detailed relativistic-feedback discharge framework.

**2017** — Enoto et al. report neutron/de-excitation and delayed 511-keV signals demonstrating lightning-triggered photonuclear reactions.

**2018** — ASIM launches to the International Space Station with coordinated high-energy and optical lightning instrumentation.

**2019–2022** — ASIM timing studies increasingly constrain the temporal ordering of TGFs and optical/current pulses.

**2023** — ALOFT aircraft campaign samples tropical thunderstorms at close range and finds widespread dynamic gamma emission plus weaker/intermediate event classes.

**2024** — ALOFT results in *Nature* describe large, long-lived but highly dynamic gamma-glowing storm regions; ASIM work strengthens cases in which gamma emission precedes a major optical/current pulse.

**2025** — Spectral analysis of flickering gamma-ray flashes and new feedback simulations sharpen the continuum hypothesis; Japanese ground arrays track rapidly re-forming gamma-ray glows.

**2026** — A ground-based New Mexico TGF associated with an intense negative return stroke is reconstructed with 3-D lightning mapping and high-energy detectors.

## Defeat conditions

The strongest synthesis in this book—that relativistic high-energy discharge is sometimes a dynamically important component of thunderstorm electrical regulation—should be weakened if coordinated measurements show that energetic-particle ionization and charge transport are consistently negligible compared with conventional discharge currents, or if gamma activity is systematically downstream of leader/current processes across well-resolved event classes.

The 'continuum' picture should be weakened if glow, flicker and TGF classes occupy separable source mechanisms after correcting for detector threshold and atmospheric propagation.

Aviation significance should be weakened if calibrated flight measurements show thunderstorm-related dose outside intentionally penetrated convective regions to be negligible relative to normal cosmic-ray exposure.

Global isotope significance should be weakened if photonuclear production integrated over realistic event distributions is orders of magnitude below other atmospheric production channels, while preserving local-event physics.

## Research priorities

1. Joint radiation/electric-field/lightning/radar campaigns with microsecond timing.
2. A cross-platform source luminosity function corrected for trigger efficiency and attenuation.
3. Quantitative charge/current accounting for high-energy discharge.
4. Neutron and delayed-annihilation measurements after bright downward events.
5. Routine calibrated aircraft radiation monitoring linked to storm geometry.
6. Multi-atmosphere simulations for planetary lightning and photonuclear chemistry.
7. Public event-level data sufficient for independent causal reconstruction.
