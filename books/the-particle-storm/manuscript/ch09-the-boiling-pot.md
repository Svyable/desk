# 9. The Boiling Pot

A storm that glows for hours does not mean every cubic meter is a gamma-ray source for hours. This distinction is where the metaphor earns or loses scientific value.

ALOFT found spatially extensive storm systems in which gamma emission appeared intermittently and dynamically as the aircraft sampled different regions. The signal rose and fell on seconds, fractions of seconds, and longer scales. The source region behaved as a collection of active cells embedded inside a larger electrified structure.

That resembles boiling not because gamma rays are thermal bubbles, but because local excursions occur inside a maintained global condition.

The maintained condition is charge separation. Collisions among graupel, ice crystals and supercooled water in convective motion separate charge. Updrafts and particle fall speeds distribute it. The storm builds electric fields. Those fields can accelerate seed electrons. The accelerated population ionizes air and emits radiation. Conductivity changes. Charge moves. Lightning reorganizes the field. Convection rebuilds it.

This is a dynamical system with feedback at multiple scales, and high-energy particles may be one of the feedback channels.

The strongest version of that claim would say relativistic feedback regulates thunderstorm electric fields. That is plausible enough to test and too strong to assume. Field measurements, gamma-ray intensity and discharge mapping need to be inverted jointly. If enhanced gamma emission systematically coincides with field saturation and precedes partial field relaxation, the case strengthens. If gamma intensity merely tracks field without altering subsequent evolution, it remains mostly a tracer.

There is an experimental opportunity here because radiation is easier to remote-sense in some conditions than electric field throughout an entire cloud volume. Gamma rays could become a proxy for otherwise inaccessible high-field regions. But proxies are dangerous when the transfer function is nonlinear. Exponential avalanche multiplication means brightness may respond dramatically to small field changes. Attenuation and source altitude can mimic intrinsic variation. A brightening may mean stronger field, longer acceleration path, a closer source, or reduced overburden.

This is why source reconstruction must combine spectral hardness with geometry. High-energy photons survive atmosphere better than low-energy ones. A spectrum arriving at aircraft altitude contains information about column depth. Lightning mapping constrains structure. Radar constrains cloud geometry. Electric-field measurements constrain the accelerator.

The science becomes compelling precisely when no instrument is allowed to win alone.

The boiling-pot picture also repairs a conceptual asymmetry. Meteorology has long treated lightning as one discharge channel inside a storm system. High-energy atmospheric physics sometimes treated gamma-ray events as special objects attached to lightning. The newer view makes energetic radiation another state variable of the storm itself.

That may be the book's most important reclassification.
