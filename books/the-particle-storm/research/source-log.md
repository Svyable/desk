## Radiocarbon denominator and lightning contribution

22. Kovaltsov, Mishev & Usoskin (2012), **A new model of cosmogenic production of radiocarbon 14C in the atmosphere**, *Earth and Planetary Science Letters* 337–338: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.epsl.2012.05.036 — Monte Carlo cosmogenic benchmark. Reports global atmospheric ^14C production of ~1.64 atoms cm⁻² s⁻¹ for the modern epoch and ~1.88 atoms cm⁻² s⁻¹ pre-industrially. Use as the global denominator; epoch/solar modulation matter.
23. Babich & Roussel-Dupré (2007), **Origin of neutron flux increases observed in correlation with lightning**, *JGR Atmospheres*: https://doi.org/10.1029/2006JD008340 — early thunderstorm-neutron/radiocarbon budget. Under then-assumed lightning frequency, qualifying fraction and neutron yield, estimated average thunderstorm ^14C production roughly two orders of magnitude below cosmic production, while noting possible stronger local contributions. Model assumptions were highly uncertain; do not treat the percentage as a modern measurement.
24. Babich (2017), **Radiocarbon Production by Thunderstorms**, *Geophysical Research Letters*: https://doi.org/10.1002/2017GL075131 — revisits the problem with a larger high-energy-thunderstorm literature. Concludes available neutron/gamma observations were insufficient for a final global thunderstorm contribution, but in severe thunderstorm regions neutron fluences required for local ^14C production comparable to cosmic irradiation could be consistent with observed/modeled values. This is a local-possibility argument, not proof of a globally important source.
25. Lyashuk (2021), **Evaluation of Radiocarbon 14C Yield Under Conditions of Thunderstorms**, *Geophysical Research Letters*: https://doi.org/10.1029/2021GL095357 — quantitative neutron/photonuclear yield treatment to incorporate in the publication pass when closing the per-event ^14C ledger.

## Source posture

Patents are unnecessary to this thesis. No classified material is claimed or required. NASA and mission pages establish instrument/program context; peer-reviewed papers carry physical claims. Risk extrapolations are not treated as measurements. The ALOFT raw-data availability statement should be followed in the publication pass; the 2024 *Nature* paper reports data deposited at Zenodo under DOI 10.5281/zenodo.12531291.

For photonuclear chemistry, keep observation and extrapolation separate. Enoto et al. establish a tightly linked event-level gamma → photonuclear neutron / unstable-isotope / positron chain. Wada et al. expand and model the atmospheric reaction network using evaluated cross sections. Neither source by itself establishes a globally important lightning contribution to atmospheric radiocarbon.

Keep four radiocarbon questions distinct:

1. **Can lightning produce photonuclear neutrons?** Yes, observationally established in suitable high-energy events.
2. **Can those neutrons enter `14N(n,p)14C`?** Physically yes; yield depends on moderation, transport and competing reactions.
3. **What is the ^14C yield per qualifying event?** Still a distribution, not one universal number.
4. **How does the integrated thunderstorm source compare with ~1.64 atoms cm⁻² s⁻¹ modern cosmogenic production globally and locally?** Not yet closed well enough to treat lightning as a major global correction.

A globally minor source may still be locally measurable. Do not turn that possibility into a claim that ordinary radiocarbon dating requires a thunderstorm correction without event-linked isotope evidence.
