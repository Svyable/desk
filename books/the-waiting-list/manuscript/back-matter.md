# Back Matter

## About the Book

*The Waiting List* is a book about the mechanism most people never learn to see: the rule that decides who goes first when there is not enough of something to go around and a price is not allowed, or not able, to do the job.

It moves from kidney exchange chains and the medical residency match to school choice algorithms, refugee resettlement pilots, ride-hailing dispatch, dating apps, vaccine rollouts, GPU shortages, water rights, and housing lotteries, treating them as instances of a single recurring problem that economists call market design. The book does not argue that markets are bad or that algorithms are neutral. It argues that a designed queue is a moral document, and that most of the queues governing modern life were never designed at all.

Manuscript body: approximately **83,000 words**, excluding front matter, back matter, and the research trail.

## Notes and Sources

The manuscript was researched chapter by chapter using a source ledger maintained alongside the book, following the sourcing conventions used elsewhere on this Desk.

The sourcing hierarchy is deliberate. Peer-reviewed economics research, first-party institutional data (OPTN/UNOS transplant registry statistics, National Resident Matching Program records, government reports, company disclosures), and primary reporting from established news organizations are preferred for the claims they can answer directly. Historical claims are supported by contemporaneous records and later scholarship rather than by anecdote alone. Figures describing an evolving shortage, such as GPU allocation, vaccine rollout status, or a housing waitlist's size, are treated as snapshots tied to the date they were recorded rather than as permanent facts.

Where evidence cuts against the book's argument that better mechanism design improves outcomes, the manuscript keeps that counterevidence rather than arguing it away. Deferred-acceptance mechanisms have real critics; kidney exchange has real limits; algorithmic matching has produced real failures of transparency and fairness. A market design that solves strategic manipulation does not automatically solve underlying scarcity, and the book tries not to conflate the two.

The research files accompanying this manuscript contain the chapter-level source ledger with source titles, dates, URLs, and the claims each source was used to support. Those materials are the canonical source record for the draft and can be converted into publication-format endnotes during final production.
