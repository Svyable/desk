## How We Really Decide Who Gets What

The central thesis is that when a price cannot or should not allocate a scarce good, some other rule always fills the vacuum — a queue, a lottery, a priority rule, or an algorithmic match — and that rule is rarely neutral. The book follows the economists who turned this problem into a rigorous discipline (market design), from the 1962 mathematics of stable matching through the National Resident Matching Program, kidney exchange, and school choice, then traces the same mechanism into ride-hailing, dating apps, pandemic vaccine rollout, refugee resettlement, AI-compute rationing, water rights, housing lotteries, and college admissions, closing with who audits these systems and how to build a better one.
