# Chapter 13 research — The Vaccine Line

## Core claim

The early U.S. COVID-19 vaccine rollout was a real-time allocation system under scarcity. Federal advisory bodies published ethical principles and broad priority groups, but actual access depended on state/local implementation and often on a second, less explicit mechanism: appointment availability.

## Verified evidence

- **CDC/ACIP ethical principles (MMWR, Nov. 27, 2020).** ACIP identified four principles for initial vaccine allocation: maximizing benefits and minimizing harms, promoting justice, mitigating health inequities, and promoting transparency. It explicitly treated these as guides alongside scientific evidence and implementation feasibility. Source: https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/69/wr/mm6947e3.htm
- **CDC/ACIP allocation update (MMWR, Jan. 1, 2021; recommendations made Dec. 20, 2020).** Phase 1a prioritized health-care personnel and long-term-care residents. Phase 1b prioritized people 75+ and non-health-care frontline essential workers. Phase 1c prioritized people 65–74, people 16–64 with high-risk medical conditions, and remaining essential workers. ACIP estimated roughly 49 million unique people in Phase 1b and 129 million in Phase 1c. Source: https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/69/wr/mm695152e2.htm
- **National Academies, Framework for Equitable Allocation of COVID-19 Vaccine (2020).** The framework combined exposure risk, severe-outcome risk, critical social function, congregate settings, and cross-cutting equity considerations rather than relying on age alone. Source: https://nap.nationalacademies.org/catalog/25917/framework-for-equitable-allocation-of-covid-19-vaccine
- **KFF state-priority tracking (Jan. 11, 2021).** KFF found 35 states broadly following ACIP Phase 1 recommendations and 16 varying from them. Used as evidence that national guidance became heterogeneous state-level lines. Source: https://www.kff.org/covid-19/the-covid-19-vaccination-line-an-update-on-state-prioritization-plans/
- **KFF state-priority update (Jan. 19, 2021).** KFF documented rapid changes after federal guidance to expand eligibility, including growth from 16 to 28 states placing people 65+ in early priority groups over roughly one week. Source: https://www.kff.org/covid-19/the-covid-19-vaccine-priority-line-continues-to-change-as-states-make-further-updates/

## Counterevidence and limits

- Variation across states was not automatically a failure. State and local conditions, public-health authority, supply, storage, staffing, and disease burden differed materially.
- Broad age thresholds are blunt but administratively legible. More finely targeted risk scores might better predict severe outcomes while being harder to verify, explain, and administer.
- The chapter distinguishes eligibility from practical access. It does not claim that every appointment system used pure first-come-first-served or that digital access barriers were identical everywhere.
- The National Academies framework and ACIP framework were not identical; they are used to show competing legitimate ways of weighting risk, social function, and equity.
- The chapter treats the first months of scarcity as a mechanism-design case. It does not imply that the same priority system remained appropriate after vaccine supply expanded.

## Falsification notes

Do not collapse the December 1 and December 20 ACIP decisions into one date. Phase 1a was recommended first; Phase 1b/1c recommendations followed later in December 2020.

Do not describe ACIP guidance as a binding national allocation law. States and other jurisdictions retained implementation authority and varied from the guidance.

Do not use retrospective claims about vaccine effectiveness to imply that allocation rules were obvious in advance. The chapter is about decisions made under contemporaneous uncertainty and constrained supply.
