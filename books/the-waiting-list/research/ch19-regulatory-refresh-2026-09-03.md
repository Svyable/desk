# Chapter 19 regulatory refresh — September 3, 2026

## Purpose

Refresh the two fastest-moving legal examples in Chapter 19 without rewriting the existing chapter note: the EU AI Act implementation calendar and Colorado's automated-decision law/rulemaking. Both examples still support the chapter's argument that algorithm-audit governance is fragmented and evolving, but their current procedural posture is now more precise than a generic "future obligations" description.

## European Union — current posture

The European Commission's current AI Act implementation page states that the Act entered into force on August 1, 2024 and became generally applicable on August 2, 2026, subject to phased exceptions.

Following the AI Omnibus, which entered into force on July 27, 2026, the Commission now gives two later high-risk application dates:

- December 2, 2027 for Annex III high-risk systems in areas including biometrics, critical infrastructure, education, employment, migration, asylum, and border control;
- August 2, 2028 for high-risk systems embedded in regulated products covered by Annex I.

The same Commission material says the August 2026 transparency rules are already applicable. The chapter should therefore distinguish the AI Act's general applicability and live transparency obligations from the later Annex III high-risk compliance regime.

- European Commission, "AI Omnibus enters into force," July 31, 2026: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/ai-omnibus-enters-force
- European Commission, "Guidelines for providers and deployers of AI high-risk systems," updated July 6, 2026: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/guidelines-ai-high-risk-systems

### Prose boundary

Do not say the EU AI Act's full high-risk employment, education, migration, or similar Annex III regime is already enforceable in September 2026. The useful current tension is that the Act is generally applicable and some transparency rules are live while the main Annex III high-risk obligations are scheduled for December 2, 2027.

## Colorado — current posture

Colorado's 2026 Senate Bill 26-189 repealed and reenacted the state's earlier AI provisions as an Automated Decision-Making Technology regime. The enacted law applies beginning January 1, 2027 and covers automated decision-making technology used to materially influence consequential decisions in areas including education, employment, housing, lending, insurance, health care, and essential government services/public benefits.

The current Colorado Attorney General rulemaking page adds a procedural development that matters for September 2026: on August 11, 2026 the Department of Law filed proposed Automated Decision-Making Technology and Conversational Artificial Intelligence Service rules with the Secretary of State. Formal written comments are being accepted through October 26, 2026, with comments submitted by September 4 requested for consideration in proposed revisions presented at the hearing. The Attorney General says interim updates to the proposed draft rules will be posted by September 23, 2026.

This means the chapter can now describe Colorado not merely as "conducting rulemaking" but as operating inside a live formal rulemaking process with filed draft rules and a defined comment calendar.

Primary sources rechecked September 3, 2026:

- Colorado Attorney General, "Colorado Anti-Discrimination in AI Law (ADAI) Rulemaking": https://coag.gov/ai/
- Colorado General Assembly, SB26-189, Automated Decision-Making Technology: https://www.leg.colorado.gov/bills/SB26-189

### Prose boundary

Do not describe Colorado's proposed August 2026 rules as final. The statute's January 1, 2027 effective date is enacted law; the Attorney General's implementation rules remain in formal rulemaking as of September 3, 2026.

## Why this matters to the chapter

These two jurisdictions now illustrate different kinds of moving target.

The EU example is mainly a phased application problem: a large regulatory framework is already partly applicable while some of the obligations most relevant to allocation systems remain deferred to later dates.

The Colorado example is mainly an implementation problem: the legislature has enacted the governing statute and effective date, while the regulator is still deciding important operational details through an active rulemaking process.

That distinction strengthens the chapter's central point. "Compliance" is not a timeless property of an algorithm. The evidence required from an institution depends on which legal layer is already operative, which requirements are still being implemented, and whether the system being audited has changed while the rules around it are changing too.

## Falsification trigger

Refresh this note again before any later status promotion or release review if either of the following changes:

- the European Commission changes the December 2, 2027 Annex III or August 2, 2028 Annex I application dates;
- Colorado finalizes materially different rules, changes the January 1, 2027 statutory effective date, or otherwise alters the obligations summarized above.
