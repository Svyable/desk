# Chapter 3 — First Come, First Served, and Why It Isn't Fair

## Claim spine

The chapter argues that first-come-first-served is valuable because it is legible, auditable, and resistant to some forms of discretion, but that its fairness is conditional. Arrival time can encode unequal access to information, transportation, schedule flexibility, connectivity, and the ability to absorb waiting costs. In domains where the cost of delay differs sharply across claimants, a strict chronological rule can conflict with the purpose of the institution.

## Sources checked

### Emergency Severity Index

Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality materials describe the Emergency Severity Index as a five-level emergency-department triage algorithm that stratifies patients from most to least urgent on the basis of acuity and expected resource needs. AHRQ funded early development of the tool. This supports the chapter's narrow use: emergency departments deliberately depart from chronological arrival order because clinical urgency is relevant to the service being allocated.

Sources:
- https://www.ahrq.gov/patient-safety/settings/hospital/resource/about.html
- https://www.ahrq.gov/topics/emergency-severity-index.html

### Queue fairness

Richard C. Larson's 1987 *Operations Research* article, “OR Forum—Perspectives on Queues: Social Justice and the Psychology of Queueing,” argues that attitudes toward queues depend on more than elapsed waiting time and highlights perceived violations of first-in-first-out order as a source of social-injustice reactions. The chapter uses this as evidence for the narrower proposition that FCFS carries a strong legitimacy norm once an institution has established a shared queue.

Source:
- https://doi.org/10.1287/opre.35.6.895

### Disney Genie+ / Lightning Lane

Contemporaneous reporting documents that Walt Disney World's Disney Genie service launched October 19, 2021 and that Genie+ initially cost $15 per person per day, providing access to Lightning Lane entrances at many attractions while some high-demand rides used separate individual purchases. The chapter treats this as a visible example of an institution allowing money to purchase a different relationship with waiting time; it does not claim that the system literally allowed unrestricted line-cutting or that every guest evaluated it the same way.

In June 2024 Disney announced that, beginning July 24, Genie+ would become Lightning Lane Multi Pass and that advance-planning features would return. Disney's own announcement said guests had expressed a preference to do more planning before their park day. Resort-hotel guests received an earlier advance-purchase window than other guests. The chapter uses this evolution to show that queue legitimacy depends on the rule bundle and the expectations surrounding it, not solely on physical arrival order.

## Falsification and counterevidence

The chapter should be revised if evidence shows that it overstates either the neutrality problems of FCFS or the relevance of the Disney example. In particular:

- FCFS is often the fairest available rule where claims are genuinely similar, arrival is easy to observe, and a richer priority system would create more discretion, lobbying, or gaming than benefit.
- A timestamp can be an important anti-corruption device. The chapter therefore should not imply that all chronological systems merely disguise privilege.
- Emergency triage is a special case because urgency is directly connected to the institution's purpose. Its logic cannot simply be imported into coffee shops, entertainment, or every public benefit.
- Paid priority may increase welfare for some users by allowing a voluntary money-for-time trade and may ration scarce shortcut capacity more efficiently than a single queue. The chapter preserves that defense rather than treating paid priority as inherently illegitimate.
- Disney's successive systems are products with many operational goals. The chapter should not attribute each design change solely to fairness concerns; Disney's 2024 statement specifically supports advance-planning preferences, not a general admission that Genie+ was unfair.

## Prose guardrails

Keep queueing theory qualitative and in service of the allocation argument. Do not turn Little's Law or service-discipline terminology into decorative mathematics. Do not invent guest reactions, hospital scenes, or patient details. The emergency-department opening is generic and illustrative rather than a purported reconstruction of a specific real event.
