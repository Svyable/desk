# Chapter 2 research note — A Taxonomy of Lines

Checked September 2, 2026.

## Claim under test

Chapter 2 argues that a “waiting list” is not one mechanism. Real allocation systems combine arrival time, randomization, need, status, compatibility, geography, eligibility, maintenance rules, and appeals. Kidney allocation, housing assistance, and military Space-Available travel are used as concrete examples.

The claim survives current-source review. The most important discipline is to resist collapsing layered allocation into a single queue metaphor: waiting time matters in several systems, but it is often only one input among many.

## Current authoritative anchors

### 1. Kidney allocation is not simple first-come-first-served

HRSA's current Kidney Allocation System FAQ states that kidney waiting time can begin when a candidate starts dialysis or meets the kidney-failure threshold, even if listing occurs later. It also confirms that highly sensitized candidates receive additional priority, with substantially greater priority at very high sensitization levels. This directly supports the chapter's point that two candidates with similar elapsed waiting time do not necessarily have equal priority for a particular kidney.

Source: HRSA / OPTN, “Kidney Allocation System FAQs,” current September 2026: https://www.hrsa.gov/optn/patients/resources/kidney/kidney-allocation-faqs

HRSA's general organ-allocation guidance also emphasizes that organs have short preservation windows and that matching depends on organ-specific factors rather than a universal national ordering. For kidneys, preservation time is longer than for hearts or lungs but still finite; physical logistics therefore remain part of allocation.

Source: HRSA / OPTN, “How organ allocation works,” last reviewed December 2025: https://www.hrsa.gov/optn/patients/organ-transplants/how-organ-allocation-works

### 2. Multiple listing exposes the role of geography and institutional access

Current HRSA guidance says multiple listing is permitted by OPTN policy and may shorten waiting time for some kidney candidates, though it does not guarantee a shorter wait. The guidance also explains that waiting time matters differently by organ and that medical urgency can dominate elapsed time for other organs.

This is useful counterevidence to any suggestion that “the national waiting list” is one simple line. Access to more than one transplant program can itself alter the opportunity set, subject to a patient's ability to meet each program's requirements.

Source: HRSA / OPTN, “Multiple Listing,” updated 2026: https://www.hrsa.gov/optn/patients/organ-transplants/multiple-listing

### 3. Medical urgency can explicitly outrank ordinary kidney allocation position

HRSA's kidney-allocation materials describe a Medically Urgent classification for candidates at imminent risk of death because dialysis access is failing or unavailable. The classification sits at different points in allocation sequences depending on donor characteristics, again demonstrating that the system is a layered priority mechanism rather than a chronological queue.

Source: HRSA / OPTN, “Addressing Medically Urgent Candidates in the New Kidney Allocation System”: https://www.hrsa.gov/optn/professionals/resources/kidney-pancreas/kidney-allocation-system/addressing-medically-urgent-candidates-in-the-new-kidney-allocation-system

### 4. The eGFR correction demonstrates that queue history can be revised

OPTN/HRSA required transplant programs to identify Black kidney candidates whose waiting-time accrual had been disadvantaged by race-inclusive eGFR calculations and to submit waiting-time modifications where appropriate. This supports the chapter's deeper claim that an administrative timestamp can encode prior policy choices and that correcting an inequitable rule can require changing recorded waiting history rather than merely changing future ranking.

Source: HRSA / OPTN, professional FAQ on eGFR waiting-time modifications: https://www.hrsa.gov/es/node/30193

### 5. Housing and Space-A remain hybrid mechanisms

The source ledger anchors the housing discussion in HUD's Housing Choice Voucher guidebook, where local public housing agencies operate within federal rules while retaining meaningful discretion over waiting-list administration and local preferences. The Space-Available example is anchored in Air Mobility Command's current guidance, which uses explicit traveler categories rather than pure arrival order.

Sources:
- HUD, Housing Choice Voucher Program Guidebook: https://www.hud.gov/helping-americans/housing-choice-vouchers-guidebook
- Air Mobility Command, Space-Available Travel: https://www.amc.af.mil/AMC-Travel-Site/AMC-Space-Available-Travel-Page/

## Counterevidence and limits

The taxonomy should not imply that every institution fits neatly into one bucket. Hybridization is the empirical norm. A lottery may occur only after eligibility and preference sorting; a priority system may use waiting time as a tiebreaker; a matching system may include geography and medical urgency; a categorical system may use sign-up time within each category.

The chapter also should not imply that complexity is evidence of unfairness. Layering often exists because pure mechanisms create their own inequities. The falsifiable question is whether each additional rule has a defensible purpose, is applied as intended, and can be challenged when data or implementation are wrong.

A further limit is that transparency and manipulability trade off. A fully legible rule may be easier to game, while an opaque rule may resist gaming at the cost of accountability. The chapter correctly treats this as a design tension rather than assuming maximal simplicity is always superior.

## Prose boundaries for later revision

- Keep “waiting list” distinct from “queue.”
- Do not say waiting time is irrelevant in kidney allocation; it remains an important factor, but not the only one.
- Do not describe the national transplant system as one total ordering across all organs.
- Do not imply that lotteries eliminate unequal access to the application pool.
- Keep administrative maintenance, notification, and appeals in the mechanism itself rather than treating them as clerical afterthoughts.
- Preserve the pro-FCFS countercase: simplicity, legibility, and reduced administrator discretion can be genuine virtues.

## Readiness judgment

Chapter 2's taxonomy is consistent with current HRSA/OPTN, HUD, and Air Mobility Command materials. Its strongest empirical point—that consequential “lines” are usually layered allocation institutions rather than simple chronological queues—is supported. No manuscript correction is required from this check; the dedicated note strengthens the book's evidence trail and makes the main overstatement risks explicit.
