# Dirty Air

## How Pollution Could Be the First Thing We Learn About an Alien Civilization

**Author:** Sven Hardy Benson

## Governing thesis

SETI usually imagines intelligence announcing itself. This book asks what happens if technology is easier to find when it is merely operating badly enough to leave chemistry behind. Earth observation already retrieves industrial pollution from orbit; exoplanet spectroscopy is beginning to retrieve atmospheric composition across interstellar distances; technosignature researchers have proposed nitrogen dioxide, CFCs and other artificial or technology-associated gases as detectable traces. The collision is uncomfortable: the first persuasive evidence of another technological civilization may not be a message. It may be an environmental externality.

The book does **not** claim that any exoplanet pollution technosignature has been detected.

## Evidence classes

1. **Verified/documented:** Earth-orbit instruments retrieve atmospheric trace gases including NO2; exoplanet atmospheric spectroscopy is demonstrated; NASA-supported technosignature studies explicitly model NO2 and CFC-like industrial pollutants.
2. **Credible but incomplete:** detectability calculations for Earth-like technological emissions under assumed telescope, atmosphere, cloud and stellar conditions.
3. **Disputed/unconfirmed:** any claim that a currently observed exoplanet spectrum contains industrial pollution or technology.
4. **Speculative synthesis:** technological civilizations may be statistically easier to notice through waste chemistry than intentional communication, and pollution could therefore be a selection effect in our first contact with technology.
