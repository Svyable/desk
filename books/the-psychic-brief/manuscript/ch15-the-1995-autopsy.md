# 15. The 1995 Autopsy

In 1995, Congress forced the long-running question toward a decision.

The program transferred to CIA, and the American Institutes for Research was asked to evaluate it. The resulting review became the closest thing STARGATE had to an autopsy performed while the body was still warm.

The review looked backward at laboratory research and operational use. It included the sharply different assessments associated with Jessica Utts and Ray Hyman. Yet the program-level recommendation was not a split decision. AIR concluded that continued operational use was not justified, and CIA did not restore the program.

CIA's later public account summarizes the paradox neatly: enough accurate remote-viewing experiences existed to raise questions about randomness, but the phenomenon was too unreliable, inconsistent and sporadic to be useful for intelligence.

That sentence should be read twice because it destroys the false binary.

A government can terminate an intelligence capability without proving the underlying scientific claim false.

This is normal in every other technology domain. A propulsion concept can work physically and fail economically. A sensor can detect a signal but fail under operational clutter. A drug can affect a biomarker without improving outcomes. A cryptographic attack can exist in theory while being impractical against deployed systems.

Remote viewing deserves the same layered analysis.

The 1995 decision also exposes a problem with retrospective program histories. Once a program is terminated, the termination becomes a magnet for totalizing narratives. Skeptics say the government finally admitted it never worked. Advocates say the government ignored proof or drove the work underground.

Neither claim follows from the public record.

The public record supports something more interesting: the government judged the available method insufficiently reliable and useful for continued intelligence operations. It does not establish that every experimental anomaly vanished. It does not prove no classified follow-on ever existed. It does not provide evidence that one did.

Absence of public continuation is evidence about public continuation.

It is not a blank check for secret-program mythology.
