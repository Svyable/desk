# The Psychic Brief

## Remote Viewing, STARGATE, and the Intelligence Value of Unreliable Information

**Sven Hardy Benson**

**Status:** Drafting · full structural manuscript

## Thesis

The United States did not spend two decades on remote viewing because intelligence officials proved psychic spying worked. Nor did the program persist simply because credulous officers lost their minds. It survived in the uncomfortable middle: laboratory results that some qualified evaluators regarded as statistically anomalous; operational products that were often vague, wrong, or impossible to score cleanly; a Cold War threat environment in which reports of Soviet psychotronics made unilateral dismissal feel risky; low program cost compared with conventional collection systems; and a bureaucracy repeatedly tempted by the possibility that even a weak channel might occasionally matter when every ordinary channel was blind.

The book treats that institutional persistence as the phenomenon to explain.

It separates four evidence classes throughout:

1. **Verified/declassified/documented:** CIA, DIA and Army sponsorship; SRI and SAIC contracts; named program transitions; released session records; operational tasking; the 1995 AIR evaluation; CIA's decision not to restore the program.
2. **Credible but incomplete:** claims that particular sessions contributed useful clues; retrospective accounts by program personnel and customers; estimates of Soviet/Russian activity assembled from open and intelligence reporting whose underlying evidence is uneven.
3. **Disputed/unconfirmed:** claims of reproducible extrasensory perception under conditions strong enough to establish a mechanism; spectacular operational success stories whose scoring or cueing cannot be independently reconstructed; alleged Soviet psychotronic capabilities inferred from adversary literature.
4. **Speculative synthesis/open conjecture:** remote viewing may represent a weak anomalous effect, an artifact of experimental structure and selective evaluation, a mixture of both, or something not captured by those categories. The institutional thesis does not require choosing among them prematurely.

## Reader promise

This is not a believer's book and not a debunker's victory lap. The archive is stranger and more useful than either genre allows. A released Army session can say a viewer had "absolutely no target correlation." Another can record a surprisingly apt description after erroneous coordinates pointed to the wrong place. A 1995 review can contain a statistician arguing that laboratory evidence is difficult to dismiss while the program-level conclusion still says the method is too unreliable for intelligence. Those are not contradictions to be cleaned up. They are the story.
