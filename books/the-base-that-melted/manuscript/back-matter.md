# Back Matter

## Evidence note

This manuscript treats official and declassified records as evidence of what institutions built, recorded, assessed or proposed. A planning document is not deployment evidence. A classified program's existence does not validate adjacent folklore. A public scientific mission can be genuine even when the same site supports classified strategic questions.

For climate claims, the manuscript preserves the chronology of model revision. The 2016 Camp Century waste paper identified a long-term remobilization pathway under high warming. The later Danish-Greenlandic monitoring program incorporated local field measurements and concluded that the debris is unlikely to emerge or be reached by surface meltwater before 2100 under the studied scenarios. Both results are part of the record.

For paleoclimate claims, preserved plants and basal sediment are evidence that northwestern Greenland experienced past ice-free conditions. They are not a direct clock for modern deglaciation and are not used here as a shortcut around attribution or ice-dynamics modeling.

## A note on the title

Current site-specific modeling does not predict Camp Century debris melting out before 2100. *The Base That Melted* refers to the failure of the premise that the ice could be treated as permanent passive infrastructure and permanent passive storage, not to a claim that the camp has physically emerged from Greenland.
