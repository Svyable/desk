# The Correction

Corrections are where scientific culture reveals whether it wanted truth or merely wanted to have been right first.

Camp Century got a useful correction.

By 2021, site-specific field measurements and firn modeling from the Camp Century Climate Monitoring Programme supported a substantially less dramatic near-term conclusion than many readers had taken from the 2016 study. Under the climate scenarios examined through 2100, the buried debris was not expected to melt out. Surface meltwater was projected to penetrate only shallowly compared with the depth of the camp remains. Continued accumulation would likely leave the debris tens of meters below the surface at century's end.

The monitoring program summarized the result bluntly: Camp Century is staying under the ice.

That sentence should have been as famous as the first one.

It was not.

There is a market reason. Threat travels better than narrowed threat.

There is also a cognitive reason. Once a story has acquired a moral shape, new information that modifies the timing feels like evasion.

The Camp Century moral shape was exquisite. A superpower built a secret nuclear facility under an ice sheet, left pollution behind, and climate change returned the waste to the present generation. That narrative contains historical hubris, environmental negligence and delayed justice in one package.

The correction does not destroy the package.

It removes the timer.

The base remains abandoned.

The wastes remain.

The site was still built inside a climate-dependent repository without a modern decommissioning plan.

Greenland is still warming.

The ice sheet is still losing mass overall.

But the specific claim that Camp Century's debris is likely to emerge or be contacted by surface meltwater before 2100 is not supported by the updated site-specific modeling.

A serious book has to let that fact make the story less satisfying.

There is a second correction buried inside the first.

Climate risk is not monotonic with public attention.

The more people learn that Greenland is losing ice, the easier it becomes to imagine every object under Greenland moving closer to daylight. But the Greenland Ice Sheet is not a bathtub draining uniformly. Its surface mass balance varies enormously across space. Ice also flows horizontally. Accumulation can continue at a specific high-elevation site even while the sheet loses mass at its margins and through outlet-glacier discharge.

A local object can become more deeply buried while the larger ice sheet loses mass.

That sounds contradictory only if scale is ignored.

Scale errors are one of the main engines of bad science communication.

A global average is applied locally.

A local anomaly is generalized globally.

A century-scale trend is mistaken for annual experience.

A mechanism demonstrated in one regime is moved into another.

Camp Century's updated result is a scale lesson disguised as a cleanup story.

The site's weather stations showed a colder and shorter melt season than the earlier modeling had effectively represented. Firn models then simulated how future meltwater would penetrate. In the scenarios analyzed, the deepest annual infiltration remained around a meter, far short of the debris depth.

This is a physical barrier, not merely a statistical confidence interval.

For contamination to enter surface meltwater through the hypothesized pathway, water has to reach the material.

If it does not, the pathway is closed for that period.

That does not close every imaginable pathway. Ice flow can eventually transport debris toward lower elevations. Long-term warming beyond 2100 can change surface mass balance. Uncertainties in debris composition and location remain. But the specific near-term surface-melt mechanism becomes much weaker.

The correction also improves the politics.

Panic is a poor basis for negotiating century-scale liability.

If governments believe a hazard is imminent, they may be pushed toward expensive intervention before the site is adequately mapped. If they believe it is irrelevant, they may stop monitoring. The updated evidence supports a third position: no current emergency, continuing obligation.

This position is psychologically difficult because institutions prefer either action or closure.

Monitoring is neither.

Monitoring means paying to preserve attention.

That may be the most appropriate form of stewardship for a site whose hazard is slow and uncertain.

Camp Century therefore becomes a case study in institutional patience.

The original military program was impatient. It belonged to an arms race in which strategic advantage could vanish within a few years. Build quickly. Test quickly. Decide quickly.

The environmental legacy demands the opposite. Measure for decades. Update models. Preserve records. Hand responsibility to people who did not create the site.

The temporal culture of the solution is the reverse of the temporal culture of the cause.

This mismatch appears everywhere in long-lived technology.

Companies can build chemical plants faster than groundwater plumes evolve.

Governments can approve mines faster than tailings dams weather.

Nuclear weapons can be produced faster than contaminated sites can be remediated.

Data centers can be constructed faster than transmission lines.

The world rewards fast creation and assigns slow maintenance to whoever remains.

Camp Century's correction is encouraging precisely because it represents slow maintenance working.

A provocative paper led to field investment.

The field investment produced measurements.

The measurements altered the risk estimate.

The risk estimate became more specific.

No one had to pretend the initial question was foolish.

That sequence should be the norm.

It is also epistemically useful for the larger thesis of this book. Secret history attracts believers and debunkers because both prefer final positions. Either the hidden program was bigger than admitted, or the entire story is hype.

Camp Century refuses both styles.

The secret program was real.

Some popular claims about it are false.

The environmental concern was real.

Some popular timelines for it are unsupported.

The ice-core discovery is profound.

Some climate extrapolations remain uncertain.

The place trains the mind to tolerate partial revelation.

This is not centrism. It is bookkeeping.

Each claim has a different evidence class.

The 2021 correction also changes the title of this book in a useful way.

*The Base That Melted* is physically premature.

Current evidence indicates the base has not melted out and is unlikely to do so this century.

What melted was the confidence attached to permanent burial.

Once governments established a climate-monitoring program, the base stopped being “preserved for eternity” and became “safe under currently modeled conditions through a specified horizon.”

That is a profound downgrade in certainty even though the near-term risk moved downward.

Engineering likes nouns: stable, safe, buried.

Science gives us sentences: under these conditions, according to these measurements, through this horizon, this pathway appears unlikely.

Sentences age better.

Camp Century's future will be managed by sentences.

No one can responsibly promise forever now.

That may be the most important correction of all.
