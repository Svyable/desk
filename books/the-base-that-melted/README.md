## Reader promise

This book is about a secret that became a scientific instrument.

In 1959 the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers began building Camp Century inside the Greenland Ice Sheet. Publicly, it was a spectacular polar-engineering station: prefabricated buildings buried inside trenches, a portable nuclear reactor, laboratories, hot showers, a chapel, a theater, and a deep ice-core program. Behind that true story sat a more politically dangerous question. Could the ice sheet hide a dispersed network of mobile nuclear missiles closer to the Soviet Union? Project Iceworm explored a system of tunnels, rail movement, and hundreds of proposed launch positions under Greenland. It was never built as an operational missile force.

The reason it failed is the first argument of this book. Ice is not ground. It flows. It closes around voids. The thing that made Greenland look like ideal concealment also made it a poor long-lived foundation for a giant buried weapons network.

The second argument is stranger. Camp Century's scientific program outlived the military logic that created it. A core drilled through roughly 1.4 kilometers of ice became foundational to paleoclimate research. Decades later, forgotten basal sediment from that core was rediscovered and found to contain preserved plant material and other evidence that northwestern Greenland had been ice-free in the geologically recent past. The military experiment that tried to use the ice sheet as permanent infrastructure accidentally helped reveal that the ice sheet is not permanent.

The third argument is about epistemology and inheritance. In 2016, researchers modeled a future in which warming could eventually expose or remobilize physical, chemical, biological, and radiological waste left at Camp Century. Later field measurements and updated modeling substantially narrowed the near-term claim: current monitoring concludes the debris is very unlikely to emerge or be reached by surface meltwater before 2100. That correction is not an embarrassment to the story. It is the story. A buried base can be simultaneously a documented environmental legacy, a poor basis for sensational certainty, a monitoring problem, and a reminder that engineering assumptions can age into political obligations.

## Distinct contribution

Most Camp Century stories choose one book and throw away the others. There is the Cold War thriller about a secret missile base. There is the nuclear-engineering story. There is the climate-change story about buried waste. There is the ice-core story. There is the Denmark-Greenland-U.S. sovereignty story.

The useful book appears when they are forced into the same room.

Camp Century is a case study in what happens when a state treats an environment as infrastructure, when public science and classified purpose overlap without being identical, when technical feasibility collides with political consent, and when an abandoned strategic experiment keeps generating evidence long after its strategic rationale is dead.

The strongest competing explanation is less dramatic: Camp Century was primarily an Arctic engineering and research station, Iceworm was one proposal among many, and later climate significance is accidental. Much of that is correct. This book does not claim that every scientific activity at Camp Century was a cover, that Denmark formally approved Iceworm, that operational nuclear missiles were installed, or that present-day waste emergence is imminent. The thesis is narrower: the same physical site can carry multiple institutional truths at once, and the collision among them is more revealing than any single secret.

## Evidence classes

1. **Verified / declassified / documented:** Camp Century construction and operation; its portable PM-2A nuclear power plant; U.S. Army investigation of under-ice construction; the existence and broad strategic concept of Project Iceworm; the first deep Camp Century ice core; tunnel deformation and abandonment; removal of the reactor pressure vessel/reactor components while other infrastructure and wastes remained; later Camp Century climate-monitoring programs; 2024 NASA radar imaging of buried structures; rediscovery and analysis of basal sediment.
2. **Credible but incomplete:** exact boundaries between Camp Century's scientific/engineering mission and Iceworm feasibility work at each date; how much specific Camp Century construction data directly informed later Iceworm designs; complete inventories and spatial distribution of all abandoned waste; internal U.S. expectations about Danish consent at every planning stage.
3. **Disputed / not established:** an operational Iceworm missile arsenal under Greenland; nuclear warheads left buried at Camp Century; a covert continuation of the missile grid after formal rejection; imminent exposure of Camp Century debris before 2100; claims that all Camp Century science was merely a deception operation.
4. **Speculative synthesis / open conjecture:** Camp Century as a model for how secret state projects become involuntary long-term experiments, creating scientific archives and political obligations whose importance exceeds the original mission.
