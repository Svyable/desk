# Research ledger — Before Disclosure

## Governing question

Before the modern UAP/disclosure era, how did anomalous claims move among scientific intelligence, White House management, presidential gatekeeping, private networks, and theological interpretation—and what evidence actually connects Christopher “Kit” Green, Alonzo L. McDonald, James A. Baker III, and the later Collins Elite narrative?

## Four evidence classes

### Class 1 — verified / declassified / documented

1. CIA-released STARGATE/precursor records document U.S. intelligence sponsorship and evaluation of remote-viewing/parapsychology work at Stanford Research Institute in the 1970s.
2. Released SRI material describes client-supplied coordinates, government participation, contract monitors, and experiments intended to approximate intelligence conditions.
3. Russell Targ's published retrospective identifies Christopher “Kit” Green as the CIA contract monitor in early SRI remote-viewing work. This is a named participant account; document-level CIA identification by name should still be recovered where possible.
4. The Miller Center identifies Alonzo L. McDonald as Special Representative for Trade Negotiations and Director of the White House Staff in the Carter administration, with his White House transfer in 1979.
5. The Miller Center identifies James A. Baker III as Reagan's White House Chief of Staff and preserves his account of access, coordination, and informal presidential decision channels.

### Class 2 — credible but incomplete

1. Green's broader role as an intelligence scientist evaluating anomalous claims can be reconstructed from declassified material plus named participant testimony, but role boundaries and dates must be documented precisely.
2. Vallée's journals are valuable near-contemporaneous records of what he heard, observed, and inferred; claims inside them need independent corroboration at the event level.
3. McDonald and Baker may have intersected with private Christian/Fellowship networks relevant to later anomalous-phenomena stories. Each asserted connection requires primary records.
4. Informal White House and intelligence relationships can route information outside ordinary paper flows, but a plausible route is not proof that a claimed briefing traveled through it.

### Class 3 — disputed / unconfirmed

1. That Green, McDonald, and Baker belonged to a single covert UFO/UAP network.
2. That McDonald or Baker received a verified presidential briefing establishing extraterrestrial visitation.
3. That a “Collins Elite” existed as a formally chartered, funded, or continuously staffed U.S. government program.
4. That named officials adopted a demonic interpretation of UFO/UAP and used it to set government-wide policy.
5. That later “disclosure” narratives accurately describe a continuous secret institutional lineage from 1970s anomaly research to modern alleged retrieval programs.
6. Any claim that a person's Fellowship/Christian ties establish his UFO beliefs.

### Class 4 — speculative synthesis

1. The pre-disclosure state may have been plural rather than unitary: separate scientific, intelligence, military, political, private-network, and theological interpretations occupying partially overlapping channels.
2. Later disclosure mythology may be produced when real secrecy, partial archives, and informal networks are retrospectively compressed into one hidden institution.
3. A genuinely compartmented program could produce the same outward archival pattern, so fragmentation is a hypothesis to test, not a skeptical conclusion.

## Contradiction ledger

### Government interest vs validated phenomenon

CIA sponsorship proves institutional interest and expenditure, not that remote viewing was established as a reliable mechanism. The book must distinguish the fact of research from claims about the phenomenon's success.

### Participant identification vs official role record

Targ names Green as CIA contract monitor. CIA files document contract-monitor participation but the cleanest by-name primary chain must be recovered. Do not silently upgrade participant memory to a government personnel record.

### White House role vs anomalous-program role

McDonald's and Baker's senior White House positions are documented. Those positions establish access and institutional relevance, not UFO involvement. Every anomalous claim needs a separate source chain.

### Journal proximity vs event proof

Vallée's journals may establish who was in conversation with whom and what was being said in real time. A recorded claim remains a claim until corroborated.

### Theological interpretation vs formal program

Even if named officials privately held a demonic or spiritual interpretation, that does not establish the existence, membership, mandate, funding, or authority of a Collins Elite.

## Strongest skeptical model

The apparent network is retrospective assembly. Green's anomaly work was one small scientific-intelligence portfolio; McDonald and Baker were senior White House figures whose later association with prayer or elite networks is ordinary Washington social history; Vallée and later UFO writers encountered overlapping names and private relationships and inferred more coherence than existed. The Collins Elite story reflects sincere recollection, theological subculture, folklore, or source contamination rather than a formal hidden program.

## Strongest hidden-network model

A small number of trusted officials and outside advisers handled anomalous material through personal relationships and restrictive compartments precisely because no durable public bureaucracy was desired. Formal records would therefore understate continuity, and apparent social adjacency could be part of the routing architecture rather than coincidence.

The hidden-network model needs evidence of repeated tasking, controlled access, document lineage, shared briefing material, authority, resources, or succession. Familiar names alone are insufficient.

## Publication holds

- Do not describe Green, McDonald, and Baker as members of one group without a primary chain.
- Do not describe a Collins Elite as established fact.
- Do not equate SRI remote-viewing sponsorship with UFO research.
- Do not claim that Christian/Fellowship association predicts a person's interpretation of anomalous phenomena.
- Do not use a Vallée diary entry as proof of the event it records unless Vallée directly witnessed that event and the text supports that reading.
- Separate “a briefing occurred,” “a person attended,” “a claim was presented,” and “the recipient believed the claim.”
- Treat presidential access as a routing fact, not proof of presidential knowledge.
