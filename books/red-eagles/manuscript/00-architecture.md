# Red Eagles
## How America Learned to Fly the Enemy's Airplanes

### Reader contract

This is a book about real reverse engineering, which means it is mostly a book about mechanics, pilots, paperwork, broken parts and embarrassment.

It does not assume that every captured machine can be understood, copied, improved or even safely operated. It does not use HAVE DOUGHNUT or CONSTANT PEG to authenticate claims about non-human craft. It asks what documented exploitation looks like when the object is real, foreign, strategically valuable and secret.

## Architecture

1. **The MiG in the Hangar** — A Soviet-built MiG-21 arrives inside an American test system. The machine is no longer an intelligence estimate. Someone has to start it.
2. **Before You Can Steal a Technology** — Foreign materiel exploitation before the MiGs: capture, technical intelligence and the difference between possession and understanding.
3. **Fishbed** — What U.S. pilots thought they knew about the MiG-21 before they could fly one.
4. **HAVE DOUGHNUT** — The clandestine MiG-21 evaluation and the questions an actual test program asks.
5. **Fresco** — HAVE DRILL/HAVE FERRY and the older MiG-17, where low sophistication did not mean low tactical danger.
6. **The Maintainer Problem** — A secret airplane without a normal supply chain becomes a machine made partly of improvisation.
7. **Instrument Everything** — Turning pilot impressions into measurements without pretending measurements eliminate judgment.
8. **Know Your Enemy's Envelope** — Comparative performance, tactics and the dangerous temptation to convert one airframe into a universal adversary.
9. **The Human Reverse Engineer** — Pilots learn foreign cockpit logic through their hands, vestibular systems, mistakes and fear.
10. **After Vietnam** — Combat losses, Topgun, Aggressors and the institutional demand for a less imaginary enemy.
11. **CONSTANT PEG** — The Red Eagles become a sustained operational training system.
12. **Tonopah** — How geography, access control, scheduling and mundane logistics manufacture secrecy.
13. **Flogger** — The MiG-23 makes the maintenance and safety problem harder.
14. **What Broke** — Accidents, scarce parts and the evidentiary value of failure.
15. **The Secret Escapes Without Leaking** — Lessons migrate into tactics and training while sources and methods remain compartmented.
16. **What Real Reverse Engineering Leaves Behind** — People, facilities, manuals, contractors, budgets, test cards, mishaps, spares, doctrine and downstream capability.
17. **The Comparator** — Apply that footprint carefully to modern allegations of retrieved exotic aerospace material. Similarity is not proof; missing expected traces are not automatic disproof.
18. **A Machine Is Not a Miracle** — The hardest lesson from the Red Eagles: possession collapses mystery only slowly. Reality produces friction.

## Defeat condition

The central thesis weakens if the documented programs turn out to have produced tactically useful knowledge without the predicted institutional footprint—maintenance burden, test infrastructure, trained personnel, operational risk, logistics and downstream doctrine—or if modern classified exploitation can be shown from comparable historical cases to leave essentially no public trace even decades later.
