# Evidence ledger — The Orbit Nobody Knew

## Thesis

JUMPSEAT is a newly declassified historical test of a recurring claim about secrecy: a technically sophisticated program can remain officially undisclosed for decades even when its physical container is observable. The important distinction is not hidden versus visible but **observable container versus legible capability**. The book will test how much outsiders could actually infer before the December 2025 declassification and which mission details remained protected.

## Class 1 — verified / declassified / documented

- In December 2025 the Director of the National Reconnaissance Office declassified the existence of JUMPSEAT and limited details about its mission. NRO public announcement, January 28, 2026: https://www.nro.gov/news-media-featured-stories/news-media-archive/News-Article/Article/4392223/declassifying-jumpseat-an-american-pioneer-in-space/
- NRO identifies JUMPSEAT as the first-generation U.S. highly elliptical orbit signals-collection satellite, developed under Project EARPOP by Program A, the USAF program at NRO.
- NRO identifies mission numbers 7701 through 7708, launched from 1971 to 1987.
- NRO says JUMPSEAT collected electronic emissions/signals, communications intelligence, and foreign instrumentation intelligence, downlinked data to U.S. ground processing, and supplied selected DoD elements and NSA.
- NRO says the system remained in service until 2006 and ultimately operated in transponder mode.
- NRO's JUMPSEAT fact sheet states that the December 2025 decision was a limited declassification. Primary: https://www.nro.gov/Portals/135/Documents/foia/JUMPSEAT%20Records/Jumpseat_SIGINT_Fact_Sheet.pdf
- NRO's declassified-program portal now lists JUMPSEAT (1971–2006) alongside the signed declassification memorandum and fact sheet. Primary: https://www.nro.gov/foia-home/foia-declassified-nro-programs-and-projects/
- Earlier declassified EARPOP records exist. Example: an NRO-released Project EARPOP final-orbit diagram, record C05098790: https://www.nro.gov/Portals/135/documents/foia/declass/SIGINTphaseIII/SC-2017-00004_C05098790.pdf

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete

- What U.S. and foreign civilian satellite observers inferred about JUMPSEAT before 2025. Specialist identifications may have been accurate, but each needs dated provenance and a distinction between orbital object, suspected SIGINT category, codeword, payload and performance.
- What Soviet/Russian intelligence knew. It is highly likely that a peer adversary tracked the launches and orbits; the level of mission knowledge requires Soviet/Russian records or strong U.S. counterintelligence documentation.
- Exact collection bands, antenna architecture, payload evolution, target decks, sensitivity, ground processing, tasking, reliability and intelligence yield remain outside the limited public description assembled in this pass.
- Relationship between EARPOP references in earlier POPPY-era documents and the later JUMPSEAT project needs a document-level chronology; do not assume every EARPOP-marked record is JUMPSEAT.

## Class 3 — disputed / not established

- Any claim that JUMPSEAT collected a particular named target, weapon system, communication or event unless supported by declassified records.
- Claims about current NRO HEO SIGINT architectures inferred from JUMPSEAT. Historical lineage does not establish current capability.
- Claims that long JUMPSEAT secrecy proves modern alleged crash-retrieval, non-human technology, exotic propulsion, or other extraordinary programs exist.
- Claims that an orbital object's publicly inferred mission category means the classified program was effectively exposed in every consequential sense.

## Class 4 — speculative synthesis / open conjecture

- **Observable container / illegible capability:** physical observability may expose that a system exists while leaving the information that determines military usefulness classified.
- **Declassification lag is not secrecy duration:** official disclosure date, public inference date, adversary discovery date and operational compromise date can differ by decades.
- **The legendless map:** classification can work by withholding relationships among visible facts rather than making each fact disappear.
- JUMPSEAT may provide a better historical comparator for contemporary secrecy arguments than singular spectacular programs because it involved repeated launches, long operations, ground infrastructure and an observable orbital signature.

## Contradiction audit

1. **Fifty-four years secret vs observable orbit.** Resolve by reconstructing who knew which layer and when; do not equate official nondisclosure with universal ignorance.
2. **NRO success narrative vs missing denominator.** The agency says JUMPSEAT proved its worth. Recover mission failures, anomalies, degradation, collection gaps and customer assessments before adopting performance claims.
3. **Limited declassification vs confident historical storytelling.** Redactions and withheld facts remain active evidence constraints. Do not fill them with technically plausible invention.
4. **EARPOP appears in older records vs JUMPSEAT chronology.** Establish codeword reuse/evolution from documents rather than assuming identity.
5. **Program ancestry vs successor inference.** A historical system can explain design pressures without revealing current architectures.

## Incentive audit

- NRO has an institutional incentive to present a newly declassified legacy system as successful, pioneering and foundational.
- Former program participants may emphasize technical achievement and memorable crises while underremembering routine failure.
- Satellite observers gain prestige from retrospective identification; require timestamped pre-2025 publications for priority claims.
- Intelligence agencies had incentives to conceal both collection capability and what they believed adversaries knew about it.
- Contemporary UAP/exotic-technology advocates have incentives to use any long-lived classified program as a generic proof-of-possibility; historical analogy must remain bounded.

## Primary-source queue

1. Obtain and page-index the signed December 2025 JUMPSEAT declassification memorandum.
2. Inventory every newly released JUMPSEAT record and distinguish genuinely new releases from older EARPOP/SIGINT releases newly interpretable after declassification.
3. Build the eight-mission launch ledger: date, booster, launch site, catalog identity, orbit, failure/anomaly, replacement cadence, public cover description.
4. Reconstruct the pre-2025 open-source identification history from amateur satellite observers, orbital catalogs and specialist publications; preserve publication dates.
5. Search Soviet/Russian technical-intelligence and space-tracking histories for the same objects.
6. Reconstruct Program A / NSA / DoD ground-processing interfaces from declassified records.
7. Separate ELINT, COMINT and FISINT collection claims and explain what each required physically.
8. Build an orbital-geometry chapter from authoritative astrodynamics sources: dwell time, inclination, apogee, line of sight and high-latitude coverage, without publishing still-sensitive details.
9. Recover anomaly/failure records and any mission-level performance evaluation. The book needs misses, not only heritage claims.
10. Establish what “transponder mode” meant in the released historical context and when the transition occurred.
11. Reconstruct the 2006 decommissioning decision and identify which stakeholder organizations participated, using only declassified records.
12. Compare JUMPSEAT's disclosure timeline with GRAB, POPPY, MOL, CORONA, GAMBIT, HEXAGON and later NRO declassifications without turning the book into a catalog.

## Research rule

A tracked orbit proves an object occupied an orbit. A codeword proves a codeword was used. A declassified memorandum proves what the government has released. None alone proves payload performance, target, intelligence yield, successor capability, or the contents of a different classified program.
