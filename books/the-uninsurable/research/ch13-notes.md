## Falsification note for Chapter 13

The chapter's claim that "the AI was wrong" has almost no dedicated insurance product would be overstated if the handful of AI-performance-guarantee products (Munich Re's aiSure, Armilla's coverage, and a small number of similar Lloyd's-backed offerings) scale rapidly from their current low-single-digit-insurer base — a possibility the chapter acknowledges but cannot rule out, since this is a fast-moving market and the estimate of "roughly five" dedicated insurers is a snapshot, not a stable fact. The Waymo/Swiss Re safety comparisons are Waymo's own commissioned or co-authored data, covering geographically concentrated, well-mapped, mostly fair-weather operating areas (Phoenix, San Francisco, and similar cities), so the claims-reduction percentages should not be read as a claim about autonomous driving safety in all conditions, climates, or road types — a boundary the chapter notes but that bears restating. The characterization of Tesla's insurance model as leaving liability "largely" with the driver is a generalization across a fast-evolving body of case law; the Benavides verdict itself is under appeal, and future rulings could shift how courts allocate fault between driver and manufacturer in ways that make the current framing look more transitional than settled.
