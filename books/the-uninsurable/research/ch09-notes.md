## Falsification note for Chapter 9

The chapter's claim is narrower than "AIG's traders were reckless" or "credit default swaps caused the financial crisis." AIG's core, regulated insurance operations were not the source of the near-collapse, and CDS were one instrument among many (subprime origination, rating-agency failures, bank leverage, mortgage securitization incentives) that combined to produce the 2008 crisis; isolating AIGFP risks overstating its causal share relative to the broader mortgage-finance system. The Maiden Lane III counterparty-payment figures come from secondary business-press reporting rather than a primary SIGTARP or Federal Reserve document read directly in this session (njcourts- and Fed-adjacent primary PDFs were blocked by network egress in several cases), so exact cents-on-the-dollar figures by counterparty should be treated as well-corroborated but not independently verified against the original SIGTARP report. The claim that AIG's failure "revealed" the insurance/derivatives regulatory gap is a retrospective framing; contemporaneous regulators and most market participants did not treat AIGFP's swap book as insurance-like until after its near-failure forced the comparison.
