## Falsification note for Chapter 14

The chapter's framing of TRIA, Pool Re, and Price-Anderson as a single pattern ("a promise the state stands behind") risks understating real structural differences among them: TRIA is a temporary, periodically-expiring program requiring active reauthorization, Pool Re is an industry-owned mutual with a standing government guarantee, and Price-Anderson combines a fixed private/pooled liability structure with a now-40-year legislative horizon — these are not interchangeable instruments, and a reader should not conclude that any one of them could substitute for another. The claim that TRIA "has never been triggered" describes only formal federal reimbursement above the certification threshold; it does not mean no terrorism losses have occurred in the U.S. since 2002, only that none has yet been large enough, or been formally certified, to cross the threshold — a distinction the chapter states but that bears re-emphasis, since a future certified event well below $100 billion in total losses could still be a genuine catastrophe for the specific people and businesses it strikes even while leaving the federal backstop mechanism itself untested. Sourcing on exact historical loss figures (Baltic Exchange and Bishopsgate insured losses in particular) varies slightly by publication and by inflation-adjustment method; the chapter uses the most commonly cited round figures rather than a single authoritative recomputation.
