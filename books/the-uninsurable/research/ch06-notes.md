## Falsification note for Chapter 6

The chapter's claim that the NFIP functions more as an unrepaid transfer than as actuarial pooling would be weakened if Risk Rating 2.0's property-specific pricing, given enough years to phase in under the statutory rate caps, brings the program's premium income into rough balance with its expected claims — a process still incomplete as of this writing, so the long-run outcome is not yet settled. The repetitive-loss statistics cited come from two GAO analyses using different FEMA datasets and time windows that do not numerically agree (35.5% vs. 48% of claims dollars), and the chapter reports both rather than resolving the discrepancy. The Write Your Own fraud material describes documented cases from a specific set of Sandy-era lawsuits and should not be read as implying that fraud or claim manipulation is characteristic of WYO claims handling generally; the overwhelming majority of NFIP claims across the program's history have been processed without any comparable finding. Finally, the flood protection gap figures (4% national uptake, 77% of at-risk homes outside mapped zones uninsured) come from industry and reinsurer-affiliated research (Munich Re, Neptune Flood) rather than a single government census of coverage, and should be read as informed estimates rather than a precise federal count.
