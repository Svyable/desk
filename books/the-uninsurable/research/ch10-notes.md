## Falsification note for Chapter 10

The chapter's central legal claim — that the Merck and Mondelez courts ruled on contract-interpretation grounds rather than finding NotPetya was or was not literally an act of war — is well supported by multiple independent secondary summaries of the rulings, but this session could not directly fetch and quote the underlying New Jersey court opinion (njcourts.gov, natlawreview.com, and several law-firm domains were blocked by network egress), so specific quoted language is triangulated across several outlets rather than verified against a single primary PDF. The chapter should not be read as establishing that "silent cyber" litigation is now resolved: the Merck and Mondelez settlements left no binding appellate precedent even within New Jersey, and a materially different fact pattern (a cyberattack more clearly tied to an ongoing shooting war, for instance, or one directly targeting military or government systems) could plausibly be decided the other way by a different court applying the same interpretive principles. The $10 billion global NotPetya cost figure is a widely repeated but loosely sourced aggregate that does not cleanly reconcile with the sum of individual companies' disclosed losses, and is presented in the chapter as an estimate rather than an audited total.
