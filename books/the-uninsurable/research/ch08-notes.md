## Falsification note for Chapter 8

The chapter's claim that Lloyd's central oversight and Central Fund reforms meaningfully reduced the risk of a repeat LMX-style spiral would be weakened by evidence of a comparable undetected concentration forming inside the modern market — evidence this chapter does not have, since no event on the scale of Piper Alpha's spiral has since tested the reformed structure end to end. The Bermuda "Class of" narrative is a real and well-documented pattern, but it should not be read as proof the underwriting cycle is perfectly mechanical or predictable in advance; each wave of capital formation was a response to a specific catastrophe whose timing nobody predicted, and the amount and speed of capital inflow varied considerably across the three waves described. The COVID-19 material draws a strong analogy between pandemic risk and this book's broader correlation argument; it should not be read as claiming pandemic risk is identical in mechanism to hurricane or cyber correlation, since a pandemic's near-total lack of geographic diversifiability is a more extreme case than most of the correlated risks discussed elsewhere in the book.
