## About the Book

*Breakeven* is a book about a question that outlives any single experiment: what actually changes when a seventy-year punchline stops being simply true?

On December 5, 2022, the National Ignition Facility put more energy out of a fusion target than its lasers put in. That fact is real, and the book treats it as real. It is also a much narrower fact than the headlines that followed it suggested — measured at the target, not at the wall plug; a single laboratory result, not a power plant; a facility built to certify nuclear weapons, not to generate electricity.

The book follows four threads that changed at roughly the same time: a genuine physics milestone at a weapons laboratory, a wave of venture capital willing to fund first-of-a-kind fusion machines, a regulator's decision that fusion does not need to be licensed like a fission reactor, and an AI-driven electricity crunch urgent enough that hyperscale companies are now buying power from plants that do not yet exist. None of those threads proves fusion is close. Together they explain why so much serious money and attention is behaving as though it might be — and why the honest answer, for now, is that both the optimism and the skepticism are supported by real evidence.

## Notes and Sources

The manuscript was researched chapter by chapter using a source ledger maintained alongside the book, following the sourcing conventions used elsewhere on this Desk.

The sourcing hierarchy is deliberate. Company and laboratory press releases, government and regulatory primary sources (the Department of Energy, the Nuclear Regulatory Commission, Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory, ITER's own organization), and peer-reviewed research are preferred for claims they can answer directly. Contemporaneous reporting is used where the event itself is the subject. Figures that are estimates, projections, or company targets are identified as such rather than presented with the certainty of a measured result.

Where evidence cuts both ways — a record that was broken within weeks by a rival lab, a funding milestone with a penalty clause attached, a critique from a credentialed skeptic — the manuscript keeps the counterevidence rather than arguing it away. The goal is not to settle whether fusion works. It is to make the case for genuine uncertainty strong enough to survive contact with either side's enthusiasm.

The research files accompanying this manuscript contain the chapter-level source ledger with source titles, dates, URLs, claim summaries, and caveats. Those materials are the canonical source record for the draft and can be converted into publication-format endnotes during final production.

## Acknowledgments

This book depends on people who make an unglamorous kind of progress legible: the plasma physicists, laser engineers, magnet designers, and regulators who publish their methods and their setbacks alongside their milestones, and the reporters and researchers who keep a public record precise enough to check.

Fusion's history is an unusually humbling one to write about. It has produced confident public claims that did not survive scrutiny, and it has also produced, eventually, a result that decades of confident dismissal did not anticipate either. The useful part is that both kinds of claim left a paper trail.

Any remaining errors of interpretation belong to the author.
