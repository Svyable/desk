# Back Matter

## The Status of These Ideas

The future scenes in this book are imagined. The envelope, shade receipt, electricity statement, dream postcard, Ministry of Almost, quiet-room library, museum, and other fictional devices are narrative experiments. They are not reports of actual interviews, public agencies, clinical procedures, or products.

The factual claims about contemporary science, law, government programs, and engineering are meant to remain checkable. Primary documents and research starting points appear in the chapters and in the accompanying research ledger. Where the evidence is early, contested, or context-specific, the prose should say so. The research trail does not transform an uncertain hypothesis into a finding by association.

Several ideas here are already being tested in parts of the world. Others are deliberately speculative. The difference matters. A reader should be able to enjoy the latter without being misled about the former.

## A Note About Forecasting

None of these chapters offers a reliable date for the arrival of a particular technology or institution. Scientific possibility, engineering demonstration, commercial viability, legal permission, social desirability, and wide adoption are different stages. The distance between them can be measured in decades, or never crossed at all.

The future may take a path that makes these proposals obsolete. That would be a legitimate result, and sometimes a welcome one.

## Source and Revision Note

The book's research folder contains an editorial brief and a chapter-level source ledger with key assertions, what sources support, and questions still requiring verification. Several essays need additional primary reporting, expert counterviews, case studies, and expansion before the full-length manuscript or publication stage. Those gaps are intentionally recorded rather than covered with invented scenes or made-up certainty.

This is a working Desk draft. It has not been authorized for release to the public Shelf.
