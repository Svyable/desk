# Architecture

1. **The Wind Won** — Insectothopter as the opening control case: a real strange machine stopped by an ordinary environment.
2. **Build the Animal** — biomimicry versus copying appearance; fluidics, scale, and why a dragonfly is more than wings.
3. **The Unowned Component** — formalize weather, adversary, target, geology, tissue, interpreter and other external variables as parts of the causal system.
4. **A Pigeon Already Knows How to Fly** — CIA camera pigeons and the engineering advantage of borrowing evolved autonomy rather than recreating it.
5. **The Cat Refused Tasking** — Acoustikitty and the opposite bargain: excellent biological mobility, poor command authority.
6. **Machine, Animal, Hybrid** — a comparative matrix of biological carriers, robotic mimics and instrumented organisms.
7. **One Gram** — mass budgets, payload fractions, stored energy, sensing aperture, communications and what miniaturization takes away.
8. **Weather Is Not Noise** — gusts, Reynolds regimes, precipitation, turbulence and the difference between laboratory disturbance and operational environment.
9. **The Operating Envelope** — why best-run demonstrations are bad proxies for capability; reconstruct field-test denominators.
10. **Hidden in Plain Sight** — ecological camouflage: behavior, silhouette, sound, motion, thermal signature and the moment camouflage itself becomes anomalous.
11. **The Laser Has to Follow** — guidance and data links; expose the infrastructure outside the tiny vehicle.
12. **What Classification Can Hide** — genuine technical surprise versus conservation, infrastructure, training, procurement and environmental signatures.
13. **Tiny Things in the Sky** — disciplined application to UAP/sensor cases; no event gets a microdrone explanation without geometry and event-specific evidence.
14. **Borrowed Senses** — animal magnetoreception, olfaction, vision and other sensing that engineering may exploit without implying paranormal biology.
15. **The Deployment Gap** — study → component → prototype → integrated field test → robust system → authorized operation → successful mission.
16. **Failure Is Technical Intelligence** — failed programs as unusually honest maps of what physics and environment demanded.
17. **The World Gets a Vote** — cross-domain tests: propulsion, directed energy, anomalous sensing, exotic materials and field biology.
18. **Not Magic** — classified technology may be ahead; the epistemic discipline is to ask what bill it must still pay.

## Required comparison set

At minimum: Insectothopter; pigeon camera; Acoustikitty; robotic aquatic platform; one later documented micro-air vehicle program; one operational system with environmental robustness; one frontier system whose lab effect failed in field conditions; one counterexample where a narrow prototype rapidly became operational.

65,000–80,000 words plus notes/source trace. Chapter 1 is substantive but remains first-draft. Chapters 2–18 require full drafting. Primary-source reconstruction takes precedence over expanding claims.
