Physics spent centuries treating space and time as the stage on which reality happens. Modern physics has spent the last century making that stage less secure.

Relativity removed a universal clock. Quantum theory made observation, measurement, and description harder to separate from what can be said to exist. Black-hole thermodynamics tied geometry to entropy and information. Holographic ideas and quantum-gravity research now take seriously the possibility that spacetime itself is not fundamental.

That creates a question stranger than the title: if spacetime is an output, what is doing the inputting?

**Spacetime Is a Lie** follows that question without pretending it has already been answered. It begins inside established physics, moves through active attempts to derive geometry from deeper structures, and only then approaches consciousness. The book's wager is not that consciousness has been proven to create the universe. It is that once space and time stop being fundamental, the old habit of treating consciousness as a late, local by-product of matter deserves to be reopened rather than assumed.

The book will keep six evidentiary layers visibly separate: established experimental physics; active theoretical physics; empirical anomalies; consciousness research; philosophical interpretation; and speculation. A beautiful idea does not become evidence because it unifies two mysteries. A disputed experiment does not become established because it is suggestive. An equation that reconstructs geometry does not, by itself, explain subjective experience.

The argument is strongest when the distinctions survive.

## Reader promise

By the end, the reader should understand why serious physicists can question the fundamentality of spacetime without abandoning empirical science; why that move does not automatically establish any consciousness-first cosmology; and why the consciousness problem becomes more interesting, not less, when matter, locality, and time are no longer treated as obvious primitives.

The reader should also be able to tell the difference between a result, an interpretation, an open problem, an anomaly, and a metaphysical leap.

## The book's distinct contribution

This is not a general quantum-mysticism book and not a survey of consciousness theories with physics used as atmosphere. Its progression runs in the opposite direction. The book first lets physics dismantle its own classical stage. Only after spacetime has become questionable on physics' own terms does it ask whether consciousness belongs in the deeper ontology.

The strongest competing explanation is simpler: spacetime may indeed be emergent while consciousness remains entirely produced by ordinary physical processes inside the emergent world. The manuscript has to take that possibility seriously enough that a consciousness-first interpretation can lose.

## Editorial principles

1. **Physics first.** No consciousness claim gets to borrow credibility from quantum gravity merely by appearing nearby.
2. **Grade every claim.** Established result, active theory, empirical anomaly, interpretation, and speculation must stay legible as different things.
3. **No observer sleight of hand.** A measurement interaction, an observer in an interpretation, and conscious awareness are not interchangeable terms.
4. **Use fewer mysteries more deeply.** Black-hole entropy, holography, entanglement, the measurement problem, and the hard problem should receive enough room to become understandable rather than forming a parade of astonishing facts.
5. **Let objections bite.** Decoherence, no-signaling, neural correlates, illusionist theories of consciousness, and ordinary methodological criticism should be developed at full strength.
6. **Do not manufacture certainty.** The speculative chapters should become more explicit about uncertainty, not less.
7. **Return to experience carefully.** The fact that every scientific observation is experienced does not establish that experience causes the observed world.
