# Research brief — Spacetime Is a Lie

## Central question

If spacetime is not fundamental, what deeper structures can generate the geometry and temporal order we experience, and does that change the scientific or philosophical status of consciousness?

## Reader

A scientifically curious general reader who is comfortable with difficult ideas but does not want equations used as stage props. The book should reward a physicist for checking the distinctions while remaining readable to someone encountering holography, decoherence, or the hard problem for the first time.

## Working answer

The defensible core is that the fundamentality of spacetime is an open question in serious physics, with multiple research programs treating geometry or temporal order as emergent. That does not entail that consciousness is fundamental. It does, however, weaken one common rhetorical move in consciousness debates: treating the everyday categories of matter-in-space-over-time as the unquestioned base layer from which mind must be derived.

The manuscript should investigate whether consciousness belongs in the deeper story while remaining willing to conclude that it does not.

## Strongest competing explanations

- Spacetime is emergent, but consciousness is still wholly generated by physical processes describable within the emergent regime.
- The measurement problem can be handled without any special role for consciousness.
- Apparent links between quantum foundations and mind are conceptual analogy rather than shared mechanism.
- Anomalous-consciousness findings fail under replication, methodological scrutiny, or ordinary statistical explanation.
- The hard problem may reflect a mistaken philosophical framing rather than missing physics.

These are not ritual objections. Each should be developed strongly enough to force the thesis to narrow if the evidence demands it.

## Evidence ladder

### 1. Established physics

Relativity, experimentally supported quantum mechanics, black-hole thermodynamics where the relevant claims are broadly accepted, no-signaling constraints, and empirical results that do not depend on a speculative interpretation.

### 2. Active theoretical work

Holography, AdS/CFT, entanglement/geometry connections, tensor-network models, causal-set approaches, loop/spinfoam programs, emergent-time proposals, relational approaches, amplitudes and other quantum-gravity formalisms. State clearly when a result is model-dependent, conjectural, or restricted to a special spacetime.

### 3. Quantum-foundations interpretation

Everettian, relational, QBist, objective-collapse, Bohmian, Copenhagen-family, and other views. Do not present disagreement among interpretations as disagreement about every experimental prediction.

### 4. Consciousness science and philosophy

Neural correlates, global-workspace families, integrated-information proposals, higher-order theories, predictive-processing approaches, illusionism, panpsychism, neutral monism, dual-aspect approaches, and related positions. Distinguish empirical programs from metaphysical interpretation.

### 5. Empirical anomalies

Psi/parapsychology, presentiment, remote-viewing literature, near-death-experience research, terminal lucidity, anomalous cognition, and altered-state findings. Record preregistration, sample size, replication history, methodological criticism, effect sizes, and publication-bias concerns. Do not collapse government investigation of a claim into government validation of the claim.

### 6. Speculation

Consciousness-first cosmologies, participatory-universe extensions, nonlocal-mind hypotheses, simulation-adjacent ideas, and other proposals that outrun present evidence. These belong in the book only when labeled as such and tested against simpler alternatives.

## Falsification and narrowing questions

1. What observation would make the book reduce or abandon the claim that consciousness deserves a role in fundamental ontology?
2. Which emergent-spacetime results survive outside highly symmetric or otherwise special models?
3. Are references to "the observer" in a given physics source operational, interpretive, informational, or explicitly conscious?
4. Can decoherence plus an interpretation of quantum mechanics do all the work that a consciousness-based account claims to do?
5. Do leading consciousness theories require any change to fundamental physics?
6. Are anomalous-consciousness effects robust to preregistration, adversarial collaboration, independent replication, and correction for researcher degrees of freedom?
7. Does an information-first account explain experience, or merely rename the physical description?
8. If time is emergent, does that illuminate the felt passage of time or simply create a second explanatory problem?

## Chapter-level research order

Research should proceed roughly in manuscript order. Establish relativity and quantum-foundations language before using it; build black-hole entropy and holography before claiming geometry-from-entanglement; separate the measurement problem from consciousness before entering observer-centered interpretations; establish mainstream consciousness science before assessing anomalies; and reserve ontology synthesis for the end.

## Source standard

Prefer primary papers, major review articles, university or collaboration pages, Nobel-level or academy material, government archives for historical programs, and high-quality replication/meta-analysis work. Record direct links and enough bibliographic detail that a reader can follow the trail without searching by paraphrase.
