# 11 — CONSTANT PEG

CONSTANT PEG is what happens when an intelligence curiosity becomes a training institution.

Established in 1977, the program put Soviet-designed MiG-17s, MiG-21s and later MiG-23s into the hands of the 4477th Test and Evaluation Squadron, the Red Eagles. The aircraft operated from Tonopah Test Range. The purpose was not simply to test foreign hardware. It was to expose U.S. aircrews to the real thing before combat.

The Air Force’s public history says the program ran until 1988, flew more than 15,000 sorties, and trained almost 6,000 aircrew. Those are not the metrics of a specimen-analysis project. They are the metrics of a curriculum.

The shift matters.

HAVE DOUGHNUT asked: what can this MiG do?

CONSTANT PEG asked: what should thousands of American pilots learn because of what MiGs can do?

That is how exploitation becomes doctrine without publishing the source.

Students often were not told in advance exactly what they would encounter. The shock was part of the lesson. A radar intercept might become a formation with a real MiG. The program then moved through familiarization, one-on-one work, two-on-two engagements and more complex scenarios. Debriefs converted surprise into rules.

This architecture is a model for how serious secret knowledge diffuses. The raw intelligence remains compartmented; the operational lesson is progressively stripped of source-sensitive detail and moved outward.

If modern alleged retrieval programs have produced real, useful knowledge, we should expect some analogue of this laundering of insight—not laundering in the criminal sense, but in the security sense: transforming a source-sensitive fact into a usable capability.

A material discovery might appear as a new manufacturing tolerance. A sensor insight might appear in threat libraries. A propulsion insight might appear as changed research priorities. The downstream effect could be visible without the upstream source becoming public.

CONSTANT PEG therefore raises an awkward question for narratives of decades-long breakthrough reverse engineering: where is the training effect?

Not every technology trains pilots. But every deployable capability trains someone. Engineers, operators, maintainers, safety officers, acquisition personnel. A fielded secret leaves a human skills footprint.

The Red Eagles also expose the difference between a real program and a story optimized for mystery. Real programs have attrition. Three members associated with Constant Peg died in accidents. Aircraft broke. Parts were scarce. Safety concerns retired types. Funding ended programs.

Nothing about this diminished the secrecy.

It made the secrecy expensive.

That cost is evidence. A black capability is still paid for in ordinary currencies: labor, risk, maintenance, time, training, opportunity cost.

A claim of immense hidden capability with no corresponding burden asks us to believe in a form of secrecy that also suspends accounting.

CONSTANT PEG did not suspend accounting. It suspended disclosure.
