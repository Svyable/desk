# 16 — The Retrieval Claim Test

Now we can stop circling the modern question.

Suppose someone claims that the United States has recovered a craft of unknown or non-human origin and hidden it inside a special-access structure for decades.

Do not begin by asking whether the witness seems sincere.

Begin with the program model.

**Acquisition:** What exactly was recovered? Intact vehicle, major section, debris, biological material, instrument? What is the provenance? Who had custody before the alleged program received it?

**Receiving inspection:** How was the object stabilized, photographed, inventoried and screened for hazards? Were contamination controls used? Was unexploded ordnance considered? Radiation? Toxic propellant? Biological risk?

**Configuration control:** Does the program know which piece came from where? Are samples traceable to parent components? Are there duplicates?

**Characterization:** Which measurements were performed? Mass, density, isotopes, crystallography, microscopy, spectroscopy, electrical behavior, magnetic behavior, thermal properties, mechanical strength?

**Mechanism:** What causal model emerged, and what observations distinguish it from competing terrestrial explanations?

**Replication:** Has any claimed effect been reproduced independently using controlled apparatus?

**Scaling:** Can the effect be made larger, more reliable, or useful?

**Integration:** Has it changed a conventional aerospace, materials, sensing or energy system?

**Operationalization:** Who was trained to use the result?

**Failure record:** What did not work?

**Security architecture:** How did knowledge move far enough to exploit the object without moving far enough to expose the source?

These questions do not assume the claim is false. They make the claim expensive in the correct way.

The four evidence classes then prevent category leakage.

Documented historical retrieval programs—HAVE DOUGHNUT, HAVE DRILL, HAVE FERRY, CONSTANT PEG—belong in Class 1 for their established existence and described functions.

Credible witnesses who report seeing records or participating in activities they cannot fully document may belong in Class 2, depending on access, specificity and corroboration.

Claims of non-human origin without inspectable chain-of-custody evidence remain Class 3.

The idea that historical foreign-materiel exploitation provides a benchmark for evaluating those claims is Class 4: synthesis.

This hierarchy is not designed to humiliate testimony. It protects testimony from being forced to do work it cannot do.

A person can sincerely and accurately report what they were told.

That proves they were told it.

A person can sincerely and accurately report what they saw.

That proves an experience occurred as described, subject to ordinary limits of perception and memory.

Neither automatically proves origin.

The retrieval claim test therefore has a deliberately high final hurdle: can the public evidence distinguish extraordinary origin from a secret terrestrial program, misidentification, deception, folklore, or institutional rumor?

At present, for the strongest public crash-retrieval allegations, that hurdle has not been cleared.

That is not the same sentence as *there is nothing there*.

It is a statement about evidence state.

The historical MiGs are useful because they show what clearing the hurdle eventually looks like: named programs, inspectable records, surviving hardware, test objectives, institutional continuity, people, dates, consequences.

A real secret can become a historical fact.

Until then, keep the ledgers separate.
