# 13 — What 15,000 Sorties Buy You

A single flight can reveal a surprise. Fifteen thousand flights reveal a distribution.

That distinction is the hidden strength of CONSTANT PEG.

The Air Force’s public account says the Red Eagles flew more than 15,000 sorties and exposed nearly 6,000 aircrew to real Soviet-designed fighters. One can argue about exactly how much later combat success should be attributed to that program rather than Red Flag, Topgun, better missiles, better sensors, better training generally, or Iraqi deficiencies in 1991. Attribution is difficult.

But repetition changed the character of the knowledge.

A one-off test tells you what happened once under known conditions. A mature operating program reveals reliability, maintenance burden, pilot variability, environmental sensitivity and the tails of the distribution.

This is the difference between discovering an effect and characterizing an effect.

The distinction should be mercilessly applied to extraordinary technical claims.

A dramatic demonstration is not a capability.

A capability has uptime.

A capability has failure rates.

A capability has tolerances.

A capability can be reproduced by more than one operator.

A capability survives different days.

This is why the phrase “we got it to work once” is both exciting and insufficient.

If a purported reverse-engineering program has operated for decades, the relevant question is not whether someone observed an anomalous effect. The question is whether the program generated a performance distribution.

How often did it work?

Under what inputs?

How often did it fail?

What damaged the apparatus?

Did independent teams reproduce it?

What is the confidence interval?

The Red Eagles did not need to phrase their work statistically for the logic to hold. Thousands of sorties create opportunities for outliers to reveal themselves as outliers. A pilot’s first impression can be corrected by another pilot’s experience. A maintenance issue can be recognized as recurrent. A tactic that succeeds in one geometry can be discovered to fail in another.

Repetition is the solvent for folklore.

It also exposes variant problems. The MiG-23MS used by Constant Peg was an export version and not equivalent to every Soviet MiG-23 configuration. That matters because the program’s findings were local before they were general.

The same caution belongs in materials analysis. A property measured from one fragment is a property of that fragment under that protocol. The leap to “this is how the craft works” may require an entire chain of unearned generalization.

The virtue of 15,000 sorties is not simply volume.

It is that volume makes overclaiming harder.

By the end of a long test and training program, people stop speaking in absolutes because the machine has shown them too many exceptions.

A real technology becomes less mythic as the dataset improves.

That may be the most counterintuitive benchmark in this book.

If decades of secret study make a claim more mystical rather than more parameterized, something has gone wrong.
