# The Reactor as an Evolution Experiment

Chernobyl is often described as though evolution began there in April 1986.

The black fungi were older than the reactor.

Melanin is older than nuclear engineering. So are cosmic rays, radioactive minerals, oxidative stress and desiccation. What the accident changed was intensity, spatial pattern and ecological competition. It may have selected pre-existing traits rather than invented new ones.

This is experimentally testable because fungi can be evolved in the laboratory under repeated irradiation. Work with *Exophiala dermatitidis* has already shown that radiation resistance can increase under serial selection and that the genetic routes can be surprising. Parallel evolved lines provide a way to distinguish predictable adaptation from historical accident.

A serious long-term experiment would run melanized and non-melanized populations under several regimes: chronic low-dose gamma, pulsed high dose, mixed radiation, oxidative stress without radiation, and matched no-radiation controls. Whole-genome sequencing, transcriptomics, pigment chemistry and growth physiology would be collected through time.

If radiotrophy is a real energy-capture strategy, selection under carbon limitation plus chronic radiation should repeatedly favor mutations that improve the coupling between radiation, melanin redox state and metabolism. Those mutations should not simply improve generic stress resistance. Their advantage should disappear when the radiation field disappears.

If instead the main benefit is protection, selection should favor cell-wall architecture, antioxidant systems, repair and pigment production without a distinct energetic flux.

The beauty of experimental evolution is that it does not care which story has better press.

Chernobyl supplied one historical draw from the evolutionary lottery. The laboratory can run the lottery a hundred times.
