# Growth Toward the Source

A fungus growing toward a radiation source is difficult to forget.

The behavior was called radiotropism, and reports from Chernobyl-associated isolates helped give radiotrophy its almost science-fictional aura. Hyphae appear to make a choice. The reactor is no longer merely endured; the organism seems to approach it.

But tropism is a behavioral geometry, not a metabolic diagnosis.

Plants bend toward light because photoreceptors trigger asymmetric growth. Roots orient with gravity. Fungal hyphae respond to nutrients, electric fields, surfaces and chemical gradients. None of these behaviors alone tells you which stimulus supplies metabolic energy. A radiation gradient could be sensed because it changes reactive oxygen species on one side of a growing tip, because it alters membrane channels, because secondary chemistry creates a directional cue, or because an actual energetic benefit exists closer to the source.

The old Chernobyl experiments need modern resurrection rather than quotation.

A good radiotropism apparatus would make the source invisible except for its radiation field. Temperature, electrostatic charge, vibration, visible light, shielding material and chemical outgassing would be balanced. The source would move between trials under automation. Investigators scoring hyphal direction would be blind to source location. Multiple radiation qualities would be tested at matched absorbed doses.

Most importantly, melanin would be manipulated genetically rather than inferred from colony color alone.

If melanized wild type bends toward gamma radiation and an isogenic melanin-deficient mutant does not, the pigment becomes part of the causal chain. Restore melanin synthesis and the behavior should return. If both bend, look elsewhere. If the direction flips with oxygen level, reactive chemistry becomes interesting. If the behavior persists behind particle shielding that preserves only one radiation component, the physical cue can be narrowed.

This is the sort of experiment that frontier claims rarely receive because the original anecdote is more seductive than the mechanism.

Radiotropism may turn out to be genuine and mechanistically separate from radiostimulation. That would be scientifically satisfying. A fungus might sense an ionizing-radiation gradient because radiation predicts a niche where competitors are impaired or where its own protective chemistry gives it an advantage. Evolution can build navigation toward opportunity without converting the cue itself into calories.

The hypha approaching the source therefore asks a more subtle question than “does the fungus eat radiation?”

It asks what the fungus knows.
