# A Fourfold Redox Change

The most interesting number in the radiotrophy literature may not be a growth rate. It may be four.

In the 2007 work, irradiated melanin increased its capacity to reduce NADH roughly fourfold in the experimental assay. That observation is awkward because it is neither merely ecological nor obviously metabolic. It sits between physics and cell biology.

Ionizing radiation deposits energy by ejecting electrons and producing cascades of ionization. In water-rich biological material this creates radicals and secondary chemistry on very short timescales. Melanin already contains a population of stable radicals and an extended, disordered electronic structure. It is therefore reasonable that irradiation can change its redox state or electron-transfer behavior.

The unreasonable step is to leap directly from that fact to “the fungus photosynthesizes gamma rays.”

NADH is central to cellular redox chemistry, but an in-vitro reduction assay is not a fungal respiratory chain. To establish energy capture, one would want to trace electrons or reducing equivalents through the pigment and into defined metabolic products while measuring absorbed dose. Ideally, isotopic or electrochemical methods would show that radiation changes a flux that vanishes when melanin synthesis is disabled and reappears when the pigment is restored.

One would also want the effect to scale in a physically intelligible way. Does doubling absorbed dose double the relevant redox flux until damage dominates? Is there a spectral dependence tied to deposition physics? Does the effect depend on melanin thickness or chemical state? Can purified melanin coupled to a synthetic redox circuit do useful work under irradiation?

These are engineering questions disguised as microbiology.

They matter because redox mediation could explain why melanin has several apparently contradictory reputations. A material that accepts electrons and quenches radicals may protect at one dose. At another, the same electronic flexibility may alter metabolic signaling. At still another, damage overwhelms any benefit. There may be no single “radiotrophic” regime, only a phase diagram.

This is a recurring mistake in extreme-biology stories. We ask whether an organism loves radiation. The organism has no obligation to love or hate a scalar variable. It responds to dose rate, total dose, radiation quality, oxygen, nutrients, temperature, cell-cycle state and previous exposure.

The fourfold redox result deserves attention precisely because it can be converted into a cleaner experiment than the ecological story. Purify the pigment. Measure its electronic states before and after defined irradiation. Couple it to a controlled donor-acceptor system. Close the energy balance.

If nothing useful crosses the bridge, the grand claim shrinks. If something does, we will know exactly which wire carries it.
