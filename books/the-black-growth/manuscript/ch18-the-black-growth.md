# The Black Growth

The reactor wall is still the right image, provided we resist improving it.

There was no cinematic moment when a scientist watched fungus drink a beam of gamma rays. There were contaminated structures, black colonies, culture plates, electron-spin measurements, mutant comparisons, conflicting growth curves and a slow accumulation of reasons not to throw the question away.

The evidence is strongest where the claim is least extravagant. Melanized fungi survive punishing radiation. Melanin can contribute to radioprotection in some organisms and conditions. Ionizing radiation can change measurable electronic properties of melanin. Fungal physiology can change under radiation. Space agencies have rational reasons to investigate melanized organisms and melanin-derived materials for radiation mitigation.

The evidence becomes thinner as the claim approaches a new metabolism.

The 2007 growth and redox results remain provocative. They deserve replication, not folklore. The 2022 null growth result deserves equal weight. The *Exophiala* work showing major roles for nutrient state, culture age and DNA repair prevents us from assigning every survival advantage to pigment. The radiotropism literature needs modern blinded reconstruction.

What I like about the problem is that none of these complications make it boring.

If melanin is only armor, it is remarkable armor. If it is an environmental sensor, biology has built a detector out of a disordered pigment. If it is a redox buffer that changes its behavior under ionizing radiation, there may be materials science hidden inside the cell wall. If it truly contributes usable energy, then a category of metabolism is waiting for a proper ledger.

Chernobyl has produced decades of bad metaphors and good organisms. We should keep the organisms.

The black growth on the wall does not prove that life eats radiation.

It proves that life found something to do there.
