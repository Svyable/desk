# Build the Experiment That Can Lose

The radiotrophy field does not need a bigger adjective. It needs an experiment designed to embarrass it.

The core study should be multicenter and preregistered. Use at least two fungal species with prior positive evidence and one species with prior null evidence. For each, compare melanized wild type, genetically matched pigment-deficient lines and rescued lines. Grow them under rich and carbon-limited conditions.

Expose cultures to calibrated gamma fields at several dose rates, with sham sources identical in heat, shielding geometry and handling. Dosimetry belongs inside the culture geometry. Investigators measuring growth and metabolomics should be blind to exposure assignment.

The primary endpoint should not be colony diameter. It should be additional chemical free-energy storage or carbon assimilation per unit absorbed radiation after accounting for reduced damage and conventional nutrient flux.

Secondary endpoints can map the mechanism: ESR changes in extracted melanin, NADH/NAD+ ratios, ATP, oxygen consumption, oxidative lesions, repair activity, transcriptomic changes, isotopic carbon flux and heat production.

A radiotropism arm should independently test directional sensing in a device that moves the radiation source automatically and controls temperature and electromagnetic artifacts.

The hypotheses need defeat conditions written in advance.

If pigmentation improves survival but does not generate a radiation-dependent metabolic flux, call it radioprotection. If metabolism changes equally in albino and melanized lines, melanin is not necessary. If apparent energetic gain disappears when damage-repair costs are measured, stop calling the gain energy harvest. If only one laboratory sees the effect, freeze the claim until the apparatus difference is found.

And if a reproducible melanin-dependent flux appears, scales with absorbed dose, closes energetically and vanishes with pathway disruption, then we have earned the right to use the exciting word.

Not before.
