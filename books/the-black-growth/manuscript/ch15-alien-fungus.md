# What an Alien Fungus Would Teach Us

Imagine a dark organism recovered from beneath the ice of a moon where surface radiation is severe and sunlight never reaches liquid water.

The worst thing we could do is call it radiotrophic because it is black.

Color is a hypothesis generator, not a metabolism assay.

The Chernobyl literature gives astrobiology a useful rehearsal for exactly this mistake. Extreme environments encourage teleological interpretation. A pigment appears where radiation is high, therefore the pigment must have evolved *for* radiation. Growth correlates with dose, therefore the organism must be using the dose. Directional behavior points toward a source, therefore the source must be food.

Earth fungi show how many conventional mechanisms can produce the same pattern.

For an extraterrestrial organism, the evidentiary hierarchy should be harsher. First identify the pigment’s structure. Measure absorption and redox behavior. Establish whether it reduces radiation damage. Then search for a dose-dependent metabolic flux that cannot be explained by protection. Finally close the energy budget.

The reward for doing this carefully is large. Ionizing radiation is ubiquitous. Galactic cosmic rays, stellar particle events, radioactive minerals and magnetospheric particle belts create environments where the radiation spectrum differs radically from terrestrial sunlight. A genuine biological transducer of high-energy radiation could expand the energetic geography of life.

But indirect pathways may matter more. Radiolysis can create redox disequilibria in water-rock systems. Radioactive decay can maintain chemistry long after starlight is irrelevant. An organism need not catch a gamma ray to live on a radioactive world.

The alien-fungus thought experiment returns us to Chernobyl with better questions. What did the pigment do? What did the cell do? What did the ecosystem do? Which of those three levels actually depended on the radiation field?

A good astrobiological theory should survive being tested first on Earth organisms we can culture.
