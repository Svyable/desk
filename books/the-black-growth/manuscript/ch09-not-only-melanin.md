# Melanin Is Not the Only Thing in the Cell

A melanized fungus is not a bag of melanin with DNA attached.

It has membranes, mitochondria, antioxidant systems, nutrient transport, repair enzymes, cell-cycle controls, stress signaling and a history. Radiation acts on all of them.

This became particularly clear in work on *Exophiala dermatitidis*. A 2020 study examining acute gamma exposure reported that melanin did not protect this organism in the way a simple pigment-shield story predicted. Nutrient availability, culture age and culture density were larger determinants of survival. The transcriptomic response mobilized pathways involved in morphological development, protein degradation and DNA repair, and the response was not governed simply by melanization state.

That result is wonderfully inconvenient.

It means two black yeasts can tell different stories about the same pigment. In one organism or assay, melanin supplies measurable radioprotection. In another, the dominant survival determinants may be physiological state and repair. The contradiction does not imply somebody must be incompetent. Biological mechanisms can be species-specific, architecture-specific and dose-specific.

The phrase “melanin protects fungi from radiation” is therefore too broad to be a law. Better: melanin can contribute to radiation response through physical and chemical mechanisms whose importance varies by organism and condition.

This weaker sentence is scientifically stronger because it can be parameterized.

How much pigment is present? Where is it located? Which melanin chemistry is being synthesized? What is the radiation quality? Are cells actively dividing? Are they starved? Are oxygen and water available to generate secondary radicals? Is the comparison mutant otherwise physiologically normal?

Albino mutants are especially treacherous. Knocking out pigment synthesis can alter more than color. The synthetic pathway may interact with cell-wall construction, oxidative stress or development. A clean causal test sometimes requires restoring the gene, supplying pigment externally, or measuring multiple independent mutants.

Anomalous biology often becomes ordinary once enough variables are discovered. That should not disappoint us. “Ordinary” in this context means a complicated system whose behavior can be predicted.

If radiotrophy survives that process, it will be worth much more than the original headline.
