# When the Replication Refuses the Story

In 2022 a Sandia-led study examined *Cladosporium cladosporioides* and *Paecilomyces variotii* under gamma and ultraviolet irradiation while attempting to control absorption and other confounders. The result was not the one the mythology ordered.

Pigmentation changed. Growth rate did not significantly increase under the tested UV or gamma conditions.

This is not a refutation of every earlier experiment. The species differed. Radiation conditions differed. Nutrient state, dose, geometry and melanin chemistry may differ. But it breaks the universal version of the claim, and that is valuable.

If radiation-enhanced growth appears only under limited nutrients, only in certain species, only after prior exposure, or only within a narrow dose-rate range, then radiotrophy is not a generic property of black fungi. It is a conditional phenotype that needs a map.

The 2022 paper also points back to an older problem in the radiotropism literature. Some reports found far fewer positive directional responses than the most-cited Chernobyl numbers, and melanization was not always sufficient to separate responders from nonresponders. The black pigment is clearly biologically important. It is not a magic variable that explains every observation carrying the word radiation.

There is a temptation in anomaly research to treat failed replication as a social event. Believers say the conditions were wrong. Skeptics say the original was wrong. Both reactions are too cheap.

The experimental conditions are the scientific object.

Suppose one organism gains biomass at five hundred times background under carbon limitation but shows no advantage under rich media. That pattern could mean radiation is contributing a tiny amount of usable energy that matters only when conventional energy is scarce. It could also mean irradiation activates scavenging, stress or transport systems useful only under starvation. Those hypotheses make different predictions.

Likewise, if pigmentation increases after UV but decreases after gamma exposure, “more radiation produces more melanin” is dead. The cell distinguishes radiation quality, or at least the chemistry those radiations produce.

A good theory becomes narrower when confronted with contrary data. A bad story becomes more vague.

The black fungi deserve the narrow theory.
