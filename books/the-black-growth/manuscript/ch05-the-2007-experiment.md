# The 2007 Experiment

The paper that turned black fungi into a scientific provocation appeared in *PLoS ONE* in 2007. Ekaterina Dadachova, Arturo Casadevall and colleagues did not simply place a fungus beside a Geiger counter and declare victory. They attacked the problem at several levels.

First, they irradiated melanin and measured its electron spin resonance behavior. The signal changed in a manner consistent with alteration of the pigment’s electronic structure.

Second, they tested redox capacity. Irradiated melanin showed roughly a fourfold increase in its capacity to reduce NADH compared with non-irradiated material under their assay conditions.

Third, they moved into cells. They compared melanized and non-melanized forms, including fungi capable of producing pigment and albino mutants. Under an ionizing-radiation field described as roughly five hundred times background, melanized *Wangiella dermatitidis* and *Cryptococcus neoformans* showed greater growth by several measures. The authors reported higher colony-forming units, dry biomass and approximately threefold greater incorporation of carbon-14 acetate in irradiated melanized cells relative to relevant controls. *Cladosporium sphaerospermum* also showed enhanced growth under limited-nutrient conditions.

This is why the paper remains difficult to dismiss with a joke about mold being tough. It joined physical change in melanin to biological change in cells and included pigmentation comparisons.

It also did not close the mechanism.

A cell under chronic low-level ionizing radiation is not a calorimeter. Radiation can activate stress pathways, alter redox balance, damage competitors, change membrane behavior, modify nutrient transport or induce repair programs. Increased incorporation of acetate is suggestive of altered metabolism, but it does not by itself tell you the energy source driving that metabolism.

The strongest version of the radiotrophy hypothesis says the sequence runs like this: radiation deposits energy in melanin; melanin’s electronic/redox properties shift; those shifts increase electron transfer useful to metabolism; the cell converts the advantage into growth.

The 2007 study illuminated each neighborhood of that chain. It did not instrument every bridge between them.

That difference is easy to lose because the result is so narratively satisfying. A black pigment, a radiation source, more growth. The human mind closes the circuit automatically.

Science earns its keep by refusing to let narrative current pass through an unmeasured wire.

The right response to the paper was not belief or debunking. It was replication under conditions designed to distinguish shielding, signaling, repair and energy transduction. Some later studies strengthened parts of the story. Others did exactly what good follow-up work should do: they made it worse.
