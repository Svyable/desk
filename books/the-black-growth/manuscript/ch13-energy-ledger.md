# The Energy Ledger

There is one experiment I want more than another photograph of a black colony beside a radiation symbol.

I want the ledger.

Take a melanized fungus and an isogenic pigment-deficient control. Grow them in a carbon-limited chemostat where every conventional nutrient flux is measured. Expose half of each population to a calibrated gamma field. Measure absorbed dose in the culture, not merely activity at the source. Track oxygen consumption, carbon dioxide production, ATP turnover, NADH/NAD+ balance, carbon incorporation, biomass yield and heat.

Then ask a rude question: where did the extra free energy come from?

Ionizing radiation carries enormous energy per photon but arrives sparsely compared with visible light in ordinary biological settings. Much of its deposited energy becomes indiscriminate ionization and heat. A hypothetical radiotrophic pathway must capture a tiny fraction of this chaos before damage costs consume the benefit.

The energy calculation does not need to be philosophically perfect. It needs to be good enough to rule out impossible interpretations. If the additional biomass contains more chemical free energy than the culture could plausibly have extracted from the measured radiation field, the radiation cannot be its primary energetic source. If the numbers are compatible, mechanistic tracing becomes worthwhile.

This is where comparison with photosynthesis becomes useful rather than rhetorical. Photosynthesis has a photon budget. Quantum yields can be measured. Reaction centers can be isolated. Electron transport can be poisoned at defined points. Carbon fixation can be traced.

Radiotrophy needs the equivalent.

What is the absorber? Which electronic state changes? What donor and acceptor complete the redox cycle? Which enzyme or membrane complex responds? What product accumulates? Does blocking that path erase the growth advantage while leaving general radioprotection intact?

Until those questions close, “energy capture” remains Class 2 or Class 4 depending on the exact claim.

That should not be read as hostility. It is an invitation to make the phenomenon harder to dismiss.

The strangest claims in biology become respectable when the accounting gets boring.
