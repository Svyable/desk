# The Energy Ledger

There is one experiment I want more than another photograph of a black colony beside a radiation symbol.

I want the ledger.

The radiotrophy claim has lived for almost twenty years in a zone where several true observations can be assembled into a stronger conclusion than any one experiment directly measured. Ionizing radiation changes measurable properties of melanin. Melanized fungi can be unusually radioresistant. In the 2007 Dadachova experiments, irradiation altered melanin electron-spin behavior, increased its measured capacity to participate in a NADH redox reaction, and under some conditions coincided with higher growth and carbon incorporation in melanized fungi. Those are serious results.

They are not yet an energy budget.

The distinction can be made brutally simple with units.

A gray is one joule of absorbed ionizing-radiation energy per kilogram of matter.

That definition is a gift to this problem because it lets us stop speaking metaphorically.

In the low-dose growth experiments reported in 2007, *Cryptococcus neoformans*, *Wangiella dermatitidis* and *Cladosporium sphaerospermum* were tested in a radiation field reported at roughly **0.05 milligray per hour**. Source: https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0000457

Convert that number before doing anything else.

0.05 mGy/hour is 0.00005 joule per kilogram per hour.

Across one day, a kilogram of perfectly absorbing material at that dose receives about 0.0012 joule.

A gram receives about 0.0000012 joule: **1.2 microjoules per day**.

That is the first number any strong claim of direct radiation-powered biomass production has to survive.

The exact energy delivered to fungal cells depends on geometry, composition, shielding, source spectrum and how much of the measured dose belongs to the cells rather than the surrounding medium. A proper experiment would measure that rather than assume it. But the order of magnitude already tells us something important. The radiation field used to produce the famous low-dose growth effect is not an enormous stream of harvestable energy. It is a very small energy input distributed through matter that is mostly not melanin and mostly not a metabolic reaction center.

This does not make the 2007 result uninteresting.

It makes the mechanism more interesting.

If irradiation truly causes substantially more biomass to appear while only microjoule-scale radiation energy reaches a gram-scale culture each day, then **direct conversion of incident gamma energy into the bulk chemical free energy of that extra biomass cannot simply be assumed**. The extra biomass is still built from carbon, nitrogen, oxygen, sulfur, phosphorus and conventional chemical substrates already present in the system. Radiation could alter how efficiently those substrates are used. It could reduce some metabolic cost. It could trigger signaling. It could change redox kinetics. It could protect enzymes or membranes. It could alter developmental state. It could act catalytically in a system whose actual energy comes from ordinary chemistry.

Those possibilities are not evasions. They are different hypotheses.

The word *radiotrophy* tends to collapse them.

## Four ledgers hiding inside one claim

A serious experiment needs at least four separate accounts.

### 1. The direct-energy ledger

This is the strongest version of the claim.

Ionizing radiation deposits energy. Melanin captures part of that deposition in a structured electronic state. That state drives electron transfer. Electron transfer feeds a metabolic gradient. The gradient supports ATP production, reductant formation, carbon fixation or another measurable unit of chemical work.

If this is happening, the experiment should be able to draw arrows from joules absorbed to joules of additional chemical potential.

The energy does not have to be captured efficiently. Photosynthesis is not perfectly efficient. Chemotrophy is not perfectly efficient. But the sign of the ledger has to make sense.

Measured radiation energy in must be large enough, after plausible efficiency losses, to account for the additional radiation-dependent chemical work attributed to it.

If the claimed additional work exceeds the measured radiation input by orders of magnitude, then radiation cannot be the primary energetic source of that work.

That does not mean radiation did nothing.

It means we move to the other ledgers.

### 2. The protection-savings ledger

Suppose melanin absorbs or quenches damaging radiation chemistry. A melanized cell may spend less ATP replacing proteins, repairing membranes, rebuilding redox buffers or repairing DNA.

The cell could therefore grow more under irradiation even if it harvested **zero** useful energy from the radiation.

The apparent energetic benefit would come from avoided cost.

This is conceptually similar to insulation. A well-insulated building does not obtain energy from cold weather. It loses less of the energy it already has.

A pigment that reduces oxidative damage can produce a growth advantage without becoming a power plant.

That distinction is experimentally accessible. Measure repair costs. Quantify DNA damage, oxidized proteins, membrane damage, antioxidant turnover and stress-response expression in melanized and pigment-deficient cells under the same absorbed dose. If the growth advantage tracks reduced damage burden, the protection-savings model gains strength.

### 3. The efficiency or catalytic ledger

The 2007 paper reported that irradiated melanin showed altered electronic properties and approximately a fourfold increase in its capacity to reduce NADH in the assay used by the investigators.

That is not trivial.

It raises a possibility more subtle than “gamma rays become sugar.”

Melanin could act as a redox mediator whose state changes under irradiation. If that altered state changes the rate of an existing metabolic reaction, the radiation may function more like a catalyst trigger or control input than a bulk energy source.

A tiny physical energy input can, in principle, change a much larger chemical flux if the system is already far from equilibrium and chemically fueled.

A transistor controls a much larger electrical current with a smaller control signal. A hormone can change the fate of large metabolic stores without supplying their energy. Light can trigger a signaling cascade whose downstream chemistry is powered by ATP made elsewhere.

If melanin behaves this way, the correct language may not be radiotrophy at all. It may be **radiation-sensitive metabolic regulation**.

That would still be remarkable biology.

It would also fit the energy scale more naturally.

### 4. The stress-response ledger

Low doses of damaging agents can sometimes produce responses that improve short-term growth or survival relative to an untreated condition. Cells activate repair, antioxidant, nutrient-scavenging or stress-adaptation programs. The resulting phenotype can look stimulatory even though the initiating agent is not a nutrient.

This is the hormesis problem.

A radiation-induced growth increase is not automatically evidence of energy capture any more than exercise-induced mitochondrial biogenesis means the mechanical energy of lifting a weight became mitochondrial fuel.

To distinguish this from melanin-specific transduction, the same experiment needs non-melanized controls, genetically rescued strains, chemically different melanins, alternative stressors and pathway-level measurements.

## What the 2007 study actually gave us

The strongest version of the historical paper deserves to be preserved rather than caricatured.

Dadachova and colleagues did not merely place a fungus near radiation and photograph it.

They reported changes in electron spin resonance of melanin after irradiation, consistent with altered electronic structure. They measured enhanced electron-transfer behavior in a NADH-related assay. They compared melanized and non-melanized forms. In *C. neoformans*, the irradiated melanized cells showed increased colony formation and roughly threefold greater incorporation of radiolabeled acetate than relevant controls under nutrient-limited conditions. They observed growth stimulation across more than one fungal species. Source: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0000457

That is enough to justify a mechanism program.

It is not enough to close one.

Carbon incorporation is especially easy to overread.

A cell incorporating more ^14C-acetate is building or processing more carbon-containing material. It does not tell us which energy source paid for that chemistry. The conventional substrate can still provide the free energy. Radiation can alter uptake, enzyme expression, redox state, membrane transport or allocation and thereby increase label incorporation without donating meaningful bulk energy.

The same caution applies to dry mass and colony-forming units.

More descendants demonstrate a biological effect. They do not identify its thermodynamic source.

## The 2014 modeling problem

A later paper tried to model radiation-induced fungal proliferation across X-ray energy and dose rate. The result is useful precisely because it does **not** look like a simple photovoltaic curve.

The authors described proliferation enhancement over some chronic low-dose regimes and growth inhibition at high dose rates, with effects dependent on melanization and X-ray energy. Source: https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0085561

That shape matters.

If fungal growth were powered primarily by harvested ionizing energy, one might naively expect more absorbed radiation—up to damage limits—to provide more usable energetic input. The observed biology is more complicated: thresholds, plateaus, pigment dependence and competing inhibition.

That is exactly what signaling, stress adaptation and damage tradeoffs often look like.

It does not refute energy transduction.

It tells us that **dose-response shape alone will not identify it**.

## The experiment that can finally close the account

Take a melanized fungus and an isogenic pigment-deficient control. Better yet, use three matched strains:

1. wild type melanized,
2. melanin-pathway knockout,
3. genetically rescued strain with melanization restored.

The rescue matters because pigmentation mutations can alter more than color. A knockout may accidentally change cell-wall structure, metabolism or stress signaling. Restoring the deleted pathway helps separate melanin-specific effects from unrelated consequences of genetic manipulation.

Grow all three in continuous culture under severe but controlled carbon limitation.

A chemostat is better than a plate for this question because input and output fluxes can be measured. Carbon source enters at a known rate. Biomass leaves at a known rate. Oxygen consumption and carbon dioxide production can be monitored continuously. The system can reach a steady state before irradiation begins.

Then measure the radiation correctly.

Do not report only source activity, distance or nominal field intensity. Use dosimetry inside the culture geometry. Determine absorbed dose in medium and, as far as technically possible, in fungal biomass. Model the fraction deposited near or within melanin-rich structures.

One gray is one joule per kilogram.

Keep converting back to joules.

Every time.

That prevents the word *radiation* from smuggling emotional magnitude into a physically tiny energy budget.

## Measurements that belong in the same run

### Conventional chemical input

Measure every carbon and energy-containing substrate entering the system.

Glucose, acetate, amino acids, lipids, trace organics in media additives—anything the fungus could oxidize has to be accounted for. A supposedly carbon-limited experiment can be ruined by an overlooked rich component.

### Gas exchange

Measure oxygen consumption and carbon dioxide production.

If irradiation increases conventional respiration, the extra biomass may simply be powered by greater oxidation of ordinary substrate.

That would still require explanation. It would not be direct radiotrophy.

### Biomass

Measure dry mass, cell number, viable counts and composition.

A cell can swell without synthesizing proportionate new biomass. Colony-forming units can rise because survival improves. Dry mass can change because storage compounds accumulate.

No single growth measure is enough.

### Heat

Use calorimetry if the geometry permits it.

Metabolism produces heat. If irradiated melanized cells do more chemical work, energy balance should eventually appear somewhere in heat and biomass.

### ATP and nucleotide ratios

Measure ATP/ADP/AMP, NADH/NAD+, NADPH/NADP+ and glutathione redox state.

A claim centered on melanin electron transfer should eventually connect to recognizable metabolic currencies.

### Isotope flux

Use ^13C-labeled conventional substrate and possibly other tracers to determine where carbon goes.

The question is not only whether more label appears in cells. It is whether irradiation changes pathway flux in a way consistent with a specific energetic mechanism.

### Repair burden

Measure DNA lesions, protein oxidation, lipid peroxidation and stress-response expenditure.

This is how protection savings gets separated from direct energy capture.

## The decisive control: dead melanin

If irradiated melanin has useful redox properties independent of living metabolism, purified or cell-wall-bound melanin should show measurable radiation-dependent electron-transfer chemistry outside a living cell.

That chemistry can be characterized directly.

Then the living-cell experiment can ask whether the same redox signature appears in vivo and connects to metabolism.

Dead melanized biomass is also an important control for radiation shielding. If living and dead melanin reduce damage similarly but only living melanized cells gain growth, then shielding alone is not enough.

## Dose rate versus total dose

Another trap is to treat total dose as the only meaningful radiation variable.

Biology cares about **dose rate**.

The same total energy delivered over one minute and one week can produce very different chemistry because radicals recombine, repair occurs, antioxidant systems recover and cells change state between events.

A radiotrophic mechanism might depend on continuous low-level perturbation rather than total deposited energy. A damage mechanism might dominate at high dose rate. The experiment should therefore cross total dose and dose rate rather than varying one while confounding the other.

This also makes the energy claim stricter.

If the biological effect turns on at a tiny dose rate and then saturates while radiation energy keeps increasing, that looks more like a control signal than an energy supply.

If additional chemical work scales quantitatively with absorbed radiation energy over a meaningful range, the direct-energy hypothesis becomes more plausible.

## Radiation quality

Gamma rays are not X-rays are not protons are not heavy ions.

Equal absorbed dose does not mean identical microscopic ionization structure.

A mechanism based on melanin electronic excitation may respond differently to photon energy and track structure. A mechanism based mainly on generalized oxidative stress may follow a different pattern.

The field should exploit that distinction.

Use multiple radiation qualities while matching absorbed dose. If the effect tracks physical energy deposition independent of radiation type, one model gains support. If it tracks particular ionization patterns or melanin absorption properties, another does.

## The impossible interpretation test

Before any molecular story is accepted, perform the crude bound.

At 0.05 mGy/hour, a one-gram sample absorbs on the order of a microjoule per day if the nominal dose applies uniformly to that mass.

Now compare that with the measured additional chemical work or biomass energy associated with irradiation.

The comparison does not have to be exact to be useful.

If extra chemical work is millions of times larger than incident absorbed radiation energy, then direct bulk energy harvesting cannot be the explanation. Radiation must be changing how the cell uses another energy source.

If the numbers are of the same order after realistic efficiency losses, direct capture remains alive.

If the measured effect is smaller than the available radiation energy but no pathway can be identified, the energetic hypothesis remains plausible but incomplete.

This one calculation would clean up years of language.

## A better vocabulary

The field may eventually need more than one word.

**Radioprotection** — radiation damage is reduced.

**Radiostimulation** — growth or metabolism increases under irradiation.

**Radioregulation** — radiation changes signaling or metabolic flux using energy supplied mainly by conventional chemistry.

**Radio-redox mediation** — radiation changes melanin's electron-transfer state in a way that couples into cell chemistry.

**Radiotrophic energy transduction** — a measurable fraction of absorbed radiation energy becomes biologically useful chemical work.

Only the last one earns the strongest analogy to phototrophy.

The others are not consolation prizes.

A polymer that survives intense radiation, protects cells, changes electronic state under irradiation and modulates metabolism would be extraordinary even if it contributes essentially zero net joules to growth.

That version could matter for space biology, radiation shielding, biotechnology and the evolution of life in high-radiation environments.

## Why the ledger is kinder than the slogan

The phrase “radiation-eating fungus” makes the claim sound easy to understand and easy to ridicule.

The ledger makes it harder.

If direct energy harvesting fails, the experiment does not collapse into nothing. It tells us whether the real phenomenon is shielding, signaling, redox mediation, repair savings or stress adaptation.

If direct energy harvesting survives, it emerges far stronger than it entered.

It would have a measured input, an efficiency, a pathway and a product.

That is how strange biology becomes ordinary biology.

Not by making the claim less strange.

By making the accounting boring enough that the strangeness has nowhere left to hide.
