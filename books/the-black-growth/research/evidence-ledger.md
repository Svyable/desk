# Four-Class Evidence Ledger

## Class 1 — verified / documented

- Fungi, including melanized species, colonized inner Chernobyl reactor-shelter structures by the late 1990s.
- Ionizing radiation can alter measured ESR/electronic properties of fungal melanin in laboratory experiments.
- The 2007 study reported increased NADH-reducing capacity of irradiated melanin and radiation-associated growth differences in specified melanized fungal systems.
- Melanin provides radioprotection in some experimentally tested fungi and dose regimes.
- Some fungal spores tolerate extremely high laboratory radiation doses.
- NASA and other space-research groups have studied fungal melanin and melanized organisms for radiation-survival or shielding applications.
- Later studies have produced null or species-dependent results for radiation-enhanced growth and melanin-dependent survival.

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete

- Melanin may act as more than passive shielding by changing biologically relevant electron-transfer behavior under ionizing radiation.
- Radiation-enhanced growth may occur in particular species, nutrient states and dose regimes.
- Some fungi may exhibit genuine radiotropism under controlled conditions.
- Living or biomimetic melanized materials may provide useful regenerative components of space-radiation shielding.

## Class 3 — disputed / unconfirmed

- Melanized fungi generally “eat” gamma radiation.
- Chernobyl fungi evolved a novel radiation metabolism after the 1986 accident.
- Melanin alone explains the extraordinary radioresistance of black fungi.
- Reported radiotropism is universal, uniquely melanin-dependent, or equivalent to movement toward a food source.
- Existing studies establish a photosynthesis-like pathway converting ionizing radiation into cellular free energy.

## Class 4 — speculative synthesis

- Fungal melanin may occupy a continuum among shield, radical quencher, redox mediator and sensory interface, with different roles dominating in different dose/nutrient regimes.
- A true radiotrophic pathway, if it exists, may become detectable only under severe conventional-energy limitation where tiny radiation-derived fluxes matter.
- Radiation selection may favor integrated pigment-plus-repair phenotypes rather than a single “radiotrophy gene.”
- Similar chemistry could widen habitable niches on radioactive or high-particle-flux worlds, but direct energy capture and indirect radiolysis-fed ecosystems must be kept separate.
