# Defeat Conditions

The strong radiotrophy hypothesis should be considered defeated, or at least unsupported, if well-powered multicenter studies show all of the following:

1. radiation-enhanced growth disappears under artifact-controlled replication;
2. any residual growth benefit is fully explained by reduced damage or repair cost rather than an added metabolic energy flux;
3. melanin’s irradiation-induced redox changes cannot be coupled reproducibly to cellular electron flow or useful chemical work;
4. isogenic melanin loss and rescue fail to alter the putative energetic phenotype;
5. apparent radiotropism vanishes when thermal, optical, electrostatic and handling gradients are blinded and controlled;
6. energetic accounting shows the observed biomass differences are incompatible with radiation being a meaningful input.

Conversely, the hypothesis strengthens only if a melanin-dependent metabolic flux scales with absorbed radiation, survives independent replication, closes energetically, and can be interrupted at a defined mechanistic step.
