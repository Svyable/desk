# Chronology

- **1986** — Chernobyl Unit 4 accident creates a highly contaminated, abandoned industrial ecosystem.
- **1990s** — Studies of fungi in contaminated Chernobyl zones report melanized populations and radiation-oriented growth phenomena.
- **1997–1998** — Extensive fungal growth sampled inside the Shelter; later published as Zhdanova et al. 2000.
- **2000** — Chernobyl reactor-shelter mycobiota survey published.
- **2007** — Dadachova et al. report irradiation-induced melanin electronic/redox changes and enhanced growth of several melanized fungi under specified conditions.
- **2008** — Experimental work links fungal melanin radioprotection to chemistry, radicals and spatial arrangement.
- **2017** — Casadevall and collaborators review melanin, radiation and possible energy transduction.
- **2020** — Space-radiation work shows high resistance of *Aspergillus niger* spores; biomimetic fungal-melanin shielding analysis published; *Exophiala* studies complicate simple melanin-protection narratives and explore radioadaptation/evolution.
- **2022** — Sandia-associated study finds irradiation-dependent pigmentation changes but no significant growth-rate increase in two tested fungi.
- **2020s** — NASA continues research on fungal melanin, DNA repair and melanin-containing shielding concepts for exploration environments.
- **Next** — The missing experiment is a preregistered absorbed-dose-to-metabolic-free-energy ledger across melanized, pigment-deficient and rescued strains.
