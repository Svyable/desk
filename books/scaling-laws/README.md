How Size Changes the Rules.

The working thesis is simple: scale is not just more. A system that becomes ten times larger often becomes a different kind of system. Costs bend, bottlenecks move, coordination changes shape, rare events become ordinary, and yesterday’s intuition quietly stops working.

This book follows scaling laws from biology, cities, networks and factories into computation, artificial intelligence, organizations, infrastructure and markets. It treats log-log plots and exponents not as mathematical decoration but as instruments for noticing when reality refuses to grow in a straight line.

The argument is also a warning. Scaling laws describe regularities; they do not grant permission to extrapolate forever. Every curve lives inside a regime. The important practical skill is learning both how to see the curve and how to notice when the world has changed underneath it.
