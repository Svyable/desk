# Reporting Map — The Retry Generation

The purpose of reporting is not to decorate the manuscript with authority. It is to pressure-test the framework, find counterexamples, replace generic illustrations with memorable reality, and make the trade edition more useful without pretending the book is a scientific monograph.

## Reporting standard

For each chapter, seek four things where available:

1. **A human scene** — a person or institution living the retry/irreversibility tension.
2. **An empirical anchor** — research, administrative data, or documented practice that helps bound the claim.
3. **A counterexample** — a case where more retries help, or where finality is harmful, so the argument cannot become a sermon.
4. **A design implication** — what a reader, parent, teacher, leader, builder, or institution should do differently.

Do not force all four into every chapter if the evidence is weak. Strong absence is better than weak sourcing.

## Prologue — The Button That Follows You

**Reporting target:** assemble a concrete sequence of retry interfaces across ordinary life—undo, respawn, reroute, retake, return, edit, regenerate—without claiming they share one causal origin.

**Need:** a vivid opening scene with a real person moving between several reversible systems in one day, or a documented product-history sequence showing how reversal became normal interface grammar.

## Chapter 1 — Undo Becomes a Habit

**Questions:** What do psychology and human-computer interaction actually show about reversibility, exploration, error cost, and willingness to act? When does undo increase experimentation? When can easy reversal reduce deliberation?

**Counterweight:** reversible systems often improve creativity, safety, and learning. The chapter should make that benefit concrete before discussing transfer into irreversible domains.

## Chapter 2 — Respawn Childhood

**Questions:** What do mastery learning, retakes, game-based learning, error-management training, and developmental research say about repeated attempts? Where do educators distinguish practice from assessment and assessment from live responsibility?

**Reporting targets:** teachers using retake systems well; teachers who abandoned poorly designed retakes; game designers thinking about failure loops; parents teaching real-world consequence without punitive absolutism.

## Chapter 3 — Regenerate Everything

**Questions:** How does abundant generation affect selection, revision, authorship, and perceived effort? What evidence exists around generative tools and learning transfer rather than merely output quality?

**Reporting targets:** writers, designers, programmers, or students who can show a before/after workflow; an expert who intentionally limits generation to preserve judgment.

**Guardrail:** avoid volatile benchmark theater. The chapter is about abundance of alternatives and human selection, not which model is currently best.

## Chapter 4 — The Retake Economy

**Questions:** How do retry opportunities differ by money, time, geography, institutional access, criminal/legal status, and social support? Which second-chance institutions demonstrably improve reentry or learning, and where can repeated chances become costly or unfair?

**Reporting targets:** adult education, bankruptcy/restructuring, credential retakes, occupational reentry, restorative processes, appeals.

**Core test:** who gets another attempt, how long does it take, and who pays while the attempt is pending?

## Chapter 5 — Love Without Save Points

**Questions:** What does relationship research distinguish between repairable conflict, repeated harmful patterns, trust repair, and incompatibility? How should the manuscript avoid medicalizing ordinary friction or romanticizing endurance?

**Reporting target:** relationship professionals or mediators who can speak to repair without promising reset.

**Guardrail:** no prescriptive mental-health or abuse advice beyond well-supported general framing; preserve the distinction between repair and a duty to remain.

## Chapter 6 — A Life in Beta

**Questions:** When does optionality help careers and when does it prevent compounding? What do labor economists, career researchers, craftspeople, founders, and vocational educators say about exploration versus depth?

**Reporting targets:** one successful reinvention; one person whose repeated restarting imposed hidden costs; one institution that makes career retries less catastrophic.

## Chapter 7 — Machines Get Infinite Tries. We Don't.

**Questions:** In automated decisions, where can systems rerun cheaply while human consequences accumulate during delay? What do appeals, false positives, incident response, safety engineering, and human-factors research teach about reversibility?

**Reporting targets:** high-stakes decision systems with documented appeal/review pathways; engineers designing rollback, human override, or staged deployment; affected users where public documentation or consented reporting permits.

**Core measure:** time-to-repair matters. A nominal appeal that arrives after the consequential window may not function as a real retry.

## Chapter 8 — The Courage of One-Way Doors

**Questions:** What makes commitment rational under uncertainty? How do people prepare for transformative choices that cannot be fully trialed in advance?

**Reporting targets:** irreversible professional, family, civic, creative, or expedition decisions; safety systems that use deliberate friction before high-consequence actions.

**Counterweight:** some apparent one-way doors are socially manufactured and should be made more reversible.

## Chapter 9 — The Undoable Self

**Questions:** What does research on identity change, rehabilitation, desistance, reputation, and moral repair say about continuity through change? How can institutions recognize trajectories without erasing history?

**Reporting targets:** people who changed substantially but still had to answer for prior conduct; systems that evaluate patterns over time rather than freezing a person at one point.

**Core distinction:** growth changes the pattern; rebranding changes the description.

## Chapter 10 — The Returns Department

**Questions:** What are the real operational, financial, labor, and environmental consequences of returns and cancellations? How do organizations design cancellation windows, deposits, waitlists, and return policies to balance flexibility with scarce capacity?

**Reporting targets:** retail returns operations, hospitality/reservation systems, appointment scheduling, contractors, logistics teams.

**AI extension:** investigate whether automated agents increase speculative reservations, ordering, or option holding; treat this as a hypothesis unless current evidence supports a stronger claim.

## Chapter 11 — The Last Human Draft

**Questions:** Which forms of difficulty create learning and which are merely waste? What do cognitive apprenticeship, desirable-difficulty research, worked examples, tutoring, and human-AI collaboration show about timing assistance?

**Reporting targets:** educators who explicitly separate output tasks from formation tasks; professionals who use AI aggressively after foundational capability exists; systems designed to delay hints or require an attempt before assistance.

**Core test:** what capability remains when the scaffold is reduced?

## Chapter 12 — The Right to Be Forgotten by Yourself

**Questions:** What do memory science, grief research, digital-reminder design, archival practice, and privacy/product design say about resurfacing versus preservation? When are reminders helpful, and when can they be intrusive?

**Reporting targets:** people managing large personal archives; bereavement technologists or grief researchers; product designers responsible for memory resurfacing; archivists who distinguish storage from display.

**Guardrail:** keep “right to be forgotten by yourself” as a cultural/design concept unless discussing a specific legal doctrine with jurisdiction and date made explicit.

## Epilogue — One Life, No Version History

No evidence dump. Return to mortality, time, and the central ethic after the reported chapters have earned the argument.

## Cross-book source families

Prioritize primary research, systematic reviews, official administrative sources, first-person reporting, technical standards, and direct practitioner interviews. Use secondary commentary to locate debates, not as a substitute for the strongest available source.

Likely source families include human-computer interaction and error recovery; learning science and mastery; game design and failure loops; behavioral decision-making and choice; logistics/returns operations; labor and career mobility; restorative justice and appeals; human factors and safety engineering; automated-decision recourse; identity/rehabilitation; memory and forgetting; grief and digital legacy.

## Publication threshold

Reporting is sufficient when it changes the manuscript—not when a bibliography becomes long. The trade edition should contain scenes, limits, and counterexamples strong enough that the framework survives contact with people who have reasons to disagree with it.
