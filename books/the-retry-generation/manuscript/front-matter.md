# The Retry Generation

*What Happens When a World Built on Undo Meets a Life That Cannot Be Replayed*

**Sven Hardy Benson**

---

For everyone who has ever reached for a button that was not there.

---

## Author's Note

This is a work of cultural argument. It names a pattern rather than a demographic cohort with fixed birth years. “The Retry Generation” includes anyone whose expectations have been shaped by environments in which mistakes can routinely be reversed, regenerated, retaken, edited, restored, restarted, rerouted, refunded, or replayed.

The examples in this book move across games, software, school, work, relationships, consumer life, artificial intelligence, medicine, politics, and the physical world. Some scenes are composites created to make recurring patterns visible. They are not presented as biographies of identifiable people.

The argument is not that retries are bad. The ability to try again is one of civilization's great achievements. The argument is that a culture can become so good at engineering reversibility that it forgets how to prepare people for the irreversible.

That forgotten skill is the subject of this book.
