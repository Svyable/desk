# Back Matter

## A Short Field Guide to the Retry Generation

**Reversible thinking**  
Act, inspect, reverse, repeat. A powerful way to learn inside systems designed to restore prior states.

**Consequence literacy**  
The ability to recognize whether a decision belongs in a sandbox, workshop, or cliff — and who bears the cost if it goes wrong.

**Sandbox**  
A place where mistakes should be deliberately cheap because the purpose is learning.

**Workshop**  
A place where consequences are real but repair, accountability, and improvement are expected.

**Cliff**  
A place where some consequences cannot be meaningfully restored and preparation must happen before action.

**Retry inflation**  
The tendency to consume more attempts as attempts become cheaper, eventually making selection and commitment more scarce than production.

**Retry infrastructure**  
Institutions that make selected forms of failure survivable enough for people to learn, recover, and reenter without pretending the first attempt never happened.

**Retry asymmetry**  
A condition in which a system, company, or machine can rerun or roll back more easily than the human being affected by its decision can recover the consequence.

**Option obedience**  
The point at which preserving options stops serving a life and begins preventing chosen possibilities from becoming real.

**Integration**  
The alternative to both shame and erasure: the event remains true, but future conduct changes what the event means.

**One-way door**  
A decision or event after which the world contains the fact that it happened, even when some practical repair remains possible.

## Ten Questions for a Retry World

1. Is this a sandbox, workshop, or cliff?
2. What exactly can be reversed here — the technical state, the money, the relationship, the body, the time?
3. Who pays for my learning if I am wrong?
4. Does the institution get another attempt that the affected person does not?
5. Is another version giving me information or helping me avoid ownership?
6. Am I preserving an option I actually value, or obeying optionality itself?
7. Is this friction evidence of damage, or the normal cost of depth?
8. What preparation is possible before this one-way door?
9. If I cannot undo the event, how can I repair or integrate it?
10. What deserves my full attention precisely because I do not get the hour back?

## The Rule

**Mercy for the reversible. Reverence for the irreversible.**

Make cheap mistakes cheap.

Make learning safe enough to be honest.

Build real second chances.

Preserve memory so retries become iteration rather than repetition.

And when a consequence belongs to someone else's body, liberty, trust, childhood, future, or life, make the threshold look different before it is crossed.
