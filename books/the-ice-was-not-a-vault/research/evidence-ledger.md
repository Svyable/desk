# Evidence ledger — The Ice Was Not a Vault

This ledger is intentionally stricter than the prose. A good Cold War story can survive a missing adjective. A nuclear-history claim cannot.

## Class 1 — verified / declassified / documented

### Camp Century existed as a U.S. Army under-ice installation

Elmer F. Clark, **Camp Century: Evolution of Concept and History of Design, Construction, and Performance**, CRREL Technical Report 174, October 1965. U.S. Army Materiel Command / Cold Regions Research & Engineering Laboratory. USACE record: https://usace.contentdm.oclc.org/digital/collection/p266001coll1/id/4529/ ; GovInfo PDF: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-D103-PURL-gpo86535/pdf/GOVPUB-D103-PURL-gpo86535.pdf

Use: construction sequence, trench geometry, engineering assumptions, deformation, logistics, nuclear-power argument. This is an Army retrospective and must not be used as an independent evaluation of strategic success.

### Camp Century was constructed in 1959–60 and later abandoned

Modern scientific history: Rittenour et al., **Scientific history, sampling approach, and physical characterization of the Camp Century subglacial material**, *The Cryosphere* 18 (2024), 4029–4057: https://tc.copernicus.org/articles/18/4029/2024/

The paper places construction beginning June 1959, completion in October 1960, year-round occupation through 1964, later seasonal operation, and final abandonment in 1967. It reports tunnel deformation rates on the order of 0.1–1.0 m/yr in the scientific/engineering program. Use the primary Army reports for exact contemporary figures where possible.

### Project Iceworm was a real Army missile-basing concept, not an operational arsenal

Erik D. Weiss, **Cold War Under the Ice: The Army's Bid for a Long-Range Nuclear Role, 1959–1963**, *Journal of Cold War Studies* 3(3), 2001, 31–58. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1162/152039701750419501

Nikolaj Petersen, **The Iceman That Never Came: ‘Project Iceworm’, the search for a NATO deterrent, and Denmark, 1960–1962**, *Scandinavian Journal of History* 33(1), 2008, 75–98. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1080/03468750701449554

Petersen's abstract describes an Army design for mobile deployment of up to 600 MRBMs under the Greenland ice cap and states that the project was never broached to the Danes; it failed for technical and organizational reasons. This is secondary scholarship based heavily on declassified archival records. Next pass must pull the underlying RG 59/PPS material rather than laundering the secondary account into “declassified documents say.”

### The 600-missile scale is a proposal denominator, not a deployment count

Colgan et al. (2016) summarize the 1962 Army proposal as a subsurface railway under approximately 1.3 × 10^5 km² of ice supporting 600 ballistic missiles. https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/2016GL069688

Boundary: never write “600 missiles beneath Greenland” without “proposed,” “planned,” “envisioned,” or equivalent.

### PM-2A nuclear power was real; abandoned warheads are not established

Clark TR-174 and the Camp Century technical record document portable nuclear power at the station. Modern scientific histories state that the reactor operated until 1963/64 and was removed. Colgan et al. state that the reaction chamber was removed for destructive testing while infrastructure and wastes remained.

Boundary: the presence of a reactor does not imply deployed nuclear weapons. “Nuclear city” can describe power source or strategic context, but the manuscript should prefer exact nouns.

### The camp produced a bed-reaching ice core and subglacial archive

Rittenour et al. (2024): the last of three Camp Century cores, collected 1963–1966, recovered about 3.5 m of subglacial material. The archive was later subdivided; two segments are unaccounted for, two were thawed, and surviving material has been resampled under modern contamination controls.

This is a remarkable chain-of-custody story and should be narrated with the missing/thawed segments intact rather than cleaned up for drama.

### Buried waste is real; present/future mobilization timing is modeled

William Colgan et al., **The abandoned ice sheet base at Camp Century, Greenland, in a warming climate**, *Geophysical Research Letters* 43 (2016), 8091–8096. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1002/2016GL069688

The paper inventories approximately 9.2 × 10^3 t physical waste, estimated 2.0 × 10^5 L diesel, at least 2.4 × 10^7 L grey water/sewage, probable PCB-bearing materials, and radiological waste associated with reactor coolant disposal. It modeled possible future transition from net accumulation to net ablation under RCP8.5.

Boundary: this is a 2016 scenario study, not a 2026 observation that waste is emerging. Any future-dated claim must be updated against the current Camp Century Climate Monitoring Programme.

### Radar detects the debris field

Karlsson et al., **Ice-penetrating radar survey of the subsurface debris field at Camp Century, Greenland**, *Cold Regions Science and Technology* (2019), DOI landing page: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.coldregions.2019.102788

The radar work describes a roughly circular subsurface debris field with radius <1 km, downwarping associated with collapsed sumps, and polarity evidence consistent with liquid hydrocarbons in one sump. Boundary: “consistent with” is not direct chemical sampling.

### Monitoring is institutional, not merely speculative

Camp Century Climate Monitoring Programme, led by the Geological Survey of Denmark and Greenland (GEUS): https://www.campcenturyclimate.dk/heritage.html

GEUS publication: **Initial field activities of the Camp Century Climate Monitoring Programme in Greenland** (2018): https://pub.geus.dk/en/publications/initial-field-activities-of-the-camp-century-climate-monitoring-p/

Use for modern site coordinates, monitoring history, and the political fact that Denmark/Greenland have treated the site as a live monitoring problem.

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete

### What exactly did Danish officials know, and when?

Petersen's archival work and the Danish Cold War investigation are strong but the knowledge chain is not a one-line morality play. Camp Century itself was known and involved Danish liaison; Iceworm's missile concept was not simply equivalent to the visible camp. The manuscript must distinguish Danish knowledge of the installation, knowledge of nuclear power, broader nuclear-policy ambiguity, and knowledge/consent concerning the specific missile deployment concept.

### Why Iceworm died

Ice deformation is central to this book, but it is not allowed to become the sole cause by rhetorical repetition. Interservice rivalry, evolution of U.S. strategic missile systems, political consent, logistics, cost, survivability, command/control and the Army's institutional bid for a strategic nuclear role all belong in the causal ledger.

### Current condition of every waste stream

Radar can constrain geometry and infer some materials; historical records can inventory disposal; climate models can project future transport. None of those equals direct present-day sampling of every tank, sump and tunnel. Maintain those distinctions.

## Class 3 — disputed / unconfirmed

- Operational deployment of hundreds of nuclear missiles under Greenland: **unsupported by the public record used here.**
- Nuclear warheads abandoned at Camp Century: **unsupported.**
- A hidden continuation of Iceworm after documented rejection/abandonment: **unsupported.**
- Any claim that Danish ignorance was total across all Camp Century activity: **too broad.**
- Any claim that the public scientific program was fake: **false framing; substantial science and engineering were real.**

## Class 4 — speculative synthesis / open conjecture

### The ice-as-geology category error

The book argues that Iceworm's deepest mistake was conceptual: strategic infrastructure wants a stable coordinate system, while an ice sheet supplies flow, accumulation, deformation, thermal history and eventually climate-driven change. This is interpretation, not a phrase found in the archival record.

### Science as the accidental survivor

The military project failed, but its drilling produced an archive still yielding paleoclimate information. The book treats this as an inversion of intended value: the “side effect” outlived the weapon. This is interpretive synthesis grounded in documented custody and subsequent science.

### Secrecy has a half-life; waste does not respect it

Classification can expire on institutional timescales. Buried material persists on physical ones. This is a framing device, not a quantitative law.

## Contradictions and incentive audit

**Army engineering reports:** incentive to demonstrate feasibility, competence and future utility. A 1965 conclusion that subsurface camps are feasible must be read beside maintenance burden and deformation data.

**Army strategic planning:** institutional incentive to obtain a long-range nuclear role during intense service competition. “Requirement” and “feasibility” language may carry bureaucratic advocacy.

**Danish political record:** incentives include alliance management, domestic anti-nuclear policy, sovereignty and later accountability. Ambiguity can be strategic rather than accidental.

**Greenlandic perspective:** avoid treating Greenland as empty terrain exchanged between Washington and Copenhagen. Sovereignty, local communities, colonial/postcolonial governance and environmental burden are not scenery.

**Climate/waste literature:** dramatic future exposure scenarios attract attention, but the 2016 RCP8.5 result is scenario-dependent. Update against observations and newer models before publication.

**Popular retellings:** routinely collapse “planned 600 missiles” into “600 missiles,” “nuclear-powered” into “nuclear-armed,” and “cover” into “fake science.” Reject all three compressions.

## Chronology

- **1951** — U.S.–Denmark Defense of Greenland Agreement establishes the Cold War framework for American facilities.
- **1959** — Camp Century construction begins in northwestern Greenland.
- **1960** — Major construction completed; public engineering/scientific identity established.
- **1961–1963** — PM-2A provides nuclear power during the core year-round period.
- **1962** — Army advances the Iceworm missile-network concept after under-ice engineering/rail experimentation.
- **1963** — Iceworm rejected in the strategic decision process described by Weiss/Petersen; reactor era ends around this period.
- **1964** — year-round Camp Century occupation ends.
- **1966** — deep drilling reaches the bed and recovers subglacial material.
- **1967** — seasonal operations end; site abandoned with minimal decommissioning.
- **1969** — last reported USACE visit in the abandonment-era record cited by Colgan et al.
- **1990s** — declassification/Danish investigation brings Iceworm into wider public documentary history.
- **2011/2014** — airborne radar observations later used to characterize buried infrastructure.
- **2016** — Colgan et al. publish climate/waste remobilization analysis.
- **2017 onward** — dedicated Camp Century climate monitoring develops.
- **2019** — radar debris-field analysis published.
- **2021–2024** — renewed analyses and formal resampling histories turn the basal archive into a modern multiproxy scientific object.
