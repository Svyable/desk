# The Ice Was Not a Vault

## Project Iceworm, Camp Century, and the Cold War Bet That Climate Would Hold Still

**Sven Hardy Benson**

### Thesis

Camp Century is usually told as a wonderfully literal Cold War secret: the United States dug a nuclear-powered town into Greenland while the Army studied a much larger hidden missile network called Project Iceworm. The stranger and more durable story is that the project depended on treating an ice sheet as if it were geology. It was not. The same moving ice that defeated the military architecture preserved an accidental scientific archive, buried the abandoned infrastructure, and now forces a second argument about waste, sovereignty, climate and responsibility. The machine failed because its landscape was alive on engineering timescales; its scientific residue matters because that landscape is alive on climatic timescales.

This book is about the category error.

It does **not** claim that 600 nuclear missiles were deployed beneath Greenland. They were not. It does not treat Camp Century's public scientific work as fictitious merely because Iceworm was secret. The science was real, productive and in some cases extraordinary. Nor does it claim that Camp Century's buried waste is presently erupting from the ice. Modern monitoring exists precisely because timing and transport remain empirical questions.

The book instead follows four things that were true at once: a public engineering-and-science station existed; a secret missile-basing concept used it as a feasibility laboratory; the ice mechanically deformed the built environment faster than the strategic fantasy could tolerate; and the drilling program left a scientific archive whose value has outlived the weapons idea by decades.

### Freshness check

At run start, the five newest Author-agent empirical families were Project AZORIAN/deep-ocean retrieval (#1933), earthquake precursors (#1929), Vela 6911/nuclear forensics (#1925), GENETRIX/reconnaissance balloons (#1921), and animal intelligence/marine mammals (#1917). Default-branch search returned no substantive manuscript centered on Camp Century, Project Iceworm, PM-2A, Greenland under-ice construction, or the Camp Century basal core.

This book therefore exits those five families. Its empirical core is cryospheric engineering, nuclear basing, Danish/Greenlandic sovereignty, abandoned military waste, and the scientific afterlife of a Cold War ice core.

### Evidence classes

1. **Verified / declassified / documented:** Camp Century construction and operation; U.S. Army engineering reports; PM-2A operation and removal; Project Iceworm planning; the 600-missile concept as a proposal rather than deployment; Camp Century drilling; abandonment; modern radar and climate monitoring; preservation and reanalysis of basal material.
2. **Credible but incomplete:** exact political knowledge and interpretation within Danish and U.S. institutions at particular moments; the full causal weight of ice deformation versus interservice, missile-technology and diplomatic factors in Iceworm's death; exact present distribution and condition of all buried wastes.
3. **Disputed / unconfirmed:** claims that an operational Iceworm missile arsenal was secretly installed; claims of nuclear warheads abandoned at Camp Century; claims that the project continued as a hidden operational missile system after the documented program ended.
4. **Speculative synthesis / open conjecture:** the book's argument that Iceworm is best understood as a failed attempt to turn a dynamic climate system into strategic infrastructure, and that its scientific afterlife is an unusually clean case of knowledge surviving the purpose that funded it.

### Defeat condition

The central thesis weakens materially if primary records show that ice deformation was operationally trivial to the basing concept, if Camp Century was not meaningfully connected to Iceworm feasibility work, or if modern glaciological work shows the site's climatic/waste afterlife to be negligible. The manuscript is written so those findings can be incorporated without protecting the thesis.
