## Reader promise

This book reconstructs the documented U.S. intelligence search for behavioral control from BLUEBIRD and ARTICHOKE through MKULTRA, then asks a less cinematic question than whether the CIA ever discovered a reliable mind-control switch: what useful knowledge emerged when an institution spent years trying to make human autonomy fail on command? The answer must be separated from mythology, later interrogation doctrine, ordinary pharmacology, coercion, suggestion, sleep loss, sensory manipulation, and the immense evidentiary damage caused when CIA leadership ordered records destroyed.

The governing tension is uncomfortable. A failed search for programmable minds can still produce operational knowledge about disorientation, dependency, memory, compliance, interrogation, and institutional impunity. That is not the same thing as successful mind control. The book will keep those propositions apart.
