# ACTIONABLE ANOMALY
## Why the Government's Psychic Program Failed the Test It Was Built to Pass

By Sven Hardy Benson

## Thesis

The strangest fact about the U.S. government's remote-viewing program is not that intelligence agencies investigated psychic functioning. It is that after decades of work, laboratory results and operational usefulness diverged. In the 1995 CIA-commissioned review, Jessica Utts argued that the experimental evidence showed anomalous information transfer beyond chance; Ray Hyman argued that methodological and replication problems prevented that conclusion. The operational review was less romantic: the product was too vague, inconsistent, and dependent on interpretation to justify intelligence use.

This book lives inside that split. It asks a harder question than whether psi is `real`: **what must an anomaly become before a decision-maker is rationally allowed to act on it?**

The distinction matters far beyond parapsychology. A statistically anomalous signal can be scientifically interesting while remaining operationally worthless. A sensor can beat chance while failing to identify a target. A phenomenon can survive aggregate statistics while disappearing when asked to tell you which door to open. Intelligence work, medicine, forecasting, AI evaluation, anomaly detection and UAP analysis all eventually face the same conversion problem: significance is not specificity; effect size is not utility; information is not action.

## Evidence classes

1. **Verified / declassified / documented:** U.S. government sponsorship of remote-viewing research; SRI/SAIC and military/intelligence involvement; operational tasking; the 1995 AIR review; the Utts/Hyman disagreement; CIA's decision not to restore the program; declassified session and tasking records.
2. **Credible but incomplete:** claims that particular laboratory protocols produced above-chance performance; evaluator and participant accounts of unusually successful sessions; end-user assessments whose denominators and scoring conditions require reconstruction.
3. **Disputed / unconfirmed:** claims that remote viewing located hostages, weapons, individuals, facilities or other intelligence targets in a way that materially changed operations; claims that the program proved clairvoyance, precognition or any particular mechanism.
4. **Speculative synthesis:** the `actionability gap`—the possibility that some anomalous-cognition experiments could contain weak information while still being structurally incapable of becoming a reliable intelligence instrument. This is a hypothesis, not a concession that psi exists.

## Defeat condition

The book's central `actionability gap` thesis weakens substantially if preregistered, independently replicated remote-viewing experiments can repeatedly deliver prospective, target-specific decisions with economically or operationally meaningful utility under blinded conditions and with the decision rule fixed before target revelation. Conversely, failure of such a test would not prove every historical statistical anomaly fraudulent; it would constrain what those anomalies are allowed to mean.
