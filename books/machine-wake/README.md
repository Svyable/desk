## Premise

Every useful machine act leaves something behind. A model answer leaves heat in a cooling loop, load on a grid, traces in a log, permissions in an identity system, retries in a queue, synthetic material in an information environment, work for people who evaluate and repair it, records for regulators, and eventually hardware that must be replaced. *Machine Wake* follows that residue. Its central argument is that the durable consequences of artificial intelligence will be found not only in what machines produce, but in the systems required to absorb, interpret, secure, clean up, preserve, and sometimes forget what their work leaves behind.

The wake is not automatically waste. Logs can make a system accountable. Provenance can make an artifact trustworthy. Failed attempts can expose weak tools. Human review can catch harms. Waste heat can sometimes be reused. The practical question is whether the residue is designed, bounded, useful, attributable, and disposable—or whether it accumulates by accident until the cleanup becomes somebody else's job.

## Review status

This Desk edition is a complete first draft built around a deliberately cross-disciplinary claim: automation has a wake in the same way a ship does. The visible product may be the thing we intended to make, while the wake reveals the energy, materials, records, permissions, labor, uncertainty, and institutional adaptation required to make that product possible at scale.

The manuscript treats environmental costs as one part of the wake rather than the whole thesis. It also follows operational residue, synthetic information, authentication and provenance, human evaluation work, regulation, software maintenance, fraud and verification burdens, retention conflicts, and the possibility that residual data becomes valuable evidence. Throughout, the book preserves counterevidence: efficiency improvements can lower per-task costs; data-center water use varies enormously by cooling system and grid; synthetic data can be useful when curated; logs can become privacy liabilities as well as accountability tools; and some forms of residue are intentionally created because they make complex systems safer.
