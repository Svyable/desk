# Evidence Ledger

## Class 1 — Verified / documented

- Fleischmann and Pons publicly announced electrochemical cold-fusion claims on March 23, 1989 and published a paper reporting excess heat and nuclear-product observations.
- Many major laboratories failed to reproduce the claimed effect under their tested conditions; several early positive reports were withdrawn or reinterpreted.
- DOE's 1989 Energy Research Advisory Board review did not find persuasive evidence for a useful cold-fusion energy source and recommended against a special federally funded program while allowing well-designed individual proposals to compete through normal peer review.
- DOE revisited LENR in 2004 and again found the evidence insufficient to establish the claimed nuclear processes; reviewers nevertheless identified some areas as potentially appropriate for carefully designed research proposals.
- Google's multi-institution re-evaluation, published in *Nature* in 2019, reported no evidence of the claimed cold-fusion effect while documenting substantial advances in loading, materials control and calorimetry.
- Palladium and related metals can absorb large hydrogen/deuterium concentrations; phase behavior, defects, stress and loading history are real materials-science variables.
- NASA-led researchers published gamma-driven lattice-confinement-fusion experiments in *Physical Review C* in 2020 reporting fusion-consistent neutron products from deuterated metals under energetic irradiation.
- NASA's lattice-confinement method uses MeV-scale external excitation and is not evidence that ordinary electrolysis causes room-temperature fusion.
- ARPA-E publicly held a LENR workshop in 2021, explicitly stating that no widely accepted on-demand repeatable LENR experiment or sound theoretical basis existed.
- ARPA-E awarded roughly $10 million across eight LENR projects in 2023, including independent diagnostics, replication-oriented platforms and materials experiments.

## Class 2 — Credible but incomplete

- Some experienced groups have reported excess heat, tritium, helium or other anomalies under specific metal-hydride conditions. Parts of this record are technically detailed enough to merit examination but lack the broad, independent, on-demand replication required for establishment.
- Loading ratio, microstructure, impurities, stress and sample history may affect hydrogen behavior in palladium enough that nominally identical experiments occupy different materials states. Whether those variables explain any nuclear anomaly is unresolved.
- Electron screening in condensed matter can alter known low-energy nuclear reaction rates. The magnitude and relevance to eV-input LENR claims remain limited and mechanism-dependent.
- Some modern government-funded teams may produce useful constraints or anomalous observations even if no energy-producing LENR mechanism exists.

## Class 3 — Disputed / unconfirmed

- Sustained commercially useful nuclear-scale excess heat from ordinary electrochemical or gas-loading LENR systems.
- Claims of large transmutation rates without commensurate radiation or a quantitatively consistent nuclear-reaction inventory.
- Specific commercial devices whose independent calorimetry, fuel inventory and isotopic outputs have not been openly replicated.
- Claims that DOE, universities, journals or industry collectively suppressed conclusive proof of cold fusion.
- Claims that NASA lattice-confinement fusion, muon-catalyzed fusion, electron screening or other established nuclear effects automatically validate the Fleischmann–Pons mechanism.

## Class 4 — Speculative synthesis / open conjecture

- The surviving LENR literature may be a mixture of measurement error, contamination, rare materials states, ordinary screened nuclear reactions and perhaps one or more weak unidentified effects rather than one coherent phenomenon.
- Scientific stigma may reduce both low-quality work and high-quality falsification work, producing a field in which claims persist longer because few skeptical laboratories want to spend years closing them.
- A distributed modern experiment with common materials, blind sample exchange, sealed controls, preregistered analysis and independent nuclear diagnostics could move the field substantially even if every result is null.
- The most durable contribution of LENR research may ultimately be metrology and metal-hydrogen science rather than energy production.
