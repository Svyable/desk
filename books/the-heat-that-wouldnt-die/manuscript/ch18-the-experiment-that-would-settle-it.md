# The Experiment That Would Settle It

No single experiment can settle every claim called LENR.

That is the first design requirement.

A field survives by becoming too broad when it can treat the failure of one mechanism as irrelevant to another and the success of one weak anomaly as support for all. The experiment I want is therefore narrower. It should target the strongest version of one recurring claim: a reproducible nuclear-scale anomaly associated with deuterium-loaded metal under chemical-scale stimulation.

Not commercial power. Not the history of 1989. One phenomenon.

The apparatus begins before the apparatus, with material.

Three fabrication teams produce samples using protocols selected because proponents regard them as capable of reaching candidate active states. The materials include deuterium-loading palladium or palladium-alloy systems and matched ordinary-hydrogen controls. Each team also fabricates deliberately inactive controls that are chemically and mechanically similar but miss one proposed necessary condition.

Every sample receives a random code generated outside the fabrication and measurement groups.

Before exposure, independent characterization labs measure composition, loading capacity, grain structure, surface state, impurities and whichever defect proxies the chosen hypothesis names. Some characterization is destructive, so witness samples from the same fabrication batch are used where needed. The point is to know the material state without using the future outcome to decide which feature mattered.

The effect-producing laboratories receive samples blind.

Their protocol is frozen in advance. Temperature, gas pressure or electrochemical current, stimulation schedule, duration, loading target and exclusion criteria are specified. If the hypothesis requires a long incubation, the run is long. There is no virtue in making a slow materials process fit a convenient week.

The calorimetry uses two conceptually different methods where practical.

A primary heat-flow measurement is paired with an internal calibration heater whose power is independently metered. Calibration pulses are inserted automatically at randomized times unknown to the analysts during the run. Environmental temperatures, gas flows, pressure, electrical input and mass inventories are logged continuously.

The analysis code is frozen before sample labels are revealed.

This sounds extreme only because most experiments are not accused of hiding an energy revolution.

Radiation diagnostics operate independently. Neutron detectors of different technologies run around the apparatus, calibrated with traceable sources before and after the campaign. Gamma spectroscopy records energy-resolved spectra. Charged-particle detectors are included if the geometry and mechanism make them meaningful. Background instruments operate in a nearby control location to capture environmental changes.

The radiation team does not receive live calorimetry labels.

This prevents a heat excursion from becoming a reason to search the neutron stream until a flattering time window appears.

Gas and solid products receive the same treatment. The system is as closed as the selected protocol permits. Initial helium, tritium and relevant isotope inventories are measured. Samples and gas fractions are archived. After the run, blind aliquots go to multiple mass-spectrometry laboratories.

The proposed mechanism must name at least one product or upper-bound consequence.

If proponents say the reaction produces helium-4 without penetrating radiation, integrated heat predicts helium inventory. If they propose another nuclear pathway, write its stoichiometry. If the mechanism is intentionally unknown, the claim can still be tested as anomalous heat, but it does not earn the word nuclear until independent nuclear evidence appears.

This is a critical semantic gate.

The primary endpoint is declared before the campaign. Perhaps it is excess heat above a threshold relative to calibrated uncertainty in deuterated samples compared with hydrogen controls. Perhaps it is a specific nuclear product rate. There can be secondary endpoints, but they do not rescue failure of the primary one.

The sample size is determined from the claimed event rate, not from budget convenience.

If proponents say only ten percent of properly prepared samples become active, the campaign needs enough samples to test ten percent against the background rate with useful power. If they cannot estimate an event rate, begin with an exploratory phase whose only purpose is to estimate one, then freeze it for confirmation.

Exploration and confirmation use different samples.

No sample that taught the team how to define success is allowed in the confirmatory dataset.

This single rule would clean a remarkable amount of anomalous science.

The laboratories also exchange samples. Material predicted active by the fabrication team and showing an anomaly in one measurement lab is moved, under controlled storage, to another lab without revealing its status. If the alleged state is expected to decay, characterize the decay prospectively. If it survives only inside the original apparatus, the hypothesis changes and the apparatus dependency becomes a variable.

A portable effect receives more weight than a local one.

The data become public after a predetermined embargo sufficient for quality checks, regardless of result. Raw time series, calibration data, detector files, sample metadata and analysis code are released. Commercial partners can protect unrelated manufacturing details, but any detail required to reproduce the claimed effect must be disclosed if the result is to carry scientific weight.

This condition will repel some inventors.

Good.

A secret process can be a business claim or a national-security program. It cannot simultaneously demand public scientific validation while withholding the causal recipe.

The experiment includes a red team with explicit authority to create artifacts.

Before the confirmatory run, red-team members are given the apparatus and asked to generate false excess heat, false neutron bursts and false isotope shifts through nonfraudulent manipulations that operators might plausibly encounter: recombination changes, sensor drift, electromagnetic interference, sample contamination, gas leaks, analysis edge cases. Their methods are then used to harden controls.

This is penetration testing for physical evidence.

The team should be rewarded for finding ways to fool itself.

What would count as a decisive positive?

Not one exciting trace.

A material-state-dependent effect appears in blinded deuterated samples at a rate matching or exceeding the preregistered threshold and not in matched controls. The signal survives at least two independent calorimetric approaches or contains an unambiguous nuclear product. A product inventory is quantitatively compatible with the claimed energy within stated uncertainties. Independent laboratories reproduce the result without the discoverer's intervention.

At that point theory can be wrong and the phenomenon still wins.

What would count as a strong negative?

The program repeatedly reaches the predeclared material states, validates sensitivity using hidden calibration injections and known nuclear sources, runs enough samples to exclude the claimed event rate, and observes no primary signature. Nuclear-product upper bounds fall far below what the proposed mechanism requires.

That would not prove no LENR phenomenon exists anywhere.

It would kill this one.

The field should celebrate that death because it shrinks the hypothesis space.

A sequence of such experiments can eventually do what the 1989 frenzy could not: convert a branded controversy into a map of surviving and excluded mechanisms.

This is where ARPA-E's current work is potentially foundational even if every project is null. It can establish modern benchmark protocols and background distributions. It can identify which old claims are experimentally well-posed enough to test. It can create shared materials and diagnostic standards.

The next program, if one is needed, should be smaller in hypothesis space and larger in replication.

The design also answers the recurring suppression claim. A positive effect generated under this architecture would be difficult to suppress socially because the evidence would already exist across independent institutions. Multiple labs would hold samples and raw data. The mechanism could remain unknown while the observation became portable.

Secrecy thrives on single points of custody.

Science should remove them.

A skeptic might object that all this effort is unnecessary because the prior probability is already too low. That is a legitimate resource argument. The answer depends on cost. A program costing tens of millions, designed to produce transferable materials and metrology even in failure, is small compared with the upside and small compared with decades of unresolved public controversy.

A billion-dollar cold-fusion initiative would require much stronger evidence.

A ten-million-dollar attempt to establish whether one reproducible anomaly exists can be rational.

The experiment I have described is not technologically ambitious. It does not try to make electricity. It tries to make disagreement expensive.

That is the missing machine.
