# Back Matter

This manuscript uses “cold fusion” mainly for the 1989 Fleischmann–Pons claim and its direct electrochemical descendants, and “LENR” for the broader modern family of proposed low-energy nuclear anomalies. Neither label is treated as a mechanism.

The evidentiary rule is deliberately asymmetric with publicity but symmetric with experiment. Extraordinary energy claims receive low prior confidence after decades of failed replication, yet any new result is evaluated by its actual method rather than dismissed because of the field name. Government funding demonstrates investigation, not validation. Peer review demonstrates that a result entered the scientific literature, not independent replication. Patents demonstrate filing, not efficacy.

The strongest documented facts are the original 1989 announcement and paper; rapid replication failures and corrected early signals; DOE's 1989 and 2004 reviews; persistent international and institutional research; Google's 2019 high-rigor null campaign; NASA's distinct, energetically driven lattice-confinement-fusion publications; and ARPA-E's 2021 workshop and 2023 portfolio.

The manuscript does not claim that cold fusion has been suppressed successfully, that any commercial LENR device produces verified net energy, that NASA validated Fleischmann–Pons, or that ARPA-E funding implies a positive result.

## What would defeat the book's thesis

The narrow thesis is that the scientifically useful modern return to LENR is a return to better falsification, not evidence that the 1989 energy claim was established. That thesis would need major revision if the ARPA-E projects or another independent consortium produced an on-demand, replicated chemical-input nuclear process with quantitative energy/product closure. In that event the book's historical caution would remain useful, but “unestablished” would no longer describe the phenomenon.

The thesis would also need narrowing if archival evidence showed that the 1989 or 2004 DOE reviews knowingly concealed decisive reproducible positive evidence rather than reaching skeptical conclusions from the experimental record they described. No such evidence is established in the sources used here.

Conversely, a complete set of high-quality ARPA-E null results would strengthen the book's argument about the value of adversarial measurement while weakening the case for further energy-oriented LENR research.

## Research still required for publication

The current draft needs a document-level reconstruction of the March 1989 patent and press chronology; laboratory-by-laboratory replication table; primary Japanese government program records; SRI/EPRI raw calorimetry and helium work; full 2004 DOE reviewer materials; quantitative D-D product/heat calculations; detailed treatment of electron-screening measurements; Google supplementary methods and raw-data repositories; NASA LCF independent replication history; and project-by-project ARPA-E outcomes as closeout publications become available.

The manuscript should also add interviews only where they can clarify experimental practice without substituting memory for records.
