# 9. The Third Interstellar Object

By the time 3I/ATLAS arrived in 2025, interstellar-object astronomy had a luxury it did not have in 2017: precedent.

The object was discovered on July 1, 2025. Observations across the following months showed a cometary body rich in volatiles. NASA-linked reporting and SPHEREx observations described water and carbon-dioxide activity. Radio technosignature searches found no evidence of artificial transmission. Whatever else remained to be learned about its nucleus and origin system, the object looked increasingly like what planetary science expects nature to eject.

That is important because technosignature science needs boring objects.

A field built only from anomalies becomes a machine for manufacturing significance. Every datum arrives already selected for strangeness. The denominator disappears. 3I/ATLAS helps restore it. The more ordinary interstellar objects we characterize, the more precisely we can say how unusual a future object actually is.

This changes the burden on artificial-origin claims. An anomalous acceleration must be compared against a distribution of natural nongravitational forces. Spectral oddities must be compared against the chemistry of multiple exoplanetary source environments. Rotation states, shapes, dust production and volatile ratios need population context.

The temptation will remain to ask the exciting question first: is this one a probe?

The better question is: what measurement would force us to stop treating it as part of the natural population?

That might be a narrowband transmission physically localized to the object after terrestrial interference is excluded. It might be resolved geometry inconsistent with rubble, fracture or contact binaries. It might be persistent controlled acceleration with no plausible momentum source and a trajectory change difficult to produce passively. It might be a material spectrum with industrially processed constituents in combinations not credibly produced by astrophysics.

Each of those would still require repetition and independent instruments.

There is another reason to follow every interstellar object carefully. Even if none are artifacts, they teach us the transport physics relevant to microscopic technosignatures. Interstellar grains and macroscopic bodies are exposed to radiation, gas, magnetic fields and collisions. The survival of natural materials bounds the survival of engineered ones.

The third interstellar object therefore does more for serious artifact SETI by being natural than a thousand speculative probes would do by being merely imaginable.
