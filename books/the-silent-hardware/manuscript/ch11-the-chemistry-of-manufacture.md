# 11. The Chemistry of Manufacture

If geometry dies first, chemistry becomes testimony.

That sentence sounds comforting until one spends time with meteorites.

Natural planetary systems are extravagant chemists. Differentiation separates metals from silicates. Hydrothermal systems concentrate elements. Impacts create high-pressure phases. Stellar nucleosynthesis creates isotopic signatures no terrestrial factory could imitate economically. Presolar grains arrive with compositions that look impossible if one assumes the Solar System is the whole laboratory.

So a chemically strange grain is not an artifact. Strange chemistry is normal in cosmochemistry.

The stronger signature is processed chemistry coupled to processing structure.

A highly purified elemental phase might be suggestive, but natural fractionation can enrich materials. A precise isotope ratio might look engineered, but nucleosynthetic reservoirs can be extreme. A doped semiconductor concentration could be interesting, but the dopant must be measured spatially: is it a controlled junction, or merely a mineral boundary? A thin-film stack with repeated nanometer-scale layer thicknesses would be harder to explain geologically, but even there the instrument must rule out sectioning artifacts and contamination.

The correct mindset comes from forensic materials laboratories, not from treasure hunting.

Start with morphology under multiple imaging modes. Map elemental composition. Measure crystal structure. Examine isotopes. Look for machining, deposition, sintering or additive-manufacturing textures. Ask whether thermal history matches the proposed phases. Run natural analog searches before announcing novelty.

Then split the sample.

A truly consequential material claim should be reproducible in laboratories that did not share preparation equipment. It should survive different analytical techniques. Raw spectra and calibration standards should be archived. Reference materials should travel with the sample. Blanks should be processed beside it.

Patents are useless here as proof of capability. A patent can show that somebody filed an idea; it cannot show a device worked. The same discipline applies to extraterrestrial material. A suggestive micrograph shows a suggestive micrograph. It does not show origin.

The dream discovery is not a mysterious metal.

It is a material whose manufacturing history can be reconstructed so tightly that natural formation becomes the strained explanation.
