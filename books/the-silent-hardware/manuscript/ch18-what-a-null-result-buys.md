# 18. What a Null Result Buys

The most likely result of a careful Solar-System artifact search is nothing.

That does not make the search pointless. It makes the scope statement essential.

A null result from a lunar imaging survey does not mean the Moon has no artifacts. It means no artifacts above a stated size, contrast and morphology were detected in the searched terrain under the survey's completeness limits. A null result from a regolith study means no particles meeting predeclared criteria were found in the analyzed mass under the instrument's sensitivity and contamination controls. A null co-orbital survey constrains objects in certain orbital and brightness ranges.

This is how absence becomes knowledge.

Radio SETI learned this lesson painfully because “we heard nothing” can sound cosmic while the actual searched fraction of parameter space is tiny. Artifact SETI should not repeat the mistake with surfaces and orbits.

The null also improves adjacent science. A complete search for specularly reflecting co-orbitals refines near-Earth object populations. A microscopic lunar-material survey finds presolar grains, impact products, industrial contaminants and new mineralogy. A pre-Sputnik transient search improves plate-defect characterization and time-domain archives. A degradation library helps preserve human space heritage and model orbital debris.

This is why the field can afford to be conservative.

It does not need an alien to justify every instrument hour.

And if one day something survives the controls—an object with dynamics, structure and composition that force manufacture into the best explanation—the history of the search will matter. We will want to know that the criteria existed before the object. We will want the blanks, the nulls, the calibration failures, the false alarms, the natural oddities that trained the classifiers.

A discovery without that history would be a story.

A discovery built on that history could become a fact.
