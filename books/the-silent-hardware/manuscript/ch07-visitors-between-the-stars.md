# 7. Visitors Between the Stars

In 2017 the Solar System acquired a category it had never possessed observationally: a confirmed macroscopic object arriving from interstellar space.

1I/'Oumuamua did not prove that interstellar objects exist. Dynamics had predicted exchange of material between planetary systems for decades. What it proved was that our surveys had become good enough to catch one.

That difference matters for technosignatures.

Before 2017, arguments about interstellar probes lived partly inside an observational vacuum. After 2017, nature supplied a control population. Then 2I/Borisov arrived looking much more recognizably cometary. In 2025, 3I/ATLAS became the third confirmed interstellar object and again showed ordinary cometary behavior, including volatile-rich activity observed by major facilities.

The natural baseline is growing.

This should make artificial-origin claims harder, not easier. Each new natural interstellar object expands the range of properties produced by planetary systems. An odd shape, color, acceleration or volatile mix must now be judged against a population we are only beginning to sample.

At the same time, interstellar-object astronomy makes artifact searches empirically respectable in a way abstract probe arguments never could. We can specify intercept opportunities, spectra, outgassing limits, nongravitational accelerations, rotation states, radar observables and future mission architectures. We can search for radio emission without declaring that radio emission is expected. We can compare objects rather than treating the first one as a metaphysical referendum.

The central epistemic mistake is category collapse.

“Interstellar” is a dynamical origin class. “Artificial” is a causal manufacturing class. One does not imply the other. The fact that an object came from another stellar system is remarkable and entirely compatible with natural ejection.

A real artifact program would exploit interstellar objects precisely because they are visitors, not because visitors are suspicious. Every well-characterized natural object improves our ability to recognize a future outlier. Comet science is not a distraction from technosignature science.

It is the calibration.
