# 12. Dead Machines Have Temperatures

A living machine spends energy. A dead machine still absorbs sunlight.

Thermal infrared therefore seems like a clean technosignature channel: machines have waste heat; artificial surfaces may possess unusual thermal inertia, emissivity or day-night behavior. The complication is that rocks also have temperatures, and rocks have spent four and a half billion years learning how to look surprising.

An active artifact can be easier. Internal power creates heat above passive equilibrium. Repeated thermal excess localized to an object, particularly if correlated with other signs of activity, would be difficult to ignore. But small objects are hard to resolve, and active heat can be intermittent.

For dead hardware the problem becomes comparative thermophysics.

A hollow shell, metal plate or multilayer structure may heat and cool differently from regolith. Its phase curve can differ from a natural body of the same optical brightness. But unknown shape, spin state, surface roughness and contact geometry create degeneracies. A metallic asteroid is not a spacecraft. A high thermal inertia is not manufacture.

The useful signature is again conjunction.

Imagine an Earth co-orbital with a light curve suggesting flat facets, radar polarization inconsistent with ordinary rubble, and a thermal response best fit by a thin-walled structure. None alone is enough. Together they justify a better observation campaign.

Technosignature astronomy should become expert at escalation rather than declaration.

Stage one identifies an outlier. Stage two measures it with independent modalities. Stage three attempts to kill the exotic model with natural analogs. Only then does origin become the question.

This matters because infrared searches are particularly vulnerable to anthropomorphism. We expect machines to radiate because ours do. An advanced artifact might be dead, dormant, tiny, deeply buried, highly reflective, cryogenic, or made of materials outside our engineering habits.

Searches should use physics we can constrain, not fantasies we cannot.
