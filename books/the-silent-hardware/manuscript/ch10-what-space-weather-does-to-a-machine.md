# 10. What Space Weather Does to a Machine

A machine does not remain a machine-shaped proposition forever.

Put aluminum, silicon, polymers, ceramics, lubricants and wiring in space and time starts sorting them. Volatiles escape. Polymers embrittle under radiation. Thermal cycling works interfaces. Solar-wind sputtering modifies exposed surfaces. High-energy particles create defects. Micrometeoroids crater and spall. Dust coats. Impacts fragment. If the object is on a surface, regolith gardening buries and exhumes it. If it is in orbit, dynamics may remove it before chemistry has finished.

Technosignature discussions often treat preservation as a yes-or-no switch. The useful question is which diagnostic survives longest.

A mirrored optical surface may lose reflectivity while its substrate alloy remains distinctive. A semiconductor stops functioning while dopant gradients persist. A multilayer coating can become chemically altered yet retain periodic interfaces. A carbon composite can fray into fibers whose dimensions and resin residues still look manufactured. A battery becomes dead chemistry but may leave concentration gradients nature rarely assembles in that geometry.

For old artifacts, materials science outruns iconography.

This also introduces a bias: the easiest technology for us to recognize may be technology like ours. We know what aluminum-lithium aerospace alloys look like, what solar cells look like, what titanium fasteners look like. A nonhuman manufacturing system might use materials we would initially catalog as geologic because our industrial priors are local.

The remedy is not imagination. It is invariants.

Manufacture tends to impose control. Control over purity. Control over thickness. Control over periodicity. Control over orientation. Control over interfaces. Control over isotope ratios in some nuclear technologies. Control over defects. Natural processes also produce order, sometimes exquisite order, so no single feature is decisive. Crystals are ordered. Biological tissues are hierarchical. Meteorites carry weird phases. Presolar grains carry isotope anomalies.

But combinations can narrow the field.

The search should therefore build degradation experiments now using known spacecraft materials. Place coupons on lunar missions. Recover exposed hardware where possible. Analyze long-duration satellites and returned components. Compare pristine manufacturing signatures to decades of radiation and impacts. Model the pathway from spacecraft to ambiguous debris.

We already possess the calibration artifacts. They have serial numbers.
