# 14. Our Own Alien Ruins

Before we search for somebody else's abandoned hardware, we should admit how quickly our own becomes strange.

Space is already archaeological.

The Moon has descent stages, crashed probes, rover tracks, retroreflectors and discarded equipment. Mars has landers that will never move again. Venus has Soviet hardware under an atmosphere that has had decades to attack it. Heliocentric space contains upper stages, dust, fragments and dead spacecraft. Earth orbit contains catalogs and uncataloged populations, paint flecks, insulation, bolts, lens caps, coolant droplets and breakup debris.

A future extraterrestrial archaeologist arriving without our manifests would face exactly the classification problem we are proposing for ourselves.

Which objects are parts of one mission? Which were deliberately placed? Which are impact fragments? Which unusual alloys belong to the visitor and which to the environment? Which apparent “site” is a landing site and which is a crash? How much history can be reconstructed from distribution alone?

This is not analogy. It is a calibration opportunity.

Planetary scientists and heritage researchers can measure how known human materials age on other worlds. Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter images document human sites from above. Returned Surveyor hardware gave early direct information about lunar exposure. Future sample-return or servicing missions may produce more examples. Space-debris laboratories already study hypervelocity impact signatures.

The artifact field should formalize this into a reference collection: alloys, coatings, composites, glasses, solders, semiconductors, propellants, lubricants and polymers exposed for known durations in known environments.

Then deliberately blind the analysts.

Mix natural lunar or meteoritic material with fragments of space-weathered manufactured material and see what the pipeline calls anomalous. If our methods cannot reliably recover known technology from degraded samples, they are not ready for unknown technology.

There is a social benefit to this exercise too. It turns “alien artifact detection” into ordinary forensic validation. The method can fail publicly without threatening anyone's worldview.

Our ruins are the training set.
