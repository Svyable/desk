# Evidence Matrix and Source Log

## Thesis

Artifact searches probe a different temporal channel from contemporaneous communication SETI. That does not imply artifacts exist locally. The scientifically tractable problem is to quantify where artifacts could survive, what observables manufacture could leave, how completely those observables have been searched, and what controls distinguish them from natural material and human contamination.

## Class 1 — verified / documented / directly established

- NASA convened a technosignatures workshop in Houston in September 2018 to assess the scientific search space beyond conventional radio SETI. https://astrobiology.nasa.gov/events/nasa-technosignatures-workshop/
- The workshop report discusses a broad technosignature search space and current/future observational opportunities. https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.08681
- The Solar System has now produced three confirmed interstellar-object discoveries: 1I/'Oumuamua, 2I/Borisov and 3I/ATLAS. Their existence establishes a natural transport channel between planetary systems; it does not establish technological transport.
- Human artifacts are present on the Moon and elsewhere in the Solar System, providing a calibration set for degradation and archaeological detectability.
- James Benford published an artifact-version Drake-equation framework in 2021, explicitly arguing that artifact residence times may differ from signal lifetimes. https://doi.org/10.1089/ast.2020.2364
- Haqq-Misra and Kopparapu published a 2012 analysis of non-terrestrial-artifact likelihood/search locations. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actaastro.2011.10.010
- Villarroel et al. released a 2025 preprint describing several empirical probe-search methods and a proof-of-concept Earth-shadow transient search. https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.17907
- On September 8, 2026, SETI Institute publicized a preprint proposing a quantitative search for microscopic engineered particles in lunar regolith. The announcement explicitly states that no extraterrestrial technology was detected. https://www.seti.org/news/researchers-propose-searching-the-moon/

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete evidence / methods

- Search strategies using co-orbitals, archival plates, glints, thermal anomalies, surface morphology, material composition or microscopic particles are scientifically testable but have sparse coverage and uncertain priors.
- Some interstellar objects have displayed unusual properties relative to then-known populations, but the population is tiny and natural explanations remain active.
- Machine-learning planetary-image searches can increase survey scale, but training-set bias and false-positive control remain central.

## Class 3 — disputed or unconfirmed claims

- Claims that any specific known Solar-System object is an extraterrestrial artifact remain unconfirmed.
- Claims that 1I/'Oumuamua was engineered are interpretations, not established fact. No direct technological signature has been observed.
- Historical reports of anomalous pre-Sputnik transients do not by themselves establish orbiting artificial objects; plate defects, natural transients, human high-altitude activity and incomplete metadata remain alternatives.
- No known lunar, Martian, co-orbital or Lagrange-region anomaly is established as non-human technology.

## Class 4 — speculative synthesis / open conjecture

- Microscopic engineered debris might outlive intact probes and could be detectable through combinations of morphology, composition, isotopic control and microstructure.
- Human lunar hardware can be used as a blind calibration set for future unknown-artifact pipelines.
- A Solar-System artifact observatory could piggyback on planetary-defense, lunar-science, orbital-debris and archival-astronomy infrastructure.
- Precommitted international custody rules would reduce both contamination risk and secrecy-driven evidentiary degradation if a genuinely anomalous sample were recovered.

## Contradictions and failure modes

1. **Longevity vs legibility:** material can persist long after its technological origin becomes unreadable.
2. **Stable dynamics vs intentional placement:** useful orbits are also available to natural objects.
3. **Chemical anomaly vs cosmochemistry:** nature creates extreme isotopic and compositional outliers.
4. **Pre-Sputnik cleanliness vs archival defects:** fewer satellites do not mean fewer detector artifacts.
5. **Secrecy vs verification:** security controls may protect legitimate interests while degrading independent replication.
6. **Machine learning vs novelty:** anomaly detectors trained on known classes can mis-rank exactly the genuinely new cases they are meant to find.
