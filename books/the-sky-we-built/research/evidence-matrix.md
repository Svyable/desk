# Evidentiary matrix

## Class 1 — verified / declassified / documented

| Claim | Evidence posture | Core source |
|---|---|---|
| Project West Ford was an Air Force/Lincoln Laboratory orbital-scatter communications experiment | Documented | MIT Lincoln Laboratory institutional history; IEEE program history |
| The first 1961 deployment failed to disperse as intended | Documented | NASA 1963 chronology; Lincoln history |
| The 1963 payload planned roughly 480 million copper dipoles | Documented | Shapiro 1964; NASA; Lincoln history |
| 1963 dipoles were about 1.78 cm long and about 17.8 μm diameter | Documented | Shapiro 1964 |
| The May 1963 deployment formed a useful orbital scatter belt over subsequent weeks | Documented | Lincoln history; IEEE |
| Coast-to-coast communications were demonstrated through the belt | Documented | Lincoln history; IEEE |
| Astronomers and astronomical organizations protested the experiment | Documented | contemporary records; Barentine 2025; primary organizational records still to page-index |
| A 1964 Space Science Board assessment found no significant optical/radio astronomy interference from the experiment | Near-contemporary documented report; original report still required | Physics Today 1964 |
| Later surveillance cataloged many small objects associated with the second West Ford experiment | Documented | NASA ODQN 17(4) |
| A post-mission investigation estimated only about 25–45% of planned dipoles dispersed properly | Documented as NASA retrospective reporting of post-mission investigation | NASA ODQN 17(4) |
| Dozens of clumps still remained in orbit in 2013 | Documented, date-specific | NASA ODQN 17(4) |
| Article IX of the Outer Space Treaty contains due-regard and harmful-interference consultation language | Primary legal text | UN A/6621 |

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete

| Claim | Why incomplete | Needed evidence |
|---|---|---|
| West Ford materially influenced the development of international consultation norms for potentially harmful space experiments | Scholarly histories support influence, but treaty language had multiple causes | COPUOS negotiating record; U.S./UK diplomatic papers; COSPAR minutes |
| Some later small debris at high altitude belongs to the failed 1961 West Ford deployment | NASA debris history discusses plausible attribution, but object-level provenance is uncertain | catalog/object reconstruction; radar history |
| Military sponsors seriously considered operational or more persistent West Ford belts after a successful demonstration | Contemporary public discussion indicates possibility, but scope/authorization unclear | Air Force requirements, program planning documents |
| Astronomical protest changed specific engineering parameters for the 1963 attempt | Plausible and reported in histories, but exact causal chain needs primary documentation | design review records; committee correspondence |

## Class 3 — disputed / not established

- The 1963 belt caused major lasting damage to optical astronomy.
- The 1963 belt caused major lasting damage to radio astronomy.
- The United States deployed a permanent operational West Ford communications system.
- The United States intended to claim or own an orbital shell through West Ford.
- All popular reports of surviving “needles” refer to freely dispersed individual dipoles rather than clumps or other catalog objects.
- Every small high-altitude object sometimes attributed to West Ford has a secure chain of provenance.
- Project West Ford by itself caused Article IX of the Outer Space Treaty.
- West Ford's surviving debris constitutes a major fraction of present-day orbital collision risk.

## Class 4 — speculative synthesis / open conjecture

- Distributed resilience and environmental controllability can be opposing design objectives when capability comes from an uncontrolled population.
- West Ford is an early example of infrastructure becoming an environmental condition rather than remaining a bounded object.
- Modern large constellations share a population-scale governance problem with West Ford even though their technical capabilities and mitigation options differ radically.
- Technological obsolescence can resolve a governance dispute politically without resolving the underlying institutional problem.

## Defeat condition

The central synthesis should be substantially weakened if primary technical records show that the long-lived aggregation state was quantitatively anticipated, accepted, and internationally reviewed before the 1963 launch to a standard materially stronger than the manuscript currently reflects, or if modern catalog reconstruction shows that the long-duration debris population attributed to West Ford has been materially misidentified.

The treaty-history claim should be strengthened only if primary diplomatic records establish a direct line from West Ford controversy to specific Article IX or COSPAR language. Until then, the manuscript says “part of the prehistory” and “made the category concrete,” not “caused the treaty.”
