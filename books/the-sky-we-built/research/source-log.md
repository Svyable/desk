# Source log

Checked 2026-09-13 unless otherwise noted.

## Primary / institutional technical record

### MIT Lincoln Laboratory — institutional history
https://www.ll.mit.edu/sites/default/files/other/doc/2018-04/MIT_Lincoln_Laboratory_history_book.pdf

Use: program origin, 1961 failure, May 1963 deployment, approximate forty-day belt closure, approximate density, demonstrated communications performance, later decline of the belt. Institutional source; useful for technical facts but not independent evaluation of program wisdom.

### Morrow et al. — Proceedings of the IEEE historical perspective
https://doi.org/10.1109/PROC.1964.2989

Use: contemporary technical history of orbital scatter communications and West Ford. Publication by program participants; treat performance claims as primary technical evidence rather than independent policy assessment.

### Shapiro (1964) — Journal of Geophysical Research
https://doi.org/10.1029/JZ069i021p04693

Use: independent/technical statement of May 1963 payload scale, dipole length/diameter, mean altitude, and use of West Ford dipoles for orbital-dynamics constraints. Important for avoiding dependence on popular summaries.

### NASA — Astronautics and Aeronautics, 1963
https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/sp-4004-1963.pdf

Use: contemporary chronology, May 1963 Air Force announcement, first-attempt failure summary, period language about reliability and invulnerability. Quote only after page-level verification in expansion pass.

### Smithsonian National Air and Space Museum — West Ford hardware
https://airandspace.si.edu/collection-objects/communications-satellite-west-ford-dipole-dispenser/nasm_A20040113000
https://www.si.edu/object/communications-satellite-west-ford-dipole-canister%3Anasm_A20040113001

Use: surviving prototype artifact, physical deployment architecture, institutional provenance.

## Orbital debris and persistence

### NASA Orbital Debris Quarterly News 17(4), 2013
https://orbitaldebris.jsc.nasa.gov/Quarterly-News/pdfs/ODQNv17i4.pdf

Use: 144 cataloged small objects associated with the second experiment since 1966; post-mission estimate that only 25–45% of planned dipoles dispersed properly; belief that remainder persisted in clumps; 46 clumps remaining at publication; only nine then with perigees below 2,000 km; discussion of possible first-mission remnants. This is the central long-tail source. Counts are date-stamped and must not be described as current in perpetuity.

### NASA Orbital Debris Program Office — current background
https://sma.nasa.gov/sma-disciplines/orbital-debris/orbital-debris-history-of-orbital-debris
https://orbitaldebris.jsc.nasa.gov/
https://orbitaldebris.jsc.nasa.gov/faq/

Use: current definitions and general lifetime/debris context. Do not use generic modern lifetime ranges as substitutes for West Ford-specific orbital analysis.

## Astronomy response / independent assessment

### Physics Today (1964) — Project West Ford
https://doi.org/10.1063/1.3051675

Use: report that the National Academy of Sciences Space Science Board concluded the 1963 dipole belt had not interfered with optical or radio astronomy. Need original Space Science Board report in next pass; this source is a near-contemporary secondary bridge.

### Barentine (2025) — Journal for the History of Astronomy
https://doi.org/10.1177/00218286251313772

Use: modern scholarly reconstruction of astronomical mobilization, international objections, and claimed relationship to later space governance. Secondary scholarly source; causal claims about treaty influence should be cross-checked against primary diplomatic records.

## Legal / diplomatic

### Outer Space Treaty — UN Audiovisual Library of International Law
https://legal.un.org/avl/ha/tos/tos.html

Use: adoption/opening/entry-into-force history and authoritative legal background.

### UN General Assembly A/6621 — treaty text
https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/853522/files/A_6621-EN.pdf

Use: Article IX language on due regard, harmful contamination, potentially harmful interference, and consultation. Primary treaty record.
