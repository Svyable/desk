## Frozen substantive expansion pass

Before release-candidate status, expand with primary-document depth rather than generic prose:

1. Reconstruct the 1958–1961 origin from Lincoln Laboratory technical memoranda and Air Force requirements, including the exact nuclear/solar/jamming communications threat model.
2. Build a quantitative West Ford link budget: transmitter power, antenna gains, geometry, scattering cross section, receiver sensitivity, bandwidth and demonstrated data rates.
3. Recover the full 1961 deployment mechanism/failure record and identify the object-level fate of the first payload as far as catalog evidence allows.
4. Reconstruct the 1963 dispenser, naphthalene matrix, spin/release dynamics and measured dispersion fraction from originating technical reports.
5. Page-index the International Astronomical Union, Royal Astronomical Society and National Academy/Space Science Board objections rather than relying on later summaries.
6. Reproduce the strongest contemporary optical and radio interference predictions numerically and compare them with the actual observation campaign.
7. Obtain the original Space Science Board West Ford report and distinguish null observations from limits of instrument sensitivity.
8. Build a date-stamped catalog ledger for all second-experiment objects, including current 2026 status where publicly resolvable.
9. Reconstruct the post-mission analysis behind NASA's 25–45% proper-dispersion estimate.
10. Model isolated-dipole versus clump area-to-mass ratios and orbital lifetime sensitivity rather than leaving the distinction qualitative.
11. Separate solar-radiation-pressure, electromagnetic/charge-drag and atmospheric-drag effects quantitatively by altitude and object state.
12. Expand the active-satellite competitor chapter with Telstar, Relay, Syncom, LES and IDCSP primary histories so West Ford's technological displacement is demonstrated rather than asserted.
13. Reconstruct COSPAR's potentially-harmful-experiment review mechanisms from primary minutes and institutional histories.
14. Search COPUOS, U.S. State Department, UK Foreign Office/RAS and UN negotiating records for direct documentary links—positive or negative—between West Ford and Article IX language.
15. Add an explicit legal analysis distinguishing non-appropriation, jurisdiction over space objects, due regard, harmful interference and consultation.
16. Quantify West Ford's actual contribution to modern debris risk so symbolic significance is not confused with collision-risk magnitude.
17. Add a controlled comparison with modern mega-constellations using measured astronomy/conjunction/deorbit evidence, while preserving the strong disanalogies.
18. Run `python3 scripts/check-book-cover-metadata.py the-sky-we-built`, the repository's book-length check, and `python3 scripts/check-desk.py` in a local checkout before any release-candidate claim.
19. Perform a whole-book source-link audit, page/section pinning pass, anti-slop pass and paragraph/rhythm pass under the repository prose standard.
20. Only after objective length and editorial gates pass should shared catalog/Reader/release surfaces be updated.

## Publication defeat conditions

Do not publish the book with the current central thesis if:

- the debris attribution materially collapses under object-level catalog reconstruction;
- primary records show a robust international consultation process already addressed the key externality before the 1963 launch, defeating the manuscript's governance framing;
- the original astronomy record shows the later “no interference” summary is materially misleading; or
- direct legal-history evidence contradicts the manuscript's bounded description of West Ford as one contributor to a broader treaty-era problem.

Those outcomes would require revision, not rhetorical defense.
