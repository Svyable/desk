# The Greenland That Wasn't White

Four hundred and sixteen thousand years ago, give or take thirty-eight thousand, water moved sediment across ground now buried under nearly a mile of ice.

That is the most defensible form of the startling claim.

Not “Greenland melted.”

Not “Greenland was a forest.”

Not “today’s ice sheet is about to disappear.”

At Camp Century, during the long interglacial known as Marine Isotope Stage 11, the local site was ice-free. Sediment was deposited by flowing water. Mineral grains had been exposed to sunlight recently enough for luminescence dating to retain a usable clock. Cosmogenic nuclides and provenance analyses indicated that the material was largely local and had seen surface exposure less than roughly sixteen thousand years before deposition.

Those statements come from the 2023 *Science* paper that tightened the age of the upper Camp Century basal sediment after the broader 2021 result had established repeated ice-free, vegetated conditions.

The dating method is worth understanding because the headline depends on what exactly was dated.

Luminescence dating does not date the formation of a quartz grain. It estimates the time since trapped electronic charges in mineral crystals were last reset by sufficient light exposure and then began accumulating again under natural background radiation. Sediment transported at the surface can be “bleached” by daylight. Once buried, its luminescence signal grows with dose. Measure the stored signal and the environmental dose rate, and one can estimate time since burial.

The method comes with failure modes. A grain may not have received enough sunlight to reset fully. Sediment can mix. Dose rates can vary. Laboratory procedures have to distinguish components of the signal with different stabilities. The Camp Century team therefore did not rely on one grain or one instrument. The age emerged from a structured dating program combined with geological provenance and cosmogenic constraints.

The result, 416 ± 38 thousand years, falls inside Marine Isotope Stage 11, roughly 424,000 to 374,000 years ago.

MIS 11 is scientifically inconvenient in a productive way. It was a long interglacial. Orbital conditions delivered prolonged summer warmth in the Northern Hemisphere. Atmospheric carbon dioxide was far lower than the concentration humans have now produced. Global sea level was several meters above modern estimates, requiring substantial reduction of one or both large Northern and Southern Hemisphere ice sheets.

Camp Century turns one part of that global inference into a local fact: northwestern Greenland lost enough ice for the bed at the drilling site to become exposed.

The local fact then has to be expanded through models.

The 2023 team used ice-sheet simulations to ask what configurations of the Greenland Ice Sheet are compatible with an ice-free Camp Century. Their result was that exposing that site requires Greenland to contribute at least about 1.4 meters of global sea-level equivalent.

The grammar matters.

The sediment did not measure 1.4 meters of sea level.

The sediment established an ice-free geographic constraint. The model translated that constraint into a minimum whole-ice-sheet volume loss.

This is not a weakness. It is how local geological evidence becomes global physical inference. The weakness would come from forgetting where the model entered.

A coastal boulder can establish that a glacier once reached a valley. It cannot by itself specify the volume of an entire ice sheet. A coral terrace can establish former relative sea level at one place. It cannot by itself separate ice volume from land motion. A sediment core can establish an ice-free bed. It needs a dynamical model to estimate what surrounding ice geometry could coexist with that state.

The Camp Century paper was designed around this division of labor.

That makes it a useful antidote to two bad habits in climate argument.

The first is treating models as if they were observations. They are not.

The second is treating anything involving a model as if it were not evidence. That is also wrong.

A model constrained by a physical observation can eliminate worlds that an unconstrained model permits. The value comes from the collision.

Camp Century is unusually good at collisions.

The Army’s engineers had a model too. Their version was not a global ice-sheet code. It was a set of assumptions and calculations about tunnel deformation, accumulation, structural load and maintenance. Camp Century supplied physical observations that constrained the model until Iceworm became unattractive.

Sixty years later, paleoclimate scientists use the same site to constrain a different model with a different failure mode.

The common principle is that a good model must be allowed to lose.

The MIS 11 result also complicates an older image of the Greenland Ice Sheet as geologically persistent in something close to its modern form. “Persistent” remains partly true. The 2021 Camp Century study concluded that the ice sheet existed through much of the Pleistocene and that basal sediment recorded at least two episodes of ice-free, vegetated conditions followed by renewed glaciation. Greenland was not alternately a continent-sized forest and an ice sheet every few thousand years.

But persistence was not permanence.

This is exactly the distinction long-lived infrastructure tends to ignore.

A system can persist for most of the interval one cares about and still fail during the interval that matters most.

Flood defenses can work for ninety-nine years and fail in the hundredth.

A waste repository can isolate material for ten thousand years and encounter groundwater in the eleven-thousandth.

An ice sheet can dominate a region for hundreds of thousands of years and retreat enough during one prolonged interglacial to expose a site far inland.

Reliability is a distribution, not a mood.

The Camp Century sediment provides only one geographic point, which is why claims that “most of Greenland was green” need care. The title of a press release can outrun the paper. Other evidence from central Greenland, including basal material from later cores, suggests substantial past ice-sheet reduction, but the exact geometry of MIS 11 Greenland remains a modeling problem.

The strongest statement from Camp Century is local and still profound.

If the modern ice sheet had been continuously thick at the site through MIS 11, the sediment could not have been exposed to sunlight and flowing surface water when the dating says it was.

That is a defeat condition with dirt attached.

The result carries modern relevance because MIS 11 provides an example of ice-sheet retreat under warmth not created by industrial carbon emissions. Paleoclimate is valuable precisely because it expands the sample beyond one contemporary forcing mechanism. If an ice sheet retreated under a past orbital configuration and lower atmospheric carbon dioxide, then modern stability cannot be inferred merely from the fact that today’s warming has a different cause.

The analogy has limits.

Past orbital forcing changed the seasonal and geographic distribution of solar energy differently from greenhouse forcing. The duration of MIS 11 matters. Ocean conditions differed. Ice-sheet geometry at the start of the interglacial differed. Atmospheric carbon dioxide around 280 parts per million was not the whole energy budget.

A paleoclimate analog is not a replay.

This is why the phrase “temperatures similar to today” is dangerous unless accompanied by duration, forcing pattern and boundary conditions. An ice sheet responds not only to how warm a year is but to how long a mass-balance deficit persists, where melt occurs and how ice dynamics transmit loss inland.

Camp Century tells us that the system has crossed a local deglaciation threshold before. It does not provide a countdown for crossing it again.

The sediment’s most unsettling contribution may therefore be epistemic rather than predictive.

It removes one easy form of reassurance.

We cannot argue that northwestern Greenland is effectively permanent simply because its ice is ancient, thick and still present. The geological record contains a counterexample.

This is the same role that Camp Century played against Iceworm. The Army did not need proof that every tunnel would collapse at once. It needed enough measured deformation to make a giant hidden network less attractive than competing systems.

A counterexample can change design before it becomes catastrophe.

The paleoclimate work also reveals an odd asymmetry in human attention. The military station lasted eight years. Its strategic proposal lived for only a few. The ice core’s climate record covered nearly one hundred thousand years. The sediment extended the useful history several times further.

The shortest-lived institution produced the longest-lived evidence.

This is partly because institutions optimize for current questions. The Army needed answers in time for budget and strategy. It could not wait centuries to see how the ice behaved. Scientists can use material that outlives the institution because the Earth keeps running the experiment after the appropriation ends.

The 416,000-year age is not the age of Camp Century’s deepest material in any simple sense. The 2021 work found evidence in lower sediment for older episodes and complex Pleistocene history. The basal archive records erosion and deposition across multiple glacial cycles. One neat age would be suspicious.

The 2023 result is powerful because it isolates one depositional event strongly enough to connect it to a known interglacial.

That event now acts as a benchmark for ice-sheet models.

A model that cannot lose enough northwestern Greenland during MIS 11 despite plausible forcing may be too stable.

A model that removes too much ice may conflict with other sea-level and geological constraints.

The evidence narrows a corridor rather than handing us a single future.

This is how a scientific archive becomes more demanding over time. The first Camp Century isotope work asked whether climate history was preserved in deep ice. Later work asked whether the bed had been exposed. Now the site is used to discriminate among reconstructions of whole-ice-sheet sensitivity.

The old military hole has become a boundary condition.

There is one final temptation to resist.

Because the Camp Century base itself contains abandoned waste, the ancient ice-free episode can be rhetorically linked to future waste exposure: Greenland melted there before, therefore the buried camp will emerge.

The inference skips a timescale.

The waste question is about decades to centuries. MIS 11 is evidence over hundreds of thousands of years. The relevant near-term variables are current accumulation, firn temperature, meltwater percolation and future climate forcing at the site. That is why the 2021 site-specific Camp Century model matters more for the year 2100 than a 416,000-year-old river bed.

Long-term vulnerability and near-term hazard are not the same claim.

A system can be nonpermanent in geological time and safely isolated for the next eighty years.

This book keeps both statements because the ice keeps both clocks.

The Greenland that was not white does not tell us exactly when the present white will change.

It tells us not to confuse duration with destiny.
