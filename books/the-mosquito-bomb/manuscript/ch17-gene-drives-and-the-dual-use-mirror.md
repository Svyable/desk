# Chapter 17 — Gene Drives and the Dual-Use Mirror

Modern mosquito control has moved beyond the technologies imagined by Cold War planners.

Genetically modified mosquitoes can be designed for population suppression or other public-health goals. Gene-drive systems are being studied as a way to bias inheritance and potentially spread selected traits through populations. These technologies raise ecological and governance questions precisely because their power is not confined to an individual released organism.

WHO's guidance framework for testing genetically modified mosquitoes emphasizes staged evaluation, risk assessment, regulation, ethics and social engagement. The National Academies and other scientific bodies have similarly emphasized phased testing and responsible governance for gene drives.

The dual-use mirror is unavoidable.

Any technology capable of altering vector populations can be described ominously if its purpose is stripped away. Conversely, beneficial intent is not a substitute for safety analysis.

The correct response is not to call modern vector control warfare.

It is to become better at distinguishing **capability**, **intent**, **governance**, and **effect**.

Those four words do a surprising amount of work.

A gene-drive paper is evidence that a mechanism has been studied.

It is not evidence of field deployment unless deployment is documented.

A field trial is evidence of a bounded release under specified conditions.

It is not evidence of a military program.

A patent is evidence that someone filed a claim.

It is not evidence that the claimed system works.

The Cold War history makes this discipline more urgent, not less. We know governments have conducted secret biological research. Therefore modern allegations deserve investigation.

We also know secrecy can generate false evidentiary ecologies. Therefore investigation has to be better than pattern matching.
