# The Mosquito Bomb

## When War Learned to Recruit Ecology

**Sven Hardy Benson**

### Thesis

The strange thing about entomological warfare is not that militaries once considered using insects as weapons. They did. The stranger thing is what happens to the idea of a weapon after the carrier can feed, disperse, die, reproduce, ignore borders, respond to weather, and participate in an ecosystem the attacker does not control.

This book argues that the documented U.S. mosquito- and flea-dispersal program of the 1950s, Japan's wartime use of infected vectors in China, the bitterly disputed Korean War biological-warfare allegations, and today's deliberately released mosquitoes for disease control belong in one history only if their differences are kept intact. The common object is the living vector. The purposes, evidence, ethics and outcomes are radically different.

The central claim is therefore narrower and more useful than the usual secret-program story: **once a military system recruits an organism, ecology becomes part of command and control.** That can make a vector weapon frightening in principle and unreliable in practice. It also means that modern beneficial mosquito-release technologies cannot responsibly be described as weapons merely because some of the same biological facts are relevant.

### Evidence classes

1. **Verified/declassified/documented:** U.S. entomological-warfare research and uninfected-vector field tests; Japanese biological-warfare attacks using plague vectors in China; U.S. open-air biological test history; Nixon's 1969 renunciation; the Biological Weapons Convention; modern regulated mosquito-control releases.
2. **Credible but incomplete:** operational-effectiveness estimates reconstructed from historical military reports; some participant recollections; incomplete archival detail about specific test populations and exposure.
3. **Disputed/unconfirmed:** claims that U.S. forces used biological weapons in Korea; claims that particular later disease outbreaks were covert insect attacks absent strong forensic chains; allegations that contemporary public-health mosquito programs are covert warfare.
4. **Speculative synthesis/open conjecture:** the proposition that ecology itself is the decisive systems-engineering constraint on any weapon that depends on living vectors, and that this provides a useful way to distinguish capability demonstrations from battlefield efficacy.
