# Contradictions and Incentive Audit

## U.S. Army / Department of Defense

**Incentives:** preserve secrecy; justify programs; minimize reputational/legal exposure after disclosure; emphasize defensive rationale after policy change.

**Contradictions to track:** tests described as safe may have relied on safety assumptions later revised; vector-dispersal success can be rhetorically inflated into weapon efficacy; public summaries differ on dates and participant consent.

## Imperial Japanese perpetrators / postwar U.S. interrogators

**Incentives:** perpetrators had obvious reasons to minimize crimes and maximize value of information; U.S. officials who granted immunity had incentives to portray data as uniquely valuable.

**Consequence:** technical claims from the immunity process require corroboration independent of the bargain.

## Chinese / North Korean / Soviet Korean War claims

**Incentives:** wartime propaganda, international mobilization, hygiene campaigns, delegitimization of U.S. war effort.

**Contradiction:** later Soviet archival material and Wu Zhili's memoir describe fabrication/false alarm, sharply undercutting the original allegation.

## United States on Korean War allegations

**Incentives:** avoid condemnation, protect classified BW capabilities, preserve alliance legitimacy.

**Boundary:** self-interested denial is not proof of innocence; independent adversary archival evidence carries special weight.

## Modern mosquito-control developers

**Incentives:** demonstrate efficacy, regulatory acceptability and community benefit; commercial actors may have financial incentives.

**Boundary:** beneficial intent does not eliminate ecological risk; historical military precedent does not convert regulated public-health programs into warfare.

## Conspiracy ecosystems

**Incentives:** documented secrecy makes maximal interpretations emotionally sticky; different code names, dates and locations are often collapsed into one continuous covert program.

**Control:** require program-specific documents, chain of custody and event-level evidence. Similarity is not continuity.
