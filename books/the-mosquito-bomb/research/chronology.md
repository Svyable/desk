# Chronology

- **1930s-1945** — Imperial Japanese biological-warfare program expands in occupied China; Unit 731 and related units conduct human experimentation and field operations.
- **1945-1948** — U.S. occupation authorities seek Japanese BW information; key perpetrators receive protection from prosecution in exchange for data.
- **1950-1953** — Korean War. China and North Korea allege U.S. biological-warfare use; International Scientific Commission reports in support in 1952.
- **1953** — U.S. Army Chemical Corps history later dates establishment of Fort Detrick arthropod-vector research program to this year.
- **1954** — Operation Big Itch tests uninfected fleas at Dugway Proving Ground.
- **1955** — Operation Big Buzz tests mass-reared uninfected Aedes aegypti and aerial/ground dissemination in Georgia.
- **1956** — Drop Kick / May Day urban mosquito work in Savannah period.
- **Late 1950s-1960s** — Continued U.S. vector and open-air biological testing; later histories describe additional Florida and sea-borne work.
- **25 Nov. 1969** — President Nixon renounces U.S. offensive biological warfare.
- **10 Apr. 1972** — Biological Weapons Convention opens for signature.
- **26 Mar. 1975** — BWC enters into force.
- **8 Mar. 1977** — Major public disclosure of 239 Army open-air BW-related tests reported.
- **1981** — William H. Rose's Army evaluation of entomological warfare reviews historical program and threat.
- **1998 onward** — Soviet-era documents bearing on Korean War BW fabrication become public; later Russian archival releases strengthen provenance.
- **2013/2015** — Wu Zhili memoir emerges in Chinese publication and English translation, calling Korean BW allegations a false alarm.
- **2020-2021** — WHO publishes positions/guidance on genetically modified mosquitoes; Yogyakarta Wolbachia RCT reports major dengue reduction.
- **2025** — WHO VCAG report includes evaluation of gene-drive-based transmission-reduction approaches.
- **11 Jun. 2026** — WHO announces updated systematic review/guideline process for Wolbachia population replacement.
