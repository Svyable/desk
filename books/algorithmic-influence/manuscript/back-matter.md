# Back Matter

## A Note on Evidence

*Algorithmic Influence* separates four kinds of claims that are too often blended together: documented system behavior, empirical findings, practitioner observation, and forward-looking scenarios. Ranking and recommendation systems change quickly, and many production systems remain partly opaque from the outside. When the manuscript makes a specific empirical claim, the research ledger—not creator folklore, platform mythology, or marketing copy—is the source of record.

There is no single thing called “the algorithm.” Search ranking, recommendation, hiring tools, dispatch systems, pricing models, reputation systems, generative answer engines, and agents differ in objectives, architecture, governance, and stakes. What joins them in this book is mediation: people adapting to computational systems that allocate access to other people, opportunities, information, and transactions.

## Key Terms

**Algorithmic influence** — Deliberate or incidental effort to shape how a consequential computational intermediary represents, ranks, predicts, selects, recommends, prices, allocates, or acts—so that something happens beyond the intermediary itself.

**Machine audience** — A computational intermediary whose interpretation of a message, person, product, action, or record affects whether and how it reaches an intended human outcome. The term is functional; it makes no claim that machines are conscious.

**Legibility drift** — Pressure on people, organizations, and artifacts to become easier for consequential systems to observe, compare, and reward.

**Folk physics** — The rough working theories people build about opaque systems from consequences, peer observation, experiments, documentation, rumor, and experience.

**Algorithmic tax** — Effort spent managing machine-facing representations or rituals for selection without improving the underlying human purpose.

**Machine-readable reputation** — Context-specific trust assembled from structured or verifiable evidence—identity, transaction history, provenance, credentials, policies, fulfillment, reviews, and other records—rather than collapsed into one universal reputation score.

**Contestability** — The practical ability to identify a consequential decision, understand enough of its basis to challenge it, supply corrective context or evidence, and obtain meaningful reconsideration.

## Acknowledgment of the Moving Target

This book was written while generative search, agentic commerce, recommender governance, automated employment tools, provenance standards, and AI regulation were moving underneath it. Product names will age. Implementations will disappear. The structural questions should survive them.

For any example in the book, the durable questions are the same:

- What is being selected?
- What representation does the system use?
- What behavior becomes rational because selection matters?
- What does that behavior do to the next round of data?
- Who can challenge the result?
- What remains outside the representation?
