# Back Matter

## A Note on Evidence

*Algorithmic Influence* distinguishes among documented system behavior, empirical findings, practitioner observation, and forward-looking scenarios. Ranking and recommendation systems change frequently, and many production systems are not fully observable from outside. Where the manuscript discusses specific effects, the underlying research ledger should be treated as the source of record rather than creator folklore or marketing claims.

The book deliberately avoids treating “the algorithm” as one universal object. Search ranking, recommendation, hiring tools, dispatch systems, pricing models, reputation systems, generative answer engines, and agents differ in objectives, architecture, governance, and stakes. The common mechanism examined here is mediation: people adapting to computational systems that allocate access to other people, opportunities, information, or transactions.

## Key Terms

**Algorithmic influence** — Effort, deliberate or incidental, to shape how a consequential computational intermediary represents, ranks, predicts, selects, recommends, prices, allocates, or acts in order to affect an outcome beyond the intermediary itself.

**Machine audience** — A computational intermediary whose interpretation of a message, person, product, action, or record affects whether and how it reaches an intended human outcome. The term is functional, not a claim that machines possess consciousness.

**Legibility drift** — The pressure for people, organizations, and artifacts to move toward characteristics that consequential systems can observe, compare, and reward.

**Folk physics** — Practical, approximate theories people build about opaque systems from consequences, peer observation, experimentation, documentation, rumor, and experience.

**Algorithmic tax** — Effort spent managing representations or rituals for machine-mediated selection that is detached from improving the underlying human purpose.

**Machine-readable reputation** — Context-specific trust assembled from structured or verifiable evidence such as identity, transaction history, provenance, credentials, policies, fulfillment, reviews, or other records rather than one universal reputation score.

**Contestability** — The practical ability to identify a consequential decision, understand enough of its basis to challenge it, provide corrective context or evidence, and obtain meaningful reconsideration.

## Acknowledgment of the Moving Target

This book was written while generative search, agentic commerce, recommender governance, automated employment tools, provenance standards, and AI regulation were changing rapidly. Product names and implementations will age. The structural questions are intended not to.

When reading any specific example, ask the same durable questions: What is being selected? What representation does the system use? What behavior becomes rational because selection matters? What does that behavior do to the future data? Who can challenge the result? What remains outside the representation?
