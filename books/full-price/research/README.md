# Research Brief

## Thesis under test

*Full Price* asks whether a single molecular discovery — a durable GLP-1 receptor agonist, first found in a desert lizard's venom and later engineered from scratch by a Danish drugmaker — has been large enough, fast enough, and unequal enough in its rollout to count as a genuine paradigm shift in the economics of human appetite, rather than merely a successful new pharmaceutical product line.

The easy, overstated version of that claim would be that these drugs have already "solved" obesity, or that every downstream industry effect reported in the press is confirmed fact. That version is false and the book does not make it. The version the evidence actually supports is narrower and more durable:

> A GLP-1/GIP receptor agonist class, discovered through decades of underfunded and in one documented case severely undercredited basic science, produced clinical trial results large enough to reclassify obesity as a directly treatable chronic disease, generated real and measurable demand-side effects across food, alcohol, retail, and travel industries, and did all of this before the regulatory, insurance, and pricing systems built for a slower era of drug adoption had caught up — producing a pricing and access regime that is, by the book's own account, still unresolved, unequal, and moving in real time as this book goes to press.

The book should survive contact with contrary evidence. Where a widely repeated claim could not be verified (Southwest Airlines' weight-assumption methodology, amusement-park ride restrictions tied to GLP-1 use, a verbatim Diageo or Kraft Heinz executive quote naming the drugs directly), the book says so explicitly rather than smuggling the claim in as settled fact. Where real-world data undercuts the trial-level efficacy story (STEP 1 extension weight regain, Prime Therapeutics persistence data, the SURMOUNT-1 lean-mass findings), that evidence is preserved rather than argued away.

## Core empirical tests

1. **Scientific credit:** Does the standard, corporate-friendly telling of the GLP-1 discovery story actually match the underlying, more diffuse and more inequitably credited history — and specifically, was Svetlana Mojsov's contribution genuinely minimized for decades, as the book claims?
2. **Clinical magnitude:** Do the STEP, SURMOUNT, and SELECT trials actually show what the book says they show, at the scale the book says they show it, without inflating a surrogate endpoint (weight loss) into a claim about a harder outcome (mortality, cardiovascular events) the data does not support without the SELECT trial specifically?
3. **Market reality:** Did Novo Nordisk's market capitalization actually move the way the book describes, and can the CagriSema-driven correction actually be traced to specific, dated trial readouts rather than asserted as a vague "market mood" shift?
4. **Downstream industry effects:** Which claimed effects on food, alcohol, travel, retail, and fitness companies are backed by a named executive's own on-the-record statement or a peer-reviewed/institutional study, and which are analyst speculation dressed as fact?
5. **Access and pricing:** Do the specific dollar figures cited for US list prices, international comparisons, employer coverage rates, and Medicare policy history hold up against primary government, KFF, and company sources — and where sources disagree (Germany/Canada price variants, ICER's net-price units), does the book flag the disagreement rather than picking a number silently?
6. **Counter-evidence:** What are the strongest, most credentialed arguments that this book's own optimism about these drugs is overstated — on safety, on real-world adherence, on financialization, and on cultural harm to people in larger bodies?

## Falsification and counter-evidence

Every chapter looks for evidence against the chapter's own framing before moving to the next one.

- Chapter 6 flags that STEP 1's own placebo arm lost real weight (2.4%), crediting structured lifestyle counseling rather than treating the semaglutide result as the only active ingredient in the trial.
- Chapter 8 treats Novo Nordisk's 2024-2026 correction as real evidence against a simple "these drugs always win" narrative, not as a footnote to the earlier valuation story.
- Chapter 14 preserves Dave Lewis's on-the-record skepticism about the GLP-1-alcohol narrative directly alongside Terry Smith's bearish bet, rather than resolving the disagreement in the book's own voice.
- Chapter 15 explicitly corrects and withdraws an unverifiable claim (Southwest's 2024 weight-assumption update) that circulates widely in casual discussion of this topic, rather than repeating it.
- Chapter 17 states plainly that the "chronic disease" framing used by manufacturers and by this book's own earlier chapters is also, not coincidentally, the framing most favorable to manufacturer revenue.
- Chapter 20 is built entirely from credentialed counter-evidence: FDA safety review findings, active multidistrict litigation, real-world persistence data showing high discontinuation rates, and financial-press skepticism about the "GLP-1 economy" investment thesis.
- Chapter 19 gives the strongest version of the fat-acceptance and eating-disorder-clinician case against the book's own celebratory science chapters a full hearing, including the specific, historically loaded "eugenics" framing, rather than softening it.

## Evidence lanes

### I. Discovery science
John Eng and exendin-4 (Bronx VA); Jean-Pierre Raufman's original Gila monster venom assay; Daniel Drucker's incretin physiology career; Svetlana Mojsov's GLP-1(7-36)amide identification at Rockefeller University and her 2021 Gairdner exclusion / 2024 Lasker~DeBakey Award; Rosalyn Yalow and radioimmunoassay.

### II. Drug development and corporate history
Novo Nordisk's founding (August and Marie Krogh, Hans Christian Hagedorn, the Pedersen brothers, the 1989 merger); Lotte Bjerre Knudsen's fatty-acid acylation program; Victoza (2010), Ozempic (2017), Wegovy (2021) approvals; Eli Lilly's tirzepatide program, Mounjaro (2022) and Zepbound (2023) approvals; SURMOUNT-5 head-to-head data.

### III. Pivotal clinical trials
STEP 1 (Wilding et al., NEJM 2021); SURMOUNT-1 (Jastreboff et al., NEJM 2022); SELECT (Lincoff et al., NEJM 2023); SURMOUNT-1 body-composition sub-study (Look et al., 2025); STEP 1 withdrawal extension (Wilding et al., 2022).

### IV. Markets and financial history
Novo Nordisk's 2023 market-cap peak and "bigger than Denmark" comparisons; the December 2024 CagriSema readout and subsequent guidance cuts; Eli Lilly's November 2025 $1 trillion market cap; Goldman Sachs/Morgan Stanley/JPMorgan total-market-size estimates.

### V. Supply chain and safety
FDA shortage designations and resolutions (2022-2025); compounding-pharmacy litigation; counterfeit-pen warnings; FDA suicidal-ideation review (2023-2026); the GLP-1 RA gastroparesis MDL (No. 3094) and NAION MDL (No. 3163); the SURMOUNT-1 lean-mass sub-study; the STEP 1 extension weight-regain data.

### VI. Pricing and policy
US list pricing and 2026-2027 price cuts; KFF/Peterson-KFF international price comparisons; the Sanders-Novo Nordisk Senate HELP hearing (September 2024); the Melissa Barber manufacturing-cost study; the 2003 Medicare Part D exclusion and its 2024 SELECT-trial-driven partial opening; the withdrawn 2024 CMS obesity-coverage rule; the November 2025 White House/TrumpRx deal; ICER's December 2025 evidence report.

### VII. Culture, industry disruption, and dissent
Walmart, General Mills, and PepsiCo earnings-call commentary; Cornell/Numerator grocery-spending study; Diageo and Constellation Brands; NIAAA/Lancet alcohol-use-disorder trials; bariatric-surgery volume data (Mass General Brigham/Harvard); Torrid and Circana retail data; Johann Hari's *Magic Pill*; Prime Therapeutics persistence data; NEDA, the Renfrew Center, and Virgie Tovar's fat-acceptance critique.

## Source discipline

- Prefer primary sources: FDA approval records and safety communications, peer-reviewed journal publications, company press releases and SEC/investor filings, congressional hearing records and committee reports, and court/MDL filings.
- Where only secondary reporting was reachable, or where a figure could only be confirmed through search-engine synthesis rather than direct retrieval of the source page, the source ledger and the manuscript both flag that limitation rather than presenting the figure as fully verified.
- Distinguish a Phase 2 finding from a Phase 3 finding, a topline press-release result from a peer-reviewed publication, and an allegation in a pending lawsuit from a court's actual holding.
- Distinguish a trial's surrogate endpoint (weight lost) from a harder clinical outcome (cardiovascular events, mortality) and never let one silently stand in for the other.
- Record the vintage of every price, market-share, and litigation-status claim; several were actively moving as this book was completed in the second half of 2026.
- Never invent a quotation, statistic, or attribution. Where a number or exact quote could not be independently confirmed with confidence, the manuscript uses hedged, qualitative language rather than a fabricated precise figure.
- Preserve counter-evidence, minority findings, and unresolved scientific disputes (e.g., GIP agonism vs. antagonism) rather than selecting only the findings that fit the thesis.
