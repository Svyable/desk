# Source Log

## Primary / official spine

1. **FRUS 1964–1968, Vol. XXVIII, Laos, Document 250 — McNamara to Johnson, “Project Pop Eye,” September 15, 1966.** Establishes the covert operational experiment, its intended area, timing near the end of the monsoon, and the stated objective of damaging trail structure by heavy rain. https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1964-68v28/d250

2. **FRUS 1964–1968, Vol. XXVIII, Laos, Document 274 — Kohler to Rusk, “Weather Modification in North Vietnam and Laos (Project Popeye),” January 13, 1967.** Central source for the 1966 test summary: more than 50 experiments, reported 82% rain response, the nine-inch/four-hour Special Forces camp incident, the internal judgment that tests were highly successful, explicit uncertainty about rainfall magnitude and area, Laos-consent concerns, civilian/crop discussion, and publicity risk. https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1964-68v28/d274

3. **National Science Foundation, Special Commission on Weather Modification, *Weather and Climate Modification*, NSF 66-3 (1965/1966).** Contemporary federal scientific review establishing the evidentiary environment immediately before Popeye: it says conclusive evidence acceptable to the scientific community on ground-precipitation effects remained lacking nearly twenty years after the first seeding experiments, while also reporting positive statistical findings from a National Academy of Sciences analysis and treating weather modification as a serious research field. Use this to avoid both retrospective dismissal and retrospective certainty. https://www.nsf.gov/nsb/publications/1965/nsb1265.pdf

4. **U.S. Senate Committee on Foreign Relations, Subcommittee on Oceans and International Environment, Weather Modification hearings, January 25 and March 20, 1974.** Primary congressional oversight record for environmental/geophysical modification as weapons and Defense weather-modification activity. https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/CHRG-93shrg29544O

5. **U.S. House Committee on Foreign Affairs, Weather Modification as a Weapon of War, September 24, 1974.** Parallel legislative record on international prohibition. https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/CHRG-93hhrg40949

6. **FRUS 1969–1976, environmental warfare policy study, Document 74.** Important later U.S. policy synthesis: distinguishes operational/near-operational weather-modification techniques from speculative capabilities and describes Southeast Asia rain augmentation from 1966–1972 as the principal U.S. combat experience in military weather modification. https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76ve14p2/d74

7. **Convention on the Prohibition of Military or Any Other Hostile Use of Environmental Modification Techniques (ENMOD).** Adopted December 10, 1976; opened for signature May 18, 1977; entered into force October 5, 1978. ICRC overview: https://www.icrc.org/en/document/1976-convention-prohibition-military-or-any-hostile-use-environmental-modification

## Contemporary disclosure / provenance

8. **Congressional Record, July 20, 1972.** Preserves contemporary discussion and reporting about military weather modification in Southeast Asia. CIA Reading Room scan of the Congressional Record item: https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/CIA-RDP74B00415R000100110006-9.pdf

## Claim discipline

- Treat senior-official descriptions of the 1966 test as evidence of official assessment, not as substitutes for the unrecovered raw mission dataset.
- Do not use `82%` as a causal effect size until untreated comparison logic and cloud-selection methods are recovered.
- Treat the contemporary scientific record as mixed rather than dismissive: serious federal reviewers saw plausible and sometimes positive evidence while emphasizing unresolved experimental proof.
- Do not describe the nine-inch camp event as wholly caused by seeding; the sequence is documented, causal increment unresolved.
- Do not infer operational capability for hurricane steering, earthquake induction, climate control, or ionospheric manipulation from Popeye.
- Do not claim ENMOD was caused solely by Popeye; document the broader scientific, congressional, environmental-diplomatic, and U.S.–Soviet context.
- Do not infer Lao governmental consent from Ambassador Sullivan's knowledge/concurrence; the 1967 memorandum explicitly distinguishes those facts.

## Highest-value source recovery

- Complete 1966 experimental mission logs and meteorological data, especially the basis for the “normal expectation” against which the 82% response rate was judged.
- 1967–1972 operational sortie records, code-name changes, aircraft/unit records, seeding-material consumption, and target maps.
- Air Weather Service / 54th Weather Reconnaissance Squadron records where relevant.
- China Lake weather-modification test documentation referenced by McNamara.
- Weekly operational summaries requested by State and any incident reports.
- Vietnamese transport-engineering and route-maintenance records.
- Lao governmental/diplomatic records and regional/local accounts.
- Gauge/radar/reanalysis weather data sufficient to build untreated counterfactuals.
- Appropriation, notification, and congressional-oversight records establishing who knew what, when.
- ENMOD negotiating records sufficient to identify Popeye's actual causal weight in treaty formation rather than repeating retrospective shorthand.
