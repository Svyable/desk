# Chronology

- **1940s–1950s:** Modern cloud-seeding research develops around dry ice, silver iodide, supercooled cloud microphysics, and field experimentation. Civilian, commercial, and military interests overlap.
- **September 15, 1966:** Secretary of Defense Robert McNamara sends President Johnson a memorandum describing Project Pop Eye as a covert, highly classified operational experiment in the Lao panhandle intended to increase rainfall and damage trail structure.
- **October 1966:** Test phase conducted in Laos. According to the January 1967 State memorandum, more than 50 seeding experiments are flown without consultation with Lao authorities, though Ambassador Sullivan knows and concurs.
- **January 13, 1967:** State Department memorandum summarizes the tests as strongly successful while recording uncertainty over rainfall magnitude and affected area; weighs operations in North Vietnam and Laos, civilian effects, crop risks, allied sovereignty, and publicity.
- **Early 1967:** Secretary of State Dean Rusk approves limited activity on an experimental basis, according to the FRUS editorial note, without committing to broader application.
- **1967–1972:** U.S. military rain-augmentation operations continue in Southeast Asia. Exact mission chronology, unit records, code names, targets, and effect estimates require further primary-source reconstruction.
- **1971–1972:** Scientific and political concern about military weather modification becomes increasingly public; National Academy and international environmental discussions raise the desirability of rules for weather modification.
- **July 1972:** Congressional Record preserves public allegations/reporting regarding U.S. weather-modification operations in Southeast Asia.
- **1973:** Senate adopts a resolution pressing for international agreement restricting environmental/geophysical modification as weapons.
- **January 25 and March 20, 1974:** Senate Foreign Relations Subcommittee on Oceans and International Environment holds weather-modification hearings, including Defense activity and proposals for international prohibition.
- **September 24, 1974:** House Foreign Affairs subcommittee holds hearing on weather modification as a weapon of war.
- **Mid-1970s:** U.S. interagency policy work treats Southeast Asian rain augmentation as the principal American operational combat experience with military weather modification while distinguishing mature techniques from speculative ones.
- **December 10, 1976:** UN General Assembly adopts ENMOD.
- **May 18, 1977:** ENMOD opens for signature.
- **October 5, 1978:** ENMOD enters into force.
- **January 17, 1980:** United States ratification becomes part of the treaty record.

## Chronology cautions

Do not silently fill the 1967–1972 operational gap with secondary lore. That interval is a principal research task. Distinguish test-phase events from sustained operational missions and distinguish authorization dates from flight dates. Code-name variants should be traced to documents rather than standardized retroactively.
