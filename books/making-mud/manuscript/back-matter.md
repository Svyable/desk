# Back Matter

## A note on evidence

Operation Popeye attracts two kinds of overstatement. One turns a documented military cloud-seeding program into proof of broad weather control. The other treats uncertainty about strategic efficacy as grounds to dismiss the operation itself. This manuscript rejects both.

The source trail uses four evidence classes throughout the research apparatus. Verified/declassified/documented facts include official authorization memoranda, Foreign Relations of the United States documents, congressional hearings, later executive-branch policy reviews, treaty texts, and technical literature whose provenance can be checked. Credible but incomplete evidence includes participant recollections, contemporary reporting, and official claims that are not accompanied by the underlying mission-level dataset. Disputed allegations include claims about specific effects or capabilities for which independent corroboration is absent. Speculative synthesis is the author's interpretive work and is labeled accordingly.

The most important unresolved empirical task is a mission-level reconstruction of the 1966 test and subsequent operational years. The surviving policy summaries establish what senior officials believed. They do not substitute for the complete meteorological and logistics dataset needed to estimate causal effect.

## Research posture

The book intentionally does not infer efficacy from classification, does not infer a program from a patent, does not treat a proposal as deployment, and does not convert official confidence into scientific proof. It also does not assume that uncertainty means zero effect.

The next edition should add Vietnamese- and Lao-language sources, mission logs, contemporaneous meteorological data, route-maintenance records, aircraft and seeding-material records, and a formal reconstruction of the operation's statistical denominator.
