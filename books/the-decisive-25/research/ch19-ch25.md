# Chapters 19–25 evidence ledger

## Chapter 19 — Martin Luther King Jr., 1956–1965

### Working claim

King did not create the civil-rights movement and did not single-handedly pass federal civil-rights laws. His distinctive leverage was conversion: he helped turn local campaigns and state repression into nationally legible moral and political crises, increasing the cost of federal inaction while organizers, lawyers, legislators, presidents, churches, students, and local communities supplied other indispensable forms of power.

### Primary / institutional anchors

- U.S. National Park Service, Martin Luther King Jr. civil-rights overview, including Birmingham, March on Washington, Selma, and his role around the Civil Rights and Voting Rights Acts: https://www.nps.gov/subjects/civilrights/martin-luther-king.htm
- U.S. National Park Service, Voting Rights Act of 1965 history, including Selma and the federal legislative response: https://home.nps.gov/articles/votingrightsact.htm
- National Archives, Civil Rights Act of 1964 milestone materials: https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/civil-rights-act
- National Archives, Voting Rights Act of 1965 milestone materials: https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/voting-rights-act
- Library of Congress, Civil Rights History Project and movement oral histories: https://www.loc.gov/collections/civil-rights-history-project/

### Secondary checks

- Taylor Branch, *Parting the Waters* and *Pillar of Fire*.
- David J. Garrow, *Bearing the Cross*.
- Barbara Ransby, *Ella Baker and the Black Freedom Movement*, as a corrective to charismatic-leader history.
- Charles M. Payne, *I've Got the Light of Freedom*, for local organizing and movement infrastructure.

### Strongest counterargument

Lyndon B. Johnson had more direct leverage over the statutes themselves through congressional bargaining and presidential authority. Ella Baker, Bayard Rustin, Fred Shuttlesworth, Diane Nash, John Lewis, Fannie Lou Hamer, Bob Moses, and many local organizers supplied movement capacity King did not control. Malcolm X transformed Black political language and movement debate.

### Falsification note

Change the selection if the federal legislative timetable proves largely independent of King's national role, or if Johnson's personal legislative leverage, Baker's organizational leverage, or another figure moves the 1956–1965 counterfactual farther.

## Chapter 20 — Richard Nixon, 1966–1975

### Working claim

Nixon's leverage spans both realignment and rupture: he helped translate white backlash, cultural anxiety, and distrust into a durable Republican electoral path; opened relations with the People's Republic of China and pursued détente; governed through significant regulatory expansion; and then abused executive power so severely that Watergate reshaped congressional, judicial, journalistic, and public expectations of the presidency.

### Primary / institutional anchors

- National Archives, Richard Nixon biography, including China rapprochement, détente, EPA creation, school desegregation, and resignation: https://www.archives.gov/presidential-libraries/events/centennials/nixon/biography.html
- U.S. Department of State, Office of the Historian, “Rapprochement with China, 1972”: https://history.state.gov/milestones/1969-1976/rapprochement-china
- National Archives, Watergate exhibit, including June 1972 break-in, executive abuses, constitutional crisis, and Nixon's August 9, 1974 resignation: https://www.archives.gov/exhibits/american_originals/nixon.html
- U.S. Environmental Protection Agency, institutional history and founding materials: https://www.epa.gov/history
- Supreme Court, *United States v. Nixon*, 418 U.S. 683 (1974), for the tapes dispute and executive-privilege boundary.

### Secondary checks

- John A. Farrell, *Richard Nixon: The Life*.
- Melvin Small, *The Presidency of Richard Nixon*.
- Rick Perlstein, *Nixonland*, for backlash politics, used with narrower electoral scholarship.
- Jeffrey Kimball, *Nixon's Vietnam War*, for secrecy, strategy, and limits of presidential control.

### Strongest counterargument

Lyndon Johnson's Great Society, civil-rights record, and Vietnam escalation shaped the first half of the interval and much of Nixon's inheritance. Henry Kissinger supplied substantial foreign-policy design. George Wallace demonstrated backlash politics independently of Nixon. Watergate depended on a network of aides, campaign operatives, prosecutors, journalists, judges, congressional investigators, and whistleblowers.

### Falsification note

Change the selection if Nixon's electoral strategy proves too derivative of broader partisan movement, or if Johnson's earlier statutory state and Vietnam choices produce greater persistent counterfactual distance across the full interval.

## Chapter 21 — Ronald Reagan, 1976–1985

### Working claim

Reagan's distinctive leverage was ideological normalization. Conservative arguments about taxation, regulation, labor, markets, anti-communism, and federal overreach predated him; he made them the governing center and changed the burden of proof surrounding domestic federal action. The chapter explicitly separates Reagan's political influence from Paul Volcker's decisive role in breaking inflation.

### Primary / institutional anchors

- Ronald Reagan Presidential Library, Reagan presidency overview, including 1981 tax legislation, regulatory agenda, military buildup, and first-term milestones: https://www.reaganlibrary.gov/reagans/reagan-administration/reagan-presidency
- Reagan Library, remarks signing the Economic Recovery Tax Act of 1981: https://www.reaganlibrary.gov/archives/speech/remarks-signing-economic-recovery-tax-act-1981-and-omnibus-budget-reconciliation
- Reagan Library, August 3, 1981 PATCO strike remarks: https://www.reaganlibrary.gov/archives/speech/remarks-and-question-and-answer-session-reporters-air-traffic-controllers-strike
- Reagan Library, December 1981 statement on discharged controllers: https://www.reaganlibrary.gov/archives/speech/statement-federal-employment-discharged-air-traffic-controllers
- Federal Reserve History and Volcker-era materials for inflation, monetary tightening, and the 1981–82 recession.

### Secondary checks

- Lou Cannon, *President Reagan: The Role of a Lifetime*.
- Sean Wilentz, *The Age of Reagan*.
- Kim Phillips-Fein, *Invisible Hands*, for conservative business mobilization before Reagan.
- William Greider, *Secrets of the Temple*, checked against Federal Reserve scholarship, for Volcker and monetary politics.

### Strongest counterargument

Paul Volcker may have produced larger direct economic leverage through monetary policy. Jimmy Carter appointed Volcker, advanced deregulation, normalized relations with China, and began strategic shifts Reagan inherited. Conservative realignment had deep roots in Goldwater, business mobilization, tax revolt, evangelical organization, racial backlash, and changing regional coalitions.

### Falsification note

Change the selection if the conservative governing shift was already sufficiently inevitable that Reagan mainly supplied rhetoric, while Volcker's anti-inflation regime or Carter's institutional decisions created greater persistent leverage.

## Chapter 22 — Bill Gates, 1986–1995

### Working claim

Gates's leverage was platform control rather than invention of personal computing. Microsoft's DOS/Windows licensing model and growing developer/user network made the operating system a common assumption across competing PC manufacturers, accelerating personal computing while creating private rule-setting power over the software ecosystem.

### Primary / institutional anchors

- U.S. Department of Justice, *United States v. Microsoft* court findings of fact, including IBM's selection of MS-DOS, Windows development, Windows 95, operating-system dominance, developer dependence, and Gates's involvement in platform strategy: https://www.justice.gov/atr/us-v-microsoft-courts-findings-fact
- U.S. Department of Justice, 1995 Windows 95 investigation statement: https://www.justice.gov/archive/atr/public/press_releases/1995/0327.htm
- U.S. Department of Justice, Microsoft antitrust complaint and later court materials for the transition from valuable platform integration to exclusion concerns: https://www.justice.gov/atr/complaint-us-v-microsoft-corp
- Computer History Museum materials on IBM PC, Microsoft, graphical interfaces, and the personal-computing ecosystem: https://computerhistory.org/

### Secondary checks

- Paul Andrews, *How the Web Was Won*.
- James Wallace and Jim Erickson, *Hard Drive*.
- Michael A. Cusumano and Richard W. Selby, *Microsoft Secrets*.
- Histories of IBM, Apple, Intel, Lotus, WordPerfect, and the PC-clone ecosystem to avoid Microsoft-only causation.

### Strongest counterargument

IBM's architecture created the opportunity; Intel supplied the hardware platform; Apple and Steve Jobs transformed interface expectations; independent developers made Windows valuable. Sam Walton, Oprah Winfrey, Alan Greenspan, George H. W. Bush, and other figures had enormous non-computing leverage in the same decade.

### Falsification note

Change the selection if PC standardization would likely have converged on a comparable platform at nearly the same speed without Gates, or if the personal-computing revolution's daily-life effect proves less persistent than a rival's economic, political, or cultural leverage.

## Chapter 23 — Osama bin Laden, 1996–2005

### Working claim

Bin Laden's leverage was asymmetric: al Qaeda's September 11 attack triggered a U.S. reorganization vastly larger than the organization itself. The chapter distinguishes the attack from American policy choices after it; bin Laden did not “cause” every later war or security policy, but without a 9/11-scale attack the timing and form of Afghanistan, homeland-security reorganization, surveillance law, airport security, intelligence reform, and the political case for preventive war would be radically different.

### Primary / institutional anchors

- National Commission on Terrorist Attacks Upon the United States, *The 9/11 Commission Report*, executive summary and full report: https://9-11commission.gov/report/911Report_Exec.htm
- 9/11 Commission report home and supporting materials: https://9-11commission.gov/report/
- FBI, September 11 investigation history and al Qaeda materials: https://www.fbi.gov/history/famous-cases/911-investigation
- GovInfo, post-9/11 statutes and congressional materials for the Authorization for Use of Military Force and USA PATRIOT Act.
- Department of Homeland Security, institutional history for the post-9/11 reorganization: https://www.dhs.gov/history

### Secondary checks

- Lawrence Wright, *The Looming Tower*.
- Steve Coll, *Ghost Wars*.
- Daniel Benjamin and Steven Simon, *The Age of Sacred Terror*.
- 9/11-era intelligence and Iraq-war scholarship used to separate al Qaeda's responsibility from later U.S. discretionary choices.

### Strongest counterargument

George W. Bush had larger direct leverage over the shape of the response, especially the Iraq War. Khalid Sheikh Mohammed was central to the 9/11 plot. The attacks depended on Taliban sanctuary, the nineteen hijackers, facilitators, security vulnerabilities, and institutional failures. Treating every post-9/11 action as bin Laden's causal achievement would erase U.S. agency.

### Falsification note

Change the selection if the 9/11 plot appears likely to have proceeded on similar scale without bin Laden's leadership and organization, while Bush's discretionary policy choices create substantially larger counterfactual distance over the decade.

## Chapter 24 — Steve Jobs, 2006–2015

### Working claim

Jobs did not invent the smartphone or build the iPhone alone. His distinctive leverage was product integration at the moment multiple technologies became ready: multi-touch interface, full web access, later the App Store, and a tightly controlled hardware/software platform helped reset consumer expectations and accelerate the smartphone from specialized device to mass social infrastructure.

### Primary / institutional anchors

- Apple, January 9, 2007 iPhone announcement, presenting the device as phone, iPod, and internet communicator with a multi-touch interface: https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2007/01/09Apple-Reinvents-the-Phone-with-iPhone/
- Pew Research Center, 2013 iPhone/smartphone adoption analysis, noting that smartphones had become majority-owned among U.S. adults and that iPhone and Android were the dominant platforms: https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2013/06/29/as-it-turns-6-a-look-at-who-uses-the-iphone-no-not-everybody/
- Pew Research Center, 2014 internet-adoption report, describing the 2007 smartphone introduction as a major leap in mobile internet access: https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2014/02/27/part-1-how-the-internet-has-woven-itself-into-american-life/
- Pew Research Center mobile phone ownership time series: https://www.pewresearch.org/chart/mobile-phone-ownership-2/
- Pew Research Center, 2012 report on app growth and the shift of usage toward mobile applications: https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2012/03/23/the-future-of-apps-and-web/

### Secondary checks

- Walter Isaacson, *Steve Jobs*, checked against Apple-independent technical histories.
- Brian Merchant, *The One Device*, for the distributed engineering, labor, supply-chain, and component history behind the iPhone.
- Histories of BlackBerry, Nokia, Android, Samsung, Qualcomm, mobile networks, and touchscreen development to prevent lone-inventor framing.

### Strongest counterargument

Smartphones were technologically ripe and would have spread without Jobs. Google/Android, BlackBerry, Nokia, Samsung, wireless carriers, chipmakers, and component suppliers were independent forces. Barack Obama, Ben Bernanke, Mark Zuckerberg, Jeff Bezos, Larry Page/Sergey Brin, and Satoshi Nakamoto all have strong claims in politics, finance, information, commerce, or money.

### Falsification note

Change the selection if the iPhone's timing and interface choices altered adoption only marginally, with smartphone convergence essentially locked in, or if Obama/Bernanke/Zuckerberg/another rival produces a larger persistent American counterfactual.

## Chapter 25 — Donald Trump, 2016–2026

### Evidence window

**Freeze date: September 6, 2026.** The chapter may state events only through this date unless the ledger and prose are deliberately refreshed together.

### Working claim

Trump did not create polarization, social media, anti-establishment politics, immigration conflict, or conservative realignment. His distinctive leverage was personal fusion: he made continuous political combat, direct digital attention, anti-institutional grievance, and loyalty to a leader central organizing features of a major party, then demonstrated the durability of that system by winning in 2016, losing in 2020, remaining dominant after the January 6 crisis, and returning to the presidency in 2024.

### Primary / institutional anchors

- National Archives, 2016 Electoral College results: Trump 304, Clinton 227, with faithless electors recorded separately: https://www.archives.gov/electoral-college/2016
- National Archives, 2020 Electoral College results: Biden 306, Trump 232; congressional count completed January 7, 2021 after violence at the Capitol interrupted proceedings: https://www.archives.gov/electoral-college/2020
- GovInfo, Select January 6th Committee Final Report and supporting-material collection: https://www.govinfo.gov/collection/january-6th-committee-final-report
- National Archives, 2024 Electoral College results: Trump 312, Harris 226: https://www.archives.gov/electoral-college/2024
- White House, current administration page identifying Trump as 45th and 47th president: https://www.whitehouse.gov/administration/

### Current snapshot anchors — September 2026

- Associated Press, September 6, 2026 midterm overview describing Trump as central to the 2026 election environment and to both parties' strategies: https://apnews.com/article/f07499f53269e37ccd2565564cc01e4e
- Reuters, September 5, 2026 federal appeals court decision involving the Trump administration's attempted use of citizenship data for voter-roll checks, illustrating continuing executive/state/court conflict in the second term: https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/federal-appeals-court-upholds-ban-trumps-bid-use-citizenship-data-voter-checks-2026-09-05/

### Secondary checks

- Serious scholarship on Trumpism, party realignment, democratic institutions, executive power, political communication, immigration, trade, and social media should be used in a later revision pass; avoid relying on campaign books or partisan instant histories as causal authorities.
- Comparative-democracy research is relevant to institutional claims, but the chapter must distinguish general frameworks from specific factual claims about the United States.

### Strongest counterargument

Joe Biden defeated Trump in 2020 and enacted a major four-year policy record. Barack Obama's legacy and the reaction to it helped structure the decade. Mark Zuckerberg and Elon Musk changed information systems through which politics operates. Vladimir Putin, external to the United States, affected elections, alliances, war, and security. Many forces associated with Trumpism predated Trump and may have found another political vehicle.

### Falsification note

Change the selection if Trump proves primarily an expression of a transformation that would have produced nearly the same party, media, institutional, and policy changes under another leader, or if a platform owner, president, foreign actor, or other rival moves the 2016–2026 American counterfactual farther.

### Language boundary

Importance is not endorsement. Describe certified election results, court and congressional records, policy choices, and institutional consequences directly. Distinguish allegations, findings, convictions, reversals, appeals, and political claims. Do not convert campaign rhetoric from either supporters or opponents into neutral narration. Because the interval is still live in 2026, prefer dated descriptions over claims about a settled legacy.
