# Evidence ledger — Without Permission

Last research pass: 2026-09-16

## Governing rule

No claim moves upward in confidence because it makes the story better. Every health claim requires exposure plus toxicology/epidemiology; every secrecy claim requires a document or attributed testimony; every test-specific claim requires test-specific evidence.

## Class 1 — verified / documented

### National program and purpose

The National Research Council's 1997 assessment states that during the 1950s and 1960s Stanford University and other Army Chemical Corps contractors conducted ZnCdS dispersion tests in Minneapolis, Corpus Christi, Fort Wayne, St. Louis and 29 other urban/rural U.S. and Canadian locations. It places these tests within the biological-warfare program and states purposes including determining how BW agents disperse and estimating munitions requirements for strategic use against selected cities/areas.

Source: https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/5739/chapter/2

### Toxicological conclusion

The NRC concluded that ZnCdS was unlikely to produce systemic toxicity given its apparent low solubility/bioavailability and that exposures during the tests were far below the much higher and longer cadmium exposures associated with lung cancer. It concluded lung-cancer risk, if any, was very low.

Sources:
- https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/5739/chapter/6
- https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/5739/chapter/8

### External review of Army risk assessments

EPA experts broadly agreed that the Army's conclusion of no health threat was likely correct but criticized narrow framing and suggested improvements, including treatment of sensitive populations. CDC and ATSDR experts also reviewed Army assessments.

Source: https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/5739/chapter/19

### Measurement limitations

NRC Appendix F states that airborne fluorescent-particle concentrations were measured using impingement and filtration methods, while methods at specific locations were not described in detail in Army risk-assessment documents.

Source: https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/5739/chapter/17

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete

- Individual exposure reconstruction is limited by location, plume timing, sampling density, archival completeness and retrospective modeling.
- Public notice/knowledge likely differed across tests. Do not generalize from one city until primary records establish a common policy.
- Contemporaneous safety reasoning for ZnCdS requires reconstruction from original Chemical Corps toxicology/procurement records; later NRC findings cannot simply be projected backward as proof of what every planner knew.

## Class 3 — disputed / unsupported unless upgraded

- Specific cancer clusters caused by ZnCdS releases.
- Claims that ZnCdS itself was a biological weapon.
- Claims that every city release contained live bacteria or pathogens.
- Claims about radioactive additives, unusual contaminants or deliberate targeting of particular racial groups without primary documentation and material chain of custody.
- Claims that secrecy itself proves malicious intent.

## Class 4 — synthesis / conjecture

### Permission by substitution

Working thesis: replacing dangerous biological agents with a low-risk tracer solved enough of the safety problem that planners could treat an inhabited city as experimental terrain, while the ethical status of involuntary civilian participation became easier to ignore.

This is interpretation. Test it against contemporaneous approval memoranda, legal review, public-affairs plans and safety documents.

### Classification debt

Working thesis: delayed disclosure creates a second-order harm independent of toxic dose by making later exposure reconstruction, trust and causal adjudication harder. This is not a medical-effect claim.

## Contradiction ledger

1. **Secret test / low estimated health risk** — both can be true. Secrecy does not establish toxicity; low toxicity does not establish legitimacy.
2. **BW purpose / inert tracer** — tests can serve biological-warfare planning without releasing a biological weapon.
3. **Population inside plume / not conventional medical subjects** — residents can be involuntarily exposed without the experiment measuring their biological response.
4. **Cadmium in name / compound-specific risk** — toxicological inference must account for chemical form, solubility, dose and duration.
5. **Incomplete records / strong claims** — archival gaps increase uncertainty; they do not license filling the gap with the most alarming scenario.

## Incentive ledger

- Army/Chemical Corps: operational knowledge, program protection, classification, institutional reputation.
- Later Army risk assessors: accurate reconstruction but also institutional interest in resolving public concern.
- Residents/advocates: legitimate demand for disclosure; illness experience may increase salience of causal explanations.
- Journalists/politicians: incentives toward memorable framing, especially words such as 'sprayed,' 'cadmium,' 'secret' and 'experiment.'
- Scientific reviewers: methodological conservatism; later review cannot recover records that no longer exist.
