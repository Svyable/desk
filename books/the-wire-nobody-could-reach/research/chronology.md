# Chronology

## 1950s–1960s — platform ancestry

- 1950s: The U.S. Navy develops Regulus cruise-missile submarine operations.
- 1960: USS *Halibut* enters commissioned service as a nuclear-powered guided-missile submarine built around Regulus.
- Mid-1960s: Polaris ballistic-missile submarines make the Regulus architecture strategically obsolete. *Halibut* is converted toward attack/special-project work rather than discarded.
- Late 1960s: U.S. deep-ocean search, diving, sonar and special-project capabilities expand. *Halibut* becomes associated publicly with the search for Soviet submarine K-129 and other highly sensitive undersea work.

## 1971 onward — Ivy Bells reconstruction

- 1971: Serious public histories place the first successful Okhotsk undersea-cable operation in this period, with USS *Halibut* entering the Sea of Okhotsk and divers working at roughly 400 feet. This remains Class 2 until a direct declassified mission record is located.
- Early/mid-1970s: Public histories describe repeat missions to service the listening system and recover recordings for NSA processing.
- 1970s: Later U.S. special-mission submarines reportedly inherit or expand related undersea collection roles. Mission-by-mission attribution remains classified/incomplete.

## 1979–1987 — Pelton and public exposure

- 1979: Ronald Pelton leaves NSA.
- January 1980: According to *United States v. Pelton*, he decides to sell classified NSA information to the Soviet Union and initiates contact.
- 1980–1983: Pelton meets Soviet representatives in Washington and Vienna, compromises several classified NSA operations and receives more than $35,000.
- 1981: Public Ivy Bells histories place Soviet recovery/discovery of U.S. cable-tap hardware around this year. The precise operational chain remains Class 2 pending stronger primary documentation.
- 1985: A Soviet intelligence defector helps U.S. authorities focus on Pelton; he is arrested on November 25, 1985, according to the FBI’s retrospective.
- 1986: Pelton is convicted. The codeword Ivy Bells surfaces publicly around legal proceedings and contemporary reporting. NBC and other outlets describe a sensitive Navy undersea eavesdropping program.
- May 1986: CIA Director William Casey asks the Justice Department to consider prosecution of NBC over its reporting, according to contemporary *Washington Post* coverage.
- 1987: The Fourth Circuit affirms Pelton’s conviction in a published opinion that provides the strongest easily accessible primary judicial timeline of his espionage.

## Post-Cold War — capability lineage and historical reconstruction

- 1990s: Major journalistic and participant histories begin reconstructing Cold War special-submarine operations in greater public detail.
- 1998: Sontag and Drew’s *Blind Man’s Bluff* becomes a major public synthesis of American submarine espionage, including Ivy Bells.
- 2000s onward: Public discussion of specialized U.S. submarines, including USS *Parche* and USS *Jimmy Carter*, makes the existence of a durable special-undersea mission family increasingly visible while leaving specific patrol targets classified.

## 2022–2026 — undersea infrastructure returns to strategic attention

- September 2022: Nord Stream pipeline explosions intensify European attention to critical undersea infrastructure.
- 2023–2024: Additional Baltic cable and pipeline incidents increase concern about accidental and deliberate seabed damage; attribution in individual cases varies and must be handled separately.
- 14 January 2025: NATO launches Baltic Sentry to strengthen critical-infrastructure security in the Baltic Sea.
- 21 February 2025: European Commission and High Representative publish a joint submarine-cable security and resilience framework organized around prevention, detection, response, repair and deterrence.
- 12 February 2026: NATO allies announce accelerated adoption of autonomous and AI-enabled technologies for Baltic Sea security and critical undersea infrastructure awareness.

## Chronology rule

Dates attached to the Pelton legal record and current official NATO/EU policy are Class 1. Dates attached to specific Ivy Bells deployment and servicing events remain Class 2 unless anchored to direct public operational records in a later research pass.
