# Document Appendix

This appendix lists the public documents that should anchor later expansion. It deliberately separates documents we have checked from records that remain retrieval targets.

## Checked anchors

### *United States v. Pelton*, 835 F.2d 1067 (4th Cir. 1987)

URL: https://m.openjurist.org/835/f2d/1067/united-states-v-pelton

Use for: Pelton’s NSA employment; January 1980 decision to sell classified information; calls to Soviet officials; meetings; payment; compromise of multiple classified NSA operations; the fact that initial telephone contacts were intercepted but not attributed to him at the time.

Do not use for: precise Ivy Bells hardware, exact seabed mission chronology or detailed content of Soviet intercepts.

### FBI — *Year of the Spy (1985)*

URL: https://www.fbi.gov/history/cases-and-criminals/year-of-the-spy-1985

Use for: official retrospective on Pelton, financial motive, years of disclosure, collection-program damage, arrest and conviction context.

Do not use for: operational engineering beyond what the FBI actually states.

## Retrieval discipline

A document that mentions a codeword proves only what the document says about the codeword. A vague citation can corroborate unusual mission value but not reconstruct a target. A museum label needs provenance. A participant recollection should be tagged with interview date and relation to the event. A later history should identify its underlying source where possible.

The goal of the next pass is not merely to accumulate more documents. It is to shorten the distance between every strong sentence in the manuscript and the best surviving record behind it.
