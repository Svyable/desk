# Evidence Ledger

## Class 1 — Verified / documented

- AMS-02 has operated on the ISS since May 2011 and is explicitly designed to search for antimatter and dark matter.
- AMS has measured hundreds of billions of cosmic-ray events; its public counter exceeded 268 billion by September 10, 2026.
- Antinuclei, including antihelium, can be produced in high-energy collisions; collider experiments have measured multiple antimatter nuclei and hypernuclei.
- Secondary antihelium production from cosmic-ray interactions is physically allowed and has been modeled in peer-reviewed work, although the expected flux is small and model-dependent.
- Public scientific presentations and peer-reviewed theory papers have discussed a small set of AMS antihelium candidate events.
- A June 2026 doctoral thesis using four neural networks reports up to 12 candidate events under some selections but only roughly 1–2 sigma tension with its background-only model; it does not claim a statistically significant excess.
- GAPS launched December 16, 2025, completed 25 days of Antarctic observations, and landed January 10, 2026.
- Fermi-LAT antistar work published in 2021 identified 14 unassociated gamma-ray sources compatible with an annihilation-like spectral selection and set population constraints; it did not identify confirmed antistars.
- ALPHA-g observed antihydrogen falling downward in Earth's gravity, consistent with ordinary attractive gravity.
- LHCb observed CP violation in baryon decays, published in Nature in 2025; this does not by itself explain cosmic baryogenesis.

## Class 2 — Credible but incomplete

- The AMS antihelium candidates may include genuine antihelium nuclei, but the collaboration has not published a definitive discovery analysis.
- Coalescence uncertainties may materially alter expected secondary and dark-matter antinucleus fluxes.
- Heavy-baryon decays and high-multiplicity hidden-sector showers can enhance antinucleus production in theoretically credible models.
- GAPS's orthogonal exotic-atom technique should provide powerful complementary constraints once first-flight science results are published.

## Class 3 — Disputed / unconfirmed

- AMS has discovered cosmic antihelium.
- Any publicly discussed candidate is definitively antihelium-3 or antihelium-4.
- The candidate isotope ratio requires antistars, dark matter, primordial antimatter, or fireballs.
- Any of the fourteen Fermi-LAT sources is an antistar.
- Macroscopic antimatter domains or antistars survive in the Milky Way at an abundance sufficient to explain a cosmic antihelium flux.

## Class 4 — Speculative synthesis / open conjecture

- A confirmed high antihelium-4 fraction could favor dense or bulk-antimatter sources over ordinary secondary production.
- A joint AMS+GAPS antinucleus signal plus Fermi counterpart population could identify an antistar contribution.
- Antihelium may function as a phase-space fossil that distinguishes hidden-sector hadronization from ordinary cosmic-ray collisions more strongly than antiprotons alone.
- The antihelium episode may become a canonical case study for rare-event machine-learning epistemology even if no signal survives.
