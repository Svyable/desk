# 11 — The Antistar Hypothesis

An antistar is less exotic than it sounds and more difficult than it looks.

Replace every proton with an antiproton, every neutron with an antineutron, every electron with a positron. Atomic spectra remain essentially the same under CPT symmetry. Nuclear fusion can run in antimatter. An anti-Sun would shine by anti-hydrogen fusion into antihelium and photons. From across interstellar space, the starlight would not politely reveal the baryon sign of the furnace.

The trouble begins at the surface.

Ordinary interstellar matter falling onto an antistar should annihilate with antimatter in its atmosphere, producing gamma rays. The rate depends on gas density, stellar wind, relative velocity and accretion physics. Compact objects can alter the signal. Old halo antistars moving through thin gas may be far harder to see than disk stars.

If an antistar ejects antihelium through winds, flares, novae or supernova-like events, those charged nuclei could diffuse through Galactic magnetic fields and eventually reach Earth. This is one reason the AMS candidate discussions revived serious antistar constraints. A cosmic antihelium nucleus would be direct baryonic evidence, while gamma rays provide population-level indirect limits.

The hypothesis immediately faces the early universe. How did a macroscopic antimatter object survive in a universe whose surrounding plasma was overwhelmingly matter? Primordial inhomogeneous baryogenesis can, in some models, create antimatter domains dense enough to collapse into compact objects before annihilation erases them. That is a model class, not an observation.

There is a useful hierarchy of claims here.

**Established:** antimatter can form atoms and nuclei; antihelium can be made; an antimatter star is allowed by known local microphysics to first approximation.

**Credible theoretical possibility:** certain baryogenesis scenarios can create compact antimatter domains.

**Unconfirmed:** any antistar exists in the Milky Way.

**Speculative:** AMS candidate antihelium comes from antistars.

The distinction matters because `allowed by physics` is one of the most abused phrases in frontier science. Almost everything interesting is allowed by some extension or initial condition. The evidentiary work is showing that the object population is required by data and survives independent constraints.

Antistars have one advantage over many exotic explanations: they are astrophysical engines built from familiar interactions. Once a sufficiently large antimatter object exists, producing antihelium is mundane. The hard problem has moved backward in time, into cosmological origin and survival.

Dark matter has the opposite structure. It can be easy to motivate cosmologically but hard to make enough antihelium without violating antiproton and gamma-ray data.

A confirmed antihelium flux could therefore become a contest between where one prefers to put the weirdness: in early-universe initial conditions, in particle physics, or in hadronic production. The data should choose. Directionality, isotope ratio, energy spectrum and gamma-ray counterparts can all help.

The antistar is not the answer. It is an explanation with an addressable set of debts.
