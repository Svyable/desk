# The Anti-Atom in the Sky

## Antihelium, Antistars, and the Matter Universe's Missing Half

Sven Hardy Benson

## Author's note

There are discoveries that arrive in a press conference. There are discoveries that arrive as a plot on a conference slide, an awkward cluster of events no one is yet prepared to call a signal, then spend years in a half-life between rumor and publication.

Antihelium has lived in that half-life.

That makes it dangerous material for a book. It also makes it unusually good material for one. The central facts can be stated without drama. The Alpha Magnetic Spectrometer on the International Space Station is a real instrument that has measured hundreds of billions of cosmic-ray events. Its scientific purpose includes a search for primordial antimatter. Public talks and later theoretical papers have discussed a small number of antihelium candidate events. A June 2026 doctoral thesis analyzing AMS-02 data with multiple neural-network selections reports up to twelve candidates, but the excess above the modeled charge-confusion background is mild, roughly one to two standard deviations depending on selection. No peer-reviewed AMS collaboration discovery of cosmic antihelium has been published.

In December 2025 another instrument, GAPS, rose from Antarctica under a NASA balloon. It spent twenty-five days above the continent and landed on the Ross Ice Shelf in January 2026. GAPS does not identify antinuclei the same way AMS does. That is precisely why it matters.

This book is about what happens when a measurement sits at the edge of a cosmological claim. It is also about how not to lose one's head there.

The reader should keep four evidence classes in view. **Documented facts** include the operation of AMS, its published cosmic-ray measurements, the public existence of antihelium candidate discussions, the methods and significance reported in the 2026 thesis, the completed first GAPS flight, accelerator production of antinuclei, Fermi-LAT constraints on antistars, and ALPHA-g's observation that antihydrogen falls downward. **Credible but incomplete evidence** includes the candidate antihelium events themselves, because candidate classification is not yet a collaboration-level detection. **Disputed or unconfirmed claims** include claims that AMS has discovered primordial antimatter, antistars, or dark matter. **Speculative synthesis** includes the possibility that a confirmed isotope and energy pattern could reveal an unrecognized astrophysical production channel or surviving antimatter population.

The fun starts only after those labels are kept intact.
