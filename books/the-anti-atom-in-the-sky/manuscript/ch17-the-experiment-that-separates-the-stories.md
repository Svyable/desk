# 17 — The Experiment That Separates the Stories

The decisive antihelium experiment is not a bigger candidate count. It is a matrix.

Rows are particle species: antiprotons, antideuterons, antihelium-3, antihelium-4. Columns are energy, arrival direction, time, detector architecture and associated gamma-ray constraints. Each source model must fill the matrix before the data are revealed.

Start with AMS. Publish event-level information to the maximum extent compatible with collaboration policy: rigidity, velocity estimator, charge measurements, tracker quality, subsystem consistency, isotope likelihood, selection efficiencies and background contributions. Show candidate stability under reconstruction variants. Freeze a signal definition.

Then GAPS. Its first flight data should provide low-energy antiproton and antinucleus constraints using exotic-atom signatures. Because its systematics differ sharply from AMS, even a modest overlapping result can carry large evidentiary weight.

Add collider calibration. Use ALICE, ALEPH and future measurements to constrain coalescence. Use AMBER's proton-hydrogen and proton-helium antiproton production measurements to tighten secondary source terms. Treat generator disagreement as uncertainty, not choice.

Add gamma rays. For every antistar population capable of producing the antihelium flux, predict the annihilation emission expected from gas accretion and compare with Fermi-LAT catalogs. Include halo and disk distributions separately. Do not apply a disk-star limit to a halo model and call it done.

Add temporal structure. Catastrophic fireball sources may create burst-like injection, although Galactic diffusion can smear arrival times over long intervals. Nearby source models can still produce spectral features. Antistar populations should be steadier statistically.

Add isotope structure. Standard secondaries, dark-sector showers, fireballs and stellar nucleosynthesis do not naturally produce the same antihelium-4/antihelium-3 ratio.

Most important, preregister the discriminants. If every model is allowed to retune after seeing the isotope ratio, the matrix becomes theater.

A possible decision table looks like this:

- **AMS-only candidates, no GAPS antinuclei, falling significance with exposure:** detector/background explanation rises.
- **AMS and GAPS antinuclei with secondary-like spectra and ratios:** production/coalescence model revisions rise.
- **Low-energy antideuteron excess plus antihelium consistent with one dark-matter spectrum, no antistar gamma counterparts:** particle dark matter rises, subject to antiproton constraints.
- **High antihelium-4 fraction plus viable annihilation-like point sources or other antimatter-star evidence:** antistar scenarios rise.
- **Exotic isotope pattern with correlated high-multiplicity signatures inconsistent with standard coalescence:** fireball/hidden-sector mechanisms rise.

None of these lines is proof by itself. The point is to make the hypotheses predict before they explain.

The field already has nearly all the required pieces. What it lacks is a culture of treating them as one experiment.
