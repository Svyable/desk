# 18 — The Matter Universe on Trial

A handful of tracks should not be able to trouble cosmology. That they can is a sign of how much physics has been compressed into the assumption that the visible universe contains essentially no macroscopic antimatter.

The assumption is not arbitrary. It is supported by annihilation constraints, nucleosynthesis, the microwave background, gamma-ray observations and decades of cosmic-ray measurements. But science becomes unhealthy when a well-supported conclusion hardens into a category error: `unlikely` quietly becomes `unmeasurable`, then `unmeasurable` becomes `impossible`.

Antihelium keeps the door empirical.

At the time of writing, there is no confirmed collaboration-level cosmic antihelium discovery. The best recent public analysis I can point to is a doctoral thesis, not an AMS paper, and its sophisticated neural-network search finds only mild statistical tension with background. That result deserves more attention than a sensational claim would, because it places the uncertainty where it belongs: in the rare tail between positive helium and a negative reconstructed nucleus.

GAPS has now flown for twenty-five days. Its data can interrogate the same cosmic antimatter landscape through a radically different detector mechanism. Collider measurements are improving the coalescence denominator. Fermi has already constrained antistar populations. ALPHA-g has removed naive antigravity from the list of escape hatches. LHCb has expanded the observed landscape of CP violation without solving baryogenesis.

This is what a mature frontier looks like. Not one decisive machine, but a set of experiments closing on the same question from incompatible angles.

If antihelium disappears under better reconstruction, the episode will still matter. It will show how a prestigious detector, public candidate events, theoretical ingenuity and machine-learning classification can generate a decade of productive but provisional physics without crossing the line into discovery. That is worth understanding in an era when more experiments will produce tiny anomalous samples processed by opaque algorithms.

If antihelium is confirmed at a flux compatible with revised secondary models, the discovery will be less cosmological but still profound. It will mean we underestimated how composite antimatter forms in ordinary high-energy astrophysics.

If antihelium is confirmed at a flux or isotope ratio that secondary production cannot explain, the consequences widen. Dark-matter hadronization becomes a candidate observable. Exotic dense antibaryonic sources become testable. Compact primordial antimatter populations and antistars move from speculative astrophysics toward constrained explanation.

And if an antistar is ever identified independently—through annihilation signatures, antinucleus emission and a coherent population model—then the phrase `matter-dominated universe` will need a footnote large enough to become a new chapter of cosmology.

The disciplined position is not excitement or skepticism. It is conditional commitment.

Show me the track.

Show me the background tail.

Show me the isotope.

Show me the second detector.

Then we can ask where the antimatter came from.

Until then, the anti-atom remains in the sky: not discovered, not dismissed, and extraordinarily well placed to teach physics how to behave when a minus sign could rewrite the early universe.
