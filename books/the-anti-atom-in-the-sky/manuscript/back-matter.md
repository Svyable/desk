# Back Matter

## Chronology

- **1928** — Dirac's relativistic electron equation implies antiparticle solutions.
- **1932** — Carl Anderson identifies the positron in cosmic rays.
- **1955** — Antiproton discovered at Berkeley.
- **1965** — Antideuteron produced in accelerator experiments.
- **1998** — AMS-01 flies on the Space Shuttle and sets antihelium limits.
- **2011-05-16** — AMS-02 launches aboard STS-134.
- **2011-05-19** — AMS-02 begins operations on the ISS.
- **2016 onward** — AMS leadership publicly discusses antihelium candidate events in conference settings; no collaboration discovery paper follows.
- **2017** — Blum et al. revisit secondary antihelium production and coalescence scaling.
- **2019** — Poulin et al. examine possible origins of publicly discussed AMS antihelium candidates.
- **2020** — Shukla et al. publish large-scale simulations of secondary antihelium production.
- **2021** — Winkler & Linden show heavy-baryon decays can enhance dark-matter antihelium; Dupourqué et al. use Fermi-LAT to constrain antistars and identify fourteen unassociated gamma-ray candidates compatible with annihilation-like spectra.
- **2023-09-27** — ALPHA announces first direct observation of gravity's effect on antihydrogen; result published in Nature.
- **2023** — Strongly coupled dark-sector calculations show potentially dramatic antihelium enhancement.
- **2024-06-21** — `Fireball antinucleosynthesis` published in Physical Review D, explicitly modeling tentative AMS candidate ratios.
- **2025-07-16** — LHCb baryon CP-violation result published in Nature after initial March announcement.
- **2025-12-16** — GAPS launches from Antarctica.
- **2026-01-10** — GAPS completes a 25-day science flight and lands on the Ross Ice Shelf.
- **2026-06-03** — Physical Review D publishes work toward more universal coalescence models.
- **2026-06-16** — Rossi doctoral thesis defended, reporting up to twelve AMS antihelium candidates under ML selections but only ~1–2 sigma tension with modeled background.
- **2026-09-03** — Physical Review D accepts a new joint antiproton/antideuteron/antihelium prediction analysis motivated in part by GAPS's completed first flight.

## Defeat conditions

The book's stronger antihelium-mystery thesis should be downgraded if a collaboration-level AMS analysis shows the candidates are fully reproduced by known detector backgrounds; if independent reconstruction systematically flips the candidate charge or isotope assignments; or if expanding exposure causes the candidate rate to scale like a shrinking background fluctuation rather than a stable flux.

The antistar branch should be downgraded if confirmed antihelium spectra and isotope ratios are quantitatively explained by secondary production or a particle-physics source while gamma-ray and population constraints make the required antistar abundance untenable.

The dark-matter branch should be downgraded if any model matching antihelium necessarily violates antiproton, antideuteron, gamma-ray, CMB or collider constraints once common production and propagation uncertainties are treated consistently.

## Research priorities

1. Collaboration-level AMS antihelium paper with event-level methodology and candidate stability studies.
2. First GAPS flight antiproton/antideuteron/antihelium results.
3. Collider-calibrated antihelium-3 and antihelium-4 coalescence across pp, pA, AA and electroweak environments.
4. Joint cosmic-ray propagation fit across p, He, antiprotons, antideuterons and antihelium.
5. Updated Fermi-LAT antistar search using later catalogs and multiwavelength classifications.
6. Explicit forward models connecting antistar accretion gamma rays to antihelium injection.
7. Public benchmark datasets for charge-confusion tails and ML robustness.
8. A preregistered multi-instrument antinucleus likelihood.
