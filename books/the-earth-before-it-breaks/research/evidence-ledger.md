# Evidence Ledger

## Class 1 — Verified / documented

- Rock fracture, fluid flow and mechanical stress can generate measurable electrical effects through several established mechanisms.
- Earthquake lights have a long observational literature; some modern case collections and peer-reviewed analyses exist, though individual reports vary strongly in quality.
- The 1989 Loma Prieta ULF magnetic precursor claim was later reexamined by USGS researchers, who concluded the canonical Corralitos anomaly was a sensor-system artifact.
- The Parkfield Prediction Experiment issued a high-profile time-window forecast that failed; the expected magnitude-six earthquake occurred in 2004, outside the original prediction window.
- CNES launched DEMETER in 2004 specifically to investigate electromagnetic and ionospheric phenomena including possible seismic associations.
- DEMETER statistical studies reported population-level perturbations associated with seismic activity; CNES explicitly states these do not establish prediction for individual earthquakes.
- CSES-01 launched in 2018. CSES-02 launched June 14, 2025. The China–Italy program continues measurements of electric and magnetic fields, plasma and energetic particles relevant to possible seismic-ionospheric coupling.
- USGS currently states that decades of work have not produced convincing evidence of electromagnetic earthquake precursors suitable for reliable prediction.

## Class 2 — Credible but incomplete

- Some reported earthquake lights may be generated by geological electrical mechanisms rather than power-grid or atmospheric sources.
- Stress-activated positive-hole charge carriers may contribute to field-scale pre-rupture electrical effects in favorable lithologies; scale, transport and prevalence remain incompletely established.
- Statistical ionospheric perturbations reported before populations of strong earthquakes may include genuine pre-rupture coupling in addition to known confounders.
- Multiple physical pathways—electrokinetic, gas/radon, atmospheric-electric, acoustic-gravity-wave and stress-charge mechanisms—may contribute differently across events.

## Class 3 — Disputed / unconfirmed

- Claims that earthquake lights provide reliable practical advance warning.
- Strong VAN claims of sufficiently precise time-location-magnitude prediction for routine operational use.
- Claims that a particular ionospheric anomaly before one famous earthquake establishes a precursor mechanism.
- Claims that retrospective CSES/DEMETER anomaly-detection rates translate directly into prospective earthquake-prediction performance.
- Claims that unusual animal behavior can be confidently explained by sensing seismo-electromagnetic precursors.

## Class 4 — Speculative synthesis

- Seismo-electromagnetic variables could eventually add weak incremental information to short-term operational earthquake forecasting without enabling deterministic prediction.
- A useful precursor, if it exists, may be restricted to particular lithologies, depths, magnitudes or tectonic settings rather than universal.
- The most productive future architecture may be adversarial multimodal forecasting in which electromagnetic variables must outperform explicit space-weather, meteorological and instrument-state null models prospectively.
