# Contradictions and Incentives

## The prediction incentive

Earthquake prediction has enormous humanitarian and scientific value. Positive precursor results therefore receive disproportionate attention. Researchers can honestly overfit because the desired application is unusually important.

## The debunking incentive

A field with repeated false alarms creates justified skepticism, but skepticism can also become identity. A failed operational prediction does not retroactively erase real fracture-electrical or co-seismic coupling phenomena.

## Retrospective selection

Famous earthquakes invite dense post hoc inspection. Event-centered analyses must be separated from prospective performance. Spatial radius, lead window, magnitude threshold and anomaly definition can all become hidden researcher degrees of freedom.

## Instrument pathology

The Loma Prieta Corralitos case demonstrates that sensor malfunction can produce structured anomalies. Long baselines, station logs, independent instruments and global geomagnetic comparison are mandatory.

## Space-weather confounding

Solar and geomagnetic activity can generate large electric, magnetic, plasma and particle disturbances. Excluding disturbed intervals after seeing the data can bias results; exclusion rules should be prospective.

## National-program inference

France, China and Italy have invested in satellite missions relevant to seismo-electromagnetic coupling. This demonstrates institutional interest and measurement commitment, not secret confirmation that earthquake prediction works.

## Mechanism laundering

Established facts such as fracture electrification, electrokinetic potentials and post-seismic ionospheric disturbances must not be used as rhetorical proof for unestablished pre-rupture prediction chains.
