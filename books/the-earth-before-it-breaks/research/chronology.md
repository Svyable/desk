# Chronology

- **1965–1967** — The Matsushiro earthquake swarm in Japan produces extraordinary repeated seismicity alongside reports later analyzed for geomagnetic, atmospheric-electric and luminous phenomena.
- **1980s** — The Greek VAN group develops and publicizes seismic electric signal methods and prospective earthquake predictions.
- **1985** — The Parkfield Prediction Experiment begins around a formal expectation of a magnitude-six earthquake before 1993.
- **October 18, 1989** — The Loma Prieta earthquake occurs; a nearby ULF magnetic anomaly becomes a prominent precursor claim.
- **October 1992** — A Parkfield magnitude 4.7 event triggers a 72-hour public warning protocol; the expected magnitude-six event does not follow.
- **1993** — The original Parkfield prediction window expires unsuccessful; James Savage publishes a methodological critique of the prediction logic.
- **September 28, 2004** — The magnitude-six Parkfield earthquake finally occurs and is recorded by the dense instrument network.
- **June 29, 2004** — France launches DEMETER, dedicated in part to measuring possible electromagnetic and ionospheric signatures associated with seismic activity.
- **2006** — St-Laurent, Derr and Freund publish a positive-hole mechanism for earthquake lights.
- **2009** — USGS researchers publish their reanalysis concluding the canonical Corralitos magnetic precursor before Loma Prieta was a sensor-system artifact.
- **2010** — DEMETER completes scientific operations.
- **2014** — Thériault and colleagues publish a geological analysis of earthquake-light reports emphasizing rift settings.
- **2017** — DEMETER ion-density population statistics and Matsushiro seismo-electromagnetic mechanism work appear in peer-reviewed literature.
- **February 2, 2018** — CSES-01 launches.
- **2020–2024** — Joint DEMETER/CSES and CSES-only statistical studies report ionospheric anomaly associations while retaining major predictive limitations.
- **June 14, 2025** — CSES-02 launches, creating a two-satellite China–Italy measurement program.
