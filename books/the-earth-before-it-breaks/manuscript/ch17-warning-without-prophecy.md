# Warning Without Prophecy

Prediction is not the only prize.

This is easy to forget because earthquakes create an unbearable fantasy: if nature produces any precursor at all, surely we should be able to turn it into evacuation time.

But weak precursory information could be useful in narrower ways.

A statistically calibrated signal might adjust short-term hazard rather than announce an event. It might raise the prior probability enough to change instrument sampling, inspect vulnerable infrastructure, place emergency staff on standby or trigger targeted observations. The action can be chosen so false alarms are cheap.

This is how weather forecasting matured. Not every forecast asks whether the city should evacuate. Different probability thresholds trigger different actions.

Seismology already uses layered products. Long-term hazard informs construction. Operational earthquake forecasting updates probabilities after seismic sequences. Early warning detects rupture and sends alerts ahead of strong shaking.

An electromagnetic precursor, if one survives, might occupy a narrow slot among these systems rather than replace them.

That would be less cinematic and more valuable.

The public often imagines scientific success as a binary. Predictable or unpredictable. Signal or no signal. The real world allows weak information.

A biomarker can be informative without being diagnostic. A radar return can increase suspicion without identifying an aircraft. An intelligence intercept can change odds without resolving intent.

The difficulty is governance. Once a precursor system exists, somebody has to decide what probability justifies public action. False alarms erode trust. Missed alarms invite blame. Political pressure pushes probabilistic language toward certainty.

This is where scientific humility becomes infrastructure.

The system must expose calibration, uncertainty and historical false-alarm rates. It must make clear whether an alert means one chance in ten, one in a hundred or merely an anomaly worthy of another sensor pass.

If that sounds less satisfying than prophecy, good.

Natural hazards punish satisfaction.
