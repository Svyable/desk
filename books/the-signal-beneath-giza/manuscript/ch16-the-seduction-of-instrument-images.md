# The Seduction of Instrument Images

Scientific instruments have acquired the cultural authority once held by eyewitnesses. We trust the machine because it seems incapable of imagination.

But the machine rarely produces the image we see. Humans choose filters, coordinate systems, thresholds, interpolation, regularization, palettes and camera angles. A volumetric rendering can make a probability field look machined. A threshold can turn a gradient into a boundary. Symmetry is especially dangerous because the human visual system rewards it before statistics have spoken.

This does not make imaging subjective nonsense. It makes provenance essential.

The honest figure should be accompanied by the ugly ones: raw observations, residuals, alternate parameterizations, sensitivity maps, null simulations and reconstructions of known targets. If the columns vanish when one defensible regularization parameter changes, the columns are not yet archaeological objects.

A beautiful image is the beginning of cross-examination.
