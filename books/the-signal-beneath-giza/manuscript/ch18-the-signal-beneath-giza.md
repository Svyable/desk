# The Signal Beneath Giza

Giza does contain unknowns. That is not a concession to mystery culture. It is a measurement.

A thirty-metre-plus void was found where the modern map had no room. A hidden corridor was first inferred from particles passing through stone and later inspected. The plateau will almost certainly yield more surprises because archaeology is unfinished and non-destructive sensing is improving.

The mistake is to treat that openness as transferable credit. One confirmed hidden corridor does not make eight 648-metre shafts more likely merely because both stories contain pyramids and instruments.

The opposite mistake is equally lazy. A disputed claim should not make us retreat to the proposition that everything important at Giza is already known. The correct response is an experimental program with teeth.

The deep-structure claim has done one useful thing regardless of its eventual fate. It has written down coordinates, dimensions and geometry dramatic enough to be attacked by independent physics. Muography, gravity, seismology, resistivity and geology can be made to answer.

There is something almost generous about a claim that large. It leaves nowhere to hide except underground.
