# Eight Shafts, Six Hundred Metres

The most spectacular 2025 claims described eight cylindrical structures beneath Khafre, descending on the order of 648 metres, with spiral features and large chambers below.

Put aside Egyptology for a moment. Treat this as a geophysical target.

A cavity or engineered shaft hundreds of metres long is not a subtle object. It displaces rock. It changes density. Depending on fill, geometry and surrounding geology it may affect gravity, seismic propagation, electrical properties and perhaps muon transmission. Excavating it would produce an extraordinary volume of spoil and demand access, ventilation, structural management and labor. These consequences become independent prediction channels.

This is the right way to investigate an extraordinary claim: make it expensive. A hypothesis that predicts only the image already used to create it is cheap. A hypothesis that predicts gravity anomalies, seismic reflectors, muographic excesses and specific geological boundaries can die.

No independently confirmed dataset currently establishes these enormous Khafre structures.
