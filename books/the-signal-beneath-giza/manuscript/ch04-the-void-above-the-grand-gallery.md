# The Void Above the Grand Gallery

In 2017 a hidden-space claim at Giza survived the test that matters: different detectors saw it.

The ScanPyramids team reported in Nature a large void above Khufu's Grand Gallery, at least thirty metres long. Nuclear-emulsion films saw the muon excess. Scintillator hodoscopes saw it. Gas detectors outside the pyramid saw it. Three technologies and independent analyses converged.

The result is important not because it licenses every future Giza anomaly, but because it demonstrates what strong non-invasive evidence looks like. The paper did not know the void's purpose. It called a density anomaly a void and stopped short of inventing a chamber full of objects or assigning a ritual function.

This restraint is more radical than it sounds. Discovery and interpretation were separated. A new volume of low-density space was Class 1 evidence. 'Secret royal chamber' would have been a later hypothesis.

Giza mystery culture often reverses that order. It begins with the room it wants and works backward to the signal.
