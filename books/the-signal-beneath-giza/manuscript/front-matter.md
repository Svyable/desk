# The Signal Beneath Giza

There are two Gizas now. One is limestone, shafts, chambers, quarries, tombs, causeways, weathered bedrock and four and a half millennia of people cutting into it. The other is an image on a screen.

In March 2025 the second Giza briefly swallowed the first. Renderings circulated of enormous cylinders descending beneath the Pyramid of Khafre, spiral structures around them, chambers at impossible-looking depths, and a subterranean architecture larger than the monuments above. The claim was not modest. It was exactly the kind of claim that makes a person stop scrolling.

This book begins by refusing the easiest reactions. It will not say the pictures prove a lost underground civilization. It will not say that because a claim sounds outrageous the plateau contains nothing undiscovered. Giza has already embarrassed certainty. Cosmic-ray muons found a large previously unknown void in Khufu's pyramid in 2017. A second anomaly became a roughly nine-metre corridor, then ground-penetrating radar, ultrasound, electrical resistivity and an endoscopic camera converged on the same piece of empty space. Instruments really can find what archaeology has missed.

That is precisely why the newer claim deserves harder treatment, not easier belief or dismissal.

The question is not whether hidden structures exist at Giza. They do. The question is what a particular instrument measured, at what depth, with what forward model, against what geology, with what calibration, and whether another independent physical method sees the same thing.
