# Radar at the Surface

Ground-penetrating radar earns its name honestly. It penetrates ground. It does not promise infinite ground.

Frequency, conductivity, water content, clay, salinity, scattering and antenna geometry determine what survives. Lower frequencies generally buy penetration at the cost of resolution. In favorable dry materials useful depths can be substantial; in lossy material the signal can die quickly.

At Khufu's chevron, the question was local: is there an air-filled anomaly just behind these blocks, where muography says a corridor should be? Radar was one member of a multimodal campaign. The 2023 NDT study combined GPR with ultrasound and numerical simulation. A 2025 Scientific Reports paper fused GPR, ultrasound and electrical-resistivity imagery. The instruments disagreed in detail because they measure different properties, yet they converged on the known corridor.

That is not a weakness. Independent physical disagreement around a common object is often more persuasive than one astonishingly clean image.
