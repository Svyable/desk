# Electricity Through Limestone

Electrical resistivity tomography is less cinematic than radar, which may be why it is so useful here.

Electrodes inject current and measure potentials. The resulting inverse problem estimates a distribution of electrical resistivity. Air, stone, fractures and moisture can differ strongly. In the Great Pyramid, researchers adapted ERT to the awkward three-dimensional geometry of the chevron area. Published inversion results supported the North Face Corridor, with dimensions broadly consistent with the other methods.

The crucial word is inversion. Instruments rarely hand us chambers. They hand us observations from which a model of hidden properties is inferred. The inversion is constrained by geometry, priors, regularization and noise.

This is where archaeology and medical imaging share an uncomfortable truth: reconstructed pictures look more certain than the mathematics that produced them.
