# The Corridor Behind the Chevron

The North Face Corridor is the best control experiment in the entire story.

Muon measurements indicated a corridor-shaped void behind the chevron masonry on Khufu's north face. A 2023 Nature Communications analysis characterized it at about nine metres long and roughly two metres by two metres in section. Then investigators moved closer. Ground-penetrating radar and ultrasonic testing were applied from the accessible surface. Electrical resistivity followed. An endoscopic camera ultimately looked inside.

This is how an anomaly becomes architecture: not by making the rendering more detailed but by shortening the distance between inference and object.

The sequence also teaches a depth lesson. GPR was useful here because investigators were interrogating masonry immediately behind an accessible surface. The successful Giza radar case therefore cannot be casually cited as precedent for radar imaging engineered structures hundreds of metres beneath limestone. Same acronym, radically different geometry.
