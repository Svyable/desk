# What SAR Actually Measures

Synthetic aperture radar is extraordinary technology without being supernatural technology.

A moving radar platform combines observations to synthesize a much larger antenna aperture, producing high-resolution imagery and, with appropriate techniques, exquisite measurements of surface displacement. Radar can interact with vegetation, snow, dry sand and shallow subsurface material depending on wavelength and conditions. None of this implies that ordinary orbital SAR can resolve architectural cylinders hundreds of metres beneath competent limestone.

The Biondi-Malanga approach made a different argument. It attempted to infer internal structure from radar observations and vibration-related Doppler information rather than claiming simple direct illumination through all that stone. That distinction deserves to be stated fairly. It also makes validation more important. An indirect inverse method can be ingenious and still be wrong.

A method that claims to extract information conventional penetration physics says should be inaccessible needs calibration against objects whose geometry is already known, blind prediction, raw-data availability and independent replication. Giza contains convenient known chambers. The burden is measurable.
