# Manuscript architecture

1. **Normal Is the Weird State** — formulate the hypothesis without promoting it to fact; distinguish presence, concentration, receptor engagement, function, and psychedelic pharmacology.
2. **Two Molecules, Two Extremes** — DMT and 5-MeO-DMT chemistry, routes, kinetics, dose-response, phenomenology, and why “hyperspace”/“void” are reports rather than mechanisms.
3. **Who Actually Makes DMT?** — human body-fluid detections, INMT/AADC, rat brain microdialysis, pineal myth, peripheral synthesis, metabolism, unresolved human-brain concentration problem.
4. **The Missing Half** — forensic reconstruction of endogenous 5-MeO-DMT evidence: historical analytical methods, positive and negative findings, precursors, bufotenine, modern uncertainty.
5. **Receptors Are Not Metaphors** — 5-HT2A, 5-HT1A, 5-HT2C, sigma-1/TAAR claims, affinities versus functional signaling, biased agonism, occupancy and endogenous concentration.
6. **Hyperconnection** — DMT neuroimaging, EEG complexity, hierarchy, global connectivity, association cortex, and what “more connected” actually means.
7. **Whiteout** — 5-MeO-DMT phenomenology and emerging imaging/electrophysiology; ego dissolution, unity, reduced differentiation, and why “disconnection” may be the wrong word.
8. **The Brain Needs Scissors** — ordinary cognition as controlled integration and segregation: binding, inhibition, predictive processing, attention, gating, salience, forgetting and pruning.
9. **A Chemical Seesaw** — strongest version of the balancing hypothesis, explicit mathematical/physiological predictions, timescales, compartments and candidate feedback loops.
10. **Why Serotonin May Be Enough** — strongest conventional countercase: existing serotonergic, cholinergic, dopaminergic, noradrenergic, GABAergic and glutamatergic systems already regulate these functions.
11. **Dose Is a Different Animal** — nonlinear pharmacology; why exogenous psychedelic phenomenology may reveal almost nothing about endogenous function; hormesis and receptor reserve without hand-waving.
12. **Dreams, Death and the Pineal Trap** — audit popular endogenous-DMT claims around REM, birth, death and NDEs; distinguish measurements from narrative inheritance.
13. **States Worth Measuring** — sleep/wake transitions, anesthesia, meditation, sensory deprivation, stress, seizure, hypoxia and flow as test beds, with ethical/technical constraints.
14. **Measure the Molecules** — analytical chemistry: LC-MS/MS, microdialysis limits, CSF/plasma inference, degradation, contamination, spatial resolution, metabolomics and isotope tracing.
15. **Measure the Mind** — simultaneous EEG/fMRI/MEG and network metrics; preregistered predictions capable of killing the hypothesis.
16. **What Would Balance Look Like?** — competing models: reciprocal concentrations, receptor-level antagonism, temporal alternation, regional specialization, shared precursor competition, or no direct interaction.
17. **Normality as Active Control** — synthesis: ordinary consciousness as maintained metastability rather than a default state, while keeping endogenous tryptamines provisional.
18. **An Experiment Beautiful Enough to Fail** — concrete research program, null outcomes, replication standards, and what would remain interesting if the central theory dies.

## Narrative rule

The book follows the seductive hypothesis because it earns investigation, then repeatedly forces it through measurement bottlenecks. No chapter may use high-dose subjective complementarity as evidence of endogenous opposition. Every escalation from chemical presence to physiological function must be visible to the reader.
