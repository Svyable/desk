On November 11, 2026, Nvidia's board accepted the resignation of the only CEO it had known. The company had just opened an investigation after reports of a foreign-intelligence effort targeting Jensen Huang. He acknowledged infidelity; U.S. intelligence officials reportedly suspected that a woman associated with an Nvidia customer worked for China; China rejected the allegation; Nvidia said it had no reason to believe secrets or national security had been compromised. Within days, the man who had spent thirty-three years building the infrastructure company at the center of the AI boom was gone.

**Chips, Lies, & Chinese Spies** follows the part of the story no single scandal headline can explain: how an extraordinary commercial platform became a strategic national asset, why America's attempts to control exports created both policy arguments and incentives for diversion, and what happens when a company's technical architecture, political relationships, and public credibility are so concentrated in one founder.

The book's stance is investigative rather than prosecutorial. It identifies admitted private misconduct, reported but unproved intelligence allegations, confirmed separate criminal smuggling, policy disputes, and the limits of corporate disclosure. **It does not claim that Jensen Huang committed espionage or participated in the $160 million Hsu smuggling case.** A serious story must be able to say what cannot yet be shown.

This is intentionally an opening draft. The remaining chapters require reporting and documentary verification; the book is not a finished 65,000–90,000-word trade manuscript.

## Reporting and source discipline

The core document trail includes Nvidia's [November 8 message](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/a-message-to-our-company), [November 8 board statement](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/Statement-from-NVIDIA-Board-of-Directors/default.aspx), [November 10 outside-counsel announcement](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/statement-from-the-independent-members-of-the-nvidia-board), [November 12 transition announcement](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-announces-ceo-transition), [SEC Form 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000082/nvda-20261111.htm), Nvidia's fiscal 2026 report and 2026 quarterly disclosures, the Commerce Department's export-rule record, and the [DOJ announcement](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/us-authorities-shut-down-major-china-linked-ai-tech-smuggling-network) regarding Alan Hsu and Hao Global.

The allegations about Alice Huang's possible intelligence role still depend substantially on attributed reporting by the New York Times, Reuters, AP, AFP, CNN, and others. The November 17 joint Justice/Commerce statement independently documents a 2024–25 Megaspeed export-control investigation, Nvidia's June 2025 no-sell decision, and a June 2025 on-site inspection. Those official investigative facts do not establish espionage or a criminal violation. The ledger distinguishes those sources from independently documented corporate and court facts. The board's investigation is ongoing as of the edition date. No unverified intimacy, intelligence role, or commercial diversion is treated as an adjudicated finding.
