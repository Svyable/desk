# Source log

Sources are grouped by evidentiary role, not by the order in which they were found. Primary and authoritative records should be preferred in the manuscript whenever available. Historical reviews are useful for locating difficult Navy documents but are not treated as neutral substitutes for those documents.

## Central wartime study

### Fleischer, W. E.; Viles, F. J. Jr.; Gade, P. E.; Drinker, P. (1946), “A health survey of pipe covering operations in constructing naval vessels”

- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21016030/
- *Journal of Industrial Hygiene and Toxicology*, January 1946, vol. 28, pp. 9–16.
- **Use:** Central published survey. Examined 1,074 pipe coverers and reported three cases of asbestosis; all three affected workers had more than twenty years in the trade. The publication also described dust-producing tasks and recommended local exhaust for selected operations while reaching the broad conclusion that naval pipe covering was not a dangerous trade.
- **Evidence class:** 1 for publication/design/findings; class 2 for claims about its downstream institutional influence unless tied to citations or correspondence.
- **Latency denominator:** Later judicial reconstructions, including *Borel v. Fibreboard*, report that about **95% of the 1,074 workers had less than ten years in the trade**. Treat this as a high-value reconstructed figure pending direct extraction of the original worker-duration table. The paper's own discussion also acknowledged that sampled dust levels did not represent a worker's multi-year exposure history and that sustained high-dust work would have produced more asbestosis.
- **Next extraction:** Recover full text in a stable archive and tabulate every reported worker-duration category, dust sample, operation, control condition, radiographic criterion, and limitation.

### Judicial reconstructions of the 1946 denominator

- *Borel v. Fibreboard Paper Products Corp.*, 493 F.2d 1076 (5th Cir. 1973). Accessible mirror: https://www.casemine.com/judgement/us/5914c6daadd7b049347de266 — reports that 95% of the surveyed workers had less than ten years in the trade and contrasts the young denominator with the long latency of diagnosable asbestosis. It also preserves the original paper's warning that sampled dust did not represent years of exposure.
- *Moran v. Johns-Manville Sales Corp.*, 691 F.2d 811 (6th Cir. 1982): https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F2/691/691.F2d.811.81-3373.html — likewise discusses the small long-term-exposure fraction and the paper's high-dust caveat.
- **Evidence class:** 1 for what the judicial opinions report about their records and quoted historical material; the final manuscript should still replace reconstructed duration statistics with the original table if a stable scan is recovered.
- **Claim boundary:** `3 / 1,074` is a cross-sectional prevalence result, not a lifetime-risk denominator. The 95% figure does not establish fraudulent sampling, deliberate selection of short-tenure workers, or that the 1946 study alone caused later Navy asbestos policy.

## Navy wartime knowledge and Bath Iron Works

### *GAF Corporation v. United States*, 932 F.2d 947 (Fed. Cir. 1991)

- Justia: https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F2/932/947/288782/
- Reporter mirror: https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F2/932/932.F2d.947.90-5079.html
- **Use:** Judicial opinion reproduces/quotes key wartime records presented in the litigation, including Commander C. S. Stephenson's March 11, 1941 memorandum that the Navy was doing considerable asbestos work and was not protecting men as it should; a March 4 memorandum referring to possible labor unrest around inspections; the 1942 Bath Iron Works description of a very real asbestos hazard; and a 1944 classified Bath report describing abnormal radiographs/asbestosis diagnoses.
- **Evidence class:** 1 for what the court opinion says and the quoted exhibit language; original records still preferred for complete context. The court's legal holding is not a historical exoneration or finding of concealment.

### U.S. Senate Committee on Finance hearing record reproducing Navy asbestos chronology

- PDF: https://www.finance.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/HRG99-457.pdf
- **Use:** Reproduces the Stephenson warning and summarizes 1941–42 government industrial-hygiene training plus the 1943 Navy/Maritime Commission minimum shipyard safety requirements, including ventilation and respiratory protection.
- **Evidence class:** 1 for the hearing record; underlying Navy records should replace the hearing summary where recovered.

### Franke, K.; Paustenbach, D. (2011), “Government and Navy knowledge regarding health hazards of asbestos: A state of the science evaluation (1900 to 1970)”

- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22200078/
- Full article: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.3109/08958378.2011.643417
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.3109/08958378.2011.643417
- **Use:** Detailed secondary reconstruction of dozens of published and unpublished government/Navy documents. Particularly useful for locating Philip Drinker's 1942 Bath survey, his 1945 Bureau of Medicine and Surgery memorandum describing a fairly serious Bath dust risk likely to exist elsewhere, the 1945 letter to Admiral Mills transmitting the pipe-covering survey, and the 1943 Navy/Maritime Commission shipyard requirements.
- **Evidence class:** 2 for historical interpretation and document transcription until original records are recovered; peer-reviewed secondary source.
- **Conflict/incentive note:** The review's framing has been used in asbestos state-of-the-art litigation and argues that the Navy was highly knowledgeable and attempted to minimize adverse effects. Use its document trail, but independently audit its interpretive conclusions.

### Reagan Presidential Library-hosted historical litigation/government compilation

- PDF: https://www.reaganlibrary.gov/public/digitallibrary/smof/counsel/roberts/box-004/40-485-6908381-004-021-2017.pdf
- **Use:** Contains reproduced historical material and quotations concerning Navy knowledge, including the 1939 Surgeon General material, 1941 Stephenson language, and Bath material.
- **Evidence class:** 2 as a reproduction/compilation; trace quotations back to original archival records before relying on surrounding advocacy.

## Prewar occupational disease

### Merewether, E. R. A.; Price, C. W. (1930), *Report on Effects of Asbestos Dust on the Lungs and Dust Suppression in the Asbestos Industry*

- Contemporary UK government report; locate through UK archival/library holdings for final citation.
- **Use:** Establishes that severe occupational asbestos fibrosis and dust-suppression methods were documented before the U.S. wartime shipbuilding surge.
- **Evidence class:** 1 once cited from an official scan; class 2 if cited only through later reproductions.

## Lung cancer evidence

### Doll, Richard (1955), “Mortality from Lung Cancer in Asbestos Workers”

- Full text (PMC): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1037613/
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1136/oem.12.2.81
- **Use:** Landmark mortality study strengthening the association between asbestos work and lung cancer. Important boundary: not the first suspicion/report of cancer, but a major epidemiological step.
- **Evidence class:** 1.

## Mesothelioma evidence

### Wagner, J. C.; Sleggs, C. A.; Marchand, Paul (1960), “Diffuse Pleural Mesothelioma and Asbestos Exposure in the North Western Cape Province”

- Full text (PMC): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1038078/
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1136/oem.17.4.260
- **Use:** Thirty-three diffuse pleural mesothelioma cases; probable crocidolite exposure identified in all but one. Includes occupational and non-occupational/environmental exposure histories. Foundational for the mesothelioma shift.
- **Evidence class:** 1.
- **Boundary:** Strong evidence for the crocidolite association; do not convert this single study into an assertion that every asbestos fiber/product carries identical quantitative mesothelioma potency.

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.1964.03060270028006

## Navy overhaul exposure

### Marr, W. T. (1964), “Asbestos Exposure During Naval Vessel Overhaul”

- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14168424/
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1080/00028896409342585
- **Use:** Navy shipyard/overhaul exposure, including fabrication, installation, and removal. Documents existing asbestosis disability experience and emphasizes that chest radiography is inadequate as an exposure-control substitute because disease takes years to develop.
- **Evidence class:** 1.

### Felton, J. S. (1979), “Radiographic search for asbestos-related disease in a naval shipyard”

- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/294186/
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1749-6632.1979.tb18737.x
- **Use:** Long Beach Naval Shipyard chest-X-ray survey of 6,640 employees; 1,061 workers (16.0%) classified with asbestos-related abnormalities. Useful as a later mature-workforce contrast, not as a directly comparable endpoint to the 1946 asbestosis count.
- **Evidence class:** 1.

## Federal exposure standards

### OSHA (1994), “Occupational Exposure to Asbestos,” regulatory history

- Federal Register rule page: https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/federalregister/1994-08-10
- **Use:** Authoritative retrospective regulatory chronology: initial 12 f/cc OSHA limit in 1971; December 1971 emergency temporary standard at 5 f/cc TWA / 10 f/cc peak; June 1972 final standard; 1976 reduction to 2 f/cc; later tightening. OSHA explicitly notes the 1972 limits were aimed primarily at asbestosis with hoped-for incidental cancer protection.
- **Evidence class:** 1.

### NIOSH (1972), *Criteria for a Recommended Standard: Occupational Exposure to Asbestos*

- CDC Stacks: https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/19321
- Archived NIOSH page: https://archive.cdc.gov/www_cdc_gov/niosh/docs/72-10267/default.html
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.26616/NIOSHPUB7210267
- **Use:** Federal criteria document reviewing biological effects, exposure measurement, work practice, warning, respiratory protection, medical surveillance, and recommended limits.
- **Evidence class:** 1.

- **Evidence class:** 1 for current VA posture; not evidence of historical wartime knowledge.

## Archival reconstruction

### National Archives, “Researching Asbestos aboard US Naval Vessel up through 1961”

- https://www.archives.gov/research/military/navy/guided-topics/asbestos
- **Use:** Authoritative research guide showing that Navy asbestos reconstruction requires ship history plus Record Group 19/Bureau of Ships correspondence, General Information Books, specifications, class/ship files, engineering documentation, and related records. Explicitly describes the topic as a frequent and complex research request.
- **Evidence class:** 1 for archival holdings/method; not itself proof of exposure aboard a specific vessel.

## Maritime/product-liability boundary

### *Air & Liquid Systems Corp. v. DeVries*, 586 U.S. ___ (2019)

- Supreme Court opinion PDF: https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/18pdf/17-1104_2co3.pdf
- **Use:** Modern maritime-law decision addressing Navy sailors, equipment requiring asbestos-containing parts, and the duty to warn in certain integrated-product circumstances. Useful for explaining why product boundaries fail to map neatly onto maintenance exposure.
- **Evidence class:** 1 for legal holding/facts recited by Court; not retroactive evidence of a 1940s duty or knowledge state.

## Competing historical interpretations

### Franke & Paustenbach (2011), above

- Interpretation: emphasizes substantial Navy knowledge plus attempts to implement protective procedures; cautions that knowledge developed by era and product form.
- **Use:** Strong counterweight to simplistic cover-up framing.

### “When errors become etched in stone: The U.S. Navy's deeply flawed, never retracted World War II asbestos epidemiology study” (2026)

- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42307414/
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1177/10482911261446696
- **Use:** Recent critique arguing that the 1946 Fleischer/Drinker survey contained major epidemiological/industrial-hygiene flaws and that its reassuring conclusion had long downstream consequences.
- **Evidence class:** 2 for historical/causal interpretation; use as critique, not as authority for the stronger claim that the study alone caused later disease or that the Navy deliberately withheld protection.
- **Value:** Forces a modern methodological audit of the central survey and directly contests state-of-the-art defense uses of it.

## Source-handling rules for revision

- No quotation from a secondary review should remain in final notes if the original document can be obtained lawfully.
- Court opinions can authenticate quoted exhibit language, but full exhibits should replace snippets for contextual claims.
- A classified stamp is evidence of access restriction, not automatically of health-concealment motive.
- A safety requirement is evidence of formal hazard recognition/control policy, not of implementation.
- A normal chest film is a contemporaneous observation, not a lifetime negative outcome.
- A large cross-sectional denominator is not automatically a mature-risk denominator; exposure duration and disease latency must travel with prevalence figures.
- Later science changes the state of knowledge prospectively; it should not be projected backward as if known in wartime.
- Current VA/OSHA/NIOSH materials establish current or later institutional posture, not what a 1940s official knew.
