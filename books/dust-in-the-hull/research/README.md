# Research notes — Dust in the Hull

This research folder keeps the book's factual spine separate from the manuscript so that a strong sentence cannot quietly become stronger evidence than the source behind it.

## Working thesis

The strongest documented contradiction in the Navy asbestos record is not that officials possessed the full modern cancer picture during World War II and secretly ignored it. It is that Navy and Maritime Commission records already recognized asbestos dust as a serious occupational hazard, identified inadequate worker protection, and prescribed controls before a 1945-46 Navy-associated cross-sectional survey found only three asbestosis cases among 1,074 pipe coverers and concluded that naval pipe covering was not a dangerous trade. The later cancer and mesothelioma literature, Navy-yard exposure studies, and long-term disease experience expose how badly a short-latency denominator could mislead policy.

## Evidence classes

1. **Verified / declassified / documented facts.** Dated government memoranda, court-reproduced primary records, the 1946 published survey, official safety standards, peer-reviewed epidemiology, OSHA/NIOSH regulatory history, National Archives guidance, VA public-health material, and other records whose existence and content can be checked.
2. **Credible but incomplete reporting, testimony, or inference.** Historical reconstructions of how widely a memorandum circulated; likely exposure inferred from ship design, job, and overhaul records; retrospective worker accounts; interpretations of why a policy persisted; and secondary reconstructions of records not yet recovered in original form.
3. **Disputed allegations or unconfirmed claims.** Assertions that wartime classification was imposed specifically to hide health risks, that a named official deliberately suppressed cancer information, that all workers were wholly unwarned, that one study caused decades of Navy asbestos use, or that a particular later disease can be assigned to one Navy product without individual exposure evidence.
4. **Speculative synthesis / open conjecture.** The broader institutional theory that a short-latency study functioned as an organizational reassurance mechanism; counterfactual estimates of disease avoided under perfect wartime controls; and analogies to modern emerging occupational hazards.

## Source hierarchy

Highest weight goes to contemporaneous primary documents and authoritative reproductions: original journal articles, official court opinions quoting exhibits, Navy/Maritime records, federal regulations, federal archival guides, and government health guidance. Peer-reviewed historical reviews are used both for discovery and interpretation, with conflicts and litigation context kept visible. Legal briefs and expert reports are discovery aids, not neutral findings. News, law-firm summaries, and popular retellings are not used as authority for a contested historical claim when a primary record is available.

## Open archival questions

- Recover the complete 1941 Stephenson memoranda in their original file context, including distribution and response.
- Recover the complete 1942 and 1944 Bath Iron Works reports and resolve exactly how the 38 chest films were classified.
- Recover the 1943 *Minimum Requirements for Safety and Industrial Health in Contract Shipyards* in full and map asbestos-specific provisions.
- Recover Philip Drinker's 1945 correspondence to Captain Thomas Carter and Admiral Edward W. Mills, including attachments, carbon-copy distribution, and the full context of the reference to possible damage suits.
- Recover the complete 1945 report underlying the 1946 Fleischer et al. publication, with worker-level or grouped duration-of-employment data if extant.
- Determine the exposure-duration distribution of all 1,074 workers. This is central to the latency thesis.
- Trace citations and internal Navy use of the 1946 paper from 1946 through 1970 rather than assuming influence from continued material use.
- Reconstruct worker-warning and training practices yard by yard from shop rules, union material, safety bulletins, respirator programs, and testimony.
- Map post-1955 and post-1960 Navy policy updates against Doll, Wagner/Sleggs/Marchand, Selikoff/Churg/Hammond, and Navy's own shipyard evidence.
- Build personal/task exposure matrices for new construction versus overhaul and for insulators versus adjacent trades.
- Recover Long Beach industrial-hygiene records around Marr 1964 and the 1979 radiographic survey.
- Map the legal and administrative route from historical exposure to current VA benefits standards.

## Methodological warning

Historical asbestos dust measurements frequently use millions of particles per cubic foot, while later standards use fibers per cubic centimeter under different sampling/counting methods. Do not make casual numeric conversions. Preserve method, task, location, duration, and control condition. A historical measurement can be above a contemporary limit without being convertible into a modern fiber count.

## Falsification posture

The book's thesis is designed to lose if the documents show that the 1946 cohort had adequate latency, that officials explicitly treated the result as provisional and built durable follow-up, that wartime controls were consistently enforced, or that worker hazard communication was substantially stronger than the present public record suggests.
