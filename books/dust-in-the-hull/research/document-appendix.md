# Document appendix

A working list of the documents the final edition should reproduce, excerpt minimally, or describe at document-level precision. “What it establishes” and “what it does not establish” are kept separate to prevent archive inflation.

## 1. Merewether & Price, 1930 asbestos-dust report

**Document:** E. R. A. Merewether and C. W. Price, British government report on effects of asbestos dust on lungs and dust suppression.

**Establishes:** official prewar recognition of asbestos-related pulmonary fibrosis in heavily exposed workers and engineering approaches to reducing dust.

**Does not establish:** universal U.S. distribution; Navy-specific receipt; modern cancer/mesothelioma knowledge.

**Recovery target:** official UK scan/library record and relevant pages on duration, disease, ventilation, enclosure, wet methods, and medical surveillance.

## 2. Navy Surgeon General / 1939 asbestos medical-survey material

**Document:** Navy material later reproduced in government/litigation compilations indicating that negative chest X-rays did not preclude future asbestosis under continued occupational exposure.

**Establishes if original confirmed:** Navy medical awareness before the 1941 Stephenson memorandum that present negative films did not settle future disease.

**Does not establish:** cancer knowledge.

**Recovery target:** original Bureau of Medicine and Surgery publication and exact citation/page.

## 3. Stephenson memorandum, March 11, 1941

**From:** Commander C. S. Stephenson, Division of Preventive Medicine.

**To:** Surgeon General Ross T. McIntire.

**Key content:** Navy doing considerable asbestos work; Stephenson says men are not being protected as they should; issue reflected in official reports from several yards.

**Establishes:** high-level Navy medical notice of asbestos protection deficiencies.

**Does not establish:** response, enforcement, cancer knowledge, worker warning, concealment intent.

**Current public route:** *GAF Corp. v. United States*, 932 F.2d 947; Senate hearing record.

## 4. Stephenson memorandum, March 4, 1941

**Key later-quoted phrase:** “possible labor unrest” in connection with outside inspection/yard conditions.

**Establishes:** worker/labor reaction was part of contemporary administrative concern, assuming quotation/context confirmed.

**Does not establish:** why Stephenson feared unrest; an intent to conceal asbestos information specifically.

**Recovery target:** complete memorandum plus related inspection correspondence.

## 5. Bath Iron Works industrial-health survey, 1942

**Associated with:** Philip Drinker / U.S. Maritime Commission.

**Key content:** pipe-covering shop makes/cuts/pounds asbestos materials; work spreads fibers; conditions described as a very real asbestos hazard; segregation and ventilation recommended.

**Establishes:** contemporary shipyard-specific hazard recognition and feasible engineering-control recommendation.

**Does not establish:** actual installation/effectiveness of controls; long-term cancer risk.

**Current public routes:** *GAF* opinion; Franke & Paustenbach 2011 transcription.

## 6. Navy/Maritime Commission Minimum Requirements, 1943

**Document:** *Minimum Requirements for Safety and Industrial Health in Contract Shipyards*.

**Reported asbestos-related content:** safety/ventilation supervision, segregation/control, respiratory protection, periodic medical examination.

**Establishes:** formal institutional policy treated asbestos exposure as requiring control.

**Does not establish:** implementation or worker understanding.

**Recovery target:** complete standard and revision history.

## 7. Bath Iron Works medical/radiographic report, 1944

**Status:** described in later court opinion as classified.

**Reported findings:** significant dust-related changes in a subset of 38 chest films, with several workers diagnosed with asbestosis.

**Establishes:** restricted wartime Navy/shipyard medical evidence of actual disease concern among exposed workers.

**Does not establish:** reason for classification; diagnostic certainty under modern criteria; fleetwide prevalence.

**Recovery target:** entire report, film-reading criteria, exposure histories, distribution/classification markings, declassification record.

## 8. Drinker memorandum to Captain Thomas J. Carter, 1945

**Reported content:** Bath evidence indicates a fairly serious dust risk and makes similar findings probable at plants/yards using the same pipe-covering materials.

**Establishes if original confirmed:** concern about generalizability immediately before the broad survey.

**Does not establish:** what Carter did; final Navy policy.

**Current route:** Franke & Paustenbach 2011.

## 9. 1945 prepublication pipe-covering report

**Document:** report transmitted before publication as the basis of Fleischer et al. 1946.

**Recovery needs:** worker tenure distribution, yard list, selection procedures, clinical/radiographic criteria, all dust samples, control conditions, rejected/excluded subjects, correspondence around interpretation.

**Central test:** How many of the 1,074 workers had ten, fifteen, or twenty years in asbestos pipe covering? The latency thesis should not be considered mechanically established until this is extracted.

## 10. Drinker letter to Admiral Edward W. Mills, September 21, 1945

**Reported content:** describes pipe covering as found in Navy yards as most unlikely to cause ill health; seeks publication permission; recommends conclusions reach insurance divisions concerned with possible worker damage suits.

**Establishes if original confirmed:** reassuring interpretation and explicit awareness of liability relevance.

**Does not establish:** manipulation/fraud; a plan to suppress worker claims.

**Current route:** Franke & Paustenbach 2011.

## 11. Fleischer et al., January 1946 published paper

**Published source:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21016030/

**Reported core:** 1,074 pipe coverers examined; three asbestosis cases; long tenure among diagnosed workers; dust observations; selected engineering-control recommendations; conclusion that naval pipe covering is not a dangerous occupation.

**Establishes:** the public scientific record and the categorical reassurance at the center of the book.

**Does not establish:** downstream policy causation by itself.

## 12. Doll, 1955 lung-cancer mortality study

**Source:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1037613/

**Establishes:** major cohort evidence strengthening asbestos–lung-cancer association.

**Does not establish:** identical risk magnitude in every naval exposure setting.

## 13. Wagner, Sleggs & Marchand, 1960 mesothelioma paper

**Source:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1038078/

**Establishes:** 33 diffuse pleural mesothelioma cases with probable crocidolite exposure in all but one; important occupational/environmental exposure pattern.

**Does not establish:** equal potency of all asbestos types.

## 14. Selikoff, Churg & Hammond, 1964 insulation-worker study

**Source:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14107207/

**Establishes:** severe cancer mortality signal in a mature U.S. insulation-worker cohort.

**Does not establish:** individual product causation for Navy veterans.

## 15. Marr, 1964 Long Beach Naval Shipyard

**Source:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14168424/

**Establishes:** continuing exposure during vessel overhaul/fabrication/installation/removal; real asbestosis disability experience; limitations of X-ray-only control.

**Does not establish:** fleetwide exposure levels.

## 16. OSHA asbestos standards, 1971–1976

**Source for regulatory history:** https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/federalregister/1994-08-10

**Establishes:** federal legal limits tightened from 12 f/cc to 5 f/cc and later 2 f/cc, with the early standard primarily aimed at asbestosis and only hoped-for incidental cancer protection.

**Does not establish:** a safe/no-risk biological threshold.

## 17. NIOSH asbestos criteria, 1972

**Source:** https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/19321

**Establishes:** federal scientific/control recommendations including exposure measurement, warning, work practices, protective equipment, and medical surveillance.

## 18. Felton, 1979 Long Beach radiographic survey

**Source:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/294186/

**Establishes:** 6,640 employees X-rayed; 1,061 (16.0%) classified with asbestos-related abnormalities in that survey.

**Does not establish:** directly comparable disease prevalence with 1946 survey; all-yard prevalence.

## 19. *GAF Corporation v. United States*, 1991

**Source:** https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F2/932/947/288782/

**Establishes:** legal holding plus public reproduction of key wartime documents used in litigation.

**Does not establish:** that the Navy was exonerated historically or that every allegation in the case was found true.

## 20. National Archives Navy-asbestos research guide

**Source:** https://www.archives.gov/research/military/navy/guided-topics/asbestos

**Establishes:** record architecture for ship-specific reconstruction and the complexity of Bureau of Ships research.

**Does not establish:** asbestos aboard a specific vessel without record work.

## 21. *Air & Liquid Systems Corp. v. DeVries*, 2019

**Source:** https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/18pdf/17-1104_2co3.pdf

**Establishes:** modern maritime duty-to-warn framework for certain integrated products involving later-added dangerous parts; Navy sailor factual setting.

**Does not establish:** a retroactive legal duty in 1940s cases.

## 22. 2026 “When errors become etched in stone” critique

**Source:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42307414/

**Establishes:** current scholarly argument that the Fleischer/Drinker study was deeply flawed and consequential; identifies specific methodological criticisms to audit.

**Does not establish:** its strongest downstream-causation claims without independent policy/citation tracing.
