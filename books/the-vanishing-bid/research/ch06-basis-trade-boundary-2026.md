# Chapter 6 research — the basis trade as liquidity and fragility

## Claim under test

Chapter 6 uses the Treasury cash-futures basis trade to show how leverage can turn ordinary-day liquidity into forced selling during stress. That mechanism is well supported, but the cleanest version is not that the basis trade is inherently destabilizing. The same trade can improve market functioning in normal conditions and become a transmission channel when leverage, funding pressure, and correlated deleveraging arrive together.

## Primary and authoritative evidence

### Federal Reserve, June 2020 Monetary Policy Report

The Federal Reserve's June 12, 2020 Monetary Policy Report documents severe deterioration in Treasury and agency-MBS market functioning in late February and March. Investors sold large volumes of longer-duration securities to move toward shorter-term, more liquid assets; long Treasury yields, after initially falling, began rising in mid-March under selling pressure, while bid-ask spreads widened sharply.

Source: Federal Reserve Board, *Monetary Policy Report*, June 12, 2020, pp. 45–47.
https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/files/20200612_mprfullreport.pdf

This supports the chapter's broad "dash for cash" mechanism without requiring the basis trade to carry the whole causal burden.

### Treasury / FSOC: hedge funds materially contributed, but were not the only sellers

In February 2023 Treasury Secretary Janet Yellen summarized FSOC's post-event assessment: hedge funds were among the top three sellers of Treasury securities in March 2020 and "materially contributed" to Treasury-market dysfunction. That wording is important. It supports a meaningful causal role while stopping short of saying hedge funds or the basis trade caused the episode by themselves.

Source: U.S. Department of the Treasury, remarks by Secretary Janet L. Yellen, February 14, 2023.
https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/jy1276

### FSOC 2024: the strongest counterargument belongs in the same paragraph

FSOC's 2024 Annual Report describes the basis trade as an arbitrage in which hedge funds short Treasury futures and hold offsetting cash Treasuries financed through repo. It explicitly records the benefit: the strategy translates futures demand into demand for Treasury securities, improves liquidity, reduces segmentation between cash and futures markets, and contributes to efficient functioning in normal conditions. Profitability generally requires substantial leverage, however, leaving funds exposed when historical price relationships or funding conditions break down. FSOC points to March 2020 as the case in which a rapid unwind could threaten market functioning.

Source: Financial Stability Oversight Council, *2024 Annual Report*, section 3.2.2.
https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/261/FSOC2024AnnualReport.pdf

## Strongest counterargument / disconfirming evidence

The basis trade is not simply a machine for manufacturing fragility. Official assessments credit it with supplying Treasury demand and connecting the cash and futures markets. A manuscript formulation that describes the strategy as built on an assumption of "near-zero risk" or implies that its normal function is parasitic risks flattening the mechanism. Relative-value traders can add real liquidity; the problem appears when thin spreads are financed with enough leverage that a funding or margin shock forces many participants to unwind together.

The March 2020 event was also a broad dash for cash. Foreign holders, mutual funds and other investors sold Treasuries alongside hedge funds. The evidence therefore supports "material contributor" more strongly than "single cause."

## Editorial implication

On the next Chapter 6 prose pass, preserve the vivid explanation of the basis trade but give the trade its peacetime function before showing its crisis failure. That makes the argument more interesting: the fragility does not come from a mechanism that is obviously bad. It comes from a useful liquidity-producing mechanism whose benefits and failure mode are produced by the same architecture.

A precise boundary sentence would be: **the basis trade can add liquidity on an ordinary Tuesday and withdraw it under stress; leverage is what connects those two facts.**
