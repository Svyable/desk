# Research Brief — The Abstraction Trap

## Purpose

This book studies recursive abstractions: representations that begin by simplifying reality and later alter the reality they describe. The research standard is therefore twofold. Concrete historical, scientific, regulatory, and quantitative claims should be traceable to primary or high-authority sources; interpretive claims should remain visibly interpretive rather than borrowing certainty from the sources.

## Evidence hierarchy

Prefer primary documents, official archives, standards and regulatory guidance, original research, and peer-reviewed scholarship. Use reputable secondary scholarship for historical interpretation where primary documents are insufficient. Current claims must carry dates because institutional guidance and AI capabilities can change quickly.

## Claim rules

- Distinguish a measurement from the construct it is intended to represent.
- Distinguish correlation, mechanism, incentive effect, and causal claim.
- Do not infer individual truth from population averages without qualification.
- Treat rankings, ratings, benchmarks, and prices as bounded representations, not universal measures of quality or value.
- Preserve counterexamples and cases where abstraction improves fairness, safety, or coordination.
- For adaptive systems, ask whether publication or use of the measure changes behavior and therefore changes later measurements.
- Current AI and banking-regulation claims are dated to the authoritative source used.

## Chapter source trails and falsification notes

### Chapter 1 — The Map That Moves the Territory

Sources:
- Library of Congress, Gerardus Mercator cartographic materials and historical collection context: https://www.loc.gov/item/2021668435/
- U.S. Geological Survey, map projections background and the unavoidable distortion of transforming the globe to a plane: https://www.usgs.gov/programs/core-science-systems/national-geospatial-program/map-projections
- Charles Goodhart, background to the later-named Goodhart's law; the book uses the principle cautiously rather than as a universal theorem.

Falsification note: The chapter does not claim that maps or metrics necessarily corrupt behavior. The narrower claim is that consequential representations can become inputs to adaptive systems and should therefore be evaluated after people begin steering by them.

### Chapter 2 — The Miracle of Leaving Things Out

Sources:
- Claude E. Shannon, “A Mathematical Theory of Communication,” *Bell System Technical Journal* 27 (1948), DOI: https://doi.org/10.1002/j.1538-7305.1948.tb01338.x and https://doi.org/10.1002/j.1538-7305.1948.tb00917.x
- Herbert A. Simon, work on bounded rationality and administrative behavior; Nobel Prize overview: https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/economic-sciences/1978/simon/facts/

Falsification note: The chapter does not equate cognition with Shannon information theory. Shannon is used as an example of a deliberately bounded abstraction whose power came partly from excluding semantics from a particular engineering problem.

### Chapter 3 — The Score Is Not the Music

Sources:
- Library of Congress, Music Division collections and notation/manuscript holdings: https://www.loc.gov/rr/perform/
- Smithsonian Institution resources on photography and the interpretive consequences of framing and photographic practice: https://www.si.edu/spotlight/photography
- Russell W. Belk, “Possessions and the Extended Self,” *Journal of Consumer Research* 15(2), 1988, DOI: https://doi.org/10.1086/209154

Falsification note: Artistic notation is used as an analogy for incomplete but powerful representation. The chapter does not claim every artistic convention has the same social effects as a business metric.

### Chapter 4 — The Number Called Money

Sources:
- Financial Accounting Standards Board, Accounting Standards Codification and standard-setting resources: https://www.fasb.org/
- U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis, GDP methodology and national accounts: https://www.bea.gov/resources/methodologies
- Federal Reserve educational material on money and payment systems: https://www.federalreserve.gov/paymentsystems.htm

Falsification note: The chapter does not claim GDP, accounting income, or money are defective because they omit nonmarket value. The claim is that they become misleading when used outside the questions their definitions can answer.

### Chapter 5 — The Price That Watches You Back

Sources:
- Federal Reserve History, “Long-Term Capital Management, 1998,” including the September 23, 1998 private recapitalization and the Federal Reserve Bank of New York's facilitation: https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/ltcm-near-failure
- U.S. Treasury testimony on LTCM, including end-1997 balance-sheet assets and capital: https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/rr3137
- President's Working Group report materials via the CFTC on hedge funds and LTCM: https://www.cftc.gov/sites/default/files/tm/tmhedgefundreport.htm

Falsification note: LTCM is not offered as proof that quantitative finance is inherently destabilizing. The narrower claim concerns leverage, liquidity, correlated responses, and the possibility that risk controls become endogenous when many actors react similarly.

### Chapter 6 — The Company in the Spreadsheet

Sources:
- Financial Accounting Standards Board, U.S. GAAP and conceptual framework resources: https://www.fasb.org/
- U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission, public-company financial reporting guidance: https://www.sec.gov/edgar
- David J. Teece, “Profiting from technological innovation,” *Research Policy* 15(6), 1986, DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/0048-7333(86)90027-2

Falsification note: Budgeting and financial controls are not presented as causes of short-termism in every firm. The chapter argues that reporting periods and incentive systems can create temporal mismatches when unmeasured future capability is sacrificed for visible current performance.

### Chapter 7 — The Target Eats the Measure

Sources:
- Donald T. Campbell, “Assessing the Impact of Planned Social Change,” originally 1976; later reprint DOI: https://doi.org/10.56645/jmde.v7i15.297
- Charles Goodhart, monetary-policy work associated with the later formulation known as Goodhart's law; Bank of England biographical context: https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/about/people/charles-goodhart/biography

Falsification note: The chapter rejects the lazy version of “all metrics are gamed.” Narrow, stable, observable tasks can be measured extremely well. Distortion risk rises when a multidimensional objective is reduced to a high-stakes proxy.

### Chapter 8 — The Factory of Categories

Sources:
- Russell W. Belk, “Possessions and the Extended Self,” DOI: https://doi.org/10.1086/209154
- U.S. Federal Trade Commission, consumer privacy and data-use resources: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/privacy-security
- U.S. Equal Employment Opportunity Commission, employment discrimination and technology resources: https://www.eeoc.gov/

Falsification note: Segmentation and personalization are not assumed to manipulate consumers or discriminate by default. The test is whether a category improves prediction or service while remaining contestable and appropriate to the decision.

### Chapter 9 — The Credential and the Person

Sources:
- Michael Spence, “Job Market Signaling,” *Quarterly Journal of Economics* 87(3), 1973, DOI: https://doi.org/10.2307/1882010
- U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, occupational licensing and labor-market resources: https://www.bls.gov/
- Edward L. Deci and Richard M. Ryan, self-determination theory overview and bibliography: https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/

Falsification note: The chapter does not claim education is merely signaling. Learning, selection, social capital, credentialing, and signaling can all contribute, with proportions varying by program, occupation, and person.

### Chapter 10 — The Model of Risk

Sources:
- Federal Reserve History, LTCM: https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/ltcm-near-failure
- Federal Reserve, SR 26-2, “Model Risk Management,” issued April 17, 2026, revised interagency guidance superseding SR 11-7 for covered institutions: https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/SR2602.htm
- U.S. Treasury LTCM testimony: https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/rr3137

Falsification note: The current regulatory claim is dated April 17, 2026. The chapter does not infer that model governance prevents tail losses; it treats governance as a response to conceptual, implementation, use, and change risks that remain irreducible.

### Chapter 11 — Seventy-Three Seconds

Sources:
- Presidential Commission on the Space Shuttle Challenger Accident, Rogers Commission Report, NASA Technical Reports Server: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/archive/nasa/casi.ntrs.nasa.gov/19870002391.pdf
- NASA History Office, Challenger accident resources: https://history.nasa.gov/rogersrep/genindex.htm

Falsification note: The chapter does not reduce Challenger to a single cultural slogan. It keeps the physical joint/O-ring mechanism and the organizational launch-decision process distinct before examining how they interacted.

### Chapter 12 — The War on the Dashboard

Sources:
- U.S. National Archives, complete Pentagon Papers collection; the study was commissioned by Secretary of Defense Robert McNamara in 1967: https://www.archives.gov/research/pentagon-papers
- U.S. Intelligence Community, “Tet at Fifty,” declassified materials and retrospective analysis: https://www.intelligence.gov/tet-declassified/tet-at-fifty

Falsification note: The chapter does not claim the Vietnam War was run only by body counts or dashboards. It uses quantitative progress indicators as one window into the larger strategic problem of connecting tactical measures to political objectives.

### Chapter 13 — The Platform's Eye

Sources:
- U.S. Federal Trade Commission, competition and digital-platform resources: https://www.ftc.gov/
- National Institute of Standards and Technology, recommender/AI risk concepts through AI RMF resources: https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework
- Research literature on recommender systems and exploration/exploitation is used conceptually; no specific numerical performance claim depends on it.

Falsification note: The chapter does not assume recommendation harms users. Personalization can reduce search costs and create discovery. The recursive risk arises because recommendations determine exposure and exposure becomes future training data.

### Chapter 14 — The Model as Product

Sources:
- NIST, *Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0)*, released January 26, 2023, DOI: https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AI.100-1
- NIST AI RMF resource center: https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework

Falsification note: Claims about specific AI winners, architectures, and future economic capture are deliberately avoided. The durable claim is about evaluation: benchmark performance, deployment reliability, workflow integration, consequence, reversibility, and monitoring are different layers.

### Chapter 15 — The Organization That Exists on Paper

Sources:
- U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission, corporate disclosure and subsidiary reporting resources: https://www.sec.gov/edgar
- U.S. Small Business Administration, business-structure overview: https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/launch-your-business/choose-business-structure

Falsification note: Legal entities and formal org charts are not treated as illusions. They are causal institutions. The chapter argues only that formal, financial, cultural, and informal maps can diverge and should not be universalized into one description of the firm.

### Chapter 16 — The Index Owns the Market

Sources:
- U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission, investor resources on index funds and ETFs: https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/glossary/index-fund
- S&P Dow Jones Indices, methodology library: https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/documents/methodologies/
- U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission, credit-rating-agency oversight: https://www.sec.gov/about/divisions-offices/office-credit-ratings

Falsification note: The chapter does not claim indexing mechanically determines market prices. It makes the narrower institutional claim that a benchmark becomes causal when mandates, flows, contracts, or regulatory treatment attach to its classifications.

### Chapter 17 — The City on Paper

Sources:
- U.S. Library of Congress, city-planning and urban-history collections: https://www.loc.gov/
- UNESCO, Brasília World Heritage description and planning context: https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/445/
- U.S. Department of Transportation, Federal Highway Administration history and planning resources: https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/

Falsification note: Modernist planning is not reduced to aesthetic arrogance; sanitation, transport, housing, and infrastructure problems were real. The chapter's claim is that street-level functions and transition costs can remain invisible at metropolitan resolution.

### Chapter 18 — The Worker in the Algorithm

Sources:
- U.S. Department of Labor, worker and workplace data resources: https://www.dol.gov/
- National Institute for Occupational Safety and Health, work organization and worker well-being resources: https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/
- Edward L. Deci and Richard M. Ryan, self-determination theory bibliography: https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/

Falsification note: The chapter does not assume algorithmic management is worse than human supervision. Standardization can reduce favoritism and improve routing. The critical variables are control, contestability, local knowledge, error correction, and the incentives attached to measured output.

### Chapter 19 — The State's Simplified Citizen

Sources:
- U.S. Census Bureau, statistical definitions and methodology: https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/cps/technical-documentation/methodology.html
- U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, labor-force concepts and unemployment definitions: https://www.bls.gov/cps/definitions.htm
- U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Consumer Price Index methodology: https://www.bls.gov/cpi/questions-and-answers.htm

Falsification note: Aggregate statistics are not criticized for differing from individual experience. Population and individual questions operate at different scales. The failure occurs when one scale is rhetorically substituted for another.

### Chapter 20 — The Scientist's Escape Hatch

Sources:
- Open Science Collaboration, “Estimating the reproducibility of psychological science,” *Science* 349(6251), 2015, DOI: https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aac4716
- National Academies, *Reproducibility and Replicability in Science* (2019): https://nap.nationalacademies.org/catalog/25303/reproducibility-and-replicability-in-science
- U.S. National Library of Medicine / ClinicalTrials.gov resources on trial registration: https://clinicaltrials.gov/

Falsification note: Replication is not presented as a universal procedure identical across fields. The deeper principle is independent reality contact appropriate to the evidence type.

### Chapter 21 — The Counterexample Economy

Sources:
- David J. Teece, “Profiting from technological innovation,” DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/0048-7333(86)90027-2
- U.S. Small Business Administration and Census Bureau resources on firm entry/exit and entrepreneurship: https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/bds.html

Falsification note: The chapter does not claim every inferior-looking entrant is disruptive or that incumbents should chase every anomaly. Its proposed defense is option-like experimentation with explicit evidence that can kill as well as support a thesis.

### Chapter 22 — Recontact With Reality

Sources:
- National Transportation Safety Board, investigation and safety-recommendation methods: https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/Pages/default.aspx
- National Academies, *Reproducibility and Replicability in Science*: https://nap.nationalacademies.org/catalog/25303/reproducibility-and-replicability-in-science

Falsification note: Direct observation is not assumed to be unmediated truth. Field visits, interviews, audits, experiments, and alternative datasets have their own biases. The point is to combine evidence streams whose failure modes are not identical.

### Chapter 23 — The Art of Useful Abstraction

Sources:
- This chapter synthesizes the preceding research rather than introducing a new empirical case.
- NIST AI RMF, FASB/BEA measurement institutions, NASA Challenger investigation, National Archives Pentagon Papers, and reproducibility literature provide the recurring examples of bounded representation plus external checking.

Falsification note: The concluding disciplines are analytical synthesis, not claimed universal laws. They should be judged by whether they improve decisions in contexts where representations are consequential, adaptive, and incomplete.

## Strongest counterargument

The strongest objection to the book is that “abstraction trap” can become so broad that it redescribes every failure after the fact. A model, metric, category, or map is involved in nearly every modern decision; blaming abstraction would then explain nothing. The manuscript addresses this by requiring a specific causal bridge: identify what the representation omitted or compressed, show how consequential use changed incentives or information flow, and identify evidence that would distinguish representation failure from ordinary execution failure. If that bridge cannot be made, “abstraction trap” should not be used as the explanation.
