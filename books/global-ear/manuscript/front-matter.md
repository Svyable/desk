# GLOBAL EAR

## The secret sensors built for enemies that discovered the planet instead

### Sven Hardy Benson

---

This is a book about instruments that were pointed at the wrong thing and therefore saw something real.

It is also about a dangerous sentence:

*The government probably has better data.*

Sometimes it does. That is a historical fact. It is not a method.

The Cold War produced sensing systems whose original customers were not geologists, biologists or astronomers. They were military and intelligence organizations trying to hear submarines, nuclear detonations, missile signatures and other strategic events. Some of those systems later supplied extraordinary scientific observations when access widened. The same physical infrastructure that listened for an adversary could listen to the Earth.

That fact creates two symmetrical errors.

The first is the **public-instrument fallacy**: if civilian science did not record an event, nobody with a capable instrument recorded it.

The second is the **classified-oracle fallacy**: because hidden sensors can exist, an unseen classified record may be treated as if it supports whichever anomaly one prefers.

This book permits neither move.

For controversial material, every working claim is separated into four classes:

1. **Verified / declassified / documented fact.** Public primary records, official histories, released program records, treaty-monitoring documentation, peer-reviewed measurements, or reproducible technical facts.
2. **Credible but incomplete reporting or testimony.** Accounts with identifiable provenance and plausible access whose decisive underlying records are unavailable or incomplete.
3. **Disputed allegation.** Claims lacking independent confirmation, including claims that unspecified classified sensors recorded extraordinary events.
4. **Speculative synthesis / open conjecture.** Explicit hypotheses about what dual-use sensing implies for anomaly detection, secrecy, scientific denominators and future observatories.

A sensor's existence is not evidence for an event.

A classified sensor's possible existence is not even a measurement.

The governing rule is narrower and more useful:

**Before treating an anomaly as unobserved, ask who was capable of observing it. Before treating hidden observation as evidence, produce the observation.**
