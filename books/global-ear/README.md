## Thesis

The most interesting anomaly detector may be the instrument built for something else. Cold War and treaty-verification systems designed to detect submarines, nuclear tests and strategic threats repeatedly became observatories for earthquakes, volcanoes, meteors and whales. That history creates a hard epistemic problem for anomalous claims: absence from the public scientific record is not the same as absence from all sensing, but classified sensing cannot be invoked as evidence unless records, releases or independently testable consequences exist.
