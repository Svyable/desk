# Cybernetics and Information-Theory Prior Art

This note records an older body of work that sharply limits what *The Gradient Universe* can claim about future control, response variety, sensing, memory, and agency.

The reachability conjecture arrived from thermodynamics and viability theory. That route makes it easy to overlook cybernetics and information-theoretic control, where several apparently novel intuitions have existed for decades.

## Ashby: requisite variety

W. Ross Ashby's *An Introduction to Cybernetics* (1956) developed the law of requisite variety: regulation is limited by the effective variety of responses available relative to the variety of disturbances that must be controlled.

Archival source: https://doi.org/10.5962/bhl.title.5851

A later paper by Conant and Ashby gave the Good Regulator Theorem under explicit assumptions: a maximally successful and simple regulator must embody a model isomorphic to the system it regulates.

Primary link: https://doi.org/10.1080/00207727008920220

The book must therefore not claim that it discovered any of the following:

- a regulator needs an adequate repertoire of responses;
- more diverse disturbances can require more effective control variety;
- restrictions on the regulator's action repertoire can make some disturbances uncontrollable;
- successful model-based regulation depends on internal structure corresponding to relevant environmental structure.

Ashby's language is especially close to the book's distinction between disturbance sets and viable response sets.

The thermodynamic question left open is narrower: **how does a physically constructed constraint alter effective disturbance/response variety once the work required to build, maintain, sense, and actuate it is charged to the same physical budget?**

A membrane can improve regulation in two conceptually different ways. It can reduce the disturbance variety presented to the interior by passively excluding some perturbations. It can also create new controllable couplings, such as selective channels and pumps. Requisite variety describes a structural control limit. It does not by itself supply the energetic bookkeeping for constructing and maintaining those regulatory structures.

## Empowerment: future controllability as channel capacity

Klyubin, Polani, and Nehaniv introduced **empowerment** in 2005 as an information-theoretic measure of an agent's potential control over future sensory states. It is defined through the channel capacity from an agent's actions to later sensory outcomes.

Primary DOI: https://doi.org/10.1109/CEC.2005.1554676

Open repository record: https://uhra.herts.ac.uk/id/eprint/282/

Empowerment is very close to any loose phrase such as “future options,” “control over possible futures,” or “the size of an agent's action consequences.”

The book must not present those ideas as new.

The proposed distinction is again physical and narrower. Empowerment is an information-theoretic channel-capacity quantity. Constraint-selective reachability asks whether a **specified embodied constraint**, after its construction and maintenance costs are included, changes which disturbances or recovery targets remain physically viable under a declared thermodynamic budget. It does not assume that maximizing channel capacity is the biological objective.

Indeed, the book's gate examples deliberately show cases in which useful regulation reduces raw outcome variety. An ion-selective membrane or safety interlock can lower unconstrained channel capacity while improving survival.

## Information thermodynamics and feedback

The thermodynamics of feedback and information processing is already a mature field.

Sagawa and Ueda generalized nonequilibrium work relations and second-law bounds in the presence of measurement and feedback. Feedback can change work extraction and entropy balances without violating the second law.

Relevant primary/review links:

- https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.104.090602
- https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.85.021104
- https://doi.org/10.1007/978-4-431-54168-4

Horowitz and Esposito developed a stochastic-thermodynamic formalism for continuous information flow between coupled subsystems:

- https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevX.4.031015

The book therefore must not claim that feedback control makes information physically consequential as a new observation, or that it discovered a thermodynamic price for measurement, memory, or feedback.

## Sensory capacity and energetic efficiency

Hartich, Barato, and Seifert introduced **sensory capacity**, an information-theoretic measure of how well a sensor state captures the information about an external stochastic signal contained in its history. They explicitly studied how adding memory changes sensory performance and derived a trade-off between sensory capacity and thermodynamic efficiency in a cellular sensing model.

PubMed record: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26986297/

Preprint: https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.02111

This is close prior art for any claim that “memory buys future control at an energetic cost.”

The reachability program must go beyond that by predicting an externally testable **recovery or viability boundary** from the physical memory/control architecture rather than merely showing an information–dissipation trade-off.

## First-principles decomposition that remains useful

The cybernetic literature suggests a better way to decompose the book's conjecture.

A self-produced physical constraint can change future regulation through at least four distinct mechanisms:

1. **Disturbance filtering.** A membrane, shell, barrier, buffer, or safety interlock prevents some external perturbations from reaching internal essential variables. The effective disturbance set shrinks.
2. **Affordable action expansion.** A catalyst, pump, stored reserve, redundant pathway, or new actuator makes responses possible within a finite work/time budget that were previously unaffordable.
3. **Response selection.** Sensors and memory improve the mapping from observed conditions to the response that should be deployed, reducing control ambiguity. Ashby and information-theoretic control already cover much of the structural logic here.
4. **Constraint reconstruction.** Some living systems use the resulting viable trajectories to rebuild the very membranes, regulators, enzymes, and stores that maintain the control architecture. This connects directly to constraint-closure literature.

None of these components is individually novel.

The candidate contribution, if one remains, is a quantitative experiment that closes the physical loop: measured work into an endogenous constraint, predicted change in budgeted viable disturbance recovery, and a separately predicted persistence/selection boundary that existing cybernetic, information-theoretic, metabolic-allocation, resilience, or control models do not already predict as well.

## Novelty kill condition

If constraint-selective reachability can always be translated without loss into existing empowerment, requisite-variety, viability-kernel, robust-control, semantic-information, or information-thermodynamics quantities, then the book has produced a useful synthesis rather than a new scientific object.

That outcome is acceptable.

The only defensible route to stronger novelty is an empirical or mathematical result that requires the joint accounting of **endogenous constraint cost + altered viable transition structure + future disturbance distribution** and makes a successful prediction that the neighboring frameworks do not make with equal economy.
