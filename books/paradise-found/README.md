*How Humanity Achieved Post-Scarcity in 2030*

A future history written from the other side of 2030. The book argues that post-scarcity does not require infinite goods or the end of markets. It arrives when involuntary material scarcity becomes optional: when the reliable cost of food, shelter, energy, connectivity, basic mobility, education, essential health care, and useful intelligence falls below what automated productive systems can provide as a universal baseline.

The central claim is deliberately narrower than utopia and larger than cheap AI. *Paradise Found* treats post-scarcity as a convergence problem across intelligence, electricity, robotics, flexible manufacturing, food systems, housing, medicine, logistics, ownership, and institutions. The future-history form keeps the epistemic boundary explicit: evidence through September 2026 is factual and sourced; events after that date are scenario.
