# Source Log

## Primary / official

1. U.S. Navy MK 30 Mod 1 ASW target. Confirms a submarine-like magnetic anomaly signature is deliberately provided for aircraft MAD training. Class 1.
https://www.navy.mil/Resources/Fact-Files/Display-FactFiles/Article/2166727/mk-30-mod-1-anti-submarine-warfare-target/

2. Commander, Undersea Surveillance, About IUSS. Confirms acoustic surveillance mission and SOSUS lineage. Class 1.
https://www.csp.navy.mil/cus/About-IUSS/

3. U.S. Navy ALMDS fact file. Confirms operational airborne lidar for surface/near-surface mine detection. It does not establish deep submarine detection. Class 1.
https://www.navy.mil/Resources/Fact-Files/Display-FactFiles/Article/2166762/anaes-1-airborne-laser-mine-detection-system-almds/

## Peer-reviewed / technical

4. Zheng, Hexing; Gu, Haitao; Gao, Tianzhu. 2026. Review of wake, magnetic and gravity anomaly methods. Treats MAD as relatively mature, wake methods as useful for cueing but environment-sensitive, gravity as immature and weak-signal limited. Class 1 for literature characterization; not proof of operational performance.
https://doi.org/10.3390/drones10060474

5. Liu, Tengjiao; Wang, Dan; Liu, Jinguo. Review of non-acoustic submarine detection technologies covering magnetic, laser, wake, electric field, infrared and SAR. Class 1 for published research interest and review claims; capability assertions require source-level verification.
https://doi.org/10.11993/j.issn.2096-3920.2023-0054

## Serious secondary

6. CSIS On the Radar, 2019. Strategic-stability primer on non-acoustic detection. Useful synthesis; some performance projections are secondary and should not be elevated above primary validation. Class 2.
https://ontheradar.csis.org/issue-briefs/non-acoustic-submarine-detection/

7. Naval News / Lockheed Martin release, 2025, MAD-XR integration. Evidence of procurement/integration activity, not independent performance validation. Class 2.
https://www.navalnews.com/naval-news/2025/06/sikorsky-and-cae-to-integrate-mad-xr-to-mh-60r-helicopters/

## Claims requiring skepticism

- Persistent claims that satellites can continuously track deep SSBNs via wake, heat, gravity, laser, quantum or combined signatures: Class 3 absent independently verifiable performance evidence.
- Claims that classified systems make the ocean effectively transparent: Class 3 unless tied to documents or test data.
- Synthesis that distributed multi-physics sensing may reduce search uncertainty even without independent long-range detection: Class 4, proposed in this book.
