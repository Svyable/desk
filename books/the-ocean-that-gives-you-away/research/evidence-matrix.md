# Evidence Matrix

## Class 1 — verified / documented

- Operational anti-submarine practice includes magnetic anomaly detection; U.S. Navy training targets reproduce submarine-like magnetic signatures.
- U.S. undersea surveillance has long depended on acoustic sensing and oceanography.
- Airborne lidar can detect and localize near-surface mines under operational conditions.
- Peer-reviewed literature studies hydrodynamic wakes, magnetic anomalies and gravity anomalies as non-acoustic underwater-target signatures.
- Navies actively manage magnetic and other physical signatures through degaussing/deperming and signature ranges.

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete

- Public industrial reporting indicates renewed compact MAD integration on MH-60R aircraft.
- Open technical literature, including Chinese research, indicates sustained state interest in SAR wake, laser, electric-field and gravity-related submarine detection.
- Some experimental work indicates submerged-body wakes can produce measurable remote signatures under favorable environmental conditions.

## Class 3 — disputed / unconfirmed

- Claims of reliable strategic-range satellite detection of deeply submerged submarines.
- Claims of operational gravity or quantum systems that make magnetic/acoustic stealth irrelevant.
- Claims that reactor heat or radionuclides allow routine wide-area SSBN tracking.
- Claims that one nation has already rendered the ocean transparent but conceals the result.

## Class 4 — speculative synthesis

- The important transition may be from independent sensor performance to distributed cueing and fusion: wide-area weak indicators reduce search boxes for mature short-range sensors.
- Countermeasures across acoustic, magnetic, thermal, wake and electric channels may create cross-signature tradeoffs.
- Strategic stability could shift before true ocean transparency if states merely become less confident in patrol-area privacy.

## Contradictions / disinformation incentives

- Defense firms benefit from presenting emerging sensors as transformational.
- Navies benefit from obscuring both strengths and weaknesses of ASW systems.
- Submarine services benefit from confidence in survivability; adversaries benefit from suggesting that survivability is eroding.
- Open papers may describe aspirational systems without operational validation.
- Classification gaps should increase uncertainty, not automatically increase belief in breakthrough claims.

## Defeat conditions for the book's synthesis

The multi-physics thesis weakens if blinded field trials show non-acoustic channels add negligible search-box reduction after ordinary acoustic/oceanographic information is included; if false-positive rates explode under realistic sea states; if signature-management countermeasures reduce all useful channels below deployable thresholds; or if platform and coverage costs erase the apparent advantage of distributed sensing.
