# Gravity Without Mercy

A submarine displaces mass. Gravity notices.

That is the cleanest possible statement and the easiest one to misuse. Gravitational attraction cannot be shielded by degaussing coils or anechoic tiles. In principle a sufficiently sensitive gravimeter or gravity gradiometer can detect mass anomalies. In practice the signal from a submerged vehicle is tiny against platform motion, ocean mass variability, waves, bathymetry and the Earth's field.

Recent reviews describe gravity anomaly detection as promising but less mature than magnetic methods, constrained by extremely weak signals and severe platform-stability requirements. That is where the public evidence sits.

The word unshieldable seduces military futurism. It sounds like inevitable detection. But physics contains many unshieldable effects that remain unusable because they are too weak. Neutrinos pass through submarines too. That fact does not give a patrol aircraft a neutrino camera.

Gravity sensing becomes interesting as instruments improve and unmanned platforms get quieter and better stabilized. It may also contribute to fusion even when it cannot independently classify a target. But the burden should be quantitative: expected anomaly at range, instrument noise spectral density, integration time, platform acceleration, environmental covariance and false alarms.

Any claim that skips those numbers is selling inevitability where only possibility exists.
