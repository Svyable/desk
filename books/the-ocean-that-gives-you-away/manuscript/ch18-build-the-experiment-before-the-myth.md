# Build the Experiment Before the Myth

The field needs an embarrassment machine.

Take a conventional submarine or instrumented surrogate. Give an independent operations team freedom to choose routes within a large exercise box. Instrument the environment aggressively. Collect satellite SAR and optical imagery when available. Fly calibrated magnetometers. Deploy surface and underwater unmanned vehicles with magnetic, electric, optical, thermal, flow and acoustic sensors. Include ordinary ships and deliberately generated wakes. Blind the analysis teams to target truth.

Before the exercise, each sensing group must specify what counts as a detection, what spatial and temporal tolerance is allowed, and how many false alarms are acceptable. No moving thresholds after truth is revealed. No trophy-image selection.

Then repeat across seasons, depths, sea states, stratification regimes and bottom geology.

The goal is not to prove that one exotic channel works. It is to map where each channel stops working. That negative map is strategically valuable. It tells designers where stealth still holds and researchers where another order of magnitude in sensitivity would matter.

A second phase should test fusion with frozen algorithms. Does adding a weak channel improve search-box reduction compared with acoustics and oceanography alone? If yes, by how much? Does it survive correlated environmental noise?

Finally, publish sanitized performance envelopes where security permits. Classification can protect useful details, but total opacity encourages fantasy on both sides of the debate.

The myth says the ocean will become transparent. The experiment asks a smaller question: under what conditions does a submarine leave enough mutually consistent evidence that an adversary can become less uncertain?

That is difficult enough. It is also real science.
