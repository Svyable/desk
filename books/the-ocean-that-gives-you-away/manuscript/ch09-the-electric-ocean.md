# The Electric Ocean

Salt water conducts. A metal vessel inside it participates in electrochemistry whether the crew likes the language or not.

Corrosion currents, cathodic protection systems, propulsor motion through Earth's field and other mechanisms can produce electric and magnetic signatures. Naval signature ranges measure multiple influences because mines and sensors can exploit them. The physical basis is not exotic.

The challenge is range. Electric fields in seawater attenuate and are contaminated by natural geomagnetic induction, waves, currents and infrastructure. Local sensors can be useful. Strategic remote detection is another matter.

This channel is especially vulnerable to classified-capability inflation. Because the public knows that navies care about electric signatures but not exactly how well modern arrays perform, the blank space invites mythology. The disciplined position is asymmetrical: accept the existence of the signature; remain agnostic about classified sensitivity; refuse to infer theater-wide detection without evidence.

One should also expect countermeasures. Impressed-current cathodic protection can be designed with signature consequences in mind. Electric-field stealth becomes part of the vessel system, just as degaussing is. That means the measurement problem changes over time. Yesterday's target model ages.

The broader argument strengthens: a submarine is a bundle of coupled signatures, and every attempt to suppress one consumes engineering attention.
