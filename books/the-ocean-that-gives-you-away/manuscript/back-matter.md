# Back Matter

## Working chronology

- 1950s: U.S. fixed acoustic surveillance expands into the system later known as SOSUS/IUSS.
- 1970s onward: U.S. Navy MK 30 ASW training targets support repeated fleet training, including submarine-like magnetic signatures.
- 2000s-2020s: satellite SAR, lidar, compact magnetometers and autonomous platforms improve the economics of non-acoustic sensing research.
- 2016: U.S. Navy ALMDS reaches initial operational capability for airborne lidar detection of near-surface mines, an important optical-control case rather than proof of deep-submarine lidar.
- 2019: CSIS publishes a strategic-stability primer on emerging non-acoustic submarine detection.
- 2023-2026: Chinese technical reviews survey magnetic, wake, laser, electric-field, infrared and gravity-related submarine detection, with unmanned sensor fusion increasingly emphasized.
- 2025: public reporting describes MAD-XR integration work for MH-60R helicopters, reinforcing the continued operational relevance of magnetic anomaly detection.

## Boundary statement

No public source reviewed for this first pass establishes reliable wide-area continuous satellite tracking of deeply submerged strategic submarines. Research interest, physical plausibility and classified possibility are not treated as operational proof.
