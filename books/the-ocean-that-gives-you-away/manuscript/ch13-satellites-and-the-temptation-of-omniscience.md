# Satellites and the Temptation of Omniscience

Every generation invents a satellite rumor that solves the ocean.

The form changes. Infrared sees reactor heat. Radar sees wakes. lasers see the hull. hyperspectral sensors see chemistry. Gravity satellites feel mass. Some future quantum instrument sees everything. The appeal is obvious: space offers enormous coverage, and submarines derive power from hiding across enormous areas.

The public evidence supports caution. Satellites are superb ocean observers. They measure surface temperature, roughness, color, height, winds, currents and many other variables. SAR sees ships and surface wakes. Those capabilities can support anti-submarine warfare indirectly through oceanography, cueing and environmental prediction.

But the leap from ocean observation to reliable deep-submarine tracking is huge. Coverage is not detection. Detection is not classification. Classification is not continuous track. Track is not weapon-quality localization.

A secret capability could exceed public performance. That possibility is real and trivial. It does not license arbitrary magnitude. Classified engineering still pays signal-to-noise ratios.

The best way to discuss satellite submarine detection is therefore through burden of proof. What physical observable is measured? At what depth and sea state? What revisit? What false-alarm rate? What is the independent calibration set? Can the system predict where a known target will appear before truth is revealed?

Without those answers, omniscience is a story told from altitude.
