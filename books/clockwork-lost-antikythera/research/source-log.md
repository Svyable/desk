# Research Trail

## Evidence classes

**Class 1 — verified / documented:** surviving fragments; gear teeth and physically preserved components; inscriptions recoverable from the fragments; imaging data; rear-dial architecture; Metonic and Saros cycles; Games dial; lunar-anomaly gearing; documented wreck excavation evidence; explicit ancient textual passages where editions can be checked.

**Class 2 — credible but incomplete / model-dependent:** the full original front arrangement; exact planetary gear trains; original case and display details; manufacturing sequence and tolerances; exact workshop, maker, ownership history, repair history, production volume, and routine accuracy in use.

**Class 3 — disputed / unsupported:** direct attribution to Archimedes or Hipparchus; claims of a Hellenistic industrial clockmaking sector from this artifact alone; navigation-computer claims without artifact support; Atlantis, time travel, perpetual motion, alien/non-human origin, or modern physics hidden in the device.

**Class 4 — author synthesis:** Antikythera as a calibration case for archaeological survivorship bias; bronze recycling as a technology-erasure mechanism; capability versus prevalence as the central inferential distinction; the idea that reconstruction should carry explicit confidence by component.

## Core primary / authoritative sources

1. Freeth, T. et al. **Decoding the ancient Greek astronomical calculator known as the Antikythera Mechanism.** *Nature* 444, 587–591 (2006). DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/nature05357
   - Core CT-driven reconstruction of rear gearing and inscriptions; use for physical evidence, gear relationships, lunar anomaly, and imaging history.

2. Freeth, T., Jones, A., Steele, J. M., Bitsakis, Y. **Calendars with Olympiad display and eclipse prediction on the Antikythera Mechanism.** *Nature* 454, 614–617 (2008). DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/nature07130
   - Core evidence for Metonic/Saros structures, Games dial, calendars, and eclipse prediction.

3. Freeth, T. et al. **A Model of the Cosmos in the ancient Greek Antikythera Mechanism.** *Scientific Reports* 11, 5821 (2021). DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-021-84310-w
   - Important front/cosmos reconstruction. Treat as a model constrained by inscriptions, physical space, period relations, and surviving evidence; the paper itself notes absence of direct surviving gearing for significant front mechanisms.

4. Anastasiou, A., Bitsakis, Y., Jones, A., Moussas, X., Tselikas, A., Zafeiropoulou, M. **The Front Cover Inscription.** *Almagest* 7.1 (2016), 250–297. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1484/J.ALMAGEST.5.110737
   - Open-access inscription study. Supports planetary synodic-cycle text and the inference that planetary motions were displayed on the front; does not uniquely recover all missing mechanical implementation.

5. Antikythera Mechanism Research Project, **The Inscriptions of the Antikythera Mechanism**, *Almagest* 7.1 (2016), special issue. Table of contents/abstracts: https://hpdst.gr/publications/almagest/issues/7-1
   - Full inscription publication spine; next pass should retrieve and page-index all five main papers rather than rely on secondary summaries.

6. Price, Derek de Solla. **Gears from the Greeks: The Antikythera Mechanism — A Calendar Computer from ca. 80 B.C.** Transactions of the American Philosophical Society 64.7 (1974). Publisher record: https://www.pennpress.org/9780871696472/gears-from-the-greeks/
   - Essential historical baseline using radiography, gear counts, metal analysis, and an earlier reconstruction. Must be read against later CT results rather than treated as current final architecture.

7. Woan, Graham; Bayley, Joseph. **An improved calendar ring hole-count for the Antikythera mechanism.** *Horological Journal* (July 2024), 282–287. University of Glasgow repository: https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/329337/
   - Bayesian/statistical reanalysis of measured calendar-ring holes. Reports estimates around 354–355 holes under model assumptions; strongly disfavors 360 and makes 365 implausible within the model. Treat as active interpretive evidence, not a license to erase all calendar debate.

8. Greek Ministry of Culture / Antikythera underwater excavation programme, 2021–2025. Ministry home: https://www.culture.gov.gr/ ; July 2025 reporting based on Ministry announcement: https://www.amna.gr/mobile/article/915792/Five-year-underwater-archaeological-research-project-in-Antikythera-concluded
   - Current shipwreck context, including recovery of articulated hull remains first identified in 2024. Next pass should recover the original Greek Ministry announcement and excavation reports directly.

9. Marchant, Jo. **Human skeleton found on famed Antikythera shipwreck.** *Nature* 537, 462–463 (2016). DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/537462a
   - Useful shipwreck excavation context and bibliography; not a mechanism-function source.

## Ancient-text targets

The full draft must use critical editions/translations rather than quote modern paraphrases. Priority passages include Cicero's references to Archimedean and Posidonian spheres/or reries in *De re publica* and *De natura deorum*, plus relevant passages in Vitruvius, Pappus, Hero, and later witnesses where they bear on astronomical mechanisms, gearing, automata, or instrument manufacture. Textual references should establish only what the text actually says; they must not be used to identify the Antikythera maker without independent evidence.

## Manufacturing / experimental archaeology targets

- metallurgy of the surviving plates and gears;
- tooth-profile measurement and tool-mark studies;
- ancient bronze sheet production and joining;
- experimental cutting of gears with reconstructed Hellenistic tools;
- bearing/friction/wear analysis;
- dimensional stack-up and assembly sequence;
- whether proposed front reconstructions can be built within surviving case-depth constraints using plausible ancient fabrication methods.

## Falsification notes

The book's central synthesis weakens if comparative evidence shows that bronze mechanisms of this type preserve readily and should be common if more than one existed; if the artifact's sophistication can be produced without any transferable workshop practice; or if future discoveries establish Antikythera as a demonstrably singular commissioned experiment with no technical lineage. Conversely, discovery of additional geared astronomical fragments would strengthen the broader-tradition hypothesis but would still require an empirical denominator before claims about prevalence.

The book must not turn “survivorship bias exists” into evidence for any specific missing machine. Preservation models constrain how much absence can prove; they do not manufacture positive evidence.
