# Notes on Evidence

Antikythera is unusually vulnerable to a visual mistake: reconstructed wholes look more certain than fragmentary originals. Throughout this manuscript, claims should be read according to the four evidence classes recorded in `research/evidence-matrix.md`.

The surviving artifact is housed at the National Archaeological Museum in Athens. Major modern technical understanding rests on successive layers of direct study: early inspection, Price/Karakalos radiography, later imaging and mechanical reconstruction, the 2005 microfocus X-ray CT / surface-imaging campaign, the Nature papers of 2006 and 2008, the systematic inscription publications in *Almagest* in 2016, and later front-display and statistical reconstruction work.

A reconstruction that satisfies several constraints can be excellent scholarship without becoming recovered metal. The final edition should label diagrams accordingly: **surviving**, **directly constrained reconstruction**, **model-dependent reconstruction**, or **illustrative/speculative**.

# Research Principle

The book should become stranger as its evidence improves. If a dramatic claim requires us to ignore the fragment, flatten a dispute, invent a workshop, or turn a literary analogy into provenance, it is not helping the story. The surviving machine is already difficult enough.
