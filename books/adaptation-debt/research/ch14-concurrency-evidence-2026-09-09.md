# Chapter 14 evidence audit — concurrency and premature commitment

Date checked: 2026-09-09

## Purpose

Chapter 14 argues that adaptation can become expensive when an organization attaches too much irreversibility to uncertainty that is still resolving. The F-35 program's production/testing overlap is used as a concrete example of this mechanism, not as a general verdict on the aircraft or on concurrent development.

## Primary source

U.S. Government Accountability Office, *F-35 Joint Strike Fighter: Actions Needed to Address Manufacturing and Modernization Risks*, GAO-20-339, published May 12, 2020.

https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-20-339

The report states that F-35 production began in 2007 while development was still at an early stage. Because testing and production overlapped, an F-35 program official told GAO that as many as 550 aircraft delivered through 2020 could require retrofits for deficiencies and design issues found during testing. The program estimated these fixes—its "concurrency cost"—at $1.4 billion.

Use boundary: this is evidence that unresolved design uncertainty can become more expensive after it has been multiplied through production. It is not, by itself, proof that all concurrency was irrational. Earlier production can deliver earlier capability and learning, so the relevant comparison includes the benefits of speed as well as retrofit risk.

## Current-status check

The GAO page for GAO-20-339 was rechecked on 2026-09-09. Its recommendation status includes July 2026 updates on F-35 Block 4 cost-estimation work. Those updates do not revise the historical 550-aircraft / $1.4 billion concurrency figures used in Chapter 14.

A separate 2026 GAO sustainment review reports that DOD operates and sustains more than 800 U.S. F-35s and estimates U.S. lifetime sustainment costs at about $1.6 trillion as of 2024. Those figures are not needed for Chapter 14's argument and are deliberately omitted from the manuscript to avoid turning a narrow timing example into a broad program critique.

U.S. Government Accountability Office, *F-35 Sustainment: Actions Needed to Ensure Updated Strategy Improves Persistent Readiness Challenges*, GAO-26-108113, 2026.

https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-26-108113

## Counterargument and disconfirming evidence

The strongest counterargument is endogenous to the case: concurrency can be rational when the value of earlier operational capability, production learning, supplier learning, or schedule acceleration exceeds expected retrofit and technical-risk costs. The chapter therefore treats concurrency as a pricing mechanism for premature irreversibility, not a synonym for bad management.

Evidence that would weaken the use of this case would include a later authoritative accounting showing that the cited $1.4 billion estimate was materially wrong, that most of the anticipated retrofits were not required, or that the acceleration benefits clearly dominated the rework costs in the relevant decision frame. The chapter does not make claims requiring those stronger conclusions.

## Secondary chapter example

The Coca-Cola Company's historical account of New Coke remains useful as a qualitatively different timing case. The company reports that nearly 200,000 consumers participated in taste tests and that the new formulation performed well in those tests; the original formula returned 79 days after launch amid consumer protest.

https://www.coca-colacompany.com/about-us/history/new-coke-the-most-memorable-marketing-blunder-ever

Use boundary: this company history supports the factual chronology and the mismatch between taste-test evidence and attachment to the existing product. It should not be treated as an independent causal audit of management's decision.

## Editorial consequence

The revised chapter now distinguishes three questions that the previous draft blurred:

- Is the new direction broadly correct?
- Which uncertainties are still open?
- How much irreversibility should be attached to those uncertainties now?

This preserves the book's main argument about the cost of moving too late while making Chapter 14 a genuine counter-thesis rather than a rhetorical disclaimer.
