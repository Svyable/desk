# The Ground Is Glowing

*When geology starts looking like a UFO*

Sven Hardy Benson

## A note about the word *is*

There are lights people report around earthquakes. That sentence is safe.

There are lights *caused by earthquakes*. That sentence is not yet safe in the same way.

There are luminous atmospheric phenomena that can be mistaken for machines. Plausible, sometimes demonstrable, still case-specific.

Therefore UFOs are geological plasma. No. That is exactly the move this book refuses to make.

The subject is interesting because the categories overlap without becoming identical. A witness can see something real and classify it badly. An instrument can register something real and leave its cause unresolved. A proposed mechanism can be physically possible and still fail the energy budget for a particular event. A government study can be declassified and still be wrong.

This manuscript keeps four evidence classes visible:

1. **Documented:** official records, instrument records, reproducible experiments, peer-reviewed observations whose provenance can be inspected.
2. **Credible but incomplete:** observations with serious provenance but missing geometry, calibration, complete sensor data, replication, or causal closure.
3. **Disputed:** allegations and interpretations that lack independent confirmation, including claims that particular luminous anomalies are controlled craft or that particular UAP cases have a geophysical cause.
4. **Speculative synthesis:** the book's own attempt to ask whether geology, atmospheric electricity, plasma phenomenology, sensor ambiguity and UFO classification have been studied too separately.

The working rule is severe: an explanation does not inherit evidence merely because it is less exotic than aliens. A plasma story can become folklore too.
