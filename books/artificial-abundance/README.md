*When Intelligence Becomes Cheap, What Becomes Priceless?*

The working thesis is that artificial intelligence does more than increase the supply of useful cognitive work. It changes where value collects. When competent generation becomes cheap and plentiful, scarcity moves into choosing, verifying, trusting, attending, taking responsibility, experiencing reality, and deciding what is worth doing at all.

This is deliberately not another book about allocating intelligence, delegating work to agents, or proving that media came from a human. Those questions already have homes elsewhere in the Desk. *Artificial Abundance* is about the repricing event that follows abundance: why a capability can become economically transformative at the same moment that producing one more unit of its output becomes less special.
