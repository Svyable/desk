# Chapters 9–12 — depth-pass controls

This note turns the existing scarcity controls into a revision checklist for the four shortest chapters in the first half of the manuscript. The goal is not to restate the thesis or pad toward a word count. Each addition must earn its space with a mechanism, case, countercase, or consequence that the current chapter does not already contain.

## Chapter 9 — The End of Average

Current strength: the chapter already distinguishes useful standards from averages imposed by production cost, and it uses the BCG jagged-frontier experiment plus the 66-firm workplace experiment to keep personalization claims bounded.

Depth to add:

- Separate *personalization of presentation* from *personalization of entitlement*. A tax explanation can vary without the tax rule varying; a retention offer or price can vary in ways that change the economic deal. This gives the chapter a sharper fairness boundary than generic personalization language.
- Develop the audit problem created by individualized surfaces. A fixed form is crude but comparable. A generated surface may be easier for one person while making disparate treatment harder to observe across people.
- Give the coordination countercase more weight: organizations may rationally retain common forms, curricula, product tiers, and protocols because shared objects reduce comparison, training, compliance, and handoff costs even when individualized generation is nearly free.
- Keep the BCG result task-specific. Do not imply that below-threshold workers generally become above-average workers or that performance compression on experimental tasks establishes labor-market convergence.

Do not add another generic list of personalized products. The chapter needs institutional consequences, not more examples of cheap variation.

## Chapter 10 — Commodity Expertise

Current strength: the chapter correctly attaches commodification first to repeatable manifestations of expertise rather than to experts as people, and it already contains the apprenticeship problem.

Depth to add:

- Make substitutability observable from the buyer's side. A deliverable becomes commodity-like when buyers can compare acceptable outputs without caring much which qualified producer supplied the routine version. Price pressure follows from credible substitution, not merely from cheaper production.
- Distinguish three layers that used to travel together: routine production, exception recognition, and accountable authorization. AI can compress the first without proving it has captured the latter two.
- Deepen the apprenticeship countercase. Some junior work is disguised training; some is merely historical drudgery. The relevant question is whether removing the task removes exposure to the failure modes that later judgment depends on.
- Preserve demand uncertainty. Productivity gains can reduce labor required per unit while lower prices expand the number of units demanded. The chapter should not infer employment direction from task productivity alone.

Avoid professional self-congratulation. “Experts move uphill” is a hypothesis, not a law; some routine professional products may simply lose margin.

## Chapter 11 — Responsibility Does Not Scale

This is the thinnest chapter in the 9–12 band and should receive the largest share of the revision.

Depth to add:

- Separate *answer production* from *authority to act*. A model may generate a recommendation, but an institution still needs rules for who may approve a payment, sign a filing, prescribe a treatment, accept a safety risk, or make a representation to a regulator.
- Develop the queueing consequence. If generation expands faster than accountable review, the organization has not eliminated a bottleneck; it has created a larger inventory of things awaiting someone empowered to say yes.
- Use professional ethics as an example of this architecture, not as proof that responsibility is permanently human. The durable claim is that adopting a production tool does not by itself transfer duties attached to a professional role.
- Distinguish verification from responsibility. A system can verify many properties automatically while the institution still assigns responsibility for choosing the verification regime, accepting residual error, and handling exceptions.
- Preserve the possibility that law, insurance, contracts, and product-liability rules redistribute responsibility over time. The chapter is about the lag between scalable production and scalable accountability, not an eternal legal arrangement.

The chapter should end closer to the operational consequence: abundant drafts increase the value of explicit authority boundaries, escalation rules, and recourse.

## Chapter 12 — The Judgment Bottleneck

Current strength: judgment is already defined operationally rather than mystically, and the chapter acknowledges that verification and selection can themselves be automated.

Depth to add:

- Distinguish *prediction*, *selection*, and *commitment*. A system can rank options accurately without determining which objective deserves priority or which downside an institution is willing to own.
- Make the moving-boundary idea concrete. As one judgment task becomes reliable enough to automate, scarcity migrates to setting thresholds, defining exceptions, resolving conflicting objectives, or deciding when the automated rule should be suspended.
- Add the organizational layer from the 66-firm experiment: individual time savings do not imply that collective approval, prioritization, or coordination becomes equally fast.
- Keep hallucination and benchmark evidence in its proper role. It establishes calibration difficulty and heterogeneous reliability, not a universal error rate for ordinary use.
- Give the strongest countercase real force: in domains with clear objectives, cheap feedback, and machine-checkable outcomes, judgment can become less scarce too. The thesis survives only if it follows scarcity to the next unresolved boundary rather than declaring judgment permanently human.

## Cross-chapter continuity

The sequence should climb rather than repeat:

1. Chapter 9: cheap variation weakens averages imposed by production economics.
2. Chapter 10: cheap competent output makes some expert deliverables more substitutable.
3. Chapter 11: multiplying acceptable outputs does not multiply authorized accountability at the same rate.
4. Chapter 12: the resulting scarcity concentrates at the moving boundary of selection, exception handling, acceptable error, and commitment.

During revision, remove repeated formulations that merely say “generation is cheap but X is scarce.” Each chapter should demonstrate a different mechanism before returning to the book-level value-migration thesis.

## Stop conditions

Do not expand a chapter merely to cross a mechanical threshold. Stop adding prose when the new material begins repeating a neighboring chapter, when a factual example lacks a checkable source, or when the argument becomes more certain than the evidence. If a chapter remains below the repository's preferred depth after these mechanisms are fully developed, strengthen it with a real case or countercase rather than another conceptual restatement.
