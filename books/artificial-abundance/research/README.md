# Artificial Abundance — research brief

## Working thesis

*Artificial Abundance* is about the repricing event that follows cheap cognitive generation.

The book's central claim is not that intelligence literally becomes free, nor that all expertise becomes interchangeable. It is that sustained improvements in capability, price-performance, and distribution make a growing set of cognitive outputs cheap enough to stop being rationed in the old way. Once that happens, economic value migrates toward complements that remain scarce: selection, judgment, verification, responsibility, attention, trust, physical presence, difficult practice, and coherent human intention.

The phrase **artificial abundance** names the user-side condition created by expensive infrastructure producing very cheap marginal cognitive output. Upstream scarcity can therefore coexist with downstream abundance.

## Boundary with adjacent Desk books

This book must stay distinct from existing manuscripts:

- **Allocating Intelligence** asks how abundant machine intelligence should be routed, verified, authorized, and governed inside institutions. *Artificial Abundance* asks what becomes relatively valuable after generation itself is plentiful.
- **Agentile** treats delegated intention as a new economic force and follows agents through firms, labor, management, and markets. *Artificial Abundance* should not become a second agent-labor book.
- **Proof of Human** is specifically about authenticity, provenance, identity, and verification under synthetic media abundance. This book may discuss a human premium, but should not retell the provenance infrastructure argument.
- **The Search Frontier** focuses on search and selection when option generation becomes cheap. *Artificial Abundance* can use selection as an early mechanism but must range beyond search into consumption, culture, status, effort, physical experience, meaning, and institutions.
- **The Value Stack** examines where value creation and capture sit across layers of economic systems. *Artificial Abundance* should use value migration as a consequence of abundance, not become a general theory of bargaining power between layers.
- **Subsidized Intelligence** follows the economics of making intelligence artificially cheap through capital, compute, and subsidy. This book treats that falling user-side price as an input and spends most of its time on what follows.

The test for each chapter is: **Does this chapter explain a new scarcity, premium, or repricing created by abundance?** If not, it probably belongs in another book.

## Current structure

### 1. The Price of a Thought

Start with the observable fall in inference cost at a fixed capability threshold. Distinguish expensive fixed infrastructure from cheap marginal output, then show why falling marginal cost expands demand into tasks that previously had no human-service market at all.

Falsification note: if price-performance improvement stalls for a sustained period, usage remains heavily rationed by price, or capable systems remain too unreliable for meaningful incremental work, the abundance framing weakens and should be narrowed to specific domains.

### 2. The Abundance Machine

Explain the industrial backstage: chips, data centers, electricity, capital, and engineering. The chapter's job is to prevent "cheap intelligence" from becoming a claim that cognition has escaped physical scarcity. The key mechanism is high fixed cost plus low marginal cost, combined with cheap variation and personalization.

Falsification note: if infrastructure scarcity dominates end-user economics to the point that marginal cognitive work remains expensive, then the book should treat abundance as a temporary subsidy regime rather than a durable economic condition.

### 3. The Value Moves

Use earlier abundance transitions to establish the method: when one layer gets cheap, find the complementary bottleneck. Photography, recorded music, digital publishing, software, and professional services provide analogies, but the chapter must avoid claiming that value is conserved or that every displaced profit pool reappears elsewhere.

Falsification note: some markets may simply shrink. The thesis is migration of relative advantage, not conservation of revenue or employment.

### 4. The Taste Economy

Define taste as discrimination among plausible alternatives after formal requirements are satisfied. Show why cheap generation increases the importance of rejection, coherent direction, and contact with consequences. Preserve the training problem: if junior production work disappears, professions may lose one of the routes by which judgment was formed.

Falsification note: selection itself can be automated. The chapter should therefore locate human taste mainly at the choice of objective, frame, tradeoff, and responsibility—not assume humans remain superior at ranking every output.

## Planned research questions

- At what price and reliability thresholds do users stop rationing AI use for common tasks?
- How quickly are organizations moving from conversational assistance toward delegated, longer-running work?
- Which professions are seeing production become cheaper without a corresponding decline in responsibility or liability?
- What empirical evidence exists for human-made, live, local, handmade, or provenance premiums after synthetic alternatives become plentiful?
- Does abundant generation increase concentration at distribution, verification, identity, compute, or data layers?
- How do apprenticeship systems change when junior cognitive production is automated?
- Which kinds of friction create skill, meaning, or trustworthy signals rather than mere waste?
- How should institutions distinguish useful personalization from harmful inconsistency or manipulation?
- Does AI assistance preserve agency by expanding options, or reduce it by allowing recommendation systems to make more decisions by default?
- What measures can distinguish increased consumer surplus from increased provider revenue and investor return?

## Source trail for the opening pass

### Stanford AI Index

The 2025 AI Index provides the fixed-performance inference-cost comparison used in Chapter 1: a model reaching the GPT-3.5-equivalent MMLU threshold fell from roughly $20 per million tokens in November 2022 to $0.07 by October 2024. The 2026 report provides newer context on capability, adoption, data centers, compute capacity, consumer surplus, and the continued jaggedness of model performance.

- https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index/2025-ai-index-report/research-and-development
- https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index/2026-ai-index-report
- https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index/2026-ai-index-report/research-and-development
- https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index/2026-ai-index-report/technical-performance
- https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index/2026-ai-index-report/economy

### METR task horizons

METR's task-completion time-horizon work is useful for describing longer agentic work without pretending there is one clean boundary between "can" and "cannot." The page was last updated May 8, 2026 and reports success probabilistically against tasks measured by human-expert completion time.

- https://metr.org/time-horizons/

### Anthropic Economic Index

Use with explicit limitation: these datasets describe Claude usage, not the whole labor market. Relevant findings for the opening chapters include the movement of coding work toward more automated/agentic workflows, growing task diversity, measures of automation versus augmentation, and evidence that expertise still improves outcomes in agentic coding.

- https://www.anthropic.com/research/economic-index-march-2026-report
- https://www.anthropic.com/research/claude-code-expertise
- https://www.anthropic.com/research/economic-index-june-2026-report

### Productivity evidence

Brynjolfsson, Li, and Raymond's customer-support study remains a useful early causal estimate of generative AI assistance at work. The reported average productivity gain was about 14%, with larger gains for novice and lower-skilled workers. Treat this as evidence from one setting, not a universal productivity multiplier.

- https://www.nber.org/papers/w31161

The broader literature also includes Noy and Zhang's writing-task experiment and the BCG knowledge-work field experiment. These can support later chapters on changing production costs and expertise, subject to their domain limits.

## Evidence discipline

- Do not use model-company benchmark claims as evidence of labor-market replacement without an external bridge.
- Do not convert price declines into a forecast that inference becomes literally free.
- Do not use consumer-surplus estimates as provider revenue or investor return.
- Treat model usage datasets as platform-specific samples.
- Distinguish cheap generation from cheap verification, cheap responsibility, and cheap physical execution.
- Do not use historical abundance analogies as proof that AI will reproduce the same labor or industry outcome.
- Preserve counterexamples where standardization, human review, or deliberate friction remains valuable even when customization becomes cheap.
- Avoid invented quotations, fictional scenes, and precise anecdotes that do not have a source trail.
