# Chapter 7 evidence notes — Prior Authorization

## Core claim

Prior authorization is a consequential gate because it joins medical judgment, contract coverage, utilization management, administrative process, and time. The strongest critique is not that the gate can never say no, but that its criteria, timing, reasons, and appeal path have to remain attached to the purpose that justifies the gate.

## Sources

### HHS Office of Inspector General — Medicare Advantage denials

- https://www.oig.hhs.gov/reports/all/2022/some-medicare-advantage-organization-denials-of-prior-authorization-requests-raise-concerns-about-beneficiary-access-to-medically-necessary-care/
- OIG reviewed denied prior authorization and payment requests from Medicare Advantage organizations.
- It estimated that 13 percent of denied prior authorization requests in its sample met Medicare coverage rules.
- This is not 13 percent of all requests; the denominator is denied requests.
- OIG identified additional clinical criteria and insufficient-documentation determinations among the reasons some requests meeting Medicare rules were denied.

### CMS — Interoperability and Prior Authorization Final Rule CMS-0057-F

- https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/cms-interoperability-prior-authorization-final-rule-cms-0057-f
- Requires affected payers to provide specific reasons for prior authorization denials.
- Establishes decision timeframes for covered items and services under the rule: generally 72 hours for expedited requests and seven calendar days for standard requests.
- Requires public reporting of selected prior authorization metrics.
- Requires a prior authorization API using standardized health-data technology on the rule's implementation schedule.

### CMS — 2024 Medicare Advantage and Part D Final Rule CMS-4201-F

- https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/2024-medicare-advantage-and-part-d-final-rule-cms-4201-f
- Addresses the use of prior authorization and utilization-management criteria in Medicare Advantage.
- Includes continuity protections for enrollees in active courses of treatment, including a minimum 90-day transition period in specified circumstances.
- Requires utilization-management committees and establishes requirements around coverage criteria and medical necessity.

### CMS — Contract Year 2025 Medicare Advantage policy updates

- https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/contract-year-2025-policy-and-technical-changes-medicare-advantage-program-medicare-prescription-drug
- Adds health-equity analysis requirements to utilization-management committee review.
- Useful for the chapter's claim that a consequential gate needs institutional maintenance, not merely operation.

## Boundaries and counterevidence

- The chapter does not claim that all prior authorization is harmful or unnecessary. Utilization management can reduce inappropriate, duplicative, or non-covered care and can protect patients from unnecessary interventions.
- A denial is not automatically evidence of misconduct. Insurance contracts legitimately define coverage conditions.
- A later reversal does not prove the initial decision was irrational in every case; additional information may legitimately change the result.
- Faster decisions are not always better if speed substitutes for needed clinical review. The chapter distinguishes transaction friction from substantive decision friction.
- Electronic prior authorization can remove avoidable administrative burden without removing necessary clinical judgment.
- The OIG's 13 percent estimate must remain tied to denied requests and to the study's Medicare Advantage context.
- Medical error is asymmetric but not one-directional: inappropriate approvals can also expose patients to harm and consume scarce capacity.

## Design implications under test

1. Criteria should be knowable and attached to the purpose of the gate.
2. Consequential denials should have specific reasons.
3. The clock matters; delay is part of the decision.
4. Appeals should remain useful at the time they resolve, not merely exist on paper.
5. Systems should preserve prior evidence and approvals where continuity is clinically appropriate.
6. Automation should remove transaction friction without laundering opaque criteria into faster decisions.
7. A gate needs an owner responsible for periodically reevaluating whether it still deserves to exist in its current form.
