# Chapter 5 evidence notes — The Credential

## Core claims

The chapter treats a credential as a portable third-party judgment. It distinguishes nongovernmental certification from government licensing, then asks when the information and accountability benefits of credentials justify making them prerequisites for work.

The intended balance is important: licensing can protect consumers where competence is hard to observe and errors are serious, while unnecessary or nonportable licensing can restrict entry, mobility, output, and choice.

## Sources

### U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics — certification and licensing status, 2025

- https://www.bls.gov/cps/cpsaat53.htm
- BLS defines a certification as a credential issued by a nongovernmental certification body conveying knowledge or skill for a job.
- BLS defines a license as a credential awarded by a government agency conveying legal authority to work in an occupation.
- For 2025 annual averages, 24.0% of employed people age 16+ had a certification or license; 21.6% had a license and 2.5% had a certification but no license.
- BLS cautions that 2025 annual estimates are 11-month averages excluding October because data were not collected during the federal government shutdown, so they are not strictly comparable with annual averages from other years.

### Michael Spence — Job Market Signaling

- https://doi.org/10.2307/1882010
- Michael Spence, *The Quarterly Journal of Economics*, Vol. 87, No. 3, August 1973, pp. 355–374.
- The chapter uses the paper narrowly: employers make hiring decisions under uncertainty and observable attributes can function as signals. It does not claim that education is merely signaling or that education lacks productive value.

### Federal Trade Commission — occupational licensing research roundtable

- https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/events/2017/11/effects-occupational-licensure-competition-consumers-workforce-empirical-research-results
- FTC frames the policy tradeoff explicitly: licensing may provide consumer or public-safety benefits, but entry restrictions can impose costs on workers and consumers and may affect mobility, prices, output, access, quality, and choice.
- FTC specifically identifies state-to-state portability as a potential burden, including for military spouses and other frequently moving workers.

## Falsification / counterevidence to preserve

- Credentials can carry real knowledge, not merely signal it.
- Inputs such as supervised practice hours may be essential in occupations where a one-time skills test cannot capture judgment.
- Certification without legal exclusion may be insufficient when failures are hard to detect until after irreversible harm.
- Lowering barriers can reduce quality if the removed barrier was genuinely predictive of safe performance.
- Wage differences between licensed and unlicensed workers do not by themselves show that licensing caused higher wages; occupation, education, experience, selection, and other factors matter.
- A credential can remain useful even if imperfect because downstream gatekeepers need a tractable alternative to personally re-evaluating every applicant.
