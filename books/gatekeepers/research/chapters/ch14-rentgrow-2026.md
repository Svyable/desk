# Chapter 14 — RentGrow and the data layer of an algorithmic gate

## Claim under test

Chapter 14 uses tenant screening to argue that an automated gate is not only a model. It is a chain of data, matching, scoring, policy, interface, and institutional choice. The chapter's 2026 RentGrow example is a useful test because the alleged failure sits primarily in the consumer-reporting data layer rather than in a claim that a particular machine-learning architecture was intrinsically defective.

## Fresh primary-source check

On July 9, 2026, the Federal Trade Commission published the federal case *United States of America v. RentGrow, Inc.*, Civil Action No. 1:26-cv-02415 in the District of Columbia. The FTC's case page says RentGrow, a tenant-screening consumer reporting agency, will be required to pay $2.25 million to settle allegations that it violated the Fair Credit Reporting Act and FTC Act, including by failing to use reasonable procedures to assure the accuracy of its reports.

Primary source: Federal Trade Commission, *RentGrow, Inc., U.S. v.*, updated July 9, 2026.
https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/222-3002-rentgrow-inc-us-v

The procedural posture matters. As of the FTC page's July 9 update, the agency labels the case **Pending** and links a complaint, consent motion, and proposed stipulated order. The manuscript should therefore continue to describe the underlying conduct as **allegations** and the payment as a settlement requirement, not as an adjudicated finding that RentGrow committed every alleged violation.

## Counterevidence and boundary conditions

This enforcement action does not establish that tenant-screening algorithms are generally inaccurate, discriminatory, or inferior to human screening. It also does not isolate an AI model as the source of the alleged errors. That limitation strengthens the chapter's actual argument: a technically competent predictor can still participate in a bad gate if identity matching, source records, dispute handling, thresholds, or deployment policy fail.

HUD's April 29, 2024 tenant-screening guidance supplies the other side of the tradeoff. It explicitly acknowledges that housing providers have a legitimate interest in selecting tenants likely to pay rent and comply with lawful lease requirements, while warning that imprecise, overbroad, inaccurate, or opaque screening can exclude applicants for reasons that do not serve those goals. The guidance is useful as evidence of the policy problem and the agency's then-stated interpretation; it is guidance, not a substitute for statutory text, regulations, or binding precedent.

Primary source: U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development, *Guidance on Application of the Fair Housing Act to the Screening of Applicants for Rental Housing*, April 29, 2024.
https://archives.hud.gov/news/2024/FHEO_Guidance_on_Screening_of_Applicants_for_Rental_Housing.pdf

A September 17, 2025 HUD notice withdrew several FHEO guidance documents and emphasized that guidance does not itself create legally enforceable duties beyond statutes, regulations, or binding precedent. Its withdrawal table includes the separate April 29, 2024 guidance on **advertising** through digital platforms; it does not list the same-day tenant-screening guidance. This distinction is worth preserving if Chapter 14 is later revised so that a historical agency recommendation is not accidentally presented as binding law.

Primary source: HUD Office of Fair Housing and Equal Opportunity, *Notice of the Withdrawal of FHEO Guidance Documents*, September 17, 2025.
https://www.hud.gov/sites/dfiles/Main/documents/Notice-of-Withdrawal-of-Guidance-Documents.pdf

## Editorial implication

The current RentGrow paragraph is appropriately cautious because it says the settlement resolves **allegations** and explicitly says the case does not prove tenant screening is generally defective. A future prose pass should preserve that calibration. The most useful addition would be procedural specificity—proposed settlement/pending case—only if it can be added without interrupting the chapter's narrative momentum.
