# Chapter 15 evidence notes — The Gatekeeper's Conflict

## Chapter claim

Gatekeeping becomes structurally more difficult to trust when the actor deciding admission, ranking, certification, or compliance can also profit from one outcome. A conflict is an incentive, not proof of misconduct. Good design therefore asks which powers and information flows require independence, symmetry, logging, appeal, or separation.

## Primary sources used

### U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission — auditor independence

- SEC, *Revision of the Commission's Auditor Independence Requirements* / Rule 2-01 framework.
- SEC, *The Critical Importance of the General Standard of Auditor Independence and an Ethical Culture for the Accounting Profession* (2022).
- SEC, *Policy Statement: The Establishment and Improvement of Standards Related to Auditor Independence*.

Claim boundary: the chapter uses auditor independence as an analogy and a mature example of structural conflict governance. It does not claim platform governance and financial auditing are legally equivalent. The SEC framework asks whether relationships create mutual/conflicting interests, cause an auditor to audit its own work, place it in management, or make it an advocate for the client.

Sources:
- https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/2000/11/revision-commissions-auditor-independence-requirements
- https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/munter-20220608

### U.S. Department of Justice — multi-sided platform conflicts

- DOJ Antitrust Division, 2023 Merger Guidelines, Guideline 9 discussion of platform operators that also participate on their platforms.

Claim boundary: the chapter uses the DOJ's stated concern that a platform operator/participant can have incentives to advantage its own products. It does not infer that vertical integration is always anticompetitive; the chapter explicitly preserves efficiency and integration benefits.

Source:
- https://www.justice.gov/atr/merger-guidelines/applying-merger-guidelines/guideline-9

### European Commission — Digital Markets Act

- European Commission DMA legislation and gatekeeper portal.
- European Commission, 23 July 2026, decisions finding Google non-compliant with DMA obligations concerning self-preferencing in Search and steering restrictions in Google Play.

Claim boundary: the July 2026 findings are described as Commission decisions, not generalized into a claim that every first-party integration or ranking preference is unlawful. The chapter uses them as a current concrete example of regulators treating rule-making plus participation as a structural concern.

## Counterevidence and constraints preserved

- Vertical integration can improve user experience, security, coordination, and economics.
- A platform's own product can win because it is better rather than because the platform manipulated the gate.
- Symmetric rules are not always technically possible because first-party and third-party actors can carry different responsibilities.
- Structural separation can destroy useful coordination and create new seams, costs, and security risks.
- Regulation and independent review create gates of their own and can become slow, captured, overbroad, or incumbent-protecting.
- A conflict should trigger scrutiny and controls; it is not by itself proof of misconduct.

## Design principles developed

1. **Integrity is not independence.** Honest people can operate inside badly aligned structures.
2. **Inside the rules versus through the rules.** A gatekeeper that also competes may have ordinary competitive levers plus rule-making levers.
3. **Symmetry with reasons for asymmetry.** Comparable actors should face comparable rules unless a difference can be explained and inspected.
4. **The expensive no.** Independence is most credible when the gatekeeping function can make a decision against a powerful internal interest.
5. **Exceptions leave fossils.** Overrides should identify the rule, requester, reason, approver, duration, and result.
6. **Purpose-bound information.** Data collected to operate a gate should not silently become privileged competitive intelligence.
7. **Proportional remedies.** Disclosure, firewalls, non-discrimination, interoperability, appeal, incentive design, and structural separation address different mechanisms.
8. **Exit disciplines conflict.** The inability to leave turns an interested intermediary into practical governance.

## Forward link

Chapter 16 should treat appeal as an operating system for correcting gate error, while measuring the price of challenge in time, expertise, money, documentation, and endurance. A formally available appeal is not meaningful if the cost of using it exceeds the value of the right being contested.
