# A Note on the Record

Diplomatic lives leave large paper trails and large blank spaces.

This book uses both.

A transcript can establish what Nicholas Burns said at a podium. It cannot tell us what he thought after leaving it. A photograph can place a Red Sox cap on his head at a particular time and place. It cannot explain why he wore it. An oral history can preserve a vivid memory decades later. It does not become a contemporaneous record because the memory is vivid.

Where primary records exist, they carry the chronology and the institutional action. Retrospective recollections remain recollections. Official accounts are sources, not automatic truth. Contested claims remain attributed. When accounts disagree, the disagreement stays visible.

The missing scenes stay missing.

Burns was assigned to Jerusalem during the 1986 World Series. The record reviewed for this book does not tell us where he watched, whether he watched live, or what he felt when Boston lost. The Red Sox won the World Series on October 27, 2004, while he was serving as under secretary of state. The perfect scene—where he was, who was with him, what he said—is absent. This book does not manufacture it.

Baseball follows the same rule. It can supply chronology, atmosphere, public identity and a second American clock. It does not become a secret training system for diplomacy. A lifetime of Red Sox fandom does not prove that Burns learned patience from losing, resilience from 2004 or alliance management from a clubhouse. If the record supports a connection, the book uses it. If it does not, the baseball remains baseball.

Burns is also the central subject, not the sole cause of the events around him. Diplomatic outcomes belong to governments, institutions, colleagues, allies, adversaries, staff, legislators, local partners and counterparts as well as to the senior official whose name is easiest to remember.

The chapter-keyed [Source Notes](source-notes.md) link readers to the main documentary spine. The underlying [research ledger](../research/source-ledger.csv) is broader and messier by design.

The standard is not omniscience. It is knowing the difference between what the record establishes, what a witness remembers, what remains disputed and what nobody has yet found.
