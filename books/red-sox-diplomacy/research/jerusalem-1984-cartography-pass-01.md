# Jerusalem 1984 cartography — media/research pass 01

## Purpose

Publication-safe visual research for the Jerusalem / 1986 material in *Red Sox Diplomacy*. This pass prioritizes a period-near map that clarifies the political geography Burns entered without pretending a map can explain his private experience or the politics of the city by itself.

## Recommended figure — JERUSALEM 01

**Working title:** *Jerusalem and the lines already on the map, 1984*

**Source object:** United States Central Intelligence Agency, *Jerusalem and vicinity*, Washington, D.C., 1984. Library of Congress Geography and Map Division. LCCN 84695860; call number G7504.J4F3 1984 .U5; CIA map number 800128 4-84.

**Canonical record:** https://www.loc.gov/item/84695860/

**What the source establishes:** The Library of Congress catalog describes the map as showing then-current and former city boundaries, armistice lines, and Israeli-developed settlements beyond the armistice line, with relief indicated by spot heights. It was produced in April 1984, immediately before Burns's Jerusalem posting rather than decades before or after it.

**Rights / commercial-publication assessment:** APPROVED AS SOURCE MATERIAL FOR REPRODUCTION OR ORIGINAL REDRAW, subject to a final item-page check at production. The Library of Congress states that Geography and Map Division digitized collections are free to use and reuse unless an item carries a contrary Rights Advisory, and requests the credit line “Library of Congress, Geography and Map Division.” The cataloged creator is the U.S. Central Intelligence Agency. Current CIA map guidance independently states that its base maps are public domain and may be copied freely; when modifying current CIA maps, CIA asks users to remove the map number. For this historical LOC object, retain the catalog/map number in provenance notes even if it is omitted from a clean redraw.

### Preferred treatment

Create an **original grayscale redraw** rather than dropping the full archival sheet into the chapter. Preserve only geography that earns its space in the narrative:

- the 1949 armistice line / Green Line as represented by the 1984 source;
- the city boundary distinctions actually needed to understand the period map;
- a restrained selection of settlement labels present on the source where relevant to the chapter's political geography;
- major orientation geography sufficient to keep east/west and the Old City legible.

Do **not** turn the figure into a retrospective map of later Oslo-era or contemporary boundaries. Do not silently update names, settlement extents, municipal limits, roads, or political status. If modern labels are necessary for reader orientation, mark them explicitly as editorial additions and keep them visually subordinate.

### Draft caption

**Jerusalem and vicinity, 1984.** A CIA map made shortly before Nicholas Burns's Jerusalem posting recorded city boundaries, the armistice line and Israeli-developed settlements beyond it. The map is useful here as a period document: it shows the political geography American diplomats were reading at the time, not a final statement on sovereignty or later boundaries. *Source: U.S. Central Intelligence Agency, 1984; Library of Congress, Geography and Map Division.*

### Draft alt text

Grayscale map of Jerusalem and its vicinity based on a 1984 CIA map, showing period city boundaries, the armistice line and selected Israeli-developed settlements beyond the armistice line. Labels and line styles reflect the 1984 source rather than present-day political geography.

### Placement

Best placed early in the Jerusalem chapter, after the manuscript establishes Burns's posting and before the narrative asks the reader to hold multiple political geographies in mind. It should orient the chapter, not interrupt the 1986 World Series material.

## Evidence guardrails

1. The 1984 CIA map is a **U.S. government cartographic representation**, not neutral adjudication of Jerusalem's status.
2. Its labels, lines and categories establish what that source depicted in 1984; they do not establish sovereignty as narrator fact.
3. The map predates Burns's posting closely enough to be useful period context, but it does not prove Burns personally used or saw this sheet.
4. Do not infer Burns's views, routes, residence, office location or daily movements from this map.
5. Keep the World Series chronology separate: cartographic proximity does not license invention about where or how Burns watched baseball in 1986.

## Why this earns a figure slot

The chapter's problem is not a shortage of picturesque Jerusalem imagery. It is that the reader needs to understand that Burns arrived in a city whose geography was already argument: municipal boundaries, an armistice line, settlements, old and new jurisdictions, and incompatible political readings occupying the same small space. A period U.S. government map makes that visible without requiring the prose to become a geography lecture.

The figure also does something a generic regional map cannot: it shows how compressed the distances are. That compression matters to diplomacy. Large political claims sit on top of short physical distances.

## Highest-value follow-up

Search authoritative State Department, Reagan Library and NARA holdings for **1985–1987 U.S. Consulate General Jerusalem / embassy-related photography or records with item-level federal provenance**, especially material that can establish the physical workplace, official visits, or consular setting during Burns's posting. Prefer an image that documents the actual institutional environment over another skyline.

A second useful target is a rights-clean 1985–1987 street/city plan precise enough to locate the U.S. Consulate General facilities as they existed then. Do not use a modern Google-style map as a substitute; buildings, roads, jurisdictional labels and diplomatic facilities have changed.
