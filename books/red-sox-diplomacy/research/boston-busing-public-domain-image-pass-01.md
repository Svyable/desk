# Boston busing public-domain image pass 01

## Scope

Chapter 1 currently places Burns's Boston College years beside court-ordered school desegregation and violence in Boston without pretending that those events were his personal experience. This pass looked for one publication-safe visual object that could make that civic backdrop concrete without turning racial conflict into decorative period atmosphere.

## Cleared candidate: March 19, 1975 anti-busing demonstration

**Object:** *Women from Boston and Charleston, West Virginia, holding signs, demonstrating against busing and textbooks, Washington, D.C.*

- **Date:** March 19, 1975
- **Repository:** Library of Congress, Prints and Photographs Division
- **LCCN:** 2011646489
- **Call number:** LC-U9-30926-13 [P&P]
- **Digital ID:** ds 00764
- **Reproduction number:** LC-DIG-ds-00764, digital file from original negative
- **Canonical record:** https://www.loc.gov/item/2011646489/
- **Rights advisory:** “No known restrictions on publication.”
- **Creator:** The catalog record does not identify a photographer. Do not invent one.
- **Credit line:** Library of Congress, Prints and Photographs Division, LC-DIG-ds-00764.
- **Commercial-publication posture:** Suitable candidate for commercial book use on the evidence currently supplied by the Library of Congress item record. Preserve the catalog rights advisory with production records. As always with LOC material, the publisher remains responsible for assessing any third-party privacy/publicity issue; this image depicts a public political demonstration and the catalog does not identify individual demonstrators.

### What the photograph actually establishes

The LOC catalog identifies the demonstrators as women from Boston and Charleston, West Virginia, protesting in Washington on March 19, 1975. Its subjects explicitly include Boston, school-integration busing, censorship, demonstrations, activists, and protest movements. Related contact-sheet images show the U.S. Capitol in the background.

This is useful for Chapter 1 because 1975 is one of the exact years already in the chapter's Massachusetts/Boston College clock. It gives the reader a documented view of Boston's desegregation conflict traveling to Washington while Burns was an undergraduate in Massachusetts. It does **not** establish that Burns saw this demonstration, followed it, held any view about busing, or connected it to his later diplomacy. Do not write any of those claims without separate evidence.

### Recommended manuscript use

Use only if Chapter 1 needs a second historical-context image. Place it near the paragraph that distinguishes Burns's baseball calendar from the simultaneous national and Boston political calendar, rather than near the Little League or family-fandom material.

Suggested caption:

> **FIG. — Boston's conflict over school desegregation did not stay in Boston.** Women identified by the Library of Congress as demonstrators from Boston and Charleston, West Virginia, protest busing and textbooks in Washington, March 19, 1975. Burns was then a Boston College undergraduate; the photograph documents the political environment around those years, not his participation in it. Library of Congress, Prints and Photographs Division, LC-DIG-ds-00764. No known restrictions on publication.

Suggested alt text:

> Women at a March 1975 Washington demonstration hold protest signs; the Library of Congress identifies participants as coming from Boston and Charleston, West Virginia, and catalogs the image under school-integration busing and protest movements.

### Production treatment

- Prefer the LOC digital file made from the original negative; do not screenshot the catalog page.
- Keep caption and credit with the image in print/PDF; avoid a page break between image and caption.
- In EPUB, image → caption → credit → source link is the reading order.
- Do not crop so tightly that the demonstration context disappears or a single unidentified person becomes an implied protagonist.
- A grayscale reproduction is acceptable if faces and sign lettering remain legible; test at final trim size.
- Do not reproduce the signs' wording in running prose unless it advances the argument. Their presence is enough; the object is evidence of mobilized conflict, not a gallery of inflammatory slogans.

## Rejected / held material

### Stanley Forman, *The Soiling of Old Glory* (1976)

Historically powerful and directly relevant to Boston's racial violence, but not cleared here. The Library of Congress record names Stanley Forman as photographer; physical custody by LOC is not a publication license. Do not ingest or reproduce without a rights path specific to the photograph.

### LOOK magazine: Operation Exodus (1968) and Louise Day Hicks (1965)

Both collections contain unusually relevant Boston school-desegregation imagery, but the LOC records warn that publication may be restricted under the LOOK Magazine Photograph Collection rights regime. Research value: high. Current commercial-book clearance: insufficient. Do not ingest.

### Herblock busing cartoon (1974)

Useful contemporary interpretation, but the LOC exhibition identifies the work as © The Herb Block Foundation. It is not a rights-clean substitute for documentary photography. Do not reproduce without permission.

## Reader-facing judgment

The 1975 demonstration photograph is stronger than a generic Fenway, skyline, Beacon Hill, or Boston street image because it clarifies the chapter's explicit warning that the baseball calendar shared space with harder civic history. It should remain contextual rather than biographical. If the chapter already feels visually crowded after layout, retain this as a provenance-cleared reserve asset rather than forcing it into the manuscript.

Find a rights-clean, item-level 1974–1976 federal or otherwise unrestricted photograph made **in Boston itself** during the court-ordered desegregation crisis, preferably one that documents school transport, public institutions, or the geography of the conflict without relying on a famous copyrighted press image.
