# Greece Baseball Network Timeline

This timeline isolates the documentary chain behind the Greek Olympic baseball effort. It separates contemporaneous reporting from later organizational retrospective so the manuscript can use the strongest evidence first.

## 1997 — federation formation and Burns arrives in Greece

The Hellenic Amateur Baseball Federation was formed during the run-up to the 2004 Athens Olympics. Nicholas Burns was sworn in as U.S. ambassador to Greece in November 1997.

**Confidence:** high for Burns appointment; organizational history for federation chronology.

**Sources:**
- https://1997-2001.state.gov/about_state/biography/burns.html
- https://greekbaseball.com/history

## 1998 — Burns frames U.S.-Greek relations around personal relationships

At Harvard in November 1998, Burns described strengthening personal relationships between leaders as a leading element of his agenda for U.S.-Greek relations.

A later ESPN profile says Greek officials approached Burns for baseball help in 1998. The exact approach date should be reconciled against the federation's later chronology, which places a key Mitsiopoulos request in 2001, and an Athens News account, which places federation contact in 1999. These sources may be describing different phases of the same project rather than a single first contact.

**Why it matters:** the baseball project fits a broader relationship-centered diplomatic method rather than appearing from nowhere.

**Sources:**
- https://www.thecrimson.com/article/1998/11/3/ambassador-to-greece-urges-improved-relations/
- https://www.espn.co.uk/espn/magazine/archives/news/story?page=magazine-20040119-article32
- https://www.helleniccomserve.com/baseball.html
- https://greekbaseball.com/history

## 1999 — public effort around Greek Olympic baseball becomes visible

A Washington Post sports item reports that Peter Angelos met Greek officials at a reception held by Burns and the Hellenic Amateur Baseball Federation. It says the commissioner's office and the Boston Red Sox were helping the effort.

An Athens News account later says the federation contacted Burns in 1999. Burns then circulated the problem through American baseball circles because Greece lacked the player and institutional base required to field an Olympic-caliber team.

**Why it matters:** contemporaneous evidence places Burns, the Greek federation, Angelos, MLB, and the Red Sox in the same institutional network before the Olympics. The Athens News account adds a description of Burns acting as connector rather than simply ceremonial host.

**Confidence:** high for the reception and Red Sox/commissioner's-office involvement; medium-high for the later Athens News reconstruction of the initial network-building sequence.

**Sources:**
- https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/sports/1999/09/08/injuries-to-teens-are-still-a-concern/fe2eb9f0-7fc2-4b43-80a4-a9cb6ac94670/
- https://www.helleniccomserve.com/baseball.html

## 2000 — Burns described as a “baseball ambassador”

An Associated Press report from Athens describes Greece's lack of baseball infrastructure, Peter Angelos's role, and Burns coordinating efforts to build a viable program. Burns discusses Greeks' unfamiliarity with the sport and the difficulty of building the effort from scratch.

**Why it matters:** this is strong contemporaneous evidence for Burns personally taking part in the baseball project.

**Confidence:** high.

**Source:** https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-2000-jan-02-mn-49873-story.html

## Burns's own account of the hands-on work

An ESPN Magazine profile published during the run-up to the Athens Olympics adds unusually specific first-person detail. Burns says the effort brought American coaches and players to Greece for demonstrations. He recalls personally:

- going on Greek television from a batting cage to demonstrate how to hit;
- playing catch with Greek children;
- lobbying the Greek government to create a baseball budget.

Burns says that within roughly two years there were 20 youth baseball teams and 10 softball teams playing in two leagues.

This materially changes the manuscript's evidentiary picture. Burns was not only a connector between officials. His own account depicts him as a public participant in teaching and promoting the game.

**Confidence:** high that Burns made these claims to ESPN; medium-high for the exact youth-team counts until independent Greek records corroborate them.

**Source:**
https://www.espn.co.uk/espn/magazine/archives/news/story?page=magazine-20040119-article32

## 2001 — federation retrospective identifies the Burns–Sarbanes–Angelos chain

The Hellenic federation says president Panos Mitsiopoulos approached Burns for help. Burns contacted Senator Paul Sarbanes, who in turn contacted Peter Angelos. The federation credits Angelos with major financial and organizational support.

**Why it matters:** gives the project a traceable network structure.

**Caution:** organizational retrospective; corroborate private contacts with papers or contemporaneous sources where possible. The date should be reconciled with sources placing federation contact with Burns in 1998 or 1999.

**Source:** https://greekbaseball.com/history

## 2002–2003 — player recruitment and professional infrastructure expand

The federation describes an organized search for players of Greek descent and later meetings involving the federation, Major League Baseball, and the Baltimore Orioles.

A 2003 Athens News account focuses on Peter Angelos's financing and the diaspora-player strategy. By this phase, the project had become a professionalized Olympic-team operation rather than a small local development effort.

**Why it matters:** shows that the project developed into a sustained baseball operation beyond Burns's initiating and public-diplomacy role.

**Caution:** do not imply Burns managed later roster construction unless further evidence shows it.

**Sources:**
- https://greekbaseball.com/history
- https://www.helleniccomserve.com/baseball.html

## 2003 — baseball and Burns's NATO office intersect

The ESPN profile also describes Burns's NATO office in Brussels as containing a small Red Sox collection: an autographed Tony Conigliaro photograph, a Ted Williams-signed baseball, and a Nomar Garciaparra-signed bat.

The same article reports that Burns watched Game 7 of the 2003 Red Sox–Yankees ALCS from Brussels on Armed Forces television and remembered the exact local time, 6:16 a.m., when Aaron Boone hit the walk-off home run.

**Why it matters:** the Greek-baseball story is being told by a diplomat whose fandom was active and concrete during the same period, not reconstructed from later institutional biographies.

**Source:**
https://www.espn.co.uk/espn/magazine/archives/news/story?page=magazine-20040119-article32

## 2004 — Olympics and retrospective judgment

Contemporary reporting on the Olympic team revisited Burns's role in helping establish the effort. Burns described himself as a confirmed, unconditional, rabid Red Sox fan and called the baseball initiative a joint American-Greek venture and one of the best things he had done as ambassador.

The ESPN profile supplies a more granular bridge from diplomatic network-building to actual sports development, while the Christian Science Monitor supplies Burns's retrospective valuation once the Olympic team existed.

**Why it matters:** together the sources support a complete chain: personal baseball competence → public demonstrations and lobbying → institutional introductions → diaspora/professional support → Olympic participation → Burns's retrospective judgment.

**Sources:**
- https://www.espn.co.uk/espn/magazine/archives/news/story?page=magazine-20040119-article32
- https://www.csmonitor.com/2004/0818/p02s01-woeu.html
- https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/sports/2004/04/12/it-appears-that-baseball-isnt-all-greek-to-the-greeks/0d5fc3ca-536e-4df4-8712-c260367f6e51/

## Documentary claim we can safely make

Burns did not create Greek baseball by himself. The strongest evidence supports a more interesting claim: as ambassador, he used diplomatic access, his own baseball knowledge, public visibility, diaspora relationships, and connections to American professional baseball to help a Greek sports initiative acquire expertise, money, legitimacy, players, public attention, and institutional reach.

He also appears, in his own account, to have done some of the unglamorous public-diplomacy work personally: demonstrations, television, playing catch with children, and lobbying for a budget.
