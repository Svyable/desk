# Greece Baseball Network Timeline

This timeline isolates the documentary chain behind the Greek Olympic baseball effort. It separates contemporaneous reporting from later organizational retrospective so the manuscript can use the strongest evidence first.

## 1997 — federation formation and Burns arrives in Greece

The Hellenic Amateur Baseball Federation was formed during the run-up to the 2004 Athens Olympics. Nicholas Burns was sworn in as U.S. ambassador to Greece in November 1997.

**Confidence:** high for Burns appointment; organizational history for federation chronology.

**Sources:**
- https://1997-2001.state.gov/about_state/biography/burns.html
- https://greekbaseball.com/history

## 1998 — Burns frames U.S.-Greek relations around personal relationships

At Harvard in November 1998, Burns described strengthening personal relationships between leaders as a leading element of his agenda for U.S.-Greek relations.

**Why it matters:** the baseball project later fits a broader relationship-centered diplomatic method rather than appearing from nowhere.

**Source:** https://www.thecrimson.com/article/1998/11/3/ambassador-to-greece-urges-improved-relations/

## 1999 — public effort around Greek Olympic baseball becomes visible

A Washington Post sports item reports that Peter Angelos met Greek officials at a reception held by Burns and the Hellenic Amateur Baseball Federation. It says the commissioner's office and the Boston Red Sox were helping the effort.

**Why it matters:** contemporaneous evidence places Burns, the Greek federation, Angelos, MLB, and the Red Sox in the same institutional network before the Olympics.

**Confidence:** high.

**Source:** https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/sports/1999/09/08/injuries-to-teens-are-still-a-concern/fe2eb9f0-7fc2-4b43-80a4-a9cb6ac94670/

## 2000 — Burns described as a "baseball ambassador"

An Associated Press report from Athens describes Greece's lack of baseball infrastructure, Peter Angelos's role, and Burns coordinating efforts to build a viable program. Burns discusses Greeks' unfamiliarity with the sport and the difficulty of building the effort from scratch.

**Why it matters:** this is the strongest contemporaneous narrative source for Burns personally taking part in the baseball project.

**Confidence:** high.

**Source:** https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-2000-jan-02-mn-49873-story.html

## 2001 — federation retrospective identifies the Burns–Sarbanes–Angelos chain

The Hellenic federation says president Panos Mitsiopoulos approached Burns for help. Burns contacted Senator Paul Sarbanes, who in turn contacted Peter Angelos. The federation credits Angelos with major financial and organizational support.

**Why it matters:** gives the project a traceable network structure.

**Caution:** organizational retrospective; corroborate private contacts with papers or contemporaneous sources where possible.

**Source:** https://greekbaseball.com/history

## 2002–2003 — player recruitment and professional infrastructure expand

The federation describes an organized search for players of Greek descent and later meetings involving the federation, Major League Baseball, and the Baltimore Orioles.

**Why it matters:** shows that the project developed into a sustained baseball operation beyond Burns's initiating role.

**Caution:** do not imply Burns managed later roster construction unless further evidence shows it.

**Source:** https://greekbaseball.com/history

## 2004 — Olympics and retrospective judgment

Contemporary reporting on the Olympic team revisited Burns's role in helping establish the effort. Burns described the baseball initiative as one of the best things he had done as ambassador.

**Why it matters:** gives us Burns's own retrospective valuation of the project.

**Sources:**
- https://www.csmonitor.com/2004/0818/p02s01-woeu.html
- https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/sports/2004/04/12/it-appears-that-baseball-isnt-all-greek-to-the-greeks/0d5fc3ca-536e-4df4-8712-c260367f6e51/

## Documentary claim we can safely make

Burns did not create Greek baseball by himself. The strongest evidence supports a more interesting claim: as ambassador, he used diplomatic access and his own baseball interest to connect a Greek sports initiative with a Greek-American political and baseball network. That connection helped the project acquire expertise, money, legitimacy, and institutional reach.
