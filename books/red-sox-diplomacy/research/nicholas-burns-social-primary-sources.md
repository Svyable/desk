# Nicholas Burns social-media primary-source dossier

This file collects **verifiable posts by Nicholas Burns himself**—especially posts made from the official `@USAmbChina` account while serving as U.S. Ambassador to China—and separates them from synthetic promotional copy. The goal is to give the manuscript usable primary evidence for voice, public diplomacy, baseball, education, travel, cultural exchange, and representation.

## Account distinction

- `@USAmbChina` — official ambassadorial account used by Nicholas Burns during his tenure in Beijing. Treat these posts as official-capacity statements by Burns unless a specific post is clearly staff-authored.
- `@RNicholasBurns` — Burns's personal/public account. Use with care to distinguish pre-government, post-government, and personal commentary from formal Mission China messaging.

## Verified ambassadorial posts

### June 12, 2022 — first trip outside Beijing after arrival

> Leaving Beijing West Station to visit our Consulate General in #Wuhan. My first trip outside of Beijing since the lockdown and my arrival three months ago. 很期待这次旅行！

Source: https://twitter.com/USAmbChina/status/1535809403651371008

Secondary preservation/context: USC Center on Public Diplomacy, “Nicholas Burns: Influencer? The Twitter Diplomacy of Biden’s New China Ambassador,” which reproduces the post and discusses Burns’s unusually personal tone on the official account.

Editorial use: strong evidence for the account’s first-person, human-scale register; useful for Chapters 18, 20, and 21.

### June 25, 2022 — defending American democratic self-criticism

> We Americans love our country and cherish our democracy. We admit our flaws openly and seek to overcome them through freedoms of speech, assembly, the press and religion in pursuit of “a more perfect union”. This is a great national strength.

Source: quoted and linked by USC Center on Public Diplomacy from `@USAmbChina`.

Editorial use: unusually compact statement of Burns’s theory of representing America abroad: pride without pretending flawlessness. Strong fit for Chapters 20 and 21.

## Why this matters to the manuscript

The social record supplies something the formal diplomatic archive often does not: Burns’s public first-person register in real time. The strongest examples show the same pattern the book is trying to document rather than invent—baseball, students, cultural exchange, humor, local travel, and plainspoken American self-description used alongside official statecraft. The October 22, 2024 Beijing baseball-clinic post is especially valuable because it directly ties China, youth baseball, and Red Sox identity in Burns’s own ambassadorial voice.
