# Boston College 2002: Little League as civic service, not childhood biography

## Finding

Boston College preserves Nicholas Burns's May 2002 commencement address as a first-party institutional transcript. In the same speech in which Burns identifies himself as a loyal member of “Red Sox nation,” he later tells the graduates that the country needs “little league coaches” alongside church volunteers and other forms of civic service.

Source: Boston College, “R. Nicholas Burns 2002 Commencement Address.”
https://www.bc.edu/bc-web/bcnews/campus-community/alumni/r--nicholas-burns-2002-commencement-address.html

## Why it matters

This is a modest but useful thematic bridge. It shows Burns publicly placing youth baseball inside his vocabulary of ordinary civic service, not merely professional sport or fandom. Because the speech is preserved by Boston College and delivered by Burns himself, the wording is authenticated first-person material.

It can strengthen the manuscript's argument that baseball appears in Burns's public language in more than one register: Red Sox identity, international/cultural contact, and local civic participation.

## Strict boundary

This source **does not establish that Burns played Little League**, coached Little League, or was describing his own childhood experience. It cannot fill the Wellesley youth-baseball gap by itself.

Do not convert “we need little league coaches” into a Wellesley scene, a Burns family anecdote, a claim about his father, a childhood team, or evidence that baseball caused his interest in public service.

The speech contains no authenticated first-person statement from Libby Baylies and should not be used to attribute any view, memory, or action to her.

## Manuscript use

Promotable only as a compact thematic observation, preferably near the already sourced 2002 Boston College material:

> Returning to Boston College in 2002, Burns put baseball in two different registers. He joked as a loyal member of Red Sox Nation about the championship he hoped Boston would finally win, then later named Little League coaches among the ordinary forms of civic service the country needed.

The juxtaposition is in the same authenticated speech. Do not imply that Burns himself served as a Little League coach.

## Retrieval consequence

The Wellesley youth-baseball question remains open and still requires item-level evidence: a roster, clipping, photograph, yearbook entry, recreation record, or authenticated first-person recollection.
