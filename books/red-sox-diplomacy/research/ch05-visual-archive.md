# Chapter 5 visual archive — The Unresolved Address

## Method

Jerusalem is unusually vulnerable to visual overstatement. A contemporary map can silently import later political boundaries into 1985–87; a city photograph can provide atmosphere without documenting Burns; a photograph of a checkpoint or street scene can imply that Burns personally encountered a specific scene the archive has not established.

The chapter therefore uses two non-photographic documentary objects:

1. an **address object** showing the unusual institutional architecture of U.S. representation;
2. an **assistance ledger** showing the scale of West Bank/Gaza assistance during Burns's tour without converting those totals into Burns's personal budget.

## ADDRESS OBJECT 01 — representation without a settled address

**Local media:** `../media/ch05-unresolved-address.svg`  
**Sources:**
- U.S. Department of State Burns biography: https://2009-2017.state.gov/s/p/fapb/185577.htm
- State Department background on U.S. representation: https://2009-2017.state.gov/outofdate/bgn/israel/35872.htm
- Chapter research on Morris Draper, Douglas Keene, and the Jerusalem Consulate General.

**Documented institutional distinction:**
- Embassy Tel Aviv maintained the formal bilateral relationship with the Government of Israel.
- The U.S. Consulate General in Jerusalem operated as a separate U.S. mission; later State Department background describes its members as not accredited to a foreign government.
- Burns served at the Consulate General from 1985 to 1987 as a political officer coordinating economic assistance to Palestinians in the West Bank; later Burns testimony explicitly includes East Jerusalem.

**Narrative job:** Make the chapter's central line visible: the organization chart itself carried foreign policy. The object is intentionally not a territorial map.

**Guardrail:** Later State descriptions are used to explain a longstanding institutional structure, not to claim every administrative detail was identical in 1985. The diagram avoids drawing sovereignty boundaries or implying final-status conclusions.

## ASSISTANCE LEDGER 01 — the practical portfolio

**Local media:** `../media/ch05-assistance-ledger.svg`  
**Source:** Congressional Research Service historical table, sourced to State/USAID. Public mirror: https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/RS21594.html

**West Bank & Gaza assistance expenditures:**
- FY1985 — $12.141 million
- FY1986 — $13.950 million
- FY1987 — $8.400 million

**Narrative job:** Give scale to the phrase “managed our aid program” without inflating Burns's authority. These are program-wide U.S. assistance expenditures for West Bank and Gaza, not a personal Burns budget and not proof that he selected or supervised every project.

**Interpretive limit:** Burns's documented role was coordination of economic assistance. The ledger establishes the surrounding program scale. Project-level attribution still requires project-level evidence.

## Burns retrospective

A June 2025 Belfer Center podcast transcript provides useful first-person calibration. Burns says he spent two years at the Jerusalem consulate managing the economic assistance program for Palestinians in the West Bank and East Jerusalem and calls it a *“seminal very important experience”* for him. He also says he then expected to spend his career in Africa and the Arab world.

Source: https://www.belfercenter.org/sites/default/files/2025-06/nick-burns-podcast-transcript-june-2025.pdf

The manuscript can use the short retrospective phrase in italics while keeping the later interpretation separate from contemporaneous 1985–87 evidence.

## Why no map or city photograph

Geography matters enormously here, but a simplified map would be forced to encode disputed political assumptions the chapter is specifically trying to keep unresolved. A city photograph would be atmospheric rather than Burns-specific. The address diagram can explain the diplomatic geometry without pretending to settle the underlying geography.

## Chapter-level restraint

Two objects are enough. The first explains why an office address could be political. The second shows what the junior officer's practical portfolio sat inside. The chapter's strongest visual move is refusing false geographic certainty.
