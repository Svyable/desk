# Boston–New York rivalry media pass 01

## Scope

Bounded media/research enhancement for the baseball-rivalry material in *Red Sox Diplomacy*, especially Chapter 19, **Yankees, Rivals, Adversaries**. This pass resolves the useful historical idea behind the previously held October 1904 Library of Congress panorama by finding a cleaner item-level reuse route: a Boston Public Library photograph from September 16, 1904 that explicitly depicts the Boston Americans playing the New York Highlanders at Huntington Avenue Grounds and carries a **No known restrictions** rights statement.

## Recommended asset — Boston Americans vs. New York Highlanders, September 16, 1904

**Item:** *Crowd in the outfield at Huntington Avenue Grounds*  
**Institution:** Boston Public Library, Print Department  
**Date created:** 1904; catalog description identifies the game as September 16, 1904  
**File name:** 06_06_000132  
**Collection note:** McGreevey no. 167  
**Medium:** photographic print, silver print  
**Repository record:** https://www.flickr.com/photos/boston_public_library/2349887141/  
**Rights statement:** No known restrictions  
**Commercial-publication assessment:** Strong candidate for documentary commercial-book use under the holding institution's item-level rights statement. Preserve a screenshot/PDF of the catalog record in final production provenance. “No known restrictions” is a risk assessment, not a warranty or transfer of copyright.

### What the catalog establishes

Boston Public Library describes the photograph as a panoramic view of spectators in the outfield and seated on the outfield fence to watch the **Boston Americans play the New York Highlanders on September 16, 1904**. The catalog also states that Boston won and later won the pennant from New York on the last day of the season.

The photograph therefore documents something more useful to this book than generic early baseball: the Boston–New York rivalry existed in recognizable competitive form before Fenway Park and before New York adopted the Yankees name. That gives Chapter 19 a historical counterweight to Nicholas Burns's modern Yankees jokes without pretending the emotional or commercial rivalry of 1904 was identical to the later Red Sox–Yankees mythology.

### Why this is preferable to the held October 8 LOC panorama

The Library of Congress panorama *American League Park, Huntington Ave., Boston, Mass., Boston vs. New York, Oct. 8, 1904, attendance 28,040* remains historically excellent, but its catalog identifies Geo. R. Lawrence Co. as both photographer and copyright claimant and records a copyright deposit. The previous research pass correctly held it rather than inferring rights from age alone.

The Boston Public Library September 16 image reaches the same underlying narrative territory — Boston, New York, Huntington Avenue, 1904, an overflow crowd — with a cleaner item-level **No known restrictions** statement. Prefer the BPL object unless later rights research supplies an equally explicit publication determination for the Lawrence panorama.

LOC held item: https://www.loc.gov/item/2007663979/

## Reader-facing use

### Finished caption

> **Boston and New York before Fenway, September 16, 1904.** Spectators crowd the outfield at Huntington Avenue Grounds as the Boston Americans play the New York Highlanders. The rivalry predates both Fenway Park and New York's Yankees name. Boston Public Library, Print Department, file 06_06_000132. No known restrictions.

### Alt text

> Black-and-white panoramic photograph of Huntington Avenue Grounds in Boston in 1904, with a large overflow crowd standing and sitting around the outfield during a Boston Americans–New York Highlanders game.

### Credit line

> Boston Public Library, Print Department, file 06_06_000132, McGreevey no. 167. No known restrictions.

### Evidence boundary for the caption

The BPL record supports the teams, venue, year and September 16 game identification. The phrase “before New York's Yankees name” is historical context and should be separately source-checked in final notes if retained in a formal caption. Do not describe the crowd's emotions, chants, loyalties or motives; the image cannot establish them.

## Suggested original contextual figure — RIVALRY 01

If the archival photograph is too visually dense at book size, pair it with a very small original chronology rather than adding a second photograph:

- **1901 — Boston:** American League club begins play at Huntington Avenue Grounds.
- **1903 — Boston:** Huntington Avenue hosts the opening game of the first modern World Series.
- **1904 — Boston / New York:** Boston Americans and New York Highlanders fight for the American League pennant; the BPL September 16 photograph documents one game in that race.
- **1912 — Boston:** Red Sox move into Fenway Park.

This should remain contextual, not mythmaking. The figure must not imply that the entire later Red Sox–Yankees rivalry was fully formed in 1904.

## Formatting and production guidance

- For Chapter 19, place the photograph near the first transition from Burns's joke to the proposition that sports rivalry presupposes shared rules. The historical image makes the shared-league premise visible before the chapter breaks the analogy with state competition.
- Keep photograph, caption and credit together in print. Do not orphan the credit or let the image float across a chapter boundary.
- Panoramic material needs width. Prefer a wide half-page or full text-block treatment rather than shrinking it into a decorative thumbnail.
- In EPUB, use image → caption → credit/source note. Alt text should describe the visible crowd and ground, not summarize the chapter's diplomatic analogy.
- Proof the crowd at final grayscale size. If individual forms collapse into noise, use the image larger or choose the original chronology instead.
- Do not overlay modern Yankees/Red Sox logos, modern team colors, faux scoreboards, newspaper texture or rivalry slogans.
- Do not crop away so much of the crowd/field relationship that the photograph becomes generic old baseball.
- Preserve institution, file number, collection note, rights wording, source URL and any crop in production provenance.

## Rights and provenance caution

Boston Public Library's item record says **No known restrictions**. Treat that wording precisely. It is materially better than an item with unresolved copyright-claim metadata, but it is not the same thing as CC0 or an affirmative copyright dedication. Before final production, preserve the live item record and, if possible, obtain the highest-resolution file directly from BPL/Digital Commonwealth together with the same rights metadata.

Avoid relying on the Flickr interface's generic “some rights reserved” chrome where it conflicts with the institution-supplied item metadata; production should record the BPL catalog's item-level rights statement and, ideally, the underlying Digital Commonwealth/BPL record.

## Research strengthened

This asset changes the historical footing of Chapter 19 in a useful way. Burns's briefing-room Yankees joke is late-twentieth-century social shorthand. The BPL image supplies evidence that Boston–New York baseball competition long predates that shorthand and took place inside a formal league with a shared field, rules, schedule and pennant race. That is exactly the institutional feature the chapter later contrasts with international rivalry.

It should not be used to claim that 1904 spectators understood the rivalry in modern terms, that today's fan culture existed unchanged, or that the photograph itself explains Burns's attachment to the Red Sox.
