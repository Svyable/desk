# Wellesley youth baseball: Bates School Little League in Burns's 2024 Beijing remarks

## Status

**Verified first-person Burns statement, preserved through an official-page trail plus contemporaneous mirror.**

This source closes an important part of the Wellesley youth-baseball gap: Burns himself publicly said that he and his friends played Little League at Bates School in Wellesley.

It does **not** establish a team, coach, position, season, exact diamond, game, score, uniform, teammate list, or family scene.

## What this establishes

Safe:

- Burns publicly connected his Wellesley childhood to organized youth baseball.
- Burns said that he and friends played **Little League at Bates School**.
- He linked that memory in the 2024 Beijing Independence Day speech to Katharine Lee Bates, the Wellesley writer of “America the Beautiful,” for whom the school was named.
- The statement is useful both for the childhood chapter and for the China-era public-diplomacy thread because Burns chose this Wellesley baseball memory while representing the United States in Beijing.

## What this does not establish

- exact seasons or dates;

- Red Sox fandom during any specific youth game;

## Manuscript value

This is materially stronger than the Wellesley Free Library's general “lifelong Red Sox” characterization because it is **Burns's own public statement of youth participation in Little League**.

A safe narrator formulation is:

> Burns would later remember playing Little League with friends at Bates School in Wellesley, the school named for Katharine Lee Bates, author of “America the Beautiful.”

If the manuscript wants to emphasize source setting:

> Speaking in Beijing on Independence Day in 2024, Burns reached back to Wellesley: he and his friends, he said, had played Little League at Bates School, named for Katharine Lee Bates.

Do not add sensory scene detail unless a contemporaneous local record or authenticated oral history supplies it.
