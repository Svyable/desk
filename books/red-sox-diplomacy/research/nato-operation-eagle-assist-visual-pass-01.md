## Why this is stronger than generic NATO imagery

Operation EAGLE ASSIST was one of the concrete military consequences of NATO's post-September 11 collective-defense response. The image therefore does more than establish that Burns worked at NATO headquarters. It connects the ambassador, the secretary general, the military committee and the personnel who carried out an alliance mission in one documented frame.

The evidentiary boundary matters. The photograph was made eight months after September 11. It does **not** show the North Atlantic Council invoking Article 5, the September 11 emergency meeting, or Burns negotiating the decision. It documents alliance officials recognizing personnel who had flown EAGLE ASSIST missions. Use it for implementation and consequence, not as a visual reconstruction of the decision itself.

## Documentary context worth pairing with the image

Burns's September 11, 2002 NATO anniversary address supplies a useful first-person chronology: he recalled the ambassadors' stunned lunch on September 11, the emergency meeting later that night, and the Article 5 decision the next morning. In the same address he pointed to NATO AWACS missions over American cities as part of the alliance's subsequent action.

Source: https://www.nato.int/docu/speech/2002/s020911c.htm

A NATO Air Base Geilenkirchen retrospective on the close of EAGLE ASSIST records Burns addressing the returning personnel and reading a letter from President George W. Bush. This is useful contextual evidence for the May 2002 ceremony, but publication should cite the official base publication rather than convert ceremonial language into an independent claim about operational effectiveness.

## Reader-facing placement / formatting

- Best placement: after the manuscript has established the September 11 / September 12 Article 5 chronology and moves from decision to implementation. The image should function as consequence, not atmosphere.
- Keep both civilian alliance leadership and AWACS personnel visible. A crop that isolates Burns destroys the institutional information that makes the image valuable.
- If the aircraft or mission context is legible in the archival master, preserve enough of it to distinguish the scene from a generic awards ceremony.
- Print/PDF: keep image, caption and credit together; test the group at final trim size and grayscale. If faces become illegible, use a wider column or full-width placement rather than an aggressive crop.
- EPUB: semantic order should be image → caption → credit/source → prose. Do not bake caption or credit into the image.
- Alt text should name the documented people and mission context without saying they are `invoking Article 5` or `negotiating`.
- If a source note accompanies the figure, distinguish the photograph's May 16, 2002 event date from Burns's September 11, 2002 retrospective speech.

## Caption discipline

Avoid `Article 5 in action` as a caption. It is rhetorically attractive but collapses a legal-political decision into one later operational scene. `Operation EAGLE ASSIST` is the documented bridge. The prose can explain why that mission mattered after establishing the chronology from NATO records.

## Source-integrity rules

- Treat the original military caption as documentation of who is pictured and why the ceremony occurred, not proof of broader causal or strategic claims.
- Preserve the distinction between the September 2001 political decision, the subsequent EAGLE ASSIST operation, and the May 2002 recognition ceremony.
- Do not infer that Burns directed AWACS operations or personally caused their deployment.
- Do not describe the crew members as having flown a particular number of sorties unless a primary operational source supplies the figure.
- Resolve the exact NARA catalog identifier before final asset ingest. If the authoritative catalog record materially conflicts with the derivative metadata, the NARA record controls.
