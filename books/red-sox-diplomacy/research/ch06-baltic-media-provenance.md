# Chapter 6 media and provenance — Baltic withdrawal

## Purpose

This packet records publication-safe visual and documentary opportunities for Chapter 6, *The Score Is Not the Game*. The chapter already has two original documentary graphics: the August 31 deadline object and an implementation ledger. This pass therefore favors a period geographic object that adds information those figures do not, rather than another decorative photograph or another rendering of the same deadline.

## Recommended source object: CIA, *The Baltic States* (1994)

- **Title:** *The Baltic States*
- **Creator:** United States Central Intelligence Agency
- **Date:** 1994; map note `802244 (R00112) 6-94`, placing the map in June 1994, during the final withdrawal negotiations
- **Holding institution:** Library of Congress, Geography and Map Division
- **Catalog record:** https://www.loc.gov/item/2005626489/
- **LCCN:** 2005626489
- **Digital ID:** http://hdl.loc.gov/loc.gmd/g7020.ct001601
- **Authority/usefulness:** contemporaneous U.S. federal cartography of Estonia, Latvia, and Lithuania, created two months before the August 31 withdrawal deadline
- **Rights statement:** the Library of Congress catalog states that content in its Geography and Map Division digitized collections is free to use and reuse unless otherwise stated. The named creator is a U.S. federal agency. No contrary restriction is stated on this item record.
- **LOC requested credit:** `Library of Congress, Geography and Map Division.`
- **Commercial-book posture:** suitable as a research/source object. Direct reproduction appears favorable from the catalog rights statement, but an original project schematic is still preferred because the chapter needs only a small subset of the source map's information and should not inherit unnecessary cartographic detail or styling.

### Evidentiary boundary

The map establishes how a U.S. federal cartographic source represented the Baltic states in June 1994. It does **not** establish Burns's travel, negotiating positions, troop locations, the boundaries of individual installations, or the causal role of the United States in the withdrawal agreements.

## Recommended original figure

**Working title:** *A deadline across three small states*

Create an original, simple north-up regional schematic derived from independently plotted geographic coordinates rather than tracing the CIA artwork. Show only:

- Estonia, Latvia, and Lithuania;
- Tallinn and Riga;
- Paldiski, Estonia;
- Skrunda, Latvia;
- the Baltic Sea;
- a restrained note that Lithuania's Russian troop withdrawal was completed in 1993 while the Chapter 6 deadline concerns Estonia and Latvia in 1994.

Do not draw troop arrows, inferred military positions, ethnic distributions, or installation footprints unless a separately verified source supports them. The point is scale and relationship: Paldiski and Skrunda were not abstractions in a policy memo but physical places inside newly restored sovereign states.

**Suggested placement:** after the paragraph beginning `Lithuania got the last Russian troops out in 1993.` This lets geography arrive before the manuscript begins asking the reader to hold Skrunda and Paldiski in memory.

**Caption:** *The Baltic states during the 1994 withdrawal negotiations. Lithuania's Russian troop withdrawal had been completed in 1993; Estonia and Latvia were working toward the August 31, 1994 deadline. Skrunda and Paldiski became concrete implementation problems inside that larger withdrawal. Original schematic; geographic orientation cross-checked against the CIA's June 1994* The Baltic States *map held by the Library of Congress.*

**Credit line:** `Original diagram by Sven Hardy Benson. Geographic orientation cross-checked against U.S. Central Intelligence Agency, The Baltic States (June 1994), Library of Congress, Geography and Map Division.`

**Alt text:** `Regional schematic of Estonia, Latvia, and Lithuania on the Baltic Sea, marking Tallinn and Paldiski in Estonia and Riga and Skrunda in Latvia, with a note distinguishing Lithuania's 1993 Russian troop withdrawal from the August 31, 1994 Estonia-Latvia deadline.`

### Reader/print formatting

- Keep north up and labels horizontal; do not rotate place names for visual effect.
- Must remain legible in grayscale and at approximately half-page print width.
- Do not rely on color alone to distinguish national boundaries, cities, and implementation sites.
- Use direct labels rather than a legend where possible.
- Keep figure and caption together across page breaks.
- In EPUB/Reader, cap the figure at the normal text-column width and retain the full alt text rather than repeating the caption as alt text.
- Avoid flags, team colors, baseball iconography, or decorative Cold War motifs. They add tone but no evidence.

## Documentary cross-checks strengthened in this pass

### Clinton, July 6, 1994 — Riga

Official White House transcript:
https://clintonwhitehouse6.archives.gov/1994/07/1994-07-06-president-in-press-conference-with-baltic-presidents.html

This contemporaneous statement supports the chapter's implementation ledger. Clinton said the United States was prepared to provide up to $4 million to help Latvia dismantle the unfinished Skrunda radar structure, had more than doubled housing-voucher support for qualified Russian officers resettling from Latvia and Estonia, and was providing $2 million toward environmental restoration at Paldiski. It also records the administration's August 31 withdrawal objective and its simultaneous discussion of ethnic-minority rights.

**Use boundary:** presidential public statements establish the administration's stated policy and announced assistance. They do not by themselves establish implementation success, final expenditure, or Burns's individual authorship of those measures.

### White House background briefing, July 1, 1994

https://clintonwhitehouse6.archives.gov/1994/07/1994-07-01-background-briefing-on-g-7-summit.html

A senior administration official estimated roughly 4,000–5,000 Russian troops remained between Estonia and Latvia and described the Skrunda arrangement as roughly four further years of Russian operation before closure. The briefing is useful contemporaneous context but is unattributed by name and the troop figure is explicitly an estimate.

**Manuscript recommendation:** do not add the 4,000–5,000 estimate merely because it is vivid. The current chapter does not need it, and an approximate anonymous-briefing number would create more qualification than narrative value.

### Yeltsin-Clinton press conference, July 10, 1994 — Naples

https://clintonwhitehouse6.archives.gov/1994/07/1994-07-10-president-and-president-yeltsin-in-press-conference.html

Asked whether all Russian troops would be out of the Baltics by August 31, Yeltsin answered no and specifically described Estonia as more difficult, citing treatment of the Russian-speaking population and military pensioners. This is unusually strong primary evidence for the chapter's claim that the Estonia deadline remained politically unsettled in July rather than moving mechanically toward completion.

**Potential sidebar/pull-quote use:** a very short excerpt could document how visibly uncertain the deadline still was in July, but any quotation should remain brief and attributed to the official White House transcript. The chapter currently works without it, so this is an optional evidence enhancement rather than a required insertion.

### White House background briefing, July 27, 1994

https://clintonwhitehouse6.archives.gov/1994/07/1994-07-27-background-briefing-on-estonia-russia-agreement.html

The briefing records the Meri-Yeltsin agreement for Russian forces to leave Estonia by August 31 and a review process, with CSCE oversight, for Russian military retirees seeking to remain resident in Estonia. This gives the chapter a useful documentary bridge between Yeltsin's July 10 public `no` and the August 31 completion statement.

### Public Papers / GovInfo, August 31, 1994

https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/PPP-1994-book2/PPP-1994-book2-doc-pg1514/context

The official Public Papers record states that Russian military-force withdrawals from Estonia and Latvia were completed that day under bilateral agreements and places the event in the wider departure of Russian troops from Eastern Europe. This remains the strongest source for the chapter's deadline object and closing historical marker.

## Source hierarchy

For claims about what the Clinton administration publicly said or announced, prefer the contemporaneous White House transcript or GovInfo/Public Papers record over later summaries. For geography, use the 1994 CIA/Library of Congress map as period orientation, not as evidence for negotiations. For Burns-specific actions, retain the separate Burns/Latvian diplomatic sources already documented in `baltic-withdrawal-dossier.md` rather than inferring his role from presidential materials.

## Rejected media

- **Generic Clinton/Yeltsin photographs:** recognizable but add little to the chapter's mechanism and risk turning a multilateral, implementation-heavy story into a two-president story.
- **Modern Baltic political maps:** geographically convenient but import later borders, labels, infrastructure, and design choices into a 1994 scene unnecessarily.
- **Commercial news photographs of Russian troop departures:** potentially vivid, but rights status is generally unsuitable or costly for commercial book reuse unless separately licensed.
- **Copied CIA map as-is:** rights posture is favorable, but the full map carries more detail than the chapter needs. An original, independently plotted schematic is clearer, more consistent with the book's existing figure language, and easier to make accessible in grayscale and EPUB.
- **1994 baseball-strike photography:** decorative here. The manuscript correctly limits baseball to chronology; an image would give that coincidence more narrative weight than the evidence warrants.

## Highest-value next opportunity

Search presidential-library and National Archives audiovisual catalogs for a **precisely cataloged July 1994 Riga image** showing Clinton with one or more Baltic presidents, or an official scene directly tied to the withdrawal talks. Accept it only if the catalog identifies the event/date and federal creator and gives a commercial-reuse posture clear enough for publication. A real diplomatic scene would complement the schematic; a generic presidential portrait would not.
