# NATO-Era Baseball Scenes — 2003–2007 triangulation

This note isolates the strongest Burns-specific baseball scenes around the NATO and early Under Secretary years. It separates direct Burns testimony, official transcripts, secondary witness accounts, and inference.

## 1. October 2003 — direct Burns testimony: Brussels at 6:16 a.m.

An ESPN Magazine profile records Nicholas Burns's own account of watching Game 7 of the 2003 Red Sox–Yankees American League Championship Series from Brussels on Armed Forces television. Burns remembered the exact local time — **6:16 a.m.** — when Aaron Boone's walk-off home run ended Boston's season.

This is high-value evidence because it establishes a real expatriate baseball scene rather than a chronological overlap inferred from his NATO posting.

**Source:**
- Jim Caple, ESPN Magazine, “Give 'Em Hellas.”
  https://www.espn.co.uk/espn/magazine/archives/news/story?page=magazine-20040119-article32

**Baseball corroboration:**
- MLB, 2003 ALCS recap.
  https://www.mlb.com/news/2003-alcs-recap

## 2. April 2004 — Thomas Friedman reports a CENTCOM / Yankees–Red Sox scene

In *The World Is Flat*, Thomas L. Friedman says Burns told him about a briefing at U.S. Central Command headquarters in Qatar in April 2004. According to Friedman's account, General John Abizaid and his staff sat in front of four flat-screen televisions. Three displayed real-time Predator imagery from Iraq; the fourth showed a Yankees–Red Sox game. Friedman says Burns's attention was on the baseball screen and describes the baseball image as **Pedro Martínez versus Derek Jeter**.

This is not an official transcript and not Burns speaking in a surviving primary source. It is a named secondary account by Friedman reporting what Burns told him.

**Source:**
- Thomas L. Friedman, *The World Is Flat: A Brief History of the Twenty-first Century* (Farrar, Straus and Giroux, 2005), early chapter discussing military information systems.
- Google Books record: https://books.google.com/books/about/The_World_Is_Flat.html?id=7SJvQgAACAAJ
- Searchable text copy used to verify the anecdote: https://studylib.net/doc/27886802/world-is-flat

### Why the scene matters

If corroborated, this is one of the book's strongest visual scenes because diplomatic/military work and Red Sox attention literally occupy adjacent screens. It demonstrates the coexistence of official responsibility and persistent personal identity without needing an authorial baseball metaphor.

It should not yet be written with novelistic certainty. We need either Burns confirmation, travel records, another witness, or a contemporaneous itinerary that pins the date and setting.

## 3. Candidate date: April 25, 2004 — strong inference, not established fact

Friedman gives only **April 2004** and says the baseball screen showed Pedro Martínez against Derek Jeter.

Pedro Martínez made one April 2004 start against the Yankees: **April 25 at Yankee Stadium**. Boston won 2–0. Martínez threw seven scoreless innings; Jeter went 0-for-4.

**Baseball source:**
- Baseball-Reference box score, April 25, 2004.
  https://www.baseball-reference.com/boxes/NYA/NYA200404250.shtml

Separate contemporaneous reporting dated April 25–26 places Burns traveling with senior NATO envoys toward Kabul for the North Atlantic Council's first Afghanistan visit. Burns is quoted speaking to reporters while flying to Kabul, and the delegation met Afghan officials on April 26.

**Travel-context sources:**
- Associated Press report reproduced by Hearst, April 25, 2004.
  https://www.mrt.com/news/article/Enviados-de-OTAN-piden-permanencia-de-fuerza-de-7804331.php
- RFE/RL, April 26, 2004.
  https://www.rferl.org/a/1052483.html

NATO/ISAF also had operational liaison relationships with CENTCOM facilities in Qatar, making a Qatar stop institutionally plausible in the wider Afghanistan mission architecture.

**Context:**
- NATO Review, “The road to Kabul.”
  https://www.nato.int/en/news-and-events/events/transcripts/2003/04/01/the-road-to-kabul

### Evidentiary status

**Hypothesis:** the Friedman CENTCOM scene may have occurred on April 25, 2004, during or adjacent to the NATO delegation's travel toward Afghanistan.

**Why it is plausible:**
- Friedman: April 2004, Qatar, Pedro Martínez vs. Derek Jeter.
- Baseball record: Martínez's only April start against New York was April 25.
- Travel reporting: Burns was in the NATO delegation traveling toward Kabul around April 25–26.

**Why it is not yet proven:**
- Friedman does not give a date.
- A television could have been showing delayed or replayed footage.
- The published travel reports do not explicitly state that the delegation stopped at CENTCOM headquarters in Qatar on April 25.
- Publication datelines and travel-day references can lag actual movements.

Do not convert this hypothesis into manuscript fact until a travel itinerary, diary, mission log, interview, or other source closes the gap.

## 4. October 2004 — championship-night gap remains

The 2003 and April 2004 evidence establishes that Burns followed Red Sox–Yankees baseball during his NATO posting, including while overseas and while traveling in the CENTCOM/NATO operational environment.

It still does **not** establish where Burns watched:
- the 2004 ALCS Games 4–7;
- the World Series;
- the October 27 championship clincher.

The correct archival targets are now sharper: U.S. Mission NATO calendars, Armed Forces Network records, October 2004 travel itineraries, Burns correspondence, and colleagues who traveled with him.

## 5. March 2005 — Burns supplies the interpretive word: “redemption”

At the U.S. Institute of Peace, Burns calls himself a native-born member of Red Sox Nation and says New Englanders had found “redemption” after Boston defeated the Yankees and Cardinals.

This is the strongest direct Burns language for interpreting 2004. It should remain the personal voice attached to the championship until a contemporaneous October account emerges.

**Source:**
https://2001-2009.state.gov/p/us/rm/2005/45781.htm

## 6. August 2007 — detailed baseball analysis before another championship

In an official State Department podcast, Burns analyzes Boston's West Coast schedule, contrasts 2007 with the club's 2006 collapse, praises the bullpen and starting rotation, and argues pitching becomes even more important in the postseason. He predicts the Red Sox will reclaim the World Series title.

**Source:**
https://2001-2009.state.gov/p/us/rm/2007/90347.htm

Boston did win the 2007 championship.

**Result source:**
https://www.mlb.com/redsox/history/postseason-results

This does not matter because Burns guessed correctly. It matters because the exchange proves current, specific baseball fluency at a moment when he was the State Department's third-ranking official.

## 7. October 29, 2007 — an official morning-after championship moment

The Red Sox completed their four-game World Series sweep of Colorado on the night of October 28, 2007.

The next day Burns convened a State Department conversation with former Under Secretaries for Political Affairs. Near the transition to audience questions, Burns said he had “the events of last evening in mind” and asked the auditorium how many were members of Red Sox Nation. The transcript records applause.

This is valuable because it is exactly the kind of contemporaneous, official reaction still missing for October 2004. It shows how Burns publicly marked a Red Sox championship in real time without requiring reconstruction.

**Source:**
- U.S. Department of State, “A Conversation With America's Senior Career Diplomats,” October 29, 2007.
  https://2001-2009.state.gov/p/us/rm/2007/95349.htm

## 8. January 2008 — Rice uses the Red Sox in Burns's departure ritual

When Burns announced his departure from the State Department, CBS's State Department correspondent reported Secretary Condoleezza Rice joking that she knew nothing good would come of the Red Sox winning the World Series again.

This is evidence of institutional recognition: senior colleagues understood Burns's Red Sox identity well enough to make it part of a formal departure announcement.

**Source:**
- CBS News, “A Career Diplomat Says Good-Bye,” January 19, 2008.
  https://www.cbsnews.com/news/a-career-diplomat-says-good-bye/

## 9. Scene-quality hierarchy for the manuscript

### Tier 1 — direct / official

- 2003 Brussels, Armed Forces TV, 6:16 a.m. — Burns quoted by ESPN.
- 2005 “redemption” — official State transcript.
- 2007 detailed pitching analysis and prediction — official State transcript.
- October 29, 2007 morning-after Red Sox Nation audience moment — official State transcript.

### Tier 2 — named secondary witness/account

- April 2004 CENTCOM Qatar four-screen anecdote — Friedman reporting Burns's story.
- January 2008 Rice departure joke — CBS State Department reporting.

### Tier 3 — research hypothesis

- April 25, 2004 as the likely date of the CENTCOM screen scene.

Tier 3 belongs in research notes, not narrative assertion, until corroborated.

## 10. Highest-value corroboration targets

1. Burns interview: ask directly whether the CENTCOM scene was April 25, 2004 and whether he remembers the 2–0 Pedro Martínez start.
2. U.S. Mission NATO / North Atlantic Council travel itinerary for April 24–26, 2004.
3. CENTCOM visitor logs or public-affairs photography from the NATO delegation's April 2004 travel.
4. Armed Forces Network schedule/feeds for April 25, 2004 in Qatar and for October 2004 in Brussels.
5. Colleagues on the NATO delegation who may remember the CENTCOM briefing.
6. Burns's calendars, correspondence, or personal papers for October 2003 and April/October 2004.
