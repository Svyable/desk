# Chapter 6 Brief — The Score Is Not the Game

## Status

Research brief. Chapter 5 is now drafted; this opens the next chronological/thematic bridge toward Burns's Washington years and State spokesman period.

## Working question

What does a diplomat learn when the visible outcome is not the whole measure of the work?

The title can support a serious distinction between scorekeeping and relationship maintenance, but it should not become an excuse for vague sports metaphor. The chapter needs a documented case in which Burns's work involved an outcome that was partial, delayed, ambiguous, or dependent on maintaining channels after a nominal win/loss.

## Strong evidence already available

Burns's later diplomatic philosophy repeatedly emphasizes:

- maintaining relationships before crises;
- keeping lines open even with difficult counterparts;
- alliances as accumulated strategic power;
- diplomacy as a profession whose value is often invisible before it becomes necessary;
- the limits of treating foreign policy as a sequence of discrete victories.

His later account of George Shultz's “diplomatic garden” is particularly useful, but Chapter 3 already introduces that material. Chapter 6 should earn a different dramatic center rather than repeating the philosophy.

## Chronological opportunity

After Jerusalem, Burns returned to Washington and served in the State Department Operations Center and Secretariat before moving into Soviet affairs and then the National Security Council. That transition offers a natural bridge from field diplomacy to the machinery through which Washington receives, filters, escalates and acts on information.

Potential case clusters:

1. **Operations Center / Secretariat, 1987–88** — shift from overseas contact work to institutional information flow.
2. **Shultz Asia trip, 1988** — only if the documentary record provides Burns-specific detail beyond his later gardening anecdote.
3. **Soviet collapse / Baltic withdrawals, 1990–94** — a strong candidate because Burns later received recognition for work connected to Russian troop withdrawal from the Baltic states; this may provide a concrete example of diplomacy as implementation after the headline political change.
4. **Ukraine/Russia economic assistance** — Burns's NSC portfolio included economic assistance as the Soviet system broke apart, potentially echoing his earlier development work without forcing a false continuity.

## Guardrails

Do not claim that Burns learned a specific lesson from Jerusalem or Cairo unless he says so.

Do not turn the collapse of the Soviet Union into an American diplomatic “win” or a baseball score.

Do not imply Burns personally caused outcomes handled by presidents, secretaries, national security advisers, allied governments, Soviet/Russian leaders, Baltic governments, or larger interagency teams.

## Next research action

Search Burns's own retrospectives and contemporaneous records for the 1987–95 period, prioritizing one documented episode with enough Burns-specific action to carry the chapter. The Baltic troop-withdrawal work is the leading candidate; Operations Center material is the second.
