# Fenway early-park media pass 01

## Purpose

Publication-safe visual/source packet for Red Sox Diplomacy passages that need Fenway Park as a historical place rather than decorative baseball nostalgia. This pass favors a documented early view of the physical ballpark and a period baseball source over modern team-controlled imagery.

**Source:** https://www.loc.gov/item/92513262/  
**Rights advisory:** “No known restrictions on publication.”  
**Commercial-publication assessment:** Suitable candidate for commercial book use under the Library of Congress item-level rights advisory. Preserve the catalog record and rights advisory in production files; do not convert “no known restrictions” into a claim that the Library owns or grants copyright.

### Why it earns space

The photograph is from Fenway’s second season. It can establish the park as a built historical environment before later mythology, branding, renovations, television sightlines, and contemporary sponsorship clutter. Use it only where the manuscript is discussing inherited baseball place, Boston memory, or the physical persistence of Fenway across generations.

### Finished caption

**Fenway Park, Boston, September 28, 1913.** The park was still new enough to be photographed without a century of accumulated mythology. George Grantham Bain Collection, Library of Congress. No known restrictions on publication.

### Alt text

Black-and-white 1913 view across the baseball field at Fenway Park in Boston, with the diamond and early grandstand and bleacher structures visible.

### Credit line

Library of Congress, Prints and Photographs Division, George Grantham Bain Collection, LC-USZ62-105854.

### Production guidance

- Prefer a full-bleed or wide half-page treatment only if the available reproduction supports the target print resolution; otherwise use a restrained half-page width.
- Preserve enough of the grandstand and field geometry to make the image function as place evidence. Do not crop to a generic patch of baseball action.
- Keep caption and credit with the image in print; in EPUB, place caption immediately after the image and credit after the caption.
- The source is already monochrome and should reproduce cleanly in grayscale.
- Do not overlay modern Red Sox marks, logos, score graphics, faux newspaper texture, or nostalgic typography.

### Modern Fenway/team photography

Do not substitute Getty/AP/Reuters/editorial-only stadium photographs, screenshots, team social posts, ticketing graphics, or modern MLB/Red Sox promotional artwork. They add rights and trademark complexity while contributing less historical information than the archival view.

## Reader-facing integration recommendation

Use **one** early-Fenway photograph in the front-end or epilogue material only if the surrounding prose makes a claim about inherited place, continuity, or Boston memory. The image should not appear merely because the book has “Red Sox” in the title.

Suggested figure callout:

> **FENWAY 01 — A place before the mythology**  
> Fenway Park in September 1913, during its second season. The documentary value is architectural and temporal: a recognizable place before a century of accumulated memory.

For print, keep figure + caption + credit together and avoid a page break between image and caption. For EPUB, image → alt text metadata → caption → credit/source note. Avoid floating the image across a chapter transition where it can be mistaken for an illustration of a later event.

## Evidence boundaries

- The 1913 image documents Fenway Park’s physical appearance on the cataloged date. It does not establish what Burns, later fans, or Boston residents thought or felt about the park.
- A period baseball manual documents period publication and language, not universal fan culture.
- “No known restrictions on publication” is an LOC rights advisory, not a warranty or transfer of rights.
- Do not infer team endorsement, sponsorship, or permission from historical subject matter.
