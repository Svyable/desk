# Deep Baseball Primary-Source Pass 07 — Galatis continuity, grassroots agency, and the Greece counter-narrative

## Purpose

This pass goes after a problem created by the success of the Greece chapter itself. The more clearly the embassy / Sarbanes / Angelos / MLB network is reconstructed, the easier it becomes to tell the story as though Greek baseball began when an ambassador activated institutions.

The surviving record does not support that simplification.

Contemporaneous sources show Greek organizers and Boston-area Greek Americans building pieces of the project on their own timelines. They also allow one especially useful person-level thread to be followed for more than twenty years: **Bill Galatis**, from the Olympic-baseball effort to Greek Heritage Night at Fenway Park.

The result is not proof of one continuous Red Sox institutional program. It is something more defensible and more interesting: documented continuity in the people who carried Greek-American baseball culture between Greece and Boston.

## 1. January 2000 — grassroots baseball in Greece predates some later origin stories

A January 2, 2000 Associated Press report preserved by the *Los Angeles Times* describes the Greek baseball buildout while Nicholas Burns was ambassador.

The article establishes several tracks at once:

- Burns was coordinating American assistance and being described as a baseball ambassador;
- Major League Baseball International planned coaching and administrative support;
- Peter Angelos and the Orioles were involved in recruiting and training;
- support had been pledged by the Boston Red Sox;
- Chuck Samiotes, a civil engineer from Wayland, Massachusetts, had already begun teaching baseball to relatives in Greece during a 1997 visit and returned in 1998 with baseball equipment.

Source:
https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-2000-jan-02-mn-49873-story.html

### Why this matters

Some later federation histories compress the Burns / Sarbanes / Angelos sequence toward 2001. The contemporary 2000 record already shows the American network operating, and it places Samiotes's own grassroots activity earlier still.

That does not make the later histories useless. It means the manuscript should resist the premise that the project had one discoverable birthday.

The chapter's existing formulation — projects like this can begin more than once — is now better supported than a clean founding scene would be.

## 2. Burns's own model was generational, not Olympic-only

The same AP account ends with Burns cautioning that baseball culture would grow **slowly** and over a generation.

That is important because it protects the episode from being read only as a stunt designed to satisfy the host country's Olympic obligation.

Samiotes independently makes the same basic distinction in the article: he rejects the idea of building a team for one Olympic appearance and then abandoning the sport, and says he wants to continue teaching children afterward.

The ambassador and the grassroots volunteer therefore arrive at similar long-horizon language from different positions.

### Manuscript use

A short paraphrase can help Chapter 10. Do not overquote the AP article. The useful contrast is:

- Olympic deadline creates urgency;
- participants say the sport's actual measure is what survives the deadline.

## 3. August 2004 — Galatis and Samiotes were not peripheral enthusiasts

CNN profiled **Bill Galatis and Chuck Samiotes** shortly before the Athens Olympics.

The report identifies them as best friends, business partners, die-hard Red Sox fans, and would-be Greek Olympic players. More importantly for the book, correspondent Dan Lothian says the pair had been **instrumental in developing baseball in Greece**: they helped build a field, trained young people, and helped recruit players.

Source:
https://transcripts.cnn.com/show/asb/date/2004-08-06/segment/00

### Why this changes the network diagram

The Greece story should not be drawn only as:

**Burns -> Sarbanes -> Angelos -> professional baseball -> Greece**

There was also a Boston Greek-American grassroots route involving Samiotes and Galatis, running alongside and sometimes into the institutional network.

The two routes eventually overlap. They should not be collapsed before the source shows where.

## 4. Galatis's 2004 account supplies motive and sequence in his own lane

A September 2004 *CFO* profile says Galatis's Olympic-baseball involvement began in 1998 after he saw a Greek newspaper notice seeking Greek-American players. Galatis, then a former finance executive and restaurant owner, wanted not merely to make the roster but to write a business plan for the sport.

Source:
https://www.cfo.com/news/a-baseball-odyssey/679439/

The profile also places him in contact with the Angelos-centered effort and records his stated goal that baseball remain alive in Greece after the Games.

### Use

This source is especially valuable because it gives Galatis an independent origin story. He does not enter the project only as somebody Burns recruited.

That reinforces the book's larger point about brokerage: the ambassador's value lies partly in connecting actors who already have motives and capabilities of their own.

## 5. The Bill Galatis identity chain can now be closed at person level

The modern Fenway research previously treated the appearance of a Bill Galatis in Greek Heritage Night reporting as a strong but still partly open identity lead.

A sequence of independent sources now makes the same-person conclusion high confidence:

### 2004 — Olympic-baseball Galatis

CNN identifies Bill Galatis, age fifty, beside Chuck Samiotes as a business partner, Red Sox fan, and Greek-baseball builder.

*CFO* identifies Bill Galatis, age fifty-one, as owner of Mezé Estiatorio and describes the same six-year Greek-baseball effort.

### 2006 — the business-partner bridge

*Boston Magazine* identifies **Bill Galatis and Chuck Samiotes** together as co-owners of Mezé Estiatorio.

Source:
https://www.bostonmagazine.com/reviews/2006/08/08/dining-out-modern-greece/

This is a useful independent bridge because it preserves the Galatis/Samiotes partnership outside sports coverage.

### 2009 — the Somerville / Lyndell's biography

*The Somerville News* profiles Bill Galatis as a man in his mid-fifties, a former corporate accountant and the owner of Lyndell's Bakery, with longstanding Greek Orthodox and philanthropic involvement.

Source:
https://www.thesomervilletimes.com/archives/1914

Lyndell's own history says Galatis became its fourth owner in 2000.

Source:
https://lyndells.com/about/

### 2018 — Galatis personally creates the Agganis / Greek Heritage Fenway event

The strongest later source is a June 2018 *Lynn Journal* interview.

Galatis describes approaching Red Sox historian **Gordon Edes** about honoring Harry Agganis. According to Galatis, Edes took the proposal to senior Red Sox management, the club agreed, and Galatis proposed placing the tribute inside a wider Greek Heritage celebration at Fenway.

Source:
https://lynnjournal.com/2018/06/21/a-labor-of-lovegalatis-worked-hard-to-create-red-sox-tribute-to-harry-agganis/

The same article identifies him as a 1971 Medford High School graduate, which is chronologically consistent with the age reported in 2004.

GreekBoston's May 2018 program listing independently identifies Bill Galatis as **Head-Coordinator of the Boston Red Sox Greek Heritage Night**.

Source:
https://www.greekboston.com/greek-program-tv-show/

### 2024–2026 — institutional memory names Galatis as a pioneer

Modern Alpha Omega / Greek Heritage Night reporting repeatedly describes the current event as building on the foundation laid by pioneers including Bill Galatis and Boston AHEPA.

Examples:
https://www.greeknewsusa.com/alpha-omega-hosts-2024-greek-heritage-night-with-boston-red-sox-at-fenway-park-june-4/
https://archons.org/boston-red-sox-greek-night/
https://greekreporter.com/2026/06/08/alpha-omega-council-celebrates-2026-greek-heritage-night-boston-red-sox/

### Evidentiary conclusion

It is now reasonable to treat the Greek-Olympic-baseball Bill Galatis and the Fenway Greek-Heritage-Night Bill Galatis as the same person without cumbersome same-name caveats.

What remains **unproven** is institutional genealogy.

The fact that Galatis worked on both does not establish that the 2018 Fenway program grew organizationally out of Burns's 1998–2001 embassy project, or that the Red Sox's late-1990s support was routed through Galatis.

That is the next question, not an answer already in hand.

## 6. The later Fenway connection is more direct than previously understood

The 2018 *Lynn Journal* account matters for another reason: Galatis does not merely appear as an attendee or community organizer after the fact.

In his own account, he **made the approach to the Red Sox** about Agganis, worked through Gordon Edes, and proposed the wider Greek Heritage celebration.

This gives the afterlife a named human mechanism:

**Galatis -> Gordon Edes -> Red Sox senior management -> Agganis tribute / Greek Heritage celebration**

That mechanism is documented in 2018.

It must not be projected backward to 1999.

## 7. “Who's on First?” supplies a necessary counter-narrative

Valerie Kontakos's 2006 documentary **Who's on First?** is a high-value archival target because its framing is not celebratory.

The distributor describes a 58-minute Greek/English documentary about Greece's Olympic-baseball construction and says the small group of passionate local Greek athletes were pushed toward the sidelines when Greek-American professionals were recruited to make the team competitive.

Source:
https://www.exile.gr/en/activities/who-s-on-first/

A 2007 film-festival description likewise says the film examines what happened when Greek-American professionals intervened in creation of the national team.

Source:
https://neomagazine.com/2007_10_october/2007_10_film.html

### Why this matters

The book's current chapter is already careful not to declare the Olympic roster an uncomplicated public-diplomacy success. But the documentary creates a concrete route to the people who may have experienced the project as displacement rather than opportunity.

That perspective matters because the diaspora solution contained a real tradeoff:

- recruiting experienced Greek-descended Americans made Olympic competitiveness possible;
- the same strategy could marginalize the local players whose participation had helped make a Greek baseball community possible in the first place.

### Guardrail

Do **not** adopt the distributor's “scam” tag or the film's implied judgment as established fact without viewing the documentary and checking the underlying participants and chronology.

For now this is a source lead and evidence that a serious counter-narrative exists.

## 8. Chapter 10 should redistribute agency

The manuscript already says a weaker version of the story would make Burns the hero simply because he is the subject of the book.

The new evidence gives that sentence more documentary force.

A stronger chapter should make visible at least four distinct kinds of agency:

1. **Greek domestic organizers** — federation, fields, government, local athletes.
2. **Embassy / political brokerage** — Burns, Sarbanes, access, legitimacy, convening.
3. **Professional-baseball infrastructure** — MLB International, Angelos/Orioles, Derksen, scouts/coaches.
4. **Greek-American grassroots labor** — Samiotes, Galatis and others teaching, building, recruiting and sustaining interest.

The four lanes overlap. None should be allowed to absorb the others.

## 9. Manuscript opportunity — a short afterlife, not a triumphal ending

If Chapter 10 carries an afterlife, Galatis gives it a better one than an anonymous institutional coda.

A defensible ending sequence is:

- in the late 1990s Galatis and Samiotes were among the Boston-area Greek Americans helping build Greek baseball;
- in 2018 the same Galatis approached the Red Sox about honoring Harry Agganis and helped create the club's wider Greek Heritage celebration;
- by 2024 current Greek national-team players and a 2004 Olympian-turned-manager were being honored on the Fenway field during that Greek celebration.

Then state the boundary explicitly:

**That is continuity in people, place, and cultural memory. It is not yet proof of continuity in the Red Sox institution's specific support for Burns's Greek project.**

## 10. Highest-value next archival/interview targets

### Bill Galatis

A direct Galatis interview is now one of the highest-value remaining sources in the entire baseball book. Specific questions:

1. When did he first meet or communicate with Nicholas Burns?
2. Did Burns or embassy staff ever ask him directly for help?
3. What did Galatis understand the **Boston Red Sox** to have pledged or provided in 1999–2000?
4. Which Red Sox employee or executive was the contact — Dick Bresciani, John Harrington, Dan Duquette, someone else?
5. Did Galatis retain correspondence, photographs, invitations, rosters, budgets, or equipment records from the Greece project?
6. Did any relationships from the Greece-baseball effort carry into his 2018 approach to Gordon Edes and the Red Sox?
7. Does he regard Greek Heritage Night as connected to the Olympic-baseball work, or as a separate Agganis/Hellenic initiative?

### Chuck Samiotes

Ask separately about:

- the 1997 family-baseball episode;
- the 1998 return with equipment;
- when he first encountered Burns, federation officials, Galatis, Angelos, MLB, and Red Sox personnel;
- what was built locally before the Olympic-team recruitment accelerated;
- the later documentary and whether he retained source material.

### Gordon Edes / Red Sox archive

The 2018 Galatis account proves Edes was a real route into Red Sox senior management for Greek-American baseball/cultural programming in the modern era.

Ask whether club archives contain older Greece-related correspondence and whether Edes inherited institutional memory from Dick Bresciani or other longtime public-affairs personnel.

### Valerie Kontakos / documentary archive

Obtain a screening copy, transcript, interview rushes if preserved, participant list, and production notes for *Who's on First?* The unused interview material could be more valuable than the finished film.

## Documentary rule

The Greece project did not have one founder, one beginning, or one clean line of causation.

Do not convert Galatis's later Fenway work into proof of the old Red Sox pledge. Do not convert Burns's brokerage into ownership of grassroots work that preceded or paralleled it. Do not convert the documentary's critical framing into adjudicated fact before viewing and sourcing it.

The better story is a network whose participants entered for different reasons, did different work, and remembered the result differently.
