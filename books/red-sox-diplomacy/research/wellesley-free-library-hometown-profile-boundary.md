# Wellesley Free Library hometown profile: useful provenance, not youth-baseball proof

## Source

Wellesley Free Library, “Nick Burns,” Wellesley High School Author profile:
https://www.wellesleyfreelibrary.org/authors/nick-burns/

Accessed 2026-09-25.

## What the institutional profile establishes

The Wellesley Free Library identifies Nicholas Burns as Wellesley High School class of 1974 and says he “grew up on Shirley Road.” It also describes his lifelong pride in Red Sox Nation and, in a parenthetical retrospective, says that when he served as State Department spokesperson he often opened briefings with updates on Red Sox scores.

The profile also places his AFS Luxembourg year in high school and records that he later returned to participate in the “Turn Out the Lights” farewell program for the old Wellesley High School.

This is useful hometown/institutional evidence because it joins a specific Wellesley geography, school chronology, and later Red Sox identity in one local source.

## Evidentiary boundary

This source does **not** say that Burns played Little League, Babe Ruth League, town baseball, Wellesley High baseball, or any other organized baseball in Wellesley. It does not identify a coach, teammate, field, season, game, position, uniform, family baseball ritual, or childhood Red Sox game.

Do not convert “grew up on Shirley Road” plus lifelong Red Sox fandom into a youth-baseball scene.

The statement about Burns opening State briefings with Red Sox-score updates is retrospective institutional prose, not a first-person Burns recollection. It should be corroborated against the surviving State Department briefing transcripts before being quoted or promoted as a recurring practice. The repository already contains primary-source work on 1997 State Department baseball transcripts; use those records for any manuscript claim that can be established directly.

## Safe manuscript use

If useful for geographic specificity, narrator prose may say that a Wellesley Free Library profile places Burns’s childhood on Shirley Road and identifies him as Wellesley High School class of 1974. Keep the baseball proposition separate: the same local institutional profile describes him as a lifelong member of Red Sox Nation.

Do not use this source to narrate Burns playing baseball as a child.
