# Deep Baseball Primary-Source Pass 03 — suffering after redemption, briefing-room religion, and the Red Sox institutional trail

This pass goes after material that changes interpretation rather than merely adding more examples of Nicholas Burns saying he likes the Red Sox. The most useful findings sharpen three things: how deep the briefing-room idiom had become by 1997; how Burns himself described Red Sox identity *after* the 2004 and 2007 championships; and where the still-unresolved Red Sox/Greece institutional paper trail is most likely to sit.

## 1. June 4, 1997 — “Bitter in Boston” was more textured than the existing summary

The official State Department briefing transcript preserves a much more specific Burns register than a generic “long-suffering fan” label.

Asked about Roger Clemens, Burns says Boston is still bitter, calls the Red Sox a team in need of help, and then jokes:

> “We’re saying our Novenas for the Red Sox.”

He continues with mock-Catholic language about resuscitation and the patron saint of lost causes.

Source:
https://1997-2001.state.gov/briefings/9706/970604db.html

### Why this matters

This is useful because the comic language is locally specific rather than generic sports talk. Burns is not merely saying the team loses. He reaches for New England Catholic devotional language to dramatize the condition of Red Sox fandom.

That should not be inflated into a claim about Burns’s religion or theology. The transcript only establishes the joke. But it gives Chapter 8 better social texture: the public Red Sox persona could pull from family, region, ritual, and inherited language without becoming a prepared branding device.

### Manuscript use

A short quotation is enough. Do not reproduce the whole exchange. The most useful sequence is:

- reporters trigger Clemens / Red Sox talk;
- Burns says Boston is bitter;
- one short novena line;
- paraphrase the “lost causes” joke;
- return immediately to the point that the room knew how to enter and exit this register.

## 2. March 28, 2012 — after two championships, Burns still defined Red Sox Nation by suffering

A Chicago Council / Atlantic Council transcript gives a surprisingly important retrospective statement.

Marshall Bouton introduces Burns by teasing his Red Sox allegiance. Burns replies with the Yankees joke, notes that Boston had sent Theo Epstein to Chicago, says Epstein remade the Red Sox and that Boston had won two World Series, and then says:

> “the thing about Red Sox Nation is, we identify with suffering.”

He uses that identification to explain sympathy for Cubs fans.

Source:
https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/03/Burns_Transcript.pdf

### Why this changes the post-2004 interpretation

The existing book arc correctly treats 2004 as “redemption,” but this 2012 evidence shows the championship did **not** erase the cultural identity built during the drought. Burns could acknowledge two titles and still define Red Sox Nation through remembered suffering.

That gives Chapter 16 a better answer to its core question: what happens to an identity partly built around losing when the losing ends?

At least in Burns’s public language, the answer is not that the identity disappears. The old suffering becomes historical memory and social inheritance. It can even be extended outward—in this case toward Cubs fans—without denying the championships.

### Guardrail

This is a 2012 retrospective statement, not evidence for Burns’s exact emotional state on October 27, 2004. It deepens the aftermath; it does not close the championship-night gap.

## 3. 2007 — State Magazine independently frames Red Sox fandom as training in patience

A State Department *State Magazine* profile of Burns introduces him as the senior career Foreign Service officer and then jokes that he arrived “well seasoned in the art of patience” as a card-carrying member of Red Sox Nation.

Source:
https://2001-2009.state.gov/documents/organization/91629.pdf

### Why it matters

This is an institutional witness rather than Burns describing himself. It shows that by the late Under Secretary period, colleagues/editors inside the Department regarded the Red Sox identity as sufficiently established to use it as shorthand in an official employee publication.

Use carefully. The magazine’s joke does **not** prove baseball made Burns more patient as a diplomat. It proves only that the association between Burns, Red Sox endurance, and patience was legible to the institution around him.

## 4. 2024–2025 — the identity remains immediately legible in teaching/public settings

Harvard Kennedy School’s 2024 announcement for Burns’s graduation address describes his lifelong Red Sox devotion as his “only lapse” from non-partisan diplomacy.

Source:
https://www.hks.harvard.edu/announcements/us-ambassador-china-nicholas-burns-deliver-2024-harvard-kennedy-school-graduation

A 2025 Davidson College student account then shows the identity functioning at very small scale rather than only in formal introductions: when Burns learned the student was also from Boston, they traded quick takes on the Red Sox–Yankees series before the discussion moved into policy analysis.

Source:
https://www.davidson.edu/news/2025/09/23/my-day-davidson-ambassador-china-nicholas-burns

### Use

Together, these sources strengthen the claim that the Red Sox identity survived every institutional transition: spokesman, ambassador, Under Secretary, professor, ambassador again, and returning professor. More importantly, it still worked both as public shorthand and as ordinary interpersonal small talk.

Do not claim that the baseball exchange caused rapport or better discussion. The Davidson source supports only the narrower sequence: common Boston identity -> quick baseball exchange -> substantive conversation.

## 5. Red Sox/Greece institutional trail — the public record now points more sharply toward public-affairs/archive files

The exact Boston Red Sox contribution to Greek baseball remains unresolved. The contemporaneous evidence already in the project establishes that the club was helping / had pledged support in 1999–2000, but it does not say what the support consisted of.

The next archival search can now be narrowed by the Red Sox front-office structure of that period.

### Dick Bresciani

The Red Sox’s own 2014 memorial says Bresciani had been with the club since 1972, served as vice president of public relations from 1987, and became vice president of public affairs in 1996. It also describes him as an institutional-memory figure; later in his career he oversaw publications and archives. He was therefore in exactly the functional area where correspondence about an ambassador, an international public-diplomacy project, publicity, community outreach, or a club-support pledge might plausibly have been routed.

Source:
https://www.mlb.com/news/longtime-red-sox-public-relations-head-dick-bresciani-passes-away/c-102858356

This is **not evidence that Bresciani personally worked on Greek baseball**. It is evidence that his office/files are a higher-value archival target than a generic search of baseball-operations records.

### Dan Duquette / baseball operations

The Red Sox Living Museum identifies Dan Duquette as general manager from 1994–2002 and notes that the club strengthened international scouting during his tenure.

Source:
https://www.mlb.com/redsox/ballpark/museum/personalities/general-managers

That makes Duquette-era baseball-operations files worth checking, especially if the promised assistance involved scouting or instruction. But the current evidence does not show that it did.

### John Harrington / ownership-executive layer

The Red Sox’s own timeline identifies John Harrington as the club’s CEO in 2000, during the exact period when press reports said the team had pledged support to the Greek effort.

Source:
https://www.mlb.com/redsox/ballpark/museum/timeline/2000-2009

Again, this is organizational context, not proof of involvement. The practical archival point is that a search request should include Harrington, Bresciani, Duquette, Nicholas Burns, Panos Mitsiopoulos, Peter Angelos, Hellenic Amateur Baseball Federation, MLB International, Greece, Athens 2004, and “Friends of Greek Baseball.”

## 6. October 2004 — useful negative evidence, but still no championship-night scene

The archived State Department index for Burns’s NATO remarks shows a public appearance on October 13, 2004, then no indexed Burns speech again until November 4 in Moscow and November 8 in Berlin. NATO’s own late-October public schedule likewise contains alliance events but no obvious Burns public transcript tied to October 27–28.

Sources:
- https://2001-2009.state.gov/p/eur/rls/rm/c11132.htm
- https://www.nato.int/docu/pr/2004/index.html
- https://www.nato.int/docu/speech/2004/index.html

### Interpretation

This does **not** establish that Burns was in Brussels on championship night, nor that he had no official duties. It only tightens the public-source problem: there is no obvious indexed speech or press appearance on October 27–28 from which a Red Sox reaction can be recovered.

The gap should therefore remain open until one of the following appears:

- U.S. Mission NATO calendar / representational schedule;
- Burns correspondence or email;
- embassy residence guest record;
- colleague diary/interview;
- local Brussels press;
- direct Burns recollection.

## 7. Editorial consequences

This pass produces three useful changes in emphasis.

First, **1997 fandom is more culturally specific than “long-suffering fan.”** The novena / lost-cause joke gives the briefing-room material a Boston/New England texture that does not need invention.

Second, **2004 redemption did not erase suffering as identity.** Burns’s 2012 statement is the cleanest evidence yet that the drought remained part of how he defined Red Sox Nation after the championships.

Third, **the Greece club-assistance search is no longer a generic “ask the Red Sox archive” target.** Bresciani/public-affairs files are now the most functionally plausible first stop, with Harrington executive files and Duquette/baseball-operations files as secondary tracks.

The unresolved items remain unresolved. This pass narrows them rather than filling them with inference.
