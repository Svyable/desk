# China 2024 — Beijing baseball clinic image provenance and public-diplomacy boundary

## Finding

A surviving U.S. government image from October 22, 2024 provides unusually clean visual evidence for Nicholas Burns's baseball-facing public diplomacy in China.

Wikimedia Commons preserves the image and its source metadata under the title **“Ambassador Burns with Chinese baseball fans.png.”** The file description says Burns stands with Chinese baseball fans wearing a jersey and Boston Red Sox cap and holding a baseball. It preserves the text of Burns's official ambassador-account post: “Great to see so many young Chinese friends attend our Baseball Clinic in Beijing. Future members of @Redsox Nation.”

## Date, authorship, and rights

The Commons record dates publication to **October 22, 2024** and identifies **Ambassador Nicholas Burns** as author/source account. It marks the image as public domain because it is a work of a U.S. Department of State employee made as part of official duties, citing the federal-government copyright rule.

The preserved file is 875 × 657 pixels (PNG, approximately 1.34 MB). The Commons page links to the original file and records the upload provenance back to the official `@USAmbChina` post.

This is therefore more useful than an unattributed social-media screenshot: it supplies a durable image record, publication date, original-source pointer, and an explicit rights classification suitable for a publication image ledger. Final book production should still retain the source URL and rights note with the image asset.

## What the image establishes

The source directly supports these bounded facts:

1. Burns participated in a baseball clinic in Beijing that his official account described as attended by young Chinese participants.
2. He appeared in Red Sox-branded clothing in the published image.
3. His official public language explicitly connected the clinic participants to “Redsox Nation.”
4. The image was published through his official ambassador account while he was serving in China.

This is strong evidence of **baseball and Red Sox identity being used in outward-facing embassy public communication**, rather than merely evidence that Burns privately remained a fan.

## What it does not establish

Do not use this image or caption to claim:

- that the Boston Red Sox organization sponsored, funded, staffed, or formally partnered in the Beijing clinic;
- that any participant actually became a Red Sox fan;
- that the clinic changed Chinese opinion of the United States or affected bilateral policy;
- that Burns personally coached a particular participant unless another image/video or account documents the act;
- what Chinese participants privately thought or felt;
- that the event was organized in response to any specific diplomatic dispute;
- any motive beyond the observable official act and published language.

The phrase “Future members of @Redsox Nation” is Burns's public-facing caption, not evidence about participants' beliefs.

## Manuscript opportunity

This evidence is strong enough to support a compact, attributed China-era beat if the manuscript does not already carry it: by October 2024 Burns's Red Sox identity was not merely biographical background. His official ambassador account used it in a Beijing youth baseball setting, with a government-produced image showing the ambassador in Red Sox gear among Chinese baseball participants.

The argument should stay observable: baseball furnished a public cultural vocabulary for an embassy event. Whether that vocabulary produced durable diplomatic effects is a separate empirical question.
