# October 2004 — NATO calendar boundary around the Red Sox championship

## Finding

A renewed search for contemporaneous October 2004 records still does **not** produce an authenticated Nicholas Burns account of where or how he watched the Red Sox win the World Series on October 27, 2004. It does, however, tighten the documentary calendar around the championship.

On October 12, 2004, contemporaneous Associated Press/Washington Post reporting places Burns in **Poiana Brasov, Romania**, traveling in the orbit of Defense Secretary Donald Rumsfeld and briefing American reporters before NATO defense-minister talks. Burns discussed the proposed integration of the U.S.-led and NATO missions in Afghanistan and said the issue would be discussed the following day.

Source:
- HeraldNet / AP and Washington Post, “U.S. asks NATO to take command in Afghanistan,” October 12, 2004: https://www.heraldnet.com/2004/10/12/u-s-asks-nato-to-take-command-in-afghanistan/

A second dated source preserves an exclusive interview with Burns published October 23, 2004 concerning Armenia’s relationship with NATO. The interview identifies him as U.S. Ambassador to NATO and discusses Partnership for Peace, Kosovo participation, and Armenia’s Individual Partnership Action Plan.

Source:
- Mediamax, “NATO's special focus on the Caucasus should not raise concerns in Moscow,” October 23, 2004: https://mediamax.am/en/news/interviews/54/

These records place Burns in an active NATO diplomatic calendar in the same month Boston completed its championship run, but neither establishes his location on October 27.

## Why this matters

The October 2004 gap is tempting precisely because the later biography makes the emotional importance of the Red Sox obvious. The archival record currently supports a more disciplined contrast: Burns was serving as U.S. ambassador to NATO during an unusually active alliance period, and dated October records show him conducting NATO business shortly before Boston’s championship. What is missing is the bridge between those two facts.

That missing bridge should remain visible in the manuscript rather than be filled by reconstruction.

## What can be said safely

- Burns was U.S. Ambassador to NATO in October 2004.
- On October 12 he was documented in Poiana Brasov, Romania, briefing reporters in connection with NATO defense-minister meetings.
- An Armenia/NATO interview with him was published October 23.
- The Red Sox won the 2004 World Series on October 27.
- No source recovered in this pass establishes Burns’s physical location, viewing circumstances, companions, communications, or reaction on October 27.

## What must not be inferred

Do **not** turn the nearby NATO records into claims that Burns:

- watched the clinching game in Brussels, Romania, or elsewhere;
- watched with NATO colleagues, embassy/mission staff, family, or friends;
- used Armed Forces television, satellite television, radio, internet scores, or any particular medium;
- stayed awake through the European night to watch the game;
- celebrated at the U.S. Mission to NATO;
- discussed the championship with NATO counterparts;
- connected the championship to his diplomatic work;
- had any particular emotional reaction on October 27 unless an authenticated first-person source supplies it.

No October 2004 memory, reaction, dialogue, or viewing detail should be attributed to Libby Baylies without an authenticated source specifically from her or clearly identifying her as the source.

## Best next retrieval targets

1. U.S. Mission to NATO public-affairs files, staff newsletters, embassy/mission community bulletins, or internal publications from October–November 2004.
2. State Department oral histories or exit interviews with Burns and named USNATO colleagues that mention the 2004 postseason.
3. Boston/New England press interviews with Burns from late October or November 2004.
4. Television/radio appearances immediately after the championship, especially Boston outlets interviewing prominent expatriate New Englanders.
5. Authenticated personal correspondence, diaries, or family material if voluntarily made available for publication.

Until one of those sources appears, October 27, 2004 should remain deliberately offstage.
