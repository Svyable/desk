# Chapter 2 visual archive — The Useful Fan

## Method

Chapter 2 should not become a highlight reel. Its visual sequence has one job: make the chapter's central distinction visible — the personal identity is durable, but the authority belongs to the office.

The chapter therefore uses four kinds of evidence in chronological movement:

1. a **room** that remembers Burns when he is not in it;
2. a **real photograph** of Burns inside the NATO institution;
3. a **time-object** built from a documented fact rather than an invented scene;
4. a **network-object** showing how personal baseball fluency became brokerage in Greece.

The China baseball photograph from Chapter 1 is deliberately not repeated. Chapter 2 should advance the archive.

## NETWORK OBJECT 01 — Greece baseball brokerage

**Local media:** `../media/ch02-greece-baseball-network.svg`  
**Sources:**
- ESPN Magazine, “Give ’Em Hellas” — Burns's playing background, batting-cage television demonstration, catch with children, lobbying for a baseball budget.
- Washington Post, September 8, 1999 — Burns/Hellenic federation reception and professional-baseball support.
- Associated Press / Los Angeles Times, January 2, 2000 — Burns as “baseball ambassador” coordinating the effort.
- Christian Science Monitor, August 18, 2004 — Burns describing the project as a U.S.–Greek joint venture.
- Hellenic Baseball Federation history — the Burns → Sarbanes → Angelos/Orioles connection.

**Narrative job:** Show brokerage rather than hero mythology. Burns/Embassy sits at the junction, not at the center of every arrow. MLB International, Greek organizers, diaspora political connections, Peter Angelos/Orioles, coaches, equipment, and the Greek federation retain their separate roles.

**Guardrail:** The exact Boston Red Sox club contribution to the Greece project remains unresolved. The diagram may note documented club support, but it must not assign Burns, a specific Red Sox executive, or the club a contribution not established by the record.

## Formatting rules carried forward

- Direct quotations used as objects are italicized.
- A photograph caption identifies who, where, when, maker/source, rights, and the evidentiary limit of the image.
- A documentary object can be visually dramatic without pretending to be a photograph.
- Negative evidence is allowed onto the page. “We do not know” is part of the archive.
- One strong image or object per narrative movement is preferable to a gallery.
- Remote public-domain photographs remain acceptable for the draft prototype; approved release media should be vendored into `books/red-sox-diplomacy/media/` before publication.
