# Jerusalem 1986 — source triangulation and oral-history targets

## Finding

A further source pass strengthens the institutional frame around Nicholas Burns's 1985–87 Jerusalem posting but still does **not** authenticate a 1986 Red Sox/World Series scene.

Three independent institutional sources converge on the same bounded fact pattern:

1. The George W. Bush White House archive says Burns was Political Officer at the American Consulate General in Jerusalem from 1985 to 1987 and coordinated U.S. economic assistance to Palestinians in the West Bank and East Jerusalem.
2. Harvard Kennedy School/Belfer Center's current biography independently places Burns at the American Consulate General in Jerusalem in 1985–87 coordinating U.S. economic assistance to Palestinians in the West Bank.
3. The American Academy of Diplomacy likewise places Burns at the Consulate General in Jerusalem in 1985–87, coordinating U.S. economic assistance to Palestinians, and separately identifies him as a member of Red Sox Nation.

Sources:
- George W. Bush White House archive, R. Nicholas Burns biography: https://georgewbush-whitehouse.archives.gov/government/nburns-bio.html
- Belfer Center / Harvard Kennedy School, Nicholas Burns biography: https://www.belfercenter.org/person/nicholas-burns
- American Academy of Diplomacy, Nicholas Burns member biography: https://www.academyofdiplomacy.org/members-1/burns/nicholas

## Why this improves the evidence base

The agreement across an official government archive and two diplomatic/academic institutional biographies makes the professional setting unusually secure. It also sharpens the negative boundary: later biographies can place Burns's Jerusalem service and lifelong Red Sox identity on the same page, but none of the sources found in this pass says he watched, listened to, discussed, or otherwise experienced the 1986 World Series in Jerusalem.

The manuscript may therefore confidently establish **where Burns was professionally and what his portfolio was** during the 1986 season. It may not connect that posting to the Mets–Red Sox World Series without a source that actually makes the connection.

## Oral-history route

The Association for Diplomatic Studies and Training (ADST), custodian of the Foreign Affairs Oral History Collection, is a particularly relevant next repository. Burns appears in ADST's current institutional ecosystem, but targeted web searches in this pass did not surface a Burns oral-history transcript covering the Jerusalem posting or a colleague transcript that supplied the missing baseball connection.

Useful next searches should target Consulate General Jerusalem personnel from 1985–87 rather than Burns alone. Search interview transcripts for:
- `Burns` / `Nick Burns` / `Nicholas Burns`;
- `baseball`, `Red Sox`, `World Series`, `Buckner`, `Mets`;
- recreation, television, radio, American community, Marine House, CLO, holiday and social-event references;
- October 1986 specifically.

ADST entry point: https://adst.org/oral-history/

## Manuscript rule

No Jerusalem baseball scene should be promoted on the strength of biographical juxtaposition. A sentence such as “Burns was serving in Jerusalem during Boston's 1986 pennant run” is chronologically supportable if properly sourced; any statement about what he saw, heard, felt, said, did, or shared with family or colleagues during the World Series is not.

## Libby Baylies boundary

Nothing found in this pass authenticates first-person material from Elizabeth/Libby Baylies. Do not infer her location on a particular game night, viewing/listening habits, fandom, reaction, dialogue, or household routine from Burns's posting biography or later family biographical material.

## Retrieval targets

1. ADST Foreign Affairs Oral History interviews of Jerusalem Consulate General officers serving 1985–87.
2. State Department historical personnel/telephone directories to reconstruct the exact Jerusalem staff roster for those years before searching names individually.
3. Consulate General Jerusalem community/CLO newsletters, Marine security-guard social calendars, employee bulletins, photographs, and recreation records.
4. Contemporary October 1986 Boston-area press and Boston College alumni publications for overseas-fan interviews.
5. Authenticated Burns correspondence, diary material, interview transcripts, or recorded oral history explicitly discussing the 1986 Red Sox.

Until one of these routes produces item-level evidence, the gap remains intentionally unresolved.
