# October 2004 Calendar Boundary Pass — What the public record can and cannot place

## Purpose

The manuscript has unusually strong evidence for Nicholas Burns watching the 2003 ALCS Game 7 loss from Brussels, but still lacks a first-person or contemporaneous source for where he watched Boston win the 2004 World Series on October 27, 2004.

This pass narrows the calendar around the championship rather than filling the blank with the 2003 scene.

## Firm public chronology immediately before the championship

### October 12–13, 2004 — Romania / NATO defense ministers

Associated Press / Washington Post reporting published October 12 places Burns in Poiana Brasov, Romania, traveling in connection with the NATO defense ministers meeting. Burns briefed American reporters about the U.S. proposal for NATO eventually to take over the broader Afghanistan mission.

Source:
https://www.heraldnet.com/2004/10/12/u-s-asks-nato-to-take-command-in-afghanistan/

This establishes that Burns was actively traveling on NATO business roughly two weeks before Boston's World Series clincher. It does **not** establish his location on October 27.

### October 23, 2004 — Armenia/NATO interview published

Armenian news agency Mediamax published an exclusive October 2004 interview with Burns on October 23. The interview concerns Armenia-NATO relations, Partnership for Peace, NATO's Caucasus policy, Russia, and alliance transformation.

Source:
https://mediamax.am/en/news/interviews/54/

The page confirms active public NATO work four days before Game 4. Its publication date is not sufficient to infer where Burns physically was when the interview was conducted, and it is not evidence of his location on October 27.

## The remaining October 27 gap

Targeted searches for October 26–29, 2004 have not produced a reliable public schedule, speech, press interview, travel report, NATO event page, or contemporaneous baseball quotation that places Burns during the World Series clincher.

Therefore the manuscript must continue to distinguish:

- **2003:** directly documented Burns first-person memory of watching the ALCS Game 7 loss on Armed Forces television in Brussels at 6:16 a.m.;
- **October 2004:** Red Sox championship occurs during Burns's NATO ambassadorship, but his exact viewing location, companions, medium, and real-time reaction remain unresolved;
- **later retrospective language:** Burns's post-2004 references to the 86-year drought and Red Sox redemption establish what the championship meant in his later public vocabulary, but cannot reconstruct championship night.

## What this rules out

Do not write:

- that Burns watched the 2004 clincher in Brussels;
- that he watched on Armed Forces television;
- that the setting resembled the documented 2003 ALCS morning;
- that NATO colleagues watched with him;
- that he was traveling in Romania or Armenia on October 27 merely because nearby dated reporting concerns those places;
- any real-time celebration, phone call, family exchange, office reaction, or sleep-deprived morning without a source.

## Highest-value next evidence

1. U.S. Mission to NATO ambassador calendar / front-office schedule for October 26–28, 2004.
2. Embassy Brussels or USNATO internal newsletters, staff photographs, or mission cables mentioning the World Series.
3. Oral histories/interviews with USNATO front-office staff who worked with Burns in October 2004.
4. Burns interview specifically asking where he watched Game 4 and whether the mission marked the championship.
5. Boston or national press interviews with Burns in the days immediately after October 27.

## Manuscript consequence

The absence is now narrow enough to be useful. We can place Burns inside a very active NATO month, including Romania travel and an Armenia/NATO interview, without pretending the archive follows him into championship night. That contrast can strengthen the book's documentary ethic: one famous Red Sox loss is preserved down to the minute; the championship that followed remains, for now, offstage.
