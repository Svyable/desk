# Deep Baseball Primary-Source Pass 04 — failed prophecy, player feedback, and baseball as diplomatic social language

This pass concentrates on material that changes the shape of the narrative rather than merely adding more references to Nicholas Burns being a Red Sox fan. The strongest new evidence shows four different functions of the baseball identity: hope before 2004, recognition by the baseball world itself, local/institutional memory in Wellesley, and shared-team language between diplomats.

## 1. May 20, 2002 — an exact pre-redemption prediction at Boston College

Boston College now preserves the full text of Burns's 2002 commencement address. The baseball line is stronger and more precise than later summaries.

After calling Boston home and identifying himself with New England, Burns tells the graduates that the Patriots are Super Bowl champions and then says that, after a pause of 84 years, he predicts as a loyal member of Red Sox Nation that Boston will be home to the World Series champions **that October**.

Source:
https://www.bc.edu/bc-web/bcnews/campus-community/alumni/r--nicholas-burns-2002-commencement-address.html

### Why this matters

The prediction was wrong. The 2002 Red Sox did not reach the postseason. That is useful rather than embarrassing to the book.

It gives the pre-2004 fan identity a documented register of annual optimism, not merely inherited suffering. Burns was not only retrospectively describing the drought after it ended. Two years before the championship, while serving as ambassador to NATO and speaking at his alma mater, he publicly predicted the end.

This helps prevent the book from flattening pre-2004 fandom into permanent gloom. The same archive supports both sides of the Red Sox identity:

- long suffering;
- repeated belief that this might finally be the year.

The chapter on October 2004 can therefore treat the championship as the fulfillment of a prediction Burns had already been willing to make and miss in public.

### Manuscript guardrail

Do not portray the 2002 line as prophetic. Its value is precisely that it failed. It demonstrates hope as a recurring practice.

## 2. May 24, 1997 — WPI puts the Red Sox identity beside Burns's parents

Burns's official WPI commencement transcript contains a compact but useful autobiographical arrangement. He calls himself a native New Englander and a long-suffering Red Sox fan, adding **“especially this year.”** In the same opening, he identifies his parents, Bob and Esther Burns, as present in the audience and notes their Worcester roots.

Source:
https://1997-2001.state.gov/policy_remarks/970524.burns.html

### Why this matters

The importance is not another “long-suffering” quotation by itself. The scene places regional identity, baseball identity, and family physically together in a primary transcript.

That becomes particularly useful when read alongside Burns's June 16, 1997 briefing-room account that his mother supported the Red Sox and his father had warned the children away after the club broke his heart in the 1930s.

The two sources should not be fused into invented dialogue or a reconstructed family scene. But together they establish that in spring 1997 Burns was publicly invoking Red Sox suffering while his parents were literally present at one commencement, and within weeks he supplied the family history behind the allegiance at State.

### Manuscript use

This is potentially useful for Chapter 1 as documentary texture, but only if the chapter benefits from naming Bob and Esther. The existing father/mother briefing-room story is already sufficient for the core point.

## 3. October 1997 — Roger Clemens answers the State Department joke

A Washington Post item from October 1997 adds a surprisingly good coda to the briefing-room Clemens story.

After Burns had left the spokesman's podium and was awaiting Senate confirmation as ambassador to Greece, CBS State Department producer Charles Wolfson — himself described as a long-suffering Red Sox fan — arranged an unusual gift. The Toronto Blue Jays supplied an 8-by-10 photograph of Roger Clemens in his Toronto uniform, signed to Burns with good wishes for the new job and thanks for Burns's earlier “kind words.”

Source:
https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/politics/1997/10/27/a-farewell-curveball-for-states-spokesman/47b5db89-bd64-4361-b19e-161126e91e86/

### Why this matters

This is more than another farewell object.

Burns had used Clemens's departure from Boston as a running joke at an official government podium. The press corps remembered the joke after Burns left the podium. A reporter/producer then carried it into the baseball world, and the player himself participated in the return gesture.

The social loop therefore runs:

**player transaction -> Burns's official-podium joke -> press-corps memory -> player-signed response -> Burns's next diplomatic posting.**

That is unusually clean evidence that the baseball persona had become a shared public ritual rather than a unilateral self-brand.

### Manuscript use

This belongs naturally in Chapter 8 after the July farewell objects. It also creates a nice delayed echo: the Clemens bit did not end when Burns's final State briefing ended.

### Guardrail

The item is a Washington Post political-insider column, not a transcript from Clemens. Attribute the inscription through the Post rather than presenting it as an independently authenticated Clemens letter.

## 4. June 12, 2007 — Red Sox allegiance as diplomat-to-diplomat social glue in Paris

At a State Department event in Paris commemorating the 60th anniversary of the Marshall Plan, Burns opened by praising U.S. Ambassador to France Craig Stapleton. Before moving into the substance of the transatlantic relationship, Burns said they shared a special bond because both supported the Boston Red Sox and opposed the Yankees, whom he jokingly called the “evil empire.”

He then immediately praised Stapleton's work in improving U.S.-French relations.

Source:
https://2001-2009.state.gov/p/us/rm/2007/87176.htm

### Why this matters

The briefing-room material proves that baseball could function as social language with reporters. This Paris scene proves the same register could operate laterally between American diplomats in a formal overseas setting.

The sequence matters:

- shared team allegiance;
- theatrical common enemy;
- transition into praise for actual alliance management.

Do not claim the Red Sox helped repair U.S.-French relations. The source does not support that. The narrower and more useful point is that Burns used baseball to locate a personal relationship inside a serious diplomatic event without confusing the joke with the policy.

### Manuscript use

This belongs in Chapter 19, where the book distinguishes sporting rivalry from strategic rivalry and enmity. It supplies an intermediate case between briefing-room Yankees jokes and geopolitical competition: two diplomats can share a mock enemy while doing the real work of alliance repair.

## 5. Wellesley Free Library — local institutional memory of the Red Sox identity

The Wellesley Free Library maintains a profile of Burns as a Wellesley High School 1974 alumnus. It highlights his lifelong pride in Red Sox Nation and says that, when he was State Department spokesman, he often began briefings with team-score updates.

Source:
https://www.wellesleyfreelibrary.org/nick-burns/

The page also confirms the local educational frame already known from other sources: Wellesley High School class of 1974 and an AFS year abroad in Luxembourg during high school.

### Evidentiary status

This is useful **local institutional memory**, not a substitute for primary transcripts. The briefing archive already proves repeated baseball openings and exchanges, but the library's summary should not be used to invent a frequency count or claim that most briefings began with Sox scores.

Its real value is different: Wellesley's own public-memory institution identifies the Red Sox affiliation as one of the stable traits through which it presents Burns back to his hometown.

## 6. August 2007 — detailed baseball competence, not ceremonial fandom

A State Department policy podcast gives an unusually granular Burns baseball analysis during the 2007 season. He discusses the West Coast schedule, says Boston will avoid another August collapse, calls the bullpen and starting five the best in baseball, emphasizes the increasing importance of pitching in the postseason, and predicts the Sox will reclaim the World Series championship.

Source:
https://2001-2009.state.gov/p/us/rm/2007/90347.htm

Boston did win the 2007 World Series.

### Why this matters

This is useful because it shows Burns talking about contemporary roster construction and pitching rather than simply performing “Red Sox Nation” identity. The fandom had baseball content underneath the symbol.

The book already has this source in the broader 2007 arc; the point of preserving it here is comparative. The 2002 prediction failed. The 2007 prediction succeeded. Neither outcome is evidence of expertise by itself. Together they show a person who kept making baseball judgments in public because he actually followed the team.

## 7. October 2004 gap audit — still a gap

A fresh targeted search for Burns on October 27–29, 2004 did not recover a championship-night source.

The State Department's 2004 European/NATO index shows Burns publicly on October 13 in Romania and again on November 4 in Moscow, but no indexed Burns event on October 27 or 28.

Source:
https://2001-2009.state.gov/p/eur/rt/nato/c11326.htm

A separate State European-affairs index likewise shows other senior officials' activity on October 27–29 but no Burns transcript that closes the gap.

Source:
https://2001-2009.state.gov/p/eur/rls/rm/c11132.htm

An Armenian Mediamax interview with Burns is dated October 23, 2004 and concerns NATO/Caucasus policy. It establishes that Burns remained publicly active in his NATO portfolio immediately before the World Series clincher but does not establish where he was on October 27.

Source:
https://mediamax.am/en/news/interviews/54/

### Consequence

The championship-night scene remains unavailable. The responsible next move is no longer another broad web query. It is mission-level material: USNATO calendars, schedules, residence logs, staff correspondence, or direct witness testimony.

## 8. What this pass changes in the manuscript

The evidence now supports a richer sequence than **suffering -> redemption** alone:

**inheritance -> suffering -> annual hope -> ritualized public identity -> redemption -> remembered suffering -> durable community.**

The 2002 Boston College prediction is especially important because it documents **hope before success**. The Clemens-signed photograph shows the persona becoming reciprocal. The Paris 2007 scene shows it becoming a portable social language among diplomats. The 2012 “identify with suffering” line then shows that winning did not erase the older inheritance.

That sequence is more human and less schematic than treating the Red Sox merely as a metaphor for patience.

## Documentary rule

Do not let improved contextual density tempt the manuscript into reconstructed memory. A documented 2002 prediction is evidence of hope in 2002. A documented 1997 family anecdote is evidence of Burns's own public family story. A signed-photo report is evidence that the press corps and Clemens participated in the joke. None of these licenses an invented living-room scene in 1975, 1978, 1986, or 2004.
