# October 2004 Witness Route Pass 02 — Koenig, the USNATO staff tree, and Truman Hall

## Purpose

Open-web searching still has not recovered Nicholas Burns's location, companions or immediate reaction when the Red Sox won the World Series on October 27, 2004.

The problem is now better treated as a **witness / calendar / residence-record question** than as a search-engine question.

This pass identifies the people and record series most likely to close the missing scene.

## 1. John M. Koenig is the highest-value surviving USNATO witness

Koenig's 2021–22 Association for Diplomatic Studies and Training oral history establishes a relationship with Burns that crossed two postings.

Koenig says that before Burns left Athens for NATO, the two had discussed the possibility that Koenig might later become Burns's deputy in Brussels. When Victoria Nuland left the deputy job, Koenig reminded Burns of the earlier conversation, and Burns put him forward as DCM at USNATO.

Primary oral history:
https://adst.org/OH%20TOCs/Koenig.John.pdf

The relevant oral-history passage is on transcript pages 60–61. Koenig says his USNATO job was roughly **“fifty-fifty”** between mission management and active negotiation.

That combination matters for the 2004 World Series question. The deputy was not merely a policy specialist across the hall; he was helping run the mission Burns led.

### Independent role confirmation

An archived State Department Belgium background note lists:

- Permanent U.S. Representative to NATO — Nicholas Burns;
- Deputy Chief of Mission, USNATO — John M. Koenig.

Source:
https://2009-2017.state.gov/outofdate/bgn/belgium/47409.htm

The University of Washington biography likewise identifies Koenig as former Deputy Permanent Representative to the U.S. Mission at NATO.

Source:
https://jsis.washington.edu/people/john-koenig/

### What Koenig's oral history does NOT contain

Targeted searches of the 143-page transcript found **no occurrence of “Red Sox” or “baseball.”**

The NATO section is rich on alliance work, Iraq, Afghanistan, enlargement, mission management and Brussels family life, but it does not supply the October 2004 baseball scene.

This negative result is useful: Koenig should now be treated as a **direct interview witness**, not mined indefinitely as if the published oral history already contains the answer.

## 2. Koenig was in the right professional position in 2004

Koenig's oral history places him at NATO during the 2004 enlargement period and describes the USNATO structure in detail. He says Burns was open-minded and diplomatic, distinguishes Burns from harder institutional tendencies inside the mission, and repeatedly describes working directly under “Nick.”

A later biographical summary dates Koenig's Deputy U.S. Permanent Representative service in Brussels from **2003 to 2006**.

Source:
https://seattle2020.nwmun.org/special-events/conference-speakers

A 2021 Yale interview separately says the Iraq invasion occurred just as Koenig was going to NATO as deputy chief of mission, again placing him at post before October 2004.

Source:
https://yris.yira.org/europe/a-life-of-public-service-ambassador-john-koenig-on-his-experience-as-an-american-diplomat-in-europe-and-beyond/

### Why this raises his priority

Koenig had:

- a prior close working relationship with Burns in Greece;
- Burns's personal support for the USNATO deputy role;
- mission-management responsibility;
- daily proximity to Burns during the exact championship period.

If a colleague remembers whether Burns watched Game 4 at home, arrived exhausted the next morning, marked the title in the office, or disappeared into a private family celebration, Koenig is a strong candidate.

Do not assume he remembers. Ask.

## 3. The February 2004 staff roster gives a small witness tree

A surviving copy of the State Department's **Key Officers of Foreign Service Posts**, last updated February 18, 2004, lists the USNATO leadership under Burns as:

- AMB — Nicholas R. Burns
- DCM — John M. Koenig
- POL — **John Heffern**
- MGT — **Robert A. Glacel**
- PAO — **Barry Levin**

Mirror of the period key-officer directory:
https://www.callyourlawyers.com/pdfcaselaw/keyoffcr.pdf

The directory is a mirrored copy rather than the preferred State host, so each name/role should be independently confirmed where practical before manuscript use.

### John Heffern

Koenig's oral history independently identifies **John Heffern** as the political adviser / political counselor at USNATO during Koenig's tenure and says the two had frequent conversations about NATO negotiations.

Later State biographies confirm Heffern had an earlier USNATO posting before returning years later as DCM.

Source:
https://2009-2017.state.gov/r/pa/ei/biog/bureau/248317.htm

For the World Series gap, Heffern is a second-tier witness: senior enough to have daily mission contact, but the current public record does not establish social proximity to Burns on October 27.

### Robert A. Glacel

The 2004 directory names Glacel as management officer. Later USNATO records independently place Robert A. Glacel in mission management.

Management staff are important because the missing evidence may be logistical rather than narrative: residence event calendars, vehicle schedules, official-guest movements, staff-duty records, household support, or administrative files.

### Barry Levin

The directory names Levin as Public Affairs Officer. Public-affairs files are worth searching for any internal note, staff newsletter, media-monitoring item or next-day reference to the championship, particularly because Burns's Red Sox identity was publicly legible.

Again, no current evidence says Levin discussed the World Series with Burns.

## 4. Truman Hall is the correct official-residence archive target

State Department material identifies **Truman Hall** as the residence of the U.S. Ambassador to NATO in Brussels.

Source:
https://2001-2009.state.gov/documents/organization/66242.pdf

The residence is a substantial official diplomatic platform rather than simply private housing. Later State material describes it hosting visitors from NATO nations and partner countries.

### A 2005 record identifies a residence manager

The July/August 2005 issue of *State Magazine* names **Cathleen Lambredis** as residence manager for Truman Hall during a student program and describes her teaching formal-dinner place-card preparation there.

Source:
https://2001-2009.state.gov/documents/organization/50902.pdf

A 2006 Art in Embassies publication likewise credits **Cathleen Lambridis** as “Truman Hall Event Coordinator, Residence Manager.”

Source:
https://uss58003.s3.amazonaws.com/uploads-migrated/2016/11/Brussels-NATO-Publication-2006.pdf

### Guardrail

These sources establish her role in **2005–06**, not October 2004.

Do not assume she was residence manager on championship night without a personnel record, direct testimony or earlier dated source.

Nevertheless, she is now a concrete archival/personnel lead for discovering:

- who managed Truman Hall in October 2004;
- whether residence calendars or event books survive;
- whether household staff from Burns's tenure can be identified.

## 5. Koenig's oral history also identifies how mission support worked

Koenig says USNATO shared a **tri-mission management support structure** based at Embassy Brussels with the U.S. Mission to the European Union, while also receiving direct support from the Office of the Defense Advisor.

This matters because the missing calendar or residence record might not be filed under “USNATO” alone.

Possible record homes include:

- USNATO front office;
- Embassy Brussels / Joint Administrative Services;
- Truman Hall residence records;
- tri-mission motor pool / protocol / general services;
- ambassador or DCM office-management files.

The search should therefore not stop if one USNATO records series is silent.

## 6. Highest-value direct questions for John Koenig

Do not ask Koenig to retell Burns's NATO career generally. The oral history already does that.

Ask:

1. **Do you remember where Nick Burns watched Game 4 of the 2004 World Series?**
2. Did Burns usually watch late-night Red Sox postseason games at Truman Hall, elsewhere, or alone?
3. Did you or other USNATO staff ever watch baseball with him?
4. Did Burns come into the mission on October 28 after the championship? What do you remember about that morning?
5. Did the staff mark the Red Sox victory in any way — congratulations, decorations, jokes, email traffic, a staff meeting comment?
6. Who on the mission staff would be most likely to remember: John Heffern, management, public affairs, OMS/front-office staff, residence staff?
7. Who managed Truman Hall in autumn 2004?
8. Were ambassador residence calendars/event books retained by the mission or destroyed at turnover?
9. Did Burns keep a private or official calendar that a researcher could request?
10. Do you recall Libby Baylies Burns or the Burns daughters being present in Brussels that week?

The last question should not be turned into a claim unless Koenig answers from memory or records.

## 9. What the current evidence permits

Safe:

> John Koenig, who had worked with Burns in Greece and became his deputy at USNATO, was in Brussels during the 2004 period and describes his deputy role as split between mission management and negotiation.

Safe:

> The surviving Koenig oral history does not mention the Red Sox or the championship, making him a high-value direct witness rather than a source that already answers the question.

Safe:

> Truman Hall was Burns's official residence, but the public record still does not establish that he watched Game 4 there.

Unsafe:

> Burns watched the clincher at Truman Hall.

Unsafe:

> Koenig watched with him.

Unsafe:

> Residence manager Cathleen Lambredis was on duty that night.

## 10. Research consequence

The October 2004 scene is no longer a vague archival gap.

There is now a concrete witness/records chain:

**Burns -> Koenig -> Heffern / mission management / public affairs -> Truman Hall staff -> tri-mission administrative records.**

That is probably the highest-value route left for recovering the real championship-night scene without fictionalizing it.

## Documentary rule

A witness being in the right building is not evidence that the witness remembers the event.

A residence being Burns's home is not evidence he was there that night.

The value of this pass is that it identifies whom to ask and where to look next.
