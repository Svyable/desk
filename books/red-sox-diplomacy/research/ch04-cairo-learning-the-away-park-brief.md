# Chapter 4 Brief — Cairo: Learning the Away Park

## Status

Research brief only. Do not draft the chapter until the early-posting record is fuller.

## High-confidence facts

- Burns served at the U.S. Embassy in Cairo from 1983 to 1985.
- His official titles were vice consul and staff assistant to the ambassador.
- Official State biographies consistently identify Cairo as his first Foreign Service posting after his earlier internship in Nouakchott.
- Burns spoke Arabic, though the available public sources do not yet establish his exact proficiency during the Cairo years.
- A 1999 contemporaneous profile by David L. Marcus, preserved in a House public-diplomacy hearing record, looks back on Burns's Cairo service and says he stamped passports and helped get American workers out of jail while serving amid major geopolitical issues.

## What the chapter should actually be about

Cairo can become the first chapter where the general “road team” idea meets the ordinary machinery of consular and embassy work.

The temptation will be to narrate grand regional politics from above. Resist that unless the record shows Burns's direct role. His titles suggest a more useful angle: early diplomatic formation through unglamorous tasks, proximity to an ambassador, consular responsibility, and learning how a large embassy actually functions.

A young officer stamping passports and helping Americans in trouble is closer to the real apprenticeship than a retrospective montage of Middle East crises.

## Context to research, not yet to narrate as Burns's experience

The 1983–85 period in Egypt sat inside a difficult regional environment: the aftermath of the Egypt-Israel peace treaty, Lebanon, continuing Arab-Israeli conflict, U.S.-Egypt military and economic ties, and Hosni Mubarak's early presidency. These conditions matter only where sources connect them to Burns's actual work.
