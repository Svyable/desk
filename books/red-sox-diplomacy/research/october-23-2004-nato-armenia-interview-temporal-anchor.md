# October 23, 2004: NATO / Armenia interview as a bounded temporal anchor

## Why this matters

A contemporaneous interview published by Armenian news agency Mediamax on October 23, 2004 places Nicholas Burns publicly at work as U.S. Ambassador to NATO at an unusually useful point in the Red Sox chronology: after Boston completed its ALCS comeback against the Yankees on October 20 and on the date of World Series Game 1.

This is useful as a **temporal and professional-context anchor**, not as evidence of a baseball scene.

## Source

Mediamax, “NATO's special focus on the Caucasus should not raise concerns in Moscow,” exclusive interview with U.S. Ambassador to NATO Nicholas Burns, dated October 23, 2004.

https://mediamax.am/en/news/interviews/54/

The interview is substantive and concerns Armenia-NATO relations, Partnership for Peace, KFOR, the Individual Partnership Action Plan, NATO enlargement, and relations with Russia. Its value here is contemporaneity: it documents Burns speaking in his NATO role during the narrow October 2004 championship window.

## What the source supports

- Burns was serving as U.S. Ambassador to NATO in October 2004.
- Mediamax published an exclusive interview with him dated October 23, 2004.
- His public work in the interview concerned Armenia's developing relationship with NATO and wider alliance policy.
- The dated record gives the manuscript a documentary professional-context marker between the ALCS victory and the World Series.

## What the source does **not** support

Do not use this interview to claim:

- where Burns physically watched any ALCS or World Series game;
- that the interview itself occurred on October 23 rather than merely being published that day;
- that Burns discussed the Red Sox with Mediamax;
- that he was thinking about baseball during the interview;
- any private dialogue, interiority, reaction, joke, celebration, schedule, travel movement, or motive;
- that NATO duties prevented or enabled him to watch a particular game.

The publication date is evidence for the date of the public record, not automatically the date or location of the interview.

## Manuscript use

Potentially useful as one sentence of documentary counterpoint in an October 2004 passage: the public record shows Burns simultaneously legible as a working NATO ambassador during the championship window. If promoted, keep the juxtaposition factual and resist turning simultaneity into a reconstructed scene.

Suggested bounded formulation:

> The surviving public record does not tell us where Burns watched the decisive games. It does show the other life running alongside them: on October 23, the day the World Series opened in Boston, Mediamax published an interview with the U.S. ambassador to NATO about Armenia, alliance integration, peacekeeping and Russia.

Before using the sentence verbatim, confirm the manuscript's exact-quotation/source-note gate and cite the Mediamax interview as a publication-date anchor.
