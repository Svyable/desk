# Greece: contemporaneous evidence of Boston Red Sox institutional assistance

## Why this note exists

Earlier research correctly separated Nicholas Burns's Red Sox fandom from documented Orioles and Major League Baseball assistance to the Greek Olympic baseball project. Two contemporaneous reports now narrow the unresolved institutional question further: they explicitly name the Boston Red Sox as an organization helping, or pledging support to, the Greek effort.

This is a meaningful correction to the previous negative boundary. It still does not tell us what the Red Sox actually delivered.

## Contemporary evidence

### Washington Post — September 8, 1999

A Washington Post sports brief on the Greek baseball project reported that Orioles owner Peter Angelos was leading the effort and was being helped by the commissioner's office **and the Boston Red Sox**, identified parenthetically as Burns's favorite team.

Source:

- *The Washington Post*, “Injuries to Teens Are Still a Concern,” September 8, 1999, Olympics/Baseball in Greece item.
- https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/sports/1999/09/08/injuries-to-teens-are-still-a-concern/fe2eb9f0-7fc2-4b43-80a4-a9cb6ac94670/

What this establishes: by September 1999, a contemporaneous American newspaper reported Boston Red Sox organizational involvement in the effort.

What it does **not** establish: the form, value, timing, personnel, equipment, money, scouting, coaching, facilities, introductions, or other deliverable behind “being helped.”

### Associated Press / Los Angeles Times — January 2, 2000

An Associated Press report published by the *Los Angeles Times* described Burns coordinating efforts to build a Greek team, MLB International planning coaching/administrative assistance, Peter Angelos undertaking recruitment, and then stated that **support had also been pledged by the Boston Red Sox**.

Source:

- Theodora Tongas, Associated Press, “Game of Baseball Is All Greek to Them,” *Los Angeles Times*, January 2, 2000.
- https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-2000-jan-02-mn-49873-story.html

This independently corroborates the existence of Red Sox institutional support, at least at the level of a pledge, within months of the Washington Post item.

Again, it does not identify the promised or delivered support.

## Evidentiary conclusion

The manuscript and research ledger should no longer say there is no evidence of Boston Red Sox organizational assistance to the Greek baseball project.

A defensible formulation is:

> Contemporary reporting in 1999 and 2000 said the Boston Red Sox were helping, or had pledged support to, the effort to build a Greek Olympic baseball program. The surviving reports do not specify what the club promised or ultimately delivered.

That is materially different from the much better documented Orioles/MLB chain. Peter Angelos, Orioles scout Rob Derksen, MLB International, recruitment, coaching, equipment/development claims, and later passport assistance have more specific documentary support. The Red Sox evidence presently establishes institutional involvement or a pledge, **not a particular deliverable**.

## Manuscript-use boundary

Safe:
- state that contemporary reports named the Red Sox as institutional supporters;
- distinguish that support from Burns's personal fandom;
- note that the exact Red Sox contribution remains unresolved;
- use the episode to show that Burns's baseball network in Greece was not confined to the Orioles.

Not safe without further evidence:
- say Burns personally caused the Red Sox to participate;
- identify any Red Sox executive, employee, scout, coach, player, payment, equipment shipment, clinic, field project, recruiting action, or other contribution;
- claim the Red Sox fulfilled the reported pledge;
- infer motives, private conversations, reactions, or diplomatic effects;
- turn “support” into a quantified or concrete contribution.

Nothing in these reports authenticates first-person material from Libby Baylies.

## High-value next retrievals

1. Search Boston Globe and Boston Herald archives, September 1999–2001, for Greek Olympic baseball / Burns / Angelos / Red Sox.
2. Search Red Sox media guides, community-relations annual material, press releases, and front-office correspondence from 1999–2001.
3. Identify the Red Sox ownership/front-office contact in the Harrington era who would have handled international/community assistance.
4. Search MLB International records and contemporary Jim Small interviews for club-by-club assistance.
5. Search Hellenic Amateur Baseball Federation correspondence for Red Sox letterhead, equipment receipts, shipping records, introductions, photographs, or acknowledgments.
6. Search Embassy Athens public-diplomacy/representational files for Red Sox contacts or deliverables.
7. Treat later retrospective claims as corroboration only until matched to contemporaneous records.

## Attribution rule

The two newspaper reports support the narrow institutional proposition above. They do not authenticate dialogue or Burns's private recollection. Any later oral history must remain attributed to its speaker. No statement may be assigned to Libby Baylies unless it is independently authenticated as hers.
