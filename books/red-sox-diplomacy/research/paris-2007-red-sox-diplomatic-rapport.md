# Paris 2007 — Red Sox allegiance as diplomatic rapport

## Finding

On June 12, 2007, Under Secretary of State for Political Affairs R. Nicholas Burns spoke at the Automobile Club of France in Paris at a commemoration of the 60th anniversary of the Marshall Plan. The State Department transcript preserves an unusually clean example of Burns using Red Sox allegiance as interpersonal diplomatic framing.

Burns introduced U.S. Ambassador to France Craig Stapleton by saying the two had a “very special bond”: both supported the Boston Red Sox and opposed the New York Yankees. He immediately moved from that baseball identification to praise Stapleton as a representative of the United States who had helped calm U.S.–French relations.

Primary source:
- U.S. Department of State, “Commemoration of the 60th Anniversary of the Marshall Plan,” June 12, 2007: https://2001-2009.state.gov/p/us/rm/2007/87176.htm

## Why this matters

This is stronger than a generic proof that Burns was a fan. The baseball reference appears in a formal diplomatic speech in Paris, attached directly to Burns’s introduction of another American ambassador and to the repair of a consequential bilateral relationship.

It supports a bounded manuscript argument already visible elsewhere in the record: Burns repeatedly used Red Sox identity as a low-cost, human-scale way to establish affiliation before returning to official business. Here the mechanism is visible in the transcript itself rather than reconstructed from later memory.

The scene is especially useful because the setting is not Boston, a baseball event, or a press briefing. It is a Marshall Plan commemoration in France. The Red Sox/Yankees language therefore travels with Burns into a ceremonial foreign-policy setting.

## Attribution boundary

The transcript establishes what Burns said publicly. It does **not** establish:

- that baseball materially improved U.S.–French relations;
- that Stapleton and Burns first bonded through baseball;
- that Stapleton shared Burns’s intensity of fandom beyond Burns’s public description;
- that French listeners understood or welcomed the Yankees joke;
- any private baseball conversation between Burns and Stapleton;
- any causal relationship between Red Sox affinity and diplomatic outcomes.

The manuscript may describe Burns *using* baseball to frame rapport because that act is directly observable in the transcript. It should not convert that rhetorical act into an outcome claim.

## Manuscript use

Best use: a compact scene or supporting beat in a chapter about the “useful fan,” diplomatic persona, or baseball as portable identity. The sequence is unusually efficient:

1. formal Marshall Plan anniversary in Paris;
2. Burns identifies a “special bond” with Ambassador Stapleton;
3. the bond is Red Sox allegiance and Yankees rivalry;
4. Burns pivots immediately to Stapleton’s diplomatic work with France.

That juxtaposition lets the manuscript demonstrate, rather than theorize, how baseball could sit beside high diplomacy without pretending it caused the diplomacy.

## Libby Baylies boundary

This source says nothing about Elizabeth/Libby Baylies and should not be used to infer her fandom, reactions, attendance, or private conversations.
