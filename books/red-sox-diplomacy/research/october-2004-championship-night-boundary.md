# October 2004 championship-night boundary and the March 2005 primary-source aftermath

## Finding

The October 2004 gap remains real. Targeted searches did not surface a contemporaneous source that places Nicholas Burns at a particular television, room, gathering, or NATO setting during the Red Sox comeback against the Yankees or the World Series clincher.

The strongest Burns-specific evidence immediately around the championship remains asymmetrical:

1. **October 2003 is directly documented.** Burns later told ESPN that, while U.S. ambassador to NATO in Brussels, he watched Game 7 of the 2003 ALCS on Armed Forces television and remembered Aaron Boone's walk-off occurring at 6:16 a.m. local time.
2. **October 2004 is not directly documented at scene level.** The baseball record establishes what the Red Sox did, but not where or how Burns experienced it.
3. **March 23, 2005 is a primary-source aftermath.** In an official State Department transcript of remarks at the U.S. Institute of Peace, Burns called himself a “native-born member of Red Sox Nation” and said New Englanders had “indeed found redemption” after defeating the Yankees and Cardinals.

Primary source:
- U.S. Department of State archive, Nicholas Burns, remarks at the U.S. Institute of Peace, Washington, March 23, 2005.
- https://2001-2009.state.gov/p/us/rm/2005/45781.htm

Earlier firsthand account:
- ESPN Magazine, “Give 'Em Hellas.”
- https://www.espn.co.uk/espn/magazine/archives/news/story?page=magazine-20040119-article32

## Why the March 2005 transcript matters

The State transcript is not merely evidence that Burns knew the Red Sox won. His language is useful because it is Burns's own public framing after the championship and connects directly to a recurring rhetorical register in the manuscript: Red Sox suffering, New England Calvinism, and eventual redemption.

The official transcript also records audience applause after Burns identified himself with Red Sox Nation. That is evidence of a public room responding to the baseball reference; it should not be inflated into evidence about the identity or fandom of individual attendees.

This gives the manuscript a documentary endpoint for the 2003–04 sequence without requiring a reconstructed championship-night scene.

## Manuscript-safe construction

A defensible sequence is:

- use Burns's directly attributed 2003 Brussels/Armed Forces Television account as the expatriate viewing scene;
- narrate the 2004 postseason from baseball records where needed;
- state plainly that no sourced October 2004 Burns viewing scene has yet been located;
- land the sequence in Burns's March 2005 State Department remarks, where he publicly described the championship as “redemption.”

This is stronger than inventing continuity between 2003 and 2004. It shows what Burns actually said before and after the championship while preserving the missing middle.

## Do not infer

The evidence does **not** establish:

- that Burns watched any 2004 postseason game on Armed Forces television;
- that he watched in Brussels, at the U.S. Mission to NATO, at home, or with colleagues or family;
- that he stayed awake for the live European-time broadcasts;
- his immediate reaction on October 20, October 27, or October 28, 2004;
- any dialogue, celebration, ritual, email, phone call, wager, gift, or NATO-colleague reaction;
- that Libby Baylies watched with him or shared his baseball preferences.

The 2003 viewing method must not be silently transferred into 2004.

## Archival targets that could close the gap

1. U.S. Mission to NATO newsletters, internal bulletins, public-affairs material, staff photographs, and embassy/community publications from October–November 2004.
2. Stars and Stripes / AFN Europe schedules and features around October 17–28, 2004, but only as broadcast context—not proof Burns watched.
3. Brussels diplomatic-community or American-club newsletters.
4. Authenticated Burns correspondence, calendars, diaries, emails, or oral-history interviews covering October 2004.
5. Interviews with named U.S. Mission NATO colleagues, with recollections attributed to the speaker rather than converted into omniscient narration.
6. Boston/New England profiles of Burns published immediately after the championship and before his March 2005 move to Washington.

## Research status

**Primary-source aftermath promotable; championship-night scene unresolved.**

The March 23, 2005 State transcript can carry Burns's own retrospective meaning. Until a contemporaneous or authenticated first-person source emerges, October 2004 itself should remain deliberately unscened.
