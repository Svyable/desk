# State Department Red Sox Nation primary-record pass

## Finding

The archival target left by the previous official-biography pass can now be narrowed with a primary U.S. government record.

A preserved Department of State biography of Nicholas Burns, hosted on the Department's 2009–2017 archive, explicitly lists him as a member of **Red Sox Nation**. The page is an official State Department biography from Burns's post-Foreign-Service period, when he served on Secretary John Kerry's Foreign Affairs Policy Board.

Primary source:
https://2009-2017.state.gov/s/p/fapb/185577.htm

This does not recover the exact 2007–08 biography described by the Washington Post at Burns's retirement. It does something nearly as useful: it independently establishes, in the Department's own later biographical record, that the Red Sox identifier was retained as part of Burns's official public biography after his career service ended.

## Why this improves the evidentiary chain

The prior pass depended on a January 2008 Washington Post report saying Burns's official State Department biography identified him with Red Sox Nation. That contemporary report remains evidence for the 2008 biography. The later archived State page is separate primary corroboration from the institution itself.

The two records should not be collapsed into one document:

1. **January 2008:** the Washington Post reports that Burns's then-current official biography notes his Red Sox Nation membership.
2. **Later State archive:** an official State Department biography preserved in the 2009–2017 archive directly lists Burns as a member of Red Sox Nation.

Together they support a stronger, carefully bounded proposition: Red Sox identity was not merely a reporter's flourish or an isolated briefing-room joke. It persisted in official State Department biographical language across Burns's transition from senior career diplomat to outside adviser.

## Narrative value

This is not a scene and should not be inflated into one. Its power is bureaucratic.

Diplomatic biographies are compressed documents. They carry titles, assignments, boards, degrees, awards and institutional affiliations. In Burns's case, alongside the Council on Foreign Relations, the Trilateral Commission, the Order of Saint John and other professional memberships, the Department's archived biography also carried Red Sox Nation.

For a book about the portability of identity through diplomatic institutions, that is a small but unusually clean receipt. The baseball allegiance had become durable enough to survive translation into official biography.

## Documentary boundary

Safe:

- an official State Department archived biography directly identifies Burns as a member of Red Sox Nation;
- the page dates from his post-career relationship with the Department through the Foreign Affairs Policy Board;
- the record independently corroborates contemporary 2008 reporting that an earlier official State biography contained the same affiliation;
- this can support the claim that Red Sox identity had become a durable part of Burns's institutional public biography.

Not safe:

- claiming this archived page is the exact biography the Washington Post consulted in January 2008;
- claiming Burns personally wrote, requested or approved the Red Sox Nation line;
- claiming the wording remained continuously unchanged from 2008 through the later archive period;
- treating the listing as evidence that the State Department endorsed the Red Sox;
- inferring any diplomatic consequence from the biographical detail.

## Manuscript recommendation

If the manuscript uses the institutional-biography beat, upgrade the source stack so the prose no longer rests solely on the Washington Post's description of an official page.

A defensible formulation is:

> By the end of Burns's Foreign Service career, even the official biography had absorbed the allegiance. The Washington Post noted the detail when he announced his departure in 2008; a later State Department biography preserved the same identifier in the government's own archive: Red Sox Nation.

Keep the sentence compact. The stronger material elsewhere in the book supplies the scenes; this supplies institutional persistence.

## Remaining archival target

The exact 2007–08 State Department biography remains worth recovering. It would establish the original surrounding wording, capitalization and page history and would allow the book to replace the Washington Post's report about that specific page with the underlying primary record.

## Evidentiary level

**Primary official U.S. government biography, corroborating contemporary national reporting about an earlier official biography.** Strong for institutional biographical persistence; not evidence for authorship, motive, continuity of exact wording, or diplomatic effect.
