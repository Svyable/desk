# Boston College / 1975 World Series — chronology and evidence boundary

## Why this pass exists

Nicholas Burns was a Boston College undergraduate during the 1975 Red Sox pennant season, but that biographical overlap is not itself evidence that he attended, watched, listened to, discussed, or reacted to any particular postseason game. This note fixes the baseball chronology around the unresolved BC gap and records exactly what can and cannot be said while campus-level evidence is still missing.

## Verified anchors

### Burns at Boston College

Institutional biographies consistently identify Burns as a Boston College graduate, BA in history, class of 1978. The American Academy of Diplomacy specifies summa cum laude and Phi Beta Kappa. The George W. Bush White House archived biography likewise gives the 1978 BC degree and records Burns as a lifelong member of Red Sox Nation.

These sources establish the educational setting and later-documented fandom. They do **not** establish a 1975 baseball scene.

### The 1975 Series calendar

Baseball-Reference's series record fixes the Red Sox–Reds World Series at October 11–22, 1975. The decisive Boston dates are especially useful for archive searching:

- Game 1: Saturday, October 11, Fenway Park — Boston 6, Cincinnati 0.
- Game 2: Sunday, October 12, Fenway Park — Cincinnati 3, Boston 2.
- Game 6: Tuesday, October 21, Fenway Park — Boston 7, Cincinnati 6 in 12 innings.
- Game 7: Wednesday, October 22, Fenway Park — Cincinnati 4, Boston 3. Baseball-Reference gives an 8:15 p.m. local start and 2:52 duration.

The series chronology gives a tight retrieval window for Boston College newspapers, student publications, photographs, residence-hall records, event calendars, and oral histories: **October 4–23, 1975**, with particular attention to October 21–22.

## What is safe for manuscript use now

A bounded contextual sentence may state that Burns was a Boston College undergraduate in the fall of 1975, when the Red Sox reached the World Series and played Games 6 and 7 at Fenway Park on October 21 and 22. If used, the sentence should immediately preserve the evidentiary limit: no source recovered so far places Burns at Fenway, in a dorm or bar, before a television or radio, or in any conversation about the Series.

That context is potentially useful because the manuscript already has authenticated later evidence of Burns's Red Sox identity. It should not be written as a remembered formative episode unless a Burns or named-witness source supplies that bridge.

## Primary-source retrieval targets

1. **Boston College student newspaper, October 1975** — search October 4–23 issues for Red Sox, World Series, Fisk, Tiant, Cincinnati, Fenway, television, dorm/residence hall, watch party, celebration, and campus reaction. Preserve full issue/page citations and images when rights permit.
2. **Boston College yearbook / student-life photography, 1975–76** — inspect fall-semester candid pages, residence-hall sections, student organizations, and sports/campus-life photographs for World Series material. Do not identify Burns in an image without caption, archive metadata, or reliable independent identification.
3. **Burns Library / University Archives** — seek October 1975 event calendars, campus bulletins, residence-life material, student-affairs photographs, audiovisual holdings, and any oral-history collection describing Games 6–7 on campus.
4. **Named BC contemporaries** — use authenticated oral histories or interviews only. Ask witnesses what they personally remember; do not prompt them with an assumed Burns game-night scene.
5. **Boston-area broadcast archives** — useful for environmental context only after the viewing/listening medium is independently established. A surviving broadcast does not prove Burns consumed it.

## Attribution and reconstruction rules

- Do not say Burns watched Carlton Fisk's Game 6 home run, saw Game 7, went to Fenway, listened on radio, watched television, celebrated, mourned, skipped class, or discussed the Series unless a source says so.
- Do not infer a viewing location from his enrollment at BC.
- Do not use the later "lifelong Red Sox Nation" description to manufacture a 1975 reaction.
- Do not assign any 1975 first-person memory to Libby Baylies unless it comes from authenticated, citable material.
- A campus-wide World Series scene, if documented, may be described as campus context; Burns may be inserted into it only if item-level evidence places him there.
