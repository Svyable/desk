# Red Sox Diplomacy — State Department Red Sox register pass 01

## Editorial target

Give the book one more primary-source receipt showing that Nicholas Burns did not merely identify privately as a Red Sox fan. He repeatedly carried the affiliation into formal diplomatic settings, where the joke worked because the room understood both his institutional role and his baseball loyalty.

This is research-only. Do not force it into the manuscript if Chapters 8, 16, or the closing material already carry enough baseball. The value is documentary specificity, not another leadership analogy.

## Primary-source finding: October 7, 2005

At an on-the-record State Department briefing before travel to Brussels and the Balkans, Burns opened with a joke about the afternoon Red Sox–White Sox playoff game. The official transcript records him saying that the Department would be shut down at 4 p.m. so employees could leave to watch Boston beat Chicago. A reporter immediately asked whether the game would be put on B-Net; Burns said they would be happy to do so.

Primary source: U.S. Department of State archive, R. Nicholas Burns, “Briefing on His October 10-14 Trip to Brussels and the Balkans,” October 7, 2005.
https://2001-2009.state.gov/p/us/rm/2005/54610.htm

### What the source establishes

- Burns used Red Sox material spontaneously enough to put it at the opening of a formal foreign-policy briefing.
- The exchange became participatory: a reporter extended the joke into the Department's internal broadcast network and Burns answered in kind.
- The baseball reference sat immediately before substantive Balkan diplomacy. That juxtaposition is useful to the book because it is not an analogy imposed later by an author; it is part of Burns's own public register while serving as Under Secretary for Political Affairs.

### What it does not establish

- Do not write that the State Department actually closed at 4 p.m. The transcript is plainly a joke unless separate administrative evidence proves otherwise.
- Do not write that B-Net actually carried the game. Burns's answer records willingness in the exchange, not transmission evidence.
- Do not infer private staff culture, morale effects, motives, or a management strategy from the joke.

## Primary-source finding: March 23, 2005

On his third day as Under Secretary, speaking at the U.S. Institute of Peace, Burns thanked the introducer for comments about the Red Sox, called himself a native-born member of “Red Sox Nation,” and described the 2004 championship in comic New England religious language after beating the Yankees and Cardinals.

Primary source: U.S. Department of State archive, Nicholas Burns, remarks at the U.S. Institute of Peace, March 23, 2005.
https://2001-2009.state.gov/p/us/rm/2005/45781.htm

The important chronology is not the joke by itself. Burns had just moved from NATO to the Department's third-ranking job. Within days, the official transcript already preserves baseball as part of the public persona he brought into that office.

## Primary-source finding: June 12, 2007

At a Marshall Plan commemoration in Paris, Burns introduced Ambassador Craig Stapleton by saying they shared a special bond as Red Sox fans and opponents of the Yankees. He then pivoted directly to Stapleton's work in U.S.–French relations.

Primary source: U.S. Department of State archive, R. Nicholas Burns, “Commemoration of the 60th Anniversary of the Marshall Plan,” Paris, June 12, 2007.
https://2001-2009.state.gov/p/us/rm/2007/87176.htm

This is the strongest diplomatic-use example in the set because baseball becomes a compact relationship marker between two American ambassadors in a speech whose actual subject is alliance history. The manuscript should resist overclaiming that fandom *caused* diplomatic rapport. The source supports something narrower and better: Burns publicly used a shared Red Sox identity to establish human connection before turning to statecraft.

## Narrative opportunity

Taken together, the three transcripts produce a clean progression without invented backstage detail:

1. **March 2005:** Burns arrives in the Under Secretary job and publicly identifies himself with Red Sox Nation after the 2004 championship.
2. **October 2005:** the identity has become briefing-room shorthand, including a joke about shutting down State and a reporter's B-Net follow-up.
3. **June 2007:** in Paris, shared Red Sox allegiance becomes an introductory bond between Burns and the U.S. ambassador to France before the speech turns to the Marshall Plan and U.S.–French relations.

That sequence can support a bounded paragraph or closing callback if the manuscript needs it. It shows continuity across settings rather than another isolated baseball anecdote.

## Why knowledgeable Red Sox readers may care

The October 2005 joke has a built-in correction against triumphalist mythology: Boston was about to play the Chicago White Sox in the American League Division Series, one year after the 2004 title. Burns predicted a Red Sox win in the briefing room; Chicago completed a three-game sweep that day at Fenway Park. If used, the manuscript should let the failed prediction stand. Fandom is more convincing when it includes the ordinary hazard of talking before the game is played.

For exact baseball chronology and score, use an authoritative MLB/Retrosheet/SABR source before manuscript integration rather than relying on this research note alone.

## Editorial recommendation

Best use is **not** a new metaphor about diplomacy and baseball. Best use is a small documentary scene or callback showing Burns's public voice crossing institutional settings. The State Department transcript already supplies the comic timing. Preserve it as an attributed exchange; do not embellish the room.

If Chapter 8 already contains enough briefing-room baseball, hold this material for a late-career or epilogue passage where the Paris example can show that the Red Sox identity remained portable after Athens, NATO, and the spokesman years.

## Publication holds

- Keep quotations short and tied to the official federal transcript; paraphrase the rest.
- Do not claim the Department literally closed early or broadcast the game without separate evidence.
- Do not claim baseball materially improved a diplomatic negotiation absent participant evidence.
- Do not turn the 2005 playoff loss into a symbolic lesson. The factual reversal is enough.
- Before manuscript use of the October 7 game result, verify the exact game/date/series endpoint against an authoritative baseball source.
