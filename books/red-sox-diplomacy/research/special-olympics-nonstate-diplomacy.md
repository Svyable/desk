# Special Olympics and Non-State Diplomacy — Research Dossier

## Why this belongs in *Red Sox Diplomacy*

Nicholas Burns's Special Olympics work is not a stray nonprofit-board credential. The documentary record supports a distinct post-government strand that helps the book answer one of its governing questions: what remains of diplomacy when a practitioner no longer possesses delegated state authority?

The material belongs primarily in Chapter 17, **The Professor in the Cheap Seats**, where Burns moves from official authority into teaching, public argument, nonprofit governance and other forms of international engagement. It should echo briefly in Chapter 20, **The Human Channel**, because Special Olympics provides a useful contrast between civic cross-border networks and communication that can formally bind a state.

This is not evidence that Burns treated Special Olympics as an instrument of U.S. policy. It is evidence that, during his Harvard years, he engaged seriously with a global non-state institution organized around inclusion, citizen networks and sport.

## Documented record

### 1. Tim Shriver enters the Future of Diplomacy Project

On February 10, 2012, Harvard Kennedy School's Future of Diplomacy Project announced Tim Shriver, then President and CEO of Special Olympics, as a Fisher Family Fellow alongside Shyam Saran and Javier Solana.

Source:
- Belfer Center, **“Harvard Kennedy School’s Future of Diplomacy Project Announces Spring 2012 Fisher Family Fellows”**  
  https://www.belfercenter.org/publication/harvard-kennedy-schools-future-diplomacy-project-announces-spring-2012-fisher-family

Useful claims from the institutional record:
- the Future of Diplomacy Project was headed by R. Nicholas Burns;
- Shriver was selected as a Fellow in a program explicitly devoted to diplomacy, negotiation and statecraft;
- Harvard described Special Olympics as operating in more than 170 countries and serving more than 3.7 million people with intellectual disabilities at that time;
- the announcement specifically noted programs in Afghanistan, Bosnia-Herzegovina and Iraq.

Do not infer that Burns personally selected Shriver unless another source documents the selection process.

### 2. Harvard explicitly treats Shriver's work as a non-state international-affairs case

On September 11, 2012, Shriver spoke to Harvard Kennedy School students and faculty as a Fisher Family Fellow. The Future of Diplomacy Project published a report titled **“Non-state Actors in International Affairs.”**

Source:
- Belfer Center, **“Non-state Actors in International Affairs”**  
  https://www.belfercenter.org/publication/non-state-actors-international-affairs

The report records Shriver's argument that non-state development organizations can:
- place practical agency in the hands of citizens;
- build networks of people around common interests and needs;
- supply knowledge and infrastructure for public-policy advocacy.

This is particularly useful because it places Special Olympics inside Burns's diplomacy program without requiring an editorial leap from “sports charity” to “diplomacy.” Harvard itself made the non-state-international-affairs frame explicit.

### 3. Burns publicly identifies Shriver as a peace-builder

In December 2012, Burns published **“The Peacemakers of 2012.”** The Harvard publication record preserves the article's premise: people without the power of the state can still push the cause of peace at village, national and global levels. Burns included Tim Shriver among those examples.

Sources:
- Harvard Kennedy School publication page, **“The Peacemakers of 2012”**  
  https://www.hks.harvard.edu/publications/peacemakers-2012
- Syndicated version preserving the Shriver paragraph, *Dallas Morning News*  
  https://www.dallasnews.com/opinion/commentary/2012/12/26/nicholas-burns-the-peacemakers-of-2012/

Safe narrative claim:
- Burns explicitly broadened his public conception of peace-building beyond state leaders and used Shriver's Special Olympics work as one example.

Do not convert this into a general Burns doctrine unless repeated evidence supports that claim.

### 4. Burns serves on the Special Olympics International board

A State Department biography from the mid-2010s lists Burns among the directors of Special Olympics. The current Special Olympics biography for Burns also identifies him as a board member and explains that the international board determines organization-wide policies.

Sources:
- U.S. Department of State archived biography  
  https://2009-2017.state.gov/s/p/fapb/185577.htm
- Special Olympics, **Ambassador Nicholas Burns — Board of Directors**  
  https://www.specialolympics.org/about/board-of-directors/nicholas-burns
- 2016 Special Olympics annual report, board listing  
  https://media.specialolympics.org/resources/reports/annual-reports/2016-Special-Olympics-Annual-Report.pdf
- June 2018 official board list  
  https://media.specialolympics.org/resources/brand-awareness-and-communication/marketing/Official-Board-List-effective-6-28-2018.pdf

Safe narrative claim:
- this was a governance position in an international nonprofit, not merely an honorary mention in a biography.

Do not claim that every Special Olympics policy during Burns's tenure was his policy or that he personally directed operations.

### 5. Burns is re-elected to a second three-year term in 2016

At the international board meeting that selected Abu Dhabi to host the 2019 Special Olympics World Summer Games, Special Olympics announced Burns's re-election to a second three-year board term.

Source:
- Special Olympics / PR Newswire, **“Special Olympics Board of Directors Awards the 2019 World Summer Games to Abu Dhabi...”**  
  https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/special-olympics-board-of-directors-awards-the-2019-world-summer-games-to-abu-dhabi-announces-new-board-members-and-welcomes-returning-board-members-at-international-board-directors-meeting-300363665.html

Safe narrative claims:
- Burns had already served one board term before the 2016 re-election;
- the board on which he served was making decisions at the scale of global events and international host selection;
- at this meeting the board awarded the 2019 Games to Abu Dhabi.

Important limit:
- the record cited here does **not** establish Burns's individual view on Abu Dhabi, his role in the selection, or how he voted. Use the board decision to show institutional scale, not individual agency.

### 6. Red Sox Nation and Special Olympics appear in the same official biography

The archived State Department biography lists Burns both as a Special Olympics board member and as a member of Red Sox Nation.

Source:
- U.S. Department of State archived biography  
  https://2009-2017.state.gov/s/p/fapb/185577.htm

This is narratively useful because it prevents an artificial separation between the book's sports identity and Burns's international civic work. The same public biography recorded both, although the two affiliations had very different functions.

The current Special Olympics major-donors/supporters page lists **R. Nicholas Burns & Elizabeth Baylies** among supporters.

## Narrative distinction to protect

### Red Sox identity

The Red Sox strand is personal identity carried into public life: a fan's vocabulary, memory, rivalry, humor and recognizable local allegiance. It can make an official representative easier to read as a person.

### Athens baseball diplomacy

The Greece baseball story is official public diplomacy. Burns was the U.S. ambassador, embassy resources and American networks were involved, and sport functioned inside an overtly representative role.

### Special Olympics

The Special Olympics strand is different again. Burns was outside government and served a global civic institution. The organization could build networks, inclusion, recurring encounters and public claims without possessing state authority.

This three-part distinction is stronger than collapsing all of them into “sports diplomacy”:

1. **fandom as human legibility;**
2. **sport inside official public diplomacy;**
3. **sport as a non-state international civic institution.**

That architecture can recur later in the book when Burns returns to official authority in Beijing.

## Evidentiary stop conditions

Do **not** claim without new evidence that:
- Burns joined the Special Olympics board for a particular personal reason;
- Burns personally designed or directed Special Olympics programs;
- Burns personally caused the Abu Dhabi host decision;
- Burns's Special Olympics board service changed any specific government's policy;
- Special Olympics activity was a covert or informal arm of U.S. public diplomacy;
- Burns's Red Sox fandom caused his Special Olympics involvement;
- Burns developed a formal theory of “sports diplomacy” from Special Olympics.

The current evidence is sufficient for a core narrative about the boundary between state and non-state international action. It is not sufficient for invented boardroom scenes, private motives or individualized operational claims.
