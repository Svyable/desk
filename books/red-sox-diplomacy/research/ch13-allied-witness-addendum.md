# Chapter 13 Allied Witness Addendum — Iraq rupture, Turkey, and the French view

The Chapter 13 brief called for a non-American allied witness to keep the 2003 NATO rupture from being narrated only through Nicholas Burns and U.S. sources. That gap is now substantially closed.

## Benoît d'Aboville — the French position in his own words

A PBS *Frontline* transcript for *Blair's War* preserves French Ambassador to NATO **Benoît d'Aboville** explaining France's objection to the Turkey-planning sequence.

D'Aboville says the basic French position was that Paris would not accept putting NATO on a path whose **working hypothesis was that a military attack on Iraq would occur**.

Burns appears in the same documentary answering from the opposite institutional premise: NATO was a collective-defense organization and had to retain the capacity to act quickly in a crisis; from Washington's perspective, France was denying the alliance that ability.

Source:
- PBS *Frontline*, *Blair's War* transcript:
  https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/pages/frontline/shows/blair/etc/script.html

### Why this is unusually strong

This is better than paraphrasing the French position through American reporting because the two ambassadors explain their incompatible interpretations directly in the same source.

The chapter can therefore establish without caricature:

- **France:** beginning planning risked making war the institution's assumed trajectory while diplomacy was still underway.
- **United States:** refusing even defensive planning weakened NATO's ability to protect an ally facing a plausible threat.

Both propositions can be accurately represented without declaring either ambassador insincere.

## Contemporary reporting confirms the dispute was genuinely about timing and signaling

Reporting during the crisis repeatedly distinguishes the question of Turkey's defense from the political meaning of beginning NATO military preparations.

The *Guardian* reported d'Aboville arguing that France remained committed to Turkey's security while objecting to NATO adopting a posture inconsistent with the ongoing UN process. Other contemporaneous accounts quote Turkish officials themselves emphasizing that the dispute concerned timing rather than the underlying principle that Turkey would be defended.

Sources:
- *Guardian*, February 2003:
  https://www.theguardian.com/world/2003/feb/11/iraq.nato1
- RFE/RL, February 10 and 12, 2003:
  https://www.rferl.org/a/1102172.html
  https://www.rferl.org/a/1102210.html

### Manuscript consequence

Avoid the lazy sentence: **France refused to defend Turkey.**

The better supported sentence is: France, Germany, and Belgium blocked the timing of NATO planning because they feared its political meaning, while Burns and most allies argued that delaying defensive preparation endangered alliance credibility.

## The procedural sequence is now clear enough to narrate

The surviving official and contemporary record supports the following sequence:

1. Turkey faced a plausible risk of Iraqi retaliation if war began.
2. A U.S.-backed package sought NATO planning for AWACS, Patriot missile defense, and chemical/biological protection.
3. France, Germany, and Belgium broke the silence procedure and blocked action in the North Atlantic Council.
4. Turkey formally invoked **Article 4**, requiring allied consultations over a perceived threat to its security.
5. Repeated North Atlantic Council meetings failed to produce consensus.
6. The issue moved to the **Defense Planning Committee**, where France did not participate because of its longstanding position outside NATO's integrated military structure.
7. Germany and Belgium ultimately joined consensus there.
8. NATO authorized defensive measures for Turkey.

### Guardrail

Do not describe the Defense Planning Committee move simply as the United States 'kicking France out.' France's nonparticipation derived from an existing institutional arrangement dating to its withdrawal from NATO's integrated military command in 1966. The move was procedurally advantageous to Washington, but the room itself was not invented for the crisis.

## The relationship did not become friendship after the vote

Later 2003 reporting is useful because it shows Burns and d'Aboville still arguing over European defense months after the Turkey crisis. *El País* described their relationship with the striking formulation that they **'hate each other amicably'** while continuing to operate within the same NATO machinery.

Source:
https://elpais.com/diario/2003/10/21/internacional/1066687212_850215.html

This should not be overquoted or turned into private psychological evidence. Its value is narrower: intense disagreement did not terminate professional contact.

## Burns's repair rhetoric

By May 27, 2003, Burns was publicly speaking about **healing the rift**. His Konrad Adenauer Foundation speech praises Germany and retiring German NATO Ambassador Gephardt von Moltke despite the severe dispute only months earlier.

Source:
https://2001-2009.state.gov/p/eur/rls/rm/2003/21241.htm

This is a useful transition from rupture to institutional maintenance. The chapter should not imply that praising von Moltke proves the underlying policy dispute was resolved. It demonstrates something more professional and more interesting: diplomats must continue working with governments they have just fought publicly.

## Baseball material belongs as contrast, not explanation

The NATO years now offer two documented baseball scenes that can humanize Chapter 13 if used sparingly:

### October 2003 — Brussels at 6:16 a.m.

Burns told ESPN he watched Game 7 of the Yankees–Red Sox ALCS on Armed Forces television in Brussels and remembered Aaron Boone's walk-off at **6:16 a.m.** local time.

Source:
https://www.espn.co.uk/espn/magazine/archives/news/story?page=magazine-20040119-article32

### April 2004 — CENTCOM screens

Thomas Friedman later reported Burns describing a CENTCOM briefing in Qatar where Predator feeds from Iraq occupied three screens and Yankees–Red Sox occupied another.

Source record:
https://books.google.com/books/about/The_World_Is_Flat.html?id=7SJvQgAACAAJ

The exact April date remains unresolved. April 25 is a strong candidate based on the Pedro Martínez–Derek Jeter matchup and Burns's NATO travel chronology, but it remains a hypothesis.

### Editorial use

These scenes should **not** turn Iraq or NATO into baseball metaphors.

Their value is contrast:

- Yankees–Red Sox was a rivalry whose theatrical hostility depended on the rival surviving.
- France–United States in 2003 was a real strategic dispute involving war, alliance commitments, and sovereign decisions.
- Burns had to distinguish harmless opposition from consequential disagreement while remaining inside relationships that continued the next day.

That is enough.
