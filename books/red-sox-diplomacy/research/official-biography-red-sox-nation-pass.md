# Official biography / Red Sox Nation pass

## Finding

A small but unusually durable institutional detail survives from Nicholas Burns's final years at the State Department: his Red Sox identity entered the official biography itself.

Contemporary reporting on Burns's January 2008 departure noted that his official State Department biography listed him as a member of “Red Sox nation.” This is more useful than another generic retrospective description of Burns as a fan because it shows the identity had crossed an institutional threshold. By the end of his career as Under Secretary of State for Political Affairs, Red Sox affiliation was not merely something reporters noticed in briefings or Burns supplied in speeches; it was part of the biographical language attached to one of the Department's most senior career diplomats.

## Why it matters

The manuscript already has stronger baseball scenes: briefing-room jokes, the documented Brussels ALCS morning, the Greek baseball project, and the later China cap. This finding should not compete with those scenes.

Its value is institutional rather than dramatic.

Burns entered the Foreign Service as a junior officer whose official work had nothing to do with baseball. By 2008 he was the Department's Under Secretary for Political Affairs, handling Iran, India, Kosovo and other major files. Yet the Red Sox identity had become sufficiently stable and public that a contemporary Washington Post account could point readers to it in his official biography while reporting his resignation.

That creates a clean connective tissue for the book: the fandom was not an occasional prop pulled out when a Boston audience appeared. It persisted across assignments, administrations and levels of seniority until it became part of the public institutional description of the diplomat himself.

## Documentary boundary

Safe:

- Burns served as Under Secretary of State for Political Affairs from March 18, 2005 through February 29, 2008; the State Department Office of the Historian provides the official dates.
- Contemporary Washington Post reporting on his January 2008 resignation says Burns's official biography noted membership in “Red Sox nation.”
- The detail can be used as evidence that his baseball identity had become part of his durable public persona by the end of his Foreign Service career.

Not safe:

- claiming Burns personally demanded that the line appear in the biography;
- identifying who wrote or approved the biography without an archival record;
- treating the biographical line as evidence that the State Department institutionally endorsed the Red Sox;
- claiming baseball affected the Iran, India, Kosovo or other negotiations Burns led;
- reconstructing an office scene in which the biography was drafted or discussed.

## Narrative recommendation

Use, if needed, as a compact end-of-era beat rather than a standalone anecdote. The useful contrast is between the gravity of the files Burns was carrying and the persistence of the small hometown identifier.

A defensible shape is:

> By the time Burns prepared to leave the State Department in 2008, the Red Sox no longer needed an anecdote to establish their place in his public identity. The affiliation had made it into the official biography. Contemporary coverage of his resignation, amid Iran, India and Kosovo, paused to note that the Department's own biography counted him as a member of “Red Sox nation.”

The quotation should remain short and attributed to the contemporary report unless the archived official biography itself is recovered.

## Next archival target

Recover the exact 2007–08 State Department biography page through State's archive or an authoritative web archive. That would upgrade the wording from contemporary secondary reporting about the official biography to the underlying official record and may establish capitalization, surrounding language, and whether the line changed over time.

## Evidentiary level

**Contemporary national reporting describing an official government biography + official State Department career chronology.** Strong for the existence of the biographical Red Sox reference; underlying archived biography remains the preferred primary-source target.
