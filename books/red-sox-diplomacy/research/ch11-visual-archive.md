# Chapter 11 visual archive — Twelve Days

## Method

Chapter 11 is about a reversal in information flow before it is about a treaty decision. The strongest documentary fact is not an iconic image of the attacks themselves. It is that the new U.S. ambassador to NATO temporarily could not reach evacuated institutions in Washington while allied ambassadors began calling toward him with offers of solidarity.

The chapter therefore uses two designed objects and preserves the strongest Burns-specific photograph as a rights-clearance target rather than embedding it without permission.

## PHOTO RIGHTS TARGET — Burns, Robertson, Ralston on September 11

The American Foreign Service Association's 2024 *Foreign Service Journal* feature, “NATO at 75,” publishes an unusually valuable scene-level photograph with the caption identifying:

- Ambassador Nicholas Burns at center;
- NATO Secretary General Lord Robertson at left;
- Supreme Allied Commander General Joe Ralston;
- NATO headquarters, afternoon of September 11, 2001;
- context: conferring after the attacks in the United States.

The published credit is **“Courtesy of Nicholas Burns.”**

Source:
https://afsa.org/nato-75

### Rights status

**RESEARCH-ONLY / PERMISSION TARGET.**

The image is exactly the kind of Burns-specific historical photograph this book should prefer, but “Courtesy of Nicholas Burns” is not a public-domain or open-license statement. Do not remote-embed the image and do not infer a federal-work status from the subjects or setting.

## SIGNAL REVERSAL 01 — the representative remains; the center goes quiet

**Local media:** `../media/ch11-signal-reversal.svg`

Primary narrative source:
https://afsa.org/nato-75

Burns's 2024 account establishes:

- he had arrived only twelve days earlier;
- the first report reached him during the weekly ambassadors' lunch through a Belgian employee entering by a side door;
- after the second plane, the accident hypothesis disappeared;
- the White House, State Department and Pentagon were evacuated/disrupted;
- his team called but initially could not reach those institutions;
- allied ambassadors were simultaneously moving toward the American mission with expressions of solidarity and the Article 5 idea.

**Narrative job:** Make the reversal visible without suggesting Washington ceased to function. The diagram's outbound links are deliberately dashed and labeled temporary; the incoming allied links are solid but labeled political solidarity rather than formal NATO policy.

## AUTHORITY GAP 01 — solidarity is not yet a treaty act

**Local media:** `../media/ch11-authority-gap.svg`

Burns's 2024 account says that at the late-night meeting of the nineteen NATO ambassadors:

- a majority gave an unequivocal yes when asked whether they would support invoking Article 5;
- some ambassadors still lacked instructions from their capitals;
- Denmark needed parliamentary consultation.

Source:
https://afsa.org/nato-75

**Narrative job:** Preserve the difference among political instinct, national authorization and alliance procedure. The object ends explicitly before formal Article 5 action.

## Handoff to Chapter 12

NATO's official September 12 statement says that if the attacks were determined to have been directed from abroad against the United States, they would be regarded as covered by Article 5.

Official source:
https://www.nato.int/docu/pr/2001/p01-124e.htm

That formal conditional decision belongs to Chapter 12, not Chapter 11. The Chapter 11 objects should stop at the late-night authority gap so the visual architecture respects the manuscript division.

## Why iconic 9/11 photography is not used here

The attacks are among the most photographed events in modern history. That abundance is not a reason to import famous images into Burns's biography. A photograph of the towers, Pentagon, or evacuation would document the national event but would not clarify Burns's role at NATO.

The exact Burns/Robertson/Ralston photograph exists. Until its rights are cleared, designed documentary objects are more accurate to the chapter than generic tragedy imagery.
