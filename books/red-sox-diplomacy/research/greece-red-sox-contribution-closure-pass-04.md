# Greece / Red Sox Contribution Closure Pass 04

## Purpose

Define the strongest publication-safe statement about the Boston Red Sox organization's role in the Greek baseball project after a focused search for a named club employee, department, payment, equipment shipment, clinic, coach, facility contribution, or other concrete deliverable.

## Contemporary evidence

### September 1999 — Washington Post

A contemporaneous *Washington Post* sports brief reported that Baltimore Orioles owner Peter Angelos met Greek officials at a reception held by U.S. Ambassador Nicholas Burns and the Hellenic Amateur Baseball Federation. It then said Angelos was being helped by the baseball commissioner's office and the Boston Red Sox, identified parenthetically as Burns's favorite team.

Source:

- *The Washington Post*, September 8, 1999: https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/sports/1999/09/08/injuries-to-teens-are-still-a-concern/fe2eb9f0-7fc2-4b43-80a4-a9cb6ac94670/

What this establishes:

- the Red Sox were publicly described, at the time, as helping the Greek baseball effort;
- the claim predates the Olympic team and is not a later Burns retrospective;
- the same item places Angelos, the commissioner's office and the Red Sox in the support network.

What it does **not** establish:

- the Red Sox employee or department responsible;
- what Boston supplied;
- whether the help was money, equipment, introductions, coaching, facilities, publicity, or an unrealized pledge.

### January 2000 — Associated Press / Los Angeles Times

A detailed AP report published by the *Los Angeles Times* said the task of recruiting and training the Greek team had been undertaken by Angelos and added that support had also been pledged by the Boston Red Sox.

The same report is substantially more specific about other actors:

- Jim Small of Major League Baseball International described plans to send a full-time coach and administrator;
- MLB also planned college instructors for teaching trips;
- Angelos was described as undertaking the recruiting and training task;
- Greek organizers were renovating diamonds and Olympic organizers were planning facilities;
- Chuck Samiotes described his independent grassroots teaching and equipment he personally brought to Greece.

Source:

- Associated Press / *Los Angeles Times*, January 2, 2000: https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-2000-jan-02-mn-49873-story.html

This asymmetry is evidentially useful. The article knew enough to name concrete MLB, Angelos, federation and Samiotes actions but described Boston only as having pledged support.

### Later institutional reporting

A 2025 MLB/Red Sox feature looking back at Greek baseball credits financing help to the late Peter Angelos while describing the larger federation and Olympic effort. It does not identify a concrete Red Sox organizational contribution.

Source:

- Boston Red Sox / MLB, 2025: https://www.mlb.com/redsox/news/featured/loukas-soukeras-last-greek-baseball-player

This later omission does not prove the Red Sox did nothing. It does mean a modern club-hosted retrospective does not close the old specificity gap.

## Nearby Boston network that must not be conflated with Greece support

Chuck Samiotes had a genuine institutional relationship with Boston baseball. His engineering firm records donated civil-engineering work on Jim Rice Field at Ramsey Park, a 1999 MLB RBI project built with the City of Boston, MLB, the Boston Red Sox and others around the All-Star Game.

Source:

- Samiotes Consultants, Jim Rice Field at Ramsey Park: https://www.samiotes.com/jim-rice-field

This proves that a Greek-baseball organizer later central to the Greece story was already working in a Red Sox/MLB civic-baseball network in Boston. It does **not** prove that the Red Sox contribution to Jim Rice Field was transferred to Greece or that Samiotes was the organizational conduit for a Red Sox Greece commitment.

The adjacency is useful for witness-routing and archival search, not for closing the contribution claim.

The search recovered the two contemporary support formulations above but did **not** recover:

## Publication-safe conclusion

The strongest public-record statement is:

> Contemporary reports in 1999 and 2000 said the Boston Red Sox were helping the Greek baseball effort or had pledged support, but the surviving public record reviewed for this book does not identify what the club actually supplied.

That sentence is stronger than omitting the Red Sox entirely and more accurate than assigning the club an undocumented deliverable.

Do not convert the following into a stronger claim without new primary evidence:

- Burns's Red Sox fandom;
- Samiotes and Galatis being Red Sox fans;
- Samiotes Consultants' work with the Red Sox and MLB on Jim Rice Field;
- Rob Derksen's earlier employment in the Red Sox farm system;
- the *Washington Post* phrase that the Red Sox were helping;
- the AP phrase that support had been pledged.

Together they establish a real Boston/Red Sox adjacency around the project. They still do not answer **what Boston supplied to Greece**.
