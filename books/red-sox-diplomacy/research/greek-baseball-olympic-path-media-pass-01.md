# Greek baseball: from embassy phone call to Olympic diamond — media/research pass 01

## Scope

Bounded RedSox Diplomacy research/production pass for the Athens-to-2004 baseball strand, especially Chapter 10. No third-party image is cleared or copied here. The strongest publication-safe output from this pass is an **original chronology/route figure** built from sourced facts rather than protected photographs, Olympic graphics, team marks, or maps.

## Why this is worth a figure

The Greek baseball story is easy to compress into a sentimental sentence about sports diplomacy. The documentary sequence is stranger and more useful: Greece had virtually no baseball infrastructure; a new federation sought help; Ambassador Nicholas Burns used an embassy relationship to reach outward into U.S. baseball and the Greek diaspora; a North American player pool was assembled; the team had to earn its way through European competition; and only then did Greece take an Olympic baseball field in Athens.

A chronology makes the mechanism visible without claiming that Burns personally created the team or that baseball altered bilateral policy.

## Source ledger and claim boundaries

### 1. Burns's official Athens role

**Source:** U.S. Department of State archived biography, `R. Nicholas Burns — Ambassador to Greece`  
**URL:** https://1997-2001.state.gov/about_state/biography/burns.html  
**Institution:** U.S. Department of State

**Useful fact:** Burns was sworn in November 10, 1997 and, as ambassador, oversaw U.S. government programs at Embassy Athens and the Consulate General in Thessaloniki.

### 2. The federation-to-ambassador connection

**Source:** Robert Bruce, `Greek-American Backs National Baseball Team`, *Athens News*, preserved by Hellenic Communication Service.  
**URL:** https://www.helleniccomserve.com/baseball.html  
**Creator/institution:** Robert Bruce / *Athens News*; republication by Hellenic Communication Service.  
**Rights:** **NOT CLEARED FOR REPRODUCTION.** Treat as reporting/source material only. Do not reproduce page images, photographs, article layout, or extended text.  
**Useful reported fact:** the Hellenic Amateur Baseball Federation approached Burns for help; the account describes Burns passing word into American baseball circles and connects the effort to Peter Angelos and the Greek diaspora.  
**Claim boundary:** this is contemporary/near-contemporary reporting, not a federal primary record of each contact. Attribute where actor-level detail matters.

### 3. Retrospective federation history

**Source:** Greek Baseball, `History`.  
**URL:** https://greekbaseball.com/history  
**Institution:** Greek Baseball / federation-history site.  
**Rights:** **NOT CLEARED FOR IMAGE OR TEXT REPRODUCTION.** Use as a participant/institutional retrospective source and research lead.  
**Useful claims to cross-check before manuscript promotion:** federation founded in preparation for Athens 2004; Panos Mitsiopoulos approached Burns; Burns contacted Senator Paul Sarbanes; Sarbanes connected the effort to Baltimore Orioles owner Peter Angelos; North American players of Greek descent were assembled; Greece won the 2002 European B-pool qualifier and finished second at the 2003 European championship; manager Rob Derksen died shortly before the Olympics.  
**Important discrepancy:** this retrospective places Mitsiopoulos's approach to Burns in **2001**, while the *Athens News* account as preserved by Hellenic Communication Service says **1999**. Do not silently choose one date. Until a contemporaneous embassy/federation record resolves it, reader-facing chronology should use `1999–2001 — federation seeks Burns's help` or omit the year.

## Publication-safe figure recommendation

### Figure: `FROM PHONE CALL TO OLYMPIC DIAMOND`

**Form:** original horizontal chronology for print/PDF; vertical semantic fallback for EPUB.

**Stops:**

1. **1997 — Athens wins the 2004 Games.** Greece now has a host-country route into Olympic team sports, while domestic baseball is barely established.
2. **1997–2001 — federation-building.** The Hellenic baseball organization forms and begins trying to create domestic play and an Olympic-capable team.
3. **1999–2001 — the embassy connection.** The federation seeks Ambassador Nicholas Burns's help. Preserve the date range because surviving secondary/participant accounts disagree on 1999 versus 2001.
4. **U.S. network expands.** Burns/Sarbanes/Angelos/baseball-and-diaspora contacts become part of the recruitment/support chain. Actor-level arrows should be labelled `reported/retrospective` unless a contemporaneous primary record is recovered.
5. **2002 — European B-pool qualification.** Greece wins through the lower European competition, converting an Olympic aspiration into a competitive path.
6. **2003 — European silver.** Greece finishes runner-up in the Netherlands and secures its Olympic baseball place through competition rather than relying only on host symbolism.
7. **15–25 Aug. 2004 — Helliniko.** Greece plays Olympic baseball on the former Athens airport site.

### Visual grammar

- No Olympic rings, MLB/team marks, Orioles marks, Greek federation logo, State seal, newspaper mastheads, or screenshots.
- Use only original typography, lines, date nodes and simple geometric baseball-field/airport abstractions.
- If a geographic inset is useful, draw an original schematic rather than tracing a commercial or Olympic map. Label it `schematic — not to scale`.
- Distinguish **documented competition dates/results** from **reported relationship links** with line style as well as text, so grayscale remains intelligible.
- Do not make the network look like a formal chain of command. It was a support/recruitment network assembled across institutions.

### Reader-facing caption draft

**From phone call to Olympic diamond.** Greece's baseball project moved through a newly built domestic federation, an embassy connection, Greek-American and baseball networks, and two years of European competition before the national team reached the Olympic Baseball Centre at Helliniko in August 2004. Surviving accounts disagree on whether the federation first approached Ambassador Nicholas Burns in 1999 or 2001; the figure preserves that uncertainty rather than manufacturing a clean date.

### Alt text

Chronology from Athens receiving the 2004 Olympics in 1997 through Greek baseball federation-building, the federation's reported 1999-to-2001 approach to U.S. Ambassador Nicholas Burns, expansion into Greek-American and U.S. baseball support networks, Greece's 2002 European B-pool success and 2003 European silver medal, and the Greek team's August 2004 Olympic games at Helliniko.

## Manuscript integration opportunity

Best placement is Chapter 10 near the point where the narrative shifts from Burns liking baseball to the actual machinery required to put a Greek team on a field. The figure should replace a paragraph of abstract `sports diplomacy` explanation if one exists; it should not be added as decorative surplus.

The useful argument is causal but bounded: an ambassador could connect people and institutions that normally lived in different worlds. The subsequent sporting achievement belonged to federation organizers, funders, coaches and players, and Greece still had to win baseball games in Europe.

## Claims not promoted in this pass

- Do **not** state a single year for the first Burns approach until the 1999/2001 discrepancy is resolved.
- Do **not** promote the federation site's `over $1 million` Angelos contribution without a better financial or contemporaneous source.
- Do **not** reproduce the Christian Science Monitor, *Athens News*, Greek Baseball, Olympedia, Olympic, MLB, Orioles, or federation photographs/graphics.
- Do **not** imply Burns selected the Olympic roster, controlled federation decisions, or caused the 2003 silver medal.
- Do **not** turn Greek-American eligibility/recruitment into a joke about authenticity; it is a substantive diaspora/citizenship and Olympic-team-building story that needs careful sourcing.
