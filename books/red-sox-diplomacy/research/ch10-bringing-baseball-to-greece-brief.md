# Chapter 10 Brief — Bringing Baseball to Greece

## Status

Draft-ready research brief. Chapter 9 is drafted and deliberately leaves the baseball network largely untouched.

## Title warning

The working title is catchy but causally too strong if read literally.

Do **not** write that Nicholas Burns brought baseball to Greece. A Greek baseball effort already existed. The documented story is a network story: Greek organizers sought help; Burns activated American and Greek-diaspora relationships; Senator Paul Sarbanes and Peter Angelos became important links; the Baltimore Orioles and MLB International supplied expertise and resources; Rob Derksen became an operational catalyst; diaspora players and Greek institutions carried the project into European competition and the 2004 Olympics.

The chapter may keep the title as a trade-book shorthand only if the prose corrects it immediately.

## Strongest Burns first-person evidence

A 2004 ESPN Magazine first-person piece by Burns is currently the best source for his own account. Burns says Greek officials came to him seeking help in 1998. He recalls:

- bringing American coaches and players for demonstrations;
- appearing on Greek television from a batting cage to demonstrate hitting;
- playing catch with Greek children;
- pressing the Greek government to develop a budget;
- connecting Greek organizers to Peter Angelos;
- seeing, within roughly two years, twenty youth baseball teams and ten softball teams operating in two leagues.

These are unusually concrete Burns-authored details and can provide the chapter's human scale.

## Network chronology

Chronology must remain explicit because retrospective accounts conflict.

### 1998

Burns's 2004 first-person account dates the approach by Greek officials to 1998.

### 1999

Contemporary reporting already shows the network operating. A Washington Post account places Peter Angelos with Greek officials at a reception held by Burns and the Hellenic Amateur Baseball Federation. It also reports involvement by the commissioner's office and the Boston Red Sox.

Athens News/Hellenic Communication Service reporting says the federation contacted Burns in 1999, Burns circulated the request through American baseball networks, and Angelos volunteered support. The Orioles then worked with the federation, with Rob Derksen in a key practical role and MLB International expected to contribute substantial support.

### Later retrospective compression

The Greek federation later published a history saying federation leader Mitsiopoulos approached Burns in 2001, Burns contacted Senator Paul Sarbanes, and Sarbanes connected Peter Angelos.

That cannot be treated as a literal first-contact chronology because contemporary 1999 sources already place Angelos in the project. Use the retrospective for network relationships, not for an uncontested date sequence.

## Credit map

### Greek organizers / HABF

They had agency and initiated the effort. The project was not an American invention.

### Nicholas Burns / Embassy Athens

Connector, advocate, public-diplomacy participant, government-budget advocate, and visible American enthusiast. Burns's role is unusually well documented in his own account and contemporary reporting.

### Senator Paul Sarbanes

Diaspora/political connector in later retrospective accounts. Verify exact timing before narrating a single causal chain.

### Peter Angelos / Baltimore Orioles

Major financial and organizational support. Contemporary reporting documents Angelos's early involvement. Later sources describe substantial Orioles funding.

### Rob Derksen

Operational baseball expert and scout; a central practical figure in identifying players and building a competitive team. His sudden death in June 2004 is part of the team's Olympic story and should not be subordinated to Burns.

### Lou Angelos / Tom Marudas / MLB International

Important supporting organizational/legal/baseball roles documented in contemporary and retrospective sources.

### Players

The eventual national team depended heavily on Greek-diaspora players. This complicates any simple story about teaching baseball inside Greece. Treat the roster debate directly rather than hiding it.

## Dramatic architecture

1. **The ask** — Greek organizers approach the American ambassador because the Olympics are coming and Greece lacks a mature baseball infrastructure.
2. **The ambassador in the batting cage** — Burns's first-person recollections give concrete, slightly absurd but genuine public-diplomacy action.
3. **The network expands** — diaspora politics, Angelos/Orioles, MLB, coaches, scouts, federation.
4. **From demonstration to institution** — youth teams, softball, leagues, funding, player identification.
5. **The roster problem** — Olympic competitiveness requires diaspora recruitment; ask what it means to build a Greek national baseball team when much of the baseball expertise resides abroad.
6. **Derksen** — give operational credit and human weight to the person players remembered as a catalyst.
7. **Athens 2004** — the project reaches the Olympics, not as Burns's personal triumph but as the visible result of a network he helped activate.
8. **Burns's judgment** — his later description of the effort as a joint American-Greek venture and one of the best things he did as ambassador can close the chapter if quoted sparingly and verified.

## The larger book argument

This is the first chapter where baseball is not merely identity or conversational register. It becomes an actual instrument of public diplomacy.

That does not mean baseball solved a diplomatic problem. It means an ambassador noticed a small request outside the traditional hierarchy of foreign-policy importance and treated it as worthy of institutional attention.

The result is useful precisely because the stakes were limited. Nobody confused baseball with Cyprus, Kosovo, terrorism, or Greek-Turkish relations. The project created a domain in which Americans and Greeks could build something together without pretending their harder disagreements had vanished.

## Guardrails

Do not:

- say Burns invented Greek baseball;
- say Burns single-handedly brought baseball to Greece;
- erase the Greek federation's initiative;
- erase Sarbanes, Angelos, MLB, Derksen, coaches, players, or Greek government support;
- repeat the federation's 2001 chronology as uncontested fact;
- imply the Boston Red Sox were the primary institutional driver unless stronger evidence establishes their exact contribution;
- invent Burns's emotional reaction to Olympic games;
- turn Rob Derksen's death into a Burns-centered tragedy;
- claim the baseball project materially changed Greek views of U.S. policy without evidence.
