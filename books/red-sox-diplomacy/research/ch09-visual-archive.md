# Chapter 9 visual archive — Athens

## Method

Chapter 9 is about representation becoming physical. The podium disappears; the embassy building, residence, country team, civic relationships, and public-facing spaces become part of what the United States looks like from Greece.

The chapter therefore uses three visual moves:

1. a **real federal archive photograph** showing that the Athens chancery had already functioned as a politically charged public symbol long before Burns arrived;
2. a **RESTORATION OBJECT** separating the physical 1998–2000 renewal from any unsupported claim about political effect;
3. a **HOUSEHOLD LEDGER** making the residence and Libby Baylies-Burns's independently documented civic work visible without confusing those activities with delegated state authority.

The Greece-baseball network remains reserved for Chapter 10.

## RESTORATION OBJECT 01 — representation, made physical

**Local media:** `../media/ch09-restoration-object.svg`  
**Primary source:** U.S. Department of State, *State Magazine*, “New Look Transforms Athens Embassy,” July–August 2000:  
https://1997-2001.state.gov/publications/statemag/statemag_jul2000/feature5.html

Documented interventions include:

- cleaning the white marble;
- restoring the reflecting pool and courtyard fountains;
- plumbing, tile, and lighting work;
- new landscaping intended to bring the property closer to the Gropius design intent;
- the George C. Marshall statue project;
- a permanent exhibit on the U.S.–Greek relationship.

The State Department article directly connects the improvements to pride in the building and the relationship-history project. Earlier *Foreign Service Journal* reporting likewise described work intended to increase the appearance of openness without increasing exposure.

**Narrative job:** Make the chapter's distinction visible: facilities work can be representational without being magical. The object carries a right-hand evidentiary boundary listing what the physical changes do **not** prove.

## State Magazine photo route — Burns, Libby, Papayiannis

The same 2000 State Department feature contains a period photograph whose caption identifies sculptor **Thodoros Papayiannis**, Ambassador **Nicholas Burns**, and **Elizabeth Allen Baylies** standing in front of the model of the George C. Marshall statue in the artist's Athens studio.

This is unusually valuable because it is a genuine Burns-era photograph and places Libby inside the cultural/physical representation project without implying authorship.

## HOUSEHOLD LEDGER 01 — shared representation, distinct roles

**Local media:** `../media/ch09-household-ledger.svg`

The ledger uses three evidence layers:

### Prior professional background, 1981–82

Libby Baylies Burns is independently credited on federal historic-preservation documentation at West Point and Watertown Arsenal. The Watertown work involved field inventory, historical/architectural evaluation, and preservation recommendations.

Research note:
`libby-baylies-historic-preservation-background.md`

**Guardrail:** This is background, not evidence that she designed, directed, or caused the Athens chancery renovation.

### Residence diplomacy, September 1999

An American School of Classical Studies at Athens newsletter records a Gennadeion fundraising dinner hosted by Nicholas Burns and Elizabeth Baylies-Burns at the Embassy Residence. About fifty guests attended and the event produced $70,000 in pledges.

Research note:
`libby-baylies-shared-representation-pass-02.md`

**Guardrail:** The source attributes the welcoming remarks to Burns. Do not invent a speech for Libby; co-hosting is enough.

### MDA Hellas, 2000–02 and after

A later organizational history says MDA Hellas was established in 2000 with Baylies-Burns's help. Contemporary Greek reporting in 2002 identifies her as honorary president and an “instigator of the project” when a specialized neuromuscular-disease unit opened at Aghia Sophia Children's Hospital.

Sources:
- https://www.ekathimerini.com/culture/9992/president-inaugurates-a-project-of-mda-hellas/
- https://musculardystrophynews.com/news/mda-hellas-gives-greek-sma-duchenne-patients-reason-to-hope/

**Narrative job:** Correct an ambassador-only account of representational life. The household had distinct actors and distinct authority. Libby's civic record belongs to her; it does not give her authority to speak for U.S. policy.

## Visual restraint

Three objects are enough for Chapter 9. The federal photograph supplies historical depth; the restoration object makes the embassy's representational choices inspectable; the household ledger broadens agency. A fourth object would need to add a genuinely different evidentiary function.

The 1999 Clinton-visit protests are important prose evidence of the limits of representation, but no additional protest photograph is needed unless a rights-clearable image is recovered that materially sharpens the argument rather than repeating FIG. 05's function.
