# Chapter 14 media/provenance pass — March 2006 New Delhi

Scope: Chapter 14, `India: Playing the Long Game`. This note adds a publication-safe visual candidate and production guidance without changing manuscript prose or importing a binary asset.

## Cleared candidate: Bush–Singh after the March 2006 press availability

**Object:** President George W. Bush and Prime Minister Manmohan Singh exchange handshakes after their press availability at Hyderabad House, New Delhi.

- **Date:** March 2, 2006
- **Creator:** Paul Morse, White House photographer
- **Institution/source:** White House / archived George W. Bush White House website
- **Source URL:** https://georgewbush-whitehouse.archives.gov/news/releases/2006/03/images/20060302-9_p030206pm-0301-515h.html
- **Source caption:** identifies Bush and Singh, Hyderabad House, New Delhi, March 2, 2006, after their press availability, and credits Paul Morse / White House.
- **Rights basis:** photograph made by a White House employee in the course of official federal duties; U.S. federal-government work, public domain under 17 U.S.C. §105. Preserve the item-level White House creator credit even though attribution is not a copyright condition.
- **Commercial-book suitability:** suitable on the federal-work basis. Do not imply endorsement by Bush, Singh, the White House, or the U.S. government. Use editorially in direct historical context; avoid merchandising or promotional treatment that could create endorsement/publicity confusion.
- **Credit line:** `White House photo by Paul Morse.`

### Why this image earns space

Chapter 14 already uses the July 18, 2005 Oval Office handshake to mark the leaders' political wager. A second generic leaders-together image would be redundant. This March 2 frame is useful only if it is assigned a different job: mark the moment when the 2005 wager had acquired a negotiated separation plan but still lacked the later U.S. statutory change, 123 Agreement, IAEA safeguards arrangement, NSG exception, and final congressional approval.

The location also matters. Hyderabad House is not decorative India scene-setting; it places the second major political checkpoint in New Delhi, after the opening wager in Washington. The two-image geography can therefore do argumentative work: Washington, July 2005 → New Delhi, March 2006.

### Proposed caption

**FIGURE CANDIDATE — New Delhi, March 2, 2006.** President George W. Bush and Prime Minister Manmohan Singh exchange handshakes after their press availability at Hyderabad House. By this point the two governments had reached an agreement on India's civilian–military nuclear separation plan. The later U.S. legal exception, bilateral 123 Agreement, IAEA safeguards arrangement, Nuclear Suppliers Group decision, and final congressional approval were still ahead. White House photo by Paul Morse; U.S. federal government work, public domain.

### Alt text

President George W. Bush and Indian Prime Minister Manmohan Singh shake hands after a March 2, 2006 press appearance at Hyderabad House in New Delhi.

### Placement / formatting

If used, place immediately after the manuscript sentence `By March 2006, during President Bush's visit to India, the two governments had a separation plan. Washington still needed Congress.` That sentence gives the image a precise documentary function and prevents it from becoming a second ceremonial handshake.

For print/PDF, keep image, caption, credit, and source note together; do not strand the credit on the next page. A landscape crop is acceptable only if both principals remain intact and the crop does not turn the handshake into a decontextualized personality portrait. Check faces and dark suit detail in grayscale. For EPUB, image precedes caption; alt text should describe the visible event, while chronology and rights information remain in the caption rather than being duplicated in alt text.

Do not place this image adjacent to the July 2005 Oval Office image. If both survive final design, separate them by enough prose that each reads as a different institutional checkpoint.

## Research strengthening: the completion endpoint

The State Department's October 10, 2008 signing transcript is useful as the far endpoint of the chapter's gate sequence. It records Condoleezza Rice and External Affairs Minister Pranab Mukherjee signing the 123 Agreement in the Benjamin Franklin Room and, in Mukherjee's remarks, explicitly notes the earlier unanimous IAEA Board approval and consensus decision of the 45-member Nuclear Suppliers Group.

- **Primary source:** U.S. Department of State, `Secretary of State Condoleezza Rice and Indian Minister of External Affairs Pranab Mukherjee At the Signing of the U.S.-India Civilian Nuclear Cooperation Agreement`, October 10, 2008.
- **URL:** https://2001-2009.state.gov/secretary/rm/2008/10/110916.htm
- **Use:** evidence/chronology and short attributed excerpt if needed; do not reproduce the archived webpage design.

The State Department fact sheet dated October 15, 2008 separately records congressional approval on October 1, signature on October 10, and describes the 123 Agreement as the legal framework for U.S. civil nuclear cooperation with India.

- **URL:** https://2001-2009.state.gov/p/sca/rls/fs/2008/109567.htm

A useful production refinement for the existing `GATE SEQUENCE 01` would be to make the final three gates explicitly date-bearing in the source map rather than merely institutional: `IAEA Board · 1 Aug 2008 → NSG consensus · 6 Sep 2008 → Congress · 1 Oct 2008 → signature · 10 Oct 2008`. Verify the IAEA and NSG dates against their own institutional records before changing the figure itself; this pass does not treat the State Department summary as a substitute for those item-level sources.

## Rejected / do not ingest

### State Department photograph of Rice testifying, April 5, 2006

The State Department gallery page for Rice's Senate Foreign Relations Committee testimony on the India agreement labels the photograph `© AP/WWP`. Government hosting does not convert an Associated Press image into a federal public-domain work. Do not ingest or reproduce it without a separate license.

Source page: https://2001-2009.state.gov/r/pa/ei/pix/2006/64143.htm

### Generic March 2006 arrival / honor-guard photographs

The Bush White House archive contains multiple rights-clean federal photographs of arrival ceremonies at Rashtrapati Bhavan. They are historically legitimate but lower-value for this chapter because they show ceremony rather than the nuclear negotiation's institutional progression. Do not add them merely for New Delhi atmosphere.
