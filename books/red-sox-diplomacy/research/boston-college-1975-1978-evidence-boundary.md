# Boston College 1974–78: verified chronology and baseball-evidence boundary

## Question

What can currently be established about Nicholas Burns's Boston College years, especially the unresolved 1975/1978 baseball thread, without turning a plausible Boston/Red Sox chronology into an invented undergraduate scene?

## Verified chronology

### Boston College degree and class year

Boston College's own institutional pages identify Burns as **R. Nicholas Burns ’78**. Harvard/Belfer and the archived George W. Bush White House biography independently state that he earned a B.A. in History/European History from Boston College in 1978.

Sources:

- Boston College, “Notable Alumni”: https://www.bc.edu/bc-web/about/notable-alumni.html
- Boston College, “Four Alumni Join the Boston College Board of Trustees”: https://www.bc.edu/bc-web/bcnews/campus-community/announcements/four-new-trustees.html
- Harvard Belfer Center biography: https://www.belfercenter.org/person/nicholas-burns
- George W. Bush White House archive biography: https://georgewbush-whitehouse.archives.gov/government/nburns-bio.html

The White House biography adds that Burns graduated **summa cum laude** and was elected **Phi Beta Kappa**.

### Sorbonne year

The White House and Harvard biographies state that Burns earned the Certificat Pratique de Langue Française at the University of Paris-Sorbonne in **1977**. This matters to any year-by-year reconstruction: the undergraduate period was not four uninterrupted years on the Chestnut Hill campus.

### Named college roommate

In an official State Department transcript of an April 11, 2007 address at Boston College, Burns identified **Kevin Donahue** as his “high school friend and college roommate,” who was present in the audience. Burns also said he had had “such a great time as a student” at Boston College and noted former professors and friends in attendance.

Primary source:

- U.S. Department of State archive, “America’s Global Leadership Challenge in the 21st Century,” April 11, 2007: https://2001-2009.state.gov/p/us/rm/2007/85332.htm

This is a useful oral-history/interview target because Donahue is a named, Burns-authenticated undergraduate witness. It does **not** itself establish anything about baseball.

### Independent graduation corroboration

Johns Hopkins University's 1980 commencement program lists “R. Nicholas Burns, of Wellesley, Mass.; B.A. Boston College, 1978” among degree candidates/recipients entering from prior institutions.

Source:

- Johns Hopkins commencement archive, 1980: https://jscholarship.library.jhu.edu/bitstream/handle/1774.2/36807/commencement1980.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1

## The unresolved baseball question

The public sources reviewed in this pass do **not** establish:

- that Burns attended any specific Red Sox game while a Boston College undergraduate;
- where he watched or listened to the 1975 World Series;
- whether he was on campus for any specific 1975 postseason game;
- where he was for the 1978 Red Sox–Yankees pennant race or October 2 tiebreaker;
- that he attended the 1978 tiebreaker at Fenway Park;
- a dorm, apartment, television, radio, bar, classroom, roommate gathering, Fenway trip, ticket, reaction, quotation, or anecdote connected to either season.

His independently documented lifelong Red Sox identity does not fill these gaps.

## Why 1975 and 1978 remain promising

The dates overlap his Boston College period, but overlap is only chronology. The evidentiary opportunity is unusually good because the search can now be narrowed to named witnesses and institutional archives rather than generalized web searching.

### High-value retrieval targets

1. **Kevin Donahue** — seek an attributed recollection specifically about Burns, Boston College, the Red Sox, 1975, 1978, Fenway, television/radio habits, and whether Burns was physically in Boston for the relevant games.
2. **Boston College Burns alumni files** — Alumni Association award files, 2002 commencement preparation, 2017 Ignatian Award materials, trustee biographical files, oral histories, questionnaires, and unpublished interview transcripts.
3. **Boston College student press** — digitized or physical *The Heights* issues for October 1975 and September–October 1978; search not merely for Burns by name but for campus viewing parties, World Series/tiebreaker coverage, Fenway trips, dorm television culture, and contemporary student reactions.
4. **Boston College yearbooks, 1975–78** — establish Burns's residence, activities, clubs, classmates, photographs, and named associates before approaching witnesses.
5. **University Archives course/student organization records** — identify history faculty and classmates who can be interviewed; do not infer attendance from course listings alone.
6. **Boston-area newspaper archives** — search Burns/Donahue plus BC and baseball terms; also look for campus reporting about 1975 and 1978 that can establish atmosphere without assigning Burns to it.
7. **1977 Paris chronology** — determine the exact academic term(s) Burns spent at the Sorbonne before narrating a continuous four-year Boston College timeline.

## Manuscript-use rule

Safe now:

> Burns graduated from Boston College in 1978 after studying history, with a period of study at the Sorbonne in 1977. Years later, speaking at Boston College, he publicly identified Kevin Donahue as both a high-school friend and his college roommate.

Safe as contextual chronology, if clearly framed:

> Burns's college years overlapped two of the most charged Red Sox seasons of the decade, 1975 and 1978.

Not safe:

> Burns watched, attended, listened to, discussed, celebrated, mourned, or otherwise experienced any particular 1975 or 1978 game unless a source places him there.

Do not use general Red Sox fandom to manufacture an undergraduate baseball scene.

## Attribution firewall

Nothing in this research establishes any statement, reaction, memory, viewing habit, or baseball preference of **Libby/Elizabeth Baylies**. No first-person material may be attributed to her unless independently authenticated as hers. Her later family relationship to Burns is not evidence about his Boston College baseball life.

## Research status

**Promotable now:** degree/class year; academic honors where sourced to the White House biography; 1977 Sorbonne credential; Burns's own identification of Kevin Donahue as high-school friend and college roommate.

**Context only:** the coincidence of Burns's undergraduate years with the 1975 and 1978 Red Sox seasons.

**Open:** every Burns-specific baseball scene from Boston College.


## Archive refinement: Mod 36A and athletics photographs

Boston College's institutional history identifies Burns's senior-year residence as **Mod 36A**. Burns's preserved 2002 commencement address separately says that he and his roommates lived in the Mods and that three former roommates were present for the ceremony. These facts narrow the witness search without creating a baseball scene.

The John J. Burns Library also holds the **Boston College athletic photographs collection (BC-1986-019)**, a substantial visual archive that includes material from the 1970s. It is a concrete place to look for dated and captioned Boston College baseball imagery, campus sports context, photographer information, and potential publication-rights metadata.

### What this permits

- Treat Mod 36A as an authenticated senior-year residential location.
- Search housing, directory, yearbook, alumni, and archival records for the six residents of that unit.
- Inspect the athletic-photograph collection for 1975–78 baseball images and metadata.
- Use contemporary campus imagery to establish BC baseball atmosphere only when captions/dates support it.

### What this does not permit

- Do not place the October 2, 1978 Yankees tiebreaker, the 1975 World Series, a television, radio, or any specific roommate inside Mod 36A without direct evidence.
- Do not assume Kevin Donahue lived in Mod 36A merely because Burns later identified him as a college roommate.
- Do not infer Burns attended a BC baseball game from the existence of athletics photographs.
- Nothing here authenticates any first-person baseball material from Libby Baylies.
