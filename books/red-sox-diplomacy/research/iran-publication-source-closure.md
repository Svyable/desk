# Iran Chapter — Publication Source Closure Pass

## Purpose

Record what the post-draft source pass has actually solved for Chapter 15 and what remains open. The chapter should not revert to a U.S.-only story during later editing simply because the Burns archive is easier to quote.

## Minimum multi-sided threshold — reached

The current research set now has independent documentary anchors for the principal actors and functions in the 2005–08 nuclear dispute:

- U.S. policy shift and the conditional May 2006 offer to join negotiations;
- Burns’s own public account of the P5+1 / Security Council strategy;
- an Iranian official position presented to the United Nations;
- European institutional description of the Solana/EU3 negotiating track;
- formal Security Council demands and sanctions sequence;
- an independent nonproliferation analysis of the U.S. offer and its precondition;
- an IAEA technical/safeguards record separate from political characterization by either government.

That is enough to sustain the chapter’s current central claim: process, coalition-building and pressure can alter the diplomatic structure without manufacturing another government’s political decision.

It is **not** enough to treat every contested factual or technical point as settled.

## U.S. side

Secretary Condoleezza Rice’s May 31, 2006 statement is the clean primary anchor for the change in U.S. posture: Washington offered to join the European-led nuclear negotiation if Iran verifiably suspended enrichment and reprocessing activities.

Nicholas Burns’s September 2006 Senate testimony is the Burns-specific anchor. It documents his own description of the coalition, repeated European travel, the P5+1 structure, the Security Council route and the administration’s argument for combining pressure with a diplomatic path.

Use these sources as U.S. policy claims. Do not automatically promote the administration’s characterization of Iranian intent into the narrator’s factual voice.

## Iranian side

The July 31, 2006 letter from Iran’s Permanent Representative to the United Nations is a direct official source for Iran’s claim that it was determined to exercise a right to nuclear technology for peaceful purposes.

The Security Council meeting record from the same day also captures the Iranian representative’s argument that the program was peaceful and that Council treatment was unwarranted.

This is sufficient to state Iran’s official legal and political position without accepting that position as a complete technical account.

Publication-stage improvement remains desirable: add a strong direct or near-direct Ali Larijani / Saeed Jalili / Manouchehr Mottaki source on the suspension precondition and bargaining logic if one is readily available.

## European side

The European Parliament’s June 14, 2006 verbatim proceedings document the European theory of the moment: Javier Solana had presented the negotiated package, European officials wanted a peaceful diplomatic resolution, and the Iranian response was being assessed rather than treated as a foregone conclusion.

This protects the narrative against a Washington-centric formulation in which Europe merely waited for the United States to decide what diplomacy meant.

If a clean Solana/EU Council primary transcript of the June 2006 presentation is located later, prefer it for fine-grained language. The current European institutional record is sufficient for structure and agency.

## Security Council sequence

The official record now supports the required sequence:

- Resolution 1696 — demand for suspension;
- Resolution 1737 — sanctions after noncompliance with the suspension demand;
- Resolution 1747 — expanded measures;
- Resolution 1803 — further measures in 2008.

Use the sequence to describe changing international pressure, not as a scoreboard proving policy success.

## IAEA technical boundary

The IAEA record is essential because it prevents both Washington’s and Tehran’s political language from substituting for safeguards findings.

The chapter must preserve several distinctions:

- declared nuclear material versus possible undeclared material or activities;
- safeguards compliance and cooperation versus confidence-building demands imposed by the Board / Security Council;
- enrichment capability versus a nuclear weapon;
- technical uncertainty versus proof of a political intention.

`GOV/2006/15` is staged in the source ledger. Before any exact quotation from that PDF enters the publication manuscript, verify it against the page image. Prefer stable IAEA HTML summaries for general institutional descriptions when they are sufficient.

## Independent arms-control perspective

The Arms Control Association’s contemporary account of the May 2006 offer is useful because it neither belongs to the Bush administration nor represents the Iranian government. It helps establish the policy shift, the EU3 background, the suspension condition and the mixed Iranian response without relying on later JCPOA hindsight.

## What remains genuinely open

These are now refinement tasks rather than threshold blockers:

1. A richer Iranian negotiator voice on why suspension as a precondition was politically unacceptable.
2. Direct documentary evidence of internal Bush-administration disagreement over sequencing, direct talks, military options and regime-change rhetoric.
3. A careful 2007 National Intelligence Estimate context pass if the final chapter keeps material extending that far; distinguish the NIE’s weaponization judgment from enrichment activity.
4. Final date/role check around Burns’s departure so later 2008 developments are not attributed to him personally.
5. If Chapter 15 retains any exact IAEA/UN quotations, page- or transcript-level verification before publication.

## Editorial consequence

No structural rewrite of Chapter 15 is required solely for balance.

During final line editing:

- make Europe visibly prior to the U.S. entry into talks;
- attribute Iranian rights/precondition arguments explicitly;
- use the IAEA for technical claims;
- describe Security Council measures as coalition outcomes;
- keep the no-deal ending as an institutional limit, not a personal Burns defeat;
- resist projecting the 2015 JCPOA backward as the answer history was always approaching.
