# Operation Eagle Assist: operational chronology and original-figure brief

Status: research/provenance note; safe for factual synthesis. This note does **not** clear NATO photographs, logos, maps, or artwork for reuse.

## Why this matters

The already-cleared May 16, 2002 U.S. military photograph of R. Nicholas Burns, NATO Secretary General George Robertson, Admiral Guido Venturoni, and NATO E-3A personnel at Geilenkirchen becomes more useful when paired with a precise operational chronology. The photograph shows the closing ceremony/recognition context; it should not be made to stand in for the September 2001 Article 5 decision.

## Authoritative chronology

### 11 September 2001 — Article 5 political decision

NATO's current collective-defence history records that the Allies invoked Article 5 within a day of the attacks, subject to determination that the attacks had been directed from abroad. The later determination allowed the collective-defence response to take effect.

Source: NATO, “Collective defence and Article 5”
https://www.nato.int/en/what-we-do/introduction-to-nato/collective-defence-and-article-5

### 4 October 2001 — operational measures agreed

After determining that the attacks came from abroad, the North Atlantic Council agreed eight measures supporting the United States. One was readiness to deploy NATO Airborne Early Warning Force elements. NATO's contemporaneous termination statement later described Eagle Assist as following the Council's October 4 decision to operationalise Article 5.

Source: NATO, “Collective defence and Article 5”
https://www.nato.int/en/what-we-do/introduction-to-nato/collective-defence-and-article-5

Source: NATO, “Statement by the Secretary General on the conclusion of Operation Eagle Assist,” 30 April 2002
https://www.nato.int/docu/update/2002/04-april/e0430a.htm

### 9 October 2001 — Operation Eagle Assist begins

The contemporaneous April 30, 2002 NATO statement gives **9 October 2001** as the operation's start date. Seven NATO AWACS aircraft ultimately supported patrols of U.S. airspace. Later NATO summaries describe this as the first deployment of NATO military assets in support of an Article 5 operation.

Sources:
- NATO, 30 April 2002 statement: https://www.nato.int/docu/update/2002/04-april/e0430a.htm
- NATO, “Countering terrorism”: https://www.nato.int/en/what-we-do/deterrence-and-defence/countering-terrorism

### 30 April 2002 — Council decides to terminate the operation

Secretary General George Robertson announced that the North Atlantic Council had agreed to terminate Eagle Assist effective May 16. The stated basis was material improvement in the U.S. air-defence posture, enhanced civil-military cooperation, and a recent U.S. evaluation of homeland-security requirements.

At that point NATO reported:

- 830 crew members;
- 13 NATO nations;
- nearly 4,300 patrol hours;
- more than 360 operational sorties.

These are NATO's own operational totals and should be presented as such rather than independently audited statistics.

Source: NATO, “Statement by the Secretary General on the conclusion of Operation Eagle Assist,” 30 April 2002
https://www.nato.int/docu/update/2002/04-april/e0430a.htm

### 16 May 2002 — operation ends; Geilenkirchen ceremony

The termination decision took effect on **16 May 2002**. NATO's 9/11 chronology separately records Robertson's May 16 visit to the NATO AWACS base at Geilenkirchen to mark the conclusion of Eagle Assist. This independently anchors the date and purpose of the ceremony depicted in the cleared U.S. military photo sequence already documented elsewhere in this research packet.

Sources:
- NATO, 30 April 2002 statement: https://www.nato.int/docu/update/2002/04-april/e0430a.htm
- NATO, “9/11 — Chronology of events”: https://www.nato.int/cps/en/natohq/events_77648.htm

## Reader-facing figure opportunity: original chronology

Do **not** reproduce a NATO infographic or timeline. Build an original, typographic chronology from the factual record above. Suggested five stops:

1. **11 Sep 2001** — Allies invoke Article 5 conditionally after the attacks.
2. **4 Oct 2001** — North Atlantic Council agrees measures to operationalise Article 5, including AWACS support.
3. **9 Oct 2001** — Eagle Assist begins.
4. **30 Apr 2002** — Council decides to end the mission after the U.S. security assessment and air-defence improvements.
5. **16 May 2002** — Eagle Assist ends; Robertson, Burns, Venturoni, and AWACS personnel are at Geilenkirchen for the closing recognition context documented by the cleared federal photograph.

A small factual footer can carry the operational scale: **7 AWACS aircraft · 830 crew members · 13 NATO nations · nearly 4,300 hours · 360+ operational sorties.** Attribute these figures to NATO.

## Claim discipline

Documented fact: Eagle Assist followed the Article 5 response, began October 9, used NATO AWACS to support U.S. airspace patrols, and ended May 16 after the Council's April 30 decision.

Documented NATO-reported scale: seven aircraft; 830 crew members from 13 countries; nearly 4,300 hours; more than 360 sorties.

Interpretation suitable for prose if signposted: the sequence makes visible the distance between a diplomatic decision and the months of personnel, aircraft, scheduling, basing, and patrol work required to enact it.

Do **not** claim without further evidence that Burns personally ordered, planned, commanded, scheduled, or operationally supervised Eagle Assist. His presence at the May 16 event documents diplomatic/institutional proximity to the operation's conclusion, not operational command.
