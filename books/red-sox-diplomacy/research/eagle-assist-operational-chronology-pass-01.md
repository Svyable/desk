# Operation Eagle Assist: operational chronology and original-figure brief

Status: research/provenance note; safe for factual synthesis. This note does **not** clear NATO photographs, logos, maps, or artwork for reuse.

## Why this matters

The already-cleared May 16, 2002 U.S. military photograph of R. Nicholas Burns, NATO Secretary General George Robertson, Admiral Guido Venturoni, and NATO E-3A personnel at Geilenkirchen becomes more useful when paired with a precise operational chronology. The photograph shows the closing recognition context; it should not be made to stand in for the September 2001 Article 5 decision.

## Authoritative chronology

### 12 September 2001 — Article 5 invoked conditionally

NATO's current collective-defence history records that the Allies invoked Article 5 within a day of the attacks, subject to determination that the attacks had been directed from abroad. The later determination allowed the collective-defence response to take effect. Keep September 12 visually separate from the later operational deployment: a political decision and an aircraft patrol are not the same event.

Source: NATO, “Collective defence and Article 5”
https://www.nato.int/en/what-we-do/introduction-to-nato/collective-defence-and-article-5

### 4 October 2001 — operational measures agreed

After determining that the attacks came from abroad, the North Atlantic Council agreed eight measures supporting the United States. One was readiness to deploy NATO Airborne Early Warning Force elements. NATO's contemporaneous termination statement later described Eagle Assist as following the Council's October 4 decision to operationalise Article 5.

Sources:
- NATO, “Collective defence and Article 5”: https://www.nato.int/en/what-we-do/introduction-to-nato/collective-defence-and-article-5
- NATO, “Statement by the Secretary General on the conclusion of Operation Eagle Assist,” April 30, 2002: https://www.nato.int/docu/update/2002/04-april/e0430a.htm

### 9 October 2001 — Operation Eagle Assist begins

The contemporaneous April 30, 2002 NATO statement gives **October 9, 2001** as the operation's start date. The U.S. Air Force 552nd Air Control Wing chronology records five NATO E-3 aircraft and more than 180 personnel deploying from Geilenkirchen to Tinker Air Force Base that day. Seven NATO AWACS aircraft ultimately supported patrols of U.S. airspace.

Sources:
- NATO, April 30, 2002 statement: https://www.nato.int/docu/update/2002/04-april/e0430a.htm
- U.S. Air Force, 552nd Air Control Wing chronology: https://www.552acw.acc.af.mil/Portals/109/Docs/AFD-070516-071.pdf

### 18 January 2002 — deployment expands

The 552nd Air Control Wing chronology records two additional NATO aircraft deploying to Tinker on January 18, bringing the deployed total to seven.

Source: U.S. Air Force, 552nd Air Control Wing chronology: https://www.552acw.acc.af.mil/Portals/109/Docs/AFD-070516-071.pdf

### 30 April 2002 — Council decides to terminate the operation

Secretary General George Robertson announced that the North Atlantic Council had agreed to terminate Eagle Assist effective May 16. The stated basis was material improvement in the U.S. air-defence posture, enhanced civil-military cooperation, and a recent U.S. evaluation of homeland-security requirements.

At that point NATO reported:

- 830 crew members;
- 13 NATO nations;
- nearly 4,300 patrol hours;
- more than 360 operational sorties.

These are NATO's own operational totals and should be presented as such rather than independently audited statistics.

Source: NATO, “Statement by the Secretary General on the conclusion of Operation Eagle Assist,” April 30, 2002: https://www.nato.int/docu/update/2002/04-april/e0430a.htm

### 16 May 2002 — operation ends; Geilenkirchen recognition context

The termination decision took effect on **May 16, 2002**. This date also anchors the cleared U.S. military photograph sequence of Burns, Robertson, Venturoni, and NATO E-3A personnel at Geilenkirchen already documented elsewhere in this research packet.

Source: NATO, April 30, 2002 statement: https://www.nato.int/docu/update/2002/04-april/e0430a.htm

## Source discrepancy: October 9 versus October 12

The U.S. Air Force Personnel Center's Article 5 NATO Medal page gives a qualifying period beginning **October 12, 2001** and ending May 16, 2002. NATO's contemporary closing statement and the 552nd Air Control Wing chronology instead place the operational/deployment start on **October 9**.

For narrative chronology, use **October 9** as Eagle Assist's launch/deployment date and treat October 12 as the Air Force medal-eligibility period unless a personnel-award discussion specifically requires it. Do not silently harmonize the dates.

Source: U.S. Air Force Personnel Center, “The Article 5 NATO Medal (Operation Eagle Assist)”: https://www.afpc.af.mil/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/873203/the-article-5-nato-medal-operation-eagle-assist/

## Source discrepancy: sortie totals

NATO's contemporary April 30 statement reports **more than 360 operational sorties** and nearly 4,300 hours. A later 552nd Air Control Wing history says NATO AWACS flew approximately **380** Operation Noble Eagle missions totaling nearly 4,300 hours in support of Eagle Assist. For a single reader-facing total, prefer NATO's contemporary **360+ operational sorties** and preserve the later Air Force figure here rather than averaging or combining them.

Source: U.S. Air Force, 552nd Air Control Wing fact sheet: https://www.tinker.af.mil/About-Tinker/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/2461278/552nd-air-control-wing/

## Reader-facing figure opportunity: original chronology

Do **not** reproduce a NATO infographic or timeline. Build an original, typographic chronology from the factual record above. Suggested six stops:

1. **12 Sep 2001** — Allies invoke Article 5 conditionally after the attacks.
2. **4 Oct 2001** — North Atlantic Council agrees measures to operationalise Article 5, including readiness to deploy AWACS support.
3. **9 Oct 2001** — Eagle Assist begins; five NATO E-3s and 180+ personnel deploy toward Tinker AFB.
4. **18 Jan 2002** — two more NATO E-3s deploy; seven aircraft total.
5. **30 Apr 2002** — Council decides to end the mission after the U.S. security assessment and air-defence improvements.
6. **16 May 2002** — Eagle Assist ends; the cleared federal photograph records alliance leadership and AWACS personnel at Geilenkirchen at the operation's conclusion.

A small factual footer can carry the operational scale: **7 AWACS aircraft · 830 crew members · 13 NATO nations · nearly 4,300 hours · 360+ operational sorties.** Attribute these figures to NATO.

### Formatting / accessibility

- Keep the chronology linear and date-led; avoid aircraft silhouettes, flags, NATO-star decoration, or faux command-center graphics.
- Use text labels and line weight, not color alone, to distinguish **alliance decision**, **deployment**, and **termination** events.
- Keep September 12 visually separate from October 9 so the graphic cannot imply that Article 5 invocation and operational deployment were simultaneous.
- Put the closing metrics in one compact endpoint block rather than scattered callouts.
- In print/PDF, keep each date with its event text and keep the operational-scale footer with the figure; where practical keep figure, caption, and source line on one spread.
- In EPUB, encode as an ordered semantic sequence rather than a flattened image whenever practical.
- If rendered as an image for fixed-layout surfaces, provide alt text that repeats the dates and operational totals; do not rely on spatial position alone.
- Do not use the NATO emblem in the figure unless separately cleared; it adds no evidentiary value.

## Claim discipline

**Documented fact:** Eagle Assist followed the Article 5 response, began October 9, used NATO AWACS to support U.S. airspace patrols, expanded to seven deployed aircraft, and ended May 16 after the Council's April 30 decision.

**Documented NATO-reported scale:** seven aircraft; 830 crew members from 13 countries; nearly 4,300 hours; more than 360 sorties.

**Interpretation suitable for prose if signposted:** the sequence makes visible the distance between a diplomatic decision and the months of personnel, aircraft, scheduling, basing, and patrol work required to enact it.

Do **not** claim without further evidence that Burns personally ordered, planned, commanded, scheduled, or operationally supervised Eagle Assist. His presence at the May 16 event documents diplomatic/institutional proximity to the operation's conclusion, not operational command.
