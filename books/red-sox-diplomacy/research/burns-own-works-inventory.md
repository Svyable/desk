# Nicholas Burns — Own Works and Speeches Inventory

This inventory is for *Red Sox Diplomacy*. It prioritizes Burns’s own words over later summaries of his career. Each item should eventually carry exact quotations, chapter targets, and archival status.

## 1990s — public diplomacy and representation

- **1997 — Worcester Polytechnic Institute commencement address** — direct self-description as a long-suffering Red Sox fan; American engagement and leadership themes.
- **1997 — “The Importance of Public Diplomacy”** — useful for Burns’s own theory of representing America beyond formal state-to-state negotiation.
- **1995–1997 State daily briefings** — hundreds of pages of Burns in the act of public representation; recurring baseball exchanges provide a rare record of personality adjacent to institutional authority.

## 2001–2008 — alliance management and high diplomacy

- **2005 — “A Trans-Atlantic Agenda for the Year Ahead”** — alliance maintenance, listening, transatlantic partnership.
- **2005 — “The U.S. and India: The New Strategic Partnership”** — strategic relationship-building and the argument for changing inherited policy.
- **2005 — “The U.S. and India: An Emerging Entente?”** — congressional explanation of the India opening.
- **2005 — U.S. Institute of Peace remarks** — Red Sox Nation and post-2004 “redemption” alongside serious diplomatic argument.
- **2006 — “International Understanding Through Citizen Diplomacy”** — unusually relevant to the book’s core theme: ordinary Americans and non-state encounters as part of national representation.
- **2006 — Washington Institute remarks on Iran** — diplomacy under pressure; willingness to preserve negotiation even amid coercive strategy.
- **2006–2007 — extensive India, Iran, NATO, Afghanistan, Latin America, and Russia remarks** — mine for recurrent vocabulary: partnership, patience, unity, listening, credibility, allies.
- **2007 — Marshall Plan anniversary remarks in Paris** — formal alliance history mixed with Red Sox/Yankees banter.
- **2007 — U.S.-Greece Relations and Regional Issues** — retrospective material for the Athens chapters.
- **2008 — “Global Challenges and Opportunities in U.S. Foreign Policy”** — valedictory career-stage view and useful self-description.

## 2008–2021 — Burns as author and teacher of diplomacy

- **2008 — “We Should Talk to Our Enemies” (Newsweek)** — clear statement of engagement as instrument rather than reward.
- **2012 — “Diplomacy is the best tool for Iran” (Boston Globe)** — argues negotiations should precede another Middle East war; useful for patience and diplomacy-first chapters.
- **2013 — “The Return of American Diplomacy” / related Belfer remarks** — Burns describes diplomacy as a “venerable and antique art” that remains necessary.
- **2014 — “Passage to India” (Foreign Affairs)** — retrospective/strategic interpretation of India policy.
- **2014 — interviews on keeping channels open** — useful for the recurring theme that difficult relationships still require conversation.
- **2018 — *Kissinger the Negotiator* (with James Sebenius and Robert Mnookin)** — negotiation craft, preparation, sequencing, leverage, and dealmaking. Mine carefully for Burns-authored or jointly authored analytical passages rather than attributing every line solely to Burns.
- **2019 — “The State of the State Department and American Diplomacy”** — one of the best direct articulations of his respect for the Foreign Service profession and the consequential nature of representation abroad.
- **2019 — testimony on the U.S.-European alliance** — alliances as an enduring source of power.
- **2020 — “The Indispensable Power: On Restoring American Leadership through Diplomacy”** — argues diplomacy’s time has come after decades of heavy reliance on military power.
- **2020 — pandemic / leadership writings** — useful for coalition-building and global leadership themes.
- **2021 — “The Diplomat as Gardener” (Foreign Affairs)** — essential. Burns’s clearest compact metaphor for diplomacy as maintenance, patience, and relationship-tending before crisis.

## 2021–2025 — Beijing-era Burns

- **2021 Senate confirmation testimony** — direct statement of how he understood the China assignment and U.S. competition/cooperation.
- **2022–2025 Embassy China speeches/interviews/posts** — systematically inventory. Especially valuable: stabilization, channels, people-to-people exchange, strategic competition, military communication, fentanyl/counternarcotics, climate, visas, education, sport, public diplomacy.
- **2024 HKS graduation address** — public service as vocation; “change the world for the better”; Roosevelt’s arena as Burns’s teaching ritual.
- **2025 Foreign Affairs / post-Beijing interviews** — retrospective lessons on China, alliances, keeping allies close, strategic competition, and the limits of diplomacy.

The book should use Burns’s own intellectual vocabulary as the professional counterpoint to the baseball material. Baseball gives the reader the man. His authored corpus gives the reader the diplomat explaining himself.
