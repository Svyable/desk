# Quote Bank Addendum 02 — Community, identity, and Libby

This addendum captures material that was missing from the first Red Sox research pass. It emphasizes the later-career evolution of Burns's Red Sox language and keeps Nicholas Burns's words separate from independently sourced material about Elizabeth “Libby” Baylies.

As elsewhere in this project, re-open the linked source before final manuscript use and keep quotations short.

## May 23, 2013 — Massachusetts sports as community

**Source:** Belfer Center / Nicholas Burns, “Nicholas Burns Addresses Massachusetts Graduates.”

**Quote:** Boston's teams “bind us together as a ‘Community’”.

**Context:** Burns names the Red Sox, Patriots, Bruins, and Celtics as institutions that connect people across Massachusetts and describes the Sox as creating a “Nation” across New England.

**Use:** This is stronger than a simple fandom reference. It supports a later-book argument that Burns understood sports as regional social infrastructure: common memory, common language, and an identity that travels.

**URL:** https://www.belfercenter.org/publication/nicholas-burns-addresses-massachusetts-graduates

## November 13, 2019 — Red Sox identity as a polished opening register

**Source:** Belfer Center / Nicholas Burns, John Courtney Murray, S.J. Lecture, “Our Future: The Pursuit of a ‘Newer World.’”

**Quote:** “an abiding faith in the superiority of the Boston Red Sox”

**Context:** Speaking in New York, Burns jokes that Father Matt Malone is living behind enemy lines as a representative of Red Sox Nation. The baseball line functions as a short New England/New York social bridge before Burns turns to the serious argument of the lecture.

**Use:** Late-career confirmation that the same register visible at the State Department podium in 1997 remained part of Burns's public method more than two decades later.

**URL:** https://www.belfercenter.org/publication/our-future-pursuit-newer-world

## Libby / Elizabeth Baylies — attribution boundary

The research supports a substantive role for Libby in the book, but not a fabricated Red Sox voice.

### Supported

- The State Department's archived biography identifies Burns's wife as Elizabeth Baylies.
- Burns's 2017 American University of Paris commencement address recalls that he and Libby met and fell in love while both were American students in Paris.
- In his 2021 Senate confirmation statement, Burns thanks Libby and describes their overseas government service as a shared life spanning Mauritania, Egypt, Israel, Greece, and Belgium.
- Contemporaneous reporting in Greece identifies Elizabeth Baylies-Burns as an instigator and honorary president of MDA Hellas and connects her to the development of a specialized neuromuscular-disease unit at Aghia Sophia Children's Hospital.
- A State Department feature from the Athens years places Elizabeth Allen Baylies alongside Ambassador Burns in embassy public cultural work.
- Burns's 2019 Versailles remarks use Libby's great-uncle Hervey Allen, and a trip the couple took tracing his First World War route, as the personal doorway into a historical speech.

### Not supported

This research pass has **not** located a reliable transcript, recording, letter, or interview in which Libby herself makes a Red Sox statement.

A 2021 Senate hearing, for example, places Libby in the room while Senator Ed Markey introduces Burns as a proud member of Red Sox Nation. That is Markey's statement, not hers.

**Editorial rule:** Do not put Red Sox words in Libby's mouth. Use her documented public-service work and the shared Foreign Service life to deepen the human story around Burns's public persona.

## Narrative implication

The Red Sox material now supports an evidence-backed progression in Burns's own public language:

**suffering → hope → redemption → community → durable identity**

- 1997: the long-suffering fan and State Department briefing-room jokes.
- 2002: a premature but revealing championship prediction.
- 2004/2005: Burns's own language of redemption.
- 2013: sports as community and regional belonging.
- 2019: Red Sox Nation still functioning as immediate shorthand for home and kinship.

Libby's documented story should run beside that sequence rather than be forced into it. Her value to the manuscript is the shared diplomatic life and her own public-service record, especially in Greece.
