# Chapter 1 visual archive prototype

## Design intent

Chapter 1 is the small-format test for a visual grammar that can scale across *Red Sox Diplomacy* without turning the manuscript into an illustrated magazine.

The governing idea is **evidence as object**. A photograph, transcript, social post, moving image, or designed archival synthesis enters only when it establishes identity, supplies primary evidence, makes a documentary limit visible, or creates a temporal echo that the prose has earned.

The layout should feel closer to a museum catalogue from the future than a conventional trade-book photo insert: generous separation, exact object labels, visible provenance, explicit rights, and enough interpretive language to explain why the object is on the page without telling the reader what to feel.

### ARCHIVE NOTE — Boston College formation, 1974–1978

**Local media:** `../media/ch01-boston-college-formation.svg`  
**Form:** Original editorial synthesis built from sourced documentary traces. It is not a scan, photograph, timeline of simultaneous events, or reconstructed college scene.  
**Sources:** Nicholas Burns's 2002 Boston College commencement address; Boston College's institutional history of the Mods identifying Mod 36A; Boston College's profile of historian Mark O'Connor preserving Burns's Siegfried Sassoon recollection; archived U.S. government biography recording the 1977 Sorbonne certificate.  
**Narrative job:** Give the undergraduate years documentary shape without manufacturing a college-baseball memory. The object places three different kinds of evidence beside one another: an actual student-life address, a remembered intellectual disturbance, and documented movement abroad.  
**Evidentiary limit:** The object does not locate Burns at any specific 1975 or 1978 Red Sox game, does not claim the three traces occurred together, and does not treat O'Connor's teaching or the Sorbonne year as the cause of Burns's later diplomatic career.  
**Rights:** Local original design based on cited text records; no third-party campus photograph is reproduced.

A generic campus photograph was rejected because it would establish atmosphere rather than Burns-specific evidence. Historic and modern Mods photographs remain research-only unless an exact item earns a narrative job and its publication rights are clear.

## Visual grammar to carry forward

1. **The prose remains primary.** Media should punctuate the argument, not decorate every few pages.
2. **Every photograph gets an object label.** Identification, date, maker/source, rights, and narrative relevance should be visible in the reading flow.
3. **Quotes are italicized when quoted as objects.** Use verified language only; quotation marks do not turn paraphrase into evidence.
4. **Social posts should be treated as documents.** Prefer durable facsimiles plus original links over screenshots that inherit disposable platform chrome, unless the interface itself is historically relevant.
5. **Video should be playable without pretending the book is television.** A deliberate card can link to a stable archive copy; the Reader may screen the local source inline when that preserves reading position.
6. **Late images cannot backfill early biography.** A 2024 Red Sox image can show continuity. It cannot prove what Burns felt in 1967, 1975, 1978, or 1986.
7. **Designed synthesis must expose its limits.** A source-backed object can make an absence or relationship legible, but it cannot impersonate an original artifact or collapse separate evidence into a scene.
8. **Rights belong in the object label, not in invisible production notes.** Readers should be able to tell what they are looking at and where it came from.

## Current media state

The two photographic figures and the introductory video are now vendored locally under `media/primary-source/`; the social, video-card, and Boston College formation objects are local SVGs. Chapter 1 no longer depends on remote media for its primary visual assets, although archive links remain visible for provenance.

No Boston College campus or Mods photograph has been added merely for period atmosphere. If a Burns-specific 1974–1978 photograph, yearbook entry, commencement record, or other archival object is recovered with clear rights and a distinct narrative job, it can be evaluated on those terms rather than inserted to fill space.
