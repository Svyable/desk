# Athens Clinton Visit Media Pass 01 — Red Sox Diplomacy

This pass identifies a publication-safe federal visual sequence for the Athens chapters without importing third-party news photography or decorative Greece imagery. The strongest cleared material is the White House Photo Office coverage of President Bill Clinton's November 19–20, 1999 visit to Athens, when Nicholas Burns was U.S. ambassador to Greece.

## Why this sequence matters

The Athens chapters deal with an embassy operating inside an unusually difficult public relationship: Kosovo, anti-American demonstrations, terrorism, Cyprus, Greek-Turkish tensions, and the effort to make official American presence feel less remote and overbearing. Clinton's visit puts several of those pressures into one documented event rather than illustrating them with generic Athens photographs.

The Clinton Presidential Library travel schedule records Clinton arriving in Athens on Friday, November 19, 1999 and staying overnight. The archived White House trip materials describe the Greece stop as a state visit and identify Burns as the U.S. ambassador to Greece in the official delegation.

Sources:
- https://www.clintonlibrary.gov/research/clinton-travel-schedule
- https://clintonwhitehouse4.archives.gov/WH/New/Europe-9911/statements/1999-11-14a.html
- https://clintonwhitehouse4.archives.gov/WH/New/Europe-9911/index.html

## Cleared visual candidates

The archived White House November 20 gallery identifies several Athens images with federal White House photographers:

1. **Clinton and Prime Minister Costas Simitis at their joint press conference in the Central Hall of the Prime Minister's office** — photographer Sharon Farmer.
2. **Departure ceremony at the Presidential Palace in Athens** — photographer William Vasta.
3. **Greek guard outside the Presidential Palace during the departure ceremony** — photographer Sharon Farmer.
4. **President and First Lady being saluted on departure** — photographer Ralph Alswang.
5. **Clinton addressing the Greek community at the Intercontinental Hotel in Athens** — photographer Ralph Alswang.
6. **Clinton speaking to the Greek community in Athens** — photographer Sharon Farmer.

Gallery/source page:
https://clintonwhitehouse4.archives.gov/textonly/WH/new/Europe-9911/photos/day6/index2.html

These are stronger than a generic portrait of Burns because they show the representational machinery he was responsible for as ambassador: presidential visit, Greek government venue, bilateral press, ceremonial protocol, and direct public address.

## Rights and commercial-publication clearance

The William J. Clinton Presidential Library's current audiovisual research guidance states that White House photographs produced by White House photographers are in the public domain. It asks users to credit them as **“Courtesy, William J. Clinton Presidential Library”** and, where appropriate, cite the photographer.

Rights/source page:
https://www.clintonlibrary.gov/research/audio-visual-research

The Library's finding aids independently state that photographs prepared by U.S. government officials as part of their official duties are public domain. The official Clinton presidential records are administered by the National Archives and Records Administration.

This clearance applies to the White House photographs themselves. It does **not** clear unrelated third-party photographs, logos, trademarks, page graphics, or external material merely because it appears on an archived federal webpage.

### Required production credit

Use, at minimum:

> Courtesy, William J. Clinton Presidential Library. Photograph by [photographer].

Where an item-level National Archives Catalog identifier is recovered, add it to the internal provenance record and preferably the production credit/source note.

## Recommended reader-facing use

### Preferred photograph

Use the **Clinton–Simitis joint press conference**, photographed by Sharon Farmer, if a sufficiently large archival master can be recovered. It carries the chapter's diplomatic argument better than ceremonial departure imagery because it shows two governments publicly sharing a room and a microphone during a politically strained visit.

Working caption:

> **Athens, November 20, 1999.** President Bill Clinton and Greek prime minister Costas Simitis hold a joint press conference during Clinton's state visit. Nicholas Burns was the U.S. ambassador in Athens and part of the official delegation. The visit put the bilateral relationship on public display at a moment of sharp Greek opposition to the Kosovo war and continuing U.S. concern over terrorism. *Courtesy, William J. Clinton Presidential Library. Photograph by Sharon Farmer.*

Claim limits: the image does not itself show public opposition, prove a change in Greek opinion, or establish Burns's personal role in any particular presidential decision. Those claims require their own sources.

### Alternate photograph

The **Intercontinental Hotel address to the Greek community**, photographed by Ralph Alswang or Sharon Farmer, is the better choice if the surrounding manuscript passage is about public diplomacy rather than government-to-government negotiation. It visually supports the difference between speaking across a negotiating table and speaking to a broader civic audience.

## Documentary companion material

The archived White House transcript of the November 20 Clinton–Simitis joint press conference is a federal primary source and can support a small documentary sidebar or pull excerpt. It records Simitis describing the relationship through NATO, diaspora ties, and historical cooperation rather than presenting the visit as uncomplicated friendship.

Source:
https://clintonwhitehouse4.archives.gov/WH/New/Europe-9911/remarks/1999-11-20a.html

The same day's remarks to government, business, and community leaders at the Intercontinental Hotel provide a second primary-source layer for the public-diplomacy scene.

Source:
https://clintonwhitehouse4.archives.gov/WH/New/Europe-9911/remarks/1999-11-20b.html

Do not reproduce long transcript blocks. Prefer a short, properly attributed excerpt only where it earns narrative space, with the surrounding argument paraphrased.

## Production specification

- Do not screenshot the archived White House webpage. Obtain the photograph master from the Clinton Library/National Archives record when possible.
- Preserve the original aspect ratio; do not crop away a principal participant merely to fit a page template.
- Keep photograph, caption, and credit line together in print/PDF.
- In EPUB, place alt text before or with the image and keep the caption immediately after it.
- Suggested alt text for the preferred image: **President Bill Clinton and Greek prime minister Costas Simitis standing together at a joint press conference in Athens on November 20, 1999.**
- Do not use the White House wordmark, archived webpage chrome, or a reconstructed presidential seal as decoration.
- Do not colorize or otherwise stylize the archival image beyond ordinary publication correction.

## Evidence context for Chapter 9

A 1999 State Department terrorism report records widespread anti-U.S. demonstrations around Clinton's visit and attacks against U.S., Greek, and third-country targets. It also describes continuing concern about Revolutionary Organization 17 November. That report is useful evidence for the political pressure surrounding the visit, but its photographs or page design are not cleared here.

Source:
https://1997-2001.state.gov/global/terrorism/1999report/europe.html

The archived State biography confirms Burns served as ambassador to Greece from 1997 and oversaw U.S. government programs at the embassy in Athens and consulate in Thessaloniki.

Source:
https://1997-2001.state.gov/about_state/biography/burns.html

## Highest-value next archival action

Recover the **item-level National Archives Catalog records and highest-resolution masters** for the November 20 Athens photographs, especially the Sharon Farmer Clinton–Simitis press-conference image and the Intercontinental Hotel public-address sequence. Record the National Archives identifier, exact image number, dimensions, and download/master URL before committing a binary asset to `media/`.
