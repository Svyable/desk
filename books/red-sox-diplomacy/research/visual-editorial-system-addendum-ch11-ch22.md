# Red Sox Diplomacy — visual editorial system addendum, Chapters 11–22

## Why this addendum exists

The canonical `visual-editorial-system.md` was established while Chapters 1–10 were being built. Chapters 11–22 introduced a second-generation vocabulary during a period of heavy concurrent branch activity. This addendum records those forms without rewriting the large canonical file blindly.

The same governing rule applies: **evidence as object**. A form becomes reusable only after a chapter proves it can expose evidence, mechanism, uncertainty, source distance or authority more clearly than prose alone.

## Late-book families

### SIGNAL REVERSAL

Use when the direction of institutional communication reverses in a documented crisis.

Chapter 11 use: allies begin calling the U.S. representative before he can reliably reconnect with Washington on September 11, 2001.

Limit: a signal of solidarity is not yet a formal state commitment.

### AUTHORITY GAP

Use when an official has standing and responsibility but incomplete instructions, information or authorization.

Chapter 11 use: Burns remains U.S. Ambassador to NATO while evacuated institutions temporarily constrain access to Washington.

Limit: authority gap is not institutional collapse and must not imply freelance policymaking.

### CONDITIONAL CLAUSE

Use when a legal or institutional commitment depends on an explicit condition that popular retellings tend to erase.

Chapter 12 use: NATO's September 12 Article 5 statement remained conditional on the attacks having been directed from abroad.

Limit: preserve the condition visibly; do not simplify history by deleting legal/evidentiary sequencing.

### SUPPORT PACKAGE / OPERATIONAL LEDGER

Use when a declaratory commitment becomes a list of concrete enabling measures or deployed capability.

Chapter 12 use: NATO's eight measures and Operation Eagle Assist.

Limit: a selected ledger is not automatically a total cost, total contribution or complete order of battle.

### TWO PREMISES

Use when opposing parties are best understood as operating from incompatible but documentable institutional premises rather than hero/obstructionist roles.

Chapter 13 use: U.S. collective-defense logic versus France's concern that early Turkey planning could make war appear institutionally assumed.

Limit: preserve each side's stated premise without declaring equivalence or hidden motive.

### CONSENSUS PATH

Use when procedure itself explains how an institution moved from deadlock to a narrower agreement.

Chapter 13 use: silence procedure, Article 4, North Atlantic Council impasse and pre-existing Defence Planning Committee route.

Limit: procedural movement must not be narrated as agreement on the larger political dispute.

### RETURN LEDGER

Use when continued shared work after a rupture is evidence of institutional survival.

Chapter 13 use: NATO returns to common work in Afghanistan and bounded support to Poland in Iraq after the 2003 rupture.

Limit: return to work is not retroactive agreement about the original conflict.

### GATE SEQUENCE

Use when an agreement must pass through multiple sovereign or institutional jurisdictions before becoming operative.

Chapter 14 use: leaders' bargain → separation plan → U.S. law → 123 Agreement → IAEA → NSG → Congress.

Limit: passing one gate is not equivalent to completing the deal.

### HANDOFF LEDGER

Use when the subject's major contribution ends before the process itself does and successor roles need to remain distinct.

Chapter 14 use: Nicholas Burns helps negotiate the bilateral 123 text; later actors carry the IAEA/NSG/congressional phase.

Limit: institutional continuity does not make successors extensions of the protagonist.

### ENTRY CONDITION

Use when parties disagree not only over the desired agreement but over what must happen before substantive negotiation can begin.

Chapter 15 use: U.S. 2006 suspension condition versus the reported Iranian position that suspension should not be the price of entry.

Limit: label government positions as positions; do not convert one side's framing into narrator-certified law or motive.

### PRESSURE LEDGER

Use for sequenced coercive or restrictive measures when the central editorial danger is confusing accumulated pressure with achieved outcome.

Chapter 15 use: UNSC Resolutions 1696, 1737, 1747 and 1803.

Limit: sanctions prove agreed measures, not political compliance or eventual success.

### OPEN FILE

Use at a transition point where desired outcomes remain unresolved but institutional architecture survives for successors.

Chapter 15 use: no comprehensive Iran bargain at Burns's departure, but P5+1 / IAEA / UNSC machinery remains.

Limit: inheritance is not authorship of a later agreement.

### ABSENCE OBJECT

Use when the archive's missing state is itself narratively important and a designed gap is more honest than reconstruction.

Chapter 16 use: precise 2003 Brussels Red Sox loss versus undocumented 2004 championship-night room.

Limit: unknown must remain unknown. Do not use a contextual image to imply a missing scene.

### AFTERLIFE LEDGER

Use when later public language changes the interpretation of a documented event without supplying the missing contemporaneous scene.

Chapter 16 use: `redemption` (2005), deliverance from inevitable losing (2006), remembered suffering (2012).

Limit: public language chronology is not private psychology.

### ROLE SHIFT

Use when the same person's experience changes institutional status.

Chapter 17 use: delegated government authority becomes contestable practitioner authority at Harvard.

Limit: practitioner experience supplies evidence and credibility, not final interpretation.

### TEACHING ARCHIVE

Use when participant memory is deliberately placed in a structure of comparison with documents, other witnesses, scholarship and outcomes.

Chapter 17 use: American Secretaries of State Project.

Limit: oral history / retrospective memory is evidence, not verdict.

### CHANNEL RECOVERY

Use when communication mechanisms resume after crisis while substantive competition or distrust remains.

Chapter 18 use: 2023 balloon crisis → Blinken Beijing visit → Woodside follow-up.

Limit: recovery of contact is not recovery of trust and not personal authorship by one diplomat.

### CONTESTED CHANNEL

Use when both governments agree that a channel or category matters but publicly dispute what is obstructing it.

Chapter 18 use: U.S. and Chinese 2024 claims over educational/cultural exchange.

Limit: preserve competing official claims unless independent evidence resolves specific incidents.

### REGISTER SWITCH

Use when the same speaker visibly changes from personal/social language to official/institutional language and the boundary itself is evidence.

Chapter 19 use: 2007 Paris Red Sox/Yankees joke → U.S.-French alliance/Marshall Plan remarks.

Limit: social shorthand may locate people inside diplomacy; it does not explain policy outcomes.

### ANALOGY BREAK

Use when a recurring metaphor is most useful at the point where its structural assumptions stop transferring.

Chapter 19 use: Red Sox–Yankees rivalry versus sovereign-state competition.

Limit: label as editorial synthesis and make the broken equivalence explicit.

### AUTHORIZED INTENT

Use when communication abundance makes authenticity, authorization, intended weight and accountability more important to distinguish.

Chapter 20 use: modern diplomacy / AI-era communication.

Limit: this is institutional analysis, not a claim that humans possess mystical capacities or that AI can never exercise judgment.

### HUMAN CONTROL

Use for a narrowly documented high-stakes principle where responsibility and automation intersect.

Chapter 20 use: Biden-Xi November 2024 human-control principle for nuclear-use decisions.

Limit: a principle is not an implementation regime, treaty or solved governance architecture.

### TWO-WAY REPRESENTATION

Use when representation needs to be shown as both advocacy outward and reporting/correction inward.

Chapter 21 use: explain America abroad / explain the host country back to America.

Limit: the ambassador is an authorized advocate, not a neutral conduit; `national self-correction` is editorial synthesis, not guaranteed truth.

### HANDOFF

Use when office continuity is more accurate than a final-score ending.

Chapter 22 use: Burns's own 2025 relay-race metaphor for handing the China post to his successor.

Limit: succession does not imply endorsement of later policy.

### REVISION OBJECT

Use when a participant explicitly revises a consequential earlier judgment and both states of the record need to remain visible.

Chapter 22 use: Burns's 2003 support for the Iraq intervention and September 2026 public regret/reversal.

Limit: revision is reassessment, not erasure or absolution.

## End-of-book restraint rule

Visual density should decline as the argument becomes synthetic.

- Ch 18 may carry three objects because it is a visually documented posting and introduces a new real figure.
- Ch 19–20 need two each because their central work is conceptual boundary-setting.
- Ch 21 needs one.
- Ch 22 needs two because handoff and revision are distinct documentary facts.
- The epilogue needs none.

This taper is intentional. A future-facing book should know when the interface has finished its evidentiary work.
