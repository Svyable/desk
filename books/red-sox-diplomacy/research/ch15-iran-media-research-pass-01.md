# Chapter 15 media/research pass 01 — Iran: When Patience Does Not Win

Scope: Chapter 15 only. This pass strengthens the documentary chain behind the 2006–08 Iran nuclear diplomacy and specifies one original, publication-safe figure. It does **not** promote third-party photography whose commercial-book reuse rights are unclear.

## Release recommendation: original figure — `THE DOOR AND THE LEVER`

**Purpose.** Put the chapter's two linked mechanisms on one page without turning sanctions into a scorecard: (1) the May 31, 2006 U.S. offer to join the EU3 negotiations if Iran fully and verifiably suspended enrichment; (2) the December 23, 2006 Security Council sanctions architecture, explicitly reversible if Iran suspended and returned to the diplomatic track.

**Design.** Two horizontal lanes sharing a central `SUSPENSION` node. Upper lane: `EU3 talks already existed` → `U.S. offers to join` → `full + verifiable suspension required` → `negotiations`. Lower lane: `Resolution 1696 demand` → `non-compliance` → `Resolution 1737 targeted measures` → `measures suspended/terminated on compliance`. A narrow side note should identify the IAEA as verifier, not as a negotiating party or sanctions author.

Do not use red/green success/failure encoding. Do not draw Tehran as moving toward a bomb, depict a weapon, or convert safeguards uncertainty into a weapons finding. Do not imply that Russia and China shared Washington's motives merely because Resolution 1737 was unanimous. The U.N. meeting record expressly preserves Russian and Chinese descriptions of sanctions as limited/reversible instruments in service of negotiation.

**Suggested caption.** `THE DOOR AND THE LEVER — On May 31, 2006, Washington offered to join the European-led nuclear talks if Iran fully and verifiably suspended enrichment-related activity. On December 23, the Security Council unanimously imposed targeted measures after Iran did not comply with the Council's suspension demand, while writing reversibility into the sanctions architecture. The IAEA's role was verification. The object shows linked diplomatic mechanisms, not shared motives among the six powers and not a finding that Iran had decided to build a nuclear weapon.`

**Alt text.** `Two-lane chronology of 2006 Iran nuclear diplomacy. The upper lane shows existing EU3 talks, the May 31 U.S. offer to join them, the requirement for full and verifiable suspension, and the proposed negotiation. The lower lane shows the Security Council suspension demand, Iran's non-compliance, Resolution 1737 sanctions, and the provision to suspend or terminate measures upon compliance. Both lanes meet at a central suspension requirement, with the IAEA identified separately as verifier.`

**Production.** Full text width in print; keep figure and caption together. Minimum 9-point labels at final trim. In EPUB, render as structured SVG with text preserved as text and reading order upper lane → lower lane → IAEA note. At narrow widths, stack the two lanes vertically. Every relationship must survive grayscale and high-contrast display; never rely on color alone.

**Rights.** Original project synthesis/redraw from factual public records. No copied artwork, logos, photographs, or proprietary diagram geometry. Credit: `Graphic by the author; sources: U.S. Department of State, United Nations Security Council, IAEA.`

## Primary-source provenance

### 1. May 31, 2006 U.S. entry offer

- **Institution:** U.S. Department of State, archived 2001–2009 site.
- **Speaker:** Secretary of State Condoleezza Rice.
- **Date:** May 31, 2006.
- **Record:** `Press Conference on Iran` and same-day ABC *Nightline* interview.
- **URLs:** https://2001-2009.state.gov/secretary/rm/2006/67103.htm ; https://2001-2009.state.gov/secretary/rm/2006/67210.htm
- **What it establishes:** The United States offered to join talks already underway between the EU3 and Iran if Iran suspended enrichment activities fully and verifiably. Rice also presented the alternative path as sanctions/isolation if Iran did not suspend.
- **Source class:** contemporaneous U.S. government statement; authoritative for the U.S. offer and U.S. framing, not for Iranian motive.
- **Rights/reuse:** U.S. federal government text is generally public domain under 17 U.S.C. §105 when produced by federal employees in official duties. Quote sparingly and retain source attribution; the original figure above relies on facts rather than reproduced page design.

### 2. Resolution 1737 and the coalition's non-identical motives

- **Institution:** United Nations Security Council, Meetings Coverage and Press Releases.
- **Date:** December 23, 2006.
- **Record:** `Security Council Imposes Sanctions on Iran for Failure to Halt Uranium Enrichment, Unanimously Adopting Resolution 1737 (2006)`.
- **URL:** https://press.un.org/en/2006/sc8928.doc.htm
- **What it establishes:** Resolution 1737 was adopted unanimously; it imposed targeted restrictions tied to proliferation-sensitive nuclear activity and required IAEA verification. The measures were designed to be suspended if Iran suspended enrichment/reprocessing and complied. The same record preserves materially different national explanations: Russia described a solution as exclusively political/diplomatic; China called sanctions a means rather than an end and stressed that they were limited and reversible; Iran rejected the suspension demand and argued that it impaired rights it claimed under the NPT.
- **Source class:** contemporaneous U.N. meeting coverage. The page itself warns that the release is information for media and `not an official record`; use it as authoritative U.N. contemporaneous coverage and use the adopted resolution text for exact legal language when necessary.
- **Rights/reuse:** Do not assume U.N. web content is public domain. Use factual synthesis and short quotations only where legally justified; do not reproduce the page, U.N. marks, or protected photography. The proposed figure is an original factual redraw.

### 3. IAEA report-status boundary — a useful publication warning

- **Institution:** International Atomic Energy Agency.
- **Date:** April 28, 2006.
- **Record:** `Report on Iran Nuclear Safeguards Sent to Agency's Board and UN Security Council`.
- **URL:** https://www.iaea.org/newscenter/pressreleases/report-iran-nuclear-safeguards-sent-agency%C2%B4s-board-and-un-security-council
- **What it establishes:** The IAEA explicitly stated that the April 2006 safeguards report's circulation was restricted and that the Agency could not authorize public release unless the Board of Governors and Security Council decided otherwise.
- **Publication consequence:** **Do not reproduce or source a facsimile from an unofficial mirror of the restricted 2006 report.** Use the IAEA's public metadata, later formally derestricted reports, and U.N. records instead. This is a provenance rule, not merely a citation preference.
- **Rights/reuse:** IAEA material is not U.S. federal public domain. Treat IAEA page design, photographs, charts, and document facsimiles as copyrighted unless an item-level reuse statement says otherwise.

### 4. Derestricted IAEA technical record suitable for factual extraction

- **Institution:** International Atomic Energy Agency, Board of Governors.
- **Document:** GOV/2007/58, `Implementation of the NPT Safeguards Agreement and Relevant Provisions of Security Council Resolutions 1737 (2006) and 1747 (2007) in the Islamic Republic of Iran`.
- **Date/status:** November 2007; document marked `Derestricted 23 November 2007`.
- **URL:** https://www.iaea.org/sites/default/files/documents/gov2007-58.pdf
- **What it can safely support:** chronology of Agency technical work, Iranian declarations as attributed to Iran, inspection/interview steps, and the Agency's expressly bounded conclusions. For example, the report says the Agency was not then in a position, on available information, to draw conclusions about the original underlying nature of parts of the program and tied further conclusions to completeness verification.
- **Source class:** primary institutional technical record, formally derestricted.
- **Rights/reuse:** `Derestricted` means publicly releasable; it does **not** by itself mean copyright-free. Prefer paraphrase/factual extraction and original graphics. Do not reproduce IAEA page design, tables, diagrams, or photography without item-level permission.

## Manuscript integration guidance

Place `THE DOOR AND THE LEVER` after the paragraph ending `The door existed. Iran did not accept the threshold on the required terms.` It can replace, or be combined editorially with, `ENTRY CONDITION 01`; do not run two nearly identical threshold diagrams back-to-back. The new object earns its space only if it also shows the reversibility mechanism that later makes `PRESSURE LEDGER 01` legible.

Keep `PRESSURE LEDGER 01`, but its visual hierarchy should subordinate resolution numbers to the change in mechanism: demand → targeted restrictions → expanded restrictions. Add a small typographic note, `sanctions ≠ agreement`, rather than another explanatory paragraph in the manuscript.

For print/EPUB consistency, use the same label grammar across all Chapter 15 figures: object title in small caps, one-sentence evidentiary claim, one-sentence boundary/what-the-object-does-not-prove, then sources. Captions should not contain a second miniature essay.

## Rejected media / rights gates

- **Generic IAEA Board-room photography:** legally and narratively weak without item-level reuse terms; it does not identify the Iran negotiation at issue.
- **Commercial news photographs of Natanz, Iranian officials, Burns, Rice, Solana, or Security Council sessions:** research-only unless an individual license explicitly permits commercial book/ebook reuse.
- **Unofficial mirrors of restricted IAEA reports:** reject even when downloadable. Public availability on a third-party server does not cure the IAEA's documented circulation restriction.
- **Nuclear-facility satellite imagery:** reject unless provenance, underlying image rights, annotation rights, date, and commercial reuse are all clear. It also risks implying technical conclusions the chapter does not make.
- **Mushroom clouds, centrifuge stock art, flags, or weapons silhouettes:** reject as decorative and analytically distorting.
