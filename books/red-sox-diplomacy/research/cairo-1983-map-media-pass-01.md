# Cairo 1983 map and visual-context pass 01

## Editorial purpose

This packet supports Chapter 4, **“Cairo: Learning the Away Park,”** with rights-clean geographic and geopolitical context for Nicholas Burns’s first Foreign Service posting. It does not add manuscript prose in this pass. The preferred production move is an original map/redraw, not decorative archival photography.

## Documentary anchor: Burns in Cairo, 1983–1985

A contemporary U.S. Department of State biography states that Burns began his Foreign Service career in the Middle East and served as **Vice Consul and Staff Assistant to the Ambassador in Cairo, Egypt, from 1983 to 1985**. This is a federal government source and should remain the basic chronology anchor for the chapter.

- Institution: U.S. Department of State
- Page: “Biography: Amb. R. Nicholas Burns”
- URL: https://1997-2001.state.gov/about_state/biography/burns.html
- Relevant claim: Vice Consul and Staff Assistant to the Ambassador in Cairo, 1983–1985
- Rights/use: factual government webpage; quote only briefly if needed and otherwise paraphrase.

## Approved map source: CIA, *Southwest Asia* (1980)

The Library of Congress holds a 1980 Central Intelligence Agency map titled **Southwest Asia**. It is close enough to Burns’s 1983 arrival to show the regional political geography he entered without silently importing present-day borders or labels.

- Creator: United States Central Intelligence Agency
- Title: *Southwest Asia*
- Date: 1980
- Repository: Library of Congress, Geography and Map Division
- LCCN: 81692538
- LOC item: https://www.loc.gov/item/81692538/
- Digital resource: https://www.loc.gov/resource/g7420.ct000719/
- Medium: color map, 84 × 109 cm
- Catalog note: “504679 11-80 (544991).” Includes glossary.
- Rights statement: **“The content of the Library of Congress Geography and Map Division digitized collections is free to use and reuse unless a Rights Advisory statement is present that indicates otherwise.”** No contrary item-level Rights Advisory is shown on this record.
- Required/preferred credit: **Library of Congress, Geography and Map Division.**
- Commercial-publication suitability: approved under the LOC collection rights statement. The underlying map is also a U.S. federal-government work (CIA), but production should preserve the LOC credit line because LOC supplies the digitized object.

### Recommended production use

Do **not** reproduce the full CIA sheet merely because it is available. Use it as the geographic and period-label source for an original figure:

**CAIRO 01 — “Cairo in the region Burns entered, 1983”**

Suggested content:

- Cairo marked prominently.
- Mediterranean, Egypt, Israel, Lebanon, Syria, Jordan and Saudi Arabia shown only as needed for orientation.
- Washington omitted unless the figure explicitly becomes a posting-route map.
- A small date label: **Regional geography, c. 1980–1983**.
- If manuscript context requires it, a restrained callout can note that Burns arrived in Cairo in 1983 as vice consul and staff assistant to the ambassador; that annotation derives from the State Department biography, not from the CIA map.

The figure should explain where Cairo sits in the diplomatic geography of the chapter. It should not imply that the CIA map represents Burns’s own briefing material or that he personally used it.

### Caption

**Cairo in the regional geography of Burns’s first Foreign Service posting.** Nicholas Burns served as vice consul and staff assistant to the U.S. ambassador in Cairo from 1983 to 1985. Original redraw using period geographic reference from a 1980 CIA map held by the Library of Congress.

### Alt text

Simplified period map centered on Egypt and Cairo, showing Cairo’s position in the eastern Mediterranean and Middle East during Nicholas Burns’s 1983–1985 posting.

### Print / EPUB treatment

- Prefer a single-column or approximately half-page map in print; the point is orientation, not atlas-level detail.
- Keep caption and credit with the figure across page breaks.
- Use line weights and labels that remain distinct in grayscale; do not depend on color to distinguish borders from water.
- EPUB: place immediately after the first developed paragraph that establishes Burns’s Cairo posting, before later interpretation of what the posting taught him.
- Keep alt text descriptive and shorter than the caption; do not repeat the credit line in alt text.
- Do not use flag icons, baseball diamonds, travel-path dotted lines, faux passport stamps or Cold War texture.

## Approved contextual source: Reagan Library Mubarak meeting, January 27, 1983

The Ronald Reagan Presidential Library catalog records White House photographs from President Hosni Mubarak’s January 27, 1983 visit to Washington. One contact-sheet record identifies Reagan, Mubarak, Egyptian officials including Boutros Ghali and Osama el-Baz, and U.S. officials including George Shultz; another records Reagan, Mubarak and Don Regan.

- Institution: Ronald Reagan Presidential Library
- Date: January 27, 1983
- Record C12670 (01): https://www.reaganlibrary.gov/archives/photo/c12670-01
- Record C12674 (01): https://www.reaganlibrary.gov/archives/photo/c12674-01
- Event: meeting with President of Egypt Hosni Mubarak
- Editorial value: establishes high-level U.S.–Egypt contact in the same calendar year Burns arrived in Cairo and gives names for further primary-source research.
- Status: **context source only in this pass**. Do not place the photograph in the book until the Reagan Library item’s reproduction/rights terms and available master file are captured explicitly. Federal authorship is likely but should not be substituted for item-level production provenance.

The State Department Office of the Historian independently lists Mubarak’s January 26–31, 1983 trip as an official working visit:

- https://history.state.gov/departmenthistory/visits/1983

This can support chronology, but it does **not** establish a connection between Burns and that Washington visit.

## Historical Cairo map: useful but not the preferred chapter figure

LOC’s *General map of Cairo* (Survey of Egypt, 1920) is a detailed city map with an inset extending toward Heliopolis.

- Creator: Egypt, Maṣlaḥat al-Misāḥah / Survey of Egypt
- Date: 1920
- LCCN: 2009580102
- URL: https://www.loc.gov/item/2009580102/
- Repository: Library of Congress, Geography and Map Division

It is potentially valuable for a later urban-history sidebar, but it is **not approved here as a proxy for Cairo in 1983**. Sixty-three years separate the map from Burns’s posting. Using it without a specific historical argument would turn age into atmosphere and risk misleading the reader about the city Burns encountered.

## Held / rejected imagery

### 1991 Cairo Public Affairs Officer residence photographs

Third-party public-domain aggregators surface National Archives photographs labeled “Cairo - Public Affairs Officer Residency - 1991” and claim “No known copyright restrictions.” These are **not approved for production use in this pass**.

Reason: the surfaced pages point generically to the National Archives rather than exposing a stable NARA catalog identifier in the retrieved record. The date is also six years after Burns left Cairo. Until the canonical NARA item is recovered with its series/item identifier and rights/provenance captured directly, these images add more uncertainty than value.

### Generic modern Cairo / embassy photography

Do not use modern skyline, pyramids, embassy exterior or street photography simply to make Chapter 4 look illustrated. Such images either misdate the scene or add decorative Egypt shorthand without clarifying Burns’s actual diplomatic environment.

## Factual boundaries for manuscript integration

This packet supports:

- Burns served in Cairo from 1983 to 1985 as vice consul and staff assistant to the ambassador.
- Cairo occupied the regional geography shown by period U.S. government maps.
- Mubarak made an official working visit to Washington in January 1983.

It does **not** support:

- that Burns attended or worked on Mubarak’s January 1983 Washington visit;
- that Burns used the cited CIA maps;
- that a particular regional crisis personally shaped Burns unless a Burns-specific source says so;
- invented embassy interiors, streets, weather, commutes, conversations or sensory details;
- a claim that Cairo was an “away park” in any literal institutional sense.
