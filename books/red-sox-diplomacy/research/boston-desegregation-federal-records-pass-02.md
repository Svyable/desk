## Cleared primary-source anchor: Morgan v. Hennigan complaint

**Object:** first page / complaint in *Tallulah Morgan et al. v. James W. Hennigan et al.*, U.S. District Court for the District of Massachusetts, Civil Action 72-0911-G, filed March 14, 1972.

**Institution:** National Archives at Boston, Record Group 21, Records of District Courts of the United States.

**National Archives identifier:** 4713835.

**Author/source authority:** federal court case file; the pleading was filed by the plaintiffs and is preserved in a federal judicial record. Do not describe the underlying pleading as a work authored by NARA merely because NARA holds and digitizes it.

**Source:** https://www.archives.gov/boston/highlights/morgan-v-hennigan

**Direct NARA educational scan:** https://www.archives.gov/files/education/lessons/slavery/images/morgan-complaint-1.pdf

**Rights status:** safe to quote as a U.S. federal judicial record and primary legal document, but do not infer that every privately authored exhibit in the 54-cubic-foot case file is public domain. For a facsimile reproduction, retain the NARA record citation and verify the selected scan/page has no third-party material before production ingestion. The complaint page itself is a court-filed legal pleading; use only the page needed to establish the action and relief sought.

**Why it matters:** NARA's item page fixes the chronology cleanly: the class action was filed March 14, 1972; Judge W. Arthur Garrity Jr.'s decision came in 1974. The complaint makes the dispute legible as litigation before it became national imagery. Its requested remedies explicitly included transportation among the methods of integration, which is more useful to the book than treating “busing” as a free-floating culture-war label.

## Scale and enforcement: NARA contextual facts

NARA's Boston teaching guide describes the *Morgan* case file as **54 cubic feet** and says it records, among other things, the school climate, facilities, and **1,792 police present on the first day of school in 1975**. Those figures are useful scene-setting because they measure the administrative and coercive scale without requiring invented atmosphere.

**Source:** https://www.archives.gov/files/boston/education/teach-constitution.pdf

Use the 1,792 figure only with its exact NARA framing (“police present on the first day of school in 1975”). Do not silently convert it into Boston Police Department staffing, federal marshals, arrests, or a claim about a particular school.

## Original figure concept: from complaint to enforcement

If the marshals memorandum clears at item level, build an original four-stage chronology rather than a collage of copyrighted news photographs:

1. **14 Mar 1972 — complaint filed.** *Morgan v. Hennigan* enters federal court.
2. **21 Jun 1974 — liability decision.** Garrity finds intentional segregation by the Boston School Committee.
3. **1974–75 — implementation and resistance.** Court supervision moves from judgment to school assignment, transportation, staffing, facilities, and public order.
4. **1975 — federal enforcement question.** Pair the U.S. Marshals memorandum with NARA's documented 1,792-police first-day figure only after the memorandum's exact date is resolved.

**Design:** one horizontal rule with four dated nodes for print/PDF; a vertical ordered chronology for narrow EPUB. No borrowed map tiles, newspaper mastheads, team marks, or protest photography.

**Caption:**

> **A lawsuit becomes an operating system.** Boston school desegregation moved from a 1972 complaint to a 1974 liability judgment and then into the harder work of assignments, transportation, staffing, facilities, policing, and federal enforcement. The paperwork is the point: constitutional judgment had to become daily administration.
