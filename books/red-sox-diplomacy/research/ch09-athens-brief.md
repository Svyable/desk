# Chapter 9 Brief — Athens

## Status

Research brief. Chapter 8 is drafted and ends with the July 1997 farewell pointing directly toward Greece.

## Working question

What changes when a diplomat leaves the briefing-room role of explaining America and becomes the person who must represent America inside another country's history, suspicions, institutions, and daily politics?

## Documentary center

Athens should not be written primarily as setup for the baseball project in Chapter 10. It needs its own diplomatic center.

The strongest evidence already assembled points toward **embassy openness and representational style**:

- David L. Marcus's 1999 profile opens inside an Embassy Athens country-team meeting and shows Burns working details of commercial diplomacy around Governor Mel Carnahan's visit and Boeing F-15 advocacy.
- The same profile describes Burns's Boston office markers, including a Red Sox cap signed by President Clinton, while also quoting his goal of persuading Greeks that the era of American intervention and "Big Brother" behavior should be over and the bilateral relationship should become more normal, mature, open, and rational. Exact quotations require clean-source re-verification before publication.
- The Gropius embassy building had been visually obscured by vines and security measures; Burns ordered vines cut back, the broken fountain repaired, and the facade illuminated. Foreign Service Journal material independently supports the physical-opening episode.
- Harvard Crimson coverage in 1998 identifies strengthening the U.S.-Greek relationship as his first priority and emphasizes personal relationships between political leaders.
- Barbara Nielsen's oral history, as Cultural Affairs Officer in Athens from 1999 to 2001, says Burns was strongly interested in public diplomacy and set the tone for the mission.
- Greek press records show sustained meetings with journalists rather than a purely ceremonial ambassadorial posture.
- The 1999 State background material gives a concrete country-team roster, useful for reconstructing the embassy as an institution rather than a one-man performance.

## Strongest dramatic center

**Open the embassy.**

Not literally in the sense of eliminating security, but visibly and institutionally.

The physical-building episode gives the chapter a concrete image without invention: an embassy designed by Walter Gropius had become screened, fortified, and partially hidden. Burns's decision to expose more of the facade, repair the fountain, and light the building can be treated as an act of representation only to the extent contemporary sources support that interpretation.

The chapter can then widen from architecture to behavior:

- country-team management;
- journalist contact;
- Greek-American civic relationships;
- high-school and university outreach;
- commercial diplomacy;
- ceremonial residence use;
- the effort to make the bilateral relationship look less like patron-client management and more like a mature alliance.

## Relationship to Chapter 10

Chapter 9 is about **the ambassador and the embassy**.

Chapter 10 is about **the baseball network**.

Do not spend the best Greece-baseball evidence here. Chapter 9 may mention that Burns's Boston identity traveled with him and that baseball would later become one channel of public diplomacy, but the Angelos/Sarbanes/MLB/Derksen chain belongs in Chapter 10.

## Possible opening

The country-team meeting in the Marcus profile is promising because it immediately puts Burns inside the work rather than introducing him through ceremony.

The detail to verify cleanly: Burns asking how to pronounce "Missouri" because Governor Mel Carnahan was coming to promote Boeing F-15s.

If the clean source can be obtained, this can open the chapter as a small but revealing act of preparation: the ambassador worrying over pronunciation before a visit tied to a major defense sale.

## Possible second scene

The embassy facade/fountain/vines episode.

This has symbolic power but should not be overwritten. Avoid turning routine facilities work into a grand theory unless Burns or contemporaries explicitly connected the changes to openness/public diplomacy.

## Narrative thesis

Athens is where Burns's representational style becomes visible at full ambassadorial scale.

At State, personality operated inside a podium controlled by institutional rules. In Greece, the institution itself—the embassy building, residence, country team, public events, press relationships, commercial advocacy, cultural programming—became part of what he was representing.

The question was no longer simply: what can America responsibly say?

It was: what does America look like from here?

## Historical context to handle carefully

U.S.-Greek relations carried deep historical memory involving the Greek Civil War, junta era, Cyprus, Greek-Turkish tensions, NATO, and recurring suspicion of American intervention. These are essential context but should not swallow Burns's biography.

Do not flatten Greek politics into anti-Americanism. Use specific polling, reporting, political statements, demonstrations, or counterpart testimony where available.

## Guardrails

Do not invent:

- Burns's sensory impressions of Athens;
- private reactions to anti-American protests;
- dialogue inside the country-team meeting beyond verified text;
- motives for building changes beyond sourced explanations;
- causal claims that the Red Sox cap or baseball made Greeks trust him;
- a claim that he personally transformed U.S.-Greek relations;
- a clean break between a "bad old" interventionist America and Burns's tenure.
