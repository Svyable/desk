### Why this image earns space

It documents the civic/national response without pretending to show Burns or to reconstruct his private experience. It also gives Chapter 17 a useful visual counterpoint to the manuscript's Fenway discussion: Boston's public rituals after the bombing were not confined to baseball. The cathedral, hospitals, streets, finish line and ballpark belonged to different institutions performing different civic functions.

**Do not caption the photograph as `Boston Strong`, as proof that sport healed the city, or as evidence of Burns's feelings.**

## Primary-source enrichment — Obama's April 18 Boston remarks

The archived White House video/transcript is explicitly labeled **Public Domain** and is useful as a primary source even if no excerpt is ultimately printed:

https://obamawhitehouse.archives.gov/photos-and-video/video/2013/04/18/president-obama-speaks-interfaith-prayer-service-boston

The speech itself joins several institutions the chapter is discussing: Patriots' Day, the Marathon, Fenway, immigration, universities, hospitals, civic ritual and Boston's global identity. It records Obama noting that fans took the T to see the Sox at Fenway that morning and describing Boston as a city that receives runners, students and immigrants from around the world.

**Editorial use:** paraphrase for scene-setting or use only a very short verified excerpt if it genuinely improves the chapter. Public-domain status removes copyright risk from the federal text, but quotation should still be proportionate and attributed. Do not use presidential rhetoric as evidence that the whole city experienced the week uniformly.

If the White House photograph cannot be cleared at item level, prefer an original typographic chronology rather than protected news/sports photography.

## Rights/relevance rejects

Do **not** promote the following without a separate rights clearance:

- MLB/Red Sox game, clubhouse, championship or Fenway photography merely because it is historically apt; league/team archive availability is not a commercial-book license.
- Newspaper front pages or screenshots of news sites.
- Broadcast frames, scorebugs or video stills.
- Boston Strong marks as decorative branding; factual reference in prose is different from reproducing protected logo/artwork.
- Marathon photography from agencies or commercial photographers without an explicit commercial publication license.
- Social-media photographs or crowd images with unclear creator/provenance.
- Surveillance images of the bombing suspects as chapter decoration. Even where a federal investigative use may supply a rights route, they add little to this chapter's argument and shift attention toward perpetrators.
