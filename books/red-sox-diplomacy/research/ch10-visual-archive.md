# Chapter 10 visual archive — Bringing Baseball to Greece

## Method

The title risks assigning ownership to Nicholas Burns that the evidence does not support. The visual system must therefore work against the title's shorthand rather than reinforce it.

The chapter uses three documentary objects:

1. **ORIGIN LEDGER 01** — several legitimate beginnings instead of one founder;
2. **CREDIT MAP 01** — separate work lanes instead of a Burns-centered network;
3. **TWO SCOREBOARDS 01** — the Olympic objective and the domestic-development objective measured separately.

A photograph would be welcome only if its rights and provenance are strong enough and it adds a distinct evidentiary function. Contemporary ESPN, CNN, Washington Post, Greek press, and Olympic imagery is useful research but should not be treated as publication-cleared merely because it is visible online.

## ORIGIN LEDGER 01 — projects can begin more than once

**Local media:** `../media/ch10-origin-ledger.svg`

Selected chronology:

### 1997 — grassroots beginning

Contemporary Associated Press reporting later described Greek-American civil engineer Chuck Samiotes teaching nieces and nephews in Greece with a tennis ball and his grandmother's cane during a family visit, then returning with baseball equipment the following year.

This matters because it predates some later institutional origin stories.

### 1998 — diplomatic ask

Burns's own later account dates the request from Greek officials for help to 1998. His account documents demonstrations, batting-cage television, playing catch with children, government-budget advocacy, and connection to Peter Angelos.

Source:
https://www.espn.co.uk/espn/magazine/archives/news/story?page=magazine-20040119-article32

### 1999 — institutional/professional network

Contemporaneous reporting places Peter Angelos with Greek baseball officials at a Burns/federation reception and reports that Major League Baseball's commissioner's office and the Boston Red Sox were helping the project.

The exact Red Sox club contribution remains unresolved and must stay unresolved in the diagram.

### 2003 — competitive proof

The Greek national team took silver at the European Championship and qualified for the Athens Games.

### 2004 — Olympic arrival

The national team played in Athens. That is a real beginning of a different kind: the first Olympic culmination, not the first appearance of baseball activity in Greece.

**Narrative job:** Refuse a ceremonial founding date. Greek organizers, diaspora volunteers, diplomatic brokerage, professional-baseball support, competitive qualification, and Olympic participation entered at different moments.

## CREDIT MAP 01 — distributed agency

**Local media:** `../media/ch10-credit-map.svg`

The map intentionally uses role boxes rather than a central node with arrows.

Documented lanes:

- **Greek organizers / federation:** local initiative, federation, leagues, host-country legitimacy, Olympic responsibility.
- **Samiotes / Galatis / diaspora volunteers:** grassroots teaching, equipment, field-building, youth training, recruitment, long-term advocacy. CNN in 2004 called the two men instrumental in developing baseball in Greece and described them as die-hard Red Sox fans.
- **Burns / Embassy Athens:** convening, introductions, demonstrations, public visibility, government-budget advocacy.
- **Sarbanes / Angelos / Orioles:** diaspora-political linkage, ownership resources, organizational support, recruiting architecture. Later sources compress the timing differently; do not turn this into a single uncontested chain.
- **MLB International:** coaches, development infrastructure, baseball expertise, a full-time presence supporting the federation and youth work.
- **Rob Derksen / baseball staff:** scouting, evaluation, roster construction, coaching and the competitive baseball decisions an ambassador could not make.
- **Players:** the people who made a national team real; local and diaspora players also embodied the project's central tradeoff.

Sources include:

- CNN Galatis/Samiotes transcript: https://transcripts.cnn.com/show/cst/date/2004-08-07/segment/04

**Narrative job:** Give credit away. The diplomat is one functional actor among several kinds of expertise.

## TWO SCOREBOARDS 01 — Olympics versus domestic baseball

**Local media:** `../media/ch10-two-scoreboards.svg`

### Scoreboard A — Olympic team

The federation's later history gives the Athens Olympic roster composition as:

- 19 Greek-Americans;
- 2 Greek-Canadians;
- 2 players from the local Greek league.

Source:
https://greekbaseball.com/history

Contemporary Greek reporting also described the team as heavily reinforced by ethnic Greek talent from North America and documented MLB support.

### Scoreboard B — domestic culture

A 2024 retrospective quotes national-team general manager Tom Mazarakis saying that creating the federation principally to assemble an Olympic team was a mistake and that domestic development should have started with children from day one.

Source:
https://greekbaseball.com/news/are-there-baseball-teams-in-greece

The same retrospective describes the present national team as still almost entirely Greek-American and identifies lost facilities as a major constraint on rebuilding local participation.

**Narrative job:** Prevent the visible success of Olympic qualification from swallowing the longer development question. “Build an Olympic team” and “build baseball in Greece” are different objectives with different time horizons.

## The local-player conflict

Contemporary 2004 reporting records that only two Greek-league players made the Olympic roster and that the Greek national coach resigned in protest after the shift toward the diaspora-heavy team.

Source:
https://www.ekathimerini.com/sports/24864/olympic-baseballers-out-on-second-strike/

Existing manuscript research also preserves Valerie Kontakos's account that locally trained players understood the skill gap but objected to how expectations had been managed.

**Guardrail:** The visual argument is not “diaspora bad / local good.” The diaspora strategy produced a competitive national team on the Olympic timetable. Local development and fair expectation-setting were separate questions.

## Rob Derksen

Derksen deserves person-level visual attention if a rights-clearable image is recovered. The public web contains professional/minor-league photographs and sports-reporting imagery, but no image has yet been cleared for book use in this pass.

Strong evidence:

- Baltimore Orioles scout and principal operational builder of the Greek Olympic roster;
- native Greek players reportedly admired him because he taught them the game even as diaspora recruiting narrowed their roster chances;
- died suddenly in June 2004, weeks before the Olympics;
- player A.J. Brack later called him the catalyst of the team.

**Status:** PHOTO ROUTE — RESEARCH-ONLY until rights are resolved.

## Other photo/video routes

### Samiotes and Galatis / CNN

CNN's August 2004 segment documents the two Boston-area Greek-American Red Sox fans, their restaurant, training, field-building, youth work and Olympic aspirations. The transcript is strong evidence; CNN video/stills are **RESEARCH-ONLY** unless licensed.

### Greek Olympic/test-event imagery

Greek press, Olympic collections and baseball-history sites contain real team and field images. Copyright/licensing varies. Use only after individual rights verification.

### Burns batting-cage demonstration

Burns's first-person account establishes that it happened, but this research pass did not recover a publication-cleared still or video of the event. Do not generate or stage a substitute image. The absence can remain documentary absence.

## Visual restraint

Three designed objects are enough for this chapter. A fourth object should be a real, rights-clearable photograph that adds person-level evidence—preferably Derksen, Samiotes/Galatis, or the actual batting-cage/public-demonstration work—not a generic Olympic stadium or baseball image.
