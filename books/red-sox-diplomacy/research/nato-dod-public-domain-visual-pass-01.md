# NATO / Defense Department public-domain visual pass 01

## Scope

This pass is limited to RedSox Diplomacy. It identifies a publication-safe federal image that can clarify Nicholas Burns's NATO work without relying on rights-managed press photography or decorative alliance imagery. No binary is ingested in this pass; production should acquire the highest-resolution source only when the figure is selected for layout.

## Cleared candidate: Rumsfeld–Robertson–Burns at NATO headquarters, December 1, 2003

**Documented subject:** Secretary of Defense Donald H. Rumsfeld speaks with NATO Secretary General Lord George Robertson at NATO headquarters in Brussels; U.S. Ambassador to NATO R. Nicholas Burns joins them.

**Date:** December 1, 2003. The migrated DVIDS record displays a `Date Taken` field of December 2, 2003 while its inherited Defense Department caption explicitly says December 1, 2003. Treat December 1 as the event date stated in the official caption and preserve the metadata discrepancy in provenance rather than silently normalizing it.

**Creator:** Tech. Sgt. Andy Dunaway, U.S. Air Force.

**Institution/source:** U.S. Department of Defense / Defense.gov archive, migrated to DVIDS.

**DVIDS photo ID:** 9212035.

**VIRIN shown by DVIDS:** 031202-D-D0439-4162. Because the migrated record's displayed date and caption date conflict, do not infer an event date from the VIRIN alone.

**Source URL:** https://www.dvidshub.net/image/9212035/secretary-rumsfeld-speaks-with-nato-secretary-general-lord-george-robertson-brussels

**Legacy federal record:** https://www.war.gov/Multimedia/Photos/igphoto/2002020629/

**Rights status:** DVIDS labels the image `PUBLIC DOMAIN` and identifies the photographer as U.S. Air Force. Its page also says use must comply with DVIDS copyright restrictions. This is materially stronger reuse evidence than an unqualified assumption that anything hosted by a federal agency is public domain.

**Commercial-publication suitability:** Cleared for editorial book use on the present federal record, subject to the normal DVIDS restriction that its marks/branding do not imply Department of Defense endorsement and subject to preserving an accurate credit. Do not use the image in advertising or cover treatment in a way that implies endorsement.

**Required/conservative credit line:** `U.S. Air Force photo by Tech. Sgt. Andy Dunaway / Department of Defense (public domain).`

**Recommended caption:** `NATO Secretary General Lord Robertson, Secretary of Defense Donald Rumsfeld and U.S. Ambassador to NATO Nicholas Burns at NATO headquarters in Brussels, December 1, 2003. U.S. Air Force photo by Tech. Sgt. Andy Dunaway.`

**Alt text:** `Lord Robertson, Donald Rumsfeld and Nicholas Burns speaking together at NATO headquarters in Brussels in December 2003.`

## Why it earns space

This is not generic NATO scenery. It puts Burns inside the small-group alliance machinery the manuscript describes: an ambassador physically between the alliance's secretary general and the American defense secretary. It can support the NATO chapters' institutional argument better than flags, headquarters exteriors, summit logos or a stock map of Europe.

The image should not be made to prove more than it shows. It does **not** document the September 11, 2001 Article 5 decision, the Turkey dispute, or a specific negotiating breakthrough. Its evidentiary value is narrower: it shows Burns operating at NATO headquarters at senior political-defense level during his ambassadorship.

## Placement / reader-facing production guidance

- Best use: a NATO chapter where the prose is explaining the ambassador's position between Washington, allied governments and the alliance secretariat; avoid placing it next to a paragraph about a different dated crisis merely for atmosphere.
- Keep the three principals in frame. Do not crop the photograph into a Burns portrait; the relationship among the three offices is the point.
- Print/PDF: keep image, caption and credit together; avoid a page break between photograph and credit. Test at final trim size and in grayscale before committing placement.
- EPUB: semantic order should be image → caption → credit/source → prose. Do not bake caption or credit into the pixels.
- Link the source in digital editions or production notes, not as a long raw URL in the reader-facing caption.
- If the photograph is reproduced, retain factual alt text rather than interpretation such as `Burns negotiating NATO policy`.

## Source integrity / chronology note

The official migrated metadata contains a one-day discrepancy: the DVIDS `Date Taken` display says 12.02.2003, while the inherited caption says the meeting occurred Dec. 1, 2003. The legacy Defense Department page likewise captions the meeting as Dec. 1, 2003. Publication copy should therefore use `December 1, 2003` only with the caption as its authority and keep this discrepancy in provenance. Do not conceal the mismatch by presenting the database field as corroboration.

## Rejected nearby material

### AFP/Getty Burns–Shyam Saran photographs, New Delhi, 2006

Searches surface strong photographs of Burns and Indian Foreign Secretary Shyam Saran during the civil-nuclear negotiations, including a March 3, 2006 handshake. Getty identifies the image as AFP, rights-managed, with `Licence type: Rights-managed` and `Release info: Not released`. Do not ingest or reproduce it without a separately purchased publication license. It can be used only as a research pointer for what an original timeline, meeting-room diagram, or documentary-source figure might need to clarify.

### Foreign Service Journal September 11 NATO photograph

The November 2024 Foreign Service Journal reproduces a compelling image of Burns with Lord Robertson and Gen. Joe Ralston on September 11, 2001. The journal's publication of the photograph does not itself establish reusable rights for this book. Do not ingest it until the underlying photographer/institution and reuse terms are independently resolved.

## Original-figure fallback

If the 2003 federal photograph proves visually weak at book trim size, make an original institutional diagram rather than substitute a protected press image. A useful figure would show three nodes—Washington/Defense and State, the U.S. Mission to NATO, and NATO's Secretary General/North Atlantic Council—with Burns's ambassadorial office at the junction. The diagram must be presented as an explanatory abstraction, not a reconstruction of a specific meeting.
