# Chapter 6 visual archive — The Score Is Not the Game

## Method

Chapter 6 is built around a visual contradiction: the outcome fits inside one date; the diplomacy does not.

The draft uses two designed objects:

1. a **deadline object** for August 31, 1994;
2. an **implementation ledger** for concrete U.S. support measures that helped make withdrawal executable.

A separate Clinton Presidential Library photo route is preserved for later frame-level selection rather than embedding an image whose participants are not individually identified.

## DEADLINE OBJECT 01 — August 31, 1994

**Local media:** `../media/ch06-deadline-1994-08-31.svg`

**Primary sources:**
- https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/statement-withdrawal-russian-forces-from-eastern-europe
- https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/PPP-1994-book2/PPP-1994-book2-doc-pg1514/context

President Clinton's August 31 statement says Russian military forces completed withdrawal from Estonia and Latvia under bilateral agreements. The design should make visible why public memory compresses the episode into a date.

**Guardrail:** Latvia's Skrunda arrangements continued temporarily under negotiated terms. A completed troop withdrawal did not mean every implementation issue disappeared at the same instant.

## IMPLEMENTATION LEDGER 01 — what sat underneath the date

**Local media:** `../media/ch06-implementation-ledger.svg`

**Primary White House source:**
https://clintonwhitehouse6.archives.gov/1994/07/1994-07-10-backgrounder-on-clinton-yeltsin-meeting.html

The July 10 White House briefing documents:
- 2,500 housing vouchers for departing Russian military officers;
- $25,000 stated value per voucher;
- $4 million to help dismantle the Skrunda radar facility in Latvia;
- $2 million for environmental cleanup at the former Soviet training facility at Paldiski in Estonia.

The voucher figures imply $62.5 million if all 2,500 are valued at the stated amount.

**Narrative job:** These are implementation mechanisms, not the full price of the diplomacy. They reduced practical obstacles around people, facilities, and environmental legacies while Latvia, Estonia, and Russia still had to reach political agreements.

## PHOTO ARCHIVE ROUTE — Burns on the July 1994 Riga trip

**Archive:** William J. Clinton Presidential Library, White House Photograph Office, FOIA 2016-0984-F Segment 64.

**Finding aid:**
https://www.clintonlibrary.gov/research/archives/finding-aids/white-house-photos-white-house-photographs-july-5-1994-through-july

The finding aid explicitly records July 5 photographs aboard Air Force One of Clinton meeting with Nicholas Burns and other advisers, and July 6 photographs of the Riga Castle bilateral meeting with Prime Minister Valdis Birkavs with Burns listed among participants. The finding aid states that official-duty U.S. government photographs in the collection are public domain.

**Editorial status:** Research-only pending exact frame selection. The collection-level record establishes that real Burns photographs from the trip exist; it does not identify him in every Riga frame. Before release, select an exact frame/contact sheet in which Burns is identifiable and then promote it to a numbered figure.
