# Quote Bank Addendum 01

This addendum contains newly surfaced lines from the State briefing sweep and the 2004–05 transition. Keep quotations short in the manuscript and re-open the primary source before final use.

## June 16, 1997 — family origin and Red Sox Nation

**Source:** U.S. Department of State daily press briefing.

**Quote:** “Really, I'm speaking for Red Sox nation.”

**Quote:** “We have to be foolhardy to be Red Sox fans.”

**Quote:** “But I didn't follow his advice.”

**Context:** Burns jokes about Calvinism and predestination, then says his mother was a Red Sox fan and his father said the team had broken his heart in the 1930s and advised the children never to root for them.

**Use:** This is the strongest primary-source evidence currently located for the family transmission of Burns's fandom. It belongs in the early Boston/formation material and should replace unsupported speculation about childhood fandom rituals.

**URL:** https://1997-2001.state.gov/www/briefings/9706/970616db.html

## May 13, 1997 — the register switch

**Source:** U.S. Department of State daily press briefing.

**Quote:** “This is the baseball briefing.”

**Quote:** “Okay, foreign policy?”

**Context:** Burns uses Fenway, the Red Sox, and a Bert Blyleven trivia question to banter with a Dutch journalist, then explicitly pivots back to policy.

**Use:** Near-perfect scene for the book's thesis that baseball served as a safe personal register adjacent to institutional authority.

**URL:** https://1997-2001.state.gov/briefings/9705/970513.html

## April 3, 1997 — baseball immediately before statecraft

**Source:** U.S. Department of State daily press briefing.

**Quote:** “a good day for baseball”

**Context:** Burns notes a Red Sox ninth-inning comeback and immediately transitions to Secretary Albright's official schedule and foreign-policy business.

**Use:** Supporting example rather than centerpiece; valuable for proving frequency.

**URL:** https://1997-2001.state.gov/briefings/9704/970403.html

## June 4, 1997 — baseball and foreign policy in one sentence

**Source:** U.S. Department of State daily press briefing.

**Quote:** “daily briefing on baseball and foreign policy.”

**Context:** Burns jokes about baseball immediately before announcing Richard Holbrooke's appointment as special presidential emissary on Cyprus.

**Use:** Strong chapter title/epigraph candidate because it compresses the book's two registers without needing interpretation.

**URL:** https://1997-2001.state.gov/briefings/9706/970604db.html

## March 23, 2005 — after the championship

**Source:** U.S. Department of State, remarks at the U.S. Institute of Peace.

**Quote:** “we did indeed find redemption”

**Context:** Burns explicitly frames the 2004 championship through the same New England/Calvinist vocabulary he had used before the championship.

**Use:** This is the cleanest documented bridge across 2004. The manuscript can show continuity in Burns's own language rather than imposing a before/after metaphor.

**URL:** https://2001-2009.state.gov/p/us/rm/2005/45781.htm

## October 7, 2005 — fandom survives winning

**Source:** U.S. Department of State briefing on Burns's Balkans trip.

**Quote:** “we're shutting down the State Department at 4 p.m.”

**Context:** Burns jokes that employees can leave to watch the Red Sox play the White Sox before beginning substantive Balkans material.

**Use:** Evidence that the public Red Sox register continued after the 2004 championship rather than depending entirely on the identity of long-suffering loser.

**URL:** https://2001-2009.state.gov/p/us/rm/2005/54610.htm
