# Deep Baseball Primary-Source Pass 02 — witnesses, institutional memory, and continuity

This pass moves beyond Burns's own quotations and asks a different question: **how did other diplomats, institutions, hometown organizations, and successors remember or interact with his baseball identity?**

That distinction matters. A public persona can be self-created, but it becomes more historically meaningful when independent witnesses remember it years later, successors inherit projects associated with it, and institutions build it into introductions, farewells, and professional lore.

The evidence below is ranked by type. Oral histories are retrospective testimony, not contemporaneous transcripts. Institutional pages establish public memory, not private fact. Each claim should be used at the level its source supports.

## 1. August 1997 — Burns leaves the podium and baseball leaves with him

When James Rubin appeared for his first State Department briefing after succeeding Nicholas Burns, Rubin thanked Burns for his work and said that Burns had been fond of baseball analogies. Rubin then joked that the Department was retiring baseball analogies from the podium in Burns's honor.

This is unusually valuable because it is not Burns describing himself. It is his successor publicly acknowledging that baseball had become recognizable enough to be treated as part of the outgoing spokesman's style.

**Source:** U.S. Department of State, Daily Press Briefing, August 7, 1997.
https://1997-2001.state.gov/briefings/9708/970807db.html

### Manuscript implication

The spokesman chapter can now end not only with baseball-themed farewell gifts but with an institutional afterimage: the next spokesman's first appearance explicitly marks Burns's departure by joking that the baseball language is departing too.

That is stronger evidence for a durable public register than any one Burns joke.

## 2. Wellesley remembers the Red Sox identity as part of the hometown biography

The Wellesley Free Library's local-author/alumni profile of Burns identifies him as Wellesley High School class of 1974 and describes lifelong pride in Red Sox Nation. The page also remembers that, as State Department spokesman, he often began briefings with updates on the team.

This is not a contemporaneous 1970s source and does not establish a specific childhood Red Sox memory. It is local institutional memory: the hometown that produced Burns now includes the Red Sox habit in the capsule biography it tells about him.

**Source:** Wellesley Free Library, “Nick Burns.”
https://www.wellesleyfreelibrary.org/authors/nick-burns/

### New archival lead

The same library points researchers to digitized **Wellesleyan yearbooks** and local Wellesley Townsman resources. Those are now priority targets for Burns's 1970–74 high-school period, especially baseball rosters, intramural references, student profiles, senior superlatives, AFS material, and photographs.

Do not infer that a Babe Ruth League player also played varsity high-school baseball unless the yearbooks or local papers show it.

## 3. Thomas Miller: Burns's Greece baseball project survived the Burns posting

Ambassador Thomas J. Miller, Burns's successor in Athens, gives important retrospective corroboration in his Association for Diplomatic Studies and Training oral history.

Discussing the 2004 Olympics, Miller says the Greek baseball effort began before his own tenure with his predecessor, Nick Burns, whom he calls a **“baseball nut.”** Miller says his embassy continued the project and helped engineer an arrangement with Major League Baseball to provide training and identify U.S.-based players of Greek ancestry for the national team. He also remembers substantial Greek-American community enthusiasm around the team.

This is significant for two reasons.

First, it independently corroborates that baseball was associated strongly enough with Burns for his successor to characterize him that way years later. Second, it shows the Greek baseball initiative outlived Burns's personal presence. It became an inherited embassy/public-diplomacy project rather than disappearing when the original ambassador left.

**Source:** Association for Diplomatic Studies and Training, Foreign Affairs Oral History Project, Ambassador Thomas J. Miller, interview begun April 19, 2010, copyright 2011, discussion of the Athens Olympics and Greek baseball.
https://adst.org/OH%20TOCs/Miller%2C%20Thomas%20J.toc.pdf

### Evidentiary caution

Miller is recalling events years later. Use his testimony to establish continuity, successor perception, and the MLB/player-development phase. For exact dates, dollar amounts, roster mechanics, and the sequence of Burns–Sarbanes–Angelos contacts, continue to prefer contemporaneous records where available.

## 4. Thomas Krajeski: Red Sox identity inside Foreign Service friendship

Ambassador Thomas Krajeski provides a different type of evidence in another ADST oral history. While discussing career models in the Foreign Service, Krajeski describes Burns as a longtime friend and notes that they are both Red Sox fans. He jokes that Burns remains a friend “because he's a Red Sox fan, primarily.”

The line is humorous, but analytically useful. It shows the baseball affiliation working horizontally inside the professional diplomatic community, not only vertically between Burns and press audiences.

**Source:** Association for Diplomatic Studies and Training, Foreign Affairs Oral History Project, Thomas Krajeski, interview begun February 23, 2016, copyright 2017.
https://adst.org/OH%20TOCs/Krajeski-Thomas.pdf

### Manuscript implication

The book can now support a narrower, evidence-based claim that Red Sox affiliation sometimes operated as **professional kinship** among Foreign Service officers. Do not generalize this into a claim that baseball routinely shaped diplomatic decision-making.

## 5. 2007 Boston College — Burns chooses a live baseball moment over generic hometown nostalgia

On April 11, 2007, speaking at Boston College, Burns identifies himself and the audience as members of Red Sox Nation and jokes that they have shown questionable judgment by attending his foreign-policy speech instead of being outside Fenway for Daisuke Matsuzaka's Boston debut.

This is particularly useful because it is timestamped to a real game happening that day. Burns is not recycling an old Yankees line or a generic Red Sox identity. He is following the live 2007 season closely enough to build the day's major baseball event into the opening of a serious foreign-policy address at his alma mater.

**Primary source:** U.S. Department of State, “America's Global Leadership Challenge in the 21st Century,” Boston College, April 11, 2007.
https://2001-2009.state.gov/p/us/rm/2007/85332.htm

**Baseball corroboration:** MLB records that April 11 was Matsuzaka's Fenway home debut; Félix Hernández one-hit Boston in a 3–0 Seattle win.
https://www.mlb.com/video/king-felix-one-hits-the-red-sox-c1871556783

### Manuscript use

This scene is an unusually clean example of simultaneity: Burns is giving a speech about American global leadership while openly aware that an important Red Sox game is taking place a few miles away.

No invented regret or divided attention is needed; the joke is in the transcript.

## 6. State Magazine turns Red Sox fandom into a professional character trait

A 2007 State Magazine profile introducing an interview with Burns says the Department's senior career Foreign Service officer arrived in his job already practiced in patience as a “card-carrying, dues-paying” member of Red Sox Nation.

This is institutional voice rather than Burns's own. It demonstrates how thoroughly the Red Sox identity had been absorbed into the way the Department presented him internally.

**Source:** State Magazine, September 2007, “Department Inner-View — Q&A with Under Secretary for Political Affairs R. Nicholas Burns.”
https://2001-2009.state.gov/documents/organization/91629.pdf

A preserved copy is also available via Wikimedia/Internet Archive-derived hosting:
https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/e/e4/State_Magazine_2007-09-_Iss_515_%28IA_sim_state-magazine_2007-09_515%29.pdf

### Editorial implication

Be careful with the easy “diplomacy requires patience / Red Sox fans learned patience” metaphor. The useful fact is not that the metaphor is profound. It is that Burns's own institution repeatedly used the fandom as shorthand for his public character.

## 7. 2008 departure — the Red Sox become part of the farewell ritual again

CBS State Department correspondent Charles Wolfson described Burns as a self-described superfan of Red Sox Nation when he announced his retirement from the Foreign Service. Wolfson reports Secretary Condoleezza Rice joking that she knew nothing good would come from the Red Sox winning the World Series again.

The story also predicts that Burns and his family would be seeing more Red Sox games after government service. That last line is the reporter's flourish, not evidence of what the family actually did, and should not be converted into biography without corroboration.

**Source:** CBS News, Charles Wolfson, “A Career Diplomat Says Good-Bye,” January 19, 2008.
https://www.cbsnews.com/news/a-career-diplomat-says-good-bye/

### Longitudinal value

The public-career arc now has two institutional bookends:

- **1997:** Rubin jokes that baseball analogies are being retired when Burns leaves the spokesman's podium.
- **2008:** Rice's farewell joke invokes the Red Sox championship when Burns leaves senior State Department service.

That eleven-year span is much stronger evidence of durable professional identity than a list of isolated quotations.

## 8. The evidence now supports three different kinds of baseball identity

### Personal baseball life

Supported directly by Burns or close contemporaneous reporting:
- Wellesley Babe Ruth League playing background;
- family Red Sox inheritance/conflict;
- active tracking of games, players, pitching and standings;
- 2003 ALCS Game 7 watched on Armed Forces TV in Brussels;
- Red Sox memorabilia in the NATO office;
- Greek television/baseball demonstrations and playing catch with children;
- detailed 2007 roster/pitching analysis.

### Professional social identity

Supported by independent witnesses and institutions:
- reporters repeatedly cueing Burns's Red Sox persona;
- Rubin “retiring” baseball analogies after Burns left the podium;
- Krajeski framing Red Sox fandom as part of their friendship;
- Miller remembering Burns as a “baseball nut” while describing the inherited Greece project;
- State Magazine using Red Sox Nation in its institutional profile;
- Rice invoking the Red Sox in Burns's departure ritual.

### Regional/cultural identity

Supported by later speeches and hometown/institutional memory:
- Wellesley Free Library treating Red Sox Nation as part of Burns's local biography;
- Boston College speeches treating Red Sox affiliation as assumed common language;
- 2013 Burns formulation of Boston sports as community glue;
- later New York and China-era uses of Red Sox identity as portable regional shorthand.

Keeping these categories separate will make the manuscript more precise.

## 9. Remaining archival gaps after the witness pass

The biggest gaps are now concentrated in early life and one crucial championship moment:

1. **Wellesley Babe Ruth League** — roster, team, position, years, coach, teammates, photographs.
2. **Wellesley High School 1970–74** — whether Burns played school baseball; local coverage; any written evidence of 1967 or later Red Sox attachment.
3. **Boston College 1974–78** — direct Burns evidence around the 1975 World Series or 1978 Yankees tiebreaker.
4. **Jerusalem 1986** — any Burns correspondence or colleague memory of the Mets World Series.
5. **October 2004** — where and how Burns followed ALCS Games 4–7 and the World Series clincher.
6. **Greek television** — locate the batting-cage footage or station/program metadata.
7. **Red Sox institutional assistance to Greece** — identify the team officials, department, equipment/training contribution, and surviving correspondence.

## 10. Best next archival sequence

The most efficient next move is not another broad web sweep. It is to attack specific repositories:

- digitized Wellesley High yearbooks and Wellesley Townsman archive;
- Boston College Heights / Sub Turri / alumni archive for 1974–78;
- ADST oral histories of Jerusalem colleagues from 1985–87;
- U.S. Mission NATO public-affairs and travel records for October 2004;
- Greek broadcast and sports-federation archives for the batting-cage footage;
- MLB International / Red Sox community or international-development records for the Greece assistance chain.

The research is now strong enough that each new source should either close one of these gaps or materially change a chapter. More generic Red Sox history has diminishing value.
