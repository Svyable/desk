# Continuity Pass 05 — India to Beijing

## Scope

Audit Chapters 14–19 after the opening and NATO compression passes. The governing question in this section is attribution: does the narrative preserve the fact that negotiations, institutions, successors, counterparts and governments own outcomes Burns participated in but did not control?

The secondary question is repetition. By this point in the manuscript, the reader already understands the bounded role of baseball and the distinction between personal identity and official authority. Chapters should no longer stop repeatedly to re-prove those premises.

## Chapter 14 — India: Playing the Long Game

Audited and left structurally intact.

This chapter already resists the heroic biography problem well.

Protect:

- `The finish line kept moving.`
- the sequence of successive jurisdictions: leaders' political bargain → separation plan → U.S. law → 123 Agreement → IAEA safeguards → NSG exception → Congress;
- Manmohan Singh's Indian strategic premise and the fact that India did not understand the bargain as nuclear disarmament;

- Burns leaving government before the IAEA/NSG/congressional completion phase;
- the distinct successor phase under William J. Burns and a broader U.S.-Indian/international team;

Do not compress the jurisdiction sequence merely for pace. It is the mechanism of the chapter.

Publication gap remains: read Shivshankar Menon's *Choices* directly and preserve the strongest counterpart/nonproliferation voices.
