# Athens: 17 November documentary media pass 01

## Scope

A publication-safety and visual-research note for the Athens chapter of *Red Sox Diplomacy*. This pass does **not** clear or import photographs of suspects, arrests, demonstrations, victims, or court proceedings. It identifies federal documentary sources that can support an original chronology/relationship figure without borrowing protected news photography or implying that Nicholas Burns personally participated in events not established by the record.

## Why this belongs in the book

The Athens chapter needs a visual object that explains why terrorism was not generic embassy background during Burns's ambassadorship. Revolutionary Organization 17 November had attacked American personnel and interests in Greece for decades. The useful visual is therefore not a dramatic photograph of masked men or a burning flag. It is a compact, sourced chronology showing the duration of the problem, the American losses, the unresolved status during Burns's tenure, and the 2002 break in the case after he had left Athens.

That chronology also protects an important historical distinction: Burns served in Athens while the organization remained unsolved. Greek authorities' first arrests came in summer 2002. A later solution should not be back-projected into the embassy's earlier uncertainty.

## Cleared source layer

### U.S. Department of State — 17 November Case Study

**Source:** U.S. Department of State archive, *17 November Case Study* (2003-era archived federal document).

**URL:** https://2009-2017.state.gov/documents/organization/20114.pdf

**Authority / useful facts:** The State Department case study says Greek law-enforcement officials dismantled Revolutionary Organization 17 November in 2002; identifies the group's first victim as U.S. Station Chief Richard Welch in 1975; describes almost twenty-eight years of operation; and states that five U.S. Embassy employees were murdered.

**Rights boundary:** The federal prose and factual chronology can be used as source material for an original figure. **Do not reuse the photographs embedded in the PDF.** The document itself visibly includes third-party news photography, including an arrest image, and federal hosting does not convert third-party photographs into U.S.-government works.

### State Department — Patterns of Global Terrorism 1999

**Source:** U.S. Department of State, *Patterns of Global Terrorism 1999*.

**URL:** https://1997-2001.state.gov/global/terrorism/1999report/patterns.pdf

**Useful facts:** The report says 17 November was suspected of seven rocket attacks and bombings against U.S., Greek, and third-country interests from March through May 1999; it describes the targets and records the unresolved state of the investigation. It also says President Clinton's November visit precipitated widespread anti-U.S. demonstrations and attacks against U.S., Greek, and third-country targets.

**Rights boundary:** Use the federal report as evidence. **Do not lift its protest photography.** The report visibly credits at least one image to *Eleftherotypia*, demonstrating why page-level federal provenance is not image-level clearance.

### State Department — later organization history

**Source:** U.S. Department of State, Country Reports on Terrorism / Foreign Terrorist Organization background, archived 2007 edition.

**URL:** https://2001-2009.state.gov/s/ct/rls/crt/2007/103714.htm

**Useful facts:** The federal history records that 17 November was established in 1975; describes its anti-U.S., anti-NATO, anti-Turkey and anti-Greek-establishment orientation; states that five U.S. Embassy employees were murdered; and records that a failed bombing attempt at the Port of Piraeus in June 2002, followed by police work, led to the first arrests of members.

**Use:** This is a retrospective federal source. It is useful for chronology but should not be made to sound like knowledge available to the embassy in 1999–2001.

### GovInfo / congressional chronology

**Source:** U.S. Government Publishing Office, congressional publication reproducing *Significant Terrorist Incidents, 1961–2001: A Chronology*.

**URL:** https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CHRG-107hhrg81394/pdf/CHRG-107hhrg81394.pdf

**Useful fact:** The chronology records the April 24, 1987 bombing of a bus carrying U.S. servicemen near Athens and attributes the attack to 17 November.

**Rights:** Federal publication. Use the factual entry as corroboration; no need to reproduce page design.

## Publication-safe original figure

### Working title

**ATHENS BEFORE THE ARRESTS**

### Form

An original vertical chronology, preferably half-page or narrow full-page. No suspect portraits, terrorist symbols, logos, newspaper clippings, State Department seals, or facsimile page screenshots.

Suggested stops:

- **1975** — Richard Welch killed in Athens; State later identifies him as 17 November's first victim.
- **1987** — Bomb attack on a bus carrying U.S. servicemen near Athens; sixteen injured in the federal chronology.
- **1997** — State Department designates 17 November a Foreign Terrorist Organization. *(Use only if the final figure cites the designation source directly.)*
- **1999** — State reports suspected 17 November rocket/bomb attacks during Burns's ambassadorship; no arrests.
- **1999 Nov.** — Clinton visit occurs amid widespread anti-U.S. demonstrations and attacks documented by State. Keep this as political/security context, not an attribution of those demonstrations to 17 November.
- **2002 Jun.–summer** — Failed Piraeus bombing attempt and subsequent investigation lead to the first arrests of 17 November members.
- **2003** — Greek court convictions belong in a postscript marker, not inside Burns's Athens-tenure band.

### Tenure band

Add a restrained horizontal or background band labeled **Burns as U.S. ambassador to Greece, 1997–2001**. Its purpose is temporal, not causal. Do not draw lines from Burns to attacks, suspects, investigations, or arrests unless a specific sourced action is being illustrated.

### Caption draft

*For most of Nicholas Burns's ambassadorship in Athens, Revolutionary Organization 17 November remained an unsolved security problem with a long record of attacks on American and Greek targets. Greek authorities made the first arrests only in 2002, after Burns had left the post. Sources: U.S. Department of State; U.S. Government Publishing Office. Original chronology by the author.*

### Alt text

*Vertical chronology of Revolutionary Organization 17 November and U.S. security context in Greece from 1975 through the first arrests in 2002. A band marks Nicholas Burns's 1997–2001 tenure as U.S. ambassador, emphasizing that the organization remained unsolved while he served in Athens and that arrests came afterward.*

## Formatting / production rules

- Keep title, chronology, caption and source line together in print/PDF; do not strand the caption on the following page.
- In EPUB, reading order should be title → alt text → dated chronology entries → caption/source.
- Do not encode the Burns-tenure distinction by color alone; use a labeled bracket or ruled band that survives grayscale and e-ink.
- Use year labels large enough to remain readable at final trim size.
- Avoid a decorative map unless it adds a sourced location relationship. A generic Athens silhouette contributes less than the chronology.
- Do not use crosshairs, blood-red styling, wanted-poster typography, or other thriller visual language. The figure is documentary, not promotional.
- If a future map is justified, use original geometry from clearly reusable geographic data and distinguish known attack sites from approximate metropolitan references.

## Claim discipline

1. **Documented fact:** State later reported five U.S. Embassy employees murdered by 17 November and described the group as operating from 1975 until the 2002 breakthrough.
2. **Contemporaneous assessment:** The 1999 State terrorism report describes suspected 17 November attacks and the state of Greek counterterrorism cooperation at that time. Preserve words such as *suspected* where the contemporary source used them.
3. **Later retrospective:** The 2002/2003 breakthrough and convictions can clarify what eventually happened, but they must not be written as information Burns possessed during the earlier embassy period.
4. **Do not infer:** Burns's personal operational role in Greek investigations, knowledge of individual suspects, or participation in later arrests without a direct source.
5. **Do not conflate:** Anti-U.S. demonstrations during Clinton's 1999 visit with 17 November operations. They occupied the same political-security environment but are not the same phenomenon.

## Integration recommendation

Place the chronology in Chapter 9 near the first passage that establishes 17 November as a persistent embassy security problem, before any later account of the 2002 arrests. The visual should answer one reader question quickly: **what did “unsolved” mean in time?** It should not interrupt a scene merely to add terrorism atmosphere.

No manuscript insertion was made in this pass because the chapter is under active prose editing. The research note is intentionally separable and reversible.

## Highest-value next search
