# Deep Baseball Primary-Source Pass 13 — the 1999 Red Sox public-affairs / community-relations cluster

## Purpose

The unresolved club-side Greece question can now be framed more precisely than “which Red Sox executive helped?”

Institutional and biographical sources identify a small cluster around the **1999 All-Star Game, Public Affairs, Community Relations, Sales/Marketing, and the Jim Rice Field development project**. None is yet proven to have handled the Greek request. But the search can now be organized around people who were actually inside the relevant machinery.

## 1. Dick Bresciani's Public Affairs umbrella included community relations

A University of Massachusetts institutional biography of Bresciani, published while he was a Red Sox vice president, says he headed the club's **Public Affairs department**, which included:

- publications and photography;
- **community and customer relations**;
- the Red Sox historical archives.

The same source says Bresciani chaired the Red Sox Task Force Committee for the **1999 All-Star Events and Game** at Fenway.

Source:
https://umassathletics.com/sports/2016/7/5/genrel-062102aaa-html

### Why this materially changes the search

Earlier research treated Bresciani/Public Affairs and Ron Burton/Community Relations as adjacent but potentially separate routes.

This source shows that, at least in the organizational description preserved near this period, **community relations sat inside Bresciani's Public Affairs umbrella**.

That means the club-side Greece search can reasonably begin with one integrated records family:

**Public Affairs -> Community Relations / customer relations -> publications / photography -> archives.**

### Guardrail

The UMass page describes Bresciani's department in the early 2000s. Do not silently convert its wording into a 1999 org chart unless the 1999 media guide confirms the exact structure for that year.

The source is strong evidence of organizational proximity, not proof that Bresciani handled the Greek request.

## 2. Larry Cancro was another named 1999 All-Star task-force member

The Red Sox's current front-office biography for **Larry Cancro** says he was Vice President of Sales and Marketing from January 1996 through 2003 and was a member of the **1999 All-Star Game Task Force**.

It also credits him with developing the Futures Game, which became part of MLB All-Star week.

Source:
https://www.mlb.com/redsox/team/front-office/larry-cancro

### Why he matters

Cancro expands the relevant club-side cluster beyond Bresciani's Public Affairs operation.

If the Greece pledge involved:

- event relationships;
- sponsorship/marketing help;
- introductions to MLB;
- community programming;
- a Boston-area development project connected to All-Star week;

then a Sales/Marketing executive on the same All-Star task force is a plausible records/witness node.

### Guardrail

No public source found in this pass connects Cancro personally to Burns, Samiotes, Greece, Greek baseball, or the Red Sox support pledge.

Add him to the witness/search map; do not add him to the Greek-baseball narrative.

## 3. Adam Shamus gives us a rank-and-file Community Relations witness from the actual 1999 event

Current biographical pages for Boston-area realtor **Adam Shamus** say that after graduating from Ithaca College he began his career in the **Boston Red Sox Community Relations Department**, spent several years at Fenway, and that the highlight of that tenure was participating in the **1999 All-Star Game festivities**.

Sources:
- https://munceygroup.com/agents/adam-shamus
- https://www.homes.com/real-estate-agents/adam-shamus/ht1lmt2/

### Why this is useful

Shamus is not a senior executive and there is no evidence he handled Greece.

That is precisely why he may be useful as an organizational-memory witness. A staffer working Community Relations during the All-Star period may remember:

- who actually opened and routed external requests;
- which executives handled equipment donations;
- whether international requests were unusual;
- how Jim Rice Field was staffed internally;
- who worked directly with Samiotes Consultants;
- what records were kept.

He may be better positioned to explain workflow than an executive asked to remember one small request twenty-seven years later.

## 4. Ron Burton Jr. remains the strongest named Community Relations lead

A 2022 local report quotes longtime Red Sox supplier Vincent Williams saying **Ron Burton Jr. gave him an opportunity to provide branded products for Red Sox Community Relations twenty-six years earlier**, placing Burton in that network around **1996**.

Source:
https://lynnjournal.com/2022/09/15/red-sox-honor-jazzy-sportswear-of-lynn/

A later *Fenway Lives* staff index lists **Ron Burton Jr., Community Relations Manager**.

Source:
https://cincinnatistate.ecampus.com/fenway-lives-team-behind-team-people-who/bk/9781579400903

The Boston Park League's historical award list also gives Burton its **Thomas Yawkey Memorial Sportsman Award in 2000**, immediately after Dan Duquette in 1999.

Source:
https://bostonparkleague.org/historical-award-winners/

### What this adds

The award does not prove a Greek connection. It does reinforce that Burton was visible in Boston's amateur/community baseball ecosystem at the precise edge of the Greece-support window.

He therefore remains a high-value witness if the club's contribution involved youth/community baseball rather than scouting.

## 5. The old club's correspondence culture may complicate archival recovery

A 2006 *Boston Magazine* profile of the post-2002 Red Sox says that when Charles Steinberg joined the new ownership's management team, he found **bags of unopened mail in the community relations offices** and was told complaint letters were answered only if the sender followed up by telephone.

Source:
https://www.bostonmagazine.com/uncategorized/2006/05/15/sox-appeal/

### Why this belongs in a research note

This source is not about Greece and should not be used to imply that Burns's or the federation's request went unanswered.

Its value is archival-methodological:

- the pre-2002 organization may not have had a clean or complete correspondence culture;
- absence of a surviving Community Relations file would therefore be weaker negative evidence than it might appear;
- executive/Public Affairs/MLB/recipient-side records may preserve a request that Community Relations files do not.

### Guardrail

Do not use a general story about unopened fan mail to explain a specific missing Greece record. It only changes how much confidence to place in a negative archive search.

## 6. Bresciani's historical archive survived him institutionally

When the Red Sox hired **Gordon Edes** as team historian in 2015, the club said he was continuing the work of the late Dick Bresciani. Reporting on the appointment says the historian's responsibilities included overseeing Red Sox publications, the **historical archives**, and alumni relations.

Sources:
- https://www.mlb.com/redsox/news/red-sox-hire-gordon-edges-as-club-historian/c-156951982
- https://www.boston.com/sports/boston-red-sox/2015/11/10/fenway-sports-group-hires-gordon-edes-as-red-sox-team-historian/

Edes had also covered the Red Sox as a reporter from the late 1990s onward, giving him a second route into the period.

### Why this matters

The Bresciani archive is not merely a hypothetical private-paper collection. The club historically maintained an internal archive as an institutional function, and Bresciani's historian role was explicitly succeeded.

The exact current custodian of 1999 working correspondence remains unresolved, but **Red Sox historical-archive staff / former historian Gordon Edes** is now a concrete access route.

## 7. Edes also closes the later Galatis/Agganis continuity independently

In July 2018, Gordon Edes published a first-person essay explaining why the Red Sox honored **Harry Agganis** at Greek Heritage Night.

Source:
https://gewrite.medium.com/why-we-honored-harry-agganis-939f3b87a61b

Separate Galatis reporting says Galatis approached Edes with the Agganis idea and that Edes carried it to senior Red Sox management.

This does not prove Edes knew anything about the 1999 Greece support. It does make him unusually well positioned to answer two separate questions:

1. did he encounter any Burns/Greece material as a 1990s–2000s beat reporter or later club historian?
2. when Galatis approached him in 2018, did either man discuss Galatis's earlier Greek-baseball work?

## Documentary rule

**Organizational responsibility is not event participation.**

Bresciani headed the umbrella that included Community Relations; Burton managed Community Relations; Shamus worked there during the 1999 All-Star festivities; Cancro sat on the All-Star task force. Those facts radically improve the archive search. None, by itself, puts any of them in the Greek baseball project.
