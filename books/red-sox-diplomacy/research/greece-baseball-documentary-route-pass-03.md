# Greece baseball documentary route pass 03 — *Who’s on First?*

## Why this is high value

The 2006 Valerie Kontakos documentary *Who’s on First?* is not merely later commentary on Greek Olympic baseball. Contemporary and current film records describe it as a feature documentary about the creation of the Greek national baseball team, and surviving cast metadata names several people already central to the unresolved institutional chain: Chuck Samiotes, Bill Galatis, Rob Derksen, Lou Angelos, Dimitris Gousios, Yiannis Penas, Mike Riskas and journalist/author Stefan Fatsis.

That makes the film a potential **recorded-witness archive** made close to the events, not just a retrospective secondary source.

## Confirmed public record

Exile Room, founded by Kontakos, lists the film as a 2006 Greek production, 58 minutes, in Greek and English with English subtitles. Its synopsis says the film follows Greece’s attempt to create an Olympic baseball team and the conflict that followed the turn toward Greek-American professional players.

Source: https://www.exile.gr/en/activities/who-s-on-first/

Samiotes Consultants’ current biography of Chuck Samiotes says he was featured in Kontakos’s documentary about starting Greece’s first baseball team for the 2004 Olympics and independently describes him as a die-hard Red Sox fan.

Source: https://www.samiotes.com/chuck-samiotes

A surviving film database cast listing names Lou Angelos, Rob Derksen, Stefan Fatsis, Bill Galatis, Dimitris Gousios, Yiannis Penas, Mike Riskas and Chuck Samiotes.

Source: https://www.okino.ua/film/whos-on-first-178526/

Festival/director biographies confirm the film screened in the United States, Greece, Finland and South Korea. A College Year in Athens record documents a 2010 screening followed by a discussion with Kontakos.

Sources:
- https://peloponnisosdocfestival.com/index.php?Itemid=2413&id=606&option=com_documentaries&view=directorwiew
- https://cyathens.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/The-Owl-Fall-2010.pdf

## What this may close

The film should be reviewed specifically for:

1. any statement by Samiotes or Galatis about Nicholas Burns’s first approach, introductions, calls or meetings;
2. any mention of the Boston Red Sox as an institution rather than as personal fandom;
3. names of Red Sox employees, MLB International staff or intermediaries;
4. photographs, letters, faxes, uniforms, equipment, signage or credits that identify a Boston contribution;
5. Lou Angelos’s account of the Orioles/Angelos side of the chain;
6. Derksen’s own recorded description of how he entered the project and who recruited or funded him;
7. local Greek testimony that distinguishes federation initiative from embassy, diaspora and professional-baseball intervention;
8. end credits naming archival providers, production contacts or organizations that may still hold interview outtakes.

## Highest-value follow-up

Locate a lawful viewing copy or institutional screening copy and make a time-coded transcript/claim ledger. If the released cut does not answer the Red Sox question, contact Valerie Kontakos / Exile Films about surviving interview transcripts, rushes or outtakes. Because the film was made close to the 2004 events and Derksen died in 2004, production materials may preserve testimony that cannot now be recreated.

## Guardrail

Do not infer from Samiotes’s Red Sox fandom that the Red Sox club supported the project through him. Do not infer from a cast listing that every named participant discusses Burns or Boston in the released cut. The value here is the existence of a near-contemporaneous witness archive and a concrete route to production materials.
