# Burns Chronology — Red Sox Diplomacy

Working chronology for chapter construction. Dates are included only when supported by sources already in the research corpus or by authoritative records. Add exact dates and archival citations as research deepens.

## Formation and early service

- **January 28, 1956** — Burns is born in Buffalo, New York, and is raised in Massachusetts.
- **Massachusetts / Wellesley** — Burns grows up in Wellesley, Massachusetts. Later institutional profiles consistently identify lifelong devotion to the Boston Red Sox.
- **Foreign Service apprenticeship** — Mauritania internship; later service in Cairo and Jerusalem. Use official State biographies for sequence and dates.
- **1988** — As a young Foreign Service officer, Burns accompanies Secretary of State George Shultz on an extended Asia trip. Burns later describes this as a formative lesson in Shultz’s practice of “tending the diplomatic garden.”

## Baseball-era overlay — context, not memoir

This section aligns major Red Sox moments with Burns's age and documented posting. It is **not** evidence that he watched a particular game or experienced an event in a particular way. Use it to identify archival and interview targets, not to manufacture scenes.

- **1967 — age 11, growing up in Massachusetts:** the “Impossible Dream” Red Sox win the American League pennant behind Carl Yastrzemski and lose the World Series to St. Louis in seven games. This is the first major Red Sox revival that overlaps Burns's childhood.
- **1975 — age 19:** Carlton Fisk's Game 6 walk-off at Fenway becomes one of baseball's defining televised images; Cincinnati wins Game 7 the next night.
- **1978 — age 22, Boston College graduation year:** Boston wins 99 games but loses the AL East tiebreaker at Fenway after Bucky Dent's three-run home run. The Yankees had erased a 14 1/2-game July deficit.
- **1986 — age 30, serving in Jerusalem:** Boston comes within one out of a World Series title in Game 6 before the Mets rally; New York wins Game 7. The Red Sox's own historical museum credits Boston Globe writer Nathan Cobb with coining “Red Sox Nation” during this World Series.
- **2003 — age 47, U.S. ambassador to NATO:** Boston is five outs from the World Series before the Yankees tie Game 7 and Aaron Boone wins it in the 11th.
- **2004 — age 48, U.S. ambassador to NATO:** Boston falls behind the Yankees 3–0 in the ALCS, becomes the first MLB team to win a postseason series from that deficit, then sweeps St. Louis to win its first World Series since 1918.

## Washington, transition, and public voice

- **1990–1995** — National Security Council work on Soviet/Russian affairs and the post-Soviet transition.
- **1995–1997** — State Department spokesman. This period produces an unusually rich record of Burns’s public register: formal policy statements interleaved with repeated Red Sox and baseball banter.
- **January–July 1997** — Multiple daily briefings document recurring baseball exchanges, including Red Sox frustration, Yankees rivalry, Opening Day, Roger Clemens, Fenway Park, and the State press corps treating Burns’s fandom as a known feature of his public persona.
- **May 24, 1997** — Worcester Polytechnic Institute commencement: Burns calls himself a “long suffering fan of the Boston Red Sox.”
- **July 1997** — Burns leaves the spokesman role; contemporaneous reporting records President Clinton giving him an autographed Red Sox cap and the press corps giving him baseball-themed farewell gifts.

## Athens and baseball as public diplomacy

- **1997–2001** — U.S. Ambassador to Greece.
- **1999** — Contemporary reporting records Burns hosting a reception with the Hellenic Amateur Baseball Federation and Peter Angelos as Greece builds an Olympic baseball program; MLB and the Red Sox are involved in support.
- **2000** — Associated Press reporting describes Burns taking on a “baseball ambassador” role and helping connect Greek baseball development to U.S. baseball institutions.
- **2001** — Hellenic Baseball Federation retrospective says federation president Panos Mitsiopoulos approached Burns for help; Burns contacted Senator Paul Sarbanes, helping connect the project to Peter Angelos.
- **2004 retrospective** — Burns describes himself as a “confirmed, unconditional, rabid fan of the Boston Red Sox” and says helping Greek baseball was “one of the best things I ever did as ambassador.”

## NATO and post-9/11 alliance diplomacy

- **2001–2005** — U.S. Ambassador to NATO.
- **September 2001** — Burns is at NATO during the invocation of Article 5 after the September 11 attacks.
- **October 2003** — The Red Sox lose ALCS Game 7 to the Yankees on Aaron Boone's walk-off home run. This belongs in the baseball context surrounding Burns's NATO years; no direct Burns reaction is yet sourced.
- **October 2004** — The Red Sox complete the unprecedented 0–3 ALCS comeback against the Yankees and sweep the Cardinals for their first championship since 1918. Burns is still at NATO; his exact viewing circumstances and immediate reaction remain unsourced.
- This period is central to the book’s deeper argument about trust, alliance maintenance, and the difference between rivalry and enmity.

## Under Secretary / India / Iran

- **2005–2008** — Under Secretary of State for Political Affairs.
- **March 23, 2005** — At the U.S. Institute of Peace, Burns identifies himself as “a native-born member of Red Sox Nation” and jokes about the 2004 championship as “redemption.”
- **October 7, 2005** — Before discussing a Balkans trip, Burns jokes that the State Department is shutting down at 4 p.m. so employees can watch the Red Sox play the White Sox. The baseball register clearly survives winning.
- **2005–2008** — Leads or plays major roles in U.S.-India civil nuclear negotiations, Iran diplomacy, alliance management, and broader political affairs.
- **2006** — In an official Charlie Rose interview on the India nuclear deal, Burns and Ed Markey are described as “both members of Red Sox Nation” before the discussion returns immediately to nuclear policy.
- **2007** — Continues using Red Sox/Yankees rivalry in official remarks, including in Paris and domestic policy settings.

## Harvard and diplomacy as a vocation

- **2008 onward** — Burns enters Harvard Kennedy School and builds the Future of Diplomacy Project.
- **2008** — Publishes “We Should Talk to Our Enemies.”
- **2011–2021** — Repeatedly argues in lectures, essays, and interviews for diplomacy as a first-line instrument of statecraft, for keeping channels open, and for alliance maintenance.
- **2012** — Publishes “Diplomacy is the best tool for Iran,” explicitly arguing that talks should precede war.
- **2013** — At Massachusetts Bay Community College, Burns describes Boston's sports teams as institutions that “bind us together as a ‘Community’” and treats the Red Sox as a “Nation” across New England.
- **2013** — Describes diplomacy as “a venerable and antique art” that remains indispensable.
- **2019** — In the John Courtney Murray lecture in New York, Burns jokes about an “abiding faith in the superiority of the Boston Red Sox” before turning to his substantive argument.
- **2019** — Publishes “The State of the State Department and American Diplomacy,” emphasizing the professional value of career diplomats and the global reach of the Foreign Service.
- **2020** — Publishes “The Indispensable Power,” arguing for restoring American leadership through diplomacy.
- **2021** — Publishes “The Diplomat as Gardener,” revisiting Shultz and the idea of tending relationships before crisis.

## Beijing

- **2021–2025** — U.S. Ambassador to the People’s Republic of China.
- **2021 confirmation** — Senator Ed Markey introduces Burns as a “proud member of Red Sox Nation.”
- **2024** — Burns appears with Chinese baseball fans; official/public caption includes “Future members of @Redsox Nation.”
- **May 22, 2024** — HKS graduation address from Beijing-era Burns emphasizes public service, courage, and changing the world “for the better.”
- **2025** — Returns to Harvard after China service.
