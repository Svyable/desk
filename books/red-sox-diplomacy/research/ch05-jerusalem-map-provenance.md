# Chapter 5 media provenance — Jerusalem, 1983–87

## Purpose

This packet supports Chapter 5, “The Unresolved Address,” with a period geographic source that can clarify the institutional and political geography without pretending a map settles sovereignty. It does not change the manuscript’s factual claims or reproduce third-party artwork.

## Source: CIA, *Jerusalem and vicinity* (1984)

- **Institution / creator:** United States Central Intelligence Agency; copy cataloged by the Library of Congress Geography and Map Division.
- **Title:** *Jerusalem and vicinity*.
- **Date:** April 1984 (map code `800128 4-84`), immediately before Nicholas Burns’s 1985–87 Jerusalem posting.
- **Library of Congress record:** https://www.loc.gov/item/84695860/
- **Catalog description:** The Library of Congress record says the map shows then-present and former city boundaries, armistice lines, and Israeli-developed settlements beyond the armistice line; relief is shown by spot heights. The catalog record itself is the authority for these descriptive claims.
- **Rights posture:** The identified creator is a U.S. federal agency. Works prepared by U.S. government employees as part of official duties are generally not subject to U.S. copyright under 17 U.S.C. §105. That does not automatically clear third-party material merely because it appears in a government or Library of Congress collection. For this project, do **not** reproduce the map scan by default; use it as a period primary geographic source for an independently designed project figure.
- **Publication suitability:** Strong as a source; conservative as artwork. The map is contemporaneous enough to show the geographic vocabulary and boundary complexity surrounding Burns’s posting without importing a modern map into a 1985–87 chapter.

## Recommended original figure

**Working title:** `The address had geography`

Create an original, grayscale-safe schematic rather than tracing the CIA sheet. Plot only the locations and lines needed to understand the chapter:

- Jerusalem, with the Consulate General identified as a U.S. post distinct from Embassy Tel Aviv;

- the 1949 armistice/Green Line as a historical reference line, clearly labeled as such rather than as a proposed final border;

Do not copy the CIA map’s typography, symbol set, colors, line weights, layout, or cartographic artwork. Do not use the figure to make territorial claims. A small note should say: “Schematic for institutional and historical orientation; not a statement of sovereignty or final borders.”

## Evidentiary boundary

These maps establish what U.S. government cartographers depicted and labeled in 1983–84; they do not establish Nicholas Burns’s personal movements, views, contacts, or interpretation of the geography. They also do not resolve the legal or political status of Jerusalem or any boundary. The manuscript’s existing distinction between documented Burns-specific evidence and the broader environment should remain intact.

## Rejected media in this pass

- **Modern political maps:** rejected because post-Oslo administrative categories can silently import later political geography into a 1985–87 narrative.
- **Generic Old City / skyline photography:** rejected as atmosphere without Burns-specific evidentiary value.
- **Commercial news photography:** rejected because rights and editorial-use restrictions are unnecessary for this argument.
- **CIA map scan as immediate book art:** legally favorable federal provenance, but the full historical sheet carries more territorial and cartographic detail than this chapter needs. An original schematic can make the narrower institutional point more clearly and with less risk of implying that the book endorses every line or label on a period intelligence map.
