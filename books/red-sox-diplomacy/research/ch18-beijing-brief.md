# Chapter 18 Brief — Beijing

## Status
Research brief after Chapter 17.

## Working question
What happens when a diplomat spends thirteen years teaching statecraft, then returns to delegated authority in the most consequential competitive bilateral relationship of his career?

## Documentary spine
Burns served as U.S. ambassador to China from 2021 through early 2025. Harvard later described him as leading personnel from forty-eight U.S. government agencies at Mission China. His tenure combined strategic competition with repeated efforts to stabilize communication and rebuild people-to-people ties after the pandemic-era collapse.

## Core tension
This is not a chapter about a professor proving his theories. It is about returning to constraints. Burns repeatedly argued that the United States should compete vigorously with China while maintaining open channels and selected areas of cooperation to reduce the risk of miscalculation.

## Public-diplomacy thread
People-to-people exchange provides the bridge back to the book's human theme. Burns advocated more students, academic links and direct contact. In 2024 he also alleged that Chinese authorities had interfered with dozens of U.S. Embassy public events after the Biden-Xi Woodside summit. China's Foreign Ministry rejected that characterization. Present this as a dispute between governments.

## Baseball continuity
The archive includes a 2024 Burns social-media image/caption referring to future members of Red Sox Nation in China. Use only as a small continuity marker. It is not evidence that baseball materially changed U.S.-China relations.

## Historical echo
In 1997, as State Department spokesman, Burns briefed publicly on U.S.-China issues and observed that diplomacy sometimes is not done until officials reach the meeting room. The later ambassador had moved from explaining other officials' China diplomacy to conducting and representing it himself.

## Guardrails
Do not credit Burns personally with stabilizing the entire relationship. Attribute contested claims. Distinguish the Chinese state, party, society and individual interlocutors where evidence permits. Do not treat exchange advocacy as a solution to structural competition. Keep Taiwan, technology, Russia, human rights, counternarcotics and military communication distinct rather than collapsing them into a generic list. Do not make baseball causal.

## Desired architecture
1. Return from Harvard to delegated authority.
2. Scale: forty-eight agencies and a relationship spanning security, economics, technology and society.
3. Competition and communication as simultaneous policy.
4. Rebuilding channels after severe deterioration.
5. People-to-people ties as both objective and contested terrain.
6. The 2024 dispute over U.S. cultural and educational programming, including China's denial.
7. A small, bounded Red Sox continuity moment.
8. Communication is most necessary when trust is weakest.

## Source anchors
Use archived State Department records; Harvard's Burns profile and return coverage; Reuters June 26, 2024 for the exchange-program dispute and Chinese denial; the June 25, 2024 State briefing; Burns's 2023 National Committee on U.S.-China Relations town hall; his 2024 CSIS interview; and a preserved official source for the Red Sox social post before publication.
