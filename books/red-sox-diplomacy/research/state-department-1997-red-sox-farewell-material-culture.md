# State Department 1997 — Red Sox material culture at Burns's spokesman farewell

## Finding

A contemporaneous Washington Post report dated July 22, 1997 documents R. Nicholas Burns's final day as State Department spokesman and records two Red Sox objects presented to him in the official press-briefing setting.

Source:
- Thomas W. Lippman, “As Burns Takes Bow at State, a Fan Sox It to Him,” The Washington Post, July 22, 1997: https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/politics/1997/07/23/as-burns-takes-bow-at-state-a-fan-sox-it-to-him/f7295a64-565a-4320-9a47-a9a75a068cc0/

The report says President Bill Clinton sent Burns an autographed Boston Red Sox cap with an inscription, while veteran Associated Press reporter Barry Schweid presented a Red Sox alarm clock tied to a joke about the briefing's start time. The same account records a Greek journalist presenting ouzo in recognition of Burns's next assignment as ambassador to Greece.

## Why this matters

This is stronger than another retrospective statement that Burns was a fan. It is contemporaneous evidence that his Red Sox identity was sufficiently public and legible inside the State Department press corps and White House orbit to become part of the ritual marking his departure as spokesman.

It also creates a documentary bridge between two phases already central to the manuscript: Burns's highly public State Department spokesman years and his move to Athens. The baseball object is not inferred by the narrator; it is recorded by a named reporter at the time.

The safest argument is about **public diplomatic persona and institutional recognition**, not causal diplomatic effect. By July 1997, Red Sox fandom had become one recognizable element of Burns's professional public identity.

## Evidentiary classification

**Contemporaneous national-newspaper report by a named State Department correspondent.**

This is not an official transcript, photograph, or surviving artifact record. The precise inscription and alarm-clock joke are reported by Lippman; if reproduced verbatim in manuscript prose, quotations should remain brief and attributed to the Washington Post report.

## What this does not establish

Do not use this source to claim:
- that President Clinton personally selected or physically signed the cap without further provenance beyond the report;
- what Burns privately felt about either gift;
- that Red Sox fandom improved press relations or policy communication;
- that Burns displayed, retained, or later carried either object to Greece or NATO;
- any private baseball conversation among Burns, Clinton, Schweid, or Greek journalists;
- any fandom, reaction, attendance, memory, or statement by Libby Baylies.

## Manuscript opportunity

This is strong enough for a compact attributed beat in the section on Burns as State Department spokesman or in the transition to Greece. The useful observable sequence is: on his last day behind the State Department podium, the departure ritual included a presidential Red Sox cap, a press-corps Red Sox gag gift, and an ouzo bottle pointing toward Athens.

Do not add Burns's facial expression, reaction, dialogue, thoughts, or motives unless a transcript, video, photograph, or authenticated first-person account supplies them.
