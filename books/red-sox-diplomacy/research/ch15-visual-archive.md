# Chapter 15 visual archive — Iran: When Patience Does Not Win

## Editorial job

Chapter 15 is the negative image of Chapter 14. India can be shown as a chain of institutional gates that were eventually cleared. Iran cannot be drawn as the same chain with a delayed finish line. During Nicholas Burns's tenure, coalition-building, incentives, sanctions and repeated offers changed the diplomatic architecture without producing the comprehensive bargain Washington sought.

The visual sequence therefore has three jobs:

1. show why the 2006 offer did not create an agreed entry point for negotiations;
2. show that multilateral pressure accumulated without confusing pressure with outcome;
3. show what remained unresolved and what institutional machinery nevertheless survived Burns's departure.

No triumphal photograph is needed. The chapter's evidence is structure, disagreement and an open file.

## ENTRY CONDITION 01

Asset: `../media/ch15-entry-condition.svg`

The left side is anchored in Secretary Condoleezza Rice's May 31, 2006 statement that the United States would join the European-led nuclear negotiations if Iran fully and verifiably suspended enrichment and reprocessing activities.

Primary source:
- U.S. Department of State, May 31, 2006: https://2001-2009.state.gov/secretary/rm/2006/67210.htm

The right side is deliberately labeled **Iranian position — U.N.-reported**. It represents the documented Iranian objection that suspension should not be imposed as the price of entering substantive negotiations and could instead be discussed in the negotiating process. Do not redesign this side as a narrator's legal judgment about treaty rights.

Supporting source trail is preserved in:
- `iran-publication-source-closure.md`
- `ch15-iran-multisided-dossier.md`
- the staged source ledger/addenda for the Iranian U.N. position and U.N. Secretary-General reporting.

Evidentiary limit: the object shows incompatible entry logic. It does not prove that either side's characterization exhausted the other side's motives.

## PRESSURE LEDGER 01

Asset: `../media/ch15-pressure-ledger.svg`

Official Security Council sequence:

- Resolution 1696 — suspension demand;
- Resolution 1737 — sanctions after Iran did not comply with the suspension demand;
- Resolution 1747 — expanded measures;
- Resolution 1803 — further measures in 2008.

Official resolution index:
- U.N. Security Council Iran sanctions resolutions: https://main.un.org/securitycouncil/en/sanctions/1737/resolutions

The design rule is printed into the object: a sanctions sequence proves coalition action, not diplomatic success. During Burns's tenure Iran continued enrichment and no comprehensive nuclear agreement was reached.

Technical claims about safeguards, declared material, undeclared activity or weaponization must remain grounded separately in the IAEA record; do not let the sanctions graphic substitute for the technical file.

## OPEN FILE 01

Asset: `../media/ch15-open-file-ledger.svg`

The left side records what had **not** been achieved by Burns's departure: no comprehensive agreement, no Iranian acceptance of the suspension entry condition, continued enrichment and continued risk of military confrontation.

The right side records institutional architecture that survived: the P5+1 negotiating framework, the IAEA safeguards role, Security Council pressure machinery, an incentives-plus-sanctions strategy and a stated U.S. willingness to join a negotiated settlement under conditions.

The 2015 boundary is essential. The later JCPOA involved different governments, different direct diplomacy and a different negotiated bargain. The Burns-era file is part of the history inherited by later negotiators; it is not authorship of their agreement.

## Placement

- `ENTRY CONDITION 01` belongs immediately after the prose establishes that the door to U.S. participation existed but Iran did not accept the required terms.
- `PRESSURE LEDGER 01` belongs after the Security Council sequence and the sentence-level distinction between pressure and outcome.
- `OPEN FILE 01` belongs after the chapter states that the diplomatic structures outlasted Burns and the Bush administration, before the later-JCPOA boundary is explained.

## Guardrails

- Do not depict sanctions as a rising score or progress bar toward agreement.
- Do not convert Iran's stated peaceful-use/right claims into narrator-certified technical findings.
- Do not convert U.S. suspicion into proof of weaponization intent.
- Do not collapse IAEA technical findings, Security Council political demands and national strategic judgments into one category.
- Do not call the absence of a Burns-era agreement a personal defeat.
- Do not call the later JCPOA the payoff Burns was inevitably building toward.

The chapter's visual argument is narrower: diplomacy can alter the structure of choices without controlling another government's political decision.
