# NATO / China public-domain image pass

## Purpose

Add publication-usable visual evidence to *Red Sox Diplomacy* without allowing images to imply undocumented scenes. This pass identifies two high-value U.S.-government images with unusually clean provenance and complementary narrative uses.

## 1. NATO: Burns with Rumsfeld and Robertson, Brussels, December 2, 2003

DVIDS / the Defense Department archive preserves a photograph made by Tech. Sgt. Andy Dunaway, U.S. Air Force, of Secretary of Defense Donald Rumsfeld speaking with NATO Secretary General Lord George Robertson in Brussels on December 2, 2003. The official caption states that U.S. Ambassador to NATO R. Nicholas Burns joined the informal meeting. The record identifies the image as public domain and gives VIRIN 031202-D-D0439-4162, resolution 3008 x 1960.

Source: https://www.dvidshub.net/image/9212035/secretary-rumsfeld-speaks-with-nato-secretary-general-lord-george-robertson-brussels

### Book use

This is a strong visual anchor for the NATO chapters because it puts Burns physically inside the alliance's senior working environment in the same season as the directly documented 2003 Red Sox ALCS viewing episode. It can illustrate the juxtaposition the manuscript has earned: official alliance work in Brussels and a portable Boston identity in the same posting.

### Boundary

The photograph is dated December 2, 2003. It is **not** evidence about where Burns watched the October ALCS, what he discussed with Rumsfeld or Robertson beyond the official caption, or whether baseball entered this meeting. Do not caption or crop it in a way that implies a baseball connection.

## 2. China: Burns pitching at the embassy July 4 celebration, July 2023

Wikimedia Commons preserves a 1706 x 1278 image originally published by the U.S. Ambassador to China account showing Burns pitching a baseball at the U.S. Mission's July 4 celebration. The Commons record identifies the work as a U.S. Department of State government work and public domain.

Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ambassador_Burns_pitching_baseball.jpg
Original official social source recorded by Commons: https://twitter.com/USAmbChina/status/1676175025446039552

### Book use

Unlike the NATO photograph, this is direct visual evidence of Burns actively performing baseball as part of an embassy public event. It belongs naturally in the China/public-diplomacy sequence and pairs well with the October 2024 Beijing clinic and Burns's 2025 first-person account of deliberately wearing his battered Red Sox cap in public around China.

### Boundary

The photograph establishes pitching at the embassy celebration. It does not by itself establish audience reaction, diplomatic effect, motive, or that the pitch was a Red Sox-specific initiative.

## 3. China: Beijing baseball clinic, October 22, 2024

The already identified State Department image of Burns with young Chinese baseball participants is especially valuable because its official caption explicitly says: "Future members of @Redsox Nation." Commons records it as public domain and traces it to the ambassador's official account.

Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ambassador_Burns_with_Chinese_baseball_fans.png

This image is stronger than generic baseball imagery because the Red Sox identity is explicit in both the visual material (cap/jersey) and the preserved official caption.

## Editorial recommendation

Use the December 2003 Brussels image as institutional texture, then use the 2023 pitching and 2024 clinic images as evidence of the later evolution from private/portable fandom into visible public-diplomacy performance. That progression is documentary; no claim that baseball caused a diplomatic outcome is necessary.

## Rights / production note

Both identified image records are marked public domain as works of U.S. federal employees made as part of official duties. Production should still preserve source credit, creator/agency metadata, date, and the original archive URL. Before final publication, obtain the highest-resolution original available from DVIDS/State rather than relying on a web preview.
