# Chapter 18 Dossier — Beijing: Competition and Communication

## Purpose

Give Chapter 18 a two-sided documentary spine. Burns returned from thirteen years at Harvard to delegated authority as U.S. ambassador to China. The chapter should test the book's human-channel argument under severe strategic competition without pretending communication resolves the underlying conflict.

## Scale of the assignment

Burns served as U.S. ambassador to the People's Republic of China from 2022 through January 2025 after Senate confirmation in December 2021. Mission China encompassed a large interagency presence; later Harvard material described Burns as leading personnel representing forty-eight U.S. government agencies.

The portfolio cannot be reduced to a single negotiation. It included Taiwan and military risk, trade and technology controls, human rights, China's support for Russia, fentanyl precursor issues, climate, consular matters, educational exchange and the basic problem of maintaining official communication.

## Competition plus communication

Burns repeatedly described U.S. policy as requiring both vigorous competition and sustained communication. This is not a contradiction to resolve. It is the operating condition.

The strongest chapter formulation is that communication becomes more important as trust declines because governments need ways to distinguish signaling from preparation, policy from accident, and escalation from misinterpretation.

Do not claim that communication produces trust. It can instead be valuable precisely because trust is absent.

## Deterioration and rebuilding

The ambassadorial period included severe deterioration in bilateral relations and interruptions to important channels. The February 2023 high-altitude balloon incident produced a fresh crisis and delayed Secretary Antony Blinken's planned Beijing visit. Later 2023 diplomacy sought to restore high-level contact.

Blinken visited Beijing in June 2023. Treasury Secretary Janet Yellen and Commerce Secretary Gina Raimondo also traveled to China. National Security Adviser Jake Sullivan and China's Wang Yi developed a senior-level channel. President Biden and President Xi Jinping met at Woodside, California, in November 2023.

The Woodside meeting produced or advanced practical areas of communication and cooperation, including counternarcotics work and resumption of high-level military-to-military communications. Attribute each outcome to the governments and leaders involved, not to Burns personally.

## Chinese official perspective

Chinese official statements repeatedly framed stable relations as requiring the United States to respect China's core interests, especially Taiwan, and objected to U.S. technology restrictions and what Beijing characterized as containment or suppression. Chinese officials also publicly endorsed dialogue and exchanges when conducted on terms they considered respectful and reciprocal.

This is important because both governments could endorse communication while disagreeing sharply over what was destabilizing the relationship.

Do not render China's position only as a rebuttal to Washington. Present its stated theory: U.S. actions in areas such as Taiwan and technology were themselves sources of instability, while Washington argued that Chinese military pressure, economic practices, support for Russia and other behavior required competition and deterrence.

## People-to-people ties

Burns made educational and social exchange a visible part of his public diplomacy. The post-pandemic environment had badly reduced American student presence and ordinary contact. He argued publicly for rebuilding student and academic exchange while maintaining the broader competitive policy.

The chapter should distinguish two claims:

1. exchange can make societies less abstract to one another;
2. exchange does not settle strategic disputes.

The first is defensible. The second prevents sentimentality.

## 2024 programming dispute

In June 2024 Burns publicly alleged that Chinese authorities had interfered with U.S. Embassy public diplomacy and educational/cultural programming, including dozens of events after the November 2023 Biden-Xi summit. The State Department publicly backed his complaint.

China's Foreign Ministry rejected Burns's characterization and argued that China supported people-to-people exchanges while opposing U.S. interference and politicization.

This is a valuable documentary center because the dispute is itself about the human channel. Both governments professed support for exchange while disputing the conditions under which exchange was occurring.

Write this as a contested government-to-government claim. Do not convert the U.S. allegation into settled fact beyond independently documented incidents.

## Red Sox continuity

A 2024 official Burns social-media post included a photograph with young people and the phrase `Future members of @Redsox Nation.` Preserve this only as a small identity marker.

The significance is continuity, not effect. Decades after Cairo, Jerusalem, the State briefing room, Athens, NATO and Harvard, the same recognizable piece of personal identity remained available in Beijing.

Do not claim it changed attitudes toward the United States, opened official access or advanced negotiations.

## Historical echo

As State spokesman in the 1990s Burns explained China policy made by others. In Beijing he represented the United States inside the relationship itself. The change is from explaining delegated policy to carrying delegated authority.

The Harvard interval makes the return sharper but should not be treated as proof that his theories worked. Government restored the constraint that teaching had removed: an ambassador cannot merely analyze policy from several angles in public. He represents the policy of the administration that sent him.

## Chapter thesis candidate

The human channel is not an alternative to power. It is part of the machinery by which power avoids becoming accident.

In a trusted relationship, communication can deepen cooperation. In a mistrustful relationship, its more modest function may be to keep competition intelligible.

That is enough to matter.
