# China Baseball / Red Sox Diplomacy Pass — 2021–2025

The China years give *Red Sox Diplomacy* a late-career test of the book's central question. By Beijing, Nicholas Burns was no longer a young spokesman discovering that baseball could loosen a room. He was the U.S. ambassador in the most consequential and politically constrained bilateral relationship of his career. The evidence shows that the Red Sox identity nevertheless remained visible in official public diplomacy.

The important claim is not that baseball changed U.S.–China relations. It did not. The stronger, supportable point is that Burns continued using the same portable personal identity — Boston, baseball, Red Sox Nation — inside a far more difficult people-to-people environment.

## 0. December 6, 2021 — the Red Sox identity arrives before the ambassador

Before Burns was confirmed and before he reached Beijing, the State Department press corps was already treating his baseball allegiance as part of his public identity.

During a December 6, 2021 briefing about the importance of getting a Senate-confirmed ambassador to China, spokesperson Ned Price praised Burns's experience and standing. A reporter interrupted: **“But he's a Red Sox fan.”** Price laughed, said the reporter had “maligned his sports preferences,” and immediately returned to the argument for confirming Burns.

Source:
- U.S. Department of State, Department Press Briefing, December 6, 2021:
  https://2021-2025.state.gov/briefings/department-press-briefing-december-6-2021/

### Why this matters

This is small but unusually clean institutional continuity. The joke appears in a discussion of one of the most consequential ambassadorial appointments in the U.S. government, years after Burns had left the daily State briefing room. The press corps did not need the Red Sox persona reintroduced; it remained legible.

Do not inflate the exchange. It proves recognition, not diplomatic significance.

## 1. July 4, 2023 — Burns literally pitches baseball at the embassy

A State Department public-domain image from the U.S. Embassy's 2023 Independence Day celebration in China shows Burns **pitching a baseball**.

The image metadata dates the photograph to **July 4, 2023** and identifies the source as Burns's official @USAmbChina account.

Source:
- Wikimedia Commons preservation of State Department image, “Ambassador Burns pitching baseball.jpg”:
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ambassador_Burns_pitching_baseball.jpg

A related Commons category for the Independence Day event also contains an image of Burns holding a Red Sox baseball cap.

Context category:

### Manuscript value

This is a visual rather than interpretive source. It establishes that baseball was physically present in Burns's official representational repertoire in Beijing. It does not tell us who organized the activity, who was playing, or what effect it had.

## 2. 2024 — Red Sox Nation appears at the Great Wall

The public archive of Burns's official China social-media imagery contains multiple explicit Red Sox references at the Great Wall.

Wikimedia's 2024 Burns category includes files captioned:

- **“Ambassador Burns — Charter members of @RedSox Nation at the Great Wall of China”**
- **“Ambassador Nicholas Burns met a German member of RedSox Nation at the Great Wall of China”**

Source/category:
https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:R._Nicholas_Burns_in_2024

These are not private vacation artifacts. They derive from official ambassadorial social-media/public-affairs output and show Burns deliberately making the Red Sox identity visible during public travel in China.

### Editorial use

The Great Wall is tempting metaphor territory. Resist it. The useful fact is simpler: Burns carried a visibly battered regional sports identity into highly recognizable Chinese public spaces and used it to identify or recruit fellow fans.

## 3. October 22, 2024 — Beijing baseball clinic

The strongest China-era baseball event located so far is a **baseball clinic in Beijing** attended by young Chinese participants.

Burns's official caption says:

> “Great to see so many young Chinese friends attend our Baseball Clinic in Beijing. Future members of @Redsox Nation.”

The accompanying public-domain image shows Burns wearing a Red Sox cap and baseball jersey, holding a baseball with Chinese baseball participants.

Source:
- Wikimedia Commons preservation of the official @USAmbChina post:
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ambassador_Burns_with_Chinese_baseball_fans.png

Date: October 22, 2024.

### Why this is stronger than a hat photograph

This is not simply Burns wearing team gear. It combines:

- an organized baseball event;
- Chinese youth / young participants;
- the ambassador's official public-affairs channel;
- explicit “Red Sox Nation” recruitment language.

That makes it the clearest China-era descendant of the Greece baseball work: a sport functioning as a low-stakes people-to-people contact point inside an official diplomatic setting.

The scale and institutional partners behind the clinic remain unclear. Do not imply it was a major sports-diplomacy program until organizers, coaches, league partners, or participant records are identified.

## 4. The political context is almost the inverse of Greece

By 2024 Burns was publicly complaining that Chinese authorities were obstructing embassy events and people-to-people engagement. Reporting on his remarks described interference with U.S. public diplomacy and exchange programming.

Sources already in the book's research corpus include:
- Reuters, June 26, 2024:
  https://www.reuters.com/world/china/china-rejects-us-envoys-comment-that-it-hinders-people-to-people-exchanges-2024-06-26/
- South China Morning Post reporting on Burns's claims of event interference.

The contrast with Greece is useful but should be exact:

- **Athens:** a locally requested baseball problem expanded into a substantial network connecting an embassy, Greek organizers, diaspora figures, MLB, the Orioles, and reportedly the Red Sox.
- **Beijing:** the public record so far shows smaller, symbolic baseball contact taking place inside an environment Burns himself described as constrained.

The same personal register persists; the political operating environment changes radically.

## 5. Burns's 2025 retrospective makes the hat deliberate

In an April 30, 2025 address, **“Lessons from the Front Lines of the U.S.-China Relationship,”** Burns gives unusually explicit retrospective evidence about the Red Sox identity in China.

He jokes about the prevalence of Los Angeles Dodgers caps on Chinese streets and says his response was to wear his **Red Sox hat in public whenever possible**. He specifically names:

- the Great Wall;
- the Bund in Shanghai;
- Xiamen;
- Chengdu;
- his *60 Minutes* interview.

Burns says he cannot claim much growth in “Red Sox Nation China” but intends to keep carrying his cherished, battered Sox hat on future China visits.

Source:
- Nicholas Burns, “Lessons from the Front Lines of the U.S.-China Relationship,” Belfer Center / Harvard, April 30, 2025:
  https://www.belfercenter.org/research-analysis/lessons-front-lines-us-china-relationship

### Why this matters

This is direct retrospective evidence that the repeated hat appearances were not merely random wardrobe. Burns himself describes wearing it publicly across China as a deliberate recurring practice.

Do not over-psychologize that practice. The evidence supports deliberate visibility and humor; it does not prove the hat was part of a formal embassy strategy.

## 6. People-to-people diplomacy in Burns's own China argument

In the same post-China period, Burns repeatedly argues that Chinese and American people form an essential stabilizing layer beneath state competition.

A November 2024 address to the U.S.–China Hong Kong Forum is titled around his proposition that the Chinese and American people are the **“ballast”** in relations between the countries.

Source:
https://www.hkforum.com/en/article/216-ambassador-nicholas-burns-address-to-the-2024-us-china-hong-kong-forum

This broader philosophy gives the baseball clinic context. The clinic should not be inflated into a strategic breakthrough, but neither is it merely personal fandom floating outside his diplomatic work. Burns placed people-to-people connection near the center of his public argument about managing U.S.–China competition.

## 7. A 2025 retrospective irony: Dodgers hats in China

Burns's 2025 Harvard remarks include one of the more specific late-career baseball jokes in the research record. He observes that Dodgers hats are highly visible in China, then links that observation to former Red Sox star Mookie Betts, adding that Boston should never have traded him.

That matters because it demonstrates current baseball fluency well after Burns left government. His Red Sox identity is not frozen in the 1997 Clemens era or the 2004 championship mythology; he is still making judgments about recent roster history.

Source:
https://www.belfercenter.org/research-analysis/lessons-front-lines-us-china-relationship

## 8. June 2025 — baseball vocabulary survives after the ambassadorship

In a Belfer Center interview about U.S.–China climate negotiations, Burns describes the work that produced the Sunnylands climate statement as negotiators working in **“overtime or in extra innings as we'd say in American baseball.”**

Source:
- Robert N. Stavins and Nicholas Burns, “Nicholas Burns on His Time as U.S. Ambassador to China and on Efforts to Address Climate Change,” Belfer Center, June 9, 2025:
  https://www.belfercenter.org/research-analysis/nicholas-burns-his-time-us-ambassador-china-and-efforts-address-climate-change

This is not Red Sox-specific and should not be over-weighted. Its value is narrower: baseball language still appears naturally in Burns's diplomatic vocabulary when he is explaining long, difficult negotiations after government service.

## 9. Longitudinal arc: Athens to Beijing

The Greece and China evidence now supports a disciplined comparison.

### Greece, 1998–2001

Burns's baseball identity meets a concrete local institutional problem: Greece must field an Olympic baseball team despite minimal infrastructure. His role expands into demonstrations, lobbying, introductions, diaspora networking, and institutional development.

### China, 2021–2024

Before Burns arrives, Washington reporters already know the Red Sox persona. In Beijing, baseball appears inside official public events, social media, travel imagery, and a baseball clinic. The identity remains playful and portable, but the surviving evidence shows a much smaller programmatic footprint than Greece.

### Post-China, 2025

Burns retrospectively acknowledges deliberate public use of the Sox hat across China, jokes about his limited success expanding Red Sox Nation, critiques the Mookie Betts trade, and still uses baseball language when describing difficult bilateral negotiations.

The comparison is useful precisely because the outcomes are not the same. Baseball is not a diplomatic technique that automatically produces a network. It is a personal/social resource whose utility depends on the host-country environment and the opportunity in front of the diplomat.

## 11. Manuscript implication

China can provide a strong late-book echo without pretending history repeats itself.

The young spokesman once used the Red Sox to turn a briefing room into a temporary social club. Before the China posting even began, reporters could still invoke that identity without explanation. The ambassador to Greece helped turn baseball enthusiasm into a real institutional network. Decades later in China, Burns was still wearing the cap, pitching a baseball, greeting young players, and joking about Red Sox Nation — but in a bilateral relationship where even ordinary people-to-people exchange had become politically contested.

That is continuity with changed conditions, not a baseball metaphor for geopolitics.
