# Chapter 7 visual archive — Speaking for America

## Method

Chapter 7 is about institutional language, not personality photography. The archive is richest when the words themselves become inspectable.

The draft uses two designed objects:

1. an **evidence grammar** built from recurring categories in Burns's briefing language;
2. a **correction object** preserving the smallest useful sentence in the archive: *“I stand corrected. Sorry.”*

No generic podium photograph is needed. Chapter 8 can carry the room's social life; Chapter 7 should keep attention on what a spokesman is allowed to make true by saying it.

## EVIDENCE GRAMMAR 01 — categories before confidence

**Local media:** `../media/ch07-evidence-grammar.svg`

**Primary examples:**
- April 28, 1997 China briefing: https://1997-2001.state.gov/regions/eap/970428_burns_us-chin_bilat.html
- June 13, 1997 Taiwan visa exchange: https://1997-2001.state.gov/briefings/9706/970613db.html
- April 15, 1997 briefing on intelligence boundaries: https://1997-2001.state.gov/briefings/9704/970415.html

**Documented language states used in the object:**
- established government position / fact the Department is prepared to state;
- reports and allegations;
- cannot confirm;
- not aware / not in a position to confirm;
- will not discuss a protected category such as intelligence.

**Narrative job:** Make the podium's grammar visible. The diagram is an editorial synthesis from documented briefing language, not a quotation from a Burns memo or a formal State Department classification system.

**Key scene:** On April 28, after Secretary Albright met Chinese Vice Premier Qian Qichen, a reporter asked whether Burns had treated chemical-weapons assistance as established while describing missile transfers as reports. Burns checked his notes and put both back under *reports and allegations*.

## CORRECTION OBJECT 01 — four words

**Local media:** `../media/ch07-stand-corrected.svg`

**Source:** State Department noon briefing, February 27, 1997; searchable mirror preserving the briefing text: https://www.globalsecurity.org/wmd/library/news/dprk/1997/970227a.htm

A reporter corrects Burns on the date of the planned New York briefing talks involving North Korea. Burns first says March 4; after the room presses him, he answers:

> *“Is it 5th? I stand corrected. Sorry.”*

**Narrative job:** Put correction itself on the page as an authority mechanism. The useful artifact is not the mistaken date; it is the speed and smallness of the repair.

## Supporting institutional-error case

The March 4, 1997 State briefing documents Burns acknowledging that language in a Vice Presidential letter on Khalistan was a U.S. government mistake and that Washington had apologized to India:
https://1997-2001.state.gov/briefings/9703/970304.html

This remains prose evidence rather than a third visual object. Two visual objects are enough.

## Chapter-level restraint

The visual system should not turn every quote into a card. Chapter 7 gets one synthetic grammar and one exact correction. The rest of the chapter's force comes from the cumulative transcript archive.
