# Twelve Days / Article 5 documentary pass 01

## Scope

Bounded media-and-research enhancement for *RedSox Diplomacy*, centered on Chapter 11, “Twelve Days,” and the first weeks after September 11, 2001.

This pass adds no third-party image, screenshot, logo, map, or manuscript prose. Its publication recommendation is an original chronology built from authoritative records. The point is to make the alliance machinery visible without importing uncertain or restrictive media rights.

## Reader-facing opportunity

### Original figure: TWELVE DAYS, THEN THE ALLIANCE MOVED

A compact documentary chronology can show that Article 5 was not one instantaneous switch thrown on September 12. The political commitment, evidentiary finding, support measures, military deployment, and public White House scene occurred in sequence.

Recommended date spine:

- **September 11, 2001 — attacks in the United States.** NATO’s North Atlantic Council condemned the attacks and expressed solidarity the same day.
- **September 12 — conditional Article 5 decision.** The North Atlantic Council agreed that, if the attack were determined to have been directed from abroad against the United States, it would be regarded as covered by Article 5. This is the first invocation of Article 5 in NATO history, but the contemporaneous wording preserved the foreign-direction condition.
- **October 2 — evidentiary condition satisfied.** U.S. Ambassador-at-Large and Coordinator for Counterterrorism Frank Taylor briefed the North Atlantic Council on the investigation. NATO then announced that the attacks had been directed from abroad and were covered by Article 5.
- **October 4 — eight support measures.** NATO agreed to a package requested by the United States, including intelligence sharing, assistance to threatened Allies and other states, increased security for facilities, backfilling selected NATO assets, blanket overflight clearances, access to ports and airfields, naval deployment to the eastern Mediterranean, and readiness to deploy airborne early-warning assets.
- **October 7 — U.S. and U.K. military operations begin in Afghanistan.** Keep this distinct from NATO’s Article 5 action: the initial strikes were U.S.-U.K. operations, not a NATO bombing campaign.
- **October 8 — five NATO AWACS approved for U.S. deployment.** Lord Robertson announced that five NATO AWACS aircraft and crews would deploy to the United States, allowing U.S. aircraft to be released for other counterterrorism operations.
- **October 9–10 — NATO aircraft and personnel arrive from Geilenkirchen.** A State Department media note records five NATO AWACS aircraft, one trainer/cargo aircraft, and about 200 personnel arriving in the United States and basing at Tinker Air Force Base, Oklahoma, to operate with NORAD.
- **October 10 — Burns appears with Bush and Robertson at the White House.** In the Colonnade, President George W. Bush publicly welcomed NATO Secretary General Lord Robertson and explicitly welcomed “Nick Burns, our Ambassador to NATO.” This is a clean documentary anchor placing Burns inside the alliance response rather than merely adjacent to it.
- **October 26 — maritime Article 5 operation begins.** NATO’s eastern Mediterranean naval activity became Operation Active Endeavour, another concrete consequence of the post-September 11 support package.
- **May 16, 2002 — Eagle Assist ends.** NATO records 830 crew members from 13 NATO nations, nearly 4,300 patrol hours, and more than 360 operational sorties over the United States before termination.

The title “Twelve Days” should not be visually forced onto the October chronology as though every consequential NATO action happened inside twelve days. If the chapter’s twelve-day interval refers to a narrower personal or institutional sequence, the figure should preserve that interval as a highlighted band while letting the later alliance machinery continue beyond it.

## Why this helps the chapter

The strongest visual argument is procedural rather than commemorative. September 12 produced a historic political commitment, but NATO deliberately left a factual condition unresolved. October 2 supplied the evidentiary finding. October 4 converted solidarity into a menu of support measures. October 9–10 put allied aircraft and crews on U.S. soil.

That sequence prevents a common retrospective blur in which “NATO invoked Article 5 after 9/11” becomes a single undifferentiated event. It also gives the reader an unusually concrete reversal of the familiar transatlantic picture: European-based NATO crews crossed west to help patrol American airspace.

For Burns, the October 10 White House record is especially useful. It does not prove private influence, authorship of the Article 5 decision, or a particular negotiating role. It does establish that the sitting U.S. ambassador to NATO was publicly present with Bush and Robertson while the president described the alliance response.

## Rights basis for U.S. federal text

17 U.S.C. §105 provides that copyright protection under Title 17 is not available for a work of the United States Government. The Copyright Office defines a U.S. Government work as one prepared by an officer or employee of the U.S. Government as part of that person’s official duties.

Operational rule for this project: federal authorship must be established item by item. A State Department or White House archive can host foreign-government, NATO, press-pool, wire-service, or other third-party material that does not become public domain by being hosted on a .gov domain.

## Figure specification

**Working title:** TWELVE DAYS, THEN THE ALLIANCE MOVED

**Format:** original horizontal chronology, preferably one page or a wide half-page. No copied NATO visual language.

**Visual grammar:**

- one line for political/legal decisions;
- a second line for operational consequences;
- a clearly labeled Burns marker on October 10;
- a light bounded band only if the manuscript itself defines the twelve-day interval precisely;
- dates carry the hierarchy, not logos or flags;
- no World Trade Center impact imagery, mourning iconography, fighter silhouettes, or generic “war on terror” decoration.

**Suggested caption:**

> NATO’s response unfolded in stages. The North Atlantic Council made its conditional Article 5 decision on September 12; the evidentiary condition was satisfied on October 2; support measures followed on October 4; and NATO AWACS aircraft and crews reached the United States on October 9–10. The sequence matters because political solidarity, legal determination, and operational assistance were related but not identical events.

**Suggested source line:**

Sources: NATO North Atlantic Council records, September 12 and October 2, 2001; U.S. Department of State archival records, October 8, 10, and 18, 2001; NATO Operation Eagle Assist termination record, April 30, 2002. Figure drawn for this edition.

**Alt text:**

Chronology of NATO’s response after September 11, 2001. It distinguishes the September 12 conditional Article 5 decision, the October 2 finding that the attacks were directed from abroad, the October 4 support measures, the October 9–10 arrival of NATO AWACS aircraft and crews in the United States, Nicholas Burns’s October 10 White House appearance with President Bush and NATO Secretary General Lord Robertson, and the May 16, 2002 end of Operation Eagle Assist.

## Print / PDF / EPUB production notes

- Keep title, chronology, caption, and source line together whenever possible.
- If the chronology must break, break after October 10 rather than separating a date from its event text.
- Use typography and line weight that remain distinct in grayscale; do not encode political versus operational events by color alone.
- Minimum label size must be tested at final trim, not only on screen.
- EPUB semantic order: figure title → alt text/accessible description → dated chronology → caption → source line.
- Avoid a fixed-width image as the only carrier of chronology. Preserve the dated event text in accessible HTML/EPUB content.
- Do not use NATO, White House, State Department, NORAD, Air Force, airline, team, or other institutional logos as decoration.
- Do not use screenshots of the archived federal pages. The original chronology is cleaner, more legible, and easier to rights-audit.

## Claim boundaries

Documented:

- NATO’s September 12 decision contained a foreign-direction condition.
- Frank Taylor briefed the North Atlantic Council on October 2; NATO then announced that the condition had been satisfied.
- NATO agreed support measures on October 4.
- Five NATO AWACS aircraft and associated personnel deployed to the United States in October and operated with NORAD from Tinker AFB.
- Burns was publicly present at the October 10 White House photo opportunity with Bush and Robertson.
- Eagle Assist accumulated the operational totals recorded above before ending May 16, 2002.

Do **not** infer without separate evidence:

- that Burns personally authored the Article 5 language;
- that his presence on October 10 proves a private negotiating role;
- that the initial U.S.-U.K. strikes in Afghanistan were a NATO operation;
- that every NATO member made the same military contribution;
- that September 12 alone completed every legal, evidentiary, and operational step later associated with Article 5;
- that a .gov-hosted photograph, NATO statement, or foreign official’s words are automatically public domain.

## Pass result

This pass produces a publication-safe visual plan rather than importing a photograph with compromised rights. It strengthens Chapter 11 by separating four things retrospective accounts often collapse: solidarity, Article 5’s conditional invocation, the evidentiary determination, and operational support. It also identifies one unusually useful Burns-specific documentary moment—October 10 at the White House—without claiming more for his role than the record supports.
