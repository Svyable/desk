# Chapter 10 media/release pass 02 — evidence before decoration

## Scope

This pass is deliberately narrow. Chapter 10 already has three original documentary objects and a useful visual archive. The next publication risk is not lack of imagery; it is allowing research-accessible sports imagery or later institutional recollection to drift into release material without a clean rights and provenance decision.

No new third-party image is approved in this pass. Web discovery was unavailable during the run, so no candidate has been promoted on incomplete rights evidence.

## Source-class labels for production

Chapter 10 mixes source types that should remain visibly distinct in research and captioning:

| Source class | Examples in current packet | Production rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Contemporary reporting | AP account of Chuck Samiotes; 2004 Greek and U.S. reporting; CNN transcript | strong for what was reported or observed at the time; images remain copyrighted unless separately cleared |
| Participant recollection | Burns retrospective | attribute memories and dates to Burns when not independently fixed |
| Later institutional history | Greek federation history / 2024 retrospective | useful for roster composition and institutional memory; label as retrospective rather than silently converting to contemporary evidence |
| Original project synthesis | three Chapter 10 SVGs | publication-safe only insofar as every label remains traceable to the underlying source map |

This distinction matters most around the project’s “first” date, the exact Boston Red Sox contribution, the Sarbanes–Angelos connection, and the local-versus-diaspora roster story. The diagrams should preserve uncertainty instead of cleaning it up graphically.

## Rights/provenance decision log

### Contemporary sports and news photography

**Status:** RESEARCH-ONLY by default.

ESPN, CNN, *Washington Post*, Greek press, professional-team, Olympic, and wire-service photographs are not publication-safe merely because a story page or transcript is publicly accessible. Do not vendor, crop, trace, or redraw recognizable protected photography without a separate license or a rights statement compatible with commercial book publication.

### Rob Derksen photograph

**Status:** HIGH-VALUE / NOT CLEARED.

A person-level image of Derksen remains the best candidate for a fourth Chapter 10 object because it would correct the natural visual tendency to make the ambassador the protagonist of the baseball work. Release only if the individual image has a clear creator, date/context, source institution, license or public-domain basis, and commercial reuse permission. A team-site or news-page appearance is not enough.

### Burns batting-cage demonstration

**Status:** HIGH-VALUE / NOT LOCATED.

The event is supported in Burns’s account, but no publication-cleared still has been established. Do not stage, generate, or use a generic batting-cage substitute. A substitute would create visual specificity the documentary record does not possess.

### Samiotes / Galatis grassroots work

**Status:** HIGH-VALUE / NOT CLEARED.

The CNN transcript is useful evidence for the two Boston-area Greek-American Red Sox fans and their field-building/youth work. CNN imagery remains research-only absent licensing. A privately held photograph could become preferable if the photographer/owner can grant commercial print and ebook rights and the depicted people/event are identified.

## Caption and credit standard

For any future third-party Chapter 10 asset that passes rights review, preserve these fields in the provenance packet before manuscript insertion:

- exact object title or archive caption;
- creator/photographer and employing institution where known;
- creation date and depicted-event date if different;
- depicted identities and location, with uncertainty stated rather than guessed;
- owning archive or source institution;
- stable source URL and catalog/item identifier;
- copyright/public-domain/license basis;
- commercial-publication compatibility;
- required attribution or credit line verbatim;
- modifications made (crop, tonal correction, redaction, etc.);
- local filename and checksum after acquisition;
- alt text that states what the image documents and what it does not prove.

Do not let a caption infer motive, causation, authorship, or institutional authority from mere co-presence in a photograph.
