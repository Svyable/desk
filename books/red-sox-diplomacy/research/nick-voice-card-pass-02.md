# Nicholas Burns Voice Cards — Pass 02

## Purpose

Extend the `NICK / THEME` visual language introduced in Pass 01 without saturating *Red Sox Diplomacy* with quotation furniture.

This pass uses a harder selection rule: a new card must change the temperature of the chapter. It should reveal a register of Nicholas Burns that paraphrase flattens—formation, maintenance, uncertainty, restraint, distinction, or revision.

The result is six additional cards. They are deliberately spread across the career rather than concentrated in the Red Sox material.

## Selection rules carried forward

- The words must be Burns's own.
- Keep the quotation compact enough to function as a designed interruption.
- Put source, date, and evidence type at the point of use.
- Prefer primary records; label contemporary or retrospective secondary evidence honestly.
- One card per chapter remains the default.
- A quote earns a card because it changes the narrative, not because it is memorable in isolation.
- Political or strategic judgments remain Burns's documented judgments, not the book's verdict.
- Retrospective self-assessment is evidence of what Burns later believed, not proof that the later judgment is correct.

## Pass 02 placements

### Chapter 1 — revelation

**NICK / REVELATION**

> “I had grown up with the notion that war could be heroic.”

Source: Boston College, *The Seminar*, a later institutional profile preserving Burns's recollection of Mark O'Connor reading Siegfried Sassoon in class.

https://www.bc.edu/bc-web/bcnews/campus-community/faculty/the-seminar.html

Function: interrupts a chapter otherwise dominated by hometown, family, education, and baseball with an early moral/intellectual disturbance. The line does not explain Burns's later judgments about war. It establishes that he later remembered one college classroom as changing an assumption he brought into it.

Evidence label: direct Burns quotation in a retrospective Boston College profile, not a contemporaneous student record.

### Chapter 9 — keep talking

**NICK / KEEP TALKING**

> “We have to set up mechanisms to make sure we are talking to one another.”

Source: *Harvard Crimson* report on Burns's November 2, 1998 Kennedy School appearance.

https://www.thecrimson.com/article/1998/11/3/ambassador-to-greece-urges-improved-relations/

Function: gives the Athens chapter a compact statement of relationship maintenance before the chapter expands into defense, public diplomacy, diaspora networks, protest, and the limits of representation.

Evidence label: contemporaneous secondary reporting quoting Burns, not a surviving primary transcript. Do not silently upgrade it.

### Chapter 14 — uncharted waters

**NICK / UNCHARTED WATERS**

> “We were in uncharted waters because, of course, there had never been a deal quite like this.”

Source: U.S. Department of State press conference with Foreign Secretary Shiv Shanker Menon, New Delhi, December 8, 2006.

https://2001-2009.state.gov/p/us/rm/2006/77555.htm

Function: resists hindsight. The civil nuclear initiative later acquired a familiar historical label, but the negotiators were building an exception whose legal and institutional path did not already exist.

Evidence label: primary State Department transcript.

### Chapter 15 — exhaust diplomacy

**NICK / EXHAUST DIPLOMACY**

> “We have time for diplomacy. We should exhaust diplomacy in this instance and we're trying very hard to do that.”

Source: Radio Free Europe/Radio Liberty interview, August 20, 2007, preserved by the State Department.

https://2001-2009.state.gov/p/us/rm/2007/91675.htm

Function: places Burns's own sequencing language inside an Iran chapter that ends without a comprehensive bargain. The card matters because diplomacy here is not retrospectively rewarded by a signing ceremony.

Evidence label: primary State Department transcript of Burns's interview.

Editorial boundary: the line is Burns's policy argument in 2007. It is not presented as proof that the policy was correct, sufficient, or destined to succeed.

### Chapter 21 — government and people

**NICK / GOVERNMENT AND PEOPLE**

> “Ultimately, we must compete with the government in China while living in peace with the Chinese people.”

Source: Fairbank Center / Belfer Center published text of Burns's April 30, 2025 lecture reflecting on his China service.

https://www.belfercenter.org/research-analysis/lessons-front-lines-us-china-relationship

Function: crystallizes the chapter's distinction between state competition and the people who live under, around, and beyond state policy. It also connects public diplomacy to the book's recurring warning against treating a government and a society as interchangeable.

Evidence label: Burns's post-service formulation in a Harvard-published lecture text. It is his interpretation of the relationship, not an editorial verdict on U.S.-China policy.

### Chapter 22 — revision

**NICK / REVISION**

> “I supported the war, I supported the intervention. I regret that now.”

Source: PBS *Amanpour & Company*, September 9, 2026.

https://www.pbs.org/wnet/amanpour-and-company/video/september-9-2026-prwre2/

Function: lets the late-career chapter end with revision rather than résumé. The quote does not erase Burns's support for the Iraq intervention in 2003. Its narrative value is that both judgments remain in the record.

Evidence label: direct Burns quotation in a dated PBS interview/transcript-video record.

Editorial boundary: regret is evidence of Burns's later assessment. It is not absolution, and the book does not convert the reversal into a score for or against his career.

## Deliberately skipped

### Chapters 11–12 — September 11 and Article 5

The most resonant short line in the current record is Condoleezza Rice's remembered remark to Burns: “It's good to have friends in the world.” It belongs in the manuscript, but it does **not** belong in a `NICK /` card because the device is reserved for Burns's own voice.

That constraint is useful. A recurring design element becomes less trustworthy if attribution is bent to serve the design.

### Chapter 13 — NATO rupture over Iraq

Burns's 2003 public record contains several card-worthy lines about transatlantic disagreement, unilateralism, and alliance repair. None was added in this pass. The chapter already has strong documentary structure, and Chapter 22's Iraq-revision card creates more biographical movement than adding another Burns argument from the moment itself.

Potential later substitution candidate:
- “None of us should want to stand alone in this new world.” — Burns, 2003 transatlantic-repair speech.

Use only if the surrounding chapter needs more direct Burns voice after later structural revisions.

### Chapter 3 — early career

Burns's later description of starting as a “lowly intern” in Nouakchott remains useful prose texture. It does not currently justify interrupting the chapter with another card so soon after Chapter 1.

## System-level result after Pass 02

The `NICK /` sequence now spans more than fandom or wit. Across the two passes it can reveal:

- inheritance;
- revelation;
- correction;
- briefing-room ritual;
- bilateral maintenance;
- public diplomacy through sport;
- post-championship language;
- negotiation under novelty;
- restraint before force;
- diplomacy as craft;
- societal ballast;
- joking rivalry;
- ambassadorial authority;
- government-versus-people distinction;
- retrospective revision.

That range is the point. The device should make Burns sound more like a person moving through different offices and decades, not like a quotation generator with one permanent register.

## Publication note

Keep the semantic Markdown stable. In print and ebook production, the strongest treatment remains:

- small-caps or condensed `NICK / THEME` kicker;
- oversized quote line;
- generous white space;
- restrained source line;
- optional hairline or margin offset;
- no decorative iconography that makes serious political or war-related cards look playful.

The baseball cards can tolerate wit. The Iran, China, war, and revision cards should derive visual force from typography and space rather than ornament.
