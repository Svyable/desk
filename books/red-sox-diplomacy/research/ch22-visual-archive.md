# Chapter 22 visual archive — Extra Innings

## Editorial job

The last main chapter must resist two false endings:

1. the diplomatic career as a final score;
2. hindsight as a mechanism that erases earlier responsibility.

Two documentary objects are enough.

- `HANDOFF 01` makes the office temporary and the relationship continuous.
- `REVISION OBJECT 01` preserves a consequential judgment at two different dates without allowing the later date to overwrite the first.

## HANDOFF 01

Asset: `../media/ch22-handoff.svg`

In his April 30, 2025 Fairbank/Belfer lecture after returning from Beijing, Burns explicitly compared diplomacy to an Olympic relay race. He said he had held the U.S. baton in China and was handing it to his confirmed successor, David Perdue.

Primary source:
- Nicholas Burns, `Lessons from the Front Lines of the U.S.-China Relationship`: https://www.belfercenter.org/research-analysis/lessons-front-lines-us-china-relationship

The metaphor is unusually well suited to the biography because it describes the office rather than the history as a game. An ambassador holds responsibility temporarily; the bilateral relationship and institutional file continue.

Guardrail: the handoff records succession in office. It does **not** imply Burns endorsed every policy of the successor or subsequent administration.

## REVISION OBJECT 01

Asset: `../media/ch22-revision-object.svg`

On September 9, 2026, in an `Amanpour and Company` interview marking twenty-five years since 9/11, Burns stated directly that he had supported the 2003 Iraq intervention while serving as U.S. Ambassador to NATO, now regretted that support and believed the decision to invade had been wrong.

Primary audiovisual/transcript source:
- PBS, `Amanpour and Company`, September 9, 2026: https://www.pbs.org/video/september-9-2026-prwre2/

Independent transcript route:
- CNN transcript, September 9, 2026: https://prod.transcripts.cnn.com/show/ampr/date/2026-09-09/segment/01

The object uses one short exact quotation: *“I regret that now.”*

The left side preserves the original judgment. The right side preserves the 2026 revision. The arrow is time, not absolution.

This is especially important because Chapter 13 already records how publicly and institutionally Burns fought the 2003 NATO dispute over Turkey. The later self-assessment belongs beside that history, not in place of it.

## Why this is not a chapter about Iraq

The September 2026 statement matters because it demonstrates continued professional reassessment after office. Chapter 22 should not reopen the full Iraq-war argument or use hindsight as a partisan verdict.

The broader closing point is revisability: officials act before history supplies a final interpretation, and mature professional memory can include saying that a consequential earlier judgment was wrong.

That capacity is not unique virtue and does not cancel consequences.

## Placement

- `HANDOFF 01` belongs immediately after the chapter introduces Burns's relay-race image and says the image is better than a final score.
- `REVISION OBJECT 01` belongs immediately after the prose reports Burns's 2026 regret and retrospective conclusion that the Iraq invasion was wrong, before the chapter explains that the later judgment does not erase the original one.

## Guardrails

- Burns is living; do not write a final legacy verdict or obituary frame.
- Do not imply hindsight automatically produces truer judgment.
- Do not present regret as absolution.
- Do not imply the relay means international politics itself has lanes, rules or a finish line.
- Do not turn `extra innings` into a forced metaphor for every later public appearance.
- Keep the 2026 judgment clearly retrospective and date-specific.

The final main chapter should end with handoff and revisability: the person leaves the chair, the file remains open, and even the story the former official tells about his own decisions can continue to change.
