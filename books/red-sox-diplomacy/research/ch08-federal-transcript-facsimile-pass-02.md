# Chapter 8 media pass — federal briefing transcripts as publication-safe documentary material

Date: 2026-09-17

## Why this pass exists

Chapter 8 already has three original diagrams and deliberately rejects a generic or rights-unclear podium photograph. The next useful media layer is not another image. It is the State Department's own 1997 briefing record: dated federal transcripts in which baseball appears, disappears, and is returned to Burns by reporters.

These records are unusually good book material because the documentary form itself clarifies the chapter's argument. The reader can see `QUESTION:` and `MR. BURNS:` alternating on the page, then watch the subject jump from baseball to Cyprus, NATO, Canada, Korea, or another policy file. A small original facsimile-style panel built from the transcript text can show the room's rhythm without reproducing a newspaper page, commercial photograph, team artwork, or third-party graphic.

## Rights posture

**Source class:** archived U.S. Department of State daily press briefing transcripts, produced and published as official executive-branch records.

**Copyright basis:** 17 U.S.C. §105 states that U.S. copyright protection is not available for works of the United States Government. The U.S. Copyright Office's Compendium describes this category as including work prepared by a federal officer or employee in the course of official duties.

- U.S. Copyright Office, Title 17, §105: https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap1.html
- U.S. Copyright Office, Compendium, Chapter 300: https://www.copyright.gov/comp3/docs/compendium.pdf

**Publication decision:** the official transcript text is suitable for commercial-book quotation and original typographic treatment, subject to ordinary accuracy and attribution. Do **not** infer that every image, seal, logo, embedded third-party item, or externally supplied work on an archived government webpage shares that status. This pass clears transcript text only.

**Trademark / presentation guardrail:** do not reproduce the Department seal, Red Sox marks, MLB marks, team logos, or archived site chrome in the proposed figure. The design should look like this book's documentary typography, not like an imitation State Department webpage.

## Primary records

### March 7, 1997 — the rules become explicit

**Record:** U.S. Department of State, Daily Press Briefing, DPB, March 7, 1997.  
**Briefer:** Nicholas Burns.  
**URL:** https://1997-2001.state.gov/briefings/9703/970307.html

**Supports:** Burns tells the room that the Red Sox are liked, the Tigers tolerated, and the Yankees hated, ending with “Those are the rules.” The transcript then moves directly into Secretary Albright's schedule, Egypt, Ukraine, Hungary, NATO enlargement, and NATO-Russia issues.

**Narrative value:** this is the cleanest compact proof that the baseball register and official register could occupy adjacent lines of the same federal record without being confused.

### April 1, 1997 — baseball as the safe April Fool's subject

**Record:** U.S. Department of State, Daily Press Briefing, DPB #46, April 1, 1997, 1:22 p.m.  
**Briefer:** Nicholas Burns.  
**URL:** https://1997-2001.state.gov/briefings/9704/970401.html

**Supports:** Burns opens with an April Fool's joke that Boston has already been mathematically eliminated, then explains that regional experts did not want an April Fool's provocation to create an international problem, so the briefing stayed with baseball.

**Narrative value:** the joke contains its own boundary condition. Baseball is useful precisely because everyone knows it is not policy.

**Guardrail:** Burns references Ronald Reagan's earlier off-camera remarks; do not expand that reference into a separate historical claim here unless independently sourced.

### April 2, 1997 — Cyprus journalists, baseball, Cuba policy

**Record:** U.S. Department of State, Daily Press Briefing, DPB #47, April 2, 1997, 12:10 p.m.  
**Briefer:** Nicholas Burns.  
**URL:** https://1997-2001.state.gov/briefings/9704/970402.html

**Supports:** Burns asks visiting Cypriot journalists whether they have baseball in Cyprus, discusses Secretary Albright's Opening Day appearance, then answers a real policy question about licensing professional sports activity in Cuba. He jokes about the Mets signing Fidel Castro and immediately distinguishes the joke from U.S. policy.

**Narrative value:** sport is not merely decorative in this transcript. It sits beside an actual legal-policy question about professional sports and Cuba, making the distinction between baseball talk and government action visible.

**Use caution:** the Castro/Mets material is comic but can easily pull the chapter sideways. Use only if the manuscript needs an example of the boundary between a joke and an actual licensing policy; otherwise the existing March/June/July material is cleaner.

### June 4, 1997 — baseball grievance meets Canada policy

**Record:** U.S. Department of State, Daily Press Briefing, DPB #84, June 4, 1997, 1:10 p.m.  
**Briefer:** Nicholas Burns.  
**URL:** https://1997-2001.state.gov/briefings/9706/970604db.html

**Supports:** reporters open by asking about Roger Clemens. Burns jokes about Boston's bitterness, novenas, and Richard Holbrooke managing the Red Sox, then welcomes everyone to a briefing on baseball and foreign policy. Later, while discussing the Pacific salmon dispute, he calls Canada a best friend and neighbor despite Toronto having taken Clemens.

**Narrative value:** this is the strongest single transcript for the book's boundary argument. The personal grievance is theatrical; the bilateral relationship remains official and serious.

### June 13, 1997 — the room receives physical baseball objects

**Record:** U.S. Department of State, Daily Press Briefing, DPB #90, June 13, 1997, 1:03 p.m.  
**Briefer:** Nicholas Burns.  
**URL:** https://1997-2001.state.gov/briefings/9706/970613db.html

**Supports:** Burns distributes baseballs signed by Secretary Albright to journalists who had attended Opening Day at Camden Yards and tosses one intended for a reporter's young son. The briefing then proceeds into Korea, Turkey, Cyprus, the United Nations, China/Taiwan, Croatia, Haiti, the Middle East, Canada, Bolivia, and North Korea.

**Narrative value:** this is more than banter. It documents a baseball object physically entering the State press corps through an official briefing. It is a useful factual companion to the later farewell inventory, where the room gives baseball objects back to Burns.

**Rights decision:** the event can be represented through original text/diagram. Do not manufacture an image of the signed baseball or imply that an extant photograph has been located.

### July 8, 1997 — the intern fails the admission test

**Record:** U.S. Department of State, Daily Press Briefing, DPB #101, July 8, 1997, 1:22 p.m.  
**Briefer:** Nicholas Burns.  
**URL:** https://1997-2001.state.gov/briefings/9707/970708db.html

**Supports:** Burns identifies Maine as Red Sox territory, asks a Colby intern his allegiance, receives “Yankee fan,” and mock-expels him through either door before granting an All-Star Game reprieve.

**Narrative value:** the scene is already in Chapter 8 and should remain there. The transcript can support a compact facsimile panel without needing a photograph of the intern or room.

## Recommended original figure

### WORKING TITLE — `THE ROOM KNEW THE RULES`

**Form:** one half-page, original transcript-strip figure. Not a screenshot. Not a webpage facsimile. A typographic documentary abstraction using short, verbatim fragments from federal transcripts plus dates and subject pivots.

Suggested structure:

| Date | Baseball register | Immediate or same-briefing policy register |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mar. 7 | Red Sox / Tigers / Yankees “rules” | Albright schedule; Egypt; Ukraine; NATO |
| Apr. 1 | April Fool's Red Sox elimination | explicit decision not to joke about foreign policy |
| Jun. 4 | Clemens / novenas / Boston grievance | Canada and Pacific salmon dispute |
| Jun. 13 | Albright-signed baseballs for press | Korea; Turkey; Cyprus; UN; Middle East |
| Jul. 8 | Maine intern / Yankees mock-expulsion | normal State briefing resumes |

The figure should not claim that the right-hand policy subject followed within a fixed number of seconds unless the transcript supports that exact sequence. Label the column **“policy register in the same briefing”** when necessary rather than “next topic.”

### Caption draft

**THE ROOM KNEW THE RULES — 1997.** State Department transcripts preserve baseball not as a separate publicity campaign but as a recurring register inside ordinary briefings. Reporters and Burns could move from Red Sox grievance to official policy without confusing the two. Selected episodes; not a complete count. Source: archived U.S. Department of State daily press briefings.

### Credit / source line

`Sources: U.S. Department of State archived Daily Press Briefings, Mar. 7, Apr. 1, Jun. 4, Jun. 13, and Jul. 8, 1997. Original figure by the author. Federal transcript text: U.S. Government work; see 17 U.S.C. §105.`

### Alt text

`Five dated 1997 State Department briefing episodes pair recurring Red Sox and Yankees banter with the policy subjects handled in the same official briefing, showing baseball as a familiar social register rather than a substitute for foreign policy.`

## Pull-quote discipline

The federal status makes the transcript text legally reusable, but the book still benefits from restraint. Do not fill a page with transcript dialogue merely because it is public domain. The strongest short fragments are already doing narrative work:

- “Those are the rules.” — March 7.
- “baseball and foreign policy.” — June 4, if a second short fragment is needed.

The June 13 baseball distribution is better paraphrased than quoted. The visual surprise is the object moving through the room, not the wording.

## Formatting guidance

- Keep the figure and caption together in print/PDF; avoid a page break between the final row and source line.
- At final trim, dates and both registers must remain legible without magnification. If five rows become cramped, cut April 1 before reducing type.
- Do not use color as the only distinction between baseball and policy. Column labels, rules, spacing, or typographic weight must carry the structure in grayscale.
- In EPUB, render the semantic fallback as date → baseball register → policy register for each row. Do not rely on a wide fixed table that forces horizontal scrolling.
- Keep source URLs in research/provenance notes rather than printing five long URLs in the reader-facing caption.
- If the figure uses quotation marks, preserve transcript wording exactly and verify against the archived page at production lock.
- Do not use the Department seal, team logos, baseball-card styling, faux typewriter distress, or other nostalgia treatment. The point is documentary adjacency, not period decoration.

## Research strengthened

This pass adds one useful mechanism to Chapter 8's evidence. The baseball persona was not only remembered by reporters; it is visible in the structure of official federal records. On June 13 the exchange even became material: Albright-signed baseballs were distributed through the press office before the briefing returned to foreign affairs. That creates a clean documentary progression already latent in the chapter:

**banter → repeated room ritual → physical baseball object → farewell objects returned to Burns.**

That progression is stronger than adding another generic portrait.

## Highest-value next opportunity

The narrow photo search remains unresolved: a publication-safe, event-level photograph of Nicholas Burns at the State Department briefing podium between 1995 and July 1997. The best next repositories are NARA Still Pictures, State Department historical/public-affairs holdings, and Clinton Presidential Library audiovisual collections. Any candidate should be rejected unless it carries an item-level identifier, date/event context, creator or photographer, and a reuse statement or federal-work basis that actually covers the photograph.

A second, non-photo opportunity is to locate an official federal record or item-level photograph of the June 13, 1997 Albright-signed baseball distribution. If found with clean rights, it would connect the chapter's transcript evidence to a physical object without depending on the rights-restricted Washington Post farewell photograph.
