# Nicholas Burns social evidence index

This is the navigation layer for the social-media evidence in *Red Sox Diplomacy*. It exists to prevent three recurring research errors:

1. treating every social reference as if it had the same provenance;
2. mixing Burns's personal `@RNicholasBurns` account with the official ambassadorial `@USAmbChina` account;
3. allowing a later first-person recollection to overwrite or impersonate a contemporaneous record.

## Evidence hierarchy for manuscript prose

Prefer, in order:

1. direct contemporaneous primary post with recoverable status URL;
2. contemporaneous federal/stable preservation carrying date, authorship, source URL, and content;
3. primary broadcast, speech, interview, or official institutional record;
4. first-person retrospective by Burns, explicitly labeled as retrospective;
5. contemporaneous secondary preservation/analysis;
6. social scraping/index mirrors, clearly labeled as preservation only;
7. unresolved candidate leads, which should not enter declarative manuscript prose.

A lower-ranked source can still be useful when it preserves something the higher-ranked layer does not. The ranking governs confidence and attribution, not whether the source is worth keeping.

## Manuscript claims currently safe

### Safe at high confidence

- Burns's Red Sox identity long predates the China posting.
- His official China account mixed formal diplomacy with first-person travel, sports, cultural participation, education, and humor from early in the posting.
- Boston sports references appeared incidentally inside ordinary official work, including the June 2022 Wuhan Celtics post and the July 2024 China-scholars/Celtics post.
- Burns publicly participated in baseball and basketball during official-capacity programming in 2023.
- In October 2024 Burns explicitly connected young Chinese baseball participants to future membership in Red Sox Nation on the official ambassadorial account.
- After leaving post, Burns explicitly described sports as a bridge and said he deliberately wore his Red Sox hat in multiple Chinese locations and on *60 Minutes*.

### Safe only with provenance qualification

- Great Wall `RedSox Nation` images are contemporaneously preserved and Red Sox-specific, but their original status IDs are still incomplete.
- The July 4, 2023 Red Sox-cap image is preserved in the federal corpus, but its originating social metadata is unresolved.
- The Bund, Xiamen, Chengdu, and *60 Minutes* independently corroborate Burns's presence/event context; except for the Great Wall preservation, the Red Sox-hat detail currently derives from Burns's later first-person account unless separately visually verified.
- The Xiamen Emmanuel Church Boston organ item has strong preserved Burns-attributed text but still lacks its original status ID.

## Claims to avoid

Do not write that:

- baseball `made` Burns a diplomat;
- every sports post was a planned diplomatic tactic;
- the July 2023 Red Sox-cap image has a recovered tweet caption;
- either Great Wall candidate has a recovered status ID when it does not;
- CBS's *60 Minutes* transcript independently proves the Red Sox hat;
- a post-government personal-account comment proves a China-era event;
- social-media evidence substitutes for missing archival evidence about the 1986 World Series, October 27, 2004, or other named manuscript gaps.

## Current synthesis

The social record is most useful when it is treated as evidence of **continuity under office**. Burns did not arrive in Beijing as a blank institutional representative and then manufacture a baseball persona. The older record establishes the fandom. The personal account establishes that he publicly identified with Red Sox Nation before government service in China. The ambassadorial account then shows what happened when that recognizable identity passed through a formal office: sometimes it disappeared behind policy, sometimes it surfaced as a Celtics aside, a baseball, a battered cap, a joke about losing, or a group of young Chinese players being welcomed into Red Sox Nation.

That is narrower than `baseball diplomacy` as a slogan and stronger as a documentary claim.
