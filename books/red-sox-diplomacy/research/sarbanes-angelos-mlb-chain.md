# Sarbanes–Angelos–MLB Evidence Chain

This dossier isolates the institutional chain behind the Greece baseball story so the manuscript does not flatten a multi-actor effort into a Nicholas Burns anecdote.

## What is well established

### 1. Greek baseball organizers sought American help

The Hellenic baseball effort was starting from almost nothing: weak domestic infrastructure, few players, and little native baseball culture. Contemporary reporting in 2000 described weed-choked fields at a former U.S. military installation and a Greek effort to build a credible Olympic program before Athens 2004.

Panos Mitsiopoulos and the Hellenic Amateur Baseball Federation became the principal Greek institutional actors. Later federation histories say Mitsiopoulos approached Ambassador Nicholas Burns for help.

### 2. Burns treated the request as a public-diplomacy problem, not merely a sports favor

An ESPN feature published before the 2004 Olympics records Burns recalling that he brought American coaches and players to Greece for demonstrations, appeared on Greek television from a batting cage to show how to hit, played catch with Greek children, and lobbied the Greek government to establish a budget. Burns said that within two years Greece had roughly twenty youth baseball teams and ten softball teams in two leagues.

This is important because it shows Burns doing more than brokering introductions. He was visible in the public-facing work of making an unfamiliar American game legible inside Greece.

### 3. Burns helped connect the Greek effort to Peter Angelos

Contemporaneous Washington Post reporting from September 1999 records Peter Angelos meeting Greek officials at a reception held by Ambassador Burns and the Hellenic Amateur Baseball Federation. The report says Angelos was being assisted by the baseball commissioner's office and by the Boston Red Sox, Burns's favorite team.

The federation's retrospective history supplies an additional link: Burns contacted Maryland senator Paul Sarbanes, who knew Angelos, the Greek-American owner of the Baltimore Orioles. Because this portion is a later organizational retrospective, the manuscript should present the Burns→Sarbanes→Angelos sequence as strongly corroborated rather than pretend every link comes from one contemporaneous record.

A 2014 Greek-American account likewise says Burns created or spearheaded a “Friends of Greek Baseball” effort and contacted Sarbanes, who then helped bring Angelos into the project. Treat that as supporting retrospective evidence, not as the sole authority.

### 4. Angelos supplied organizational and financial muscle

The Orioles organization became a major operating partner. Reporting before the Olympics says Peter Angelos assigned scout Rob Derksen to the Greek project. His son Lou Angelos served in a managerial role. Derksen searched across North America and elsewhere for players of Greek descent who could qualify for citizenship and Olympic eligibility.

Los Angeles Times reporting in 2004 put the Orioles' contribution at roughly $750,000 at that stage. Federation histories give a higher lifetime figure for Angelos's personal support. Because those numbers are not identical, final manuscript prose should either use the contemporaneous $750,000 figure with its date or describe Angelos as contributing substantial personal and organizational resources without collapsing the estimates.

### 5. Major League Baseball International became an institutional partner

Contemporary and near-contemporary accounts say MLB International helped with coaching, field construction, organizational development, and team building. One Greek-American account reported an expected MLB International contribution of about $500,000.

The strongest manuscript point is not the exact dollar figure. It is that the ambassadorial introduction became a network involving a Greek federation, diaspora political figures, a Major League club owner, MLB International, coaches, scouts, and eventually a national Olympic team.

### 6. Rob Derksen turned the network into a team

Derksen is the operational hinge who should not disappear from the story. Federation history records him traveling widely to identify eligible players, assembling a pool of more than seventy-five candidates, and coaching the team through European qualification. Greece won silver at the 2003 European Championship and secured its place in the 2004 Athens Olympic tournament.

Derksen died suddenly of a heart attack in June 2004, two months before the Olympics. Contemporary player A.J. Brack later described him as the catalyst who had assembled the team and helped bring baseball into Greece. Any chapter on the Olympic culmination should give Derksen real narrative weight rather than making the outcome look like a Burns project that simply ran to completion.

## Burns's own retrospective

By 2004 Burns was calling the introduction of baseball to Greece a joint American-Greek venture and one of the most satisfying things he had done as ambassador. The phrasing is useful because it preserves shared ownership. It also fits a larger pattern in his diplomatic practice: use official position to connect institutions, then let other people with resources and expertise do work the embassy cannot do itself.

## Manuscript implications

The Greece chapters should resist the easy sentence “Nicholas Burns brought baseball to Greece.” He did not. Greek organizers were already trying; Sarbanes connected diaspora influence; Angelos and the Orioles contributed money and personnel; MLB International supplied expertise; Derksen recruited and coached; Greek and Greek-diaspora players had to perform; the Greek government and Olympic structures had to recognize the effort.

What Burns appears to have done exceptionally well was identify a small, culturally resonant problem and assemble a network around it.

That is a more interesting form of diplomacy than hero attribution.

## High-confidence scene candidates

- Burns at a Greek television batting cage demonstrating how to hit.
- Burns playing catch with Greek children during baseball demonstrations.
- The 1999 reception hosted by Burns and the Hellenic Amateur Baseball Federation with Peter Angelos.
- The move from almost no domestic infrastructure to youth leagues and a national project.
- Derksen's recruitment campaign across the diaspora.
- The 2003 European silver medal that secured Olympic participation.
- Derksen's death eight weeks before the Athens Games.

No sensory detail, dialogue, crowd reaction, or private thought should be added unless another source supplies it.

## Confidence note

The broad network is exceptionally well supported. The exact chronology of who first contacted whom varies slightly across later retellings. The safest formulation is: Greek organizers approached Burns; Burns helped activate American baseball and Greek-diaspora networks; Sarbanes and Angelos became key links; the Orioles and MLB International supplied major practical support.
