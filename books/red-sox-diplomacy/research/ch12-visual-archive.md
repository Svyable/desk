# Chapter 12 visual archive — Article 5

## Method

Chapter 12 begins where Chapter 11 stops. The problem is no longer whether allied solidarity exists. It is how an alliance turns solidarity into law, law into authorized requests, and requests into actual capability.

The visual sequence therefore uses three layers:

1. **CONDITIONAL CLAUSE 01** — September 12 commitment paired with the still-open evidence condition;
2. **IMPLEMENTATION PACKAGE 01** — the eight October 4 measures requested by the United States;
3. **FLIGHT LEDGER 01** — Operation Eagle Assist as measurable multinational action over American airspace.

## Burns / Rice call — primary retrospective quotation

Burns's 2024 first-person account says that early on September 12 he called National Security Adviser Condoleezza Rice, sought presidential authority to cast the U.S. vote, and remembers her ending the call with:

> *“It’s good to have friends in the world.”*

Source:
https://afsa.org/nato-75

**Editorial status:** Burns's retrospective recollection of Rice's words. Attribute the quotation to his memory, not to a contemporaneous White House transcript.

## CONDITIONAL CLAUSE 01 — September 12 → October 2

**Local media:** `../media/ch12-conditional-clause.svg`

### September 12

NATO Press Release (2001)124 states that the Council agreed that **if** the attacks were determined to have been directed from abroad against the United States, they would be regarded as covered by Article 5.

Primary source:
https://www.nato.int/docu/pr/2001/p01-124e.htm

### October 2

NATO's official history states that, after the Council was briefed on the results of the investigation, it determined that the attacks were covered by Article 5.

Source:
https://www.nato.int/en/what-we-do/introduction-to-nato/collective-defence-and-article-5

**Narrative job:** Keep solidarity and verification in the same frame. The September 12 conditional decision is historically stronger when its condition is visible rather than edited away.

## Burns as channel for the U.S. request

The October 3 State Department daily briefing identifies Nicholas Burns as the ambassador who delivered the U.S. list of requested NATO steps.

Primary source:
https://2001-2009.state.gov/r/pa/prs/dpb/2001/5211.htm

Spokesman Richard Boucher also makes an important legal/political distinction in the same briefing: allies were obligated to assist in collective defense, but they retained a choice about the specific steps they would take within the consensus process.

This supports the chapter's argument that Article 5 organizes different capabilities rather than ordering identical action from every ally.

## IMPLEMENTATION PACKAGE 01 — the eight measures

**Local media:** `../media/ch12-eight-measures.svg`

NATO's October 4 statement records eight measures requested by the United States and agreed by the Allies:

1. enhanced intelligence sharing and cooperation;
2. assistance to allies or other countries facing increased terrorist threat because they support the campaign;
3. increased security for U.S. and allied facilities;
4. backfilling selected allied assets;
5. blanket overflight clearances for relevant military flights, subject to national procedures;
6. access to ports and airfields, including refuelling;
7. readiness to deploy standing naval forces in the Eastern Mediterranean;
8. readiness to deploy NATO airborne early-warning assets.

Primary source:
https://www.nato.int/en/news-and-events/events/transcripts/2001/10/04/statement-to-the-press

**Narrative job:** Make the phrase “collective defense” operational without pretending every ally was ordered into the same military action.

## FLIGHT LEDGER 01 — Operation Eagle Assist

**Local media:** `../media/ch12-eagle-assist-ledger.svg`

NATO's current official history records:

- seven NATO AWACS aircraft;
- 830 crew members;
- thirteen NATO countries represented in the crews;
- more than 360 sorties;
- operation through mid-May 2002;
- first deployment of NATO military assets in support of an Article 5 operation.

Source:
https://www.nato.int/en/what-we-do/introduction-to-nato/collective-defence-and-article-5

A U.S. Air Force chronology independently records NATO AWACS deployment to the United States after September 11 and 360 sorties by the end of the operation.

**Narrative job:** Let capability, duration and multinational participation carry the symbolism. The alliance literally supplied aircraft and crews to help patrol the attacked ally's airspace.

## Naval counterpart — Operation Active Endeavour

NATO states that naval forces began deploying to the Eastern Mediterranean in October 2001 and that the mission was formally named Operation Active Endeavour on October 26.

Source:
https://www.nato.int/en/what-we-do/operations-and-missions/operation-active-endeavour-2001-2016

No separate visual object is necessary in Chapter 12 unless later page pacing requires it. The eight-measures object already includes the naval commitment; an additional ship image would risk decorative duplication.
