# Boston College 1978: the Mods, roommates, and retrospective Red Sox identity

## Finding

Boston College's published transcript of R. Nicholas Burns's 2002 commencement address supplies unusually useful authenticated first-person evidence about his undergraduate setting. Burns told the graduating class that three of his 1978 roommates were present at the ceremony and that they had lived in the Mods. In the same address, he identified himself as a loyal member of Red Sox Nation and predicted a Boston World Series championship that October.

Boston College later identified the specific undergraduate residence as Mod 36A. A university feature on the Mods says Burns mentioned the Mod in his 2002 commencement address and visited 36A after the speech.

These sources improve the Boston College chapter because they replace generic campus biography with a specific, institutionally documented residential setting and a first-person retrospective link between Burns's BC identity and his Red Sox identity.

## What the evidence supports

- Burns graduated from Boston College in 1978.
- In a 2002 BC commencement address, Burns said three of his former roommates were present and that they had lived in the Mods.
- In that same address, Burns publicly described himself as a loyal member of Red Sox Nation.
- Boston College later identified his Mod as 36A and reported that he visited it after the 2002 commencement address.

## What it does **not** support

This evidence is retrospective. It does **not** establish that Burns watched, listened to, attended, discussed, celebrated, or mourned any particular Red Sox game while a BC student. It does not establish where he was during the 1975 World Series, whether his roommates shared his baseball interest, whether baseball was watched in Mod 36A, or whether any particular undergraduate baseball ritual occurred there.

Do not turn Mod 36A into a reconstructed 1975 scene. Do not invent a television, radio, beer, crowd, conversation, reaction, or roommate baseball anecdote.

The source also does not authenticate or license any first-person material attributed to Libby Baylies.

## Manuscript use

Safe promotion language can say, in substance, that Burns later remembered living in the Mods with roommates during his BC years and, from the same commencement podium, publicly identified himself as a loyal Red Sox partisan. That pairing is evidence of how Burns himself presented Boston College and Red Sox identity in later public memory; it is not evidence of a specific 1970s game-night scene.

This is stronger than merely juxtaposing a 1978 graduation date with later biographies calling him a Red Sox fan because the two identities occur in the same authenticated Burns speech.
