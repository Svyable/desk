## `media/ch03-shultz-asia-1988.svg`

**Function:** documentary itinerary showing the repeated travel underlying Burns's later recollection of George Shultz's diplomatic-gardening lesson.

**Artwork:** original project diagram. It is not a trace, crop, adaptation, or redraw of a State Department or Harvard graphic.

### Primary-source stack

1. **Office of the Historian travel chronology — George P. Shultz.** Official stop-by-stop chronology with dates and meeting summaries. https://history.state.gov/departmenthistory/travels/secretary/shultz-george-pratt
2. **Foreign Relations of the United States, 1981–1988, vol. I, doc. 328.** Shultz's July 21, 1988 Honolulu address says he was concluding his Asia travels and calls the journey a **nine-stop trip**. https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1981-88v01/d328
3. **FRUS vol. I, doc. 316, footnote 11.** Contemporary editorial chronology identifies Bangkok, Kuala Lumpur, Jakarta, Manila, Beijing, Seoul, and Tokyo and points forward to the July 21 Honolulu address. https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1981-88v01/d316fn15
4. **Nicholas Burns, “The Diplomat as Gardener,” Harvard Kennedy School.** Retrospective witness source for Burns's memory of accompanying Shultz and the professional lesson he drew from the trip. https://www.hks.harvard.edu/publications/diplomat-gardener

### Source reconciliation

The sources describe the same journey using different counting conventions. Burns retrospectively calls it an **eight-country, three-week tour**. Shultz's contemporaneous Honolulu address calls it a **nine-stop trip**. The Office of the Historian travel ledger provides the granular itinerary used by the SVG: Bangkok, Kuala Lumpur, Jakarta, Manila, Hong Kong, Beijing, Seoul, Tokyo, and Majuro, followed by Shultz's July 21 Honolulu address.

These descriptions are not silently normalized into one number. “Country” and “stop” are not equivalent units, and Hong Kong in 1988 was a British dependent territory. Chapter 3 therefore names the stops rather than claiming that one source's count invalidates the other.

**Rights posture:** the SVG is original project artwork. The Office of the Historian/FRUS material is U.S. federal government material and is used for factual chronology and citation. 17 U.S.C. §105 is the governing U.S. copyright rule for federal works; it does not clear any separately credited third-party material. The Harvard essay is used as a linked retrospective source only; no Harvard artwork is reproduced or adapted.

**Attribution/credit:** `Original diagram by Sven Hardy Benson; itinerary data from the U.S. Department of State, Office of the Historian.` This credit is suitable for a production credits list if one is used; the manuscript's object caption should remain concise.

**Alt text:** `Documentary itinerary of George Shultz's July 1988 Asia travels observed by Nicholas Burns.` The existing alt text is concise and functional; do not replace it with decorative description.

**Print/EPUB treatment:** keep the itinerary and caption together. Preserve enough width for city labels to remain legible; if a narrow EPUB viewport makes the SVG labels unreadable, allow the SVG to scale to container width rather than rasterizing it at a fixed small size. The text caption carries the complete stop sequence, so the evidence remains accessible if a reader cannot inspect the graphic.

## Reuse decisions

- **Do not copy the Office of the Historian travel-page layout or any institutional graphic.** The original itinerary communicates the chapter-specific point more directly.
- **Do not reproduce Harvard Kennedy School page artwork or branding.** The essay is a source, not a visual asset.
- **Do not add generic airport, passport, baseball-road-trip, or embassy photography.** It would decorate the metaphor without strengthening the documentary record.
- **Do not use an archival Shultz photograph merely to put a face beside the itinerary.** A photograph would imply scene evidence the chapter does not possess unless its date, location, creator, and rights can be tied to the July 1988 journey.

## Next high-value media target

The strongest next addition for this chapter would be a **verified federal photograph from one of the July 1988 Shultz stops** whose catalog record identifies date/location and whose creator/rights status clearly supports commercial reuse. It should be added only if it documents the actual journey rather than serving as generic Shultz portraiture. A second-best option is an original small timeline aligning Burns's early postings with communications technology available to Americans overseas, but only after the factual claims about period media access are sourced tightly enough to avoid turning atmosphere into pseudo-precision.
