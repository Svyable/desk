# Boston College 1974–75 — Mark O'Connor and Burns's own formative memory

## Finding

Boston College's profile of historian Mark O'Connor preserves a useful first-person recollection from R. Nicholas Burns '78 about one of his earliest BC courses. Burns identifies O'Connor as one of his first professors and recalls hearing him read Siegfried Sassoon's antiwar poetry "in the last year of the Vietnam War." Burns says he had grown up with the notion that war could be heroic and describes the encounter as a revelation, then credits O'Connor with forcing students to ask ethical and moral questions of history.

Source:
- Boston College, "The Seminar" (profile of Mark O'Connor): https://www.bc.edu/bc-web/bcnews/campus-community/faculty/the-seminar.html

## Why this matters

The manuscript's Boston College gap is partly chronological: what can be said with confidence about Burns as a student around 1975 rather than only from later résumé summaries?

This institutional profile supplies a bounded answer in Burns's own retrospective voice. It places him in an early BC history classroom during the closing phase of the Vietnam War and identifies a specific intellectual experience he later remembered as formative. It is useful evidence for the development of the diplomat's historical and ethical frame without requiring invented student-life texture.

The source is especially valuable because Burns does not merely praise O'Connor generically. He remembers a specific teaching act—reading Sassoon—and explains the change in question it produced for him: from an inherited heroic conception of war toward explicit ethical interrogation.

## Date boundary

Burns graduated from Wellesley High School in 1974 and Boston College in 1978. The source calls O'Connor one of Burns's first professors and Burns places the Sassoon reading in "the last year of the Vietnam War." That makes the 1974–75 academic year the natural chronological frame.

Do **not** silently turn this into an exact class date, semester, course number, classroom, or April 1975 scene unless a syllabus, registrar record, notebook, or other contemporary record supplies those details.

## Evidentiary classification

**First-person retrospective quotation published by Boston College.**

The article is an institutional secondary source carrying Burns's direct retrospective recollection. It is not a contemporaneous 1975 diary, student newspaper report, transcript, or recording. Any exact quotation used in manuscript prose should be checked against the live BC page at copyedit/quote-verification stage.

## What this strengthens

1. **BC was intellectually formative in a specific, documented way.** Burns later singled out O'Connor's history teaching and ethical questioning.
2. **The Vietnam-era context can be made concrete without invention.** Sassoon's antiwar poetry is the documented classroom object Burns remembered.
3. **The biography can resist hindsight flattening.** The later diplomat did not describe himself as arriving at BC with a fully formed view of war; his own account describes a change in how he thought about it.

## What this does not establish

Do not use this source to claim:

- that Burns opposed the Vietnam War as a student;
- that the Sassoon reading caused his diplomatic career or later policy positions;
- that the episode occurred on a particular day, in a particular room, or in a named course;
- what Burns did immediately before or after class;
- what classmates thought of him;
- any Red Sox or baseball behavior during the 1974–75 academic year;
- that Burns attended, watched, or reacted to the 1975 World Series while at Boston College;
- any view, memory, fandom, attendance, dialogue, or reaction by Libby Baylies.

## Manuscript opportunity

This is strong enough for a short, attributed addition in the Boston College section if the chapter currently needs an intellectual rather than baseball-only bridge. The safe narrative unit is compact: Burns arrived at BC in the final year of the Vietnam War; one of his first professors, Mark O'Connor, read Sassoon; decades later Burns remembered the reading as a revelation because it challenged his earlier heroic conception of war and made ethical judgment part of historical inquiry.

The scene should remain retrospective. Do not reconstruct the classroom beyond what Burns actually supplied.

## Remaining Boston College / 1975 baseball gap

This source does **not** close the high-value baseball question. Still needed:

- 1975 *Heights* / student-newspaper references to Burns, Red Sox viewing, World Series campus rituals, or dorm/common-room broadcasts;
- BC yearbook or student-directory evidence locating Burns's residence or activities;
- oral history from named classmates or faculty with authenticated provenance;
- any contemporaneous Burns writing from 1974–78;
- archival evidence connecting him personally to the 1975 Red Sox season.

Until one of those appears, the manuscript should not manufacture a 1975 World Series scene simply because Burns was a BC student and later a lifelong Red Sox fan.
