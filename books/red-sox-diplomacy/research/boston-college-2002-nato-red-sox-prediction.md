# Boston College, May 2002 — the NATO ambassador predicts October

## Finding

On May 20, 2002, U.S. Ambassador to NATO Nicholas Burns returned to Boston College to deliver its commencement address. Boston College preserves the full institutional transcript.

Burns opened by locating himself among New Englanders and Boston institutions. He noted that the New England Patriots were the reigning Super Bowl champions, then made a much riskier prediction. After what he called a mere 84-year pause, Burns said, as a loyal member of Red Sox Nation, that Boston would be home to the World Series champions that October.

Primary institutional transcript:
https://www.bc.edu/bc-web/bcnews/campus-community/alumni/r--nicholas-burns-2002-commencement-address.html

The timing gives the line more value than another generic proof of fandom. Burns was speaking as the sitting U.S. ambassador to NATO, nine months after September 11 and at the end of the period in which NATO aircraft had been helping patrol U.S. airspace under Operation Eagle Assist. The speech quickly turns from Boston and the Red Sox to public service, September 11, NATO, Afghanistan, Kosovo, the Middle East and the argument that the United States needed friends and allies.

This is therefore a clean first-person example of the two registers coexisting in one speech: intensely local Boston identity and the responsibilities of representing the United States through an alliance.

## The baseball correction

Burns's prediction failed.

MLB's official Red Sox/Fenway history records that the 2002 club finished 93–69, second in the American League East, and did not qualify for the postseason. MLB's 2002 standings show Boston 10.5 games behind the Yankees in the division and six games out of the Wild Card. The Wild Card went to Anaheim, which went on to win the World Series.

Authoritative baseball sources:
- https://www.mlb.com/redsox/ballpark/museum/timeline/2000-2009
- https://www.mlb.com/standings/league/2002

The failed prediction should remain failed. It is more revealing as ordinary fan behavior than as setup for the 2004 championship. Burns was not prophetic; he was hopeful two seasons too early.

## Why this is useful

The manuscript already has powerful NATO material and later first-person evidence for the 2003 ALCS loss in Brussels. This 2002 speech adds a different texture:

- Burns is back in Boston while serving abroad;
- the Red Sox line appears before the speech turns to the post-9/11 world;
- he uses "Red Sox Nation" publicly before the 2004 championship made the phrase globally triumphant;
- the prediction immediately supplies an anti-mythology check because Boston missed the playoffs despite winning 93 games.

The scene does not need a baseball-as-diplomacy interpretation. Its value is biographical portability: the NATO ambassador can stand in Alumni Stadium, sound unmistakably like a Boston fan, and then spend the same address arguing that American power requires international engagement and allies.

## Documentary boundary

Safe:

- Burns delivered the Boston College commencement address on May 20, 2002 as U.S. Ambassador to NATO;
- he publicly identified himself with Red Sox Nation and predicted Boston would win the World Series that October;
- the same speech discussed September 11, NATO, Afghanistan, Kosovo, the Middle East, public service and American international engagement;
- the 2002 Red Sox finished 93–69, second in the AL East, and missed the postseason;
- MLB's official history and standings provide the baseball correction.

Not safe:

- claiming the baseball opening was designed to make Burns's NATO argument more persuasive;
- inferring audience reaction beyond anything preserved in the transcript;
- claiming Burns connected the Red Sox prediction to alliance politics;
- treating the failed prediction as foreshadowing that Burns himself recognized;
- saying the 2002 disappointment shaped his reaction to 2003 or 2004 without first-person evidence.

## Manuscript recommendation

Do not interrupt Chapter 12's Article 5 machinery merely to insert another baseball joke. The strongest eventual use is a short hinge between the Article 5/NATO sequence and the later Brussels baseball material: in May 2002 Burns could return home, make the kind of prediction a Boston fan had been making for decades, and then fly back to an alliance whose post-9/11 work had become his daily life.

If used, keep the baseball correction close. The Red Sox won 93 games and still missed October. The sentence earns trust by allowing Burns to be wrong.

## Evidentiary level

**Primary institutional transcript + authoritative MLB team history and standings.** Strong for Burns's exact public prediction, role and speech context, and for the baseball outcome. No evidence of diplomatic effect or private reaction.
