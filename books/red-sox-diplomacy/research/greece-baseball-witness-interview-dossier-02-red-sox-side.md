# Greece Baseball Witness / Interview Dossier 02 — Red Sox side

## Purpose

The missing question is now specific:

**Who inside the Boston Red Sox handled the 1999–2000 Greece baseball request, and what did the club actually promise or deliver?**

This dossier is limited to the Red Sox institutional side. It follows the organizational map now visible in the record rather than treating “the front office” as one undifferentiated actor.

## Working organizational map

Near the relevant period, institutional sources place:

- **Dick Bresciani** — Vice President of Public Affairs; head of a department described as including publications/photography, community/customer relations, and historical archives; chairman of the 1999 All-Star task force.
- **Ron Burton Jr.** — Community Relations Manager; independently documented doing Community Relations work by roughly 1996.
- **Adam Shamus** — Community Relations staffer whose own biography says the highlight of his Red Sox tenure was working the 1999 All-Star festivities.
- **Larry Cancro** — Vice President of Sales and Marketing, 1996–2003; member of the 1999 All-Star task force.
- **John Harrington** — executive authority / club president.
- **Dan Duquette** — general manager / baseball operations.

The purpose of interviews is to find the handoff among those structures, not assume one existed.

---

## 1. Ron Burton Jr. — first living institutional witness to approach

### Why first

If “support” meant equipment, clinics, youth-development help, donations, community programming, tickets/promotional objects or a small external request, Community Relations is the most plausible operational home.

Burton is named as Community Relations Manager and is documented in that function before the Greece window.

### Questions

1. Who supervised Community Relations in 1999?
2. Was the department formally inside Dick Bresciani's Public Affairs operation?
3. Who opened and routed incoming letters/faxes from outside organizations?
4. Who approved in-kind baseball-equipment donations?
5. Did requests from embassies or foreign sports organizations ever reach Community Relations?
6. Does “Greece,” “Greek baseball,” “Nicholas Burns,” “Hellenic Amateur Baseball Federation,” “Chuck Samiotes,” “Bill Galatis,” “Chris Karalekas,” or “Jim Small” trigger any memory?
7. Who from the department worked on Jim Rice Field / Ramsay Park?
8. Did Burton know Chuck Samiotes or Samiotes Consultants through that project?
9. What All-Star task-force meetings did Community Relations attend?
10. What records were kept: donation logs, correspondence binders, contact sheets, calendars, event books, telephone logs?
11. Where did those records go after the 2002 ownership change?
12. Does he know who currently holds pre-2002 Community Relations records?

### Document request

Ask for any surviving:
- donation/equipment log;
- Community Relations correspondence index;
- 1999 All-Star staffing list;
- Jim Rice Field contact list;
- task-force book;
- photographs / event binders;
- old Rolodex/contact sheet with Samiotes or Greek-baseball names.

## 2. Adam Shamus — workflow witness

### Why he may be unusually useful

Shamus's biographies say he began his career in Red Sox Community Relations, spent several years at Fenway, and worked the 1999 All-Star festivities.

A staff-level witness may remember the actual routing and work better than a senior executive remembers one small request.

### Questions

1. What years exactly did Shamus work in Community Relations?
2. Who was his direct supervisor in 1999?
3. What did Community Relations do for the All-Star Game?
4. What role, if any, did it have in Jim Rice Field?
5. Who at the Red Sox worked directly with outside contractors on that project?
6. Does he remember Samiotes Consultants or Chuck Samiotes?
7. How were donation requests recorded and fulfilled?
8. Was there a standing equipment inventory for community donations?
9. Were international requests routed differently from local requests?
10. Does he remember a Greece / Olympics / Hellenic baseball request?
11. Who would be the best surviving colleague to ask if he does not?

### Why a negative answer is still useful

If Shamus can establish that Community Relations did **not** handle international baseball requests and names the department that did, that is real progress.

## 3. Larry Cancro — 1999 All-Star task-force witness

### Why he matters

Cancro was VP Sales and Marketing and is explicitly documented as a member of the Red Sox 1999 All-Star task force.

He also had a long history of using baseball programs for community access, including helping establish the Red Sox as a founding sponsor of Little League's Challenger Division.

### Questions

1. What was the structure of the 1999 All-Star task force?
2. Who sat on it besides Bresciani and Cancro?
3. Which subcommittee owned community legacy projects such as Jim Rice Field?
4. Who dealt with Samiotes Consultants?
5. Did the task force create relationships with MLB International or RBI staff that continued after All-Star week?
6. Does Cancro remember a request relating to Greek baseball / Athens 2004?
7. If a foreign ambassador asked the Red Sox for help with baseball development, where would Cancro expect the request to land?
8. Did unusual external partnerships require Harrington's approval?
9. Did he know Ron Burton Jr. / Community Relations' role in the field project?
10. Are any task-force binders, planning books or participant lists still accessible?

### Guardrail

Cancro's community-minded career and All-Star role make him a good witness. They do not make him part of Greece until he or a document says so.

## 4. Gordon Edes — archive route and later Greek-American continuity

### Why he matters

Edes has three distinct qualifications:

1. he covered the Red Sox during the original period;
2. he later succeeded Bresciani as club historian, with responsibility for the historical archive;
3. in 2018 Bill Galatis approached him about Harry Agganis / Greek Heritage Night, and Edes published his own account of the Agganis tribute.

### Questions

1. Does Edes remember contemporary 1999–2000 reporting that the Red Sox were supporting Greek baseball?
2. Did he ever report on it or hear it discussed in the press box?
3. During his historian tenure, did he encounter Burns / Greece / Hellenic federation correspondence?
4. What happened to Bresciani's working correspondence and All-Star task-force material?
5. Was it incorporated into the Red Sox historical archive, discarded, or retained by another department?
6. Who currently administers the historical archive?
7. Can the archive be searched by correspondent name or only manually?
8. When Galatis approached Edes in 2018, did Galatis mention his earlier Olympic-baseball work?
9. Did Edes already know Galatis from that earlier history?
10. Who in senior management did Edes approach about the Agganis idea, and could that person know older Greek-American outreach history?

### Highest-value ask

Rather than asking Edes to remember the answer, ask whether he can identify the **box, binder, database, staff member or former employee most likely to contain it**.

## 6. John Harrington — approval-chain witness

Ask only after operations-level witnesses if possible.

Questions:
- Would a Red Sox commitment to an overseas federation have required his approval?
- Does he remember Nicholas Burns or Embassy Athens asking for help?
- Did Peter Angelos discuss the project with him owner-to-owner/executive-to-executive?
- Who would have implemented a small non-baseball-operations support pledge?
- Did Harrington receive courtesy correspondence from ambassadors or senators such as Paul Sarbanes?

## 7. Dan Duquette — use only for the baseball-personnel lane

The Duquette interview should test whether Red Sox help was baseball-operational rather than community-relations work.

Questions:
- Did MLB International ask Boston for instructors, coaches, scouts or player-development help for Greece?
- Did any Boston minor-league coach or instructor visit Greece?
- Did Duquette know Peter Angelos's Greek roster effort?
- Was former Red Sox minor-league manager Rob Derksen's Greece role known or relevant in Boston?
- Were international-development requests handled through Baseball Operations or MLB headquarters?

## 8. How to interpret a missing file

A 2006 *Boston Magazine* account says new management in 2002 found bags of unopened mail in the old Community Relations offices.

That anecdote should **not** be used to explain the Greece record away.

It does mean an unsuccessful search in one Red Sox correspondence series cannot be treated as proof no request existed. Search redundancy is essential:

- Red Sox Public Affairs;
- Community Relations;
- executive office;
- MLB International;
- Samiotes personal/business files;
- Embassy Athens;
- Greek federation recipient files.

## 9. Interview order

The goal is to reach senior witnesses after lower-level witnesses have supplied names, dates and documents that make the questions specific.

## 10. Stop condition

This line of research is considered closed when one reliable source establishes both:

- **a named Red Sox person/department**, and
- **the content of the Greece commitment or delivery**.

Until then, manuscript language remains:

> Contemporary reports said the Boston Red Sox were helping the Greek baseball effort and had pledged support. The surviving public record does not yet identify what the club supplied.

That sentence is less satisfying than a solved chain and more credible than filling it in.
