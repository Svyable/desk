# State Department 1996 — Roger Clemens briefing and primary-source retrieval lead

## Finding

A contemporaneous Associated Press report, preserved in a December 17, 1996 newspaper scan, records State Department spokesman Nicholas Burns using the official briefing podium for an explicitly comic Red Sox aside after Roger Clemens left Boston for Toronto. The AP account says Burns called Clemens a “traitor,” immediately framed the remark as the State Department’s tongue-in-cheek position, contrasted Clemens with Ted Williams and Carl Yastrzemski as lifelong Red Sox, and identified Burns as a Boston native and diehard Red Sox fan.

Contemporaneous newspaper scan carrying the AP report:
- Hereford Brand, December 17, 1996, via Texas Tech University Southwest Collections: https://newspapers.swco.ttu.edu/bitstream/handle/20.500.12255/76208/Hereford_Brand_1996_12_17.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1

A later Washington Post item by Al Kamen, October 26/27, 1997, independently recalls the same podium episode while reporting a farewell joke arranged by the State Department press corps:
- https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/politics/1997/10/27/a-farewell-curveball-for-states-spokesman/47b5db89-bd64-4361-b19e-161126e91e86/

## Why this matters

This is stronger than a later generic statement that Burns was a Red Sox fan. It is near-contemporaneous evidence that, while serving as the Department’s official spokesman, he let baseball identity enter the public ritual of the daily briefing and did so in a form the press corps recognized and remembered.

It also supplies a concrete pre-Greece antecedent for the later pattern documented elsewhere in the manuscript: baseball functioning as portable public persona inside formal diplomatic institutions.

## Exact-quotation boundary

The AP newspaper scan is contemporaneous reporting, but it is **not the official State Department transcript**. Until the relevant December 1996 daily briefing transcript or video is recovered, exact wording should be attributed to the AP account rather than silently presented as a quotation verified against the government transcript.

The strongest next retrieval target is the State Department/USIA daily briefing for the first working day after Clemens signed with Toronto in December 1996. Search surviving 1996 daily-briefing transcript mirrors, National Archives/State Department audiovisual holdings, C-SPAN, AP/CBS/CONUS footage logs, and USIA text archives.

A CONUS/Footage.net catalog entry confirms that State Department briefing video from December 13, 1996 survives in a commercial news archive (CONUS Archive record 261702). That clip is not itself evidence of the Clemens exchange, but it demonstrates surviving audiovisual coverage from the same briefing room and week and is therefore a concrete footage-retrieval route:
- https://www.footage.net/clipdetail?key=14792623&supplier=conus

## What this does not establish

Do not use these sources to claim:
- that the Clemens aside was scripted or planned;
- Burns’s private feelings beyond the public comic performance;
- that the State Department institutionally held any position on Clemens;
- that the aside affected diplomacy, policy, press relations, or Burns’s Greece appointment;
- any private conversation with reporters beyond what a sourced account reports;
- any reaction, fandom, dialogue, memory, or view by Libby Baylies.

## Manuscript opportunity

This is potentially promotable as a short, attributed beat in the State Department-spokesman section because it shows rather than merely summarizes the coexistence of official role and local identity. For publication-quality verbatim quotation, first recover the official transcript/video or retain explicit AP attribution.
