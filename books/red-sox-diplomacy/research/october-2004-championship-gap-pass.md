# October 2004 Championship Gap Pass — what we know, what we still do not

The 2004 Red Sox championship is one of the obvious emotional hinges in *Red Sox Diplomacy*. It is also the place where the manuscript is most vulnerable to accidental invention because we now possess a vivid **2003** Burns viewing scene in Brussels but still lack an equivalent **2004** scene.

This note exists to keep those years separate.

## 1. What is directly established before the 2004 postseason

ESPN records Burns's own account of watching Game 7 of the **2003** ALCS from Brussels on Armed Forces television. He remembered the exact local time — 6:16 a.m. — when Aaron Boone's home run ended Boston's season.

Source:
https://www.espn.co.uk/espn/magazine/archives/news/story?page=magazine-20040119-article32

Thomas Friedman later records Burns telling him about an **April 2004** CENTCOM briefing in Qatar where three screens showed Predator feeds from Iraq and a fourth showed Yankees–Red Sox, with Pedro Martínez facing Derek Jeter.

Source record:
https://books.google.com/books/about/The_World_Is_Flat.html?id=7SJvQgAACAAJ

A cleaner university-hosted searchable copy of the passage has also been located in the research sweep.

These sources establish that Burns actively followed Red Sox–Yankees baseball overseas during the NATO posting. They do not establish his October viewing circumstances.

## 2. What is directly established after the championship

On March 23, 2005, Burns told a U.S. Institute of Peace audience that he was a native-born member of Red Sox Nation and that New Englanders had found **“redemption”** after Boston defeated the Yankees and then the Cardinals.

Source:
https://2001-2009.state.gov/p/us/rm/2005/45781.htm

This remains the strongest Burns-authored interpretation of the championship.

## 3. The focused October 2004 search

A targeted search was run across:

- State Department and NATO-era public remarks;
- October and November 2004 interviews;
- combinations of Burns / Red Sox / World Series / Brussels / NATO / Yankees / Cardinals;
- date-specific searches around ALCS Games 4–7 and the World Series;
- November and December 2004 retrospective references.

No reliable source located in this pass states:

- where Burns watched ALCS Games 4–7;
- whether he used Armed Forces television again;
- where he watched the World Series;
- who was with him;
- what he said when Boston clinched on October 27;
- whether U.S. Mission NATO colleagues marked the victory;
- whether Libby or the Burns daughters were with him for any decisive game.

That negative finding should be treated as meaningful editorial information, not as an invitation to interpolate from 2003.

## 4. What the public work calendar tells us — and does not tell us

The archived State Department NATO index places Burns at an informal NATO defense ministerial in Poiana Brasov, Romania on **October 13, 2004**. It does not list a Burns public appearance during the decisive October 17–27 Red Sox run. His next listed major public remarks in that archive are in Moscow on November 4 and Berlin on November 8.

Source:
https://2001-2009.state.gov/p/eur/rt/nato/c11326.htm

A Mediamax interview published October 23 shows Burns actively discussing NATO/Caucasus issues during the same week as the ALCS/World Series transition, but the article does not establish his physical location or baseball viewing.

Source:
https://mediamax.am/en/news/interviews/54/

### Editorial boundary

An absence in the public schedule is **not** evidence that Burns was in Brussels. It only means the current public archive does not locate him for the decisive games.

## 5. Armed Forces Network is plausible infrastructure, not proof

Burns used Armed Forces television for the 2003 ALCS Game 7. Contemporary histories of AFN show that the network distributed live American sports in Europe, and later reporting says 2006 was the first year in sixty years that AFN radio did not carry the World Series — implying the Fall Classic remained part of the overseas military broadcast ecosystem in 2004.

Relevant background:
- https://www.stripes.com/news/2006-10-19/no-world-series-on-afn-radio-this-year-1974176.html1
- https://www.stripes.com/news/2004-09-03/weather-takes-its-toll-on-afn-signal-in-europe-middle-east-1959978.html1

The especially curious historical coincidence is that AFN-Europe was itself moving its headquarters from Frankfurt to Mannheim at the end of October 2004; Stars and Stripes reported the Frankfurt studio's final broadcast on October 28, the day after the Red Sox clinched.

Source:
https://www.stripes.com/news/2004-10-29/silence-in-frankfurt-afn-fades-to-black-moves-to-mannheim-1914651.html1

This is atmosphere and broadcast context only. Do **not** write that Burns watched the 2004 clincher on AFN without direct evidence.

## 6. Why the book does not need to fake the missing scene

The surrounding record is already unusually strong:

- 2003: Burns's own Brussels / Armed Forces TV / 6:16 a.m. memory.
- April 2004: Friedman account of Burns watching Yankees–Red Sox during a CENTCOM briefing.
- October 2004: the documented baseball sequence — 0–3 deficit, Roberts steal, Ortiz, Schilling, Game 7, Cardinals sweep.
- March 2005: Burns's own word, **redemption**.
- 2007: a fully documented before-and-after championship record, including Burns's detailed preseason/postseason analysis and his morning-after Red Sox Nation audience moment.

That allows the manuscript to make 2004 emotionally legible without manufacturing a couch, television, room, companion, phone call, or reaction.

## 7. Highest-value sources that could actually close the gap

1. **Burns interview** — ask where he watched ALCS Games 4–7 and World Series Game 4 in 2004.
2. **U.S. Mission NATO calendars / ambassador schedule** — October 17–28, 2004.
3. **Mission newsletters / internal emails / public-affairs photographs** — celebration or staff references.
4. **Burns personal correspondence or papers** — family/friend reaction, travel details.
5. **AFN Europe schedules** — confirm which games/feeds were carried and at what European times.
6. **Colleague oral histories** — NATO mission staff who were present during the 2004 postseason.
7. **Boston-area press** — any contemporaneous interview with Burns immediately after the title.

## 8. Current manuscript rule

Until one of those sources appears, use one of these structures:

- **Documented juxtaposition:** Burns was serving as U.S. ambassador to NATO when the Red Sox completed the unprecedented comeback and won the World Series.
- **Retrospective first person:** months later, Burns called it redemption.
- **Deliberate omission:** acknowledge that the record currently tells us exactly where he was at 6:16 a.m. for the 2003 heartbreak but not where he was for the 2004 release.

That last contrast may actually be better nonfiction than a reconstructed scene. The archive remembers the loss more precisely than the victory.
