# Greece baseball — Orioles and MLB institutional assistance

## Finding

The unresolved Greece question benefits from separating **Boston Red Sox identity** from **which baseball institutions actually supplied documented assistance**.

A contemporaneous *Christian Science Monitor* report from August 18, 2004 says Hellenic baseball organizers asked U.S. Ambassador Nicholas Burns for help. Burns, describing himself as a confirmed and unconditional Red Sox fan, brought Baltimore Orioles owner Peter Angelos and Major League Baseball's International Division into the effort. Burns retrospectively called introducing baseball to Greece a joint American-Greek venture.

Source:
- Christian Science Monitor, "Greek baseball team, made in the USA," August 18, 2004: https://www.csmonitor.com/2004/0818/p02s01-woeu.html

A contemporaneous *Washington Post* report from April 12, 2004 independently says Burns contacted Angelos and that, with MLB's blessing, Orioles scout Rob Derksen and Orioles officials conducted a broad recruiting effort for players with Greek ancestry.

Source:
- Washington Post, "It Appears That Baseball Isn't All Greek to the Greeks," April 12, 2004: https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/sports/2004/04/12/it-appears-that-baseball-isnt-all-greek-to-the-greeks/0d5fc3ca-536e-4df4-8712-c260367f6e51/

An Athens News article preserved by Hellenic Communication Service adds unusually specific institutional detail. It reports that the Orioles had worked with the Hellenic Amateur Baseball Federation since 1999; Angelos assigned Orioles scout Rob Derksen to the project; and MLB International was expected to contribute about $500,000 to the federation by the 2004 Games for a wider program that included field construction, coaching and team development.

Source:
- Hellenic Communication Service preservation of Robert Bruce, Athens News, "Greek-American Backs National Baseball Team": https://www.helleniccomserve.com/baseball.html

The current Greek baseball history site independently preserves a photograph captioned as a 2003 Camden Yards meeting among the Hellenic Baseball Federation, Major League Baseball and the Baltimore Orioles, creating a concrete visual/provenance lead for institutional collaboration.

Source:
- Greek Baseball, "History": https://greekbaseball.com/history

## Why this materially strengthens the manuscript

The evidence clarifies the institutional chain. Burns's **Red Sox fandom** is part of why contemporary accounts found his involvement narratively striking, but the documented baseball organizations doing the operational work in these sources are the **Baltimore Orioles** and **Major League Baseball / MLB International**.

That distinction matters. The book can now tell a stronger story precisely because it does not force the Boston Red Sox club into a role the available evidence does not establish. A Red Sox fan serving as ambassador used relationships across organized baseball; the institutional help that can presently be named and quantified came from elsewhere in MLB.

The sources also add concrete operational texture: a Greek federation asking for help; Angelos accepting the challenge; an Orioles scout assigned to recruit eligible players; MLB International supporting development; and a documented 2003 Camden Yards meeting among the federation, MLB and Orioles.

## Boston Red Sox club boundary

These sources do **not** establish that the Boston Red Sox organization supplied equipment, money, coaches, clinics, scouting, travel, staff time, sponsorship, or another deliverable to the Greek effort.

Until Red Sox archival records, contemporaneous correspondence, a named participant interview, or another reliable source establishes such assistance, manuscript prose should distinguish:

1. Burns's documented Red Sox identity;
2. his diplomatic role in connecting Greek baseball organizers to American baseball networks;
3. documented Orioles and MLB institutional assistance; and
4. the still-unresolved question of any Boston Red Sox club contribution.

Do not infer Red Sox institutional participation merely because Burns was a lifelong fan.

## Visual lead

The Greek Baseball history page's 2003 Camden Yards image is a useful discovery lead, not yet publication-cleared art. Before manuscript image use, recover the original photograph if possible and establish photographer, date, depicted participants, ownership and reproduction rights.

## Attribution boundary

The $500,000 figure is reported by the preserved Athens News article and should remain attributed unless corroborated by MLB/HABF financial records. The Greek Baseball history page is a federation/history source and its captions should be treated as institutional retrospective evidence rather than automatically as contemporaneous metadata.

No dialogue, private reaction, motive, or interiority is supplied by these records. None of these sources supports any claim about Libby Baylies.
