# Wellesley Free Library local biographical anchor

## Finding

The Wellesley Free Library maintains a local-author page for Nicholas (“Nick”) Burns as a Wellesley High School 1974 graduate. The page is useful because it is a Wellesley institutional source rather than a later national biography.

It states that Burns grew up on **Shirley Road**, graduated from **Wellesley High School in 1974**, and retained a “lifelong pride” in membership in **Red Sox Nation**. It also says that while serving as State Department spokesman he often opened briefings with updates on Red Sox scores. The latter point is independently supported elsewhere in the project by State Department briefing transcripts, so this page should be treated as local corroboration rather than the sole source for that practice.

Source: https://www.wellesleyfreelibrary.org/authors/nick-burns/

## What this adds

The strongest contribution is geographic and social specificity for the early-life chapter. “Wellesley” no longer needs to remain an undifferentiated suburb in the biographical setup: the library gives a street-level childhood anchor, Shirley Road, and directly connects the adult diplomat’s Red Sox identity to his Wellesley biography.

This does **not** authenticate the separate Legacy guestbook entries about Little League or St. James softball. The library page contains no youth-baseball roster, team, league, coach, season, position, field, or game memory.

## Safe manuscript use

Safe:

- Burns grew up on Shirley Road in Wellesley.
- He graduated from Wellesley High School in 1974.
- A Wellesley Free Library local-biographical page describes his Red Sox allegiance as lifelong.
- The local institution remembers the Red Sox identity as part of the public biography he carried out of town and into diplomacy.

Not safe without additional evidence:

- placing a baseball game on Shirley Road;
- saying he listened to or watched particular Red Sox games there;
- treating the library’s “lifelong” language as proof of a specific childhood memory;
- using this page to authenticate the 2007 Little League condolence or 2015 St. James softball condolence;
- assigning any baseball memory or attitude to Libby Baylies.

## Research consequence

The Shirley Road detail creates a narrower local-archive search target. Highest-value follow-up records are Wellesley Townsman sports pages, town recreation/Little League rosters, parish bulletins or St. James team records, and contemporaneous Wellesley High School material. Searches should prioritize Burns family names plus Shirley Road, rather than only “Nicholas Burns baseball.”

## Evidentiary level

**Institutional local retrospective / corroborative.** Strong for hometown biographical facts and for how a local institution characterizes Burns’s enduring Red Sox identity; insufficient for a specific youth-baseball scene.
