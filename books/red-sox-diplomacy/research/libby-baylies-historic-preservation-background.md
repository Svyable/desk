# Libby Baylies — historic-preservation background before the senior diplomatic years

## Why this deserves its own note

A search for Elizabeth “Libby” Baylies's independent professional background produced a significant new result: before Nicholas Burns's senior diplomatic assignments, Baylies worked as a historian on federal historic-preservation documentation projects.

This is useful for *Red Sox Diplomacy* because the Athens chapter already turns on architecture, historical presentation, the embassy's physical appearance, and the George C. Marshall statue/history project. The new evidence gives Libby an independent background in precisely the kind of work that studies how buildings carry institutional history.

The evidence **does not** establish that she designed, directed, or caused the Athens embassy renovation. Do not write that inference as fact. Its value is biographical context and a new archival route.

## 1. West Point, summer 1981

A Historic American Buildings Survey report for the United States Military Academy at West Point credits **Libby Baylies Burns and Katherin Grandine** with rapidly assembling a structures inventory of the entire Academy during the closing weeks of summer 1981.

Source:
https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/pnp/habshaer/ny/ny1300/ny1393/data/ny1393data.pdf

### What it establishes

- Baylies Burns was doing professional historic-structures inventory work by 1981.
- The work was connected to HABS, the federal historic-documentation program associated with the National Park Service and Library of Congress.
- The task involved institutional architecture rather than generalized historical research alone.

### What it does not establish

- her exact job title or employer beyond the project credit;
- her full education/training in architecture or preservation;
- any direct link between the West Point work and later diplomatic-residence or embassy projects.

## 2. Watertown Arsenal, 1982 — historian, fieldwork, preservation recommendations

The Library of Congress catalog for **Watertown Arsenal, Watertown, Middlesex County, Massachusetts** credits **Baylies, Libby — historian** among the project contributors.

Source:
https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/ma1291/

The underlying historical report identifies **Libby Baylies Burns and Betsy Bahr** as the historians who conducted the field work and prepared the report. The project scope included:

- documentary research on the history of the installation and its properties;
- a field inventory of Army-owned properties;
- an architectural, historical, and technological overview;
- evaluation of historic properties;
- recommendations for preservation.

A readable derived presentation of the filed federal report preserves this authorship and scope:
https://blockbefore.com/structure-ma1291.html

Later Library of Congress HABS/HAER documentation cites a 1982 **Libby Baylies** draft National Register nomination for the Watertown Arsenal gun-carriage manufacturing complex and a 1982 **Libby Baylies Burns and Betsey Bahr** Historic American Engineering Record study of the Army Materials and Mechanics Research Center.

Source:
https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/pnp/habshaer/ma/ma1600/ma1639/data/ma1639data.pdf

### Independent federal afterlife

The work was substantive enough to remain part of the federal technical-historical literature. A National Park Service history of the Charlestown Navy Yard cites **Libby Baylies Burns and Betsy Behr/Bahr** on Watertown Arsenal architecture. NASA's institutional history *The First A: The Other NASA* cites **Libby Baylies Burns and Betsy Bahr, Watertown Arsenal**, for the Army's early laboratory work.

Sources:
- https://npshistory.com/publications/bost/hrs-charlestown-navy-yard-1.pdf
- https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2015/04/NASAsFirstA-508-ebook.pdf

### Why this matters

This establishes a real professional identity separate from Burns's diplomatic career: historical fieldwork, architecture, institutional history, preservation evaluation, and federal documentation.

That makes later evidence of Baylies participating in Athens cultural/physical-representation projects less anomalous. It does **not** prove she was responsible for the Athens renovation. The strongest responsible sentence is simply that the ambassador's spouse arrived in that life with her own prior experience documenting and evaluating historic institutional buildings.

## 3. The naming issue is unusually helpful here

The archival trail appears under several forms:

- **Libby Baylies**;
- **Libby Baylies Burns**;
- Library of Congress contributor credit: **Baylies, Libby**.

The Watertown bibliography itself helps connect those forms because it lists both the Baylies draft nomination and the Baylies Burns / Bahr survey in the same 1982 body of work.

This is stronger identity evidence than merely finding a person with the same first and last name in an unrelated source.

## 4. Possible Chapter 9 use

The Athens chapter currently describes the chancery's marble cleaning, landscaping, fountains, history exhibit, and Marshall statue as representational choices. It now also documents Libby's co-hosting and MDA Hellas work.

A short paragraph can add this prehistory:

- years before Athens, Libby had worked as a historian on HABS/HAER projects at West Point and Watertown Arsenal;
- the Watertown work included field inventory and preservation recommendations;
- nothing in the current archive proves she directed the Athens physical-renewal project;
- the background nevertheless establishes that historic buildings, institutional memory, and preservation were subjects she had worked on professionally before the ambassadorship.

That is enough. Do not turn coincidence of subject matter into undocumented causation.

## 5. Highest-value next archival targets

1. **HABS/HAER project administrative files, 1981–1982** — contracts, résumés, correspondence, field notes, and assignment records for Baylies Burns.
2. **Watertown Arsenal draft National Register nomination** — recover the full 1982 typescript attributed to Libby Baylies.
3. **West Point HABS project files** — determine her formal role and whether she authored specific inventory entries.
4. **Connecticut College alumni/career files** — possible education and early-professional biography.
5. **Direct Libby interview** — ask about preservation work before the Foreign Service years and whether it shaped how she saw embassy/residence spaces later. Do not presuppose that it did.

## Editorial boundary

The discovery supports **background**, not motive.

Safe:

> Before the Athens posting, Libby Baylies Burns had worked as a historian on federal historic-preservation surveys at West Point and Watertown Arsenal.

Safe:

> Her 1982 Watertown work included field inventory and recommendations for preserving historic properties.

Not yet supported:

> Libby's preservation background drove the Athens embassy renovation.

That last claim requires testimony, correspondence, project records, or another source connecting the two.
