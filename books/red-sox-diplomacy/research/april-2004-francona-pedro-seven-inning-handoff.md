# April 25, 2004 — seven innings, then the bullpen

## Finding

Six months after the most argued-over pitching decision in modern Red Sox history, Boston returned to Yankee Stadium with Pedro Martínez on the mound and a new manager making the handoff.

SABR's reconstruction of April 25, 2004 records Martínez completing seven scoreless innings with Boston leading 2–0. The account, drawing on contemporaneous Boston Globe coverage, says Martínez returned to the dugout after the seventh with the expectation that his night was over. Terry Francona then went to Scott Williamson for the eighth. Williamson retired Travis Lee, Derek Jeter, and Bernie Williams in order; Keith Foulke handled the ninth, and Boston completed a three-game sweep of New York.

Authoritative historical synthesis with contemporaneous-source provenance:
- Society for American Baseball Research, “April 25, 2004: Pedro Martínez, Scott Williamson blank Yankees as Red Sox complete sweep at Yankee Stadium”: https://sabr.org/gamesproj/game/april-25-2004-pedro-martinez-scott-williamson-blank-yankees-as-red-sox-complete-sweep-at-yankee-stadium/

The historical context is unusually sharp. In 2003 ALCS Game 7, Martínez had also completed seven innings against New York with Boston ahead. Grady Little sent him back for the eighth; the Yankees tied the game, Aaron Boone later homered, and Little was replaced after the season. SABR's Terry Francona biography records that Francona's hiring followed that collapse and that his interview with Theo Epstein and Josh Byrnes lasted six hours.

Supporting managerial history:
- Society for American Baseball Research, “Terry Francona”: https://sabr.org/bioproj/person/terry-francona/

## Why this is narratively valuable

The value is not that Francona “learned the lesson” of 2003; that would assign a motive the sources do not establish. The value is the observable institutional contrast.

Same pitcher. Same opponent. Same ballpark. Again Martínez had finished seven innings with Boston ahead. This time the new manager made the bullpen handoff before the eighth began, and two relievers finished the shutout.

For a knowledgeable Red Sox reader, April 25 is useful precisely because it is not October mythology. It shows a concrete managerial operating decision early in Francona's first season, only months after the franchise's defining failure to make that same transition cleanly. The sequence also gives Williamson and Foulke their proper place rather than reducing the result to a manager-versus-starter morality play.

## Documentary boundary

Safe:

- Martínez completed seven scoreless innings at Yankee Stadium on April 25, 2004 with Boston leading 2–0;
- SABR, citing contemporaneous coverage, records that Martínez returned to the dugout expecting his night was finished;
- Francona used Scott Williamson in the eighth and Keith Foulke in the ninth;
- Williamson retired the side in order and Boston completed the shutout and series sweep;
- the preceding October, Martínez had also completed seven innings of ALCS Game 7 with Boston ahead before Grady Little left him in for the eighth;
- Francona replaced Little after the 2003 season.

Not safe:

- claiming Francona made the April move specifically to repudiate Little unless Francona or another participant says so;
- inventing a dugout conversation between Francona and Martínez;
- assigning Martínez relief, anger, approval, or any other interior state;
- saying the April decision proved how Francona would manage the 2004 postseason;
- claiming the handoff caused the championship;
- turning the sequence into a generic leadership or diplomacy lesson.

## Manuscript recommendation

Use if the manuscript needs one manager-specific Red Sox beat outside the already crowded 2004 ALCS comeback. Keep the parallel factual rather than interpretive: six months after Game 7, Martínez again finished seven innings at Yankee Stadium with Boston ahead. Under Francona, the eighth belonged to Williamson and the ninth to Foulke.

The best ending is the baseball result, not an abstraction: three outs from Williamson, three from Foulke, a 2–0 win, and a sweep.

## Evidentiary level

**SABR historical reconstruction grounded in contemporaneous Boston Globe reporting + SABR managerial biography.** Strong for the pitching sequence, game result, and managerial chronology. The contrast with 2003 is observable; motive, private dialogue, and any claim that Francona consciously staged the move as a corrective remain unsupported.
