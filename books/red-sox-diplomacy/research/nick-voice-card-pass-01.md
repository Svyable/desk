# Nicholas Burns Voice Cards — Pass 01

## Purpose

Give *Red Sox Diplomacy* more of Nicholas Burns's actual voice without turning the manuscript into a quotation scrapbook.

The recurring device is a compact, portable Markdown pull-quote that can later receive stronger typographic treatment in print and ebook layouts while remaining legible in the source manuscript:

```md
> **NICK / THEME**
>
> ### “Short exact quotation.”
>
> *Source or event · date · [Reader-facing source](https://example.com)*
```

The design should feel like a book object, not a web callout. The label is deliberately terse. `NICK` tells the reader whose voice has entered the page; the second term tells the reader what register is being revealed.

## Editorial rules

1. **The words must be Burns's.** Do not promote a paraphrase, institutional description, reporter summary, or reconstructed sentiment into a NICK card.
2. **Keep it short.** The card should stop the eye for a sentence, not create a second page of transcript. Longer passages belong in prose or source notes.
3. **Source it where it appears.** Date, venue, and a usable reader-facing link sit directly beneath the quotation.
4. **Prefer primary records.** State Department transcripts, Burns-authored speeches, and complete event transcripts are preferred. A credible secondary source can carry a short quotation when the primary record is unavailable and the attribution is clear.
5. **Use one strong card per chapter by default.** Repetition creates a visual language only if the device remains scarce.
6. **Vary the register.** The system should reveal the whole person: inherited fandom, briefing-room humor, correction, diplomacy doctrine, public-diplomacy practice, strategic language, and the formal burden of representing the United States.
7. **Do not manufacture a motif.** If a chapter does not contain a Burns line worth interrupting the prose for, it does not need a card.
8. **Keep the quote doing narrative work.** A card should sharpen the paragraph around it: establish character, change register, crystallize doctrine, or create a hinge between eras.
9. **Do not turn humor into policy.** Baseball quips remain personal/social language unless the surrounding record independently supports a policy claim.
10. **Do not turn doctrine into endorsement.** Burns's strategic or political judgments are presented as his documented views in the relevant period, not as the book's verdict.

## Pass 01 placements

### Chapter 2 — inheritance

**NICK / INHERITANCE**

> “He advised us never to root for them. But I didn't follow his advice.”

Source: U.S. Department of State briefing, June 16, 1997.
https://1997-2001.state.gov/www/briefings/9706/970616db.html

Function: makes the Red Sox allegiance inherited and irrational before the book asks it to carry any diplomatic significance.

### Chapter 7 — correction

**NICK / CORRECTION**

> “I stand corrected. Sorry.”

Source: State Department briefing, February 27, 1997.
https://www.globalsecurity.org/wmd/library/news/dprk/1997/970227a.htm

Function: four words show the craft of correcting a government podium without theatrical self-defense.

The chapter also weaves Burns's May 1997 line, “our first line of defense is our diplomacy,” into the surrounding prose rather than turning every quotable sentence into a card.

Primary source:
https://1997-2001.state.gov/policy_remarks/970509.burns.html

### Chapter 8 — the rules

**NICK / THE RULES**

> “We do like the Boston Red Sox. We don't mind the Tigers. We hate the Yankees. Those are the rules.”

Source: State Department briefing, March 7, 1997.
https://1997-2001.state.gov/briefings/9703/970307.html

Function: establishes the harmless rule system that lets the chapter later distinguish sports rivalry from institutional speech.

The July 8 mock-expulsion of a Yankees-supporting intern—“You can use that door or that door”—stays in prose as a secondary beat rather than competing visually with the main card.

### Chapter 10 — Hellas

**NICK / HELLAS**

> “It was a joint venture between America and Greece, and it was one of the best things I ever did as ambassador.”

Source: *Christian Science Monitor*, 2004 retrospective reporting.
https://www.csmonitor.com/2004/0818/p02s01-woeu.html

Function: the word *joint* distributes agency at the exact point where the chapter resists a Burns-centered hero story.

Evidence label: credible secondary quotation, not a surviving embassy transcript.

### Chapter 16 — redemption

**NICK / REDEMPTION**

> “I'm a native-born member of Red Sox Nation ... we did indeed find redemption.”

Source: U.S. Institute of Peace remarks, March 23, 2005.
https://2001-2009.state.gov/p/us/rm/2005/45781.htm

Function: supplies Burns's own post-championship language without inventing the undocumented room in which he experienced the 2004 clincher.

### Chapter 17 — the craft

**NICK / THE CRAFT**

> “a venerable and antique art, little understood and sometimes maligned, but of great value to all of us—Diplomacy.”

Source: Chautauqua Institution / Belfer Center prepared remarks, August 5, 2013.
https://www.belfercenter.org/publication/return-american-diplomacy

Function: gives the professor years a sentence of doctrine in Burns's own voice before the chapter expands diplomacy beyond formal state authority through Harvard, Special Olympics, and Aspen.

### Chapter 18 — ballast

**NICK / BALLAST**

> “you need ballast in the relationship and you need the societies to remain connected”

Source: CSIS *Pekingology* interview, July 25, 2024.
https://www.csis.org/analysis/ambassador-nicholas-burns-us-china-relationship

Function: crystallizes the people-to-people argument immediately before the chapter enters the dispute over whether those channels were being obstructed.

### Chapter 19 — the evil empire

**NICK / THE EVIL EMPIRE**

> “We are opponents of the evil empire, the New York Yankees.”

Source: Marshall Plan commemoration, Paris, June 12, 2007.
https://2001-2009.state.gov/p/us/rm/2007/87176.htm

Function: provides the deliberately unserious language that the chapter then contrasts with the consequential vocabulary of ally, competitor, adversary, and enemy.

### Chapter 20 — the ambassador's job

**NICK / THE AMBASSADOR'S JOB**

> “represent the perspectives and policies of one’s government ... and to be the primary point of communication between them.”

Source: Fairbank Center / Belfer Center lecture text, April 30, 2025.
https://www.belfercenter.org/research-analysis/lessons-front-lines-us-china-relationship

Function: puts delegated authority in Burns's own words at the opening of the chapter about authentication, human channels, and AI-era abundance of language.

## Deliberately not used as cards in this pass

Several good Burns lines remain available but should not all be promoted into visual objects:

- “We have to be rabid. We have to be foolhardy to be Red Sox fans.” — June 16, 1997 State briefing.
- “a confirmed, unconditional, rabid fan of the Boston Red Sox” — 2004 Greek-baseball retrospective.
- “we identify with suffering” — 2012 Atlantic Council appearance.
- “Ultimately, we must compete with the government in China while living in peace with the Chinese people.” — April 30, 2025 Fairbank/Belfer lecture.

These can remain prose texture, chapter-end echoes, or candidates for a later substitution. The visual system improves when selection is harder than collection.

## Layout note for publication

The Markdown is intentionally semantic and simple. A print/ebook renderer can later style these cards with a distinctive typographic treatment—small-caps kicker, oversized quotation, generous white space, hairline rule or margin placement—without changing manuscript content or creating an HTML-only dependency.

The source line should remain visible in reader-facing editions. The cool part is the typography. The trust comes from showing where the words came from.
