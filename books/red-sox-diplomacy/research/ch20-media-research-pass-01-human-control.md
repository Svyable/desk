# Chapter 20 media/research pass 01 — human control, authorization, and nuclear command

## Scope

This pass is limited to Chapter 20, **The Human Channel**. It strengthens the existing `HUMAN CONTROL 01` route with an independent U.S. federal source that makes the chapter's distinction between machine assistance and accountable human authority more concrete. It does not add another decorative China photograph or broaden the chapter into a survey of military AI.

## New primary-source route: STRATCOM on NC3, authentic orders, and human control

On November 20, 2024, Gen. Anthony Cotton, commander of U.S. Strategic Command, spoke at the Center for Strategic and International Studies about modernization of U.S. nuclear command, control, and communications (NC3). The Department of Defense account records two unusually useful points for Chapter 20:

- NC3 exists in part to connect the president to the nuclear force and validate orders as authentic.
- Cotton said advanced technologies including artificial intelligence can help process large quantities of information, while human oversight remains required; the DOD account quotes him saying, **“AI supports decision-making, but humans will always stay in control.”**

This is not evidence about Nicholas Burns's personal theory of diplomacy. It is a contemporaneous federal statement from the U.S. nuclear command system that independently supports the chapter's narrower institutional distinction: information processing and decision authority are different functions.

## Editorial integration

### Best use: strengthen `HUMAN CONTROL 01`, not add a third object

Keep Chapter 20 visually spare. The new STRATCOM source should sit in the provenance behind the existing original `HUMAN CONTROL 01` graphic. It adds a second U.S. institutional layer to the November 16, 2024 Biden–Xi principle already documented in `ch20-visual-archive.md`:

1. **Presidential diplomatic principle, Lima, November 16:** both sides record agreement that decisions over nuclear-weapons use should remain under human control.
2. **Operational U.S. command context, November 20:** the STRATCOM commander describes AI as decision support while keeping humans in control, and describes NC3 as validating orders as authentic.

The four-day proximity is useful but should not be turned into a causal claim. There is no evidence in these sources that Cotton's remarks were prompted by the Lima statement.

### Suggested figure-source note

> **Source note:** Original graphic. The November 2024 U.S.–China presidential record states a shared principle that decisions over nuclear-weapons use should remain under human control. Four days later, U.S. Strategic Command commander Gen. Anthony Cotton publicly described AI as support for nuclear decision-making while keeping humans in control, and described NC3 as validating orders as authentic. These statements establish principles and institutional functions, not a binding bilateral control regime or a complete technical architecture.

### Caption refinement

If the current caption is more categorical, prefer:

> **HUMAN CONTROL 01.** In Lima on November 16, 2024, Biden and Xi recorded a shared principle that nuclear-weapons-use decisions should remain under human control. Four days later, the commander of U.S. Strategic Command described AI as decision support inside an NC3 system built to connect presidential authority to the force and authenticate orders. Principle is not implementation.

### Alt text

> Two dated documentary points in November 2024. On November 16, the U.S. and Chinese presidents state that decisions over nuclear-weapons use should remain under human control. On November 20, the U.S. Strategic Command commander describes artificial intelligence as supporting nuclear decision-making while humans remain in control, and describes nuclear command-and-control systems as authenticating orders. A final line distinguishes stated principle from implementation architecture.

## Formatting / production guidance

- Keep `HUMAN CONTROL 01` as a compact two-date chronology rather than a flowchart. A flowchart would falsely imply a settled chain from diplomatic principle to operational implementation.
- Use dates as the primary hierarchy; keep institutional labels secondary.
- Do not use presidential portraits, mushroom-cloud imagery, missiles, command-center stock photography, AI-brain iconography, flags, or DOD seals.
- Print: keep the object on one page with caption and source note; do not orphan the November 20 entry onto the next page.
- EPUB: reading order should be title → November 16 point → November 20 point → limit statement → caption/source note.
- Grayscale: distinguish the two records through typography and spacing rather than color alone.
- Links belong in notes/provenance, not as raw URLs inside the reader-facing figure.
- If a future production pass uses a DOD photograph from the Cotton event, treat it as an optional replacement for the diagram, not an additional image. Verify the individual asset's creator, VIRIN/identifier, date shot, download master, item-level rights statement, and any visible protected marks first.

## Evidence boundaries

Documented fact:

- Biden and Xi's November 16, 2024 Lima records support the shared human-control principle already logged in the chapter visual archive.
- The November 20 DOD account attributes the NC3/authenticity and AI/human-control statements to Gen. Anthony Cotton.
- DOD describes its imagery as generally public domain unless otherwise noted, with separate limitations governing marks, depicted people, endorsement, and commercial use.

Interpretation suitable for the book:

- These records sharpen the distinction between abundant machine processing and scarce accountable authority.
- “Authentic” and “authorized” should not be collapsed. NC3 authentication is relevant evidence for the chapter, but the chapter's broader phrase `authorized intent` remains Sven's synthesis, not a formal STRATCOM doctrine.

Do **not** claim:

- that the Lima principle created an arms-control agreement;
- that China and the United States share the same nuclear command architecture;
- that Cotton's remarks implemented or responded to the Biden–Xi statement;
- that AI is absent from nuclear-related analysis, warning, planning, or support systems;
- that a technical authentication mechanism by itself establishes political meaning or lawful authorization;
- that Nicholas Burns authored either government's human-control language.
