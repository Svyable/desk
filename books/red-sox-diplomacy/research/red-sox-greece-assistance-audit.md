# Red Sox–Greece Assistance Audit — what the club did, what MLB did, and what Boston fans did

The phrase “the Red Sox helped Greek baseball” appears in contemporaneous reporting, but the surviving public record does not yet specify what the Boston club itself delivered. This audit separates four overlapping assistance channels so the manuscript does not accidentally credit one actor with another actor's work.

## 1. Boston Red Sox organization — participation is documented; mechanics are not

A September 1999 *Washington Post* sports item reports that Orioles owner Peter Angelos met Greek officials at a reception held by U.S. Ambassador Nicholas Burns and the Hellenic Amateur Baseball Federation. It says Angelos was being helped by the **commissioner's office and the Boston Red Sox — Burns's favorite team**.

Source:
https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/sports/1999/09/08/injuries-to-teens-are-still-a-concern/fe2eb9f0-7fc2-4b43-80a4-a9cb6ac94670/

A January 2000 Associated Press report is similarly explicit but still nonspecific: after describing MLB International's planned assistance, it says **support had also been pledged by the Boston Red Sox**.

Source:
https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-2000-jan-02-mn-49873-story.html

### What these sources establish

- the Red Sox organization was part of the assistance network by 1999–2000;
- the connection was known contemporaneously, not invented in later Burns biographies;
- reporters explicitly linked the Red Sox involvement to Burns's favorite team.

### What these sources do not establish

They do not identify:

- which Red Sox executive or department authorized the support;
- whether support meant equipment, coaching, money, scouting, introductions, facilities, publicity, or some combination;
- whether the support was delivered or merely pledged;
- whether the club worked through MLB International, Friends of Greek Baseball, the embassy, or directly with the federation;
- the value, timing, or duration of any Red Sox contribution.

Until an archival record closes that gap, the manuscript should say **the Red Sox pledged or provided support according to contemporaneous reporting**, not invent the form of that support.

## 2. Major League Baseball International — concrete development assistance is documented

The AP report quotes Jim Small, vice president of international market development, saying Major League Baseball planned to send a full-time coach and administrator to Greece and college instructors to teach the sport.

A detailed 2007 history of Greek baseball by Tom Mazarakis provides a more granular retrospective account. It says MLB International's first assistance included **a full container of equipment shipped to Athens in summer 1999**, distributed by the Hellenic federation to newly formed teams. It then says MLB International sent former Pomona College coach **Mike Riskas** in 2001 as a **Coach in Residence in Greece** to promote youth and grassroots programs, run clinics, and train players and coaches around the country.

Sources:
- Associated Press / *Los Angeles Times*, January 2, 2000:
  https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-2000-jan-02-mn-49873-story.html
- Tom Mazarakis, “The History of Baseball in Greece,” January 2007:
  https://www.oocities.org/spartakosglyfadas/history_of_baseball_in_greece2.htm

### Evidentiary status

The AP source is contemporaneous and establishes planned MLB development assistance. The Mazarakis history is retrospective organizational/community history and supplies the equipment-container and Riskas details. Those details should be treated as strong leads unless independently corroborated by MLB records.

### Manuscript consequence

Do not write that the Red Sox supplied the container or the resident coach. The surviving source attributes those acts to **MLB International**.

## 3. Peter Angelos / Baltimore Orioles — elite-player recruitment and Olympic team construction

Multiple sources place Orioles owner Peter Angelos at the center of the effort to build a team capable of competing internationally.

The 2007 Mazarakis history says Burns contacted Major League Baseball and a network of Greek Americans, with Angelos the most consequential connection. It says Angelos made the project an important Orioles undertaking and assigned scout **Robert Derksen** to search the United States and Canada for high-level players of Greek descent.

The Greek federation's current historical timeline likewise credits Angelos with major personal financial support and shows a 2003 meeting involving the federation, Major League Baseball, and the Orioles.

Sources:
- https://www.oocities.org/spartakosglyfadas/history_of_baseball_in_greece2.htm
- https://greekbaseball.com/history

### Distinction from grassroots development

Angelos/Orioles activity should be kept analytically separate from MLB International's grassroots development work. The Orioles strand was heavily concerned with identifying and assembling an Olympic-caliber diaspora roster; MLB International also worked on local development, equipment, coaching, and clinics.

## 4. The Boston Greek-American / Red Sox-fan layer — independent grassroots work

The assistance network also contained a distinctly Boston-area, Greek-American baseball strand that should not be confused with the Boston Red Sox organization.

A 2007 Greek baseball history says Burns contacted several Greek-American baseball enthusiasts in addition to Angelos, including **Chuck Samiotis and Bill Galatis**.

Source:
https://www.oocities.org/spartakosglyfadas/history_of_baseball_in_greece2.htm

A 2004 CNN report describes Galatis and Samiotis as **two Boston baseball fans** and explicitly calls them **diehard Red Sox fans**. CNN says they had spent roughly six years helping develop baseball in Greece: building a field, training young people, and helping recruit top players. The report also identifies them as Boston-area restaurant partners who originally responded to a Greek-American newspaper advertisement seeking players.

Source:
https://transcripts.cnn.com/show/cst/date/2004-08-07/segment/04

This distinction matters enormously for *Red Sox Diplomacy*. There were at least two different “Red Sox” presences in the Greece story:

1. **the Boston Red Sox club**, which contemporaneous reporting says pledged/helped with support whose exact form remains unresolved;
2. **Boston-area Greek-American Red Sox fans**, who independently performed documented grassroots baseball work.

Conflating those two would make the story cleaner and less accurate.

## 5. Chris Karalekas / Baseball Acropolis — another grassroots and visual archive

Baseball Acropolis founder Chris Karalekas describes beginning his own effort in 1998 to help introduce baseball to Greece. Its surviving photo archive shows:

- Karalekas coaching children in Greece;
- equipment deliveries;
- meetings with MLB International staff;
- meetings with Greek federation leaders and Orioles personnel;
- a photograph captioned **“U.S. Ambassador to Greece R. Nicholas Burns with Chris and Gus Karalekas”**;
- a group photograph whose caption identifies members of the Hellenic federation alongside MLB, the Orioles contingent, **Boston businessmen**, and Karalekas.

Sources:
- Baseball Acropolis “About Us”:
  https://www.baseballacropolis.org/About_us.htm
- Baseball Acropolis photo archive:
  https://www.baseballacropolis.org/photos.htm

### Value of the photo archive

This is the strongest public visual lead yet found for reconstructing the human network around Burns's baseball project. The photos are organizational archive material rather than neutral press photography, so captions should be independently checked where possible before identifying every participant in manuscript prose.

## 6. A more precise assistance map

The currently documented network is best represented as follows:

### Burns / U.S. Embassy

- public visibility and diplomatic sponsorship;
- introductions / network brokerage;
- Burns's own baseball demonstrations and youth engagement;
- lobbying Greek authorities for a baseball budget;
- connection to Greek-American and professional-baseball networks.

### MLB International

- international-development expertise;
- planned full-time coaching/administrative support;
- retrospective evidence of a 1999 equipment shipment;
- retrospective evidence of Mike Riskas as Coach in Residence beginning in 2001;
- clinics and grassroots development.

### Peter Angelos / Orioles

- financial and organizational backing;
- professional scouting expertise;
- Robert Derksen and diaspora player identification;
- Olympic-team construction.

### Boston Red Sox organization

- contemporaneously reported assistance / pledged support;
- **specific contribution not yet recovered**.

### Boston-area Greek-American baseball network

- Galatis and Samiotis: field-building, youth training, recruiting, according to CNN;
- Karalekas / Baseball Acropolis: clinics, equipment, promotion, federation/MLB networking, and surviving visual archive;
- strong overlap with Red Sox fandom and Boston sports culture, but organizationally independent of the Red Sox club unless a source says otherwise.

## 7. Why the Boston layer matters to the book

The Greece chapter becomes richer if “Boston” is not reduced to Burns carrying a Red Sox cap overseas.

The surviving record suggests a transatlantic cultural network in which:

- Burns's own Boston/Red Sox identity made baseball personally legible;
- Greek-American baseball enthusiasts in Boston helped with grassroots development;
- Harry Agganis — the Boston/Greek-American Red Sox figure — served as inspiration for Karalekas's Baseball Acropolis effort;
- the Red Sox club itself appears in contemporaneous reporting as an institutional supporter;
- the Orioles, not the Red Sox, became the dominant professional-baseball organization in constructing the Olympic roster.

That is a better story than “Burns got the Red Sox to bring baseball to Greece.” It is a network story with several Boston nodes and one major Baltimore node.

## 8. Highest-value archival targets for the Red Sox role

1. **Boston Red Sox correspondence / front-office files, 1998–2001** — Greece, Hellenic Amateur Baseball Federation, Nicholas Burns, Jim Small, Peter Angelos, Panos Mitsiopoulos.
2. **Dick Bresciani papers or Red Sox public-relations archive** — he was a long-serving club PR executive during this period and may have preserved correspondence or press material.
3. **MLB International files** — records distinguishing league-level equipment/coaching from club-specific contributions.
4. **U.S. Embassy Athens correspondence** — requests sent to or received from the Red Sox.
5. **Burns–Red Sox correspondence** — particularly around the 1999 reception and early development phase.
6. **Boston Globe / Boston Herald archives, 1999–2001** — local coverage may have named Red Sox personnel involved.
7. **Friends of Greek Baseball records** — board lists, donors, correspondence, equipment inventories.
8. **Baseball Acropolis photo negatives/captions** — identify the “Boston businessmen” and date the Burns/Karalekas photograph.

## 9. Manuscript rule

Until those records appear, use language at the correct level:

**Safe:** “Contemporaneous reports said the Boston Red Sox were helping the Greek baseball effort and had pledged support.”

**Safe:** “MLB International supplied development expertise; a later Greek baseball history credits it with a 1999 equipment shipment and a resident coach in 2001.”

**Safe:** “Boston-area Greek-American Red Sox fans such as Bill Galatis and Chuck Samiotis performed documented grassroots work in Greece.”

**Unsafe:** “Burns persuaded the Red Sox to send equipment and coaches to Greece.”

The last sentence may eventually prove true. The current public record does not prove it.
