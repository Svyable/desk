# Epilogue visual archive — The Next Inning

## Decision

**No new visual object.**

This is intentional, not an unfinished design pass.

The epilogue has already inherited the book's visual memory: cap, archive, briefing room, embassy, route, deadline, unresolved room, communication channel, handoff. Adding another object would turn the final pages back into an exhibition just as the prose is reducing the book to its simplest claim: institutions speak through people, people interpret other people, and the conversation continues.

## Why restraint is the design

The epilogue explicitly rejects several overextended baseball metaphors before allowing one last bounded image: **the schedule**. The point is continuation, not a final scoreboard.

A designed schedule card would merely literalize a metaphor the prose already controls. A final portrait would imply closure around a living subject. A Red Sox photograph would return the reader to fandom rather than the larger representational argument. A diplomatic photograph would suggest one scene could summarize the career.

None passes the removal test.

## Relationship to Chapter 22

Chapter 22 already provides the final two documentary objects the book needs:

- `HANDOFF 01` — office and relationship continue beyond the individual;
- `REVISION OBJECT 01` — judgment can remain open after office.

The epilogue should be the first sustained stretch in the closing sequence where the archive goes quiet and the prose is allowed to carry the reader out.

## Production rule

Do not add media to the epilogue merely to maintain a visual cadence.

Only reconsider if a genuinely primary artifact surfaces that changes the ending's evidence—for example, a document materially altering the handoff/reassessment record. A decorative photograph, cap, baseball, classroom image or quotation card is insufficient.

The absence of a final object is part of the future-book grammar: the interface knows when to stop displaying evidence.
