# 2003–2004 Championship Hinge and Beijing Continuity

This research note addresses two chronological hinges in *Red Sox Diplomacy*: the 2003–04 Red Sox sequence during Nicholas Burns's NATO posting and the persistence of Burns's personal/public-diplomacy register during his China ambassadorship.

## 2003: a directly documented expatriate Red Sox scene

The ESPN Magazine profile “Give 'Em Hellas” supplies a crucial piece of firsthand evidence that was missing from the first research pass.

Burns, then U.S. ambassador to NATO in Brussels, said he watched **Game 7 of the 2003 American League Championship Series on Armed Forces television**. He remembered the exact local time — **6:16 a.m.** — when Aaron Boone's walk-off home run ended the Red Sox season.

This is not a scene reconstructed from travel calendars. It is Burns's own account of following the decisive game while posted overseas.

The exact time matters editorially because it establishes salience without requiring invented emotion. A diplomat in Brussels stayed with an extra-inning Red Sox–Yankees elimination game until dawn and later remembered the minute it ended. The manuscript does not need to add dialogue, gestures, or interior thought.

## The office around the 2003 scene

The same ESPN profile describes Burns's NATO office as containing a small Red Sox collection: an autographed Tony Conigliaro photograph, a baseball signed by Ted Williams, and a bat signed by Nomar Garciaparra.

That evidence matters because the 2003 Game 7 scene did not occur against a blank diplomatic backdrop. Red Sox history was physically present in the ambassador's workspace.

The three objects span different eras of Boston baseball. This is stronger evidence of durable fandom than a generic institutional biography calling Burns a lifelong fan, but it still should not be overinterpreted: the objects document affiliation, not what Burns thought about each player or why he selected each item.

https://www.espn.co.uk/espn/magazine/archives/news/story?page=magazine-20040119-article32

## 2004: what Burns himself says afterward

The best directly attributable Burns language about the championship currently located comes from March 23, 2005, shortly after he became Under Secretary for Political Affairs.

At the U.S. Institute of Peace, Burns called himself “a native-born member of Red Sox Nation” and said New Englanders had “indeed found redemption” when Boston defeated the Yankees and then the Cardinals.

The wording is useful because it explicitly links the championship to the pre-2004 vocabulary Burns had already used in 1997: Calvinism, predestination, suffering, and the expectation of failure. This is not a metaphor imposed retrospectively by the manuscript. Burns himself carried that vocabulary across the championship boundary.

Source:
https://2001-2009.state.gov/p/us/rm/2005/45781.htm

## The baseball sequence behind “redemption”

The manuscript should be more precise than saying Boston simply “broke the curse.” The 2004 title acquired its meaning through the order of events.

### 2003: the wound immediately before the breakthrough

In Game 7 of the 2003 American League Championship Series, Boston led the Yankees 5–2 in the eighth inning and was five outs from the World Series. New York rallied against Pedro Martinez to tie the game, and Aaron Boone hit a walk-off home run off Tim Wakefield in the bottom of the 11th.

Because Burns directly documented watching that game in Brussels, this loss can now function as a Burns-specific prehistory to 2004 rather than only generic fan culture.

Source:
https://www.mlb.com/news/2003-alcs-recap

### 2004 ALCS: the unprecedented reversal

The Yankees won the first three games of the 2004 ALCS, including a 19–8 Game 3 at Fenway Park. No Major League team had ever recovered from a 0–3 deficit to win a postseason series.

In Game 4, Boston trailed 4–3 in the ninth inning with Mariano Rivera on the mound. Kevin Millar walked. Dave Roberts pinch-ran, stole second, and scored on Bill Mueller's single. David Ortiz won the game with a two-run home run in the 12th.

Game 5 lasted 14 innings. Boston again came from behind and won on an Ortiz single, sending the series back to New York.

The Red Sox then won Games 6 and 7 at Yankee Stadium. Boston became the first MLB team to win a postseason series after losing the first three games.

Sources:
- https://www.mlb.com/redsox/news/dave-roberts-steal-set-amazing-2004-red-sox-playoff-run-in-motion/c-98844328
- https://www.mlb.com/redsox/news/david-ortizs-heroics-trot-nixons-grab-kept-2004-ws-in-sight-for-red-sox/c-98920062
- https://www.mlb.com/news/2004-alcs-recap

### World Series: no final collapse

Boston then swept the St. Louis Cardinals in four games. The Red Sox won the final eight games of their postseason and secured their first World Series title since 1918.

That sequence makes Burns's later word “redemption” unusually exact. The title reversed the Yankees ending of 2003, required an unprecedented recovery against the same rival, and then ended the championship drought without another Game 7.

Sources:
- https://www.mlb.com/news/2004-world-series-recap
- https://www.mlb.com/news/classic-seasons-2004-red-sox

## What is still missing for October 2004

The 2003 ESPN account proves that Burns followed decisive Red Sox postseason baseball from Brussels on Armed Forces television. It does **not** tell us how or where he watched the 2004 ALCS comeback or World Series clincher.

We still do not have a securely sourced, contemporaneous October 2004 account establishing:

- where Burns watched Games 4–7 of the ALCS;
- where he watched the World Series clincher;
- who he watched with;
- his immediate reaction on October 27–28;
- whether NATO colleagues marked the event with him;
- whether he corresponded with friends or family about the championship;
- whether Armed Forces television again carried the games he watched.

Do not transfer the 2003 scene into 2004 by inference. The proper research move is narrower: use the 2003 evidence to target Armed Forces Network archives, U.S. Mission NATO newsletters, Burns correspondence, and colleague recollections for October 2004.

The baseball record is strong enough that the chapter does not need an invented championship-night scene. The documented postseason sequence can carry the stakes until direct Burns evidence appears.

## NATO context

Burns was U.S. ambassador to NATO throughout the 2003 and 2004 postseasons. The State archive shows him actively engaged in alliance diplomacy through the period, including NATO-Russia, Iraq, enlargement, defense ministerials, and transatlantic relations.

This creates a legitimate juxtaposition: early-morning baseball fandom and senior alliance diplomacy occupied the same Brussels life. It does not justify a causal claim that one shaped the other.

Archive index:
https://2001-2009.state.gov/p/eur/rt/nato/c11326.htm

## 2005: fandom returns immediately to official life

By October 2005 Burns was again using Red Sox language inside formal State Department business. At a briefing on a Balkans trip he joked that the State Department was shutting down at 4 p.m. so employees could watch the Red Sox play the White Sox.

This is evidence of continuity after the championship: winning did not retire the persona. It remained a recurring public register.

Source:
https://2001-2009.state.gov/p/us/rm/2005/54610.htm

## 2007: from suffering to baseball analysis

An official State Department policy podcast in August 2007 ends with Burns discussing the Red Sox in detail. He talks about the West Coast schedule, the prior year's collapse, Boston's bullpen and starting rotation, and the outsized importance of pitching in the postseason. He predicts that Boston will win the World Series.

The Red Sox did win the 2007 championship.

The point is not that Burns made a successful prediction. The exchange demonstrates that even after 2004 his fandom remained current and analytical rather than merely nostalgic or ceremonial.

## China: personal identity persists in the hardest posting

Burns's China nomination record documents a long history with China dating back to trips with Secretary George Shultz in 1988 and President George H.W. Bush in 1989, plus later work as spokesman, under secretary, and organizer of policy dialogue with the Central Party School.

Source:
https://2021-2025.state.gov/burns-r-nicholas-the-peoples-republic-of-china-september-2021/

That long record matters because the Beijing chapters should not portray Burns as arriving in China with only the tools of a sports-minded public diplomat. Baseball, where it appears, sits on top of decades of substantive China experience.

## Beijing public diplomacy became contested terrain

By 2024 Burns was publicly arguing that Chinese authorities were obstructing U.S. Embassy public events and people-to-people exchange. Reporting on his remarks described dozens of embassy events that he said had faced interference.

This creates a useful contrast with Greece. In Athens, baseball helped expand a people-to-people network. In Beijing, Burns was operating in an environment where people-to-people contact itself had become politically constrained.

## Baseball in China

A 2024 image/caption associated with Burns shows him with Chinese baseball fans and uses the phrase “Future members of @Redsox Nation.” This is modest evidence, but narratively valuable: the same personal register visible at the State podium in 1997 and in Greece appears again in China decades later.

Source lead:
https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ambassador_Burns_with_Chinese_baseball_fans.png

A 2024 Committee of 100 program also described Burns as a proud New Englander and passionate supporter of the Red Sox, Celtics, Patriots, Bruins, and Revolution while serving as ambassador to China. That is institutional biography rather than Burns's own statement, but it corroborates the persistence of the public identity.

Source:
https://www.committee100.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/2024-Gala-Program_web.pdf

## 2025: the rapport habit survives government service

A Davidson College student who met Burns in 2025 recounts that once Burns learned they were both from Boston, they immediately traded observations about the Red Sox–Yankees series. The student describes the exchange as helping create an open tone before the conversation moved into substantive international affairs.

This is a useful late-career behavioral echo of the 1997 briefing-room pattern: baseball remains a rapid route from formal status to ordinary human conversation.

Source:
https://www.davidson.edu/news/2025/09/23/my-day-davidson-ambassador-china-nicholas-burns

## The stronger structural contrast

The evidence now supports a more precise long arc:

- **1997 Washington:** baseball creates a safe informal register inside official briefings; reporters learn and participate in it.
- **1999–2001 Athens:** Burns uses baseball knowledge and diplomatic access in a hands-on people-to-people project.
- **2003 Brussels:** direct Burns testimony documents an expatriate fan watching the Yankees end Boston's season at 6:16 a.m.
- **2004–2005:** the same rival is overcome through an unprecedented comeback, followed by Burns's own language of championship “redemption.”
- **2007 Washington:** fandom remains current enough for Burns to analyze pitching, schedule, and postseason prospects in detail.
- **2022–2025 Beijing and after:** personal identity remains available, but public diplomacy operates in a much more constrained strategic environment; after government service, Red Sox–Yankees talk still functions as immediate rapport.

This is stronger than a simple “baseball followed him around the world” narrative. The role of the same personal language changes as the political environment and Burns's office change.
