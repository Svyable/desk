# Chapter 12 Brief — Article 5

## Status

Draft-ready research brief. Chapter 11 ends on the night of September 11 with allied solidarity ahead of formal procedure. Chapter 12 begins the next morning.

## Working question

What does an alliance promise mean when it is finally used?

## Documentary center

The strongest sequence is unusually well documented and should remain precise:

1. On September 11, the North Atlantic Council condemned the attacks and expressed solidarity with the United States.
2. On September 12, less than twenty-four hours after the attacks, the Council agreed that **if** the attacks were determined to have been directed from abroad against the United States, they would be regarded as covered by Article 5.
3. Burns's later accounts place an early-morning call to National Security Adviser Condoleezza Rice before the vote. He says he sought President George W. Bush's authorization because the alliance was preparing to make a collective-defense decision of extraordinary consequence.
4. Burns consistently remembers Rice ending that call with the observation that it was good to have friends in the world.
5. On October 2, after the Council received the results of the U.S. investigation into the attacks, NATO determined that the attacks were indeed covered by Article 5.
6. On October 4, NATO agreed eight concrete measures to support the United States.
7. Operation Eagle Assist put NATO AWACS aircraft over the United States; Operation Active Endeavour sent NATO naval forces into the Mediterranean.

## The conditional point

Do not flatten September 12 into a legally unconditional declaration divorced from the evidence question.

The Council's statement was conditional: if the attack was directed from abroad, it would be regarded as an Article 5 action. That condition was satisfied after the October 2 briefing.

This distinction matters because it shows alliance law operating through both solidarity and verification.

## Burns's role

Burns was not the author of NATO's response by himself.

His specific roles included:

- carrying the U.S. position into the North Atlantic Council;
- obtaining Washington authorization for the September 12 decision;
- communicating with allied ambassadors and NATO leadership;
- later delivering U.S. requests for concrete allied support;
- representing the United States as NATO translated a treaty clause into operational measures.

A State Department briefing on October 3 identifies Burns as the ambassador who delivered the U.S. list of requested steps to NATO.

## Operational reality

Article 5 did not mean every ally automatically declared war in the same form.

The treaty requires each member to assist by taking such action as it deems necessary. Assistance may include armed force but need not take identical form across allies.

NATO's October 4 measures included:

- enhanced intelligence sharing;
- assistance to allies facing increased terrorist threats;
- greater security for U.S. and allied facilities;
- backfilling selected allied assets;
- blanket overflight clearances for relevant military flights;
- access to ports and airfields;
- deployment of standing naval forces to the Eastern Mediterranean;
- deployment of NATO airborne early-warning assets.

The U.S. later requested and received NATO AWACS support over American airspace.

## Narrative argument

The chapter should resist treating Article 5 as merely ceremonial.

Its force came from the conversion of language into action.

A treaty written in 1949 for a feared Soviet attack on Europe was used in 2001 after a terrorist attack on the United States. The wording was durable enough to survive the change in threat because it specified the obligation to assist without requiring every ally to respond identically.

For Burns, this became a durable argument about American power: allies were not diplomatic decoration. They could supply intelligence, access, military capabilities, political legitimacy, and, eventually, soldiers who would fight and die alongside Americans.

## Chapter 11 / Chapter 12 boundary

Do not repeat the full September 11 lunch/phone-call sequence. Chapter 11 owns that.

Chapter 12 begins with Burns reconnecting to Washington on September 12 and asking for authorization to convert allied instinct into alliance policy.

## Guardrails

Do not:

- say NATO automatically entered the Afghanistan war on September 12;
- say every ally was legally required to use military force in the same way;
- omit the condition that the attack be externally directed;
- collapse the September 12 political decision and the October 2 evidentiary determination into one moment;
- make Burns the sole architect of Article 5;
- imply later divisions over Iraq erase the reality of 2001 solidarity;
- use Rice's remembered line without attributing it as Burns's retrospective recollection.
