# Cairo ambassador transition and period-map pass 02

## Scope

Focused media/research enrichment for Chapter 4, covering the institutional transition Burns entered in Cairo in 1983 and period cartography that can clarify the posting without decorative Middle East imagery. This packet does not alter manuscript prose.

## Documentary chronology: the embassy Burns entered

The State Department Office of the Historian records Alfred L. Atherton Jr. as U.S. Ambassador to Egypt from July 2, 1979, until November 12, 1983. Nicholas A. Veliotes was appointed Ambassador to Egypt on October 7, 1983, presented credentials on November 24, 1983, and left post April 1, 1986. The same State Department biographical record shows Veliotes moving directly from Assistant Secretary of State for Near Eastern Affairs (May 1981–October 27, 1983) to Cairo.

This is useful Chapter 4 context because Burns's documented 1983–1985 Cairo assignment overlapped an ambassadorial handoff at the beginning of his posting. It does **not** establish Burns's role in the transition, his relationship with either ambassador, or his presence at any specific handoff event. Those claims require separate evidence.

Sources:

- U.S. Department of State, Office of the Historian, “Egypt — Chiefs of Mission”: https://history.state.gov/departmenthistory/people/chiefsofmission/egypt
- U.S. Department of State, Office of the Historian, “Nicholas Alexander Veliotes”: https://history.state.gov/departmenthistory/people/veliotes-nicholas-alexander
- U.S. Department of State, Office of the Historian, *Foreign Relations of the United States, 1981–1988*, principal-officer/person chronology noting Atherton as Ambassador to Egypt until November 12, 1983: https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1981-88v01/persons

### Manuscript-safe use

A short chronology or sentence may say that Burns arrived in Cairo during the Atherton-to-Veliotes ambassadorial transition. Do not imply that the transition shaped Burns personally, that he participated in a particular meeting, or that either ambassador mentored him unless a Burns-specific source establishes it.

## Formatting and editorial rules

1. One Cairo map is enough unless a later chapter makes a genuinely different geographic argument. Prefer CAIRO 02 because its projection itself does narrative work.
2. No flags, pyramids, passport stamps, baseball diamonds, airline-route swooshes, or “Middle East” ornamental motifs.
3. Keep creator, date, repository, LCCN, rights statement, canonical URL, credit, caption, and alt text together in production provenance.
4. Historical boundaries and labels should be period-correct. If a modern basemap is used for an original redraw, mark it explicitly as a contemporary orientation graphic rather than silently presenting it as 1983 geography.
5. A reproduced historical map should never be cropped so aggressively that its date/provenance becomes misleading. If the production crop removes identifying marginalia, retain the full identification in caption/credit and provenance notes.
6. Do not infer embassy location, Burns's housing, travel routes, meeting sites, or movements from these maps.

## Evidence distinctions

**Documented:** Atherton's ambassadorship ended November 12, 1983; Veliotes was appointed October 7, presented credentials November 24, and served until April 1, 1986. The CIA produced the Cairo-centered projection in April 1985 and the cited Africa/Arabian Peninsula maps in 1983–1984.

**Supported interpretation:** Burns's Cairo posting began during an ambassadorial transition, and a Cairo-centered period map can help the reader reverse the usual Washington-centered geographic perspective.

**Not established:** Burns's participation in the ambassadorial handoff; mentorship by Atherton or Veliotes; any specific embassy scene; any causal effect of the transition on Burns's later diplomatic style.
