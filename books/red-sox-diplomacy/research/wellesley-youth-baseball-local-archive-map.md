# Wellesley youth baseball — local archive map and current evidence boundary

## Finding

The Wellesley Free Library now exposes a concrete local-history retrieval route for the still-unresolved question of Nicholas Burns's youth baseball life in Wellesley.

The library's Wellesley History page identifies two digitized collections with unusually high value for this question:

- **The Wellesley Townsman, 1906–current**; and
- **Wellesley High School yearbooks, 1931–2018**.

Source:
- Wellesley Free Library, "Wellesley History": https://www.wellesleyfreelibrary.org/engage/your-community/wellesley-history-pages/

The Wellesley Historical Society independently states that the digitized, searchable *Wellesley Townsman* for **1906–2005** is available through the Wellesley Free Library. Its new History & Exhibit Center also houses the Society's archives and collections and permits researchers to work with staff on site.

Sources:
- https://www.wellesleyhistoricalsociety.org/research-town-of-wellesley-archives
- https://www.wellesleyhistoricalsociety.org/wellesley-history-center/

The library also maintains a local-author profile for **Nick Burns, Wellesley High School class of 1974**. That institutional profile says Burns grew up on **Shirley Road**, spent his junior year in Luxembourg through American Field Service, and has lifelong pride in Red Sox Nation. It does **not** say that he played organized baseball, Little League, Babe Ruth, high-school baseball, or any other youth baseball in Wellesley.

Source:
- https://www.wellesleyfreelibrary.org/authors/nick-burns/

## Why this matters

This narrows the Wellesley gap from a generic biographical search to a local-primary-source program. The Townsman spans Burns's entire Wellesley childhood and school years and is the likeliest surviving searchable record for youth-team rosters, game reports, league photographs, awards, recreation-department notices, neighborhood teams, and school sports. The 1960s–1974 Wellesleyan yearbooks can independently test school-team and extracurricular claims.

The Shirley Road detail is useful as a geographic anchor for local archival searching, but it should not be converted into a reconstructed childhood scene.

## Evidentiary boundary

At present there is **no verified source in this pass establishing that Burns himself played organized youth baseball in Wellesley**.

Do not infer participation from:

- his lifelong Red Sox fandom;
- growing up in suburban Boston;
- the existence of youth baseball in Wellesley;
- later diplomatic use of baseball;
- later descriptions of him as a member of Red Sox Nation.

Do not invent a father-son game, neighborhood pickup game, Little League roster, Fenway trip, position, batting habit, glove, radio ritual, or childhood conversation unless a source supplies it.

Nothing in these sources supports any childhood baseball claim, memory, or scene involving Libby Baylies.

## Retrieval plan

Search the digitized *Wellesley Townsman* across approximately 1962–1974 for:

1. `Nicholas Burns`, `Nick Burns`, `N. Burns`, and `Burns` paired with baseball, Little League, Babe Ruth, recreation, team, pitcher, catcher, infield, outfield, or roster;
2. Shirley Road neighborhood/team references;
3. Burns family names only where independently authenticated and relevant, avoiding identity-by-surname guesses;
4. Wellesley youth-league season summaries, team photographs, championship rosters, and recreation-department pages that can be checked visually when OCR is weak.

Inspect Wellesleyan yearbooks for the Burns class years and earlier school years for:

- baseball team photographs and rosters;
- intramural or club listings;
- student activity indexes;
- Burns's individual entry and any cross-referenced activities.

If those searches remain negative, ask Wellesley Historical Society staff specifically about recreation-department records, Little League/Babe Ruth collections, youth-sports photographs, donated scrapbooks, and local family collections from the 1960s and early 1970s.

## Manuscript use

The local-author profile can support a compact, attributed factual anchor—Burns grew up on Shirley Road in Wellesley and graduated from Wellesley High in 1974—but the youth-baseball question should remain visibly open until a roster, clipping, photograph, yearbook entry, or authenticated first-person recollection establishes more.
