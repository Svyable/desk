# Chapter 9 media/research enrichment pass 02 — the visit that tested representation

Accessed 2026-09-15. Scope is Chapter 9, “Athens,” only. This pass adds a publication-safe federal visual route for the November 1999 Clinton visit and production guidance that preserves the chapter’s central contradiction: official bilateral cooperation and public opposition existed at the same time.

## Strongest newly sourced visual route — White House Athens trip photography, November 20, 1999

The archived Clinton White House trip gallery contains event-identified official photographs from the Athens visit. The November 20 gallery identifies, among other frames:

- President Bill Clinton and Greek Prime Minister Costas Simitis holding a joint press conference in the Central Hall of the Prime Minister’s office — **photo by Sharon Farmer**;
- the Clintons’ departure ceremony at the Presidential Palace — **photo by William Vasta**;
- a Greek guard outside the Presidential Palace — **photo by Sharon Farmer**;
- President Clinton addressing the Greek community at the Intercontinental Hotel in Athens — **photos by Ralph Alswang and Sharon Farmer**.

Primary event gallery:
https://clintonwhitehouse4.archives.gov/textonly/WH/new/Europe-9911/photos/day6/index2.html

NARA describes the Clinton White House Photograph Office series as official-event photography covering foreign and domestic presidential trips, press conferences, speeches and other presidential activities:
https://www.archives.gov/research/cartographic/aerial-photography/aerial-photography-presidential-libraries-clinton

NARA’s current copyright guidance states that materials created by U.S. federal officers or employees as part of official duties are U.S. Government works and are not eligible for U.S. copyright protection; it also warns that not every item in NARA holdings is necessarily federal/public-domain material and that item-level restrictions still need checking:
https://www.archives.gov/research/still-pictures/permissions

The White House archive separately identifies Sharon Farmer as a White House photographer responsible for documenting presidential events:
https://clintonwhitehouse4.archives.gov/WH/kids/inside/html/fall99/html/farmer.html

Before final production, obtain the highest-resolution frame from the Clinton Presidential Library/NARA record, preserve its photograph identifier and item-level use-restriction field, and confirm no contrary donor/third-party restriction attaches to that exact image. Do not treat the low-resolution legacy web-gallery derivative as the production master.

## Recommended image — Clinton and Simitis joint press conference

Of the newly located federal frames, the **Clinton–Simitis joint press conference** is the strongest Chapter 9 candidate.

Why it earns space:

1. It documents the governmental cooperation side of the chapter at the exact moment public opposition to U.S. policy was part of the visit’s context.
2. It avoids reducing Athens to protest imagery. The manuscript already has the 1975 demonstration-damage photograph as evidence that the embassy had long been a political object.
3. It is more specific than a generic presidential arrival or honor guard.
4. It gives the chapter a period human scene without inventing Burns’s presence in a frame where the gallery does not identify him.

**Do not caption the photograph as evidence that the visit was harmonious.** A press conference proves that the two governments met publicly and spoke together. It does not prove public approval, agreement on Kosovo, or resolution of the security/protest dispute.

### Caption draft

*FIG. 05A — President Bill Clinton and Greek Prime Minister Costas Simitis at a joint press conference in the Prime Minister’s office, Athens, November 20, 1999. Official White House photograph by Sharon Farmer. The image documents the governmental channel that remained active during a visit shaped by public anger over U.S. policy in the Balkans; it does not turn official cooperation into public consent. Clinton White House Photograph Office / William J. Clinton Presidential Library, NARA. U.S. Government work; verify exact archive identifier and item-level restrictions before release.*

### Alt text

“President Bill Clinton and Greek Prime Minister Costas Simitis stand together at microphones during a joint press conference in the Prime Minister’s office in Athens on November 20, 1999. The photograph represents the official bilateral channel during a visit also marked by public opposition to U.S. policy.”

### Print / EPUB treatment

- Place near the manuscript’s Clinton-visit section, after the paragraph establishing the Kosovo-war protests and before the discussion of revised arrangements.
- Use approximately 65–75% text-block width in print; this is documentary evidence, not a heroic presidential spread.
- Keep image, caption and credit together. Do not orphan the federal credit line across a page turn.
- In EPUB, image first, then caption/credit in reading order; retain the date and both leaders’ names in actual text.
- Avoid aggressive cropping. Keep enough of the press setting to make the institutional context legible.
- Do not pair it on the same page with a protest photograph in a false “two sides” montage unless both frames have exact dates and the layout makes clear that they document different actors rather than a direct confrontation.

## Research strengthened

The new White House visual route sharpens a central Chapter 9 distinction that should remain visible in both prose and design:

- **documented governmental cooperation:** Clinton and Simitis met and held a joint press conference;
- **documented public opposition:** the visit occurred after the Kosovo campaign amid demonstrations and security disputes;
- **interpretation:** an ambassador can maintain useful governmental channels in a country where important parts of the public strongly oppose U.S. policy.

The photograph supports the first proposition. It does not prove the third by itself and must not be used to erase the second.
