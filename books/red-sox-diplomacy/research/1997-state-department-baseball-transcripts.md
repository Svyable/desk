# 1997 State Department baseball transcripts: fandom, podium culture, and diplomacy

## Finding

The State Department's permanent electronic archive preserves several on-the-record 1997 daily press briefings in which spokesman Nicholas Burns discussed the Boston Red Sox from the official podium. These are primary transcripts, not retrospective recollections.

### April 15, 1997

After reporters told Burns the Red Sox had won, he asked about the score, Mo Vaughn's home run, and Tim Wakefield's knuckleball before turning to the day's diplomatic business.

Primary transcript: https://1997-2001.state.gov/briefings/9704/970415.html

### June 4, 1997

Asked about former Red Sox pitcher Roger Clemens, then with Toronto, Burns replied that Boston was still bitter and joked about the team's need for help. The exchange is preserved verbatim in the official daily briefing transcript.

Primary transcript: https://1997-2001.state.gov/briefings/9706/970604db.html

### June 16, 1997

A reporter asked why every Red Sox fan was "rabid." Burns answered at length, describing Red Sox fandom in terms of predestination and repeated failure, then volunteered that his mother was a fan and that his father had warned the family away from the team after having his heart broken in the 1930s.

Primary transcript: https://1997-2001.state.gov/www/briefings/9706/970616db.html

### July 14, 1997

When asked about Cuba canceling a baseball team's planned visit to Connecticut, Burns moved directly from baseball into an official U.S. critique of the Cuban government and player defections. This is useful evidence that baseball could enter the substance of a State Department briefing, not merely serve as pre-briefing banter.

Primary transcript: https://1997-2001.state.gov/briefings/9707/970714db.html

## Manuscript value

These transcripts establish a documented public pattern before Burns's ambassadorships: Red Sox identity was repeatedly visible in his official press-facing role. They also provide a clean example of baseball and foreign policy occupying the same podium exchange in the Cuba discussion.

A safe manuscript formulation is:

> By 1997, Burns's Red Sox allegiance was not private background color. It repeatedly surfaced in the State Department's own briefing transcripts—scores, players, inherited family disappointment—and, in one July exchange about a canceled Cuban baseball visit, baseball moved directly into the substance of U.S. foreign-policy argument.

Keep any quoted language attached to the dated official transcript. Do not infer that Burns deliberately developed a baseball-diplomacy strategy from these exchanges.

## Boundaries

These records do not establish Burns's baseball habits at Boston College in 1975 or 1978, his viewing circumstances in Jerusalem in 1986, or his location during the 2004 World Series. They do not establish private motives or reactions beyond what Burns actually said on the record.

Nothing here authenticates or supports first-person material attributed to Libby Baylies.

## Retrieval targets

- Search the complete 1995–97 State Department briefing corpus for additional baseball references, especially 1995 and 1996 transcripts not exposed by current web indexing.
- Retrieve audiovisual copies or press-room photographs corresponding to these briefings, if preserved by State, NARA, C-SPAN, AP, Reuters, or network archives.
- Use the transcript dates to locate contemporaneous press stories that may describe the room, delivery, or reporter response without reconstructing it.
