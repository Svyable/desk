# Chapter 6 media provenance — Baltic withdrawal and Riga, 1994

## Scope

This packet supports Chapter 6, **“The Score Is Not the Game,”** with period-correct, publication-safe visual evidence. It does not reopen the chapter’s settled claim that August 31, 1994 is a useful historical marker but an inadequate description of the diplomacy that produced the Baltic withdrawals.

## Priority asset — Clinton White House photographs, Riga, July 6, 1994

**Institution:** William J. Clinton Presidential Library and Museum / National Archives and Records Administration (NARA)

**Collection:** White House Photograph Office, *Photographs Relating to the Clinton Administration*, finding-aid segment covering July 5–7, 1994.

**Finding aid:** https://www.clintonlibrary.gov/research/archives/finding-aids/white-house-photos-white-house-photographs-july-5-1994-through-july

**Date:** July 6, 1994 (with in-flight photographs on the surrounding travel dates).

**What the finding aid documents:**

- Clinton’s flight to Riga includes photographs of the President meeting with Nicholas Burns, Tony Lake and other White House staff.
- In Riga, Clinton meets the Baltic presidents and participates in bilateral and multilateral events.
- The Riga Castle bilateral meeting/signing sequence identifies **Nicholas Burns** among the U.S. participants alongside Secretary Warren Christopher, Tony Lake, David Gergen and Ambassador Ints Silins.
- The same segment includes Clinton at the Freedom Monument, his address in Riga, embassy-staff greetings, and the working luncheon with Latvian President Guntis Ulmanis, Estonian President Lennart Meri and Lithuanian President Algirdas Brazauskas.

### Recommended production object

**BALTIC 01 — “Burns in Riga, July 6, 1994.”**

Prefer a frame that places Burns visibly inside the Riga policy work rather than a generic presidential arrival photograph. First-choice candidates are the Riga Castle bilateral/signing sequence identifying Burns as a participant, or an in-flight working photograph identifying Burns with Clinton and Tony Lake if the frame is compositionally stronger at book size.

**Caption draft:** *Riga, July 6, 1994. White House photographs from President Clinton’s Baltic trip place Nicholas Burns inside the working delegation as Washington pressed toward completion of Russian troop withdrawals from Latvia and Estonia. The photographs document his presence in the policy process; they should not be used to imply that a single meeting produced the August agreements.*

**Credit draft:** *Courtesy, William J. Clinton Presidential Library. White House Photograph Office. [Photographer and frame identifier to be added from production master.]*

**Alt text draft:** *President Clinton and U.S. officials during the July 1994 Riga visit; Nicholas Burns is among the White House delegation participating in meetings with Baltic leaders.*

### Placement / formatting

Place after the paragraph introducing Clinton’s July 1994 Riga visit and before the implementation ledger. Do not place beside the August 31 deadline object: the photograph should show the diplomatic work before the outcome, not become a victory image after it.

For print, favor a horizontal frame at approximately 5.5–6.0 inches wide if faces remain identifiable. Keep image, caption and credit together across page breaks. In EPUB, image → caption → credit → alt text metadata should remain one semantic figure. Do not crop Baltic participants merely to enlarge Burns. Avoid flags or monuments as decorative crops when a working-meeting frame is available.

### Recommended redraw

**BALTIC 02 — “The Baltic withdrawal problem, summer 1994.”**

Use the June 1994 CIA sheet as the period baseline. Show Lithuania, Latvia, Estonia and adjacent Russia; Riga and Tallinn; Skrunda and Paldiski only after checking their coordinates against an authoritative source; and a restrained indication that Lithuania’s Russian troop withdrawal had already concluded in 1993 while Latvia and Estonia remained active in 1994. Do not silently import present-day borders, NATO membership, or later military infrastructure.

**Caption draft:** *The Baltic states in the summer of 1994. Lithuania had completed the withdrawal of Russian troops in 1993; Latvia and Estonia were still negotiating the terms by which Russian forces and installations would leave. The map makes the regional geometry visible without turning three different national negotiations into one Baltic story.*

**Credit draft:** *Original redraw. Period geography based on U.S. Central Intelligence Agency, “The Baltic States,” June 1994; Library of Congress, Geography and Map Division.*

**Alt text draft:** *Map of Lithuania, Latvia and Estonia with adjacent Russia, orienting Riga, Tallinn and the locations relevant to the 1994 Russian troop-withdrawal negotiations.*

For grayscale print, use line weight and pattern rather than color to distinguish national boundaries and any installation markers. At six-inch e-reader width, no essential label should depend on a legend smaller than body-note type. Keep the redraw informational: no NATO logos, Soviet insignia, troop silhouettes, baseball marks, or decorative arrows suggesting a single mass withdrawal route.

## Primary-source chronology anchors

These sources strengthen captions and prevent visual shorthand from outrunning the record:

- **April 30, 1994 — Latvia agreement:** William J. Clinton, “Statement on the Agreement To Withdraw Russian Military Forces From Latvia,” *Public Papers of the Presidents*, p. 810. GovInfo: https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/PPP-1994-book1/PPP-1994-book1-doc-pg810
- **July 6, 1994 — Riga:** Clinton’s joint press availability with Baltic leaders describes the Latvia agreement, the remaining Estonia–Russia differences, and the U.S. effort to help bridge them. Archived White House record: https://clintonwhitehouse6.archives.gov/1994/07/1994-07-06-president-in-press-conference-with-baltic-presidents.html
- **July 6, 1994 — Freedom Monument:** the presidential remarks provide contemporary public language about Baltic independence and Russian-minority rights. Archived White House record: https://clintonwhitehouse6.archives.gov/1994/07/1994-07-06-president-and-latvia-president-freedom-monument-riga.html

### Evidentiary guardrails

1. A White House photograph proves presence in the photographed event, not authorship of every policy discussed there.
2. Do not caption the July Riga visit as the moment the withdrawals were “secured.” Latvia’s agreement had been announced April 30; Estonia’s remaining differences were still unresolved in July.
3. Do not collapse Lithuania, Latvia and Estonia into a single withdrawal chronology. Lithuania’s final Russian troops left in 1993; Latvia and Estonia reached the August 1994 marker through distinct negotiations and implementation arrangements.
4. Do not treat the CIA map as neutral adjudication of every political claim. It is a period U.S. government cartographic representation useful for orientation.
5. Keep Skrunda’s temporary arrangements visible in prose/captioning when an August 31 visual might otherwise imply that every Russian military function vanished from Latvian territory that day.

## Why this improves Chapter 6

The existing deadline object and implementation ledger already explain **date** and **mechanism**. The missing visual register is **presence** and **place**. The Clinton Library sequence can put Burns inside the actual July 1994 Riga work without inventing a scene; the June 1994 CIA map can orient readers before Skrunda, Paldiski, Riga and Tallinn accumulate as names. Together they make the chapter less diagrammatic while preserving its central distinction between a legible outcome and the messier diplomacy underneath it.
