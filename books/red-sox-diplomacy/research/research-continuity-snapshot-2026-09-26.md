# Red Sox Diplomacy — research continuity snapshot

**Date:** 2026-09-26  
**Branch:** `book/red-sox-diplomacy-20260912-sol`

## Purpose

This file is a durable continuity record for research developed after the original manuscript PR. It is intentionally redundant with focused research notes. The goal is to prevent useful findings, negative findings, retrieval targets, provenance leads, and attribution boundaries from being lost between research passes.

A Desk commit is a reversible save point, not a publication event. Nothing in this file by itself authorizes publication, quotation, image reuse, or promotion into the manuscript.

## Evidence labels

- **Verified / promotable with attribution:** supported by a primary source or strong contemporaneous source already recovered.
- **Verified context only:** chronology, place, institutional role, or general background is supported, but it does not establish a Burns-specific baseball scene.
- **Candidate / needs source recovery:** a useful lead or previously surfaced source whose exact text, original metadata, or provenance should be recovered before manuscript use.
- **Negative boundary:** research has not established the scene or claim; do not manufacture it from chronology or fandom.

# 1. Greece / Red Sox institutional assistance

## Verified / promotable with attribution

### Boston Red Sox organizational support was contemporaneously reported

Two contemporaneous reports directly establish a Boston Red Sox organizational role at the level of help/support:

1. **The Washington Post, September 8, 1999** reported that Peter Angelos's Greek baseball effort was being helped by the commissioner's office **and the Boston Red Sox**, parenthetically identifying Boston as Burns's favorite team.  
   https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/sports/1999/09/08/injuries-to-teens-are-still-a-concern/fe2eb9f0-7fc2-4b43-80a4-a9cb6ac94670/

2. **Associated Press / Los Angeles Times, January 2, 2000**, Theodora Tongas, “Game of Baseball Is All Greek to Them,” reported that support had also been pledged by the Boston Red Sox.  
   https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-2000-jan-02-mn-49873-story.html

**Safe formulation:** contemporary reporting in 1999 and 2000 said the Boston Red Sox were helping, or had pledged support to, the effort to build a Greek Olympic baseball program.

**Unresolved:** neither report identifies the responsible Red Sox employee or specifies money, equipment, personnel, scouting, coaching, facilities, introductions, shipping, clinics, or any other concrete Boston deliverable. Do not say the pledge was fulfilled without further evidence. Do not say Burns personally caused Red Sox participation without evidence.

### Orioles / MLB chain is more specifically documented

A 2004 Christian Science Monitor account reports that Burns brought Peter Angelos and MLB International into the Greek baseball effort, and quotes Burns describing the project as an American-Greek joint venture and as one of the best things he did as ambassador.

Source:  
https://www.csmonitor.com/2004/0818/p02s01-woeu.html

A Washington Post report dated April 12, 2004 says Burns asked Angelos to lead the search for Greek-American players; Orioles personnel and scout Rob Derksen pursued recruiting; Burns's successor Thomas J. Miller helped expedite Greek-passport paperwork.

Source:  
https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/sports/2004/04/12/it-appears-that-baseball-isnt-all-greek-to-the-greeks/0d5fc3ca-536e-4df4-8712-c260367f6e51/

The project also has Cynthia P. Schneider's 2003 cultural-diplomacy paper as retrospective context. Use its evaluative claims as Schneider's analysis, not narrator fact.

### Visual/archive leads

The Baseball Acropolis archive contains captions identifying Burns with Chris and Gus Karalekas; Hellenic baseball figures meeting MLB/Orioles participants and “Boston businessmen”; Jim Small; Syd Thrift; Lou Angelos; Tom Marudas; clinics; and equipment-related activity.

These are archive leads until original-file date, photographer, caption provenance, and rights are established. “Boston businessmen” must not be converted into “Red Sox personnel.”

### John Stephen Bailey / Athens relationship

An American College of Greece annual-report tribute by Burns to John Stephen Bailey is a usable retrospective Burns source for their Athens-era relationship, shared Boston/Irish-American background, and Red Sox enthusiasm. Any reference to Libby in Burns's tribute remains Burns's retrospective statement; it does not authenticate Libby's own baseball fandom, dialogue, memory, or reaction.

## Correction to preserve

Any note saying there is **no reliable evidence of Boston Red Sox organizational assistance** is obsolete. The correct boundary is:

# 2. Wellesley youth baseball

## Verified context

The Wellesley Free Library local profile identifies Burns as Wellesley High School class of 1974, says he grew up on Shirley Road, and describes enduring Red Sox pride. It also retrospectively says he often opened State briefings with Red Sox score updates; use surviving State transcripts as the stronger primary evidence for specific podium examples.

Source:  
https://www.wellesleyfreelibrary.org/authors/nick-burns/

The Wellesley Free Library and Wellesley Historical Society provide important local-archive routes through Wellesley Townsman coverage and school/yearbook material.

A later Burns interview provides a strong non-baseball Wellesley formation point: his recollection of turning seventeen in Wellesley just after the Paris Peace Accords and hearing church bells. Keep that as formative diplomatic context, not baseball causation.

A 2025 Harvard/Belfer transcript records Burns's own account of Wellesley public schools, Wellesley High School, Boston College, study in Paris, and meeting his future wife there. Burns's testimony authenticates Burns's chronology only; it does not authenticate Libby Baylies first-person material.

The **July 4, 2024 Beijing Independence Day remarks** provide direct first-person evidence. Burns connected Wellesley, Katharine Lee Bates / “America the Beautiful,” and his childhood by saying that he and his friends played Little League at Bates School.

Original U.S. Mission China page:
https://china.usembassy-china.org.cn/ambassador-nicholas-burns-remarks-on-independence-day/

Contemporaneous Chinese-language preservation:
https://www.sina.cn/news/detail/5052829243279266.html

https://www.linkedin.com/posts/james-zimmerman-b3b1a313_ambassador-nicholas-burns-remarks-on-independence-activity-7214682257557442561-GTkq

This is the strongest direct youth-baseball source in the project and is already reflected in Chapter 1. It establishes Little League, friends, and Bates School. It does **not** establish team, coach, position, season, exact diamond, uniform, statistics, or a particular game. Present-day Bates/Kelly field layouts remain location context only.

# 3. Boston College, 1975–1978

## Verified / promotable

A Boston College profile of historian Mark O'Connor records Burns identifying O'Connor as one of his first professors and recalling O'Connor reading Siegfried Sassoon antiwar poetry during the last year of the Vietnam War. Burns describes the encounter as a revelation and credits O'Connor with forcing ethical and moral questions. This is a strong formation source, but not a baseball scene.

Source:  
https://www.bc.edu/bc-web/bcnews/campus-community/faculty/the-seminar.html

Boston College's institutional history identifies Burns's senior-year residence as **Mod 36A**. Burns's preserved 2002 commencement address says that he and his roommates lived in the Mods, that three former roommates were present, identifies himself as a loyal member of Red Sox Nation, and makes a Red Sox championship prediction.

Source:  
https://www.bc.edu/bc-web/bcnews/campus-community/alumni/r--nicholas-burns-2002-commencement-address.html

Burns later publicly identified **Kevin Donahue** as a high-school friend and college roommate. Do not assume Donahue lived in Mod 36A unless a separate record places him there.

The John J. Burns Library has a specific 1978 commencement archival route:

- Office of Public Relations “Commencement, 1978,” BC-1986-003, Box 36 folders 37–39 and Box 43 folder 4.
- Separate commencement holdings include photographs/contact sheets/slides, invitations, programs, honorary lists, and addresses.
- The Boston College athletic photographs collection **BC-1986-019** is a concrete visual archive for 1970s athletics and should be searched for dated/captioned baseball imagery.

1978 commencement archive:  
https://findingaids.bc.edu/repositories/2/archival_objects/162944

Commencement collection:  
https://findingaids.bc.edu/repositories/2/resources/11

The archived White House biography gives the degree/academic credential chronology: B.A. in European History from Boston College in 1978, summa cum laude / Phi Beta Kappa, with a Sorbonne credential in 1977.

## Baseball chronology / context only

Boston College varsity baseball records during Burns's undergraduate period have been noted as:
- 1975: 13–12
- 1976: 9–14
- 1977: 17–10
- 1978: 14–10–1

These team records do **not** show that Burns played, attended, or followed BC baseball.

The 1975 World Series ran October 11–22, with Games 6 and 7 at Fenway on October 21–22. October 2, 1978 remains a major Red Sox chronology point. Neither chronology creates a Burns scene.

## Negative boundary

There is no authenticated evidence yet that Burns watched, listened to, attended, discussed, celebrated, or mourned any specific 1975 or 1978 Red Sox game while at Boston College.

Do not put a television, radio, Fisk Game 6, the 1978 tiebreaker, dialogue, reaction, roommates, or Libby Baylies inside Mod 36A without direct evidence.

## Highest-value next retrievals

- Identify all six 1977–78 residents of Mod 36A from housing, directory, yearbook, alumni, or university-archive records.
- Search *The Heights* for October 1975 and September–October 1978 campus baseball atmosphere.
- Search *Sub Turri* 1975–78 and intramural records.

# 4. Jerusalem, 1985–1987 / 1986 World Series gap

## Verified / promotable

Official archived biographies place Burns at the American Consulate General in Jerusalem from 1985 to 1987 as a political officer, coordinating U.S. economic assistance to Palestinians in the West Bank and East Jerusalem.

A June 9, 2025 Harvard/Belfer Environmental Insights transcript records Burns's own recollection that he spent two years at the U.S. Consulate in Jerusalem, managed an economic-assistance program for Palestinians in the West Bank and East Jerusalem, regarded the posting as seminal/very important, and was working as an Arabic speaker.

This supports the formative significance of Jerusalem. It does not supply a baseball scene.

ADST's Jerusalem Country Reader identifies overlapping senior officers including Douglas R. Keene (Deputy Principal Officer through 1986) and Morris Draper (Consul General 1986–88). They are witness/oral-history targets, not inferred baseball companions.

# 6. NATO-era baseball scenes / CENTCOM Qatar

## Verified / promotable with source hierarchy

Thomas L. Friedman's *The World Is Flat* (2005) attributes to Burns a recollection from an April 2004 CENTCOM headquarters briefing in Qatar involving Gen. John Abizaid's staff. Burns's recollection describes military/intelligence imagery on three screens and a Yankees–Red Sox game on a fourth, involving Pedro Martínez and Derek Jeter.

This is a published secondary source relaying Burns's first-person recollection. It is not a contemporaneous primary record.

A likely baseball-date candidate is April 25, 2004, when Martínez started against the Yankees at Yankee Stadium and Jeter went hitless, but the exact date/game is **not established** without travel/visitor or briefing records. The screen could also have shown a delayed feed or replay.

## Other NATO visual/context evidence

An official Department of Defense/DVIDS photograph places Donald Rumsfeld, NATO Secretary General George Robertson, and Burns at NATO Headquarters in Brussels on **December 1, 2003**. Useful institutional visual; not a baseball scene.

A NATO transcript dated October 30, 2003 documents an organized public-diplomacy effort and names Burns among senior speakers. This supports the institutional public-diplomacy environment, not the proposition that baseball was a NATO instrument.

Source:  
https://www.nato.int/en/news-and-events/events/transcripts/2003/10/30/video-interview

A separate authenticated October 2003 Brussels episode in project research places Burns watching the ALCS Game 7 loss on Armed Forces television. Keep that episode separate from the unresolved October 2004 championship scene and preserve the exact source hierarchy attached to the focused note.

## Highest-value next retrievals

- CENTCOM visitor records, schedules, briefing photos, and audiovisual material for Burns's April 2004 visit.
- NATO / State travel-party records around April 25–27, 2004.
- AFN Europe records as availability/context evidence.
- U.S. Mission NATO calendars, newsletters, and oral histories.

# 7. China-era public diplomacy / Red Sox imagery

## Verified / strong first-person evidence

At an April 30, 2025 Fairbank/Belfer event, Burns said he deliberately wore his Red Sox hat in public whenever possible in China, naming places including the Great Wall, the Bund, Xiamen, Chengdu, and his 60 Minutes appearance. He described the hat as cherished/battered and discussed Dodgers caps / the Mookie Betts trade in the same broader baseball register.

Use this as Burns's retrospective first-person account. Do not convert it into proof that the hat produced a diplomatic effect.

## Official/public-domain image trail

Project research has identified:

- **October 22, 2024 Beijing baseball clinic image:** Burns in Red Sox cap/gear with young Chinese baseball participants. Official U.S. Mission/federal provenance. Treat “future Red Sox Nation” style caption rhetoric as the caption's voice, not proof of participant identity or sentiment.
- **July 4, 2023 Independence Day image:** Burns pitching a baseball at the embassy celebration; federal/public-domain provenance.
- **2023 image corpus:** an indexed image titled “Ambassador Burns holding Red Sox baseball cap”; individual event/date/original-post metadata should be recovered before strong publication use.
- **June 22, 2024 Great Wall image:** project research identified a date-specific federal-image record involving Burns and a German member of “Red Sox Nation.” Preserve exact original caption/status metadata before manuscript/image publication.
- Separate Great Wall “charter members” imagery remains provenance-sensitive until individual metadata is nailed down.

These images are evidence of visible public baseball/Red Sox identity. They are not evidence of MLB or Red Sox sponsorship unless a separate source establishes sponsorship.

## Public-diplomacy context

An October 11, 2023 China Town Hall transcript records Burns describing a severe decline in U.S.–China official/citizen contacts and emphasizing rebuilding student, NGO, academic, and citizen relationships as mission priorities/ballast. That is useful strategic context. Do not claim baseball programming was designed as a response unless an event record or Burns source says so.

A June 26, 2024 Reuters report records Burns alleging Chinese interference with numerous embassy public events and records the Chinese government's denial. Keep the allegation and denial separately attributed; do not extend the dispute to baseball events without evidence.

A University of Chicago Center in Beijing visit in October 2024 provides additional cultural-exchange context, not proof of baseball purpose or effect.

## Highest-value next retrievals

- Chinese-language coverage of the October 22, 2024 baseball clinic, especially local organizer/partner names.

# 8. State Department / pre-Greece Red Sox public identity

## Verified primary record

Project research has recovered multiple 1997 State Department transcripts showing baseball/Red Sox material entering Burns's formal spokesman role:

- April 1: Red Sox April Fools material.
- April 15: Burns opens with the score and discusses Mo Vaughn and Tim Wakefield.
- June 4: Roger Clemens / Boston bitterness.
- June 13: briefing index records baseballs autographed by the Secretary for journalists who attended Opening Day at Camden Yards — an institutional press-room baseball ritual, not Red Sox-specific.
- June 16: Burns gives a public family-fandom account involving his mother and father. Do not turn this into reconstructed childhood scenes.
- July 14: discussion of a canceled Cuban baseball-team visit enters substantive U.S.–Cuba policy exchange.

A December 1996 AP report preserved in the Texas Tech newspaper archive records Burns's podium reaction after Roger Clemens left Boston for Toronto; surviving video holdings should be checked before claiming the specific exchange is on tape.

A 1997 farewell report says President Clinton sent Burns an autographed Red Sox cap, AP reporter Barry Schweid gave him a Red Sox alarm clock, and a Greek journalist gave him ouzo. Useful evidence of a publicly legible Red Sox identity around the spokesman role.

A June 12, 2007 State Department transcript in Paris records Burns describing a special bond with Ambassador Craig Stapleton based partly on shared Red Sox allegiance/opposition to the Yankees before pivoting to U.S.–France relations. This is observable rhetorical use of baseball; do not claim it caused improved bilateral relations.

# 9. Global attribution and reconstruction rules

These rules supersede any tempting narrative inference:

1. **No invented dialogue, interiority, motives, reactions, composite scenes, or manufactured baseball anecdotes.**
2. A chronology overlap is not a scene.
3. Broadcast availability is not proof of listening/viewing.
4. A photograph proves only what its authenticated image/caption/metadata support.
5. Burns's fandom is not proof of Red Sox organizational action.
6. Institutional support is not proof of a specific deliverable or effect.
7. Later Burns recollections must remain Burns's recollections unless independently corroborated.
8. Secondary authors relaying Burns must be identified as such when source hierarchy matters.
9. **Libby Baylies firewall:** do not assign her baseball fandom, dialogue, memories, reactions, motives, attendance, viewing habits, or participation unless an authenticated source specifically belonging to or quoting her establishes it.
10. The existence of a spouse/family relationship is never evidence of shared baseball experience.
