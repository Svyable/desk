# Chapter 4 media and source provenance — Cairo

## Purpose

This pass looked for a publication-safe visual that would add geographic or documentary information to Chapter 4, not merely decorate the Cairo posting. The strongest candidate is a period-adjacent CIA cartographic object preserved by the Library of Congress. It is useful primarily as a source for an original project map rather than as a full-page facsimile.

## Candidate: 1985 CIA Cairo-centered world map

- **Title:** *Azimuthal equidistant projection centered on Cairo, Egypt, 30°03′N, 31°15′E*
- **Creator:** United States Central Intelligence Agency
- **Date:** 1985; map marking `800354 4-85`
- **Repository:** Library of Congress, Geography and Map Division
- **Catalog/resource URL:** https://www.loc.gov/resource/g3201b.ct002665/
- **LCCN:** 89690152
- **Rights posture:** U.S. federal-government work; 17 U.S.C. §105 generally excludes works of the U.S. government from U.S. copyright. The Library of Congress Geography and Map Division also describes its digitized collections as free to use and reuse unless a specific Rights Advisory says otherwise. Before reproducing the scanned sheet itself, confirm the item-level Rights & Access panel and download metadata at production time. For this book, the lower-risk and more useful treatment is an original redraw using factual geography rather than reproduction of the CIA artwork.
- **Credit if a scan is ultimately reproduced:** follow the item-level Library of Congress credit guidance; default repository credit is `Library of Congress, Geography and Map Division.`
- **Why it matters:** the map was made in 1985, at the end of Burns's 1983–85 Cairo posting. Centering the world on Cairo reverses the habitual Washington-centered visual frame and makes a chapter-level point without claiming anything about Burns's personal use of the map.
- **Evidentiary boundary:** this object establishes that the CIA produced a Cairo-centered azimuthal-equidistant map in 1985. It does **not** establish that Embassy Cairo, Ambassador Veliotes, or Nicholas Burns used or saw it.

### Recommended original figure

**Working title:** `Cairo was not the edge of the map`

Create a clean, grayscale-safe original azimuthal-style locator centered on Cairo. Mark only locations already doing argumentative work in Chapter 4: Cairo, Washington, Jerusalem, Beirut/Lebanon, the Red Sea/Ras Banas area, Israel/Sinai, and the broader Middle East. Do not trace the CIA sheet's typography, linework, layout, or decorative treatment. Use modern geographic coordinates/data or independently plotted points and simple great-circle relationships.

**Reader-facing purpose:** make the chapter's recurring change-of-vantage argument spatial. Washington is far from Cairo; Cairo is not peripheral to the regional system described in the chapter.

**Proposed caption:**

> **FIGURE — Cairo as the center, not the edge.** An original project map centered on Cairo, locating the places that shape Chapter 4's diplomatic environment: Washington, Jerusalem, Lebanon, Sinai and the Red Sea. The visual premise is informed by a 1985 CIA Cairo-centered azimuthal-equidistant map preserved by the Library of Congress; the project figure should be independently drawn rather than copied from the archival artwork. Source context: CIA, 1985; Library of Congress Geography and Map Division.

**Alt text:** `Original map centered on Cairo showing Washington across the Atlantic and the chapter's nearby regional reference points, including Jerusalem, Lebanon, Sinai and the Red Sea.`

**Placement:** after the paragraph beginning `Cairo was a large place to learn that machinery.` The map should precede the detailed Atherton/Veliotes discussion, so geography arrives before the chapter asks the reader to hold several officials and policy problems in memory.

**Print/EPUB treatment:** full text width; keep caption with figure; avoid labels smaller than normal footnote type at final print size; ensure all distinctions survive grayscale and high-contrast rendering. Do not use color as the only encoding.

## Supporting biographical verification

The archived Department of State biography for Burns states that he began his Foreign Service career in the Middle East and served as **Vice Consul and Staff Assistant to the Ambassador in Cairo between 1983 and 1985**. It is useful as an official-source check on the chapter's role/date language.

- U.S. Department of State archived biography, R. Nicholas Burns: https://1997-2001.state.gov/about_state/biography/burns.html
- State Department *State Magazine* appointment notice likewise says Burns began his Foreign Service career in Cairo in 1983 as vice consul and staff assistant to the U.S. ambassador: https://1997-2001.state.gov/publications/statemag/statemag_nov-dec97/appoint.html
- Office of the Historian career record: https://history.state.gov/departmenthistory/people/burns-r-nicholas

These official biographies establish roles and broad dates, not the sequence or overlap of the two Cairo assignments. The manuscript is correct to leave that narrower chronology unresolved unless a personnel record, contemporaneous embassy directory, or direct witness resolves it.

Chapter 4 already uses White House Photo Collection roll C19990 (02), February 14, 1984, documenting President Reagan's working visit with Egyptian President Hosni Mubarak. The catalog record names Nicholas Veliotes among the personal references and explicitly notes that not every listed person appears in every frame.

- Record: https://www.reaganlibrary.gov/archives/photo/c19990-02
- Photographer: Kightlinger
- Collection: White House Photo Collection / National Archives

The manuscript's present caption correctly says Burns is **not** placed in the meeting or frames. Preserve that distinction. The image is valuable as a documentary object showing the senior bilateral relationship that Embassy Cairo was servicing during Burns's posting, not as evidence of his presence.

## Rejected material

- **Generic Cairo skyline/street photography:** rejected as decorative and usually rights-complicated; it would also invite the reader to mistake an undated city image for Burns's Cairo.
- **Later portraits of Burns:** rejected for Chapter 4 because they visually import the senior diplomat into a chapter about a junior officer.
- **Modern embassy photographs:** rejected because the built environment may postdate the posting and would falsely imply continuity.
- **CIA map scan as an immediate manuscript insertion:** legally favorable, but rejected for now in favor of an original redraw. The archival sheet contains more cartographic information than the chapter needs, and reproducing the entire artifact would make the reader decode the source's design rather than the book's argument.

## Highest-value next archive search

Find a cataloged U.S. federal photograph or contemporaneous embassy document from **Cairo, 1983–85** whose item record identifies the date, location, federal creator, and event. Best targets are a State Department/USIA embassy exterior or working scene, a Veliotes–Mubarak diplomatic event in Cairo, or a declassified embassy organizational/visit document. Do not use an image merely because it is period-correct; it should show a piece of the machinery the chapter describes.
