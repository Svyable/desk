# April 2004 CENTCOM baseball-screen scene: source hierarchy and date boundary

## Finding

Thomas L. Friedman's *The World Is Flat* preserves a specific baseball scene attributed directly to Nicholas Burns. Friedman writes that Burns told him he was at U.S. Central Command headquarters in Qatar in April 2004 for a briefing by General John Abizaid and staff. Four flat-screen televisions were behind the briefing team: three carried Predator imagery from Iraq; the fourth carried a Yankees–Red Sox game. Friedman's account identifies the baseball matchup as Pedro Martínez versus Derek Jeter.

A searchable PDF copy reproduces the passage. This is useful scene evidence, but it is **secondary-source first-person relay**: Friedman is reporting what Burns told him. It is not a contemporaneous CENTCOM record or a transcript/audio recording of Burns.

Source:
- Thomas L. Friedman, *The World Is Flat* (2005), passage describing Burns at CENTCOM headquarters in Qatar in April 2004.
- Searchable copy surfaced at: https://g-city.sass.org.cn/_upload/article/files/c5/d3/35562efc4f4b8dcc467f989f901f/467b7b5a-891e-4bf2-a737-c2fadb7e099a.pdf

## What is established

- Burns was U.S. ambassador to NATO during this period.
- Friedman attributes to Burns a recollection of being at CENTCOM headquarters in Qatar in April 2004.
- The briefing was by General John Abizaid and staff.
- Burns described four screens, with three showing real-time Predator imagery from Iraq and one showing Yankees–Red Sox.
- Friedman/Burns identify Pedro Martínez and Derek Jeter in the baseball image.

The scene is unusually valuable because baseball is not reconstructed from Burns's general fandom. It appears inside a named, attributed recollection of an official trip.

## Date boundary

The passage gives **April 2004**, not a day.

Baseball chronology makes April 25, 2004 a strong candidate: Pedro Martínez started against the Yankees at Yankee Stadium that day and Derek Jeter was in the Yankees lineup. Boston won 2–0; Martínez threw seven scoreless innings.

That match does **not** by itself prove the CENTCOM screen was carrying that game. The television could have been live, delayed, replayed, or the Friedman/Burns recollection could compress details. A day-level date should not be assigned until travel, visitor, schedule, cable, press, or CENTCOM records place Burns at the Qatar headquarters on April 25.

## Manuscript-safe use

Safe:
- narrate the physical screen juxtaposition with explicit attribution to Burns as relayed by Friedman;
- identify Qatar and April 2004;
- use the scene as evidence that Red Sox baseball remained visually present inside Burns's NATO-era working world;
- note that three screens carried war imagery while a fourth carried baseball.

Not safe:
- silently convert Friedman's relay into a contemporaneous primary source;
- assert April 25 as the scene date;
- say Burns watched a complete game;
- describe his emotions, thoughts, attention beyond what Friedman reports, or conversations not in the source;
- infer that the baseball screen affected policy, briefing dynamics, morale, or relationships;
- invent reactions from Abizaid or other officers.

Nothing here authenticates any first-person material from Libby Baylies or establishes her presence, reaction, or baseball preferences.
