# Chapter 7 media/research pass — speaking for America

Accessed 2026-09-16. Bounded to Chapter 7, `Speaking for America`. This packet adds a publication-safe primary-source object and a rights-clean archival-photo lead. It does not alter manuscript prose or ingest a media binary.

## Why this pass

Chapter 7 turns Burns from an adviser inside government into an official required to explain American policy in public. The useful media is therefore not a generic State Department portrait. It should show or reproduce the machinery of public diplomacy: the podium, the words, the institutional setting, and the difference between speaking personally and speaking as the Department's spokesman.

## SPOKESMAN 01 — May 9, 1997 public-diplomacy address

**Object:** Nicholas Burns, `The Importance of Public Diplomacy`, Foreign Service Day, Dean Acheson Auditorium, Washington, D.C., May 9, 1997.

**Canonical source:** U.S. Department of State permanent electronic archive, Office of the Spokesman.

**URL:** https://1997-2001.state.gov/policy_remarks/970509.burns.html

**Provenance:** U.S. Department of State; Nicholas Burns, State Department Spokesman; delivered May 9, 1997; archived as an official Department release.

**Rights posture:** U.S. federal-government text. Treat as public-domain federal material under 17 U.S.C. §105 unless an item-level notice identifies third-party content. The archived page itself contains no third-party copyright notice for the speech text. For production, reproduce only short excerpts needed by the argument and credit the Department even though copyright permission is not required.

**What it establishes:**

- Burns was State Department Spokesman and Acting Assistant Secretary for Public Affairs in this period.
- The speech is a first-person primary source for how he publicly described the purpose and mechanics of public diplomacy while occupying the job.
- It can support Chapter 7's distinction between private advice and delegated public speech without relying on retrospective recollection.

**What it does not establish:**

- It is not a transcript of a daily press briefing.
- It does not prove that every principle Burns articulated was consistently followed in every briefing or crisis.
- It should not be mined for private motive or treated as an unfiltered description of internal deliberation.

### Recommended manuscript use

Use one short pull-quote or a small facsimile-style text object after the chapter has established the spokesman role. The design should look like an official archival document, not a motivational quotation card.

**Caption draft:** `PUBLIC DIPLOMACY 01 — Nicholas Burns speaking on “The Importance of Public Diplomacy,” Foreign Service Day, Washington, May 9, 1997. As State Department spokesman, Burns was not merely commenting on foreign policy; he was occupying one of the offices through which policy became public language. Source: U.S. Department of State, Office of the Spokesman.`

**Alt text:** `Excerpt from the U.S. Department of State's May 9, 1997 transcript of Nicholas Burns's Foreign Service Day address on public diplomacy.`

**Credit:** `U.S. Department of State, Office of the Spokesman, May 9, 1997.`

### Formatting

- Prefer live typeset excerpt plus source note over a raster screenshot; this remains legible in print and EPUB and avoids reproducing obsolete web chrome.
- Keep excerpt, caption, and credit together across page breaks.
- In EPUB, place the source note immediately after the excerpt rather than relying on spatial proximity.
- Do not exceed a short excerpt; the evidentiary value is Burns's own institutional language, not reproduction of the whole speech.
- Preserve original date and title exactly in the note.

**Creator/collection:** White House Photograph Office (Clinton Administration); photographer Bob McNeely; January 26, 1993.

**Rights posture:** federal White House Photograph Office work; public domain under 17 U.S.C. §105. The canonical NARA record should remain the production provenance rather than a Wikimedia derivative.

**Narrative value:** This image belongs primarily to the transition into the Clinton NSC years rather than the 1995–97 spokesman podium. It can establish Burns inside the presidential foreign-policy apparatus immediately before the public-facing State role. Do not miscaption it as spokesman-era imagery.

**Hold:** Do not ingest this photograph into Chapter 7 yet. Chapter 7's highest-value visual remains an item-level federal photograph of Burns at the State Department podium or in an identifiable 1995–97 briefing. The 1993 Oval Office sequence is rights-clean but temporally adjacent, not exact.

## Institutional chronology check

The State Department's archived Burns biography says he served as Department spokesman and Acting Assistant Secretary for Public Affairs from 1995 to 1997, gave daily press conferences on U.S. foreign-policy issues, accompanied Secretaries Warren Christopher and Madeleine Albright on foreign trips, and coordinated Department public outreach. This is a stronger institutional anchor than later secondary biography.

**Source:** https://1997-2001.state.gov/about_state/biography/burns.html

Treat `daily press conferences` as the Department's own biographical characterization. Do not silently convert it into a claim that Burns personally briefed every business day without checking the briefing archive.

## Production-safe research use

The May 1997 speech can enter the chapter now as text because the federal provenance and date are clear. The White House photograph can be used elsewhere if the manuscript needs the Clinton NSC setting, but Chapter 7 should wait for exact-period State podium imagery rather than accept a merely available picture.

2. Build a compact chronology from archived State briefing transcripts showing how one consequential policy issue moved across several days at the podium. The figure should visualize changing questions and official formulations, not imply that rhetoric caused policy.
3. Search Clinton Library trip photography for Burns traveling with Warren Christopher or Madeleine Albright only where the finding aid gives exact event/date provenance.
4. If no rights-clean podium photograph survives online, produce an original diagram of the State briefing-room information flow: internal policy process → spokesman preparation → briefing → press questions → public record. Keep it institutional, not decorative.

## Evidence boundary

The primary-source speech tells us what Burns publicly said public diplomacy was for. The archived biography tells us what office he held and how State characterized its duties. A White House photograph can establish presence in a photographed meeting. None of these sources by themselves establish private deliberation, personal motive, off-camera influence, or causal effect on policy.
