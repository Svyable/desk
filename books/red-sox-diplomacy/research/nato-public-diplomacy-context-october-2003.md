# NATO public-diplomacy context, October 2003

## Why this matters

The manuscript already has a directly attributed October 2003 Brussels baseball scene. This note adds contemporaneous institutional context without claiming that baseball itself was a NATO public-diplomacy program.

## Primary source

NATO, “Video interview,” 30 October 2003:
https://www.nato.int/en/news-and-events/events/transcripts/2003/10/30/video-interview

The official NATO transcript concerns the Alliance’s public-diplomacy strategy for 2003–04. It says NATO was convening public-diplomacy staff, delegations and allied-capital participants to coordinate efforts, and specifically names U.S. Ambassador to NATO Nicholas Burns among the senior speakers expected to address the audience. The interview describes target audiences as people who shape public opinion on NATO, security and defense questions.

## What this establishes

* In late October 2003, Burns’s formal NATO role included visible participation in an Alliance public-diplomacy environment.
* This is nearly contemporaneous with the authenticated 2003 Brussels Red Sox/ALCS material already in the project.
* The juxtaposition can strengthen the book’s argument that Burns’s sports identity existed alongside—not outside—his professional work of alliance representation and public communication.

## What this does **not** establish

Do not say that NATO used the Red Sox, baseball, or Burns’s fandom as an official public-diplomacy instrument on the basis of this transcript. The source contains no baseball reference.

Do not turn temporal proximity into a scene. It does not place Burns at a baseball viewing, identify companions, establish a conversation about baseball with NATO colleagues, or document audience reaction to his fandom.

Do not infer Burns’s motive for mentioning baseball in any other source. Where baseball and diplomacy overlap, attribute only what the underlying source actually records.

Nothing in this source supports any statement, memory, reaction, dialogue, motive, or baseball involvement by Libby Baylies.

## Manuscript-safe use

A defensible construction is that the Brussels baseball episode occurred during a period when Burns was simultaneously a senior alliance representative and a named participant in NATO’s organized public-diplomacy work. That is useful institutional context. It should remain separate from any stronger claim that baseball was itself a NATO diplomatic tactic.
