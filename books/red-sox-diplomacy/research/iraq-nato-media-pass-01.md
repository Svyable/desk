# Iraq / NATO media pass 01

## Scope

Bounded media-and-research enhancement for the Iraq retrospective and NATO material in *Red Sox Diplomacy*. This pass adds a rights-clear documentary image candidate and contemporary primary-source anchors around Nicholas Burns's role as U.S. ambassador to NATO. It does **not** treat later regret as evidence of what Burns privately believed in 2003, and it does not use generic combat imagery as a substitute for the institutional story.

## Recommended documentary asset — Burns, Rumsfeld, and Robertson at NATO, Brussels, December 2003

**What it shows:** U.S. Secretary of Defense Donald Rumsfeld, NATO Secretary General Lord George Robertson, and U.S. Ambassador to NATO R. Nicholas Burns in Brussels during the December 2003 allied defense-ministers meeting.

**Why it earns space:** The useful visual fact is institutional proximity. Burns is physically inside the NATO channel during the period in which the alliance was arguing over Iraq and considering a larger role there. This is more specific to the book than battlefield photography, a flag montage, or a generic image of Baghdad. It should not be captioned as an Iraq-war decision meeting: the surviving federal record describes an informal meeting during a NATO defense-ministers session about expanding NATO's mission.

**Canonical federal record:** https://www.dvidshub.net/image/9212035/secretary-rumsfeld-speaks-with-nato-secretary-general-lord-george-robertson-brussels

**Creator:** Tech. Sgt. Andy Dunaway, U.S. Air Force.

**Institution/repository:** Department of Defense / DVIDS, migrated DefenseLink/War.gov archive.

**Date:** The DVIDS item metadata lists December 2, 2003 while its supplied caption says December 1, 2003. Treat the exact day as a repository metadata conflict until resolved against the original VIRIN/archive record; caption as **December 2003** rather than silently choosing one date.

**Place:** Brussels, Belgium.

**DVIDS Photo ID:** 9212035.

**VIRIN:** 031202-D-D0439-4162 as currently displayed by DVIDS. Preserve this identifier in production notes, but verify it against the downloadable file metadata before final publication because the migrated record contains the date inconsistency above.

**Resolution:** 3008 × 1960 px.

**Rights:** DVIDS marks the item **PUBLIC DOMAIN** and identifies a U.S. Air Force photographer. Commercial book reuse is suitable on copyright status, subject to DVIDS/DoD non-copyright restrictions and no implication of endorsement. Do not extract or feature military insignia or agency marks as branding.

**Commercial-publication boundary:** Public-domain status does not mean the Department of Defense, NATO, Rumsfeld, Robertson, or Burns endorses the book. Keep the use documentary and editorial. Do not materially alter documentary content beyond ordinary crop/tonal production work, and record any crop.

## Contemporary primary source — Burns on NATO and the Greater Middle East, October 19, 2003

**Canonical State Department archive:** https://2001-2009.state.gov/p/eur/rls/rm/2003/25602.htm

In Prague on October 19, 2003, Burns publicly argued for a NATO oriented toward crises beyond Europe and discussed Afghanistan, Iraq, terrorism, and weapons of mass destruction. This is contemporary evidence for what Burns publicly advocated as NATO ambassador. It is preferable to reconstructing his 2003 position from later recollection.

**Evidence boundary:** The speech establishes Burns's public policy argument in October 2003. It does not establish his private doubts, private advice, or responsibility for the Bush administration's decision to invade Iraq. Keep those categories separate.

**Text rights:** U.S. Department of State federal-government text is generally public domain in the United States. Use short attributed excerpts where exact language matters; otherwise paraphrase and preserve the archive URL/date in notes.

## Retrospective primary source — Burns's 2011 Iraq judgment

Harvard Kennedy School preserves the citation and abstract for Burns's December 21, 2011 *Boston Globe* essay, “Iraq War Damaged US Credibility.” Burns states that he supported the initial invasion while serving as U.S. ambassador to NATO and later concluded that the war was a strategic miscalculation whose costs outweighed its benefits.

HKS record: https://www.hks.harvard.edu/publications/iraq-war-damaged-us-credibility

**Rights caution:** The underlying *Boston Globe* article is copyrighted newspaper material. Do **not** reproduce a page image, screenshot, or extended excerpt without permission. The HKS bibliographic record is useful for chronology and attribution; manuscript prose should paraphrase the retrospective judgment or quote only a very short phrase when necessary under the project's quotation policy.

**Chronology value:** This source establishes that Burns's public reversal was on record by **2011**, not merely in a 2026 retrospective. That distinction matters for Chapter 22's argument about revisability: later statements can deepen or sharpen the judgment, but the book should not accidentally imply that he first repudiated his Iraq position decades later.

## Suggested original figure — IRAQ / NATO 01: Position, institution, revision

If a photograph does not earn the space, use an original chronology rather than protected newspaper imagery.

**Structure:** original typography and geometric marks only.

- **2001–2005 — Brussels:** Burns serves as U.S. ambassador to NATO.
- **2003 — Iraq:** Burns publicly supports the invasion; NATO is deeply divided over Iraq.
- **19 Oct 2003 — Prague:** Burns publicly argues for a NATO role beyond Europe and toward the Greater Middle East.
- **Dec 2003 — Brussels:** Burns appears with Rumsfeld and Robertson during the NATO defense-ministers meeting.
- **22 Sep 2004 — NATO:** alliance ambassadors agree to expand NATO's Iraq training mission; contemporary U.S. reporting attributes the announcement to Burns.
- **21 Dec 2011 — retrospective:** Burns publicly calls Iraq a strategic miscalculation and damage to U.S. credibility.

**Design rule:** the line represents chronology, not moral progress. Do not turn the figure into a redemption arc or imply that later criticism cancels earlier advocacy.

## Production and formatting guidance

- Keep photograph, caption, and credit together as one object in print; never strand the public-domain credit on the next page.
- The 3008 × 1960 DVIDS files are adequate for substantial print placement, but proof the downloaded master at final physical size rather than relying on repository dimensions alone.
- In EPUB, image → caption → credit is the reading order. Alt text describes visible people/context and does not carry the evidentiary qualification; that belongs in the caption.
- Resolve the DVIDS December 1/December 2 metadata conflict before putting a day number in reader-facing copy. Until then, **December 2003** is the publication-safe date.
- Preserve DVIDS Photo ID, VIRIN, creator, federal repository URL, rights status, and any crop/modification in production provenance notes.
- Grayscale proof should preserve separation among the three officials and the room background without aggressive contrast that changes documentary appearance.
- Do not add NATO, DoD, Red Sox, or MLB logos to an original chronology. Dates, names, rules, and simple marks are enough.
- Do not use the 2011 *Boston Globe* page or Harvard's page design as a facsimile. The underlying article remains copyrighted.

## Rejected routes

- **AP/AFP/Getty NATO photographs:** strong documentary content, but licensing/editorial restrictions create unnecessary commercial-book permissions risk when federal DVIDS material covers the institutional scene.
- **Generic Iraq combat photographs:** even public-domain examples can pull the chapter toward war illustration rather than Burns's diplomatic responsibility, institutional role, and later revision.
- **“Jubilant Iraqis” imagery:** public-domain federal provenance may be clean, but selecting triumphal invasion imagery without a specific argument risks reproducing the administration's visual framing rather than clarifying the book.
- **Newspaper front pages:** copyright and layout rights are unnecessary complications.
- **NATO/Getty handouts:** repository availability does not itself establish a clean commercial-book license; use the DVIDS federal alternative instead.
