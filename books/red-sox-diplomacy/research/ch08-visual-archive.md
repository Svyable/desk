# Chapter 8 visual archive — The Briefing Room Bleachers

## Method

Chapter 8 is the first chapter where repetition itself becomes evidence. The Red Sox material matters less as a collection of jokes than as a recurring social register that other people learned well enough to activate, parody, and return to Burns.

The visual sequence therefore avoids a generic podium portrait. The strongest available farewell photograph is preserved in contemporaneous press coverage, but it is not treated as publication-cleared government photography. The chapter instead uses three original documentary objects built from sourceable events.

### Primary-source strengthening: June 1997

Two State Department transcripts make the chapter's central boundary unusually explicit and should be treated as primary evidence, not merely color:

- **June 4, 1997 — DPB #84:** https://1997-2001.state.gov/briefings/9706/970604db.html. Reporters open with Roger Clemens and the Red Sox; Burns jokes about Boston's bitterness, novenas, and Richard Holbrooke becoming Red Sox manager, then calls the event the “State Department daily briefing on baseball and foreign policy.” In the same briefing, while discussing the Pacific salmon dispute, he says Canada remains a best friend and neighbor despite Toronto having taken Roger Clemens from Boston. This is direct evidence for the chapter's distinction between personal baseball grievance and official bilateral policy.
- **June 16, 1997 — daily briefing:** https://1997-2001.state.gov/www/briefings/9706/970616db.html. A reporter asks why every Red Sox fan is “rabid.” Burns answers with the Calvinist/predestination joke and then gives the more important biographical detail: his mother was a Red Sox fan; his father said the club had broken his heart in the 1930s and advised the children not to root for it; Burns says he did not follow that advice. This supports family continuity of the fandom without requiring inference from later profiles.

**Quotation discipline:** The manuscript already paraphrases most of these exchanges. Keep direct quotations short and tied to the transcript. Do not convert Burns's Calvinist or Catholic jokes into claims about his theology.

## FAREWELL INVENTORY 01 — other people choose the symbols

**Local media:** `../media/ch08-farewell-inventory.svg`  
**Date:** July 22, 1997.  
**Primary corroboration:** White House press briefing, July 22, 1997: https://clintonwhitehouse6.archives.gov/1997/07/1997-07-22-press-briefing-by-mike-mccurry.html  
**State same-day record:** https://1997-2001.state.gov/policy_remarks/970722.shattuck.html  
**Contemporaneous reporting:** https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/politics/1997/07/23/as-burns-takes-bow-at-state-a-fan-sox-it-to-him/f7295a64-565a-4320-9a47-a9a75a068cc0/

Documented objects / gestures:

- an autographed Boston Red Sox cap from President Clinton;
- a Red Sox alarm clock from AP reporter Barry Schweid, keyed to Burns's loose briefing start times;
- a bottle of ouzo from Greek journalist Lambros Papantoniou, pointing toward Athens;
- Mike McCurry returning to the State podium to praise Burns's command of the briefing room.

**Narrative job:** The objects were not selected by Burns to brand himself. They were selected by other people to summarize the persona they had learned over two and a half years.

### Independent federal corroboration of the farewell

The Clinton White House archive records Mike McCurry's own briefing later on July 22, 1997. McCurry says he was late because he had been wishing Nicholas Burns farewell on his last day briefing at State and says that, in the Clinton administration, no one had commanded the State Department podium better. This independently corroborates McCurry's participation in the farewell without relying on the Washington Post photograph or caption.

**Source:** https://clintonwhitehouse6.archives.gov/1997/07/1997-07-22-press-briefing-by-mike-mccurry.html  
**Source class:** U.S. federal executive-branch transcript / primary record.  
**Reuse posture:** Text produced by U.S. government employees as part of official duties is generally not protected by U.S. copyright under 17 U.S.C. §105; nevertheless, quote only what the narrative needs and preserve source attribution. This rights posture does not extend automatically to third-party photographs or other contributed material appearing on a government site.

**Rights note:** The Washington Post farewell article includes a photograph of Burns and McCurry at the podium. Treat that image as **RESEARCH-ONLY** unless separate publication rights are obtained. Do not scrape or reproduce it in the book merely because the caption is useful.

## CIRCUIT OBJECT 01 — the Clemens joke comes back signed

**Local media:** `../media/ch08-clemens-circuit.svg`  
**Key source:** Washington Post, October 26/27, 1997: https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/politics/1997/10/27/a-farewell-curveball-for-states-spokesman/47b5db89-bd64-4361-b19e-161126e91e86/  
**Reporter callback source:** April 25, 1997 State briefing: https://1997-2001.state.gov/briefings/9704/970425.html

Documented circuit:

1. Burns makes a mock “treason” grievance after Roger Clemens leaves Boston for Toronto.
2. A reporter later throws Burns's own language back at him during an unrelated evidentiary exchange.
3. CBS State Department producer Charles Wolfson arranges for a photograph of Clemens in Toronto uniform.
4. Clemens signs the photograph with good wishes for Burns's new job and thanks for Burns's earlier remarks.

**Narrative job:** This is stronger than another quotation from Burns. The persona has become sufficiently shared that other people can carry it beyond the briefing room and return it from baseball itself.

**Guardrail:** The signed photograph itself has not been recovered as a publication-cleared artifact. The diagram therefore represents the documented chain and explicitly does not impersonate the missing original.

## Publication-safe image search result

A fresh search did **not** locate a 1995–July 1997 photograph of Burns at the State Department podium with both event-level provenance and publication-safe rights. The contemporaneous Washington Post farewell image remains evidentially strong but rights-restricted for our purposes. Later Harvard portraits, later NATO-era press photographs, and modern photographs of State Department interiors are rejected because they depict the wrong time, role, or room.

One Library of Congress photograph of the Harry S. Truman Building's Thomas Jefferson State Reception Room is downloadable, but it is a modern Carol M. Highsmith photograph of a different room. It should **not** be used in Chapter 8: legal availability does not cure documentary mismatch.

**Current decision:** keep the three original documentary graphics. Do not add a generic federal-building or modern Burns image merely to satisfy a photo quota.

## Formatting / accessibility guidance

- Keep each SVG with its caption in print/PDF; avoid a page break between image and caption.
- Preserve the manuscript's descriptive alt text rather than replacing it with filenames or “diagram.”
- The recurrence ledger must remain legible in grayscale and at approximately half-page width; meaning cannot depend on color alone.
- The farewell inventory should preserve object labels in text so the Red Sox cap, alarm clock, ouzo, and podium tribute remain understandable in EPUB/reflow and to screen-reader users.
- The Clemens circuit should read in a single directional sequence at phone width. If arrows collapse in a narrow reader, the numbered chain in this provenance note is the semantic fallback.
- Do not place a rights-uncleared photograph behind or beside these diagrams in a way that makes the original graphics look like derivative adaptations of that photograph.

## Why there is no full-page photo in this chapter yet

A real photograph would be valuable if it showed Burns in the 1995–97 briefing room with clear provenance and publication-safe rights. The currently located farewell photograph is useful as evidence but not yet cleared for reproduction. A later or generic podium portrait would add less than the documentary objects above and could imply the wrong room or date.

The correct next image-search target is therefore narrow: **State Department, White House, NARA, AP/press pool, or other rights-clearable photography of Nicholas Burns at the State Department briefing podium between 1995 and July 1997.** A useful hit must identify the event/date and photographer or federal creator; a search-result thumbnail or unattributed web image is insufficient.

## Visual restraint

Three objects are enough. The chapter already contains unusually dense verbal evidence. The design should make recurrence, collective memory, and the farewell handoff visible without converting the press room into a baseball scrapbook.
