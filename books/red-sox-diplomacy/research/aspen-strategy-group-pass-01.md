# Nicholas Burns and the Aspen Strategy Group — Research Pass 01

## Purpose

Close a real gap in *Red Sox Diplomacy*: Nicholas Burns's post-State Department years have been framed mainly through Harvard, but from July 2009 through December 2021 he also led the Aspen Strategy Group. That work matters because it kept him inside recurring foreign-policy networks, Track II dialogues, public national-security forums, and long-horizon relationship maintenance while he was outside government.

This pass asks four narrow questions:

1. What did Burns actually do at the Aspen Strategy Group?
2. Which parts of that work bear directly on the book's recurring themes of representation, channels, trust, rivalry, and diplomatic maintenance?
3. What is the strongest documentary bridge between the Aspen years and his later China ambassadorship?
4. Is there a documented Red Sox/Aspen scene, or would adding one require invention?

## Bottom line

The Aspen material belongs in Chapters 17 and 18, but not as a résumé paragraph. It supplies a missing institutional bridge between Burns the professor and Burns the ambassador.

The strongest finding is the China connection. The Aspen Strategy Group launched a U.S.-China Track II dialogue in June 2013 with the Party School of the Central Committee of the Communist Party of China. Aspen's institutional history says the dialogue had President Xi Jinping's explicit endorsement. More importantly for evidentiary balance, the U.S. Department of State's June 25, 2021 certificate of demonstrated competence for Burns's ambassadorial nomination independently highlighted that, at Aspen, he had organized a policy dialogue with the Chinese government's Central Party School. That makes the Aspen work part of the documented prehistory of the Beijing appointment without requiring a causal claim that Aspen produced the appointment.

No primary source located in this pass documents Burns using Red Sox identity inside Aspen Strategy Group programming. Do not manufacture a baseball scene. The absence is editorially useful: Aspen shows the relationship-maintenance method without the baseball register.

## Chronology

### July 2, 2009 — Burns becomes director

Aspen announced that Burns would become director of the Aspen Strategy Group, succeeding Kurt Campbell after Campbell entered the Obama administration as Assistant Secretary of State for East Asian and Pacific Affairs.

Primary institutional source:
- Aspen Institute, ["Aspen Strategy Group Welcomes Nicholas Burns"](https://www.aspeninstitute.org/news/aspen-strategy-group-welcomes-nicholas-burns/)

The announcement describes ASG as a bipartisan forum focused on major national-security and foreign-policy questions, with an existing U.S.-India dialogue and an Aspen Atlantic Group. This matters because Burns did not arrive to invent the institution; he inherited a mature convening structure and then altered it over time.

### 2009–2012 — annual policy workshops and publications

Burns's first years at ASG included the annual Summer Workshop and policy-book series. Harvard's publication record lists Burns as co-editor of the 2009 volume on the global economic crisis, the 2011 South Asia volume, the 2012 cyber-security volume, and the 2013 Arab revolutions volume.

Useful records:
- Harvard Kennedy School, [The Global Economic Crisis and Potential Implications for Foreign Policy and National Security](https://www.hks.harvard.edu/publications/global-economic-crisis-and-potential-implications-foreign-policy-and-national-security)
- Harvard Kennedy School, [American Interests in South Asia](https://www.hks.harvard.edu/publications/american-interests-south-asia-building-grand-strategy-afghanistan-pakistan-and-india)
- Harvard Kennedy School, [Securing Cyberspace](https://www.hks.harvard.edu/publications/securing-cyberspace-new-domain-national-security)
- Harvard Kennedy School, [The Arab Revolutions and American Policy](https://www.hks.harvard.edu/publications/arab-revolutions-and-american-policy)

These publications do not by themselves prove private diplomatic influence. They do show Burns repeatedly organizing structured argument among senior practitioners and experts around live strategic problems.

### January 2013 — the U.S.-India Track II dialogue continues

The Aspen Strategy Group and Aspen Institute India convened the eighteenth meeting of the U.S.-India Strategic Dialogue in New Delhi. Aspen's account dates the dialogue's origin to 2002, before Burns became director.

Primary institutional source:
- Aspen Institute, ["The U.S.-India Strategic Dialogue"](https://www.aspeninstitute.org/blog-posts/us-india-strategic-dialogue/)

Editorial boundary: Burns should not be credited with creating the India dialogue. The 2021 Aspen retrospective properly says he continued it.

### June 11–13, 2013 — U.S.-China Track II dialogue launches

Aspen records the launch of a bilateral U.S.-China Track II dialogue in June 2013 in partnership with the Party School of the Central Committee of the Communist Party of China. The current institutional page says the effort had President Xi Jinping's explicit endorsement.

Primary institutional source:
- Aspen Institute, ["The U.S.-China Track II Dialogue"](https://www.aspeninstitute.org/programs/aspen-strategy-group/the-u-s-china-policy-dialogue/)

The same page describes the objective as sustained, long-term engagement among high-level Chinese and American delegates outside formal government negotiation. That distinction is central for the manuscript: Track II participants can preserve contact, test arguments, and build networks, but they cannot bind governments.

### 2014 — U.S.-Brazil dialogue begins

Aspen says the Strategy Group began a U.S.-Brazil Track II partnership in 2014 with Centro de Liderança Pública, drawing on previous dialogue experience with Russia, India, and China.

Primary institutional source:
- Aspen Institute, ["The U.S.-Brazil Strategic Dialogue"](https://www.aspeninstitute.org/programs/aspen-strategy-group/the-u-s-brazil-strategic-dialogue/)

### 2017–2019 — transatlantic Track II dialogue

Aspen says ASG convened a U.S.-Europe Track II dialogue with the Aspen European Strategy Group in Washington in 2017 and 2019 and in Brussels in 2018. Subjects included the transatlantic relationship, emerging technology, Russia, China, trade, and NATO.

Primary institutional source:
- Aspen Institute, ["Transatlantic Strategic Dialogue"](https://www.aspeninstitute.org/programs/aspen-strategy-group/transatlantic-strategic-dialogue/)

### 2018 — Burns as public convener

An Aspen public event on August 3, 2018 records Burns moderating a discussion among former national security advisers Condoleezza Rice, Stephen Hadley, Susan Rice, and Thomas Donilon. This is useful as a visible example of the role: Burns was not only writing about policy from Harvard; he was convening people who had exercised national-security authority across administrations.

Primary institutional source:
- Aspen Institute, ["America and the World: A Conversation with Former National Security Advisors"](https://www.aspeninstitute.org/events/hurst-series-asg-panel/)

### 2019–2020 — U.S.-China becomes a central ASG subject

The Aspen Strategy Group devoted its 2019 annual meeting to the future of U.S.-China relations and published *The Struggle for Power: U.S.-China Relations in the 21st Century* in January 2020. Burns wrote the preface as ASG executive director.

Primary sources:
- Aspen Institute, ["How to Meet the China Challenge"](https://www.aspeninstitute.org/news/how-to-meet-the-china-challenge/)
- Aspen Institute, [The Struggle for Power PDF](https://www.aspeninstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/TheStruggleForPower.pdf)

This does not make Burns the author of every position in the volume. The editorial value is different: immediately before returning to government, he was still organizing sustained argument over China as a strategic relationship.

### 2010 forum / later merger — important attribution correction

The first Aspen Security Forum was announced in 2010 by the Aspen Institute's Homeland Security Program. Burns should not be described as the founder of the Forum based on the records in this pass.

Primary source:
- Aspen Institute, ["The Aspen Institute Announces 2010 Aspen Security Forum"](https://www.aspeninstitute.org/news/aspen-institute-announces-2010-aspen-security-forum/)

Aspen's 2021 retrospective says that during Burns's tenure the Aspen Strategy Group and Aspen Security Forum were merged. The safe manuscript claim is therefore that he oversaw the merger/integration, not that he created the Forum.

### 2020–2021 — mature program structure

By 2020–2021, Aspen described the Strategy Group as running an annual Summer Workshop, Track II dialogues with Brazil, China, Europe, and India, and the Aspen Security Forum. This is useful as a period snapshot independent of the later farewell summary.

Primary sources:
- Aspen Institute, [2020 Impact Report](https://www.aspeninstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/2020-Impact-Report.pdf)
- Aspen Institute, [2021 Aspen Security Forum agenda announcement](https://www.aspeninstitute.org/news/asf-2021-agenda-speakers/)

### June 25, 2021 — State Department independently identifies the Aspen-China work

The State Department's certificate of demonstrated competence for Burns's nomination to Beijing notes that he was executive director of the Aspen Strategy Group and Security Forum and specifically says that at Aspen he organized a policy dialogue with the Chinese government's Central Party School.

Primary U.S. government source:
- U.S. Department of State, ["Burns, R. Nicholas — The People's Republic of China — September 2021"](https://2021-2025.state.gov/burns-r-nicholas-the-peoples-republic-of-china-september-2021/)

This is the strongest non-Aspen corroboration found in the pass. It establishes that the U.S. government itself treated the Aspen-China work as relevant background when presenting Burns's qualifications to serve as ambassador.

### December 22, 2021 — Burns steps down to become ambassador

Aspen announced Burns's departure as executive director after his confirmation as U.S. ambassador to China. The Institute credits his tenure with expanding and diversifying membership; creating Track II dialogues with Brazil, China, and Europe; continuing the India dialogue; merging the Strategy Group and Security Forum; and inaugurating the Rising Leaders Program.

Primary institutional source:
- Aspen Institute, ["Nicholas Burns Stepping Down as Executive Director..."](https://www.aspeninstitute.org/news/nicholas-burns-anja-manuel-aspen-strategy-group/)

Evidence label: this is an institutional retrospective and should be cited as such. It is strong for Aspen's own account of its organizational history, but it is not independent evaluation of Burns's effectiveness.

### After Beijing — return to Aspen leadership

Harvard's current faculty profile identifies Burns as co-chair of the Aspen Strategy Group and Aspen Security Forum, alongside his return to Harvard after serving in China from 2021 to 2025. Aspen's current program page likewise lists Burns and Condoleezza Rice as co-chairs.

Current institutional sources:
- Harvard Kennedy School, [Nicholas Burns faculty profile](https://www.hks.harvard.edu/index.php/faculty/nicholas-burns)
- Aspen Institute, [Aspen Strategy Group](https://www.aspeninstitute.org/programs/aspen-strategy-group/)

This return belongs more naturally in Chapter 22 or the epilogue than in the Chapter 17 insertion.

## What this changes in the manuscript

### Chapter 17 — *The Professor in the Cheap Seats*

The chapter currently makes Burns's outside-government life feel overwhelmingly academic. That is incomplete. Add a compact Aspen section after the paragraph beginning "Burns did not withdraw from policy while teaching."

The section should do five things:

- date his appointment to July 2009;
- distinguish convening from delegated state authority;
- show the breadth of the Track II network without turning it into a list of achievements;
- identify the 2013 China dialogue as the most consequential bridge for the next chapter;
- explicitly avoid claiming that Aspen caused his later appointment or that private dialogues produced government policy.

A useful thematic formulation is that the professor was in the cheap seats only relative to state authority. Aspen gave Burns another kind of field position: he could keep people returning to the same room without being able to order an outcome.

### Chapter 18 — *Beijing*

The current opening says Burns spent thirteen years teaching diplomacy and then returned "inside" it. Revise the opening so it also acknowledges twelve years leading ASG and the 2013 China Track II dialogue. This strengthens the handoff into Beijing and makes the State Department's 2021 competence certificate especially valuable.

Do not overstate continuity. A Track II dialogue is not an embassy, and a Central Party School relationship is not the same thing as negotiating with the Chinese foreign ministry as ambassador.

## What not to claim

- Do not say Burns founded the Aspen Strategy Group. It dates to the 1980s, with antecedents earlier.
- Do not say Burns founded the Aspen Security Forum. The first forum was announced in 2010 by the Aspen Institute Homeland Security Program; the 2021 retrospective credits Burns with overseeing the later merger with ASG.
- Do not say Burns created the U.S.-India dialogue. It began in 2002; he continued it.
- Do not say the 2013 U.S.-China Track II dialogue directly produced official policy outcomes unless a separate source demonstrates a specific causal chain.
- Do not say Aspen caused Burns to be nominated as ambassador. The State Department record shows that the Aspen-China work was part of his documented qualifications, not that it determined the appointment.
- Do not turn Aspen's institutional praise into an independent assessment of effectiveness.
- Do not add a Red Sox anecdote to the Aspen section unless a primary or credible near-primary source is found.

## Red Sox-specific finding

This pass searched Aspen Institute records for combinations of Nicholas Burns, Red Sox, baseball, and Red Sox Nation. It did not locate a primary Aspen record in which Burns used his Red Sox identity as part of an Aspen Strategy Group event or Track II dialogue.

That is not proof that no such moment occurred. It is enough to set the current editorial boundary: there is no earned Aspen baseball scene in the record assembled here.

This is consistent with the book's evidence rule. Baseball should appear where the archive earns it, not because a chapter needs a motif.

## Open research routes

1. **2013 U.S.-China participant record.** Locate agendas, participant lists, contemporaneous photographs, or Chinese-side institutional records for the June 2013 dialogue.
2. **2014 Beijing follow-up.** The current Aspen page records a December 2014 Beijing meeting; find contemporaneous records rather than relying only on the current program history.
3. **Merger timing.** Pin down exactly when the Aspen Security Forum formally moved under the Strategy Group rather than using the broader "during Burns's tenure" formulation.
4. **Rising Leaders launch.** Establish the exact launch date and Burns's role from contemporaneous records.
5. **Aspen oral history.** Anja Manuel, Jonathon Price, Leah Bitounis, Joseph Nye, Condoleezza Rice, or other long-serving ASG participants could clarify Burns's working method as convener.
6. **Red Sox search.** Search archived video/transcripts of Burns-moderated Aspen events for spontaneous baseball references before concluding the register was absent in practice.

## Editorial conclusion

The Aspen years are not a separate chapter. They strengthen an existing one.

Chapter 17 becomes more accurate if Harvard is presented as one part of Burns's post-government life and Aspen as the other institutional half: one place where diplomatic practice became teachable, another where relationships could be maintained without formal authority. Chapter 18 then begins with a more precise reversal. Burns had spent more than a decade talking, teaching, convening, and running unofficial channels around problems governments had to solve. In Beijing, the words again belonged to the United States.
