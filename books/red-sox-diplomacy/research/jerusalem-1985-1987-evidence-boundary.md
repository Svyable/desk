# Jerusalem, 1985–1987: evidence boundary and retrieval targets

## What is verified

Authoritative U.S. government biographical material places Nicholas Burns at the American Consulate General in Jerusalem from 1985 to 1987 as a political officer. The George W. Bush White House archive states that he coordinated U.S. economic assistance to the Palestinian population in the West Bank and East Jerusalem. A later State Department biography independently describes the same 1985–1987 Jerusalem posting and economic-assistance responsibility.

Those records also identify Burns as a lifelong member of Red Sox Nation, but they do **not** establish any particular baseball episode in Jerusalem.

Sources:
- George W. Bush White House archive, “R. Nicholas Burns - Former Under Secretary of State for Political Affairs”: https://georgewbush-whitehouse.archives.gov/government/nburns-bio.html
- U.S. Department of State archive, “Biography: Nicholas Burns”: https://2009-2017.state.gov/s/p/fapb/185577.htm
- American Academy of Diplomacy member biography: https://www.academyofdiplomacy.org/members-1/burns/nicholas

## What remains unverified

This pass did not locate a contemporaneous or authenticated first-person source establishing:
- where Burns lived in Jerusalem;
- whether, where, or by what medium he followed the Red Sox during 1985–1987;
- a specific 1986 Red Sox/World Series viewing or listening scene;
- companions, reactions, dialogue, celebrations, disappointments, or interiority connected to the 1986 postseason;
- any use of baseball in Burns's official Jerusalem work.

The fact that the 1986 Red Sox season overlapped with his Jerusalem posting is chronology, not a scene.

## Manuscript boundary

Safe: Burns served in Jerusalem during 1985–1987, coordinating U.S. economic assistance in the West Bank/East Jerusalem; the 1986 Red Sox pennant season and World Series therefore occurred during that posting.

Not safe without additional evidence: reconstructing him watching, listening to, discussing, celebrating, or reacting to the 1986 postseason; identifying a venue or broadcast source; assigning baseball significance to his diplomatic work.

Do not use later general statements about lifelong Red Sox fandom as proof of a specific 1986 act.

## Libby Baylies / family attribution firewall

No source found in this pass authenticates a baseball memory, statement, reaction, motive, or scene by Elizabeth “Libby” Baylies in Jerusalem. Do not transfer Burns's later baseball statements to her or use unauthenticated first-person material as her testimony.

## High-value retrieval targets

1. Association for Diplomatic Studies and Training oral histories for Burns, Jerusalem Consulate colleagues, and chiefs of mission/consuls general serving 1985–1987.
2. State Department Foreign Affairs Oral History Collection and retired-FSO interviews for Jerusalem social life and media habits.
3. American Consulate General Jerusalem public-affairs files, newsletters, community bulletins, staff directories, photographs, and event calendars for 1985–1987.
4. AFRTS/AFN program schedules and contemporary expatriate press for evidence of whether the 1986 postseason was available locally. Availability alone must not be treated as evidence Burns consumed it.
5. Boston-area and diplomatic press interviews in which Burns was asked when/how he followed the 1986 team overseas.
6. Authenticated interviews with Burns or named Jerusalem colleagues specifically asking about October 1986.

## Editorial recommendation

Do not promote a Jerusalem baseball scene into the manuscript yet. Use the posting as verified chronology only until a source ties Burns himself to the 1986 Red Sox while in Jerusalem.
