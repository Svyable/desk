# October 20, 2004 — Lowe and Varitek change the plan in Game 7

## Finding

The decisive game of Boston's 2004 ALCS comeback contains a less familiar piece of battery-level improvisation beneath the famous home runs.

In MLB's tenth-anniversary reconstruction, Derek Lowe said that he and catcher Jason Varitek changed his usual approach for Game 7 because they believed the Yankees had become increasingly aggressive as the series progressed. Lowe recalled that they threw an unusually heavy diet of breaking balls — in his words, "almost exclusively" — to exploit that aggression. The account is retrospective and should remain attributed to Lowe rather than narrated as access to Varitek's private thinking.

Authoritative first-person retrospective:
- Major League Baseball, Ian Browne, “Game 7 rout capped historic comeback in '04,” October 20, 2014: https://www.mlb.com/news/red-soxs-game-7-rout-capped-historic-comeback-in-2004/c-99055554

MLB's reconstruction supplies an immediate observable example: Gary Sheffield was well in front of a breaking ball for the strikeout that ended Lowe's 1-2-3 first inning. Lowe ultimately worked six innings on 69 pitches, allowing one hit and one run, as Boston won 10–3.

The assignment itself was already irregular. Lowe had been left out of Boston's initial postseason rotation after an inconsistent regular season and poor September. Tim Wakefield's Game 3 relief work helped make Lowe available to start Game 4; Terry Francona then chose Lowe for Game 7 on two days' rest. The recent Wakefield dossier documents that roster handoff separately. This note isolates what Lowe and Varitek did once the emergency starter actually took the mound.

## Why this is narratively valuable

Game 7 is usually remembered through Johnny Damon's grand slam, David Ortiz's first-inning homer, and the final fact of the comeback. The Lowe-Varitek account reveals a quieter adjustment underneath the rout.

Boston did not simply ask a displaced starter to reproduce his normal game on short rest. According to Lowe's later account, pitcher and catcher read a change in the opposing hitters across the series and altered the pitch mix accordingly. The first inning gives the reader a concrete receipt, and the final line gives the scale: six innings, 69 pitches, one hit, one run.

For knowledgeable Red Sox readers, this is useful because it restores Jason Varitek's operational role without inflating it into mythology. Catching was part of the decision system. Lowe's retrospective credit to Varitek can be presented as Lowe's account; the manuscript need not invent mound conversations, signs, scouting meetings, or a pregame conference.

It also complements rather than duplicates the recent 2004 dossiers. Wakefield explains why Lowe was available. Roberts explains opponent-specific video preparation. Schilling explains medical improvisation. LeVangie explains the bullpen communication chain. This pass adds the battery's in-game tactical adjustment in the final elimination game.

## Documentary boundary

Safe:

- MLB records that Lowe started Game 7 on two days' rest after being left out of the initial postseason rotation;
- Lowe retrospectively said he and Varitek changed the usual approach because Yankees hitters had become increasingly aggressive;
- Lowe recalled throwing an unusually high proportion of breaking balls and described the approach as almost exclusive;
- MLB's reconstruction identifies Sheffield's first-inning strikeout on a breaking ball as an early example;
- Lowe pitched six innings, allowing one hit and one run on 69 pitches;
- Boston won 10–3 and completed the first comeback from a 3–0 deficit in a Major League postseason series.

Not safe:

- reconstructing a pregame strategy meeting unless a source establishes one;
- inventing dialogue, pitch signs, mound conversations, or Varitek's interior reasoning;
- treating Lowe's retrospective estimate of the pitch mix as pitch-tracking data;
- claiming the breaking-ball plan caused New York's defeat or the series comeback;
- assigning the plan to Terry Francona, Dave Wallace, advance scouts, or analysts without additional evidence;
- turning the adjustment into a generic lesson about diplomacy, adaptability, leadership, or intelligence work.

## Manuscript recommendation

Use if the 2004 sequence needs one compact manager/battery beat after the roster improvisations are established. The clean narrative shape is: Lowe reaches Game 7 on two days' rest after beginning October outside the rotation; he later says he and Varitek saw the Yankees becoming more aggressive and changed the pitch mix; Sheffield's first-inning strikeout supplies an example; Lowe exits after six innings and 69 pitches with Boston in control.

Do not tell the reader what this symbolizes. The baseball decision is sufficiently interesting on its own.

## Evidentiary level

**Authoritative MLB retrospective with named first-person participant recollection, plus MLB game/result record.** Strong for Lowe's own account of the tactical adjustment and the observable pitching line; retrospective for the stated rationale and approximate pitch-mix description, and not evidence of Varitek's unspoken reasoning or a formal staff directive.
