# Boston busing federal-records media pass 01

## Purpose

Publication-safe documentary enrichment for the early Boston material in *Red Sox Diplomacy*. This pass prioritizes records that make the 1974–76 school-desegregation crisis physically and institutionally legible without implying that Nicholas Burns personally witnessed, participated in, or formed views about events for which the manuscript has no evidence.

## Cleared primary-source route: National Archives federal court records

The National Archives' presentation **The Federal Government and Black Education** includes a Boston busing/desegregation section and identifies four concrete federal-record objects from the litigation and implementation record:

- **Tallulah Morgan et al. v. James W. Hennigan et al., Complaint** — National Archives Identifier (NAID) **4713835**, from *Civil Action Case Files, 1938–1998* (series NAID 568007).
- **Massachusetts Department of Education Decision on Delayed Opening of School Year** — NAID **4713934**.
- **Letter from the Massachusetts Black Caucus to Judge W. Arthur Garrity, Jr.** — NAID **4713898**.
- **Memorandum of the United States Regarding Deployment of United States Marshals in Boston Public Schools** — NAID **4836722**.

Source authority: U.S. National Archives and Records Administration (NARA).

Source URL: https://www.archives.gov/files/calendar/know-your-records/handouts-presentations/ligon-presentation-black-education.pdf

Rights/provenance status: the cited objects are records held by NARA and are suitable as **research and redraw/facsimile candidates**, but do not infer blanket public-domain status merely from federal custody. Before reproducing a scan, inspect the item-level catalog record and the document's authorship. In particular, a federal court filing can contain material created by non-federal parties. The U.S. Marshals memorandum is the strongest likely federal-work candidate, but still requires item-level confirmation before binary ingestion. Until then, use the records as factual/chronological sources or create an original text-based chronology rather than reproducing scans.

### Why this is stronger than another protest photograph

The existing research already has a rights-cleared March 19, 1975 Library of Congress photograph of Boston/Charleston demonstrators in Washington. These NARA objects move the reader inside the machinery of the crisis: complaint, state decision, Black Caucus letter, federal enforcement memorandum. They can show that desegregation was simultaneously litigation, state administration, political pressure, and federal enforcement rather than a single iconic street confrontation.

Commercial-publication assessment: acceptable candidate subject to the Library's normal reminder that users remain responsible for third-party rights. The item-level record explicitly supplies the “No known restrictions on publication” advisory. No trademark or publicity-rights issue is apparent from the architectural-model subject.

Use only if the manuscript needs to establish the institutional geography of Boston's Government Center. It is not evidence of the school-desegregation conflict and should never be captioned as such. Its value is geographic/institutional context: a period object showing the civic architecture being built around the city in which the crisis unfolded.

## Rights discipline and exclusions

Do not treat every photograph reproduced in a NARA slide deck as a federal public-domain image. The deck is an authoritative research locator, not a blanket license for embedded third-party photography. Resolve any photograph back to an item-level catalog record before use.

Do not substitute famous 1974 Boston press photographs whose copyright remains with photographers, agencies, estates, or publications merely because copies appear in archives or government presentations. The project should prefer the federal documentary record or an original visualization when rights are not explicit.

Do not use the four-record strip to imply a simple causal sequence unless dates from the item records establish one. The present figure concept is institutional, not yet a day-by-day chronology.

## Research strengthened

This pass gives the Boston section a source path beyond retrospective accounts and iconic photography. The NARA identifiers make four primary records directly traceable and separate four actors/functions: litigants, Massachusetts education authorities, Black elected officials, and the federal enforcement apparatus. That distinction is useful to the book's larger interest in how institutions translate conflict into decisions and action.

It also preserves the necessary biographical boundary: these records establish Boston's civic and institutional environment, not Nicholas Burns's personal relationship to desegregation.
