# Deep Baseball Primary-Source Pass 10 — diaspora before the roster, Baseball Acropolis, and direct Burns contact

## Purpose

The Greece baseball story becomes less accurate whenever the Greek-American diaspora appears only after Nicholas Burns asks for help.

Several sources now show a broader sequence:

- Burns was already publicly cultivating Greek-American institutional ties in 1998;
- Chris Karalekas says Baseball Acropolis began independently in 1998, inspired by Harry Agganis;
- Baseball Acropolis met the Hellenic Amateur Baseball Federation in January 1999;
- its surviving photo archive documents direct contact among Karalekas, Burns, MLB International, the Orioles contingent, the Greek federation, and unidentified “Boston businessmen.”

This does not identify the missing Red Sox contribution. It does establish another grassroots/diaspora lane that converged with Burns rather than originating with him.

## 1. October 1998 — Burns was already working the Greek-American bridge

The *Orthodox Observer* reports that Burns, then ambassador to Greece, made an official visit to the Greek Orthodox Archdiocese in New York on October 27, 1998.

Source:
https://fliphtml5.com/iput/gvbs/Orthodox_Observer_-_November_5%2C_1998/

The report says Burns discussed what the United States was trying to do to strengthen the relationship with Greece. It says Archbishop Spyridon welcomed Burns's interest in helping the Greek-American community increase its relationships with Greece and Greek people around the world.

### Why this matters

This predates the best-documented 1999 baseball-network activity.

It does not show that Burns visited the Archdiocese *because* of baseball. It shows that mobilizing and strengthening transatlantic Greek-American ties was already part of the representational environment in which the baseball project later developed.

That makes the baseball-diaspora brokerage less anomalous. Burns was already treating Greek Americans as a consequential bridge between the societies.

## 2. 1998–January 1999 — Baseball Acropolis begins independently

Baseball Acropolis says founder Chris Karalekas, inspired by the life of Greek-American Red Sox figure **Harry Agganis**, decided in 1998 to dedicate himself to bringing baseball to Greece.

Source:
https://www.baseballacropolis.org/About_us.htm

The organization says Karalekas and his cousin Gus first met the Hellenic Amateur Baseball Federation on **January 11, 1999**, at Olympic Stadium in Athens.

### Why this matters

The date lands before some later institutional histories place the federation's major turn to Burns, Sarbanes, Angelos and MLB.

Karalekas therefore belongs beside Samiotes and Galatis as evidence that Greek-American baseball initiatives were already developing on independent tracks.

Do not call any one of them “the founder” of Greek baseball. Their own origin stories overlap with a Greek federation that had already formed.

## 3. The Baseball Acropolis photo archive documents direct Burns contact

The organization's surviving photo archive includes an image captioned:

**“U.S. Ambassador to Greece R. Nicholas Burns with Chris and Gus Karalekas.”**

Source:
https://www.baseballacropolis.org/photos.htm

The same page contains photographs/captions showing:

- Peter Angelos with Karalekas at an Athens press conference about national-team recruitment;
- Jim Small of MLB International with Karalekas at MLB offices;
- a meeting at MLB's Park Avenue offices involving Syd Thrift, Chris Karalekas, Lou Angelos and Tom Marudas;
- Chris Karalekas with Panos Mitsiopoulos and Dimitri Gousios of the Greek federation;
- a group described as Hellenic federation members together with **MLB, the Baltimore Orioles contingent, “Boston businessmen,” and Chris**;
- youth clinics and equipment deliveries in Greece.

### What the archive establishes

At minimum, Baseball Acropolis was not a distant parallel effort. Its founder had documented direct contact with Burns and with multiple institutional nodes already central to the manuscript.

### What it does not establish

The captions do not identify the “Boston businessmen.”

Do not assume they were Chuck Samiotes, Bill Galatis, Red Sox employees, or any other specific people without a caption, contemporary article, or independent identification.

That photograph is now a high-value visual-identification target.

## 4. Harry Agganis sits upstream in another diaspora lane

Karalekas says Harry Agganis's life inspired Baseball Acropolis.

The photo archive separately shows Karalekas meeting **Mike Agganis**, Harry Agganis's nephew, whom the caption calls a connection to Karalekas's “spiritual hero.”

This matters because Agganis already appears elsewhere in the project as a durable Greek-American / Red Sox bridge figure and later becomes the focus of Bill Galatis's 2018 Fenway heritage initiative.

### Guardrail

Do not turn Agganis into the hidden institutional origin of the Burns-era Red Sox pledge.

The evidence supports cultural inspiration and later community memory. It does not prove Red Sox front-office mechanics.

## 5. The timeline now looks less linear

A more accurate late-1990s sequence is:

- **1997** — Greece wins the 2004 Olympics; Greek federation forms; Samiotes independently introduces baseball to relatives in Greece.
- **1998** — Samiotes returns with equipment; Karalekas launches Baseball Acropolis; Burns is publicly engaging Greek-American institutions around stronger U.S.-Greece / diaspora ties.
- **January 1999** — Baseball Acropolis meets the Greek federation.
- **1999** — Burns/federation reception brings Angelos into the visible professional-baseball network; contemporary reporting says the commissioner's office and Red Sox are helping.
- **1999** — Samiotes Consultants says it is simultaneously working with MLB and the Red Sox on Jim Rice Field in Boston.
- **2000 onward** — MLB International development, local league growth, diaspora recruitment and professional scouting increasingly overlap.

The chronology argues against a one-call founding myth.

## 6. The phrase “Boston businessmen” is now an archival target

The Baseball Acropolis caption is especially valuable because it distinguishes:

- MLB;
- Orioles contingent;
- Boston businessmen;
- Chris Karalekas.

If the Boston businessmen can be identified, they could clarify whether the visual archive contains Samiotes/Galatis, other Greek-American donors, or people with direct Red Sox connections.

## 7. Manuscript implication

Chapter 10 already says the embassy was not activating empty space.

This pass makes that statement stronger:

- Greek domestic organizers had formed their own federation;
- Samiotes had a family/grassroots origin story;
- Karalekas had an Agganis-inspired nonprofit origin story;
- Burns had a broader Greek-American bridge-building agenda;
- these lanes converged with MLB, Angelos/Orioles and the Red Sox on different timelines.

The story's diplomatic value is brokerage among actors with independent agency, not invention of a constituency by the ambassador.

## Documentary rule

Do not let photographs tempt the book into overidentification.

A caption identifying Burns and Karalekas is evidence of contact. A caption saying “Boston businessmen” is evidence that Boston businessmen were present. It is not permission to name them from resemblance.

The archive is most useful when it sharpens the next question rather than pretending the next question is already answered.
