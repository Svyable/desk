# Deep Baseball Primary-Source Pass 05 — loyalty, reciprocity, and the Clemens test

## Purpose

This pass sharpens the State Department baseball material by asking a narrower question than “how often did Nicholas Burns mention the Red Sox?”

The better question is: **what values did Burns put inside the baseball jokes, and how did the room learn to use those jokes back at him?**

Three source clusters matter: Roger Clemens leaving Boston in December 1996; reporters reviving the “treason” joke in April 1997; and Burns's June 1997 ability to separate mock baseball grievance from an actual friendly bilateral relationship with Canada.

## 1. December 1996 — Burns makes the Clemens dispute about lifetime loyalty

Contemporary Associated Press coverage of a routine State Department briefing records Burns's mock denunciation of Roger Clemens after the pitcher left Boston for Toronto.

The familiar part is already in the manuscript: Burns called Clemens a “traitor,” jokingly declared that the official State Department position, and said a move to the Yankees would have been worse.

The more revealing part is the standard Burns chose for comparison.

He said Clemens would never be able to say what **Ted Williams and Carl Yastrzemski** could say: that they were lifelong Red Sox.

Sources:
- contemporaneous AP text preserved in the *Hereford Brand*, December 17, 1996:
  https://newspapers.swco.ttu.edu/bitstream/handle/20.500.12255/76208/Hereford_Brand_1996_12_17.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1
- contemporaneous sports report preserving the same Williams/Yastrzemski comparison:
  https://www.sfgate.com/sports/article/rangers-bet-farm-on-wetteland-3109588.php
- SFGate “Top of the Sixth” version preserving the State Department joke and Canada/Yankees distinction:
  https://www.sfgate.com/sports/article/TOP-OF-THE-SIXTH-2955829.php

### Why this matters

The Clemens bit was not merely tribal anger at a player leaving.

Burns chose two franchise figures—Williams and Yastrzemski—as the counterexample because they represented continuity with one club. In his joking baseball moral universe, the valuable category was not simply greatness. Clemens was already great. The category was **staying**.

That does not license a claim that Burns's diplomatic worldview was formed by player loyalty. It does make the baseball persona more specific. His Red Sox language repeatedly organized around inheritance, continuity, belonging, and staying with an affiliation through disappointment.

That is a better description than generic “sports fan.”

### Manuscript use

Chapter 8 should add the Williams/Yastrzemski comparison immediately after the first Clemens “traitor” episode. It gives the joke content before the chapter later reaches Burns's family inheritance.

## 2. April 25, 1997 — the press corps throws “treason” back at him

The strongest new evidence of reciprocity comes from the official State Department transcript on April 25, 1997.

During a serious exchange about whether there was adequate evidence for allegations against American citizens, a reporter interrupts the logic by reminding Burns:

> “You accused Roger Clemens of treason not too long ago.”

Burns instantly accepts the premise of the joke and says the Clemens case had already been confirmed. Another reporter points out that Clemens merely worked for the Toronto baseball team. Burns then volunteers that the Red Sox had beaten Baltimore 2–1 in twelve innings the previous night and teases an Orioles-supporting reporter in the room.

Primary source:
https://1997-2001.state.gov/briefings/9704/970425.html

### Why this is stronger than another Burns baseball remark

Burns did not introduce baseball here.

A reporter did.

The exchange therefore proves something the manuscript has mostly inferred from recurrence: the press corps remembered Burns's baseball vocabulary well enough to use it as a counterexample inside a completely unrelated policy argument.

The baseball persona had become **reciprocal shorthand**.

That matters because a self-announced persona can be branding. A persona that other people recall, tease, and redeploy has become social memory.

### Editorial guardrail

Do not romanticize this into friendship or trust. The same journalists continued to question Burns aggressively on real policy. The point is narrower: shared ritual coexisted with scrutiny.

## 3. June 4, 1997 — baseball grievance and actual Canada policy are explicitly separated

The June 4 briefing contains the already-used “Novenas for the Red Sox” and patron-saint-of-lost-causes material.

The full transcript adds another useful detail.

After joking that Toronto had taken Clemens from Boston, Burns immediately distinguishes the baseball grievance from the real bilateral relationship: the United States and Canada remain close friends and neighbors.

Primary source:
https://1997-2001.state.gov/briefings/9706/970604db.html

### Why this matters

The manuscript's larger argument depends on Burns being able to move between personal performance and official authority without confusing them.

Here the transcript almost annotates that distinction for us:

- Toronto baseball club: mock grievance;
- Canada: close ally/friend/neighbor;
- same speaker, same briefing, different register.

It is a small but unusually clean example of the boundary Chapter 8 is trying to describe.

## 4. The Clemens episode ultimately becomes a loop, not a one-off joke

The later record now forms a complete sequence:

1. **December 1996** — Burns calls Clemens a traitor and invokes Williams/Yastrzemski as lifelong Sox.
2. **April 1997** — a reporter reuses Burns's own “treason” accusation during an unrelated policy exchange.
3. **June 1997** — reporters return to Clemens; Burns jokes about Boston bitterness, novenas, and lost causes while explicitly keeping the Canada relationship separate.
4. **October 1997** — after Burns leaves the spokesman's job, CBS State producer Charles Wolfson arranges a Roger Clemens-signed Toronto photograph thanking Burns for his “kind words.”

The point is no longer simply that Burns made a recurring joke.

The joke moved among **Burns -> reporters -> Burns -> baseball player**.

That is documentary evidence of a public identity being socially maintained by the people around him.

## 5. A useful Red Sox value structure emerges

Taken with other primary sources already in the project, Burns's Red Sox language now has a more precise internal structure:

- **inheritance** — mother supported the Sox; father warned the children away after decades of heartbreak;
- **place** — Wellesley Little League at Bates School; Wellesley Babe Ruth League;
- **loyalty** — Williams and Yastrzemski as lifelong Red Sox contrasted with Clemens leaving;
- **suffering** — novenas, lost causes, later “we identify with suffering”;
- **hope** — failed 2002 championship prediction;
- **redemption** — 2004 title in Burns's 2005 language;
- **community** — later Massachusetts / Red Sox Nation language;
- **portability** — Paris, Beijing, classrooms, diplomatic events.

This is richer than using baseball only as a metaphor for patience.

## 6. Rob Derksen — a separate, intriguing Red Sox link in the Greece network

A contemporaneous AP obituary carried by *Kathimerini* after Rob Derksen's death says he had managed in both the Milwaukee Brewers and **Boston Red Sox minor-league systems** before becoming a Pacific Rim scout for the Baltimore Orioles and manager of Greece's Olympic team.

Source:
https://www.ekathimerini.com/sports/23715/greece-s-baseball-team-coach-dies-at-44/

Independent minor-league records identify the specific Boston assignment: Derksen managed the **1997 Sarasota Red Sox**, Boston's Class A Florida State League affiliate.

Source:
https://www.statscrew.com/minorbaseball/roster/t-ss14430/y-1997

### Why this is interesting

The Greece baseball network therefore contains another Red Sox connection beyond Burns and the club's reported support: the operational baseball figure who later built the Greek Olympic roster had himself managed inside Boston's farm system.

### Why it must not be overclaimed

There is currently **no evidence that Derksen's prior Boston employment explains the Red Sox organization's reported support for Greek baseball**, or that Burns recruited him through a Red Sox connection.

The documented chain still points to Peter Angelos / the Orioles as the route through which Derksen took on the Greek project.

This is a biographical/network resonance, not the missing answer to the Red Sox-support question.

## Documentary rule

The baseball material is strongest when it is allowed to remain baseball.

Burns admired lifetime Red Sox affiliation in the Clemens joke. That does not prove a diplomatic theory of loyalty. Reporters remembered the joke. That does not prove personal friendship. Derksen once managed in the Red Sox system. That does not explain Boston's later institutional assistance to Greece.

Use each fact at the level the source earns.
