# NATO era — CENTCOM Qatar, April 2004: Red Sox on the fourth screen

## Finding

Thomas L. Friedman's *The World Is Flat* preserves a highly specific second-hand account attributed directly to Nicholas Burns from Burns's NATO tenure. Friedman writes that Burns, then U.S. ambassador to NATO, told him about an April 2004 briefing at U.S. Central Command headquarters in Qatar with General John Abizaid and his staff. According to Friedman's account, four flat-screen displays were behind the briefing team: three showed real-time Predator imagery from Iraq, while the fourth carried a Yankees–Red Sox game. Friedman says Burns's attention was drawn to the baseball screen and identifies the baseball matchup as Pedro Martinez versus Derek Jeter.

Discovery/verification source:
- Thomas L. Friedman, *The World Is Flat* (book text preserved in searchable scans/editions). A searchable PDF copy exposing the passage is currently indexed at: https://g-city.sass.org.cn/_upload/article/files/c5/d3/35562efc4f4b8dcc467f989f901f/467b7b5a-891e-4bf2-a737-c2fadb7e099a.pdf

## Evidentiary classification

**Named secondary source reporting a first-person account from Burns.**

This is stronger than an anonymous anecdote because Friedman explicitly identifies Burns as the person who told him the story. It is nevertheless not a contemporaneous CENTCOM transcript, photograph, visitor log, briefing record, Burns diary, recording, or direct published interview with Burns. Treat the scene as **Burns-to-Friedman recollection**, not omniscient fact.

Before final publication, verify wording and pagination against an authorized print or ebook edition of *The World Is Flat*. Do not quote from an unauthorized online reproduction.

## Why this materially strengthens the manuscript

This is the clearest NATO-era baseball scene recovered so far because baseball and official work occupy the same documented recollection rather than being stitched together from independent sources. Burns's own story, as relayed by Friedman, places a Yankees–Red Sox game literally beside live operational imagery during an Iraq briefing at CENTCOM.

The value is not that baseball somehow affected policy. It is that the anecdote demonstrates how portable Burns's fandom had become by 2004: even inside a senior military briefing far from Boston, the Red Sox game was salient enough for him to remember and later recount.

The juxtaposition also gives the manuscript a strong image without requiring invented sensory detail: four screens; three carrying Predator imagery from Iraq; one carrying Yankees–Red Sox; Burns in a briefing led by General Abizaid. Anything beyond those sourced elements should be omitted.

## Date-resolution opportunity

Friedman gives **April 2004**, not an exact date. Because he identifies Pedro Martinez and Derek Jeter as the visible matchup, the next research step is to reconcile the 2004 Red Sox–Yankees schedule, Martinez starts, CENTCOM/DoD travel records, and any Burns/NATO itinerary. If those independent records converge, the date may be narrowed without inference from prose alone.

Do not assign an exact game until that reconciliation is complete.

## What this does not establish

Do not use the Friedman passage to claim:

- that Burns ignored or was distracted from the military briefing;
- that the baseball screen was placed there for Burns;
- that General Abizaid or his staff were Red Sox or Yankees fans;
- that anyone discussed baseball during the briefing;
- what Burns said, felt, thought, or did while watching beyond Friedman's reported characterization;
- that the scene occurred on a specific April date without independent calendar corroboration;
- any causal connection between baseball and U.S. policy, NATO policy, or military operations;
- any memory, reaction, dialogue, fandom, or presence by Libby Baylies.

## Manuscript use

This is strong enough for promotion into the NATO chapter **with explicit attribution to Friedman/Burns**. Safe form:

> Thomas Friedman later wrote that Burns told him about an April 2004 briefing at CENTCOM headquarters in Qatar with General John Abizaid's staff. Three screens behind the briefers carried live Predator imagery from Iraq. A fourth carried a Yankees–Red Sox game. Friedman remembered Burns, a Red Sox loyalist, focusing on that fourth screen too.

The manuscript should resist Friedman's own rhetorical contrast if it overstates the scene. The documentary point is the coexistence of war-room technology and baseball, not a claim that Burns treated them as equivalent.
