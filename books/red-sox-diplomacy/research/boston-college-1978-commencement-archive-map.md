# Boston College 1978 — commencement archive map and documentary boundary

## Finding

Boston College's John J. Burns Library finding aids expose two unusually concrete archival routes for Nicholas Burns's graduation year.

The Office of Public Relations **Commencement, 1978** file is open for research and is physically identified as **Box 36, folders 37–39, and Box 43, folder 4**. The collection-level description says commencement files can contain programs, newspaper clippings, press releases, speech typescripts, graduate lists, honorary-degree material, and anniversary-class lists.

Primary archival finding aid:
- Boston College John J. Burns Library, "Commencement, 1978": https://findingaids.bc.edu/repositories/2/archival_objects/162944

A separate Boston College Commencement materials collection contains individual and group photographs of graduates and attendees, programs, contact sheets, prints, slides, invitations, honorary-degree lists, and some commencement addresses; portions are digitized. The finding aid specifically points researchers to related **Boston College alumni photographs (BC.1999.056)** and **athletic photographs (BC.1986.019)**.

Collection finding aid:
- https://findingaids.bc.edu/repositories/2/resources/11

Burns's own 2002 Boston College commencement address independently confirms that he graduated in 1978, remembers living in the Mods with roommates, and publicly identifies himself as a loyal member of Red Sox Nation. That speech is retrospective, not evidence of what he did during the 1975 or 1978 baseball seasons.

Institutional transcript:
- https://www.bc.edu/bc-web/bcnews/campus-community/alumni/r--nicholas-burns-2002-commencement-address.html

## Why this matters

This converts "search Boston College 1978" from a broad research instruction into a box-and-folder retrieval plan. The 1978 commencement files are especially promising for contemporaneous images, graduate documentation, campus press coverage, and material that can establish what Burns's final BC days actually looked like without reconstructing them from later memory.

The commencement photograph collection is also a rights/provenance lead. A located image of Burns would still require item-level identification and rights checking before publication use.

## Evidentiary boundary

These finding aids establish **what archival material exists and where it is held**. They do not establish that Nicholas Burns appears in any particular photograph, clipping, program annotation, or contact sheet until the underlying item is inspected.

The 2002 speech supports only Burns's retrospective statements that he graduated in 1978, lived in the Mods with roommates, and identified publicly as a Red Sox fan. Do not use it to claim:
- that his three roommates were baseball-watching companions;
- that he watched the 1975 World Series or any 1978 game in the Mods;
- that a dorm television, radio, newspaper, or Fenway trip was involved;
- any dialogue, reaction, interiority, or motive from his student years;
- any memory, fandom, attendance, or statement by Libby Baylies.
