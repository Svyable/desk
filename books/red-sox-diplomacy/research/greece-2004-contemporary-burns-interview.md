# Greece 2004: contemporary Burns interview and institutional chain

## Status

**Promotable with attribution.** Contemporary reporting materially strengthens the Greece section by preserving Nicholas Burns's own retrospective characterization of the Greek baseball project and independently identifying the institutional actors involved.

## Contemporary source

In an August 18, 2004 *Christian Science Monitor* report, Fred Weir described the creation of Greece's Olympic baseball program. The report says the Hellenic Amateur Baseball Federation sought help from the U.S. ambassador, Nicholas Burns. Burns is quoted describing himself as a committed Boston Red Sox fan. More importantly for the manuscript, Burns characterized the effort as a U.S.-Greek "joint venture" and said it was among the best things he had done as ambassador.

The same report says Burns brought Peter Angelos, the Greek-American owner of the Baltimore Orioles, into the effort and also enlisted Major League Baseball's International Division.

Source: Fred Weir, "Greek baseball team, made in the USA," *Christian Science Monitor*, August 18, 2004.
https://www.csmonitor.com/2004/0818/p02s01-woeu.html

## Independent corroboration

An April 12, 2004 *Washington Post* report independently says Burns contacted Angelos and asked him to lead a search for U.S. players with Greek ancestry. It reports that Orioles scout Rob Derksen and Orioles officials conducted the recruiting effort with MLB's blessing. It also says Burns's successor, Ambassador Thomas J. Miller, helped expedite paperwork enabling players to obtain Greek passports.

Source: "It Appears That Baseball Isn't All Greek to the Greeks," *Washington Post*, April 12, 2004.
https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/sports/2004/04/12/it-appears-that-baseball-isnt-all-greek-to-the-greeks/0d5fc3ca-536e-4df4-8712-c260367f6e51/

## Manuscript use

This evidence supports a stronger, carefully bounded claim than simple fandom: Burns himself later placed the baseball-building effort inside his ambassadorial record, and contemporary independent reporting documents a chain from the Greek federation through the U.S. ambassador to Angelos/Orioles personnel and MLB.

A defensible formulation is:

> Contemporary reporting shows Burns treating baseball not merely as private fandom but as part of his ambassadorial work in Greece. When the fledgling Greek federation asked the U.S. ambassador for help, Burns connected it to Peter Angelos and Major League Baseball. Looking back during the Athens Olympics, he called the effort a U.S.-Greek joint venture and ranked it among the best things he had done as ambassador.

## Attribution boundaries

- The evidence supports **Burns's Red Sox allegiance** and **Orioles/MLB institutional assistance**. It does **not** establish Boston Red Sox organizational assistance.
- Do not collapse Burns, the U.S. Embassy, the Orioles, MLB International, the Greek federation, or the Greek government into a single actor.
- Do not infer private dialogue, motives, reactions, diplomatic outcomes, or audience effects.
- Do not use the 2004 reporting to authenticate any separate first-person account.
- Nothing here is attributable to Libby Baylies.
- Claims that the Boston Red Sox organization supplied a specific deliverable remain unresolved unless supported by separate archival or witness evidence.
