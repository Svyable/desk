# Quote Bank — Red Sox Diplomacy

This file gathers short, publication-usable excerpts and scene leads for *Red Sox Diplomacy*. Quotes are intentionally short. Always revisit the linked source before final manuscript use, confirm transcription, and capture surrounding context.

## 1. Identity: Red Sox Nation

### 1997 — Worcester Polytechnic Institute commencement

**Source:** U.S. Department of State archive, Nicholas Burns commencement address, May 24, 1997.

**Quote:** “A long suffering fan of the Boston Red Sox.”

**Use:** Early-life / Boston identity; establishes fandom in Burns’s own public voice well before the 2004 championship.

**Additional scene lead:** In the same address Burns jokes that Japanese players had reached nearly every major-league park except Fenway and the Red Sox bullpen.

**URL:** https://1997-2001.state.gov/policy_remarks/970524.burns.html

### 2005 — U.S. Institute of Peace

**Source:** U.S. Department of State archive, March 23, 2005.

**Quote:** “I’m a native-born member of Red Sox Nation.”

**Use:** Strong opening or recurring identity line; also useful for post-2004 “redemption” material.

**Context:** Burns had just begun as Under Secretary for Political Affairs and immediately folded Red Sox identity into a serious foreign-policy appearance.

**URL:** https://2001-2009.state.gov/p/us/rm/2005/45781.htm

### 2007 — official biography / later institutional bios

**Source:** State Department and later Harvard biographies.

**Fact:** Official biographies repeatedly identify Burns as a lifelong member of Red Sox Nation.

**Use:** Evidence that fandom became part of his public institutional identity, not a one-off joke.

**URLs:**
- https://2009-2017.state.gov/s/p/fapb/185577.htm
- https://www.hks.harvard.edu/faculty/nicholas-burns

## 2. State Department podium: fandom as public register

### June 4, 1997 — Roger Clemens exchange

**Source:** State Department daily press briefing.

**Quote:** “We’re still bitter in Boston.”

**Quote:** “We’re poor losers in Boston.”

**Use:** Excellent scene for “The Briefing Room Bleachers.” Shows Burns using self-deprecating sports identity in the most official recurring public forum of his job.

**Scene note:** The exchange began with a reporter asking about Roger Clemens. Burns joked about Boston bitterness, the struggling Sox, novenas, and the “patron saint of lost causes.” The humor is revealing because it occurs inside a formal foreign-policy briefing rather than a sports interview.

**URL:** https://1997-2001.state.gov/briefings/9706/970604db.html

### April 2, 1997 — visiting Cypriot journalists

**Source:** State Department daily press briefing.

**Quote:** “Do you have baseball in Cyprus?”

**Use:** Small but vivid proof that Burns naturally used baseball as conversational connective tissue with foreign visitors.

**Context:** Opening Day; Secretary Albright was scheduled to throw a ceremonial first pitch in Baltimore.

**URL:** https://1997-2001.state.gov/briefings/9704/970402.html

### January 6, 1997 — sports vs. foreign policy

**Source:** State Department daily press briefing.

**Quote:** “This is not a sports briefing — right?”

**Use:** Comic chapter epigraph candidate. Captures the running relationship between Burns, the press corps, and sports banter.

**URL:** https://1997-2001.state.gov/briefings/9701/970106.html

## 3. Leaving the podium: the Red Sox as part of Burns’s public persona

### July 1997 — final State briefing as spokesman

**Source:** Washington Post, Thomas W. Lippman.

**Fact:** President Bill Clinton sent Burns an autographed Red Sox cap on his last day as State Department spokesman.

**Inscription quoted by the Post:** “To Nick, you’re still Number One.”

**Use:** Strong end-scene for the spokesman section. The farewell gifts demonstrate that colleagues and reporters associated Burns with the Red Sox strongly enough for the symbolism to be immediate.

**Additional scene lead:** AP reporter Barry Schweid gave him a Red Sox alarm clock as a joke about briefing start times.

**URL:** https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/politics/1997/07/23/as-burns-takes-bow-at-state-a-fan-sox-it-to-him/f7295a64-565a-4320-9a47-a9a75a068cc0/

## 4. Greece: baseball becomes diplomacy

### 2000 — Greek baseball development

**Source:** Associated Press report republished by the Los Angeles Times.

**Quote:** “Baseball—we can’t figure out what’s going on.”

**Use:** Scene opener for the Greece/baseball chapter. Burns is describing the response he heard from Greeks encountering the game.

**Fact:** Contemporary reporting described Burns as taking on an additional “baseball ambassador” role while coordinating help for Greece’s Olympic baseball effort.

**URL:** https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-2000-jan-02-mn-49873-story.html

### 2004 — retrospective on Greek Olympic baseball

**Source:** Christian Science Monitor, August 18, 2004.

**Quote:** “a confirmed, unconditional, rabid fan of the Boston Red Sox”

**Quote:** “one of the best things I ever did as ambassador.”

**Use:** These are central-book quotes. The second is especially important because Burns himself ranks the baseball initiative among his most satisfying ambassadorial work.

**Context:** The article reports that Burns helped connect the Hellenic baseball effort with Peter Angelos and Major League Baseball’s international operation.

**URL:** https://www.csmonitor.com/2004/0818/p02s01-woeu.html

### Greek baseball federation history

**Source:** Greek Baseball history page.

**Fact:** The federation records that its president approached Burns for help; Burns contacted Senator Paul Sarbanes, helping connect the effort to Peter Angelos.

**Use:** Useful corroboration for the institutional chain. Treat as an organizational retrospective and corroborate important details with contemporaneous reporting or archival material.

**URL:** https://greekbaseball.com/history

### 1999 — Angelos / Olympics effort

**Source:** Washington Post sports report.

**Fact:** A reception hosted by Burns and the Hellenic Amateur Baseball Federation brought Peter Angelos into the Greek Olympic baseball effort; the report also notes support from MLB and the Red Sox.

**Use:** Concrete institutional scene lead.

## 6. Rivalry as social language

### 2007 — Connecticut / Red Sox territory

**Source:** State Department remarks to editorial writers.

**Quote:** “That’s Red Sox territory.”

**Use:** Demonstrates how quickly Burns moves from geography to fandom to policy without losing the room.

**Context:** He jokes about Connecticut’s divided Red Sox/Yankees loyalties before turning immediately back to Iran.

**URL:** https://2001-2009.state.gov/p/us/rm/2007/82900.htm

### 2007 — France and the Yankees

**Source:** State Department, Marshall Plan anniversary remarks in Paris.

**Quote:** “the greatest baseball team in the States, the Boston Red Sox.”

**Use:** Shows Burns exporting the rivalry joke into formal diplomacy overseas.

**Context:** He describes himself and Ambassador Craig Stapleton as opponents of the Yankees while praising Stapleton’s role in U.S.-French relations.

**URL:** https://2001-2009.state.gov/p/us/rm/2007/87176.htm

### 2012 — Atlantic Council

**Source:** Atlantic Council event transcript.

**Fact:** The introducer teases Burns for his Red Sox allegiance; Burns replies that he will answer the speaker’s Yankees allegiance “in due course.”

**Use:** Good evidence that the rivalry became a durable public ritual across decades.

**URL:** https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/03/Burns_Transcript.pdf

### 2025 — Davidson

**Source:** Davidson College student account.

**Fact:** A student from Boston reports that Burns immediately traded Red Sox–Yankees observations with him, helping set an informal tone before a substantive policy discussion.

**Use:** Excellent late-career echo showing the same social habit after government service.

**URL:** https://www.davidson.edu/news/2025/09/23/my-day-davidson-ambassador-china-nicholas-burns

## Editorial note

The strongest evidence so far supports a narrower and better claim than “baseball made Burns a diplomat.” Baseball functioned as a recurring social language through which Burns expressed locality, humor, rivalry, continuity, and human identity while exercising institutional authority. In Greece, that language became literal public diplomacy. That distinction should govern the manuscript.
