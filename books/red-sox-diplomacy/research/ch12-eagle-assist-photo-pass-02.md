# Chapter 12 media pass 02 — Burns and Operation Eagle Assist

## Why this pass exists

The existing Chapter 12 visual archive deliberately left one route open: an operation-specific, rights-clearable photograph of NATO's Operation Eagle Assist rather than generic AWACS imagery.

That route now has a strong candidate. This pass does **not** ingest the image into `media/` yet because the searchable derivative record does not expose a canonical National Archives catalog identifier. The image is valuable enough to preserve; the provenance chain should be completed before production use.

### Rights analysis

The scene-camera operator is identified as a U.S. Air Force master sergeant and the source collection as U.S. military digital photographic files. Defense Department guidance states that DoD photographs and imagery are public domain unless otherwise noted and requests appropriate credit. Defense.gov also states that information on its site may be distributed or copied unless otherwise specified.

DoD reuse guidance:
https://www.defense.gov/Contact/Help-Center/Help-Center-Listing/Tag/130833/photos/

Defense.gov public-information notice:
https://www.defense.gov/Legal-Administrative/Privacy-Security/

The derivative NARA record independently labels the image `Released to Public` and `No known copyright restrictions`.

**Commercial-publication assessment:** high-confidence federal public-domain candidate, but do not mark final RELEASE-SAFE until the canonical NARA catalog item/NAID and original-resolution master are captured. This is a provenance-completeness hold, not a known copyright conflict.

Do not use NATO seals, DoD seals or other protected insignia as decorative additions. The photograph should be reproduced as documentary evidence, not converted into promotional branding.

## What the image adds that the existing graphics do not

Chapter 12 already has three useful original objects: the September 12 conditional clause, the October 4 implementation package and the Eagle Assist flight ledger. Those explain procedure and scale. This photograph supplies the missing human endpoint.

The important visual fact is not an aircraft glamour shot. It is Burns standing with the alliance's military and political leadership and the crews after the machinery described in the chapter had actually operated for months. The scene joins the diplomat who carried the U.S. request to NATO with people who flew the resulting mission.

It should therefore appear **after** the flight-ledger discussion or near the chapter's closing Eagle Assist paragraphs, not beside the September 12 Rice recollection. The photograph is dated May 2002 and must not be used to imply that this group was present together during the September decision.

## Chronology cross-check

The Air Force Personnel Center describes Operation Eagle Assist as NATO assistance in North American airspace following September 11 and gives the qualifying operation period as October 12, 2001 through May 16, 2002:
https://www.afpc.af.mil/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/873203/the-article-5-nato-medal-operation-eagle-assist/

Canada's Department of National Defence gives the mission period as October 9, 2001 through May 16, 2002 and records that NATO E-3A aircraft operated from Tinker Air Force Base, Oklahoma, with more than 830 NATO Airborne Early Warning Force members participating, more than 4,300 hours flown and 360 sorties:
https://www.canada.ca/en/department-national-defence/services/military-history/history-heritage/past-operations/north-america/eagle-assist.html

The discrepancy between October 9 and October 12 reflects different official descriptions of operation start/medal qualifying dates. **Do not silently collapse them.** Chapter prose should retain the NATO source chronology already used there unless a date-specific sentence is being revised; this packet records the difference for production fact-checking.
