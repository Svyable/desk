# India negotiation media pass 01

## Scope

Bounded media-and-research enhancement for the U.S.–India civil-nuclear negotiation material in *Red Sox Diplomacy*. This pass adds a rights-clear documentary image candidate and a primary-source companion. It does **not** claim that a photograph proves private bargaining, motive, authorship of the final agreement, or policy causation.

## Recommended documentary asset — Burns and Shyam Saran, New Delhi, January 20, 2006

**What it shows:** U.S. Under Secretary of State for Political Affairs Nicholas Burns and Indian Foreign Secretary Shyam Saran addressing a press conference in New Delhi on January 20, 2006.

**Why it earns space:** Burns and Saran were identifiable officials on opposite sides of the negotiating table. The image gives the India material two named people, a place, and a date rather than another generic flag/handshake/nuclear-reactor illustration. Its value is documentary: it establishes the public-facing negotiating channel. Do not caption it as the moment an agreement was reached unless the manuscript/source record supports that narrower claim.

**Source/provenance:** Wikimedia Commons preserves the State Department-origin file record and identifies the original U.S. Department of State page as `https://2001-2009.state.gov/r/pa/ei/pix/usec/2006/59513.htm`.

**Commons record:** https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Burns_Saran_Delhi_jan2006.jpg

**Creator/institution:** U.S. Department of State; individual photographer is not identified in the surviving record reviewed in this pass.

**Date:** January 20, 2006.

**Place:** New Delhi, India.

**Rights:** Public domain in the United States as a work made by a U.S. Department of State employee in the course of official duties; 17 U.S.C. §§ 101, 105. Commons explicitly records the federal-work basis rather than relying on a generic repository assumption.

**Commercial-book suitability:** Yes on copyright status, subject to ordinary non-copyright constraints. Do not imply endorsement by Burns, Saran, the Department of State, the Government of India, or the Red Sox. No standalone agency seal or team mark should be extracted or promoted.

**Resolution caution:** the surviving Commons original is only 600 × 380 px. Treat it as a modest-width documentary object in print unless a higher-resolution State Department/NARA master is recovered. Do not upscale into a full-page hero image.

## Primary-source companion — Burns briefing, New Delhi, March 2, 2006

**Canonical archive:** George W. Bush White House archive, “Press Briefing by Under Secretary of State for Political Affairs Nick Burns,” March 2, 2006, Maurya Sheraton Hotel and Towers, New Delhi.

URL: https://georgewbush-whitehouse.archives.gov/news/releases/2006/03/20060302-11.html

The briefing is useful because Burns states, in contemporary official language, what the March 2 civil-nuclear agreement did and what remained to happen. He describes the separation of Indian military and civilian nuclear facilities, the need for changes in U.S. law, and the need for action through the IAEA and Nuclear Suppliers Group. This is better evidence for chronology and the U.S. government's contemporaneous case than retrospective summary.

**Evidence boundary:** Burns's briefing is an official U.S. explanation, not a neutral adjudication of the agreement's nonproliferation merits. Statements such as the initiative being a “major win” are advocacy by a principal U.S. official and should be attributed as such. The archive can support what the administration said, the procedural sequence it described, and Burns's public role; it cannot by itself prove private motives, Indian assessments, or later outcomes.

**Text rights:** White House federal-government text is generally public domain in the United States. For book production, use short attributed excerpts only where they do more work than paraphrase; preserve the archive URL and date in notes.

## Closure image — October 10, 2008 signing

Commons preserves a State Department photograph of Secretary of State Condoleezza Rice and Indian External Affairs Minister Pranab Mukherjee signing the U.S.–India Civilian Nuclear Cooperation Agreement on October 10, 2008.

Commons record: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Rice-Mukherjee_sign_US-India_Civilian_Nuclear_Cooperation_Agreement.jpg

**Rights:** Public domain; U.S. Department of State federal work.

**Narrative use:** this is a useful endpoint if a figure sequence needs to distinguish *negotiation* from *formal signing*. It should not replace the Burns/Saran or Burns/Menon photographs in a Burns-centered chapter: the contrast is the point. The people doing years of negotiation need not be the people in the ceremonial signing frame.

## Suggested original figure — INDIA CHANNEL 01

If three photographs feel visually repetitive, use one photograph plus an original chronology rather than a montage of protected newspaper pages or editorial photography.

**Title:** `INDIA CHANNEL 01 — From negotiation to signature`

**Structure:** a horizontal or vertical chronology using original typography and geometric marks only:

- **20 Jan 2006 — New Delhi:** Burns + Shyam Saran public negotiating channel.
- **2 Mar 2006 — New Delhi:** Bush–Singh civil-nuclear agreement announced; Burns publicly explains separation plan and next procedural steps.
- **1 Jun 2007 — New Delhi:** Burns meets Foreign Secretary Shiv Shankar Menon during the continuing negotiation phase.
- **11 Sep 2008 — Washington:** President Bush announces approval of the 123 Agreement and calls for congressional passage.
- **10 Oct 2008 — Washington:** Rice and Mukherjee sign the bilateral agreement.

The September 11, 2008 White House archive is: https://georgewbush-whitehouse.archives.gov/news/releases/2008/09/20080911-1.html

**Do not imply:** a five-point chronology is a complete history of the initiative; Burns alone authored the agreement; congressional, IAEA, NSG, Indian domestic, or other actors were incidental.

## Production and formatting guidance

- Keep photograph + caption + credit as one unbroken object in print; never strand the credit on the next page.
- For the 600 × 380 January 2006 file, target roughly 40–55% text-block width in print and proof at final physical size. If pixelation is visible, use the 2007 GODL image or the original chronology instead.
- In EPUB, place caption immediately after image and credit immediately after caption; alt text should describe only visible content, not interpret diplomatic significance.
- Do not put raw source URLs in captions. Keep URLs and license detail in source/provenance notes.
- Do not crop away either negotiating counterpart in the Burns–Saran or Burns–Menon images. The relationship is the documentary subject.
- Grayscale proof must preserve facial separation and readable tonal contrast; do not solve weak grayscale by aggressive contrast that changes documentary appearance.
- No Red Sox, MLB, State Department, White House, or Government of India logos should be added to the chronology. Typography, dates, names, rules, and simple geometric marks are sufficient.
- For all federal photographs, retain the public-domain credit even where legally unnecessary; it tells production why the asset is safe.
- For GODL-India material, retain the required Ministry of External Affairs attribution and record any material adaptation/crop in production notes.

## Rejected routes

- Newspaper front pages and wire-service negotiation photographs: rights are normally publication-specific and add little that the federal/GODL records cannot establish more safely.
- Generic nuclear-reactor photography: visually literal but weak on the chapter's actual subject, which is negotiation among institutions and people.
- Indian or U.S. flags as standalone decoration: no evidentiary value.
- A signing photograph captioned as Burns's achievement: collapses institutional authorship and the distinction between negotiation and formal execution.
- Social-media screenshots: even when an underlying federal image may be public domain, the platform chrome, comments, avatars, and third-party material create unnecessary rights and production noise.
