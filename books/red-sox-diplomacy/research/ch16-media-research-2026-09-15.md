# Chapter 16 media/research pass — October 2004

Date checked: 2026-09-15

Scope: Chapter 16 only. This pass does not reconstruct Nicholas Burns's undocumented location, companions, viewing circumstances, or immediate reaction on October 27, 2004. It strengthens the chapter's documented *afterlife* of the championship and identifies a publication-safe federal visual that can carry that work without pretending to supply the missing room.

## Release candidate: 2004 World Series champions at the White House

**Event:** President George W. Bush with the Boston Red Sox, 2004 World Series champions, South Lawn, White House, March 2, 2005.

**Preferred frame:** official White House photograph by Eric Draper showing President Bush posing with the championship team.

**Primary asset page:** https://georgewbush-whitehouse.archives.gov/news/releases/2005/03/images/20050302-17_hp8c1565-515h.html

**Alternate frame:** official White House photograph by Paul Morse showing Bush congratulating the team during the same ceremony: https://georgewbush-whitehouse.archives.gov/news/releases/2005/03/images/20050302-17_w8n5418-515h.html

**Institution/provenance:** White House Photo Office; archived George W. Bush White House website preserved by the National Archives and Records Administration. NARA describes archived White House sites as Presidential records and specifically identifies them as resources for photographs, speeches, press releases and other public records: https://www.archives.gov/presidential-records/research/archived-white-house-websites

**Creator:** Eric Draper, White House photographer (preferred frame); Paul Morse, White House photographer (alternate).

**Date:** March 2, 2005.

**Rights basis:** U.S. Government work / public-domain route, subject to item-level confirmation. NARA states that material created by federal agencies or federal officers/employees as part of official duties is a work of the United States Government and is not eligible for U.S. copyright protection. NARA also cautions that not every item in its holdings is public domain and that researchers remain responsible for other restrictions: https://www.archives.gov/research/still-pictures/permissions

**Commercial-publication suitability:** strong, once the production master is tied to the official White House Photo Office record and checked for any item-level restriction. The archived page itself identifies the photographer and event. No third-party news agency is credited. Do not substitute a wire-service, MLB, Getty, AP, Reuters, newspaper, team, or fan copy of the same ceremony.

**Publicity/endorsement caution:** use editorially as historical documentation. Do not crop or caption the photograph so that President Bush, the players, Major League Baseball, or the Boston Red Sox appear to endorse this book. NARA notes that likeness/publicity claims can exist independently of copyright; keep the use factual and contextual.

**Credit line:** `White House photo by Eric Draper, March 2, 2005; archived by the National Archives and Records Administration.` If the NARA production master supplies an identifier or preferred credit, use that exact identifier/credit in the final edition.

### Why this belongs in Chapter 16

The photograph does **not** solve the missing October 27 scene. That is precisely why it is useful. It documents the public, ceremonial afterlife of the championship five months later. The manuscript already moves from the unknowable private room to Burns's documented March 2005 public language of “redemption.” A federal photograph from the same month can mark the transition from private absence to public commemoration without inventing Burns into the frame.

It also adds a useful political register. The 2004 championship did not remain merely a Boston event: by March it had entered a presidential ceremony on the South Lawn. The archived White House caption records Bush telling the team that they had answered “86 years of prayer” and that people around the world cheered. For the book, paraphrase rather than enlarging that quotation; the photograph is stronger as evidence of public ritual than as a source of another baseball aphorism.

### Recommended manuscript placement

Place after the paragraph beginning **“Five months later, Burns supplied language the missing October scene cannot.”** The image should sit close to the March 2005 USIP material, not beside the October 27 championship description. That placement preserves the chapter's evidentiary architecture: October remains absent; March is documented.

**Working caption:** *Five months after the final out, the championship had become public ceremony. President George W. Bush with the 2004 Boston Red Sox on the White House South Lawn, March 2, 2005. The photograph documents the championship's official afterlife, not Nicholas Burns's whereabouts or reaction on October 27.*

**Alt text:** `President George W. Bush stands outdoors on the White House South Lawn with members of the 2004 Boston Red Sox during a March 2, 2005 ceremony honoring the World Series champions.`

### Production treatment

- Print: approximately 60–70% text width; do not run full bleed. The point is documentary scale, not triumphalist spectacle.
- Keep image, caption, credit and any source note together across page breaks.
- EPUB: image → caption → credit → source link in reading order.
- Preserve enough horizontal width that the group remains legible; avoid aggressive portrait crops that isolate Bush or a single player.
- Test grayscale conversion before release; faces and suits must remain separable from the background.
- Do not place immediately adjacent to a second celebratory team photograph. One official ceremony image is sufficient.
- If the available archived JPEG is below production resolution, obtain the corresponding high-resolution White House Photo Office/NARA master rather than upscaling the web derivative.

**Purpose:** show the ALCS reversal without reproducing MLB broadcast frames, newspaper pages, logos, scorebugs, ticket art, or copyrighted sports photography.

Source every datum to an authoritative statistical/game record before production. **Design rule:** no Yankees or Red Sox marks, uniforms, player cutouts, stadium silhouettes, faux scoreboards, or nostalgic baseball textures. Typography and neutral geometric marks are enough. The visual argument is the reversal from 0–3 to 4–3.

## Material deliberately rejected

- **2004 World Series / ALCS game photography:** usually controlled by news agencies, MLB, clubs, photographers or publishers; no need to create permissions exposure when the chapter's distinctive evidentiary problem is absence.
- **Television screenshots or broadcast scorebugs:** protected audiovisual material and unnecessary to the argument.
- **Newspaper front pages:** rights are publication-specific and the typography/headlines would turn the page into nostalgia rather than evidence.
- **Championship parade photography from uncertain web sources:** municipal setting does not make a photograph public domain; creator rights must be established item by item.
- **Fenway Park beauty shots:** decorative here. The chapter is about what the record can and cannot establish, not stadium atmosphere.

## Evidence boundary to preserve

The White House photograph establishes a March 2, 2005 federal ceremony honoring the 2004 champions. It does not establish that Burns attended, watched it, discussed it privately, or experienced the championship in any particular way. Burns's later “redemption,” “glorious autumn of 2004,” and “we identify with suffering” remarks remain separate first-person/public-speech evidence. Do not use the ceremony image as visual shorthand for his private feeling.
