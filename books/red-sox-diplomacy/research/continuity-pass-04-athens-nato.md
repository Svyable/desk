# Continuity Pass 04 — Athens to NATO

## Scope

Audit Chapters 9–13 after the opening-third compression. The goal is not uniform shortening. These chapters carry different kinds of documentary density, and cutting the wrong detail would recenter Burns in stories that are valuable precisely because agency is distributed.

## Chapter 9 — Athens

Audited for chapter function. No structural rewrite in this pass.

The chapter has to hold several distinct representational lanes:

- the embassy building as a public political object;
- Burns's public-diplomacy instincts and official bilateral work;
- John Koenig and Barbara Nielsen as mission witnesses;
- diaspora institutions including AHEPA;
- the Marshall statue and embassy/AHEPA collaboration;
- Elizabeth Baylies Burns's independent civic and preservation record;
- the Kosovo/Clinton-visit problem of representing unpopular American policy.

The density is not automatically repetition. It prevents an ambassador-only account of the posting.

Future compression should target explanatory paragraphs after evidence has already landed, not remove the independent Libby record, colleague testimony, Greek political context, or the physical-embassy thread.

Guardrail: embassy renovation/public diplomacy may demonstrate representational intent; it cannot prove broader Greek political consent or a reduction in anti-Americanism.

## Chapter 10 — Bringing Baseball to Greece

Audited for agency. No structural rewrite in this pass.

This chapter is intentionally multi-actor and should remain so. Preserve separate lanes for:

- Greek organizers and federation initiative;
- Chuck Samiotes / Bill Galatis and diaspora grassroots work;
- Burns / Embassy Athens brokerage and advocacy;
- Paul Sarbanes / Peter Angelos and political-diaspora connections;

The exact Boston Red Sox club contribution remains an archival gap and must not be inferred from adjacent MLB/Red Sox networks.

Future compression should remove duplicate explanation of convening/brokerage once the role map and sequence have already shown it.

## Chapter 11 — Twelve Days

Substantially compressed.

The chapter now stays close to the information and authority problem of September 11:

- Burns was twelve days into the NATO job;
- the United States was attacked;
- Washington institutions were evacuated/disrupted and temporarily difficult for USNATO to reach;
- allied ambassadors called toward Burns before he could fully reconnect with the capital;
- political solidarity moved faster than formal authorization;
- the late-night ambassadorial meeting stopped short of a formal Article 5 decision.

Removed backward career-summary material and much of the retrospective alliance theory. The baseball/American-history braid survives only after the immediate September 11 scene, in compressed form, through the resumed season and the 2001 World Series first pitch.

Ending now hands cleanly to Chapter 12: `Solidarity moved faster than procedure. By morning, procedure would have to catch up.`

## Chapter 12 — Article 5

Audited and left substantially intact.

This chapter owns the institutional sequence and needs precision more than compression:

1. September 12 conditional Article 5 decision;
2. Burns's remembered Condoleezza Rice authorization call, explicitly retrospective rather than contemporaneous transcript;
3. October 2 determination that the attacks were externally directed;
4. October 4 eight support measures;
5. Eagle Assist as implementation.

Do not collapse September 12 and October 2 into one act. Verification is part of the strength of the sequence.

Any future compression should target generic alliance commentary, not the conditional clause, implementation package, or dates.

## Chapter 13 — The Clubhouse Called NATO

Substantially compressed and recentered on procedure.

Protected facts:

- France, Germany and Belgium opposed/delayed Turkey defensive planning over timing and political signal, not a rejection of Turkey's right to defense;
- Burns was an American advocate inside the dispute and used strong credibility language;
- George Robertson provides a more institutional reconstruction;
- France, Germany and Belgium broke the silence procedure;
- Turkey invoked Article 4;
- the North Atlantic Council could not reach consensus at nineteen;
- the move to the pre-existing Defence Planning Committee occurred only after broader consensus failed and with member concurrence;
- France did not participate in that committee because of its standing institutional position at the time;
- Germany and Belgium remained in the room and still had to agree;
- Belgium proposed amendments;
- consensus at eighteen produced Operation Display Deterrence;
- Germany's defensive contributions show that opposition to the road to war could coexist with defense of Turkey;
- later NATO roles in Afghanistan and support for Poland in Iraq demonstrate institutional return to work after rupture.

Removed broad passages about alliance theory already demonstrated by the procedural record.

The bounded `clubhouse` metaphor now fails quickly and usefully: NATO ambassadors have repeated-contact density, but sovereign governments are not players under a manager.

The chapter's final proposition remains institutional rather than sentimental: after behaving like adversaries across a table, the allies returned to the same table.

## Cross-chapter result

The NATO sequence now has clean ownership:

- Chapter 11: information gap and incoming solidarity on September 11;
- Chapter 12: treaty authorization, verification and implementation;
- Chapter 13: serious allied disagreement, procedural resolution and repair.

This reduces the tendency for all three chapters to become versions of `alliances matter`.
