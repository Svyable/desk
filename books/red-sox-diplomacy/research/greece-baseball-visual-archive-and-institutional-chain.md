# Greece baseball assistance: institutional chain and visual archive follow-up

## Status

Research note. This file separates contemporaneous reporting from later/community-hosted material and preserves the manuscript's documentary boundary. Separate 1999/2000 contemporaneous reporting establishes Boston Red Sox organizational help or pledged support, but not the exact club deliverable.

## Verified / strong evidence

### Christian Science Monitor, 18 August 2004

Contemporaneous reporting says the Hellenic Amateur Baseball Federation asked U.S. Ambassador Nicholas Burns for help. Burns, identified in the story as a devoted Boston Red Sox fan, connected the Greek effort to Peter Angelos, the Greek-American owner of the Baltimore Orioles, and rallied Major League Baseball's International Division. Burns described the baseball effort as a U.S.-Greek joint venture and as one of the best things he did as ambassador.

Source: https://www.csmonitor.com/2004/0818/p02s01-woeu.html

**Safe use:** Burns's Red Sox allegiance is documented personal context; the assistance chain identified by this source runs through Burns, Angelos/Orioles, and MLB International. Separate 1999/2000 reports establish Red Sox organizational help or pledged support. Do not infer the form or fulfillment of that support from Burns's fandom.

### Washington Post, 12 April 2004

A contemporaneous Washington Post account reports that Burns contacted Peter Angelos and asked him to lead a search for U.S. players with Greek ancestry. Lou Angelos recalled his father's affirmative response. The story says Orioles officials and Rob Derksen undertook recruiting with MLB's blessing, while Burns's successor Thomas Miller helped expedite Greek-passport paperwork.

Source: https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/sports/2004/04/12/it-appears-that-baseball-isnt-all-greek-to-the-greeks/0d5fc3ca-536e-4df4-8712-c260367f6e51/

**Safe use:** this independently reinforces the Orioles/MLB institutional chain and identifies Lou Angelos, Rob Derksen, and Thomas Miller as useful witness/archive targets.

## Valuable visual / archival leads requiring provenance care

### Baseball Acropolis photo archive

A surviving project website presents photographs captioned as:
- U.S. Ambassador to Greece R. Nicholas Burns with Chris and Gus Karalekas;
- a meeting between Hellenic Amateur Baseball Federation members, MLB representatives, a Baltimore Orioles contingent, and Boston businessmen;
- Jim Small of Major League Baseball International with Chris Karalekas at MLB offices;
- an MLB-office meeting including Syd Thrift, Lou Angelos, Tom Marudas, and Chris Karalekas;
- baseball clinics and equipment deliveries in Greece.

Source: https://www.baseballacropolis.org/photos.htm

These images are potentially useful documentary objects, but the page does not by itself supply sufficient photographer, date, rights, or original-negative metadata for publication. Treat captions as **archive leads**, not independently authenticated scene narration. Before manuscript/image use, retrieve original files if possible and establish creator, date, location, rights holder, and whether captions are contemporaneous.

### Reproduced Athens News account

A community archive reproduces an Athens News story by Robert Bruce stating that the Orioles had worked with the Hellenic federation since 1999, that Angelos assigned scout Rob Derksen, and that MLB International planned substantial financial support. Because this is a reproduction rather than the original publication, use it to locate the Athens News issue or archival copy rather than as the preferred citation.

Source: https://www.helleniccomserve.com/baseball.html

## Corrected Red Sox / attribution boundary

Two contemporaneous reports recovered in a later pass establish a narrow Boston Red Sox institutional role:

- *The Washington Post*, September 8, 1999: the Angelos-led effort was being helped by the commissioner's office and the Boston Red Sox.
- Associated Press / *Los Angeles Times*, January 2, 2000: support had also been pledged by the Boston Red Sox.

Sources:
https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/sports/1999/09/08/injuries-to-teens-are-still-a-concern/fe2eb9f0-7fc2-4b43-80a4-a9cb6ac94670/
https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-2000-jan-02-mn-49873-story.html

The evidentiary chain therefore includes:
1. the Hellenic federation / Panos Mitsiopoulos;
2. Nicholas Burns;
3. Peter Angelos and the Baltimore Orioles;
4. Major League Baseball / MLB International;
5. reported Boston Red Sox organizational help or pledged support;
6. later U.S. Ambassador Thomas Miller on passport facilitation.

The exact Red Sox employee, contribution, value, timing, and fulfillment remain unresolved. Do not turn the two reports into a specific delivery of money, equipment, personnel, scouting, coaching, or facilities without further evidence.
