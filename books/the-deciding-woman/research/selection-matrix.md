## Hard chapters

### 1776–1786
Abigail Adams is culturally irresistible, but her most famous “remember the ladies” letter predates July 4, 1776. Reed wins the actual first birthday decade on organized action inside the new nation’s wartime emergency. The chapter should still open with Adams as the obvious expected answer.

### 1876–1886
Frances Willard’s scale is extraordinary and her legacy morally mixed. That makes the chapter useful: importance cannot mean moral cleanliness. Later drafting must include the WCTU’s “Do Everything” model, suffrage connection, prohibition consequences, and the racial dispute that Ida B. Wells would force into public view.

### 1986–1996
Ruth Bader Ginsburg is the strongest rival. The case for Anita Hill is temporal: Ginsburg’s foundational sex-equality litigation largely predates the window, while Hill’s 1991 testimony occurred squarely inside it and changed public discussion of workplace harassment. This verdict should remain provisional pending evidence on reporting, litigation, organizational, and electoral effects after the hearing.

### 2016–2026
This is deliberately not awarded by office rank. Harris’s vice presidency is historically unprecedented; Lisa Su’s leadership is central to strategic computing; Jennifer Doudna’s CRISPR work reshaped biotechnology. Corbett currently wins because the pandemic makes survival a uniquely deep consequence and because her documented role connects long-term prototype-pathogen work to one of the decade’s major U.S. vaccine platforms. The chapter must not individualize team science beyond what the evidence supports.
