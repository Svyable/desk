# Back Matter

## After the twenty-fifth verdict

A list can make history look settled. This one is meant to do the opposite.

The twenty-five names in *The Deciding Woman* are not a canon of the best American women, nor a ranking of virtue, celebrity, intelligence, courage, or representativeness. They are twenty-five causal arguments bounded by ten-year windows. Change the window, weight the criteria differently, or choose a different counterfactual and some winners should change.

That instability is a feature of the method.

History usually reaches readers after selection has already occurred. The textbook has chosen which life receives a paragraph. The museum has chosen which object enters the case. The holiday has chosen whom to celebrate. The familiar photograph has become so familiar that its survival feels like proof of the subject’s importance.

This book exposes the selection step by making it impossible to hide. Exactly one woman must occupy each interval. Every inclusion creates an exclusion. Every verdict therefore carries a question: what did we count as consequence?

Across the 250 years, one pattern becomes difficult to miss. Women’s influence does not wait for formal permission. Before the vote, before officeholding, before admission to professions, women build networks, alter logistics, circulate arguments, create schools, move information, manage social space, organize consumers, shelter fugitives, document violence, and force institutions to respond. Formal power matters enormously when it arrives, but it arrives into a country already changed by informal power.

A second pattern is the conversion of workaround into institution. The Philadelphia canvass becomes a model of organized civic action. Private escape collides with federal law. Reform reports become public appropriations. Volunteer battlefield relief becomes a durable humanitarian organization. Settlement work becomes professional social work. Agitation becomes an amendment. Investigative writing becomes regulation. Representation becomes office. Testimony becomes workplace vocabulary. Audience trust becomes a distribution system. Laboratory preparation becomes a vaccine platform.

The third pattern is less flattering. Progress does not arrive as a clean relay in which each generation hands more freedom to the next. Movements compete. Gains are distributed unequally. A woman can widen opportunity for some people while repeating prejudices that narrow it for others. Institutions absorb reform and blunt it. Symbols outlive the coalitions that made them. The same country that celebrates a “first” can preserve the structure that made the first so late.

That is why the runners-up matter. They show the road not taken by the book’s argument and the roads history itself was taking at the same time.

The reader is invited to make a replacement list. But there is one rule worth preserving: replacements should be defended in causal language. “I admire her more” is not enough. “She was more famous” is not enough. “She represents the decade to me” is not enough.

What changed because she acted?

Who felt it?

How long did it last?

Who else would likely have made it happen?

What became thinkable, legal, survivable, profitable, sayable, teachable, or governable afterward that was less so before?

Those questions turn commemoration back into history.

## Research record

The working edition maintains a book-local research record:

- `research/README.md` defines the selection method, evidence standards, and falsification rules;
- `research/selection-matrix.md` records the winner and strongest runner-ups for all twenty-five windows;
- `research/source-ledger.csv` records checkable sources and what each source can and cannot support.

The manuscript favors primary sources and institutional historical collections when available. Later editorial passes should add specialist scholarship for interpretation, dispute, historiography, and counterfactual judgment. Institutional biographies are useful for dates and documented actions; they are not neutral substitutes for historical argument.

No quotation should be introduced without a retrievable source. No private thought should be invented. Composite scenes are not permitted. When a famous story is contested—such as the exact role of an individual in saving an object, guiding an expedition, initiating a movement, or designing a scientific intervention—the prose should state the narrower claim the evidence supports.

## A note on the title

“The Deciding Woman” does not mean that one woman decided each decade. It names the book’s imposed decision: if we have to choose, what do we reveal about our theory of importance?

The women decided things. So did millions of other people around them.

The author decides a winner.

The reader decides whether he is wrong.
