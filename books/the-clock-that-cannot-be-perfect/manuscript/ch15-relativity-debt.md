# Chapter 15 — The Relativity Problem Collapse Models Owe Us

Nonrelativistic collapse is easier than relativistic collapse for the same reason a stage play is easier than a city: simultaneity can be smuggled into the scenery.

Relativity does not provide a universal present slicing the universe into one shared instant. Collapse, if imagined as a physical event that updates a spatially extended quantum state, therefore raises immediate questions about covariance, causality, fields, and energy.

There are relativistic collapse proposals, but no single universally accepted completion settles the issue. The 2025 clock paper openly works with nonrelativistic models and extracts a weak-field relativistic consequence through gravitational time dilation.

That makes the result a probe and a warning. If a theory says stochastic mass-density localization is physically tied to gravity, eventually it must say what the stochastic object is in spacetime rather than merely in Newtonian potential. Does the noise become a covariant field? How is stress-energy treated? What correlations are permitted across spacelike separation? How is energy bookkeeping handled?

A clock is merciless about these questions because a clock traces a worldline. Its observable is proper time. A fully relativistic prediction should tell us how stochastic geometry accumulates along that worldline and how two worldlines correlate.

This suggests a research program larger than the original calculation: derive clock observables from competing relativistic collapse completions and ask whether precision timing distinguishes them even when ordinary laboratory collapse signatures overlap.

The clock may be less useful as a detector of “collapse yes/no” than as a discriminator among ways of making collapse compatible with spacetime.

That would be a better outcome than a headline. It would force the theory to grow up.
