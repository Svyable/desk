# Chapter 3 — GRW: Make the Weirdness Physical

The useful historical break came in the 1980s. GianCarlo Ghirardi, Alberto Rimini, and Tullio Weber proposed that localization events could occur spontaneously. Microscopic systems would almost always behave according to familiar quantum mechanics. Composite macroscopic systems, containing enormous numbers of constituents, would be localized with overwhelming effectiveness.

The maneuver is easy to caricature as adding noise until the cat behaves. That misses its scientific virtue. GRW gave the embarrassment parameters. Once a collapse rate and localization scale exist, one can ask whether atoms, molecules, solids, stars, and laboratory detectors behave consistently with them.

Continuous Spontaneous Localization made the process continuous rather than a sequence of discrete jumps. Mass-proportional variants became especially important because amplification then tracks the stuff from which macroscopic objects are built. The model contains a localization rate and a correlation length. Different proposed values carve out different phenomenologies.

That parameter space became a battlefield. Matter-wave interference attacks the loss of coherence directly. Non-interferometric experiments look for side effects: unexplained heating, Brownian-like force noise, radiation. Cold-atom experiments constrain anomalous expansion. Mechanical resonators listen for force noise. Germanium detectors look for spontaneous photons. Gravitational-wave instruments, built for astrophysics, can be repurposed as exquisitely quiet witnesses against additional stochastic motion.

This is a theme worth keeping beyond collapse physics. Instruments are promiscuous. They answer questions their builders did not necessarily intend to ask.

The clock calculation belongs to this tradition but changes the object under interrogation. A clock has internal dynamics chosen precisely because a transition frequency can be reproduced and compared. If stochastic gravitational potential alters proper time, then the clock becomes a transducer from hypothetical spacetime noise into phase or timing uncertainty.

No current clock is close to the predicted level in the paper. That negative fact is not a disappointment. It tells us where the theory hides. A good speculative theory should become more constrained as measurement improves. A bad speculative culture responds to every null by moving the claim somewhere instruments cannot reach.

GRW’s deepest legacy may therefore be methodological. If you want collapse to be real, make it vulnerable.
