# Chapter 1 — A Clock Is a Theory With Hands

The first clock in this story is not a clock. It is a potential well.

Put one clock lower in a gravitational field and another higher. General relativity says they do not accumulate the same proper time. This is no longer a cathedral-sized thought experiment. Precision timekeeping has made gravitational potential a laboratory variable. The useful inversion is easy to miss: clocks do not merely measure time while gravity happens elsewhere. They are instruments for gravity because gravity changes the rate at which clocks run.

Now make the gravitational potential uncertain.

That is the move at the center of Bortolotti and colleagues’ paper. Certain spontaneous-collapse models introduce stochasticity into the evolution of matter. The Diósi–Penrose family has long been associated with gravity; Continuous Spontaneous Localization is usually presented through a stochastic modification of quantum dynamics. The 2025 work builds a quantitative bridge from collapse dynamics to fluctuations in Newtonian gravitational potential and then asks what general relativity makes those fluctuations do to clock time.

The answer is not a skipping second hand. It is an irreducible uncertainty in elapsed time, far below contemporary clock sensitivity. ScienceDaily’s September 2026 headline calls this a tiny glitch in time. The paper itself is more disciplined: the effect is conditional on collapse models, derived in a nonrelativistic framework, and negligible for present devices.

That gap between headline and paper is where the book starts.

Physics has a recurring habit of turning philosophical discomfort into instrumentation. The atom was once argument; then spectroscopy gave it fingerprints. Gravitational waves were a perturbative consequence before kilometer-scale interferometers heard black holes. The measurement problem remains unusually resistant because standard quantum mechanics is so successful. Interpretations can disagree extravagantly while sharing predictions. Objective-collapse models are different. They alter the equations. Altered equations can lose.

A theory that says superpositions physically collapse must specify enough machinery to prevent tables, dust grains, cats, and laboratory notebooks from behaving like isolated electrons. That machinery is not free. Stochastic localization can imply diffusion, heating, radiation, or suppressed interference. Experiments have therefore attacked collapse theories from directions that do not look like philosophy at all: germanium radiation spectra, cold atoms, cantilevers, gravitational-wave detectors, levitated masses.

The clock paper adds a new ledger entry. If collapse noise is gravitational noise, the bill may also arrive as time noise.

The important word is **if**. We will keep it visible.
