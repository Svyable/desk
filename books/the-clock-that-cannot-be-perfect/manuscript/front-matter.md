# The Clock That Cannot Be Perfect

## Time Noise, Collapse Physics, and the Experiment Hidden Inside Every Clock

Sven Hardy Benson

There is a clean way to misunderstand the new result: say that physicists discovered time is glitchy. That is not what happened. Nicola Bortolotti, Catalina Curceanu, Lajos Diósi, Simone Manti, and Kristian Piscicchia asked what follows if two families of objective-collapse models are taken seriously as physics rather than interpretation. Their answer is conditional and much stranger than the headline. If the stochastic structure used to collapse mass-density superpositions is tied to fluctuations of Newtonian gravitational potential, then general relativity turns uncertainty in potential into uncertainty in elapsed time. A clock inherits noise from the world it occupies.

The predicted noise is fantastically small. Existing atomic clocks do not see it. The paper is not a detection of fluctuating time, nor evidence that either Continuous Spontaneous Localization or the Diósi–Penrose model is correct. It is a derivation of a possible observable consequence of theories designed to solve another problem entirely: why macroscopic things do not remain in the sprawling superpositions permitted by ordinary quantum dynamics.

That is enough for a book because clocks are where metaphysics gets audited. A clock is an argument about time built as hardware. Modern clocks have become precise enough to register gravitational redshift over human-scale height differences, to compare distant gravitational potentials, and to make previously philosophical questions operational. Collapse models, meanwhile, are valuable precisely because they risk being wrong. They modify dynamics and therefore leave residue: heating, diffusion, spontaneous radiation, loss of interference, perhaps clock noise.

This book follows that residue. It does not assume time is granular, consciousness causes collapse, gravity solves measurement, or the 2025 calculation has revealed quantum gravity. It asks a narrower question with larger consequences: what happens when a theory of definite outcomes is forced to pay its bill in seconds?

## Evidence posture

**Class 1 — verified/documented:** the published 2025 Physical Review Research calculation; the mathematical existence of CSL and Diósi–Penrose model families; published experimental bounds on collapse-model parameter spaces; established gravitational redshift and precision-clock physics.

**Class 2 — credible but incomplete:** physical interpretations connecting collapse noise to gravity; prospects for future clock-based constraints; model-dependent mappings between stochastic Newtonian potential and proper-time noise.

**Class 3 — disputed/unconfirmed:** objective collapse as a real process in nature; gravity as its cause; claims that existing anomalies already reveal collapse-induced time noise.

**Class 4 — speculative synthesis:** precision clocks may become not merely timekeepers but detectors of whatever physical process selects classical history; networks of clocks may eventually test whether time is a perfectly smooth bookkeeping parameter or a noisy physical observable.

The distinction is the book.
