# Publication pass — Agentile

Date: 2026-09-06

## Publication thesis

*Agentile* argues that agentic software creates a distinct economic capability: delegated intention becoming cheaper, more persistent, more parallel, and more programmable. The manuscript follows the consequences as software moves from answering toward acting—changing the cost of delegation, firm boundaries, queues, entrepreneurship, management, wages, ownership, verification, interoperability, public capacity, and responsibility.

The durable claim is not that software literally becomes labor or capital in a legal or accounting sense. Those categories are analytical comparisons used to understand a new productive force whose behavior can overlap both.

## Whole-book coherence pass

The complete manuscript should preserve these distinctions through release editing:

- **Tool versus actor:** agency is a spectrum of delegated authority and persistence, not a binary declaration that software is a person.
- **Capability versus permission:** an agent that can perform an action does not automatically have legitimate authority to perform it.
- **Delegation versus abdication:** cheap delegation raises the value of specifying goals, limits, evidence, escalation, and accountability.
- **Labor analogy versus worker identity:** the book can analyze substitution and complementarity without implying legal personhood or human equivalence.
- **Capital analogy versus ownership fact:** persistent productive software can behave like a scalable productive asset while remaining dependent on compute, models, data, access rights, and organizations.
- **Output abundance versus verification:** lower production cost can move scarcity toward review, proof, trust, and consequence-bearing judgment.
- **Autonomy versus interoperability:** an economy of agents still depends on standards, identity, permissions, settlement, dispute resolution, and interfaces among principals.
- **Private capacity versus public capacity:** states gain access to agentic capability too; the manuscript should not frame agency as exclusively a firm or consumer phenomenon.
- **Efficiency versus responsibility:** faster execution does not dissolve liability, due process, fiduciary duty, safety obligations, or the need for a responsible principal.

## Falsification boundaries

The central argument should be revised if:

- agentic systems remain largely confined to conversational assistance and do not acquire economically meaningful delegated action across real workflows;
- the cost of safe delegation stays so high that persistent software actors are rarely preferable to ordinary automation or human coordination;
- verification and supervision costs rise roughly one-for-one with agent output, eliminating the expected delegation advantage;
- interoperability remains too weak for agents to transact, coordinate, or act across organizational boundaries at meaningful scale;
- firms do not materially change boundaries, management structures, or entrepreneurship as agentic capacity becomes cheaper;
- ownership of models, compute, data, permissions, and distribution overwhelms the manuscript’s broader claim about a newly abundant “agentile force.”

These are empirical boundaries. The released edition should not protect the thesis by redefining every automation improvement as proof of agentic transformation.

## Current-source refresh before release

Recheck any passage depending on:

- current agent reliability, tool use, autonomy, planning, browser/computer-use, coding, or long-running-task capability;
- model and inference costs;
- current product names or vendor capabilities;
- labor-market or productivity claims;
- current policy or regulatory treatment of automated agents;
- current payment, identity, authorization, or agent-to-agent standards;
- government deployment examples;
- security incidents or fraud patterns involving autonomous systems.

Durable economic reasoning should remain separated from examples that may age quickly.

## Chapter-level release checks

- Chapters 1–4: make the transition from tool to delegated actor precise without anthropomorphism.
- Chapters 5–7: verify permission, management, queue, and capital analogies against actual organizational practice.
- Chapters 8–9: refresh machine-customer and machine-seller examples; distinguish technical possibility from observed adoption.
- Chapter 10: keep the one-person corporation as a boundary-case argument, not a universal firm forecast.
- Chapter 11: ensure “new middle management” describes control, escalation, and exception handling rather than merely fewer managers.
- Chapters 12–14: keep skill, wages, and ownership arguments distributional and contingent rather than deterministic.
- Chapter 15: verify that the verification economy is treated as a receiving-side capacity problem, not only an output-quality problem.
- Chapter 16: refresh interoperability and agent-to-agent protocol claims.
- Chapter 17: refresh public-sector capacity and preserve due-process / legitimacy distinctions.
- Chapter 18: ensure the synthesis names credible limits and failure modes as clearly as the upside.

## Structural readiness

The Desk manuscript contains front matter, all 18 planned numbered chapters, the interlude, back matter, and chapter-specific research notes for the evidence-heavy second half. This pass is intended to make the full manuscript releasable as one coherent edition rather than simply exposing more of the existing public proof.
