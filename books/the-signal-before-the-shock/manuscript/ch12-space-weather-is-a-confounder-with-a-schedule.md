# Chapter 12 — Space Weather Is a Confounder With a Schedule

The Sun is the most prolific false earthquake precursor in the dataset.

Charged particles, solar wind structures and geomagnetic storms perturb the magnetic field and ionosphere on timescales that overlap almost perfectly with reported seismic anomalies. They can create ULF fluctuations. They alter TEC. They change satellite plasma measurements. Their effects vary with latitude, local time and season.

USGS is explicit that no causal relationship between solar flares or magnetic storms and earthquakes has been demonstrated. That statement addresses a separate claim sometimes smuggled into precursor discussions: not only that solar activity mimics precursors, but that it triggers earthquakes. The evidence required for triggering is much stronger.

For precursor testing, space weather should be treated the way epidemiology treats age: a covariate so obvious that failing to model it is disqualifying.

Geomagnetic indices are not enough by themselves. A global Kp value can hide local structure. Solar wind parameters, magnetometer arrays, ionospheric maps and local-time dependence all matter. The strongest design uses differential measurements: compare candidate stations near future earthquakes to stations with similar geomagnetic exposure but different seismic outcomes.

One reason old precursor papers age badly is that background datasets improve. A signal that once looked local can later be compared against global archives and shown to be widespread. This is what happened to parts of the Loma Prieta magnetic story.

That suggests a rule for new claims: archive enough external context that future scientists can kill them.

A paper should be proud to contain its own execution mechanism.
