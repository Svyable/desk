# Back Matter — Chronology and Research Questions

## Selected chronology

- **1960s–1980s:** modern earthquake-prediction programs expand across multiple candidate precursor classes, including strain, groundwater, electrical and magnetic observations.
- **1985:** intensive Parkfield earthquake prediction experiment begins around the expected recurrence of a moderate San Andreas event.
- **17 October 1989:** Loma Prieta earthquake; Corralitos ULF magnetic measurements later become a canonical precursor claim.
- **1990:** Fraser-Smith et al. publish the low-frequency magnetic observations near Loma Prieta.
- **1992:** Parkfield alert follows a magnitude 4.7 event; expected magnitude 6 earthquake does not follow during the warning interval.
- **1993–1994:** statistical and geological assumptions behind the Parkfield timing prediction receive prominent criticism and review.
- **28 September 2004:** magnitude 6 Parkfield earthquake occurs inside the dense instrument network, years later than the original high-probability window and without an obvious successful short-term precursor.
- **2004–2010:** French DEMETER satellite collects plasma and electromagnetic observations designed in part to investigate earthquake-associated phenomena.
- **2009:** Thomas, Love and Johnston publish a long-record reanalysis concluding that the famous Corralitos magnetic precursor was an instrument-system artefact; Campbell separately emphasizes natural magnetic disturbances.
- **11 March 2011:** Tohoku-Oki earthquake; subsequent claim of a TEC enhancement beginning about forty minutes before rupture ignites a long analysis dispute.
- **2012:** USGS-linked reanalysis of a reported Hector Mine ionospheric precursor concludes the apparent anomaly was ordinary global TEC variation.
- **2017:** DEMETER ion-density statistical analysis reports a pre-event population-level effect under specified nighttime conditions while cautioning that ion density alone is not a forecasting tool.
- **2021:** multiple JGR papers challenge claimed pre-seismic TEC enhancements, including demonstrations that detrending choices can generate apparent pre-event structure.
- **28 March 2025:** magnitude 7.7 Myanmar earthquake; subsequent studies report both radon and Swarm magnetic anomalies, renewing the need for prospective testing rather than event-by-event adjudication.
- **2025:** Zhang et al. report that ULF magnetic and CO anomalies add forecast performance to a temporal ETAS framework around Kakioka in a retrospective dataset with randomized-time testing.

## Questions the publication pass must answer quantitatively

1. What are the likelihood ratios, not merely p-values, for each precursor class under continuous deployment?
2. How do those likelihood ratios change after conditioning on geomagnetic indices, local weather, instrument health and seismic clustering?
3. Which physical mechanisms predict absolute amplitudes above detector thresholds?
4. How many independent earthquakes—not aftershocks treated as independent events—support each effect?
5. What does a strict walk-forward reconstruction do to published TEC and ULF results?
6. Can a multimodal model improve prospective log likelihood or information gain over strong ETAS/spatiotemporal baselines?
7. What minimum forecast gain would matter scientifically even if it remains operationally useless?
