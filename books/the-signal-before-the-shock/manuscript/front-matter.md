# The Signal Before the Shock

## Earthquake Precursors, Bad Clocks, and the Experiment Seismology Never Quite Finished

**Sven Hardy Benson**

There are subjects that become embarrassing before they become false.

Earthquake prediction is one of them.

The embarrassment was earned. Predictions were made after looking at the earthquake. Anomalies were selected because they sat near the event. Quiet instruments were forgotten. A curve was called strange because the earthquake gave the eye permission to find strangeness. The prediction literature accumulated a cabinet of marvels: lights, radon, radio noise, fish, snakes, clouds, electrical potentials, missing pets, ionospheric holes, ionospheric humps. Every large earthquake became an invitation to reopen the cabinet.

Then Parkfield happened.

Or rather, it did not happen when it was supposed to. A famously instrumented segment of the San Andreas Fault was expected to produce a moderate earthquake within a forecast window. The window passed. When the magnitude 6 earthquake finally came in 2004, the instruments were there and the obvious short-term precursors largely were not. That is one of the cleanest humiliations in modern geophysics, and therefore one of the most useful.

This book begins on the skeptical side of that humiliation. The United States Geological Survey says there is no convincing evidence for electromagnetic earthquake precursors after decades of work. I have no reason to improve that sentence by making it less severe.

But a scientific field can be wrong in two directions. It can believe a signal that is not there. It can also stop asking the right version of a question because too many people asked the wrong version badly.

The interesting question is no longer: *Can somebody find an anomaly before an earthquake?* Given enough channels, windows, filters, stations and earthquakes, the answer will be yes. The interesting question is: *Does a prospectively defined, physically interpretable measurement add forecast information beyond the information already carried by earthquake clustering, seasonality, instrumental drift, geomagnetic weather and the ordinary statistical ugliness of the Earth?*

That is a different problem. It demands denominators. It demands control stations. It demands locked algorithms. It demands predictions that exist before the earthquake. It demands negative sites. It demands space-weather covariates. It demands a mechanism that makes quantitative promises. Most cruelly, it demands that the community publish the beautiful anomaly next to the hundred boring days when the same instrument twitched and nothing happened.

Four evidentiary classes are used throughout. **Verified/documented facts** include the existence and design of Parkfield, the reported Corralitos ULF anomaly, later reanalyses identifying instrumental and geomagnetic problems, DEMETER's mission and published statistical analyses, and the current absence of an accepted operational precursor. **Credible but incomplete evidence** includes statistical associations reported in satellite ion-density, radon, ULF and geochemical studies whose prospective performance and causal interpretation remain unresolved. **Disputed allegations** include event-specific claims—Loma Prieta, Tohoku TEC and many famous animal reports—when independent analysis provides plausible artefactual or non-seismic explanations. **Speculative synthesis** includes models in which stressed crust generates charge carriers, gas transport or atmosphere-ionosphere coupling strong enough to produce detectable, forecast-useful signals.

The distinction is not cosmetic. It is the book.
