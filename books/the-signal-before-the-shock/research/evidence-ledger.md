# Four-Class Evidence Ledger

## 1 — Verified / documented facts

- Parkfield was explicitly instrumented as an earthquake prediction experiment; the expected timing model failed, and the 2004 event lacked an obvious successful short-term precursor in the dense network.
- Fraser-Smith et al. reported unusual Corralitos ULF magnetic behavior before Loma Prieta.
- Later long-record reanalyses identified serious instrument/system and regional geomagnetic explanations and rejected the canonical seismic-precursor interpretation.
- DEMETER flew from 2004–2010 and generated a large plasma/electromagnetic archive used for statistical earthquake-associated studies.
- Tohoku produced enormous post-event ionospheric effects; reported *pre*-event TEC enhancement is disputed.
- USGS currently states there is no convincing evidence for electromagnetic earthquake precursors.
- ETAS-family models encode powerful baseline information from earthquake clustering and therefore are a necessary benchmark for precursor forecast claims.

## 2 — Credible but incomplete

- Some DEMETER population studies report statistically detectable pre-event ion-density differences under limited conditions.
- 2025 GRL work reports added retrospective forecast performance from ULF magnetic + CO anomalies beyond a temporal ETAS baseline around Kakioka.
- Continuous radon monitoring has produced event-associated anomalies worthy of prospective network testing.
- Stress-dependent electrical phenomena in rocks are physically measurable in laboratory settings, but relevant field scaling is unresolved.

## 3 — Disputed / unconfirmed

- The Loma Prieta Corralitos anomaly as a true earthquake precursor.
- The claimed ~40-minute pre-Tohoku TEC enhancement as a genuine preseismic signal.
- Event-specific Swarm magnetic anomalies as causal earthquake precursors.
- Famous animal-behavior anecdotes as evidence of a unique non-seismic pre-rupture cue.
- Any operationally reliable present-day electromagnetic earthquake prediction method.

## 4 — Speculative synthesis / open conjecture

- A preparatory damage or fluid-transport phase may generate a *sequence* across gas, electric, magnetic, biological and ionospheric modalities.
- Multimodal signals might improve probabilistic forecasts even if no modality supports deterministic prediction.
- Causal-independence accounting may be more important than simply adding channels.
- A sealed, prospective global precursor competition could resolve classes of claims that event-centered papers cannot.

## Promotion rule

Evidence moves upward only through independent replication, prospective definition, explicit null controls and physically coherent scaling. Testimony, reputation, funding and media repetition do not change class.
