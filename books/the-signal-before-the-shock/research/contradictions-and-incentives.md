# Contradiction and Incentive Audit

## Core contradictions

**Loma Prieta:** original ULF paper reports a possible precursor; later reanalyses reject the interpretation using longer records, logbooks and regional geomagnetic context. Manuscript keeps both sequences and does not convert the later papers into proof that every ULF claim is impossible.

**Tohoku TEC:** Heki-style analyses report pre-event enhancement; multiple later studies show detrending/reference choices and post-event structure can create or erase apparent pre-event signals. Manuscript treats the precursor claim as disputed and makes causal filtering a future protocol requirement.

**DEMETER:** some population analyses report statistically significant pre-event ion-density effects, while operational prediction remains unsupported. Statistical existence and usable forecasting are separated.

**2025 Japan ULF+CO:** reported gains beyond ETAS are scientifically important but retrospective. The result is treated as a candidate for prospective replication, not a solved prediction method.

**2025 Myanmar:** radon and Swarm case studies create apparent multimodal convergence, but both are vulnerable to event-centered selection and distinct environmental backgrounds. Coincidence is not assumed independent.

## Incentives and selection effects

- Large earthquakes are publication magnets; null station-years are not.
- An anomaly becomes interesting after an earthquake, creating outcome-conditioned selection.
- Satellite researchers can inspect many variables, regions, radii, lags and thresholds.
- Prediction claims generate media attention disproportionate to incremental forecast gains.
- Skeptical institutional memory can create the opposite pressure: results may be dismissed by category before prospective testing.
- Funding rationales can encourage broad claims about societal warning benefits before physical effects are established.
- Commercial prediction services have incentives that differ from academic forecast validation.

## Disinformation / state-program note

Unlike intelligence-program subjects, there is no need in this manuscript to invoke hidden state knowledge. Earthquake-prediction programs in multiple countries can be explained by obvious civil-defense value. Government investment is evidence of perceived stakes, not efficacy. Claims of classified successful earthquake prediction require public evidence before inclusion.
