# The Signal Before the Shock

**Earthquake Precursors, Bad Clocks, and the Experiment Seismology Never Quite Finished**

Sven Hardy Benson

## Thesis

Earthquake prediction acquired a deservedly bad reputation by confusing retrospective anomalies with prospective information. But the scientific question was never cleanly killed. It fractured. Ground magnetometers, ionospheric TEC, satellite plasma measurements, radon, gas chemistry, strain, groundwater, animal behavior, and laboratory rock electrification now live in different literatures with different controls. The book asks whether any non-seismic precursor adds information beyond seismic clustering and base rates when tested prospectively, and whether the field can design an experiment in which failure means failure.

The book does **not** claim that earthquakes can currently be predicted from electromagnetic or other non-seismic precursors. USGS states that decades of research have produced no convincing evidence for electromagnetic earthquake precursors. That negative result is part of the story, not an obstacle to it.
