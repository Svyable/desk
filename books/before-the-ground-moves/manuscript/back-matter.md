# Back Matter

*Before the Ground Moves* is a book about evidence under terrible conditions for evidence: rare targets, flexible search spaces, noisy instruments, strong public stakes and almost irresistible hindsight.

The sourcing rule throughout is intentionally asymmetric. A declassified or official record can establish that a program existed or that an agency took a position. It cannot establish that the physical claim under investigation is true. A peer-reviewed case study can establish what was measured and how an analysis was performed. It cannot by itself establish prospective predictive value. Testimony and historical reports can justify an experiment. They do not become instrument records by surviving a long time.

The research trail accompanying this draft keeps four evidence classes separate: documented facts; credible but incomplete evidence; disputed or unconfirmed claims; and speculative synthesis. That classification is provisional by design. The point is not to freeze controversial subjects in permanent uncertainty. It is to make clear what kind of new evidence would actually move a claim.

Several distinctions do much of the work in the manuscript.

An **earthquake precursor** is a measurable change occurring before dynamic rupture and hypothesized to carry information about a future earthquake. An **earthquake forecast** assigns probabilities to future seismic outcomes. A **prediction**, in the strict usage adopted here, specifies a future event within stated time, location and magnitude bounds that can be prospectively scored. **Earthquake early warning** begins after rupture starts and attempts to warn locations not yet reached by the strongest shaking.

The manuscript also distinguishes a physical association from a prediction. A phenomenon can be caused by earthquake preparation and still have poor predictive specificity if it also occurs frequently for other reasons. Conversely, a statistical predictor can show skill without proving the mechanism researchers believe it uses.

The detailed source log, chronology, evidence matrix, contradiction audit and publication gate live in the book's `research/` directory. Links there are preserved in usable form so the next pass can work from primary papers, official program records and technical documentation rather than from remembered summaries.

The strongest unresolved question is deliberately narrower than “Can earthquakes be predicted?” It is whether any pre-rupture electrical, chemical, geodetic, biological or ionospheric measurement adds reproducible prospective information beyond established seismicity and geodetic forecasting, after realistic false-alarm rates and environmental controls are included.

That question can be answered experimentally.

It has not been answered merely because somebody once saw something strange before the ground moved.
