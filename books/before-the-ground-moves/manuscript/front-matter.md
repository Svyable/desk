# Before the Ground Moves

*Earthquake Lights, Electric Rock, and the Dangerous Hope of a Warning*

Sven Hardy Benson

There is a category of fact that science handles badly for good reasons. It is rare, brief, badly instrumented, easy to imitate, historically contaminated by folklore, and attached to a claim people desperately want to be true.

Earthquake lights live there.

This book is not an argument that earthquakes can presently be predicted. They cannot, in the ordinary meaning of that sentence: a reliable specification of where, when, and how large, with a false-alarm rate low enough to act on. Nor is it an argument that every strange light filmed near an earthquake belongs to one geophysical phenomenon. Power transformers arc. Clouds flash. Cameras bloom. Social media supplies old footage to new disasters with impressive speed.

The narrower possibility is stranger and more useful. Some luminous reports associated with earthquakes may describe real electrical phenomena produced by stressed crust. Laboratory rock physics supplies mechanisms worth taking seriously. Historical case work supplies patterns worth testing. Satellite and ground measurements supply tantalizing anomalies. None of those propositions, separately or together, grants us an earthquake alarm.

That gap is the book.

I use four evidence classes throughout. **Class 1** is documented observation, reproducible experiment, instrument record, official program record, or peer-reviewed result whose provenance can be inspected. **Class 2** is credible but incomplete observation: a contemporaneous witness, photograph, video, or analysis whose chain is plausible but does not independently establish mechanism. **Class 3** is disputed or unconfirmed claim: alleged precursor, extraordinary interpretation, retrospective correlation, or anecdote lacking adequate controls. **Class 4** is synthesis: a proposed connection, mechanism, experiment, or implication that remains conjectural.

These classes are not a ladder on which Class 4 eventually graduates into Class 1 by repetition. A beautiful mechanism does not authenticate an old witness. A real light does not make it predictive. A satellite anomaly does not become a precursor because an earthquake later occurred beneath it.

The book has a defeat condition. If well-controlled prospective monitoring shows that candidate electromagnetic and luminous precursors do not outperform appropriate null models once geomagnetic activity, weather, infrastructure, sensor artifacts, multiple testing, and the enormous denominator of earthquakes are included, then the forecasting strand should contract accordingly. If, conversely, prospective preregistered networks repeatedly detect a physically coherent signal before earthquakes and beat those controls out of sample, the skeptical boundary must move.

The point is not to protect mystery. It is to make mystery pay rent.
