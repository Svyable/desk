# Evidence ledger — Before the Ground Moves

## Working thesis

Earthquake lights are a useful hard case in anomaly science because three propositions that are usually bundled must be tested separately: whether unusual luminous/electromagnetic phenomena sometimes accompany earthquakes; whether stressed crust can physically generate them; and whether any such signal is reliable enough before rupture to forecast an earthquake. A real phenomenon can survive while the prediction claim fails.

## Evidence classes

### Class 1 — verified/documented/reproducible

- St-Laurent, Derr & Freund (2006), *Physics and Chemistry of the Earth*, proposes stress-activated positive-hole charge carriers and surface corona discharge as a mechanism for earthquake lights. USGS Publications Warehouse record and DOI: https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70028206 ; https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pce.2006.02.003
- Freund (2000), *Journal of Geophysical Research: Solid Earth*, reports time-resolved charge generation/propagation experiments in igneous rocks and connects mobile charge carriers to possible earthquake-related electrical signals. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1029/1999JB900423
- Thériault, St-Laurent, Freund & Derr (2014), *Seismological Research Letters* 85(1), 159–178, analyzes 65 historical earthquake-light cases and reports a strong association in its selected catalog with rift environments/subvertical faults. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1785/0220130059
- CNES's DEMETER mission was explicitly designed to investigate electromagnetic/ionospheric disturbances associated with earthquakes and volcanic activity. Program existence establishes a serious measurement effort, not successful earthquake prediction. Primary CNES mission documentation to recover and page-index.
- Current scientific reviews continue to state that reliable earthquake precursors have not been demonstrated at the level required for an early-warning/prediction system. This is the baseline the manuscript must preserve.

### Class 2 — credible but incomplete

- L'Aquila 2009 pre-mainshock light report attributed to a resident who reportedly moved his family outside roughly two hours before the earthquake. Preserve as a reported case until original interview/publication provenance is recovered; do not treat the retelling as an instrument record.
- Tagish Lake/Yukon luminous-orb photographs used in the 2014 case literature. Need original image provenance, date uncertainty, photographer statement, astronomical/weather controls and exact relationship to seismicity.
- Pisco 2007 security-camera footage reported as luminous flashes around the earthquake. Requires frame-level timing against seismic-wave arrival and local electrical-infrastructure reconstruction before classification.

### Class 3 — disputed/unconfirmed

- Individual social-media videos labeled `earthquake lights` without verified time/location/original file provenance.
- Claims that a particular ionospheric, thermal-infrared, radon or radio anomaly predicted a named earthquake when the analysis was selected retrospectively and lacks an adequate non-earthquake denominator.
- Claims that unusual animal behavior supplies a reliable operational earthquake forecast. Animal sensing may be biologically interesting without satisfying prediction requirements.
- Claims that earthquake lights explain a broad class of UFO/UAP observations. Phenomenological resemblance is not attribution.

### Class 4 — speculative synthesis/open conjecture

- A subset of historically reported luminous anomalies may be surface manifestations of a wider lithosphere-atmosphere-ionosphere electrical coupling process under particular geology and stress regimes.
- Multiple precursor classes sometimes treated independently—surface potential, corona/light, air ionization, radio noise, ionospheric perturbation—could in principle be different measurement surfaces of one underlying charge-transport process. This is a unifying hypothesis, not established fact.
- The best future test may be a deliberately failure-seeking network that combines optical, electric-field, magnetometer, radio, weather, grid and seismic measurements and preregisters what counts as a hit before earthquakes occur.

## Contradiction audit

1. **Rare-event selection:** A catalog of reported lights is not a random sample of earthquakes. Geological clustering may partly reflect where observers, archives or memorable events occurred.
2. **Mechanism plurality:** Positive-hole transport is not the only proposed electrical mechanism. Granular/frictional charging experiments demonstrate that mechanical failure can generate voltage without establishing Freund's specific crustal mechanism.
3. **Timing heterogeneity:** Reports occur before, during and after earthquakes. Effects produced by seismic shaking cannot serve as pre-event predictors.
4. **Infrastructure confounding:** Transformers, transmission lines and urban electrical failures can produce dramatic flashes during shaking.
5. **Atmospheric/space-weather confounding:** Lightning, sprites, aurora, geomagnetic activity and ionospheric variability can mimic or overwhelm proposed signals.
6. **Prediction leap:** Even perfect proof that some earthquakes produce lights would not establish sensitivity, specificity or actionable lead time.
7. **Publication/multiple-testing bias:** Searching many frequencies, altitudes, pixels, time windows and precursor variables around a known earthquake can manufacture apparently unusual pre-event patterns.

## Incentive audit

- Researchers have incentives to emphasize positive precursor correlations because null results are less publishable and prediction is consequential.
- Agencies have incentives to investigate potentially useful precursors but mission funding is not evidence that the hypothesized signal works.
- News outlets and social platforms reward spectacular `mystery lights before earthquake` footage and often erase timing/provenance uncertainty.
- Skeptics can overcorrect by treating every luminous report as electrical-grid failure without case-specific infrastructure evidence.
- Communities exposed to earthquake risk have an unusually strong incentive to believe in warning signs because the desired benefit—time—is enormous.

## Defeat conditions

The central forecasting argument weakens materially if preregistered prospective monitoring shows candidate signals do not outperform matched null periods after correcting for space weather, meteorology, infrastructure, sensor artifacts and multiple testing. The physical-phenomenon argument weakens if purported high-quality luminous cases collapse under provenance/timing reconstruction or if laboratory charge mechanisms cannot scale under plausible crustal conditions.

The skeptical boundary must also move if independent networks prospectively detect a repeatable signal with useful location/lead-time information and a measured false-alarm rate that survives out-of-sample testing.
