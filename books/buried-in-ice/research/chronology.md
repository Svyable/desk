# Chronology

- **1951** — United States and Denmark enter the defense agreement governing U.S. military facilities in Greenland.
- **1959** — Camp Century construction begins on the northwestern Greenland Ice Sheet.
- **1960–1961** — Camp facilities and PM-2A nuclear plant come into operation; under-ice engineering work expands.
- **1962** — Experimental subsurface railway and related engineering feed the feasibility case for a larger under-ice network. Army strategic planning for Project Iceworm reaches senior review.
- **1963** — PM-2A is deactivated after roughly thirty-three months of operation. Iceworm does not proceed to deployment.
- **1964** — Reactor removal and shift away from year-round Camp Century operations.
- **1966** — Camp Century drilling reaches the base of the ice sheet and recovers basal sediment beneath roughly 1.4 km of ice.
- **1967** — Camp Century is abandoned with major physical infrastructure and multiple waste streams left in the ice.
- **1969** — Last reported USACE survey visit in the Colgan et al. reconstruction.
- **1990s** — Danish historical investigation and declassification bring Project Iceworm much more clearly into the public record.
- **2011 / 2014** — NASA Operation IceBridge radar profiles record strong anomalous reflections consistent with buried Camp Century infrastructure.
- **August 4, 2016** — Colgan et al. publish the climate/waste-remobilization analysis in *Geophysical Research Letters*.
- **2021** — Christ et al. publish the Camp Century basal-sediment study in *PNAS*, documenting past ice-free and vegetated conditions.
- **2026** — Camp Century core research remains active, including an inter-journal special issue on new science from the 1966 core.
