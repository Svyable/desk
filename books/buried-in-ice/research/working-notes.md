# Working Notes

## Reader promise

By the end of the book, the reader should understand why Camp Century cannot be reduced to either a “secret nuclear base” story or a climate-warning anecdote. It is a single physical site through which four different systems can be inspected: Cold War strategic secrecy, Arctic engineering, long-lived waste responsibility, and the deep-time instability of the Greenland Ice Sheet.

## Distinct contribution

Nearby Desk books have recently dealt with orbital debris, nuclear recovery, anomalous sensing, animal-machine guidance, earthquake luminosity and nuclear medical devices. This book's empirical core is different: firn mechanics, under-ice construction, portable military nuclear power, alliance secrecy, ice-core science, basal sediment, paleoclimate and climate-driven remobilization of abandoned infrastructure.

The book's distinctive collision is that Camp Century's military builders relied on the apparent stability of ice twice—first as a strategic construction medium, then as a disposal medium—while the scientific program supported at the same site ultimately produced evidence that northwestern Greenland had not been continuously ice-covered over deep time.

## Strongest competing explanation

Camp Century may be less a story about hubris or secrecy than about ordinary technological selection. The Army tested an unusual basing concept, found its maintenance burden unattractive relative to Minuteman and Polaris, removed the high-value reactor equipment, and left a remote site under disposal assumptions reasonable for the era. Later climate science changed the boundary conditions. If this explanation accounts for most of the record, the manuscript must not inflate the case into deliberate recklessness.

## What evidence could change the book

- Danish records showing full informed approval of the Iceworm strategic concept would substantially narrow the secrecy chapter.
- A complete Iceworm decision file showing service rivalry or missile competition overwhelmingly dominated ice mechanics would revise the engineering-centered account of cancellation.
- Modern sampling showing the principal waste inventories were removed or remain immobile would reduce the environmental stakes.
- Updated climate modeling showing persistent accumulation at the site over much longer horizons would narrow the remobilization thesis.
- Revision of the Camp Century basal-sediment chronology would change the deep-time argument.

## Chapter progression

Chapters 1–3 establish the public camp, physical medium and layered mission without making the visible science fraudulent. Chapters 4–8 scale into Iceworm, reactor logistics, ice deformation and alliance politics. Chapters 9–10 turn abandonment into an environmental-timescale problem. Chapters 11–12 follow the core from military logistics into paleoclimate. Chapters 13–15 return to the buried camp through radar, climate modeling and responsibility. Chapters 16–18 use the evidence streams together without converting them into a universal theory.

## Prose risks

- Overusing “ice moves” as a refrain. Expansion should replace repetition with engineering measurements and named reports.
- Turning every chapter ending into an aphorism. Several current architecture-pass endings should be broken or quieted during expansion.
- Treating “secrecy” as an agent. Name offices, documents and decisions.
- Making the Greenlandic political dimension decorative. Recover Greenland-language or Greenland-government sources before finalizing.
- Letting modern climate concern flatten what participants plausibly knew in 1960–1967.

## Current state

Architecture-first draft. Eighteen chapter files exist, but none yet meets the Desk 3,000-word chapter gate. Expansion must come from primary documents, quantitative reconstructions and actor-level chronology rather than restating the book's central reversal.
