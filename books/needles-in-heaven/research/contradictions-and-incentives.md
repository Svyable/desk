# Contradictions and Incentive Audit

## Functional success versus strategic adoption

The radio experiment can succeed while the architecture loses. Avoid calling West Ford a failure merely because active satellites displaced it.

## Astronomy protest versus measured astronomy harm

Scientists had legitimate reasons to oppose the precedent and uncertainty. Post-deployment reviews did not find the catastrophic interference some feared. Preserve both facts.

## Temporary design versus persistent debris

The lifetime argument was strongest for properly dispersed individual dipoles. Clumps created by failed or imperfect dispersal had different orbital behavior. Do not collapse these populations.

## National security versus transparency

Military communications requirements created legitimate pressure for secrecy and speed. The same secrecy weakened outside communities' ability to evaluate shared-environment risk. Neither incentive proves bad faith.

## Modern debris standards projected backward

Do not judge 1961 procedures as if contemporary orbital-debris standards already existed. Do show that lifetime and interference concerns were known enough to be debated contemporaneously.

## Space-law origin stories

Policy histories reward simple causal stories. Treat West Ford as one pressure among many unless archival material directly ties the program to specific treaty language.
