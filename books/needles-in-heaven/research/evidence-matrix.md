# Evidence Matrix

## Class 1 — verified / documented

- MIT Lincoln Laboratory conducted Project West Ford under U.S. military sponsorship.
- The project pursued long-range microwave communication using orbiting copper-wire dipoles as passive scatterers.
- A 1961 deployment attempt failed to disperse as intended.
- A May 1963 experiment successfully deployed a very large dipole population and demonstrated communications between ground stations.
- Astronomers objected before and during the program on optical/radio-interference and precedent grounds.
- Space Science Board review reported no significant optical or radio astronomy interference from the 1963 belt.
- NASA later tracked/catalogued surviving West Ford debris clumps.
- Active communications satellites overtook the architecture and the belt was not developed into an operational repeated-deployment system.

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete

- Exact dipole count in the successful 1963 deployment; sources variously summarize several hundred million and often cite ~480 million. Freeze only after reconciling program papers.
- Exact dispersal fraction and number of clumps from each flight.
- Full chronology and wording of White House conditions placed on the 1963 test.
- Degree to which West Ford accelerated later international space-governance language rather than merely contributing to the policy atmosphere.

## Class 3 — disputed / not established

- That West Ford caused the 1967 Outer Space Treaty.
- That the project permanently damaged astronomy.
- That hundreds of millions of individual intact needles remain in orbit today.
- That the program was hidden from the scientific community until after deployment.
- That the experiment was abandoned primarily because of protest rather than technological competition.

## Class 4 — speculative synthesis

- West Ford as an early environmental-governance case for intentional modification of near-Earth space.
- The project's dispersal failure as an early example of environmental acceptability depending on hardware state, not merely material choice.
- Astronomers' mobilization as a precursor to modern organized defense of observational environments against large-scale orbital infrastructure.
- A distinction between freedom to use a commons and freedom to alter its physical conditions.
