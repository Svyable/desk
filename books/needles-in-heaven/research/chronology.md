# Chronology

- **1958** — Walter E. Morrow and Lincoln Laboratory begin the orbital-scatter concept that becomes Project West Ford.
- **1958–1961** — Engineering development and growing astronomical concern.
- **October 21, 1961** — First orbital deployment attempt; dipoles do not disperse as intended.
- **1961–1963** — Program redesign, scientific review, political negotiation and renewed test planning.
- **1962** — Telstar demonstrates active satellite communications, sharpening the competitive technological context.
- **May 9, 1963** — Successful West Ford deployment begins the second major orbital experiment.
- **1963** — Communications tests conducted using the artificial dipole belt; optical/radio effects monitored.
- **1964** — Technical results published in Proceedings of the IEEE; Space Science Board conclusions reported publicly.
- **Mid-1960s** — Individual dipole belt substantially decays/disperses; active satellite communications dominate future architecture.
- **1967** — Outer Space Treaty enters signature era; do not claim direct West Ford causation without document chain.
- **2001** — European debris analysis later cited in secondary histories revisits clumped West Ford material; retrieve original report.
- **2013** — NASA Orbital Debris Quarterly News publishes `West Ford Needles: Where are They Now?`
- **2025** — Barentine publishes a detailed history of astronomical mobilization around West Ford.
