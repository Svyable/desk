# Chapter 4 research — GLP-1 drugs and the Ozempic economy

Traces semaglutide/tirzepatide's regulatory timeline, the Novo Nordisk/Eli Lilly valuation swing, the SELECT cardiovascular-outcomes trial, the bariatric-surgery volume decline, and the unresolved Medicare/employer coverage fight.

Primary-source anchors strengthened on 2026-09-02:

- FDA, March 8, 2024: Wegovy (semaglutide) received a new indication to reduce cardiovascular death, heart attack, and stroke in adults with established cardiovascular disease and overweight or obesity. FDA reported major adverse cardiovascular events in 6.5% of Wegovy participants versus 8.0% on placebo in the supporting randomized trial. This is an outcome indication, not merely a weight-loss claim.
- FDA, December 20, 2024: Zepbound (tirzepatide) became the first medication approved to treat moderate-to-severe obstructive sleep apnea in adults with obesity. The approval rested on two randomized trials totaling 469 adults without type 2 diabetes and broadens the institutional consequences of incretin drugs beyond weight management alone.
- Novo Nordisk Annual Report 2025: the company reported 31% obesity-portfolio sales growth at constant exchange rates in 2025, 3.6 million people reached with obesity medicines, and Wegovy availability in 52 countries by year-end. These are issuer-reported commercial figures and should be attributed as such rather than treated as independent market measurement.

Editorial implication: the chapter is strongest when it treats GLP-1 medicines as a case of category migration. A product first understood primarily through diabetes and weight loss acquired cardiovascular and sleep-apnea indications, forcing insurers, employers, surgical specialties, drug manufacturers, and public programs to reconsider which budget and benefit category bears the cost. That institutional repricing is better supported than a simple claim that one company or molecule permanently "won" the market.

Counterevidence / limits:

- SELECT enrolled people with overweight or obesity and established cardiovascular disease; its result is not evidence that semaglutide produces the same cardiovascular benefit in every person using it for weight loss.
- Zepbound's sleep-apnea approval applies to adults with obesity and moderate-to-severe OSA and was studied alongside reduced-calorie diet and increased physical activity. It does not imply that pharmacotherapy replaces PAP or surgery for every patient.
- Company annual reports are primary for the company's own sales and reach but interested sources for competitive interpretation.
- Market-cap and market-share snapshots remain time-sensitive and should be dated. They are evidence of repricing, not proof of durable dominance.

Falsification note: the chapter's "institutions racing to catch up" argument weakens if coverage rules, manufacturing capacity, and benefit design converge quickly and durably around the expanded indications, and if the observed market revaluation proves to be a short-lived competitive swing rather than a persistent institutional adjustment. It also weakens if later randomized evidence fails to reproduce important non-weight endpoints outside the specific populations in which they were demonstrated.
