# Chapter 3 research — the reclassification of aging

## Claims supported

Chapter 3 argues that aging is undergoing a real but partial, contested regulatory and scientific reclassification, anchored on three pillars: the hallmarks-of-aging framework, the TAME trial's regulatory workaround, and the ICD-11 XT9T extension code (deliberately distinguished from the withdrawn standalone "old age" code).

## Sources

- López-Otín et al., "The Hallmarks of Aging," Cell 153 (2013): established nine hallmarks; the field-defining taxonomy the chapter treats as organizational/rhetorical rather than a settled causal theory.
- López-Otín et al., "Hallmarks of Aging: An Expanding Universe," Cell 186 (Jan 2023): expanded to twelve hallmarks; the chapter uses the growth from nine to twelve as evidence the list is a live working document.
- "The hoverfly and the wasp: A critique of the hallmarks of aging as a paradigm," PMC7611451: the published critique the chapter cites for the framework's circularity and rhetorical-versus-explanatory function.
- American Federation for Aging Research, "TAME Trial" overview (afar.org/tame-trial): trial design (~3,000 participants, 65–79, 6-year composite-disease endpoint), Barzilai's role, and its still-unfunded status.
- Fight Aging!, "The TAME Trial for Metformin Remains Only Partially Funded" (April 2024): confirms the trial's incomplete funding as of 2024.
- NPR, "A cheap drug may slow down aging" (April 22, 2024): $30–50M funding estimate — flagged in the chapter's discussion as one of two conflicting figures.
- Alliance for Aging Research, "Dr. Nir Barzilai on the TAME Study": competing $75M budget figure ($40M private donation + $35M AFAR) — the chapter states both estimates and flags the discrepancy explicitly rather than picking one.
- WHO ICD-11 browser, extension code XT9T ("Ageing related"): the chapter's basis for describing XT9T as a modifier/extension code, not a standalone diagnosis.
- The Lancet Healthy Longevity, "How 'old age' was withdrawn as a diagnosis from ICD-11" (2022): the withdrawal of the standalone MG2A code after diagnostic-overshadowing concerns — used as the chapter's central "partial, contested" nuance.

## Falsification note

The chapter's claim would weaken if: TAME secures full funding and its composite endpoint fails to show benefit (undercutting the geroscience hypothesis); or if WHO further narrows or removes the XT9T extension code, showing the reclassification reversing rather than advancing.
