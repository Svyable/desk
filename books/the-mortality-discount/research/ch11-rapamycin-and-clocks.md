# Chapter 11 research — off-label rapamycin and epigenetic clocks

Covers rapamycin's discovery and mTOR mechanism, mouse-lifespan evidence (including the NIA Interventions Testing Program's cross-site design, cited as well-established general scientific background), and the off-label self-experimentation community; the Horvath clock (2013, Genome Biology) and GrimAge (2019, Aging); and the direct-to-consumer biological-age testing industry (TruDiagnostic, Elysium Health) with the Mendelian-randomization critique of what these clocks causally measure. Falsification note: weakens if a large, controlled human rapamycin trial for aging-related outcomes reports a clear, unambiguous benefit with a well-tolerated dosing protocol, or if a validated causal link is established between specific clock CpG sites and aging mechanisms.
