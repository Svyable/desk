# Chapter 9 research — the global fertility collapse

Covers South Korea's fertility rate (0.72 in 2023, 0.75 in 2024, 0.8 in 2025) and Eberstadt's 2050 projections; Japan's 2025 census (123,049,524, -3.09M, 30.1% in greater Tokyo) and the genkai shūraku concept (Ōno 1991) plus Nagoro; China's 2022 first population decline since 1961 and the January 2026 record-low birth data, alongside the Our World in Data nuance that fertility decline predates and outlasted the one-child policy; and the UN World Population Prospects 2024 revision (peak ~10.3B around 2084) versus the IHME/Lancet 2020 independent projection (peak ~9.73B around 2064). Falsification note: weakens if South Korea's fertility rebound (0.75→0.8) continues for several more years rather than reverting once the post-pandemic marriage cohort is exhausted, or if a future UN revision moves substantially toward slower decline rather than continuing its historical pattern of moving toward faster decline.
