## Thesis

In 1963 the United States deliberately dispersed an enormous population of hair-thin copper dipoles into orbit so radio could be bounced off an artificial belt around Earth. Project West Ford is useful now not because it was a hidden superweapon, but because it was stranger: a technically serious, publicly disputed military experiment that changed the planetary environment before humanity had developed mature rules for doing so.

The book uses West Ford as a control case for claims about secret frontier technology. Real exotic programs leave engineering constraints, launch hardware, institutional conflict, scientific criticism, geopolitical consequences, successor technologies, failure modes, and physical residue. The central question is not merely what governments can hide. It is what a civilization permits itself to alter when strategic urgency arrives before governance.

## Evidence classes

1. **Verified / documented:** U.S. Air Force sponsorship; MIT Lincoln Laboratory engineering; 1961 deployment failure; 1963 deployment; resonant copper dipoles; radio experiments; contemporary scientific objections; surviving museum hardware and orbital-debris scholarship.
2. **Credible but incomplete:** exact deployed/dispersed counts vary by source and definition; operational communications performance and the long-tail debris population require primary-source reconciliation.
3. **Disputed / unconfirmed:** claims that West Ford was principally a concealed weapons experiment, weather-control system, or precursor to exotic electromagnetic manipulation are not established by the evidence currently collected.
4. **Speculative synthesis:** West Ford may be a useful historical baseline for evaluating modern claims of classified planetary-scale technology because genuine programs generate a measurable 'residue budget' even when their purpose is sensitive.
