*What the Body Clock Still Knows in an Always-On World*

Human physiology keeps a roughly twenty-four-hour clock, built from genes, run by a hierarchy of clocks in the brain and body, and synchronized mainly by light. Civil institutions — shift schedules, hospitals, cockpits, trading floors, school bells, chemotherapy suites, and now markets and AI systems that never close — increasingly demand behavior that ignores it.

This is the working thesis of *The Third Shift*: the mismatch between biological time and civil time is not evenly distributed. It concentrates in a predictable window of biological night, and it shows up in measurable ways — in accident investigations, disease risk, error rates, athletic performance, and drug efficacy. The book traces the science of that internal clock from its first proof in an underground bunker to its molecular machinery, then follows the institutions — nuclear power, aviation, medicine, shipping, professional sports, oncology, and now the 24/7 economy — that have had to reckon, unevenly and often only after tragedy, with what the science means for how they operate.
