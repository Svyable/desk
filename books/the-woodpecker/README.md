# The Woodpecker

## The Soviet Radar Everyone Could Hear and Nobody Could See

**Sven Hardy Benson**

### Thesis

From 1976 into the late Cold War, shortwave listeners around the world heard a hard repetitive pulse that became known as the Russian Woodpecker. The source was secret, but the signal was public. The strongest explanation—eventually borne out by the physical and historical record—is Soviet over-the-horizon radar: a strategic-warning system using the ionosphere to see beyond the geometric horizon. The interesting story is not that a conspiracy theory was secretly true. It is that a classified machine could hide its purpose while broadcasting one of the loudest clues imaginable, creating an evidentiary ecology in which technically correct inference, folklore, intelligence analysis, interference complaints, and extravagant claims about weather and minds all occupied the same spectrum.

This book asks what happens when **the secret is observable but not legible**.

### Evidence classes

1. **Verified / documented:** the Woodpecker signal existed; it occupied HF spectrum and caused widespread interference; Western technical observers identified an over-the-horizon-radar explanation during the Cold War; Soviet/Russian records and the surviving Duga installations support the radar identification; OTH radar is physically real and uses ionospheric propagation to detect targets beyond line of sight.
2. **Credible but incomplete:** exact operational performance, launch-detection reliability, system readiness, power, site-by-site chronology, Soviet internal assessments, and the relative contribution of Duga versus satellite early warning remain unevenly documented in accessible primary sources.
3. **Disputed / unconfirmed:** claims that Duga was principally a weather-control system, mind-control transmitter, population-behavior experiment, earthquake weapon, or intentional global communications jammer are not established by the evidence assembled here.
4. **Speculative synthesis:** Duga is a useful model of an “audible secret”: secrecy can conceal institutional purpose without concealing physical side effects, and those side effects can simultaneously enable correct inference and feed false explanatory systems.

### Defeat condition

The book's central synthesis weakens substantially if primary Soviet records show that the globally observed Woodpecker signal was not principally associated with the Duga/5N32 over-the-horizon radar system, or if the supposed gap between public observability and institutional legibility disappears under a better reconstruction of what Western governments, engineers, and civilian listeners actually knew at the time.
