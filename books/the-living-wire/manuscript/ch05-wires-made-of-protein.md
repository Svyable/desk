# Wires Made of Protein

By the time researchers isolated the conductive sheath, one of the oldest distinctions in biology had become inconvenient: structural proteins belong to bodies; electron-transfer proteins belong to metabolism. Cable bacteria had built something that was both.

The periplasmic fibers are proteinaceous conductive structures. In the 2019 measurements, extracted fiber sheaths preserved conduction. That result shifted attention away from a hypothetical soluble carrier and toward a durable biological material.

Protein conduction is not unique in the broadest sense. Redox proteins move charge. Bacterial pili and extracellular cytochrome networks can participate in electron transfer. What is unusual is the combination of conductivity, distance, multicellular continuity, and architecture.

The fibers sit in a protected compartment between membranes rather than being naked in the sediment. Their arrangement is parallel. Their junctions are organized. The design resembles infrastructure because it performs an infrastructural function: it allows the metabolic state of one region of the filament to depend on another region far away.

There is a conceptual danger in celebrating conductivity numbers without context. A material can have impressive conductivity and still be biologically irrelevant if it is measured after extraction under conditions unlike life. The decisive evidence therefore comes from convergence: sediment electrochemistry, intact filament current, extracted sheath measurements, microscopy, chemical perturbation, and physiological behavior all point toward the same structure.

That convergence is stronger than any one spectacular micrograph.

Biomaterials researchers have already begun treating cable-bacterium skeletons as electrodes. This is a legitimate technological direction, but it is not yet evidence that the organism evolved for general-purpose conduction. The natural function remains embedded in redox ecology.

The wire exists because mud is stratified.

That is the detail worth keeping. The conductor is not a biological party trick. It is a response to a world in which the fuel and the oxidant occupy different places.
