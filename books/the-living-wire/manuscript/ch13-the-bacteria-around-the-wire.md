# The Bacteria Around the Wire

A strange observation accumulated around cable filaments: other bacteria gathered near them.

The 2023 Nature Communications study describing bacterial ‘flocks’ around cable bacteria used microscopy, metagenomics, laser microdissection, and Raman work to investigate these associations. Many of the associated organisms appeared to be aerobes even though the interactions could occur below the ordinary reach of oxygen.

One proposed interpretation is that neighboring bacteria may transfer electrons to the cable bacterium and thereby gain indirect access to a distant electron acceptor.

This is a profound possibility, but the evidence boundary matters. Association is strong. The metabolic routes are plausible. Direct accounting of every electron from partner metabolism through the cable to oxygen remains harder.

If the interpretation holds broadly, cable bacteria are more than self-contained distributed organisms. They are infrastructure for communities.

That would change the unit of ecological analysis again. The cable would become a shared electron sink, a biological utility line through which unrelated cells can dispose of reducing power.

Nature already contains extensive metabolic outsourcing. Microbes exchange hydrogen, formate, sulfur compounds, vitamins, and other metabolites. Electron sharing through a conductive partner would be another form of syntrophy, but one with unusually little material transport.

The distinction is worth dwelling on. Molecule exchange requires making, moving, and consuming a chemical carrier. Direct or mediated electron transfer can couple metabolism without moving equivalent bulk matter.

The flocking bacteria suggest that once evolution builds a wire, neighbors may learn to plug into it.
