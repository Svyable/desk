# Evidence Ledger

## Class 1 — verified / documented

- Cable bacteria conduct electrons over centimetre-scale distances in redox-stratified sediments.
- Direct electrical measurements have been made on intact filaments and extracted conductive sheaths.
- Periplasmic fibers form the conductive pathway and continue across cell junctions.
- Sulfur-ligated nickel is associated with the conductive protein core; perturbing nickel chemistry changes conductivity.
- Cable-bacterium activity produces characteristic pH and electric-potential gradients and alters sulfur and iron chemistry.
- Cable bacteria occur in marine and freshwater systems.
- Seasonal cable-bacterium activity can alter iron–phosphorus dynamics in sediments.
- A 2023 study documented diverse bacterial flocks associated with cable filaments.
- A 2026 study showed extracellular electron transfer by Electronema aureum GS to electrodes/insoluble acceptors under anoxic conditions, including direct and riboflavin-mediated pathways.

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete

- Some associated microbes may use cable bacteria as conduits to distant oxygen or other acceptors.
- Cable-bacterium activity may substantially suppress methane emissions in particular freshwater sediment systems through sulfur-cycle restructuring.
- Long-range electron transfer may be more globally important in sediment biogeochemistry than existing surveys capture.

## Class 3 — disputed / unconfirmed

- Cable bacteria as globally important regulators of climate-scale methane flux.
- Cable filaments as universal community-scale electrical utilities.
- Claims that extracted conductivity under laboratory conditions fully specifies in vivo charge-transfer physics.

## Class 4 — speculative synthesis

- Distributed respiration may be a broader evolutionary strategy in poorly sampled subsurface biospheres.
- Nonlocal mineral-redox patterns might form a useful biosignature class.
- Extraterrestrial metabolisms could bridge spatially separated redox couples through conductive biological structures.
- Biological individuality may need an explicitly electrical criterion in some multicellular microbial systems.
