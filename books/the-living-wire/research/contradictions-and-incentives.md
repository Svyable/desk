# Contradictions and Incentives

The cable-bacteria literature is unusually strong by anomalous-research standards because its core claims converge across microsensors, microscopy, direct electrical measurements, spectroscopy, geochemistry, genetics, and growth experiments. The main epistemic risks are therefore not fraud narratives but overextension.

## Tensions

- Ex vivo conductivity is not automatically identical to in vivo electron-transfer behavior.
- Association of neighboring bacteria with filaments does not by itself prove that every partner respires through the cable.
- Local methane suppression does not establish global climate importance.
- Electrode respiration in a controlled reactor does not prove equivalent mineral respiration dominates natural sediments.
- The exact microscopic charge-transport mechanism in nickel-containing fibers remains less resolved than the existence of long-range conduction.

## Incentives

- “Living wire” language attracts attention and can compress important mechanistic distinctions.
- Bioelectronics applications create incentives to emphasize material performance outside ecological context.
- Astrobiological extrapolation can outrun the terrestrial data.
- Climate framing can inflate local sediment effects into global claims before scaling is justified.

The manuscript should preserve the extraordinary core result while resisting these expansions unless data catch up.
