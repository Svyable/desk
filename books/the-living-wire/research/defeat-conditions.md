# Defeat Conditions and Research Priorities

The book’s strongest synthesis would need revision if intact-filament current were shown to arise primarily from an artefactual pathway unrelated to physiological electron transfer, or if the nickel-containing fibers were demonstrated not to carry biologically relevant current in vivo. Current evidence makes those outcomes unlikely, but they remain conceptually decisive.

High-value next work:

- derive full electron and energy budgets for intact filaments under environmental conditions;
- resolve the molecular charge-transfer mechanism in the nickel protein fibers;
- quantify failure tolerance at cell-cell junctions;
- directly trace electrons from associated bacteria through cable filaments to terminal acceptors;
- test natural mineral acceptors against controlled electrodes;
- map global abundance with methods that preserve filament continuity;
- quantify methane effects across independent ecosystems and seasons;
- develop spatial geochemical signatures that distinguish electrogenic cable activity from conventional diffusion-reaction systems;
- compare cable-bacterium architecture with other long-range biological conductors without collapsing distinct mechanisms.
