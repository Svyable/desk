# Back Matter

*The Far Room* treats the U.S. remote-viewing programs as a documented history of government exploration and an unresolved methodological literature, not as proof of paranormal perception or as proof that every reported anomaly was ordinary error.

The strongest public record runs from the early SRI work in the 1970s through Army/DIA operational programs and the CIA-commissioned 1995 American Institutes for Research evaluation. Readers should distinguish the existence of a program from the efficacy of its subject matter, and laboratory effect size from intelligence utility.

Useful primary and serious sources include:

- CIA FOIA, 1973 SRI parapsychology final-report memorandum: https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp96-00787r000100070001-5
- Targ & Puthoff, 1974, *Nature*, “Information transmission under conditions of sensory shielding”: https://doi.org/10.1038/251602a0
- Marks & Kammann, 1978, *Nature*, critique/replication: https://doi.org/10.1038/274680a0
- Tart, Puthoff & Targ, 1980, *Nature*, response: https://doi.org/10.1038/284191a0
- CIA FOIA, 1980 Tehran remote-viewing session CCC12: https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp96-00788r000800760001-1
- CIA FOIA, 1982 training session 876 recording very little target correlation: https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp96-00788r001400250001-0
- CIA released STAR GATE operational tasking summary: https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/CIA-RDP96-00791R000200300002-2.pdf
- CIA FOIA, May et al. 1995 “Decision Augmentation Theory”: https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp96-00791r000200280002-5
- American Institutes for Research, 1995, *An Evaluation of Remote Viewing: Research and Applications*, declassified CIA copy mirrored with full page access: https://www.declassifiedcollection.com/archive/002/
- Tressoldi et al., 2023, remote-viewing systematic review and meta-analysis: https://doi.org/10.31275/20232931

The manuscript deliberately does not use patents as evidence of psychic efficacy, does not claim Soviet research proved a matching capability, and does not treat participant testimony as equivalent to contemporaneous target-blind records.
