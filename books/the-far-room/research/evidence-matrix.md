# Evidence matrix and source log

## Class 1 — verified / declassified / documented

- CIA documentation confirms SRI parapsychology work and government interest by 1973. Source: https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp96-00787r000100070001-5
- Targ and Puthoff published sensory-shielding experiments in *Nature* in 1974. Source: https://doi.org/10.1038/251602a0
- Marks and Kammann published a failed verification / methodological critique in *Nature* in 1978. Source: https://doi.org/10.1038/274680a0
- Declassified Army/DIA records document operational remote-viewing tasking, including Tehran hostage-related work. Source: https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp96-00788r000800760001-1
- Declassified training records explicitly preserve poor performance, including “very little correlation” and other no-correlation sessions. Source: https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp96-00788r001400250001-0
- A 1995 operational summary states the program received more than 200 tasks from military organizations from 1986 into FY1995 and later developed customer accuracy/value scoring. Source: https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/CIA-RDP96-00791R000200300002-2.pdf
- The CIA commissioned AIR to evaluate the program in 1995; Jessica Utts and Ray Hyman reached differing conclusions about the laboratory evidence, while the broader review did not find sufficient operational value to justify intelligence use. Archive: https://www.declassifiedcollection.com/archive/002/
- A 2023 published meta-analysis reported a positive average effect across included remote-viewing studies through 2022. This documents the authors' statistical conclusion, not the truth of anomalous cognition. Source: https://doi.org/10.31275/20232931

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete

- Participant and program-manager accounts of unusually successful operational cases. Treat case by case and require contemporaneous tasking, full raw response, target knowledge available to taskers, and independent later confirmation before upgrading.
- Accounts of Soviet/Russian psychotronics or anomalous-cognition programs that are supported by state activity or publications but lack a fully reconstructed independent performance record.
- Claims that certain named viewers were consistently exceptional. Requires prospective independent validation rather than retrospective selection of famous sessions.

## Class 3 — disputed / unconfirmed

- Claims that remote viewing reliably located hostages, submarines, secret facilities, or other targets with intelligence-grade accuracy when the full contemporaneous record is unavailable.
- Claims that U.S. or Soviet programs achieved a mature operational psychic capability hidden from the public record.
- Claims that classification status itself validates technical efficacy.
- Claims of specific physical mechanisms such as nonlocal quantum information transfer without a predictive experimentally supported model.

## Class 4 — speculative synthesis / open conjecture

- A small laboratory anomaly could be real while remaining operationally useless.
- Strategic fear of adversary breakthroughs may have sustained parallel programs even if the underlying capability was weak or nonexistent.
- Secrecy and compartmentalization may generate a false evidentiary ecology by making non-independent reports appear mutually corroborating.
- Post-response target generation plus cryptographic commitment could provide a particularly clean modern test by eliminating ordinary pre-target information leakage.

## Contradictory evidence retained

The manuscript preserves failed sessions, failed replications, operational-utility criticism, and Hyman's objections rather than treating them as hostile noise. It also preserves Utts's positive statistical assessment and the later meta-analytic literature rather than asserting that early cueing critiques automatically explain every subsequent dataset.
