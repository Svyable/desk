# Chronology and contradiction audit

## Working chronology

- 1972–1973: SRI anomalous-cognition/parapsychology work attracts CIA attention; 1973 CIA memorandum records final-report presentation involving work with Uri Geller and Ingo Swann.
- October 1974: Targ and Puthoff publish sensory-shielding experiments in *Nature*.
- August 1978: Marks and Kammann publish a critique and failed verification in *Nature*.
- Late 1970s–1980s: U.S. Army/DIA programs under names including GRILL FLAME and later CENTER LANE/SUN STREAK conduct training and operational remote-viewing tasking.
- 1980: declassified sessions are tasked against aspects of the Tehran hostage crisis.
- 1982: declassified training session 876 explicitly records very little correlation with target.
- 1986–1995: later operational summary says more than 200 tasks came from military organizations.
- 1994–1995: STAR GATE office formalizes customer scoring for a subset of operational tasks.
- September 1995: AIR completes CIA-commissioned evaluation; Utts and Hyman disagree on whether laboratory results establish anomalous cognition; operational evidence is judged insufficient for continuation as an intelligence capability.
- 2023: Tressoldi and colleagues publish a systematic review/meta-analysis reporting a positive average effect in included remote-viewing studies through 2022.

## Contradiction audit

**Program duration vs efficacy:** Long government sponsorship demonstrates sustained interest, not successful capability. Low-cost strategic exploration can persist under uncertainty.

**Published anomaly vs failed replication:** Early positive SRI claims coexist with published criticism and failed verification. Later protocols improved, so early flaws cannot simply be projected forward without checking later designs.

**Positive statistics vs operational weakness:** A small laboratory effect, if genuine, need not provide actionable open-world intelligence. These are separate hypotheses.

**Famous hits vs archived misses:** Retrospective stories are subject to selection. Full-session denominators, including explicit failures, are required.

**Foreign interest vs independent confirmation:** Soviet/Russian interest may reflect genuine independent observations, mistaken research, bureaucratic incentives, or strategic mirroring. Foreign funding is not automatically replication.

**Secrecy vs validation:** Classification can protect both successful and unsuccessful exploratory programs. Declassification does not convert protected material into confirmed science.

**Mechanism talk vs empirical burden:** Quantum or consciousness-based explanations remain Class Four unless they generate specific testable predictions beyond the behavioral effect.
