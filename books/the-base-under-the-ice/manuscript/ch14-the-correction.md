# The Correction

The least dramatic sentence in the Camp Century story may be the most important: the current models do not show the base melting out this century.

By 2021 GEUS was publicly reporting that the remains of Camp Century were expected to stay under the ice through 2100 under the available climate scenarios, and that meltwater was not projected to reach the debris over that period. The conclusion came from the monitoring program created after the 2016 concern, incorporating better site-specific observations and updated modeling.

A book interested only in warning would hide this in a footnote. It belongs in the center.

The correction changes the thesis. Camp Century is not useful because it proves that climate change will uncover a secret nuclear base on a cinematic schedule. It is useful because a credible scientific warning triggered measurement, and measurement narrowed the warning. The system improved its knowledge without needing to decide that one side had been foolish.

There are reasons for the difference. The 2016 study explored a high-emissions pathway and uncertainty between climate-model forcings. Later work had local data from weather stations, snow and firn observations, radar mapping and improved understanding of how meltwater would move at the site. The relevant quantity is not simply Greenland's average warming. It is whether the local surface mass balance becomes persistently negative and whether meltwater penetrates deeply enough to intersect debris that remains tens of meters below the surface.

The site is high and cold. Snow accumulation still matters. A warmer atmosphere can increase both melt and snowfall. The sign of the annual balance depends on which term wins locally. Greenland can be losing enormous amounts of ice overall while Camp Century remains in net accumulation for a considerable time.

This is exactly the kind of detail a global story tends to flatten.

It also demonstrates why “climate change” cannot be treated as a single knob turned equally everywhere. Regional physics, elevation, circulation and firn processes shape the pathway from global forcing to local consequence. A headline about the Greenland Ice Sheet losing mass does not, by itself, tell you when water reaches a particular buried sump.

The later result does not restore the 1967 assumption of eternity. The models stop at 2100. Climate and ice continue afterward. The point is not that the problem disappeared. It moved from urgent-remediation rhetoric into long-term stewardship.

That is a harder category to fund politically because nothing is happening yet.

Monitoring is easiest to defend after an alarm and hardest to sustain during decades of reassuring measurements. But those reassuring measurements are the product. If the climate station records conditions that keep the waste isolated, the program is succeeding, not failing to find a disaster.

Camp Century therefore offers a small lesson in epistemic hygiene. A model can be good enough to reveal a neglected risk and still be too uncertain to dictate the timetable. New data can reduce estimated risk without invalidating the earlier question. A correction can strengthen a research program by making its claim smaller.

The political temptation runs the other way. Governments prefer certainty when certainty lowers liability. Activists prefer certainty when certainty raises urgency. Media prefer certainty because dates fit headlines. Scientific monitoring lives in the less satisfying grammar of conditional futures.

At Camp Century, that grammar is the honest one.

The base is still under the ice.

For now, the ice is still winning.
