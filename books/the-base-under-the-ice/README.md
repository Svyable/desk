## Reader promise

In 1959 the U.S. Army cut a city into the Greenland Ice Sheet, installed a portable nuclear reactor, and used the camp to learn whether a vastly larger ballistic-missile network could live inside moving ice. The missiles were never deployed. The tunnels collapsed. The wastes stayed. The ice core survived. Decades later the same place became evidence for ancient Greenland ecosystems, a problem in climate diplomacy, and an object visible to modern radar. This book follows one installation across those incompatible afterlives.

## Distinct contribution

Most accounts treat Camp Century as either a Cold War curiosity, a climate-waste warning, or an origin story for deep ice-core science. The book treats those as one system. Project Iceworm failed partly because ice is not stable ground. That same instability later preserved and then complicated the camp's scientific legacy, its buried waste, and the politics of who inherits a secret project after the assumptions used to abandon it stop holding.

## Strongest competing explanation

Camp Century can be read more simply: an ambitious but ordinary military engineering experiment whose later climate significance has been exaggerated. Modern GEUS work is important counterevidence to dramatic melt-out narratives: available scenarios through 2100 do not show the site simply emerging from the ice, and current monitoring indicates the abandoned material remains deeply buried. The argument therefore is not that Camp Century is about to surface. It is that a supposedly permanent disposal assumption became a century-scale governance problem precisely because the physical system kept moving after the program ended.

## Evidence classes

1. **Verified/declassified/documented:** Camp Century construction and operation; PM-2A reactor installation; Army cold-regions engineering reports; the 1962 Iceworm proposal and its rejection; abandonment; later radar mapping; the climate-monitoring program; recovery and scientific analysis of the Camp Century ice core.
2. **Credible but incomplete:** the exact relationship between Camp Century's public scientific role and Iceworm planning; the full distribution and condition of wastes; the weight individual engineering failures carried in program cancellation; some Danish and Greenlandic officials' knowledge at different moments.
3. **Disputed/unconfirmed:** claims that the camp was only a cover, that Danish authorities uniformly knew nothing, that buried radioactive material creates an imminent catastrophe, or that climate change will expose the installation on a specific timetable.
4. **Speculative synthesis:** Camp Century is a useful model of how secret technological systems acquire physical afterlives. Classification can expire while tunnels, contaminants, treaty obligations, scientific samples, and political liabilities remain.
