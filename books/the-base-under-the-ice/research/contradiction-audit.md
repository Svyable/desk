# Contradiction and Incentive Audit

## Feasible camp vs. failed weapon system

The 1965 Army technical history concludes that subsurface ice-cap camps are feasible and practicable. The manuscript preserves that finding. It does not rewrite Camp Century as an engineering fiasco merely because Iceworm was rejected. The stronger distinction is scale and mission: a maintainable camp does not establish a maintainable strategic network of hundreds of missile positions.

**Incentive:** Army organizations had institutional reasons to demonstrate competence and preserve a role in Arctic and strategic systems. That does not invalidate measurements, but it matters when reading broad conclusions drawn from them.

## Real science vs. “cover story”

Camp Century's public scientific and engineering programs produced durable results, including the deep ice core. The secret Iceworm purpose therefore cannot be used to declare all visible science a sham.

**Incentive:** Public-facing science made a military installation more politically legible and useful, while scientists benefited from extraordinary logistics. Overlap is better supported than a simple fake/real binary.

## Danish nuclear policy vs. U.S. Arctic practice

Denmark publicly maintained a peacetime no-nuclear-weapons policy, while Cold War arrangements around Thule involved ambiguity later exposed by archival work and the 1968 crash. The manuscript does not infer from this that Denmark knowingly approved Iceworm deployment.

**Incentive:** Copenhagen and Washington both benefited at times from ambiguity that preserved alliance cooperation without forcing every strategic fact into public policy language.

## Nuclear waste vs. abandoned reactor

Radiological waste associated with PM-2A operations remained at Camp Century. The reactor itself was removed. Popular retellings often merge these claims.

**Incentive:** “Nuclear reactor buried under Greenland” is more dramatic and memorable than a mixed waste inventory dominated by ordinary physical, chemical and biological material.

## 2016 remobilization warning vs. 2021 through-2100 correction

Colgan et al. (2016) showed that a high-emissions pathway could eventually shift the site toward net ablation and waste remobilization. Later GEUS monitoring/modeling concluded the debris should remain buried and meltwater should not reach it through 2100 under available scenarios. Both findings are retained.

**Incentive:** Climate-warning narratives reward early dates and irreversible language; governmental reassurance rewards low-risk readings. Site-specific monitoring is weighted above either rhetorical incentive.

## “Rediscovery” vs. improved observability

NASA's 2024 UAVSAR image provided a striking new structural view, but prior radar flights had already detected Camp Century and GEUS had mapped the debris field.

**Incentive:** Rediscovery is a stronger media frame than instrument-geometry improvement.

## Ancient vegetation vs. present-day forecast

The basal sediment demonstrates substantial past ice retreat at the Camp Century location. It does not specify a deterministic modern collapse date for the whole Greenland Ice Sheet.

**Incentive:** Paleoclimate evidence is often recruited into present-day political argument with more precision than its temporal and spatial resolution warrants.

## Book-level synthesis under test

The book proposes that classified technical systems can be administratively closed while remaining physically active as liabilities, archives, or scientific objects. This is an interpretation, not a declassified finding. It should narrow if comparison with other abandoned secret facilities shows Camp Century to be unusually dependent on glaciology rather than representative of a broader pattern.
