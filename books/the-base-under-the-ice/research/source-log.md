# Source Log

Research checked for this run, September 14, 2026.

## Primary and official engineering records

- U.S. Army CRREL, Elmer F. Clark, *Camp Century: Evolution of Concept and History of Design, Construction, and Performance* (1965). Establishes construction method, operating history, engineering conclusions, and the Army's own feasibility framing. https://usace.contentdm.oclc.org/digital/collection/p266001coll1/id/4529/
- U.S. Army, *Construction of the Army Nuclear Power Plant PM-2A at Camp Century, Greenland: Final Report* (1961). Establishes the installation program and reactor logistics. https://usace.contentdm.oclc.org/digital/collection/p266001coll1/id/3968
- McCool et al., *Startup Testing of the PM-2A Nuclear Power Plant* (1962). Establishes startup tests, shielding issues, thermal/hydraulic and radiochemistry work. https://digital.library.unt.edu/ark:/67531/metadc1201987/
- Austin Kovacs, *Camp Century Revisited: A Pictorial View — June 1969* (1970). Establishes post-abandonment physical condition from the last reported CRREL visit. https://usace.contentdm.oclc.org/digital/collection/p266001coll1/id/3499/

## Iceworm and political context

- Erik D. Weiss, “Cold War Under the Ice: The Army's Bid for a Long-Range Nuclear Role, 1959–1963,” *Journal of Cold War Studies* 3 (2001): 31–58. Secondary archival reconstruction of Army strategic motives and Iceworm.
- Nikolaj Petersen, “The Politics of US Military Research in Greenland in the Early Cold War,” *Centaurus* 55 (2013): 294–318. https://doi.org/10.1111/1600-0498.12023
- National Security Archive documents on U.S.–Greenland nuclear history and the 1968 Thule crash. Useful for nuclear-policy ambiguity and cleanup context; the Thule crash is not used as evidence that Iceworm deployed. https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/

## Waste and climate pathway

- Colgan et al., “The Abandoned Ice Sheet Base at Camp Century, Greenland, in a Warming Climate,” *Geophysical Research Letters* 43 (2016): 8091–8096. Establishes the widely cited historical waste inventory and high-emissions remobilization analysis. https://doi.org/10.1002/2016GL069688
- Colgan et al., “Initial Field Activities of the Camp Century Climate Monitoring Programme in Greenland” (2018). Establishes the Danish–Greenlandic monitoring program's goals and early field work. https://doi.org/10.34194/geusb.v41.4347
- Karlsson, Colgan & Andersen, *Camp Century radarundersøgelser* (GEUS 2019/18). Establishes 2017–2018 radar mapping. https://doi.org/10.22008/gpub/32618
- GEUS, “Camp Century is staying under the ice” (23 March 2021). Establishes the later through-2100 model conclusion that debris will remain buried and meltwater is not expected to reach it under available scenarios. https://eng.geus.dk/about/news/news-archive/2021/march/camp-century

## Ice core and basal sediment

- Christ et al., “A multimillion-year-old record of Greenland vegetation and glacial history preserved in sediment beneath 1.4 km of ice at Camp Century,” *PNAS* (2021). Establishes fossils and geochronological evidence for an ice-free/vegetated northwestern Greenland during the Quaternary. https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2021442118

## Modern remote sensing

- NASA Earth Observatory, “New View of the ‘City Under the Ice’” (25 November 2024). Establishes the April 2024 UAVSAR observations and clearly notes that prior radar surveys had already detected Camp Century. https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/new-view-of-the-city-under-the-ice-153616/

## Source discipline

The manuscript treats patents, plans and proposals as evidence of intent or study, not efficacy. It treats declassified government records as evidence of what agencies documented, not automatic proof of every claim in those records. The waste inventory is reconstructed rather than excavated. The climate-risk trajectory is deliberately presented as a sequence from 2016 scenario analysis to later site-specific correction rather than choosing whichever date produces more drama.
