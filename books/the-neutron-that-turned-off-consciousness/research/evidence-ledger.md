# Evidence ledger

## Class 1 — verified/documented

- Xenon is a clinically used general anesthetic with experimentally characterized concentration-response behavior.
- Stable xenon isotopes differ in nuclear spin: 129Xe and 131Xe carry nonzero nuclear spin; 132Xe and 134Xe are spin zero.
- Li N, Lu D, Yang L, et al. reported in *Anesthesiology* 129 (2018), 271–277 that nuclear-spin-bearing xenon isotopes were less potent in a mouse loss-of-righting-reflex assay than spin-zero isotopes. DOI/source to recover and page-index before quotation.
- Nuclear-spin-dependent radical-pair chemistry is an established physical mechanism class. Biological magnetoreception is an important comparator; it is not evidence for xenon anesthesia.
- Xenon has documented molecular effects including NMDA-receptor antagonism; the complete causal route to anesthesia remains mechanistically plural.

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete

- The 2018 isotope effect is sufficiently large to merit independent replication and mechanistic work.
- A spin-dependent mechanism is physically conceivable if xenon perturbs a radical-pair or hyperfine-sensitive intermediate, but the relevant in-vivo intermediate has not been established here.
- Some anesthetic/microtubule studies report associations worth testing, but they do not establish that the xenon isotope result is microtubule-mediated.

## Class 3 — disputed/unconfirmed

- Nuclear spin itself is the causal variable behind the reported potency ordering rather than an isotope-correlated classical variable.
- The isotope effect replicates independently.
- Xenon anesthesia depends materially on radical-pair chemistry.
- Xenon isotope behavior demonstrates quantum computation in neurons, Orch-OR, or a quantum basis of consciousness.
- Loss of righting reflex is a direct measurement of consciousness.

## Class 4 — speculative synthesis/open conjecture

- Systems near an arousal/consciousness transition might amplify a small molecular spin-dependent effect into a large behavioral difference.
- Anesthesia may provide a cleaner experimental route into biologically relevant quantum effects than introspective consciousness experiments because the perturbation is controllable and dose-response curves can be preregistered.

# Contradiction and incentive audit

The result is unusually narratable: inert atom + nuclear spin + consciousness. That creates selection pressure toward overinterpretation. Quantum-consciousness advocates benefit rhetorically from treating the isotope effect as mechanism-neutral support. Conventional anesthesia researchers may have the opposite incentive to classify an unreplicated isotope result as peripheral. Neither posture substitutes for replication.

A second trap is the phrase `same chemistry`. Isotopes are not physically identical. Mass, transport and other isotope-correlated quantities must be modeled before spin receives causal credit.

# Strongest conventional explanation

The original result is a false positive, laboratory-specific artifact, endpoint artifact, isotope-delivery difference, or consequence of a classical isotope-correlated physical property. This explanation wins by default unless a preregistered independent replication survives controls designed to separate spin from those variables.
