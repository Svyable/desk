# Back Matter

The Moscow Signal leaves an unusual documentary residue: enough declassified material to establish the physical exposure, the American research response, the diplomatic dispute, and the disclosure failure; not enough public material to settle the Soviet technical purpose.

That asymmetry governs the book.

The primary documentary spine is the declassified PANDORA/BIZARRE collection assembled by the National Security Archive, including Jerome Wiesner’s 1964 embassy-security memorandum, Richard Cesaro’s 1967 BIZARRE progress report, 1975–76 State Department cables, White House and Kissinger material, and records released for congressional review. These are used as evidence of what U.S. officials measured, considered, funded, and said—not as automatic proof that every hypothesis appearing in a classified document was true.

The medical argument relies on the original Foreign Service Health Status work and later peer-reviewed reassessments, with special attention to exposure classification, multiple outcomes, latency, and the difference between failure to demonstrate a coherent population effect and proof of zero individual risk. High-power or otherwise mismatched microwave experiments are never treated as direct evidence about the chronic embassy field merely because they share a frequency family.

Modern anomalous health incidents are included only as a test of historical analogy. The January 10, 2025 Intelligence Community assessment still judged foreign-adversary responsibility very unlikely overall while preserving minority judgments that left greater uncertainty for a small subset. That is not a contradiction to be resolved rhetorically. It is a distribution of analytic judgments. The 2024 GAO work on care and registry implementation is used to separate care obligations from unresolved attribution.

The book’s research files contain the live source log, four-class evidence matrix, chronology, contradiction and incentive audit, and explicit defeat conditions. The intention is that a future archival release can change the manuscript without requiring the author to defend the current hierarchy of explanations.

## Working conclusion

The strongest public-evidence account is still that the Moscow transmissions belonged primarily to the world of technical intelligence, electronic surveillance, or countermeasures. A deliberate biological purpose remains a disputed hypothesis rather than an established historical fact. The documented biological research matters because it shows that U.S. agencies considered the possibility serious enough to test, and because secrecy around that testing changed consent, risk communication, and the later quality of evidence.

The most durable lesson is methodological. A true fact can be made to support a false conclusion by putting it beside another true fact and leaving the connector unstated. Secret programs, unusual safety standards, illness cases, animal experiments, and later directed-energy hypotheses must therefore remain attached to their own evidentiary class.

The unresolved part is not empty. It is simply unresolved.
