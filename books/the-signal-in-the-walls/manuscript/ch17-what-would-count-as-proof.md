# What Would Count as Proof

A field this contaminated by secrecy needs a stopping rule.

What would change your mind?

For a directed RF explanation of a specific AHI, the strongest evidence would be contemporaneous detection of an anomalous waveform at the affected location, with calibrated instrument data preserved in raw form. Better still if multiple independent sensors captured it. Better again if the timing aligned with acute symptoms and the field geometry localized a source.

Then the signal should survive engineering analysis. Could the measured exposure produce the proposed sensory or biological effect at the target? Laboratory work using the recorded waveform or a faithful reconstruction should reproduce at least a relevant acute endpoint under blinded conditions. If the hypothesized mechanism is thermoacoustic, the predicted pressure transient should match. If a different biological mechanism is proposed, it needs its own dose-response and replication.

Attribution would require another layer. Collection tying the emitter to an actor. Hardware recovered or imaged. Procurement or training records. Signals intelligence. Operator communications. Repeated incidents with the same technical fingerprint. A human source with direct access whose reporting is independently corroborated.

No single item is mandatory in every real-world case. Intelligence rarely offers courtroom completeness. But the burden should rise with the claim.

The same framework specifies what would weaken the thesis.

If robust reanalysis showed that the Moscow embassy fields were entirely mismeasured or not externally generated, the historical control case would collapse. The public record strongly argues otherwise.

If modern RF detectors were deployed broadly in relevant posts and repeatedly captured no candidate emissions during well-characterized acute events, RF explanations would lose probability, especially for models predicting easily detectable fields.

If blinded prospective medical studies found no reproducible acute phenotype separating reported incidents from matched controls, the idea of one distinctive external insult would weaken.

Conversely, if a future case produced synchronized RF detection, acute clinical change, and independent source attribution, the debate would change in an afternoon.

That last sentence is worth dwelling on.

The directed-energy question is not inherently unfalsifiable. It has been treated that way because the decisive measurements were absent when the most important incidents occurred.

Moscow shows what happens when the source is measured.

The next investigation should not settle for less if technology gives us a choice.
