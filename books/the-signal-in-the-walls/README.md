# The Signal in the Walls

## Reader promise

A documented Soviet microwave beam hit the U.S. Embassy in Moscow for years. The United States secretly studied whether such exposure could alter behavior or health. Decades later, American personnel in Cuba and elsewhere reported strange directional sensations and neurological symptoms. This book asks the narrower and more useful question: what does the Moscow Signal actually teach us about how to investigate directed-energy claims without turning plausibility into proof?

## Distinct contribution

The book treats the Moscow Signal as a historical control case. It separates a verified exposure from uncertain intent, separates a real bioeffect literature from operational capability, and separates mechanism plausibility from event attribution. That lets the modern Anomalous Health Incident debate be tested against something unusually rare in this domain: an episode where the source field was actually measured.

## Strongest competing explanation

The most important competing explanation is that the Moscow Signal was primarily a counterintelligence or technical-surveillance system—possibly related to activating, interrogating, or supporting clandestine listening devices—rather than a human-effects weapon. For modern AHIs, the strongest official assessment is that most reported cases were probably caused by factors not involving a foreign adversary and that a global hostile campaign is very unlikely.

## Evidence classes

1. **Verified/declassified/documented:** measured microwave illumination of the Moscow embassy; U.S. diplomatic protests; PANDORA/BIZARRE studies; the established microwave auditory effect; official AHI medical and intelligence reviews.
2. **Credible but incomplete:** historical interpretations of Soviet technical purpose; reported exposure parameters where records conflict or remain redacted; expert judgments that pulsed RF could plausibly explain a subset of AHI features.
3. **Disputed/unconfirmed:** Moscow Signal as a deliberate health or mind-control weapon; claims that a specific adversary caused particular modern AHI cases; claims of deployed long-range neurological weapons without instrumented exposure evidence.
4. **Speculative synthesis:** the most durable lesson of Moscow may be epistemic: a real secret signal can coexist with a wrong causal story about what it is for, and classification can preserve both the real event and the false explanation for decades.
