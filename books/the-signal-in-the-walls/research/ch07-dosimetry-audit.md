# Chapter 7 Research Audit — The Wrong Number

## Scope

This audit tightens the quantitative and evidentiary spine for Chapter 7 without treating the corrected 1967 Moscow measurements as proof that every biological question was resolved.

## Primary documents checked

### Samuel Koslov, ARPA to State, May 13, 1965

National Security Archive document page:
https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/document/28786-document-2-arpa-samuel-koslov-memorandum-state-department-biomedical-phenomena

Koslov argued for primate testing while stating that the embassy irradiation exceeded the Soviet ambient safety level by a factor of 100. The National Security Archive's document description identifies this as an erroneous assessment produced before the later power-density measurements.

Safe use: establish that an early safety comparison was materially wrong and that the number helped frame the biological-research problem.

Do not use: as evidence of Soviet intent, as a reliable reconstruction of the actual Moscow field, or as proof that the later corrected measurement invalidated every PANDORA research question.

### Joseph Johnston, CIA Project BIZARRE summary, September 25, 1967

National Security Archive document page:
https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/document/28791-document-7-cia-memorandum-joseph-johnston-aid-memoir-sic-summary-statement-my

Johnston wrote that the TUMS power analysis had been revised substantially downward. He described reasonable certainty that the power level was not above 50 microwatts/cm² and put the average high level closer to 2 microwatts/cm². He also recorded a pronounced behavioral/performance effect in an earlier higher-level primate experiment, stressed that the single-animal observation required independent confirmation, and stated that subsequent experiments at the adjusted lower radiation level produced no behavioral effects.

Safe use: establish both the scale of the corrected Moscow field and the fact that the strongest early behavioral result was not reproduced at the adjusted lower exposure.

Important boundary: Johnston's confidence that personnel were at no risk of injury was an expert judgment in 1967, not a timeless demonstration that every possible endpoint, duration, or later signal configuration was harmless.

### Richard S. Cesaro, ARPA Project BIZARRE progress report, September 27, 1967

National Security Archive document page:
https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/document/28792-document-8-arpa-richard-s-cesaro-memorandum-project-bizarre-top-secret

Document OCR:
https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/media/28792/ocr

Cesaro reported:

- the latest Moscow measurements were always below 50 microwatts/cm² (0.05 mW/cm²);
- earlier primate performance disruption had been observed during modulated exposure around 4–5 mW/cm²;
- non-modulated exposure at similar 4–5 mW/cm² levels did not produce the same overt performance disruption;
- a recent experiment spanning lower power levels associated with the corrected Moscow field showed no overt primate performance degradation below 1.0 mW/cm²;
- the low-level work at that stage measured overt task performance and did not yet settle other physiological or biological endpoints.

Cesaro also noted that the new Moscow recordings covered a 50-MHz bandwidth rather than the earlier 3-MHz recordings and included a partial power-density map, underscoring that the intelligence input itself had materially improved.

## Quantitative boundary

The higher-level behavioral condition of roughly 4–5 mW/cm² was about 80–100 times the later stated Moscow upper bound of 0.05 mW/cm².

Compared with Johnston's approximately 2-microwatt/cm² estimate for the average high level, the gap was much larger still.

Those comparisons are useful because they make the relevance problem concrete. They should not be converted into a universal dose-response law: the documents do not establish that power density alone captures modulation, duration, geometry, absorbed dose, or every possible endpoint.

## Strongest counterargument

The corrected 1967 dosimetry is a major negative update for using the dramatic higher-level primate performance result as evidence about embassy exposure. It is not equivalent to a comprehensive null biological result.

Cesaro explicitly limited the low-level result to overt primate performance and listed other proposed measurements still to come. The positive higher-level result itself was preliminary and required replication. A fair chapter therefore has to keep two propositions alive at once:

1. the most dramatic behavioral finding was produced at an exposure far above the later measured Moscow field, sharply weakening its operational relevance;
2. the 1967 low-level experiment did not measure every biological endpoint and therefore could not logically close every health question.

## Historical consequence

The measurement correction had already arrived after the earlier, more alarming number had begun doing institutional work. Koslov's 1965 memorandum used the supposed Soviet-safety-standard exceedance in arguing for primate research. In June 1967, Secretary of State Dean Rusk told Soviet Foreign Minister Andrei Gromyko that the radiation level was above what Soviet experts considered safe. The diplomatic record should therefore be checked against the timing of the newer measurements before the manuscript implies that every policymaker had the corrected number at the same moment.

## Recommended Chapter 7 revision

Replace generic language about an upstream number being wrong with the actual sequence:

- 1965: ARPA presents an erroneous Soviet-safety comparison while advocating biological testing;
- 1967: better on-site instrumentation sharply lowers the estimated Moscow field;
- higher-level modulated primate exposure had produced a concerning performance result;
- lower-level exposure closer to the measured embassy field produced no overt performance degradation;
- the investigators themselves still treated other endpoints and replication as unresolved.

This gives the chapter a stronger narrative hinge than abstract discussion of institutional momentum: the interesting object is a number that moved from intelligence estimate to laboratory condition to diplomacy, then changed underneath all three.

## Wording to avoid

Do not say that PANDORA "proved the Moscow signal affected behavior." The most concerning behavioral result was obtained at substantially higher power density than the corrected Moscow field.

Do not say that corrected measurements "proved the Moscow signal harmless." The low-level experiment reported no overt task-performance degradation and did not settle all possible biological endpoints or later signal configurations.

Do not call the 4–5 mW/cm² result a replicated law of modulation. Johnston treated the key behavioral observation as requiring independent confirmation.

Do not use the early Soviet-safety-standard comparison as a reliable dose fact. The later measurement record materially contradicted it.

## Editorial gain

The chapter can become both more engaging and more rigorous by letting three numbers carry the argument: the early factor-of-100 safety claim, the later 50-microwatt/cm² upper bound (with Johnston's roughly 2-microwatt/cm² average-high estimate), and the 4–5-mW/cm² higher-level primate condition. The counterevidence belongs beside those numbers, not in a generic caveat paragraph.
