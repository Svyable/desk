# Chronology

- **1953 (reported in later U.S. records):** U.S. technicians detect unusual microwave energy associated with the Moscow embassy environment.
- **1963–1964:** monitoring and concern sharpen; Maclyn Musser identifies the phenomenon as microwave radiation; Jerome Wiesner urges stronger investigation.
- **1964:** clandestine listening devices are found in multiple embassy offices, making technical-interrogation explanations operationally concrete.
- **Mid-1960s:** U.S. defense/intelligence agencies launch PANDORA/BIZARRE-related biological and behavioral investigations.
- **1967:** Rusk raises the microwave issue with Gromyko at Glassboro. Revised exposure estimates weaken early primate-performance concerns at Moscow-comparable levels.
- **1975:** U.S. monitors detect additional/stronger signal activity; Ambassador Walter Stoessel pushes Washington to act and brief staff.
- **1976:** embassy personnel are briefed; the story quickly becomes public and politically explosive.
- **1977:** State Department medical review is publicly discussed; no clear harmful health effect is established in the exposed cohort.
- **2016:** U.S. personnel in Cuba begin reporting unusual acute sensory experiences and neurological/vestibular symptoms later grouped under AHI/Havana Syndrome.
- **2020:** National Academies report judges directed pulsed RF a plausible mechanism for important features of reviewed cases.
- **2022:** IC Experts Panel says pulsed electromagnetic energy plausibly explains core characteristics of a subset, while not addressing attribution.
- **2023-03-01:** NIC updated assessment says most IC agencies judge a foreign adversary very unlikely to be responsible for reported AHIs in aggregate.
