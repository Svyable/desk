# Contradictions and Incentives Audit — The Signal in the Walls

The book should not resolve contradictions by choosing the document that best fits the thesis. Each contradiction below is either a real update, a difference in institutional function, or an unresolved conflict that needs more evidence.

## The signal was described as low risk, yet senior officials demanded it stop

These propositions can coexist. A field may be below an accepted U.S. thermal safety limit and still be an unacceptable adversarial technical operation. Officials also faced uncertainty about chronic exposure, modulation, changing signal characteristics, and stricter Soviet standards. Diplomatic protest does not prove a health effect.

## PANDORA existed, yet the corrected primate experiment was negative

This is not evidence of a cover-up by itself. PANDORA began under broader uncertainty and an earlier, higher Moscow power estimate. Improved measurements changed the experimentally relevant condition. The corrected-level null should update the behavioral hypothesis, not erase the reason the program began.

## Soviet standards were stricter, yet Soviet operators continued transmitting

Possible explanations include different regulatory philosophy, operational exceptions, different assumptions about actual indoor dose, disregard of the standard for intelligence purposes, or awareness of biological effects. The standard alone does not discriminate among these.

## U.S. officials privately discussed a potential health hazard while publicly saying no causal relationship was established

“Potential hazard” and “causal relationship established” are different evidentiary thresholds. Both statements can be logically true. The governance problem is that employees were not given the full uncertainty history needed to understand that distinction.

## Employee symptoms and serious illnesses occurred, yet cohort studies were broadly reassuring

Individual illness is compatible with a null or weak population association. A cohort result cannot prove a particular individual’s illness was unrelated; an individual case cannot establish a population-wide causal effect. Exposure misclassification and multiple comparisons complicate both directions.

## Technical espionage is the strongest public hypothesis, yet decisive intelligence records remain unavailable

The hypothesis earns priority from demonstrated Soviet RF-assisted espionage, known embassy penetrations, and serious official analysis. It remains incomplete because the public archive lacks a recovered signal-specific architecture or Soviet mission order tying the principal transmissions to a specific device or function.

## Modern AHI assessments are skeptical overall, yet minority IC components preserve more uncertainty

This is not a contradiction. Coordinated intelligence assessments can contain different component judgments. The January 2025 assessment’s overall judgment remains very unlikely foreign responsibility while a minority leaves roughly even odds on narrower subset propositions. Do not collapse aggregate judgment and tail uncertainty.

# Incentive map

## Soviet intelligence / technical operators

Possible incentives: collect information, defeat U.S. collection, preserve deniability, learn from U.S. countermeasures, avoid revealing transmitter purpose, continue a productive technical operation despite diplomatic protest.

Known disinformation incentive: deny or normalize targeted technical activity; offer benign explanations; exploit U.S. uncertainty about function.

## U.S. technical security / intelligence agencies

Incentives: understand signal purpose, protect sources and methods, find hidden devices, avoid revealing detection capability, investigate worst-case adversary capability before proof is complete.

Failure mode: compartmentalization can turn uncertain intelligence parameters into overprecise laboratory inputs and prevent medical personnel from receiving the whole technical picture.

## ARPA/DARPA and defense researchers

Incentives: investigate potentially consequential frontier effects, avoid technological surprise, retain program relevance after early anomalies, explore broader parameter space.

Failure mode: exploratory positive findings can become institutionally memorable even when corrected measurements or replications weaken them.

## State Department / White House

Incentives: protect personnel, preserve diplomatic flexibility, avoid panic, avoid compromising intelligence methods, prevent an uncontrolled political scandal, keep broader Soviet negotiations on track.

Failure mode: delaying disclosure can improve short-term flexibility while destroying long-term credibility and consent.

## Embassy leadership

Incentives: protect people physically present at the post, obtain better measurements, force Washington to make a decision, preserve mission effectiveness.

Structural difference: the chief of mission bears immediate human consequences that distant policymakers can treat abstractly.

## Medical investigators

Incentives: identify an effect, protect patients, obtain unbiased data, avoid suggestion effects, preserve follow-up cohorts.

Failure mode: blinding and cover explanations may improve some kinds of measurement while violating informed consent and poisoning later trust.

## Exposed employees and families

Incentives: understand personal risk, receive care, reconstruct past exposure, obtain institutional accountability, explain illness.

Failure mode: common illnesses can be retrospectively recruited into a causal story once a hidden exposure becomes known; this does not make the illnesses unreal.

## Journalists, authors, and advocates

Incentives: expose secrecy, find a coherent narrative, identify victims, connect historical and modern cases.

Failure mode: adjacency laundering—placing true facts together so an unsupported connector is felt rather than stated.

## Skeptics / institutional defenders

Incentives: resist pseudoscientific escalation, protect evidence standards, avoid false attribution.

Failure mode: treating failure to prove a mechanism as proof that no meaningful exposure or governance failure occurred.

# Disinformation and strategic ambiguity checklist

- Did the Soviet side have reason to conceal a real technical collection purpose? Yes.
- Does that imply a biological purpose? No.
- Did U.S. agencies have reason to conceal technical measurement capability? Yes.
- Does that imply they possessed proof of harm? No.
- Can genuine classification make unsupported later claims feel more plausible? Yes.
- Can foreign services exploit the existence of secrecy to seed false narratives around real programs? Yes in principle; any specific claim requires evidence.
- Can U.S. internal secrecy itself generate contradictory public stories without an organized disinformation campaign? Yes.

The manuscript should keep all three possibilities available where justified: adversary deception, domestic institutional self-protection, and ordinary uncertainty.
