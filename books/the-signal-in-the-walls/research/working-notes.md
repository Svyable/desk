# Working Notes — The Signal in the Walls

## Reader promise

Reconstruct the Moscow microwave episode closely enough that the reader can distinguish the documented physical signal from the stories later built around it. The book should leave the reader with a better model of how classified uncertainty propagates through engineering, biology, medicine, diplomacy, and public trust.

## Distinct contribution

This is not a general directed-energy-weapons book and not a Havana Syndrome book. It uses the Moscow case as a bounded laboratory for epistemology under secrecy. The contribution is the collision of four histories usually told separately:

- Soviet technical espionage and embassy penetration;
- U.S. microwave-bioeffects research under PANDORA/BIZARRE;
- occupational-health and epidemiological consequences of delayed disclosure;
- the modern tendency to use historical resemblance as causal evidence.

The central authorial risk is to let the mystery create drama without letting mystery substitute for evidence.

## Strongest competing explanation

The strongest competing explanation is deliberately mundane: the microwave transmissions were primarily part of technical intelligence, surveillance, or electronic countermeasures; American agencies investigated biological possibilities because technological surprise was strategically costly; noisy early research, changing measurements, and secrecy later made the episode look more exotic than it was.

The book should remain publishable if that explanation becomes the final answer.

## Chapter progression

1. Establish the real beam and the real bug-saturated embassy.
2. Establish the Great Seal as strong evidence of RF-assisted technical espionage while refusing to make it proof of later signal purpose.
3. Turn the beam into a measurement problem.
4. Explain what microwave biology can and cannot mean.
5–7. Open PANDORA, follow the primate work, and center the corrected Moscow power estimate as a major negative update.
8–9. Move from animals to human observational ethics and occupational comparison populations.
10–12. Show the technical problem becoming a diplomatic and disclosure problem.
13–14. Let epidemiology and congressional review narrow the strongest claims without manufacturing closure.
15–16. Rank purposes and unpack the U.S./Soviet standards discrepancy.
17. Use modern AHIs only as a stress test for historical analogy and evidence discipline.
18. Synthesize uncertainty as an operational cost without claiming the Soviets intentionally designed epistemic warfare.

## Voice notes

The best passages begin from an object, number, memo, or institutional decision, then let the principle emerge. Avoid chapter-ending slogans. Keep the prose willing to sit with unglamorous details—power density, office location, study design—because that is where the grand claims live or die.

Do not use “mind control” except when naming or bounding a specific allegation. Do not give “Havana Syndrome” causal status; use anomalous health incidents for current official context.
