# Research Appendix — Measurement Before Myth

## Minimum signal record for every historical claim

For each TUMS/MUTS/related measurement recovered, capture:

- date and local time;
- source document and classification status at creation;
- instrument and calibration information;
- frequency / bandwidth;
- modulation or pulse description;
- power density: peak, average, or unspecified;
- measurement location inside/outside embassy;
- antenna orientation / polarization if known;
- daily duration / duty cycle;
- spatial footprint and uncertainty;
- whether shielding was present;
- whether U.S. equipment performance changed simultaneously;
- any Soviet explanation or response;
- any biomedical conclusion that explicitly relied on that measurement.

Never combine numbers from different dates into a synthetic “Moscow dose.”

## Minimum biological-study record

For every PANDORA/BIZARRE or related study, record:

- species / human population;
- sample size and denominator;
- exposure and sham/control design;
- blinding status;
- carrier frequency;
- waveform / modulation;
- incident power density and absorbed-dose metric if available;
- exposure duration and repetition;
- temperature monitoring and thermal controls;
- predefined versus exploratory endpoints;
- result including nulls;
- replication attempts;
- relevance to actual Moscow parameters at that date;
- whether later technical measurement changed that relevance.

## Epidemiology reconstruction

Build an individual-level conceptual model even if privacy prevents obtaining individual records:

`person × date × building location × signal regime × duration × shielding → estimated exposure distribution`

Then separately map:

`person × date → symptom / diagnosis / mortality endpoint`

The existing posting-level exposed/unexposed classification should be treated as a proxy whose likely misclassification direction is explicitly discussed.

## Purpose-ranking test

For each proposed Soviet purpose—passive-device interrogation, electronic jamming/countermeasures, remote sensing, biological impairment, harassment, accidental industrial emission—ask:

1. What signal characteristics would this purpose predict?
2. What timing pattern would it predict?
3. What target location would it predict?
4. What recovered hardware or intelligence would distinguish it?
5. What observation would make it less likely?
6. Does the hypothesis require one continuous system across decades or tolerate multiple systems?

Rank only after filling this table. Do not use “secret” as a likelihood multiplier.

## Archive targets

- National Archives and Records Administration collections connected to State Department technical security, PFIAB, ARPA/DARPA, CIA, NSA, and the 1979 Senate inquiry.
- Gerald Ford Presidential Library records around 1975–76 microwave diplomacy.
- Kissinger telephone-conversation and memorandum-of-conversation files involving Anatoly Dobrynin.
- State Department Foreign Relations and embassy security files.
- DARPA/ARPA historical PANDORA board minutes and contractor reports.
- Johns Hopkins / State records for the Foreign Service Health Status Study.
- George Washington University Human Cytogenetics Research Laboratory records where lawfully accessible.
- Navy / medical research records for Project Big Boy and USS Saratoga exposure comparison work.
- Soviet/Russian published technical histories, archives, memoirs, and declassified intelligence-service material where provenance can be established.

## Modern comparator protocol

Modern AHI material must remain an appendix-level comparator unless new evidence directly connects mechanisms. Track separately:

- patient care;
- medical findings;
- proposed mechanism;
- device feasibility;
- adversary capability;
- event-specific collection;
- attribution judgment.

No column may be used as a substitute for another.

## Claim labels in notes

Use `[D]` documented/verified, `[C]` credible-incomplete, `[A]` allegation/disputed, `[S]` speculative synthesis next to research notes until the prose itself makes the evidence posture unmistakable.
