## A note about secrets

This book contains a secret operation that actually happened. That fact is dangerous.

It is dangerous because once you learn that the CIA built an enormous ship behind a Howard Hughes deep-sea-mining story in order to raise a Soviet submarine from the Pacific floor, a small intoxicating thought arrives: *then anything could be hidden.*

No.

AZORIAN is evidence for AZORIAN. It is evidence that institutions can construct expensive covers, compartmentalize contractors, solve absurd engineering problems and preserve a core operational secret for years. It is also evidence that such operations generate bills, machines, contractors, failures, leaks, journalists, artifacts, secondary uses and eventually archives.

The book therefore keeps four evidence classes separate.

**Class 1 — verified, declassified or documented.** Officially released records, institutional histories, surviving artifacts, contemporaneous public records and primary technical material whose provenance can be checked.

**Class 2 — credible but incomplete reporting or testimony.** Accounts by participants, journalists or officials with relevant access where important details remain uncorroborated or the underlying records remain unavailable.

**Class 3 — disputed allegations.** Claims for which attribution, provenance, chain of custody or independent confirmation is inadequate.

**Class 4 — speculative synthesis.** My attempt to connect documented mechanisms into a broader model. These are propositions to test, not facts smuggled in through typography.

One further rule matters here: **an authentic artifact can belong to a false story.** The CIA Museum holds a manganese nodule recovered during the Glomar era. The nodule is real. Manganese nodules are real. Deep-ocean mining was technically plausible. None of those facts made the public explanation of the ship's mission true.

That is the book.
