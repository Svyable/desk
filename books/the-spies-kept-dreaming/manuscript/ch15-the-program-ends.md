# The Program Ends

Programs die for administrative reasons more often than philosophical ones.

By the mid-1990s, the remote-viewing effort had been transferred back to CIA for review. The agency says it agreed to reconsider the work on the condition of an independent evaluation. The resulting assessment did not produce an operational case strong enough to continue.

That ending is less satisfying than revelation. No final experiment proved psychic perception impossible. No declassified memo says the viewers were secretly indispensable. The program encountered a more ordinary death: it could not demonstrate dependable value.

The distinction matters because later retellings often convert termination into evidence. Skeptics treat closure as proof the phenomenon was debunked. Advocates treat decades of prior funding as proof officials knew it worked. Both read bureaucracy as nature.

The stronger conclusion is narrower. Government customers had years to experiment with an unconventional collection method. By the end, the public evaluation did not justify retaining it as an intelligence capability.

That is a significant finding. It is not the same as a metaphysical verdict.
