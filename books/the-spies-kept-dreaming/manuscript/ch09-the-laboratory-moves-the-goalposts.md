# The Laboratory Moves the Goalposts

Parapsychology's critics often say controls tightened because earlier experiments were vulnerable. They are right. Advocates answer that effects sometimes persisted under tighter controls. They are also pointing at a real literature.

The history of ganzfeld and related anomalous-cognition experiments is a long argument over exactly this residue. Meta-analyses have reported above-chance aggregate performance; critics have challenged study quality, heterogeneity, publication bias and analytical choices. Later exchanges did not produce the clean extinction one expects from an error exposed once and for all. Neither did they produce the robust, portable phenomenon that would end the argument.

That is why remote viewing should not be evaluated by slogan. “Replicated” can mean a statistically nonzero aggregate across many studies. An intelligence officer hears a different word: can I use it Tuesday?

The laboratory can legitimately ask whether a tiny effect exists. Operations asks whether the effect can carry a decision. Those are different thresholds.

A great deal of confusion disappears once both questions are allowed to exist. One can regard a persistent statistical anomaly as scientifically interesting while judging it operationally useless. One can also judge the anomaly methodologically unresolved without claiming every participant was foolish or dishonest.

The goalposts did move. They were supposed to. Better experiments move them.
