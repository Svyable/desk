# Coordinates Without Maps

Coordinate remote viewing had a beautiful procedural audacity. Give a viewer numbers associated with a location while denying ordinary knowledge of the place. Ask for impressions. The coordinates were supposed to function as an address without functioning as a clue.

This solved one problem and created several others.

The method reduced direct cueing, but it did not automatically eliminate experimenter effects, target-set regularities, judging flexibility or feedback contamination. Training records show how much craft accumulated around the practice: stages, sensory descriptors, sketches, interviewer behavior, warnings about “analytical overlay.” The viewer was taught to distrust the mind's eagerness to name what it had only dimly described.

That is an unexpectedly sophisticated observation about cognition whether or not psi exists. Perception is inference-heavy. Once a person decides a shape is a tower, later impressions can be recruited to the tower. Remote-viewing protocols developed a vocabulary for trying to stop that cascade.

The irony is sharp. A program built around anomalous perception spent enormous effort protecting itself from ordinary cognition.

Coordinates also dramatized the mechanism problem. No known physical theory explained why latitude and longitude printed on paper should connect a mind to the corresponding landscape. Advocates sometimes treated the numbers as arbitrary labels, which only deepened the mystery. If any agreed identifier could cue a target, what exactly was being addressed?

The protocol could be operationalized before the ontology was understood. Science has occasionally tolerated that sequence. Medicine used anesthetics before fully understanding them. Magnetism was exploited before modern field theory. But those phenomena became reliable under controlled manipulation. Remote viewing never earned that escape hatch. Its mechanism remained obscure while its effect remained unstable.

A method can postpone theory. It cannot postpone reliability forever.
