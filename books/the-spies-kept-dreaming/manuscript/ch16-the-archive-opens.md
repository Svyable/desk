# The Archive Opens

Declassification created a second life for STARGATE.

Once the records became searchable, the program escaped the institutions that had scored and contextualized it. A session page could circulate alone. A striking sketch no longer had to travel beside the misses. Classification markings became visual rhetoric.

The internet is exceptionally good at removing denominators.

This does not make the archive less valuable. It makes archival method more important. A session must be tied to its target, cueing conditions, date, protocol, feedback, evaluator and neighboring sessions. A memorandum must be read for what it says, not what its existence is imagined to imply. Redactions are unknowns, not blank checks.

Declassified anomalous programs produce a peculiar evidentiary ecology. Secrecy once prevented outside scrutiny; release later supplies fragments that can be overinterpreted precisely because the former secrecy makes them feel privileged.

The cure is not distrust of documents. It is document literacy.
