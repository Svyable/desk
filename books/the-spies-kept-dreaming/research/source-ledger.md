# Research ledger — The Spies Kept Dreaming

## Evidence posture

**Declassified-heavy on program existence and operational tasking; mixed on laboratory anomaly; weak on claimed operational successes; speculative only in institutional synthesis.**

### Class 1 — verified / declassified / documented

- CIA publicly acknowledges that it studied remote viewing, transferred work to DIA, later received the program back for independent review, and did not restore it after the 1995 evaluation: https://www.cia.gov/stories/story/ask-molly-did-cia-really-study-psychic-powers/
- CIA STARGATE/GRILL FLAME session records document operational tasking concerning the Tehran hostages and explicitly label viewer impressions raw intelligence data. Example: https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp96-00788r000800580001-1
- The archive contains explicit poor-performance records. Session 876 says post-session analysis found very little correlation: https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp96-00788r001400250001-0
- A released 1995 operational summary says the DoD program received more than 200 operational tasks from 1986 through early FY1995 and describes later customer scoring: https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/CIA-RDP96-00791R000200300002-2.pdf

### Class 2 — credible but incomplete

- Participant recollections about particular successful sessions, internal reactions, and program politics. These require session-level archival corroboration before narrative use.
- Accounts of Soviet/Russian parapsychology programs. American threat assessments establish U.S. concern; they do not by themselves establish Soviet capability.

### Class 3 — disputed allegations

- Claims that remote viewing reliably located hostages, submarines, aircraft, individuals or facilities in ways unavailable to conventional intelligence.
- Claims that program duration or classification proves officials privately knew psi was real.
- Claims that termination proves psi was scientifically disproven.

### Class 4 — speculative synthesis / open conjecture

- The program may have persisted because a small statistical anomaly survived some controls while failing operational calibration.
- Strategic fear of Soviet research may have lowered the threshold for continued investigation.
- Institutional survival may reflect a mixture of occasional striking results, selection effects, sponsor interest and low program cost rather than one cause.

## Peer-reviewed controversy spine

- Storm, Tressoldi & Di Risio and related ganzfeld meta-analytic debate, PubMed entry: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20565166/
- Storm & Ertel discussion of Milton/Wiseman and aggregate ganzfeld datasets: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11393304/
- Registered-report-stage meta-analysis covering more than four decades of ganzfeld research: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33628430/

These sources establish an active statistical/methodological dispute, not settled proof of anomalous cognition.

## Contradictions and incentive audit

1. **Sponsor incentive:** intelligence agencies are penalized for missing adversary capabilities, which can rationalize investigation without strong prior evidence.
2. **Researcher incentive:** unusual positive results are more publishable and memorable than null sessions.
3. **Skeptical incentive:** reputational protection can encourage premature dismissal of stigmatized subjects; this is a sociological possibility, not evidence for psi.
4. **Participant incentive:** retrospective memory can sharpen successes and blur misses without deliberate deception.
5. **Archive incentive:** declassification preserves what was documented, not necessarily a balanced experimental dataset.
6. **Internet selection:** striking session pages circulate independently of denominators and neighboring failures.
