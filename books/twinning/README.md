## Premise

Digital twins are becoming an operating layer between reality and decision-making.

A useful twin is more than a rendering, dashboard, or static simulation. It is a model connected closely enough to a real thing, process, body, machine, or place that people and machines can use the model to test decisions before committing those decisions to the world.

The book follows that idea across four scales:

1. **Companies** — factories, products, operations, supply chains, and organizations that can increasingly be rehearsed before they are changed.
2. **Humans** — emerging patient-specific models that may let medicine move from the average patient toward the modeled individual.
3. **Robots** — simulated worlds and machine counterparts in which physical AI can practice, fail, and learn before expensive mistakes happen in reality.
4. **Cities, countries, and the planet** — infrastructure, urban systems, national digital-twin programs, and Earth-scale models that turn public policy into a new kind of simulation problem.

## Working thesis

**Digital twins are the technology of rehearsal.**

The important shift is not that reality can be copied. Humans have made maps and models forever. The shift is that the copy can now remain connected to the original, update as the original changes, and increasingly influence what happens next.

That creates a second thesis for the book: **when decisions move into the twin, power moves there too.** Whoever chooses what the twin measures, what it ignores, what it predicts, and what it optimizes can shape the real system through its model.
