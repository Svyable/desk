# THE ARTIFACT TEST
## What would it take to prove we found technology that did not come from Earth?

### Author's rule

This book does not argue that an interstellar object is a spacecraft. It asks a harder question: **what observation would allow us to know that one was?**

The distinction matters because the Solar System has begun receiving identified visitors from elsewhere. `1I/ʻOumuamua`, `2I/Borisov`, and `3I/ATLAS` have turned a hypothetical observing problem into a recurring empirical one. Natural objects are the baseline. Artificial origin is an extraordinary alternative. Neither sentence tells us how to design the test.

## Evidence classes

1. **Verified / documented:** interstellar objects have been detected on hyperbolic trajectories; 3I/ATLAS was discovered July 1, 2025; NASA describes its observed characteristics as consistent with a comet; multiple spacecraft observed it; Breakthrough Listen performed targeted radio searches and reported no technosignature detection.
2. **Credible but incomplete:** published SETI/technosignature literature treats extraterrestrial artifacts or probes as physically possible search targets. A nondetection constrains specified transmitters, bands, epochs, geometries and sensitivities; it does not test every artificial-object hypothesis.
3. **Disputed / unconfirmed:** claims that any known interstellar object is manufactured, controlled, reconnaissance hardware, a probe, or evidence of extraterrestrial intelligence.
4. **Speculative synthesis:** the most useful legacy of the interstellar-object controversy may be an explicit *artifact protocol*: a precommitted escalation ladder for deciding when an astronomical anomaly deserves increasingly expensive artificial-origin tests.

# 1. The Third Visitor

On July 1, 2025, a telescope in Chile found something leaving.

That is not literally how discovery worked. Astronomers found an object and then reconstructed its path. The path was the important part. Run backward, it did not close around the Sun. It arrived from outside. The archive then gave the object a past: observations before discovery, hiding in images nobody had yet known were important.

The object became 3I/ATLAS. The third recognized interstellar object.

Three is not a census. It is barely a category. But three changes the grammar. The first can be an astonishment. The second can be confirmation that the first was not a unique accident. By the third, somebody has to write the checklist.

NASA's public description is admirably boring. 3I/ATLAS has an icy nucleus, a coma, dust and gas, and properties consistent with a comet. It was watched by an absurdly capable accidental observatory: Hubble, Webb, TESS, Swift, SPHEREx, Mars spacecraft, Europa Clipper, Lucy, Psyche, Parker Solar Probe, PUNCH and SOHO. The visitor crossed not an empty Solar System but an instrumented one.

Then another scientific culture looked at the same object and asked a different question.

Breakthrough Listen searched for radio technosignatures. Its public record says there is no evidence 3I/ATLAS is anything other than natural. It also says interstellar probes are a physically plausible technosignature category and therefore worth testing. Green Bank observations around closest approach searched 1–12 GHz and reported no artificial radio emission localized to the object. The important sentence is not *aliens disproved*. The important sentence is that somebody specified an instrument, frequency range, sensitivity, time and target, then got a null result.

That is how extraordinary claims are supposed to become smaller.

Not through ridicule. Through denominators.

The popular argument about anomalous interstellar objects usually starts too late. One side collects oddities. Another side explains them. Each new measurement is fed into an argument whose conclusion already has a constituency.

I want to start earlier.

Suppose tomorrow's survey finds 4I.

What would make us escalate?

A hyperbolic orbit is not enough; that is the admission ticket to the category. An unusual shape is not enough if the shape is inferred from a light curve with degeneracies. Non-gravitational acceleration is not enough if outgassing, radiation pressure, fragmentation or measurement systematics remain viable. A radio signal is not enough unless it tracks the object, survives terrestrial-interference rejection, repeats or carries structure, and can be independently observed. An isotope ratio is not enough merely because it is unfamiliar. Nature owns unfamiliar chemistry.

The artificial hypothesis has an asymmetric burden. It cannot merely explain an anomaly. It has to explain the measurements better than a growing family of natural models *and* survive tests that those natural models predict differently.

This sounds obvious. It is violated constantly.

The artifact problem is therefore partly an astronomy problem and partly an intelligence problem. Intelligence analysts know a version of it already. A strange radar track is not an aircraft type. A recovered component is not provenance. A capability is not deployment. An intercepted statement is not an event. The mistake is causal promotion: evidence earns one rung and prose quietly carries it six rungs higher.

Astronomy has its own version:

**trajectory → anomaly → artificiality → intention → origin → intelligence.**

Every arrow is a separate argument.

The book will live inside those arrows.

## The beautiful null

A null observation is often treated as dead narrative. It is actually where the epistemology becomes visible.

Breakthrough Listen did not detect a technosignature from 3I/ATLAS. That result is useful because its boundary can be written down. A narrowband radio search says something about transmitters of particular apparent power in particular frequency coverage during particular observing windows. It says less about a silent artifact. It says nothing direct about an object communicating optically when nobody is looking, an inert manufactured fragment, a dead probe, a natural comet carrying biology, or a hypothesis invented after the measurement to evade it.

That last category matters.

A theory that survives every null by changing what the artifact was supposed to be doing is not becoming stronger. It is becoming unfalsifiable.

So the artifact protocol needs a rule before the next visitor arrives: **write down what observation would lower the artificial-origin probability, not only what would raise it.**

That sounds almost embarrassingly basic. It may be the whole book.

## The artifact ladder

The first draft of the ladder is deliberately severe.

**Level 0 — foreign rock.** Interstellar provenance only.

**Level 1 — physical anomaly.** A measurement resists the currently adequate natural models, with instrument/systematic explanations explicitly audited.

**Level 2 — convergent anomaly.** Independent modalities produce constraints that point toward the same unresolved physical property. Spectroscopy and dynamics. Imaging and thermal behavior. Radio and optical timing. One weird residual is not convergence.

**Level 3 — engineered signature.** A property has a credible manufacturing interpretation that is difficult to generate naturally: not merely low probability, but structure, composition, modulation or thermodynamic behavior with an independently specified artificial alternative.

**Level 4 — independent replication.** Another instrument, team or observing geometry sees the signature.

**Level 5 — artifact.** Artificiality becomes the best-supported explanation.

**Level 6 — extraterrestrial artifact.** Terrestrial hardware, contamination, classified human systems and other human provenance are excluded where relevant; trajectory and physical evidence support origin beyond Earth.

**Level 7 — intelligent provenance.** The artifact contains evidence of intentional construction rather than an accidental technological by-product.

Notice how late the exciting words arrive.

This is intentional.

The protocol is not designed to prevent discovery. It is designed to make discovery survive contact with everybody who desperately wants it to be false.

## Why 3I matters even if it is only a comet

Especially if it is only a comet.

A natural interstellar comet is calibration material from another planetary system delivered free of charge. It tells us what alien *nature* looks like. Every well-characterized natural visitor expands the null distribution against which a future artificiality claim must compete.

That is the inversion at the center of this book.

SETI does not only need stranger anomalies. It needs a much better library of ordinary alien things.

A hundred interstellar comets with measured volatiles, dust behavior, rotation states, non-gravitational accelerations, fragmentation histories and thermal properties would make the hundred-and-first anomaly more interpretable than any argument conducted from a sample of one. The search for artifacts is therefore helped, not harmed, every time an exciting object turns out to be natural.

Nature is building the control group.

We should let it.
