# Exponential Compression — Book Brief

## Working subtitle

How Falling Costs Turn Scarcity Into Abundance — and Rebuild the World Around What Comes Next

## Premise

Technology advances by compressing costs. The cost of moving goods falls. The cost of transmitting a message falls. The cost of storing information falls. The cost of performing a calculation falls. The cost of publishing an idea falls.

At first, cheaper looks like cheaper. Then the cost falls far enough that people stop conserving the capability. They use it in places where nobody previously would have considered using it. The technology ceases to be a specialist tool and becomes part of the environment.

Artificial intelligence is beginning to do this to cognition.

The book's central question is not simply what AI replaces. It is what civilization does when useful thinking becomes cheap enough to spend casually, continuously, and at enormous scale.

## Central thesis

When the cost of a capability falls exponentially, demand does not remain constant. New uses appear until another constraint becomes scarce.

Technological progress therefore does not remove bottlenecks so much as reveal the next one.

## Core ideas

- A ten-percent cost decline tends to produce optimization; a tenfold decline can produce a new category of behavior.
- Abundance is visible when waste becomes rational.
- Efficiency gains are often consumed rather than saved.
- Forecasts based only on current demand miss uses that become sensible only after a cost threshold is crossed.
- AI is unusual because cognition sits upstream of software, research, coordination, design, persuasion, and many other economic activities.
- Cheap cognition may make energy, trust, taste, authority, verified data, physical execution, and human attention relatively more valuable.
- The deepest compression is the shrinking distance between intention and execution.

## Planned structure

### Part I — The Price Falls

1. **The Expensive Way** — How scarcity shaped behavior before capabilities became cheap.
2. **Ten Times Cheaper** — Why order-of-magnitude cost changes create new markets rather than merely cheaper old ones.
3. **The Staircase to Free** — The behavioral thresholds crossed as a capability moves from luxury to infrastructure.
4. **Waste Is the Signal** — Why abundance often appears as apparently irrational overuse.

### Part II — We Consume the Savings

5. **Jevons Was Everywhere** — Why efficiency frequently expands total consumption.
6. **The Things Nobody Used to Do** — Applications that only become imaginable after the cost curve moves.
7. **Demand Appears Behind the Curve** — Latent demand for tutoring, legal review, research, software, translation, and other expensive cognition.

### Part III — Cognition Becomes Cheap

8. **The Most Expensive Ingredient** — The cognitive bill hidden inside every organization.
9. **Tokens** — The historical oddity of buying pieces of cognition as metered computation.
10. **The Infinite Intern** — Continuous low-cost assistance as infrastructure rather than occasional tool use.
11. **Software That Writes Software** — Temporary, personal, one-use, and disposable software.
12. **A Researcher for Every Question** — Falling search and synthesis costs expand what is worth investigating.
13. **The Synthetic Firm** — What happens to organizational scale when coordination and expertise become callable.

### Part IV — Compression Propagates

14. **The Last Interface** — The movement from procedural interfaces toward expressed intention.
15. **Compression of Time** — When a month becomes a minute, even at the same monetary price, the use case changes.
16. **Compression of Organization** — More capability can be invoked instead of owned.
17. **Compression of Distance** — Expertise becomes locally callable even when the expert is elsewhere or nonexistent.

### Part V — The Bottleneck Moves

18. **What Becomes Expensive Now?** — Energy, chips, data, permits, attention, trust, judgment, taste, and responsibility.
19. **The Value of Reality** — Why authenticated physical and human things may gain a premium amid generative abundance.
20. **Taste** — Production abundance shifts value toward selection.
21. **Authority** — Capability does not imply permission.
22. **Energy Is Waiting** — Digital abundance eventually runs into the physical economy.

### Part VI — From Intention to Execution

23. **The Distance Between Wanting and Having** — Civilization as machinery for converting intention into outcome.
24. **Describe It** — Natural language becomes a general interface to creation and action.
25. **The Remaining Friction** — Which delays, checks, and constraints remain valuable when accidental friction disappears.
26. **Exponential Compression** — The world after cognition, software, and coordination become cheap enough to waste.

## Research directions

- Historical cost curves for illumination, computation, storage, bandwidth, shipping, sequencing, solar power, and other candidate compressions.
- Jevons paradox and rebound effects, including cases where efficiency did and did not expand total consumption.
- Price-per-token and inference-cost trajectories, carefully separating model quality from cost.
- Organizational economics: transaction costs, firm boundaries, and the effect of cheaper coordination.
- Disposable software and generated interfaces as a new consumption category.
- Energy and datacenter constraints as examples of bottlenecks moving downstream.
- Verification, provenance, licensing, and authority as potentially scarce complements to abundant generation.

## Guardrails

Do not turn the book into a catalogue of exponential charts. The argument should live in concrete transitions where falling cost changed behavior. Avoid claiming that every technology follows the same curve or that costs inevitably reach zero. Compression can stall, reverse, or expose constraints that dominate the original improvement.

The AI sections should distinguish observed cost decline from speculative future capability. The stronger argument does not require omnipotent models. It only requires useful cognition to become materially cheaper and more available than it was before.
