## Author's Note

This is a book about imagination after it became cheap to render.

Generative AI is often described through capability: what a model can write, draw, compose, simulate, or code. Those questions matter. This book asks a different one. What happens to people when ideas can become visible, conversational, and revisable before they have fully formed?

The argument is neither that machines have acquired a human imagination nor that human imagination is being replaced. It is that the interface between an internal possibility and an external representation has changed dramatically. That change affects creative work, planning, identity, memory, taste, childhood, persuasion, and the ordinary pleasure of trying on lives we do not intend to live.

I call that last behavior *vibecariously*: experiencing the emotional preview of a possibility through a generated representation without paying the full cost of inhabiting the underlying reality.

The word is playful. The shift is not.

This manuscript distinguishes generated representations from evidence, prototypes from working systems, simulations from forecasts, and synthetic memories from records of events. Where factual claims depend on research or contemporary policy, the working source trail lives in `research/README.md` alongside the manuscript.

## A Note on the Title

*Vibecariously* combines *vibe* and *vicariously*. It names the increasingly ordinary experience of inhabiting the feel of an unrealized possibility: the house before the mortgage, the city before the move, the brand before the company, the film before the shoot, the self before the decision.

Generative AI did not invent fantasy, mood boards, previsualization, role-play, counterfactual thinking, or vicarious experience. It made them responsive at a new scale. The imagined thing can now answer back.
