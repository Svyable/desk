## A Note on Evidence

*Vibecariously* is an argument about a changing creative medium, not a claim that every observed change in culture can be causally assigned to generative AI. The manuscript therefore separates three kinds of material wherever possible: documented capabilities and institutional facts; research findings about creativity, cognition, automation, or media; and interpretive claims about how those developments may change habits of imagination.

The detailed working source trail is kept in `research/README.md`. Primary technical documentation, court and government material, original research papers, and creator or institutional statements are preferred where available. Contemporary examples are used to establish capability and practice, not to imply that one anecdote proves a population-wide effect.

Several claims are intentionally bounded. Generative models can produce novel outputs without that resolving philosophical questions about whether machines “imagine.” Research showing productivity gains in some creative or knowledge tasks does not establish that the resulting work is more original, culturally valuable, or diverse. Evidence of convergence toward common outputs in some human-AI experiments does not prove inevitable cultural homogenization. Copyright law concerning training, output authorship, substantial similarity, and style imitation remains jurisdiction-specific and actively contested. Synthetic reconstruction can support visualization and reflection but is not evidence that an imagined or reconstructed event occurred.
