# Evidence Ledger

## Class 1 — verified / documented

- LiDAR records surface geometry through timed laser returns and can produce bare-earth terrain models where sufficient ground returns penetrate vegetation.
- PACUNAM airborne scanning mapped extensive Maya settlement and infrastructure across roughly 2,100 km² of northern Guatemala.
- The 2025 Dzibanché survey covered 103 km² and reported 13,928 structures, with higher measured structure density than an equivalent Tikal comparison area.
- Aguada Fénix is a very large, low-relief earthen monumental platform identified through LiDAR and tested by excavation, with main construction dated to the early first millennium BCE.
- The 2024 Upano Valley work maps an extensive system of earthen platforms, roads and agricultural modifications supported by excavation and radiocarbon chronology.
- The 2026 southwestern Amazon survey documented 432 earthworks in new LiDAR data, only 36 previously known.
- Mahendraparvata LiDAR maps a road grid, water-management works and distributed features over the Phnom Kulen plateau.

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete / model-dependent

- Dzibanché population estimates around 36,000–55,000 under stated multipliers.
- Regional Late Classic Maya population estimates derived from LiDAR samples and demographic modeling.
- Pärssinen et al.’s estimate of roughly 24,000–30,000 earthworks and 1.25–3 million people in the Aquiry area around AD 100–300.
- Functional interpretations of many mapped roads, ceremonial enclosures, reservoirs and residential platforms where only subsets have been excavated.
- Interpretation of several tropical systems as low-density or garden urbanism.

## Class 3 — disputed / unconfirmed / overextended

- Media claims that LiDAR has discovered wholly unknown “lost civilizations” where sites or traditions were already locally or archaeologically known.
- Treating modeled regional site totals as directly observed counts.
- Treating all visible features in a composite terrain model as contemporary.
- Using recent Amazonian archaeology to claim the entire Amazon basin was densely urbanized.
- Using evidence of ancient landscape management to justify modern industrial deforestation or extraction.
- Claims that recent LiDAR discoveries substantiate Atlantis, a vanished global parent civilization, or other narratives not evidenced by the archaeology.

## Class 4 — speculative synthesis / open conjecture

- Archaeological visibility bias may explain a significant fraction of older cross-regional differences in inferred settlement complexity.
- Existing government and commercial LiDAR archives may contain substantial archaeological information not yet systematically inspected.
- Machine-assisted terrain analysis may shift archaeology’s primary bottleneck from data acquisition to validation and field verification.
- Some modern forest composition and biodiversity patterns may preserve cultural legacies at scales not yet mapped, but this must be demonstrated region by region.
