## Indigenous data governance and heritage access

- Global Indigenous Data Alliance, **CARE Principles for Indigenous Data Governance**. https://www.gida-global.org/careprinciples — CARE = Collective Benefit, Authority to Control, Responsibility and Ethics. Explicitly frames CARE as complementary to FAIR and identifies open-data systems' tendency to omit power, historical context and Indigenous rights/interests. Used in Ch 16 to distinguish technical openness from governance authority.
- Local Contexts, **Grounding Indigenous Rights**. https://localcontexts.org/ — Indigenous-data-sovereignty infrastructure for community authority over collection, management, display, access and future use of heritage/data. Used as a practical governance example rather than as a claim that one framework fits every community.
- Local Contexts, **Traditional Knowledge Labels**. https://localcontexts.org/labels/traditional-knowledge-labels/ — community-controlled provenance, protocol and permission labels, including culturally sensitive, secret/sacred, seasonal, community-use-only, non-commercial and collaboration categories. Used to show that authority metadata can travel with digital heritage data.
- Local Contexts, **About the Labels**. https://localcontexts.org/labels/about-the-labels/ — explains TK and Biocultural Labels as tools for embedding community-specific access/use protocols in datasets, publications, collections and digital systems.
- Local Contexts / Creative Commons (2026), **Guidance for Indigenous Collections and Data** summary. https://localcontexts.org/new-guidance-for-indigenous-collections-and-data/ — practical 2026 guidance showing that ordinary licensing and Indigenous data-governance tools address different layers of authority and can be used together.
