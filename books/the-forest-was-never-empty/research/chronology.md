# Chronology

- c. 1000–800 BCE — major construction phase at Aguada Fénix.
- c. 600 BCE–AD 850 — broad dated range for many geometric earthworks associated with the southwestern Amazonian Aquiry phenomenon, with regional variation.
- c. 500 BCE–AD 300/600 — major Upano occupation and mound/road construction sequence reported by Rostain et al.
- c. AD 100–300 — period of strongest modeled overlap for Aquiry earthwork use in the 2026 study.
- ninth century CE — early Khmer capital landscape at Mahendraparvata.
- sixth–eleventh centuries CE — broad occupational frame for medieval highland Central Asian sites including Tugunbulak/Tashbulak, pending primary-paper reconstruction.
- 2010s — airborne archaeological LiDAR scales rapidly across Mesoamerica and Cambodia.
- 2018 — major PACUNAM northern Guatemala results publicized and published.
- 2019 — Mahendraparvata urban-landscape synthesis published in *Antiquity*.
- 2020 — Aguada Fénix reported in *Nature*.
- 2024 — Upano Valley garden-urbanism paper published in *Science*; new southwestern Amazon ALS survey flown.
- 2025 — Dzibanché LiDAR urbanism study and Reese-Taylor’s lost-city media critique published.
- 29 July 2026 — Pärssinen et al. publish the new southwestern Amazon survey and regional earthwork/population extrapolations in *Nature*.
