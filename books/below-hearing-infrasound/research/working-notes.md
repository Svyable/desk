# Working notes

## Central question

What happens when a sensor network designed for an adversarial legal purpose becomes a scientific instrument for a planet that produces many of the same physical observables?

## Thesis

The CTBT infrasound network is useful precisely because its verification problem is harder than “hear an explosion.” Low-frequency atmospheric pressure waves can travel thousands of kilometres, but they are produced by meteors, volcanoes, mining blasts, chemical explosions, rockets, aircraft, storms, ocean-wave interactions and other sources. The network therefore had to become an instrument not just for sensitivity but for discrimination under a moving propagation medium. Scientific “background” events became calibration data, and treaty infrastructure became a planetary observatory.

## Four evidence classes

### 1. Verified / documented

- The CTBT Protocol establishes the IMS and IDC verification architecture.
- The planned infrasound component consists of 60 array stations; CTBTO reports the wider IMS as 321 monitoring stations plus 16 laboratories when complete.
- IMS infrasound stations recorded Chelyabinsk in 2013; peer-reviewed work used those observations in energy and network-performance analysis.
- Infrasound has detected remote volcanic explosions, including events obscured from some satellite analyses by weather.
- The 2022 Hunga eruption generated extraordinary global atmospheric waves and was recorded across the infrasound network.
- The IMS has detected declared DPRK nuclear tests with multiple technologies, establishing that the verification network performs its intended mission as well as recording natural events.

### 2. Credible but incomplete / model-dependent

- Yield or source-energy estimates inferred from distant infrasound.
- Atmospheric-path reconstructions and duct attribution.
- Source directivity corrections.
- Global automatic classification performance for heterogeneous event classes.
- The exact degree to which scientific calibration events improve operational treaty verification.

### 3. Disputed / not established

- That infrasound alone uniquely identifies nuclear versus chemical explosions in every geometry.
- That every nondetection rules out an event.
- That unusual atmospheric arrivals without corroboration establish covert weapons testing, anomalous aerospace technology, or unknown physics.
- That source energy can be recovered from one distant amplitude without a propagation model and source-geometry assumptions.

### 4. Speculative synthesis / open conjecture

- Adversarial verification systems may become unusually good scientific observatories because false positives are not disposable; they must be understood.
- “Noise” becomes a durable epistemic asset when it is catalogued across years by authenticated, geographically stable instruments.
- The atmosphere is part of the detector, not merely the channel between source and instrument.
- A mature verification regime depends as much on modeled nondetection and cross-sensor consistency as on spectacular detections.

## Contradictions and incentive audit

CTBTO has an institutional incentive to demonstrate verification capability and peaceful scientific benefits. Scientific authors have incentives to make exceptional natural events methodologically useful. National authorities retain sovereign interpretation of treaty data and may have security reasons not to publicize all analytical detail. None of those incentives invalidates the measurements; they make source triangulation important.

Natural events can look like useful calibration shots because their independent location or energy can be estimated from other data. They are not perfect substitutes for controlled explosions: source geometry differs, altitudes differ, wave spectra differ, and the atmosphere changes between events.

## Defeat conditions

The book’s synthesis weakens if primary records show that non-treaty detections played no meaningful role in infrasound calibration, atmospheric modeling, or algorithm development; if the network’s scientific uses are largely independent of its treaty-derived geometry and continuity; or if a much older global civilian infrasound network already supplied equivalent continuous authenticated coverage. The argument should narrow rather than resist such evidence.

## Chapter movement

1. Start with the treaty problem.
2. Explain the physical signal without mystifying it.
3. Show why arrays and geography matter.
4. Make the atmosphere part of the instrument.
5. Introduce persistent natural noise.
6. Return to the forbidden source class.
7–11. Let meteors, volcanoes, Hunga, industrial explosions and aerospace sources stress the classifier.
12–14. Move from detections to calibration, false positives and nondetections.
15–17. Follow treaty data into science and governance.
18. End with the narrower idea of a signal: not a waveform, but a claim that survives alternatives.
