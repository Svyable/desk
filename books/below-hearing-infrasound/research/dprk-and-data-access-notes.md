# DPRK and data-access notes

## 2017 DPRK event: keep the evidentiary ladder explicit

CTBTO’s public technical record for the 3 September 2017 DPRK event is unusually useful because it refuses a shortcut the manuscript also needs to refuse.

Primary source: https://www.ctbto.org/our-work/detecting-nuclear-tests/2017-dprk-nuclear-test

Documented sequence:

- IDC automatic processing detected an unusual seismic event at 03:30 UTC.
- The initial magnitude estimate was 5.8 and was revised to 6.1.
- More than 100 monitoring facilities ultimately contributed; the reviewed event used 41 primary and 90 auxiliary seismic stations, and signals were associated at two hydroacoustic stations and one infrasound station.
- The location was the DPRK test-site area.
- CTBTO’s reviewed characterization was **consistent with a man-made explosion**.
- CTBTO explicitly stated that the event could only be classified as nuclear with corresponding nuclear-specific evidence such as airborne radioactivity; no radioactivity from this 2017 event was detected by the IMS.
- A second event roughly 8.5 minutes later was analyzed separately and later evidence supported a cavity-collapse interpretation rather than a second explosion.

This is a stronger example than a generic statement that the four IMS technologies are complementary. The mechanical sensors can strongly constrain *explosion*. Nuclear identity is a narrower claim. The public CTBTO wording should be quoted/paraphrased precisely in a later chapter expansion rather than simplified into “the IMS proved it was nuclear.”

Related CTBTO overview: https://www.ctbto.org/our-work/detecting-nuclear-tests

The overview records a different evidence chain for the 12 February 2013 announced DPRK test: 94 seismic and two infrasound stations registered the event, and radioactive xenon isotopes were later detected at Takasaki, Japan, and Ussuriysk, Russia, providing nuclear-fission-specific evidence. This is an ideal contrast case for the manuscript.

CTBTO Science & Technology abstracts also document seismoacoustic work on source depth and atmospheric variability:

- Assink et al. / ST2017, seismo-acoustic source-depth analysis: https://ctnw.ctbto.org/ctnw/abstract/21329
- Koch / ST2017, detection patterns at IS45 and IS30: https://conferences.ctbto.org/event/16/contributions/2794/
- Stump / ST2017, five DPRK explosions with regional seismic and infrasound analysis: https://conferences.ctbto.org/event/16/contributions/2956/
- Koch / ST2019, 2017 test signals and modeled propagation at IS45: https://conferences.ctbto.org/event/17/contributions/3528/

These sources support a deeper eventual chapter section on the same underground source class producing very different acoustic observability under different depth/atmospheric conditions. Conference abstracts are not substitutes for full peer-reviewed papers or IDC operational records; use them as leads and bounded evidence of the analyses presented.

## vDEC: scientific access is structured, not synonymous with unrestricted public data

Primary CTBTO sources:

- https://www.ctbto.org/our-work/civil-and-scientific-applications
- https://www.ctbto.org/resources/for-researchers-experts/vdec

CTBTO states that its virtual Data Exploitation Centre gives approved scientific organizations access to IMS data for research at no charge. Researchers use the data for published studies and exchange results through CTBTO conferences, workshops, and meetings.

The institutional claim worth preserving is bidirectional: CTBTO itself says scientific use helps its experts improve nuclear-explosion detection, including through better understanding of the Earth, atmosphere and transport processes. That is institutional evidence for the manuscript’s *feedback* thesis, though not yet a quantitative demonstration of how much any specific paper improved operational verification.

A CTBTO anniversary booklet additionally describes scientific access through vDEC as contract-based and distinguishes scientific access from the most recent treaty-monitoring data. Before manuscript expansion, page-index the current governing document and verify whether the stated delay remains current rather than freezing an older policy into the book.

## Revision targets

- Chapter 6 should eventually center the 2013-versus-2017 DPRK evidence chains rather than speaking generically about multi-technology verification.
- Chapter 14 can use the 2013/2016 seismoacoustic contrast as a real example of how atmospheric state and source depth change the evidentiary value of an infrasound nondetection.
- Chapters 15–16 should describe vDEC as controlled scientific access, not “open data,” and should distinguish the public research ecology from States Signatories’ real-time treaty data flows.
