# Breathless Lake — Working Chronology

- **15 August 1984 — Lake Monoun:** lethal gas burst kills 37 people in the standard scientific account. Subsequent investigation identifies unusually CO2-rich deep water and helps establish the stored-gas mechanism.
- **21 August 1986 — Lake Nyos:** catastrophic gas release kills roughly 1,700–1,800 people and thousands of livestock. Exact totals vary by source and counting convention and should be frozen from primary Cameroonian/investigation records in the next pass.
- **September–November 1986:** international scientific and medical teams investigate survivors, lake chemistry, geology, and plume effects. Deep-water stratification and high dissolved CO2 remain measurable after the event.
- **1987:** U.S. scientific team publishes USGS Open-File Report 87-97; geochemical and limnological papers develop the CO2-release interpretation.
- **1988–1989:** separate work highlights the weakness of Lake Nyos’s natural dam and investigates the origin/age of the maar.
- **Late 1980s–1990s:** repeated measurements show continuing CO2 recharge in Lakes Nyos and Monoun, demonstrating that the hazard can rebuild.
- **2001 — Lake Nyos:** controlled degassing begins with a permanent pipe system.
- **2003 — Lake Monoun:** controlled degassing begins.
- **2005:** PNAS analysis reports gas removal without dangerous destabilization but concludes that additional pipes are required for faster risk reduction.
- **2008:** long-term monitoring analysis documents CO2 evolution before and during controlled degassing and highlights the possibility of spontaneous release near saturation.
- **2010s:** additional degassing infrastructure and monitoring deepen the engineering record; comparative limnology work tracks chemocline changes and water-column response.
- **2016:** thirty-year scientific retrospective emphasizes both the rarity of the event class and the unusual possibility of active mitigation.
- **2020s:** Lake Kivu risk literature continues to use Nyos and Monoun as critical analogues while stressing major differences in scale, methane, stratification, extraction, and system dynamics.

## Chronology cautions

This timeline is a scaffold, not yet a publication-ready chronology. The next pass should add exact dates for each scientific mission, first public hypotheses, pipe installation milestones, dam-remediation decisions, resettlement/return policies, and Cameroonian institutional actions.
