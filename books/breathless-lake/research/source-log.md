# Breathless Lake — Source Log

## Core primary / authoritative sources

1. Tuttle, M.L., Clark, M., Compson, H., Devine, J., Evans, W.C., Humphrey, A., Kling, G., Koenigsberg, E., Lockwood, J.P., Wagner, G.N. (1987). **The 21 August 1986 Lake Nyos gas disaster, Cameroon; final report of the United States scientific team to the Office of U.S. Foreign Disaster Assistance of the Agency for International Development.** USGS Open-File Report 87-97. https://doi.org/10.3133/ofr8797
   - Use for chronology, field observations, medical findings, geology, lake chemistry, plume reconstruction, and the limits of immediate post-disaster inference.

2. Kusakabe, M. & Ohsumi, T. (1987). **Outburst of carbon dioxide from Lake Nyos, Cameroon.** Chikyukagaku 21(2), 39–47. https://doi.org/10.14934/chikyukagaku.21.39
   - Geochemical and limnological survey roughly 50 days after the event. Important for dissolved CO2, Fe2+, carbon and helium isotope evidence, and argument against direct hot volcanic-gas injection as the fatal mechanism.

3. Sigurdsson, H. et al. (1987). **Lethal gas bursts from Cameroon Crater Lakes.** Eos 68, 570–573. https://doi.org/10.1029/EO068i023p00570-04
   - Critical Lake Monoun precursor record and early articulation of stored dissolved CO2 hazard.

4. Lockwood, J.P. & Rubin, M. (1989). **Origin and age of the Lake Nyos maar, Cameroon.** Journal of Volcanology and Geothermal Research. USGS record: https://www.usgs.gov/publications/origin-and-age-lake-nyos-maar-cameroon
   - Geological setting, maar age, and deep-carbon context.

5. Lockwood, J.P., Costa, J.E., Tuttle, M.L., Nni, J., Tebor, S.G. (1988). **The potential for catastrophic dam failure at Lake Nyos maar, Cameroon.** Bulletin of Volcanology. https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01073590
   - Separate natural-dam hazard; important to keep flood risk distinct from gas-burst risk.

6. Kling, G.W., Evans, W.C., Tanyileke, G., Kusakabe, M., Ohba, T., Yoshida, Y., Hell, J.V. (2005). **Degassing Lakes Nyos and Monoun: Defusing certain disaster.** PNAS. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0502274102
   - Gas recharge, saturation, early degassing performance, and need for additional pipes.

7. Kusakabe, M. et al. (2008). **Evolution of CO2 in Lakes Monoun and Nyos, Cameroon, before and during controlled degassing.** USGS record: https://www.usgs.gov/publications/evolution-co2-lakes-monoun-and-nyos-cameroon-and-during-controlled-degassing
   - Twenty-one-year monitoring perspective; especially important for spontaneous-release possibility at high saturation.

8. Kling, G., Evans, W.C., Tanyileke, G. (2015). **The comparative limnology of Lakes Nyos and Monoun, Cameroon.** https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-36833-2_18
   - Meromixis, seasonal mixing, chemocline movement, and post-degassing water-column behavior.

9. Rouwet, D., Tanyileke, G., Costa, A. (2016). **Cameroon’s Lake Nyos Gas Burst: 30 Years Later.** Eos. https://doi.org/10.1029/2016EO055627
   - Later scientific community perspective and degassing context.

10. **On the risk of a dissolved gas-triggered limnic eruption in Lake Kivu** (2025/2026 publication cycle), Environmental Science: Processes & Impacts. https://doi.org/10.1039/D5EM00513B
   - Current Kivu comparison. Use carefully: Kivu is not a scaled Nyos; methane, basin geometry, stratification, gas inventories, extraction, and population context differ.

## Evidence classes

### Class 1 — Verified / documented

- Lake Monoun disaster, 15 August 1984, 37 deaths in the standard scientific account.
- Lake Nyos disaster, 21 August 1986, roughly 1,700–1,800 deaths depending on source counting conventions.
- High dissolved CO2 in deep waters after the disaster.
- Mantle/deep geological contribution supported by isotope measurements.
- Persistent water-column stratification and deep isolation.
- Continued CO2 recharge after the 1986 event.
- Controlled degassing installations and measured gas reduction.
- Weak natural dam at Lake Nyos recognized as a separate hazard.

### Class 2 — Credible but incomplete

- Detailed pre-event gas inventory reconstructed from post-event conditions.
- Exact local cloud concentrations and exposure durations village by village.
- Exact role of groundwater pulses, rainfall, internal waves, slope failure, or other initiating disturbance.
- Full social history of warning, evacuation, return, and local risk perception.

### Class 3 — Disputed / not established

- A single proven external trigger for the 1986 release.
- A direct explosive magmatic eruption at the fatal moment as the primary release mechanism.
- Universal applicability of Nyos mechanics to all gas-rich volcanic lakes.
- Any claim that Kivu is certain to undergo an imminent Nyos-like catastrophe.

### Class 4 — Author synthesis / open conjecture

- Nyos as a case of “category failure”: a lake treated visually as landscape while physically functioning as a gas-storage system.
- Hazard recognition as a problem of vertical invisibility and disciplinary fragmentation.
- The degassing pipe as an unusually pure example of mitigation that harnesses the same feedback responsible for catastrophe.

## Source-quality rule

Where possible, manuscript expansion should cite the original measurement or investigation rather than later retellings. Later syntheses are useful for chronology and scientific consensus, but should not overwrite uncertainty present in the primary record.
