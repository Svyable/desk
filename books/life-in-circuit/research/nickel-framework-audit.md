# Nickel-framework audit — Life in Circuit

## Editorial target

Strengthen Chapters 4, 9, and 14 without turning a fast-moving materials result into a solved biological mechanism. The opening is already strongest when it distinguishes an observed current from the story we want the current to tell. The same discipline now needs to apply one level down: **conductive fibre → nickel dependence → molecular structure → charge-transport mechanism** are separate propositions.

## Primary evidence recovered

### Direct conduction and the current-bearing structure

Meysman et al., *Nature Communications* (2019), “A highly conductive fibre network enables centimetre-scale electron transport in multicellular cable bacteria.”

https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-12115-7

This is stronger than the current ledger's generic hold on direct conductivity. Intact filaments deposited across electrodes carried nanoampere currents; cutting a suspended filament stopped the current. Extracted periplasmic fibre sheaths retained similar electrical behavior, supporting the fibres as the current-bearing structures. The paper reported fibre conductivities up to 79 S cm−1 under its geometry assumptions and noted rapid conductance loss in air but stability under vacuum. The manuscript can therefore say that direct electrical measurements exist; it should not leave that fact as merely inferred from sediment geochemistry.

Important boundary: these measurements are on prepared filaments/fibre sheaths interfaced with electrodes. They establish intrinsic electronic conduction and identify the fibre network as the conduit; they are not a direct ammeter reading of the intact organism's in-sediment respiratory current.

### Nickel dependence

Boschker et al., *Nature Communications* (2021), “Efficient long-range conduction in cable bacteria through nickel protein wires.”

https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-021-24312-4

Microscopy, spectroscopy and chemical imaging support a conductive protein core with an insulating protein shell and a sulfur-ligated nickel cofactor. Conductivity decreased when nickel was oxidized or selectively removed. This is strong evidence that nickel chemistry is functionally involved in conduction and that cable-bacterium fibres differ from the iron/cytochrome architectures found in other electroactive bacteria.

Important boundary: the 2021 paper explicitly left the molecular structure of the nickel centre and the detailed electron-transport mechanism unresolved. “Nickel-dependent” was warranted; a precise molecular wire model was not yet warranted from this paper alone.

### Fresh September 2026 structural result

Meysman et al., *Nature Communications* (published 1 September 2026), “Highly efficient long-range conduction through a biosynthetic nickel-organic framework.”

https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-76989-0

This paper materially changes the source queue. The authors report that each conductive fibre contains a bundle of intertwined nanoribbons built from stacked nickel centres linked by organic dithiolene ligands, forming an extended nickel-organic framework. The paper estimates that more than 95% of cellular nickel is associated with the fibres under its accounting assumptions and argues that the supramolecular structure supplies an extended conductive conduit. It also notes a distinct Cu/S entity at the cartwheel junctions.

The paper cites previously measured fibre conductivities spanning roughly 5–500 S cm−1. Its much larger 150–15,000 S cm−1 nanoribbon range is **not a new direct conductivity measurement**: it is a geometric recalculation that assigns the previously measured conductance to the much smaller inferred nanoribbon cross-section. Keep those numbers separate in prose.

The structural claim is unusually important and unusually fresh. It deserves explicit dating in the manuscript rather than being folded invisibly into a timeless description of cable bacteria.

### Mechanism remains live

Smith & Beratan, *Chemical Science* (2026), “A theoretical framework to understand high electron mobilities in cable bacteria.”

https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2026/sc/d5sc04393j

This theoretical analysis argues that vibronically coupled multistep hopping can account for long-range transport and emphasizes that the mechanism remained poorly understood. It is a model paper, not experimental proof of the transport mechanism.

Russell et al., *Journal of Physical Chemistry Letters* (10 September 2026), “Model Structures and Electron Transfer Properties of Conductive Nickel-Organic Nanoribbons in Cable Bacteria.” DOI 10.1021/acs.jpclett.6c01926.

https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42720348/

The DFT study tests plausible nickel-bis(dithiolene) nanoribbon packings and finds structures with electronic coupling compatible with efficient charge transport and, in some cases, delocalization beyond a simple small-polaron picture. Again, this is mechanism modeling constrained by the new structural evidence, not a measurement of charge motion inside a living cable bacterium.

## Strongest counterargument / disconfirming pressure

The book's attractive phrase “living conductor” can make the molecular problem sound more settled than it is. A high conductivity measured on an extracted or electrode-mounted structure does not by itself tell us how charge moves under physiological conditions, how contacts at donor/acceptor cells work, or which transport regime dominates over centimetres in vivo. The 2026 structural paper narrows the possibilities dramatically, but competing transport descriptions are still being worked out.

A second caution is numerical. Conductivity values in this literature depend on preparation, environmental exposure, assumed current-bearing cross-section and whether the denominator is the whole fibre, conductive core, or newly inferred nanoribbons. The highest nanoribbon figures are model-dependent rescalings. The book should never put “15,000 S/cm” beside copper or silicon without explaining that denominator.

The result survives these cautions. The conservative claim is already remarkable: cable bacteria contain a continuous, nickel-rich conductive architecture spanning cell boundaries, and direct electrode experiments show that the isolated biological structure can carry electronic current over macroscopic distances relative to a cell. What remains open is the detailed physics and its physiological implementation.

## Recommended later manuscript move

Do not add another paragraph of generic wonder to Chapter 1. Use the 2019 cut-current experiment and the September 2026 nickel-organic-framework result when Chapters 4/9 reach the conductor itself. A useful narrative sequence is:

1. sediment geochemistry implied a current;
2. electrodes and physical cutting made the conductor experimentally tangible;
3. extraction localized conduction to the periplasmic fibres;
4. nickel perturbation implicated an unfamiliar cofactor;
5. the September 2026 structural work resolved intertwined nickel-organic nanoribbons;
6. the charge-transport physics remains contested enough that theory papers published days later are still testing competing regimes.

That sequence gives the reader an actual discovery process instead of a catalogue of astonishing properties.

## Evidence ladder to preserve

**sediment redox coupling → direct filament conductance → fibre-localized conductance → nickel dependence → nickel-organic nanoribbon structure → microscopic charge-transport mechanism → in-vivo energetic performance.**

Evidence can be strong at one rung without automatically settling the next.

## Source-queue consequence

The existing queue item “Cable-bacterium fiber ultrastructure and direct conductivity papers” is now partly resolved. Remaining work should focus on:

- full methods/supplements for the 2019, 2021 and September 2026 papers;
- preparation-dependent conductivity ranges and environmental sensitivity;
- direct versus inferred in-vivo current magnitudes;
- donor/acceptor-cell electron injection and extraction;
- cartwheel junction function and the Cu/S entity;
- reconciliation of temperature-dependent/hopping, delocalization and nickel-organic-framework transport models;
- independent replication outside the core cable-bacteria research groups where available.

## Publication boundary

Do not write that cable bacteria have “solved” biological long-distance conduction. As of September 2026, the physical conductor is far less mysterious than it was a few years earlier, but the detailed charge-transport mechanism and physiological interfaces remain active research questions.
