# Evidence ledger — Life in Circuit

## Class 1 — verified / documented

### Cable bacteria electrically couple separated redox reactions
- Risgaard-Petersen et al., 2015, *Applied and Environmental Microbiology*, “Cable Bacteria in Freshwater Sediments.” Documents pH signatures, electric fields, Desulfobulbaceae filaments and distant redox coupling in freshwater sediment. https://journals.asm.org/doi/10.1128/AEM.01064-15
- Schauer et al., 2014, *ISME Journal*, “Succession of cable bacteria and electric currents in marine sediment.” Reports centimeter-distance electron conduction associated with filamentous Desulfobulbaceae and population/current succession. https://www.nature.com/articles/ismej2013239

### Electric potential is measurable
- Damgaard et al., 2014, *JGR Biogeosciences*. Electric-potential microelectrodes measured an approximately 2 mV increase across ~20 mm of active marine sediment, consistent with inferred current direction. https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/2014JG002665

### Surface photosynthesis rapidly affects deeper sulfide chemistry
- Malkin & Meysman, 2015, *AEM*. Light-dark changes in a photosynthetic biofilm produced immediate/repeatable sulfide changes at depth, interpreted as rapid transmission through cable-bacterium-mediated electrogenic coupling. https://journals.asm.org/doi/10.1128/AEM.02682-14

### Cable bacteria are motile
- Bjerg et al., 2016, *AEM*. Time-lapse microscopy measured gliding motility, reversals and chemical-gradient-associated behavior. https://journals.asm.org/doi/10.1128/AEM.01038-16

### Ecosystem geochemical consequences
- Seitaj et al., 2015, *PNAS*, “Cable bacteria generate a firewall against euxinia in seasonally hypoxic basins.” Field observations connect cable-bacterium activity with iron-mineral cycling that can delay free-sulfide accumulation. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1510152112

### Electroactive microbial nanostructures
- Reguera et al., 2005, *Nature*, “Extracellular electron transfer via microbial nanowires.” Conducting-probe AFM and mutant experiments supported a role for *Geobacter sulfurreducens* pili in extracellular Fe(III) reduction. DOI 10.1038/nature03661. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15973408/
- Later work complicates the original simple pili story; do not freeze 2005 terminology into current consensus.

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete

### Neighboring bacteria may exploit cable-bacterium electrical access
- Bjerg et al., 2023, *Nature Communications*. Diverse bacteria flocked around anoxic portions of oxygen-connected cable bacteria and dispersed when laser cutting disrupted the connection. Raman measurements indicated redox-state differences with proximity. Authors suggest possible electron transfer through unidentified soluble intermediates. Observation is strong; exact transfer chemistry is unresolved. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-37272-8

### Exact conductive machinery
Cable bacteria possess longitudinal conductive structures, but the molecular mechanism and quantitative division of labor across structures require primary-paper reconstruction before strong prose. For *Geobacter*, pili-versus-cytochrome debates make “nanowire” a mechanism-sensitive term.

## Class 3 — disputed / unconfirmed

- “Microbial nanowires are universally conductive pili.” Too broad; organism and preparation matter, and cytochrome-based structures are central in current literature.
- “Cable bacteria have nervous systems.” Unsupported category error.
- “Electrical flocking proves electrical symbiosis.” The observed association is real; mechanism remains incompletely identified.
- “Cable bacteria communicate information through their current in a neuron-like code.” No evidence located for this claim.

## Class 4 — speculative synthesis / open conjecture

- Spatial electrical connectivity may be an underweighted term in astrobiological habitability models where oxidants and reductants are physically separated.
- Electrogenic ecosystems might produce spatial mineral/redox patterns worth testing as biosignature candidates, but abiotic geobattery processes are an obvious competing explanation.
- Distributed microbial electron transport may be conceptually useful in the history of biological organization before nervous systems, but evolutionary continuity must not be asserted without evidence.

## Contradiction and incentive audit

**Nanowire branding:** The phrase is visually irresistible and can hide mechanistic disputes. Every use must identify organism, structure and evidence.

**Neural analogy:** Trade prose benefits from “wires,” “signals” and “networks,” creating an incentive to imply cognition. Manuscript rule: metabolic current is not information processing unless an experiment establishes encoding, transmission and response beyond ordinary redox coupling.

**Astrobiology expansion:** Terrestrial demonstration of long-distance electron transport does not imply occurrence elsewhere. Use it to widen the hypothesis space, never as evidence for extraterrestrial life.

## Defeat conditions

The book's broader synthesis weakens materially if:
1. cable-bacterium long-distance current proves to be an artifact of sediment chemistry independent of filaments;
2. direct electrical measurements fail under improved controls;
3. the claimed ecosystem-scale effects cannot be separated from ordinary sulfur/iron cycling;
4. purported long-range biological conduction reduces to local redox reactions plus abiotic mineral conduction with no essential biological transport component.

Existing evidence makes (1) and (2) appear unlikely, but the manuscript must reconstruct the decisive perturbation and direct-conductivity experiments before assigning confidence beyond the published claims.
