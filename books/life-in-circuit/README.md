# Life in Circuit

## How bacteria discovered electricity before animals had nerves

**Author:** Sven Hardy Benson

### Governing thesis

Life did not merely evolve inside chemical gradients. Some organisms learned to wire gradients together. Cable bacteria move electrons across centimeter distances through multicellular filaments, allowing sulfide oxidation in anoxic sediment to remain electrically coupled to oxygen reduction near the surface. Other electroactive microbes export electrons to minerals, electrodes, and neighboring cells. These are not metaphors for electricity. They are measurable currents, potentials, conductive structures, and redox reactions.

The book asks a larger but bounded question: what changes in our picture of life when metabolism is allowed to be spatially distributed through electrical circuitry? It does **not** claim that bacterial electrical conduction is cognition, a nervous system, or consciousness. It investigates why those comparisons are tempting, where they fail, and what genuinely new biological organization remains after the analogy is removed.

### Evidence discipline

1. **Verified/documented:** centimeter-scale electron transport associated with cable bacteria; measured sediment electric potentials; spatial coupling of sulfide oxidation and oxygen/nitrate reduction; conductive extracellular structures in electroactive microbes; ecosystem-scale geochemical consequences.
2. **Credible but incomplete:** exact molecular conduction pathway and division of labor within cable-bacterium filaments; ecological electron exchange between cable bacteria and neighboring microbes; the relative roles of pili, cytochromes and matrix conduction in different organisms.
3. **Disputed/unconfirmed:** universal claims that one named structure explains all microbial nanowire conduction; strong analogies between microbial electrical networks and neural information processing.
4. **Speculative synthesis:** electrical access may be an underused dimension of habitability and biosignature theory; spatially distributed metabolism may offer a useful prehistory for thinking about biological networks without implying neurons or minds.

A 65,000+ word trade-science book built from primary microbiology, biogeochemistry, electrochemistry and astrobiology literature. Current state is an opening architecture and evidence spine, not a finished manuscript.
