# The Artificial Sky

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Title | The Artificial Sky |
| Subtitle | HAARP, Artificial Aurora, and the Physics Behind a Modern Myth |
| Author | Sven Hardy Benson |
| Status | Drafting |
| Chapters | 18 |

**Reader promise:** follow the real hardware and experiments far enough that the strange parts remain strange, while the unsupported parts become easier to see.

**Thesis:** HAARP became a nearly perfect conspiracy object because its documented capabilities sound like conspiracy copy when stripped of scale: it can heat a patch of ionosphere, make visible airglow, create temporary plasma structures, turn natural ionospheric currents into an ELF/VLF radiator, and launch waves into the magnetosphere. Those are real results. They are also physically bounded. The neglected story is how a defense-built research machine learned to perturb one thin electrical layer of the upper atmosphere, why those perturbations mattered to communications and surveillance, and how the language of real plasma physics was enlarged into claims about weather, earthquakes, minds, and planetary control that the evidence does not establish.

**Strongest competing explanation:** HAARP is simply an unusually capable scientific transmitter whose mythology is sociological noise. This book accepts most of that explanation, then asks what it leaves out: the genuine defense requirements, the international history of ionospheric heating, the historically rational secrecy reflex around radio systems, and the fact that the machine really can produce effects that sound impossible until their scale is specified.
