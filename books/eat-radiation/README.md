# Eat Radiation

## How Black Fungi Turn a Nuclear Ruin Into an Astrobiology Experiment

**Author:** Sven Hardy Benson

## Thesis

Chernobyl produced a biological fact stranger than the folklore around it: melanized fungi colonized intensely radioactive structures, some isolates grew directionally toward ionizing-radiation sources, and controlled experiments later found radiation-associated changes in melanin chemistry and fungal growth. The evidence does **not** yet justify the popular shorthand that these organisms simply "eat radiation." The interesting book lives inside that gap. It asks whether ionizing radiation can be more than damage—whether, under narrow biological conditions, evolution can turn a hostile energy flux into protection, information, altered redox chemistry, or metabolically useful work—and what that possibility does to our assumptions about habitable environments on Earth and elsewhere.

The book's governing distinction is **survival ≠ attraction ≠ growth stimulation ≠ energy capture ≠ autotrophy**. Each arrow requires its own evidence.

## Reader promise

Follow one black fungal colony from the ruined Unit 4 Shelter through Ukrainian radiobiology laboratories, electron-spin measurements, isotope incorporation experiments and an International Space Station payload. At every stop, separate what the organism demonstrably does from what the word *radiotrophic* tempts us to imagine.

## Evidence classes

1. **Verified / documented:** melanized fungi were abundant in highly contaminated Chernobyl niches; peer-reviewed experiments report directional hyphal growth toward beta/gamma sources in some isolates; melanin is radioprotective; irradiation can alter measured melanin electronic/redox properties; controlled studies report radiation-associated growth effects.
2. **Credible but incomplete:** melanin may participate in transducing some radiation-derived energy into biologically useful redox chemistry; long exposure may select for radiation-responsive phenotypes; melanized biomass may have useful self-renewing shielding properties.
3. **Disputed / not established:** a universal fungal "radiosynthesis" pathway analogous to photosynthesis; ionizing radiation serving as a sufficient carbon/energy source; all melanized Chernobyl fungi being radiotrophic; simple causal transfer from Chernobyl ecology to deep-space performance.
4. **Speculative synthesis:** radiation-rich worlds and subsurface niches may support metabolisms or symbioses that use high-energy radiation in ways terrestrial habitability models underweight; engineered melanized organisms could combine repair, shielding and resource cycling in off-world habitats.

## Strongest competing explanation

Melanin is primarily a superb stress-management pigment. Radiation-resistant fungi persist where competitors fail; ordinary carbon metabolism supplies their energy; irradiation triggers generic stress and growth pathways; and apparent "radiotrophy" is an evocative over-reading of protection plus hormesis. The manuscript has to beat this explanation experimentally, not rhetorically.

## Defeat condition

The strong thesis fails if matched-genotype, matched-nutrient experiments show that radiation-associated growth advantages disappear when generic stress responses and passive shielding are controlled, and if no reproducible radiation-dependent energy/redox flux can be connected to biomass production through melanin.
