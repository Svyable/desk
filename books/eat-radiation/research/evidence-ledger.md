# Eat Radiation — evidence ledger

## Governing rule

Do not permit evidence to jump this ladder without an explicit bridge:

**radiation tolerance → ecological enrichment → directional response → growth stimulation → melanin dependence → usable energy capture → ecologically meaningful metabolism.**

## Class 1 — verified / documented

### Chernobyl Shelter mycobiota
Zhdanova et al. surveyed the damaged Unit 4 Shelter in 1997–98 and reported extensive fungal growth, 37 species/19 genera, with melanin-containing species dominant at heavily contaminated sites.

Primary bibliographic source: https://doi.org/10.1017/S0953756200002756

Claim limit: dominance in a contaminated niche does not establish radiation feeding or even radiation as the sole selective variable.

### Directional growth toward radiation
Zhdanova et al. (2004) experimentally exposed spores/hyphae to directional beta and gamma sources and reported promoted directional growth toward the source.

https://doi.org/10.1017/S0953756204000966
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15506020/

Claim limit: radiotropism is not energy capture. Later comparative work found the response in only a minority of tested species/strains.

### Heterogeneity of radiotropism
Karpenko et al. reported positive radiotropism in only 19% of fungi examined from radioactive substrates; responses occurred in dark and light micromycetes and appeared strain-related.

https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02931449

This is mandatory counterevidence against a universal melanin-seeking-radiation story.

### Irradiation, melanin electronics and growth
Dadachova et al. (2007) reported irradiation-associated changes in melanin ESR/electron-transfer behavior and enhanced growth/metabolic measures in melanized fungi under tested conditions, including roughly threefold greater 14C-acetate incorporation in irradiated melanized cells under the reported comparison. The authors framed the result as raising a question about energy capture and utilization, not as demonstrating a complete radiosynthetic pathway.

https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0000457
PLOS full text: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0000457
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17520016/
PMC: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1866175/

Claim limit: increased NADH-reduction capacity, growth, metabolic-assay signal, or acetate incorporation does not by itself identify where the extra free energy came from. A radiation-triggered regulatory response that changes use of conventional nutrients can produce a growth effect without radiation supplying a quantitatively meaningful share of metabolic work.

### Quantitative growth modeling: the alternative survives
Shuryak, Bryan, Nosanchuk & Dadachova (2014) modeled X-ray-associated proliferation in *Cryptococcus neoformans* across photon energies and dose rates. Crucially for this book, the paper's own framing leaves two causal families open: melanin–photon interactions might generate energy useful for growth **and/or** irradiation might regulate growth-promoting genes. A fitted radiation-growth relationship therefore does not close the energy-causation gap.

https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0085561
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24454887/

Claim limit: a quantitative dose-response model is evidence about the shape of a growth response, not direct calorimetry or proof that captured radiation energy pays the energetic cost of the added biomass.

### Molecular response complicates a pure energy-harvesting story
A 2012 *Wangiella dermatitidis* study confirmed radiation-associated increases in cell growth while reporting broad transcriptional changes: more than 3,000 genes were differentially expressed under chronic low-dose irradiation, with antioxidant and membrane-fluidity genes among those up-regulated and many amino-acid/carbohydrate-metabolism and cell-cycle genes down-regulated. This supplies a concrete stress/regulatory mechanism that a radiosynthesis account must distinguish from energy harvesting rather than merely acknowledge in passing.

PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23139812/

Claim limit: transcriptomic response does not disprove energy capture. It does show that irradiation perturbs ordinary cellular regulation extensively enough that growth enhancement cannot be assigned to photon-derived metabolic energy from growth data alone.

### Melanin radioprotection
Dadachova et al. (2008) found fungal melanin radioprotection depended on chemical composition, stable radicals and spatial arrangement, supporting shielding/free-radical-quenching mechanisms.

https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1755-148X.2007.00430.x

Later work comparing melanized and non-melanized fungi under deuteron and X-ray exposure also found strong radioprotection while metabolic readouts did not move as one simple energy-harvesting story would predict: XTT activity could increase in melanized strains while ATP decreased after deuteron exposure.

PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28127878/

Claim limit: "metabolic activity" is assay-dependent. XTT, ATP, growth, biomass and carbon incorporation are related measurements, not interchangeable proxies for captured usable energy.

### ISS cultivation / attenuation
Averesch, Shunk & Kern (2022) published peer-reviewed work on cultivation of *C. sphaerospermum* aboard the ISS and measured a small radiation-count difference beneath fungal biomass. The payload recorded roughly 147 versus 151 counts per minute beneath experimental and control sides over the run. The authors concluded that the biomass **may** have radioprotective properties and described the orbital growth advantage as potentially associated with increased radiation/radioadaptation.

https://doi.org/10.3389/fmicb.2022.877625
Full text: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/microbiology/articles/10.3389/fmicb.2022.877625/full

Claim limit: this was a single small orbital payload, not a radiation-energy-capture experiment. Growth in orbit does not isolate radiation as causal; attenuation does not establish active energy harvesting; and the detector geometry/count difference cannot be naively extrapolated to practical shielding against the full charged-particle spectrum.

## The energy-causation boundary

This is the book's highest-value unresolved question and should eventually be made quantitative rather than rhetorical.

A convincing claim that radiation supplies useful metabolic energy needs to close an energy balance. At minimum, later research should estimate:

1. incident radiation energy on the biologically active volume;
2. the fraction actually absorbed rather than transmitted or scattered;
3. the fraction of absorbed energy entering a melanin-dependent redox pathway;
4. the chemical free energy made available downstream;
5. the energetic cost of the additional biomass or maintenance benefit attributed to irradiation.

If the maximum physically available radiation energy is too small to account for the observed growth increment, the strong energy-harvesting interpretation fails even if irradiation reproducibly changes growth. If the budget is large enough, that only establishes plausibility; pathway-level evidence is still required.

The strongest conventional alternative is therefore not "nothing happens." Something plainly can happen. The alternative is that radiation acts chiefly as stressor, signal and selective pressure: melanin limits damage; irradiation changes redox state and gene regulation; cells alter division, size, antioxidant activity or nutrient use; ordinary chemical substrates still pay most or all of the energetic bill. That model can coexist with every headline result unless an experiment measures the missing energy bridge.

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete

- Melanin's radiation-induced redox changes may be biologically coupled to metabolic work.
- Chronic radiation exposure may select radiation-responsive phenotypes in some fungi.
- Melanized living biomass may offer useful self-renewing shielding as part of a larger habitat system.
- Radiation-induced chemistry may contribute a small energy subsidy under nutrient limitation.

Each requires quantitative energetic accounting.

## Class 3 — disputed / unconfirmed

- "Chernobyl fungi eat radiation" as a literal nutritional description.
- A universal radiosynthetic pathway equivalent to photosynthesis.
- Radiation as sufficient energy/carbon support for fungal growth without conventional substrates.
- All black fungi in contaminated environments being radiotrophic.
- Radiation-seeking behavior being caused specifically by melanin.
- Practical Mars/GCR shielding thicknesses inferred directly from small orbital payload measurements.

## Class 4 — speculative synthesis / open conjecture

- High-radiation niches elsewhere may support organisms that have evolved true energy-harvesting pathways from ionizing radiation.
- Melanin or melanin-like disordered pigments may be one member of a larger class of biological high-energy transducers.
- Radiation response could be split into sensing, protection and energy capture modules with different evolutionary origins.
- Engineered off-world microbial systems might combine passive shielding, repair, waste metabolism and a modest radiation-derived energetic subsidy.

## Contradiction ledger

1. **Melanin association vs non-melanized response.** Some growth/radiotropic effects are not exclusive to melanized strains.
2. **Protection vs harvesting.** The strongest established melanin function under radiation may be damage mitigation rather than energy acquisition.
3. **Chernobyl enrichment vs environmental confounding.** Reactor niches differ in more than radiation.
4. **Growth vs energetic causation.** Hormesis/stress signaling can alter proliferation without meaningful photon-to-metabolism energy transfer.
5. **Growth assay vs energy assay.** CFUs, dry mass, XTT/MTT, ATP and isotope incorporation answer different questions; none alone closes an energy balance.
6. **ISS growth vs radiation causality.** Microgravity, culture geometry, launch history and other orbital variables complicate attribution.
7. **Photon attenuation vs GCR shielding.** Detector response and low-energy photon attenuation cannot be naively generalized to the full high-energy charged-particle environment.

## Incentive audit

No disinformation program is evidenced here. The distortions are ordinary and worth naming:

- headlines reward "eats radiation" over conditional redox chemistry;
- astrobiology rewards implications beyond the terrestrial experiment;
- space-biotech pitches reward low-upmass/self-growing-shield narratives;
- skeptical summaries can make the opposite error by treating absence of a photosynthesis-like pathway as evidence that radiation-associated biological effects are unreal;
- Chernobyl tourism/pop culture encourages mutation narratives that can contaminate scientific retelling.

## Defeat experiments

A strong radiosynthesis claim should eventually survive all of these:

1. isogenic melanized/non-melanized strains;
2. identical nutrient/carbon budgets;
3. controlled dose rate and photon spectrum;
4. direct accounting of incident, absorbed and chemically captured radiation energy sufficient to explain the biomass or maintenance difference;
5. pathway interruption that abolishes the melanin-dependent benefit;
6. rescue of that pathway independent of generic stress response;
7. orthogonal energetic readouts rather than one metabolic proxy;
8. replication by an independent laboratory;
9. ecological demonstration that the effect changes fitness at realistic field dose rates.

Until then: intriguing energy-capture hypothesis, not settled metabolism.
