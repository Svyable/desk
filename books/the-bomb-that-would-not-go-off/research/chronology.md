# Chronology

- **1960s:** 178m2Hf becomes established as an unusually long-lived high-spin nuclear isomer.
- **1980s:** Strategic Defense Initiative-era research sustains military interest in gamma-ray lasers and nuclear-isomer energy-release concepts.
- **Mid-1990s:** International workshops and nuclear-structure research increasingly focus on induced isomer depopulation; hafnium-178m2 emerges as a particularly attractive candidate.
- **1998:** Collins and collaborators conduct the x-ray irradiation work later published in *Physical Review Letters*.
- **January 25, 1999:** Collins et al. publish “Accelerated Emission of Gamma Rays from the 31-yr Isomer of 178Hf Induced by X-Ray Irradiation,” reporting enhanced selected decay-cascade transitions under dental-machine x rays.
- **1999–2000:** Nuclear physicists publish comments challenging the magnitude and mechanism of the claimed effect.
- **2000–2001:** Argonne, Lawrence Livermore and Los Alamos collaborate on synchrotron tests using a 178m2Hf sample and the Advanced Photon Source.
- **August 2001:** Ahmad et al. publish a null result in *Physical Review Letters*, finding no detectable induced acceleration and setting much lower upper limits.
- **2002–2003:** Additional Advanced Photon Source and related searches probe low-energy x-ray regions without reproducing the large claimed enhancement.
- **2003:** Public records and reporting show DARPA interest in stimulated isomer energy release and larger-scale isomer production; a Hafnium Isomer Production Panel examines production pathways.
- **July 24, 2003:** Becker, Gemmell, Schiffer and Wilhelmy produce *The (178m2)Hf Controversy*, summarizing the experimental conflict and DARPA-era context.
- **March 28, 2004:** Sharon Weinberger’s Washington Post Magazine investigation brings the proposed hafnium weapon and internal defense enthusiasm to a broad audience.
- **2004:** Collins and collaborators continue publishing nuclear-resonance work on the isomer.
- **2006:** Weinberger publishes *Imaginary Weapons*, placing hafnium in a larger critique of fringe or weakly validated defense technologies.
- **June 2007:** Peter Zimmerman publishes “The Strange Tale of the Hafnium Bomb” in *APS News*.
- **2015:** Kirischuk and collaborators publish another positive induced-decay claim using bremsstrahlung, demonstrating that the spectroscopy controversy did not end simply with the early Argonne null.
- **2023:** A separate production study measures 178m2Hf cross sections from alpha irradiation of tantalum, showing continued legitimate nuclear-science interest independent of the old weapon narrative.
