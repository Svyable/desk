# Evidence ledger — Acceptable Dose

This ledger is intentionally stricter than the narrative. A memorable allegation is not promoted because it is memorable.

## Class 1 — verified / documented

- European metal-mining districts had documented extraordinary lung-disease/lung-cancer experience before the postwar U.S. uranium boom; Germany and Czechoslovakia recognized the occupational disease before mid-century. Source trail: Brugge & Goble historical review; later radon/miner reviews.
- The U.S. Public Health Service began a Colorado Plateau uranium-miner study in 1950 because of concern about the European experience and measured mine radon/exposure and health outcomes.
- Federal uranium procurement drove rapid postwar Colorado Plateau mining.
- Navajo men were heavily represented in uranium mining on and near Navajo lands.
- Published Navajo epidemiology found a strong association between uranium-mining history and lung cancer. Samet et al. (NEJM, 1984) reported 23 of 32 Navajo lung-cancer cases had uranium-mining employment versus none of 64 controls with nonrespiratory cancers. Gilliland et al. (2000) reported 63 of 94 incident lung cancers in Navajo men from 1969–1993 occurred in former uranium miners and estimated RR 28.6 (95% CI 13.2–61.7) for mining history.
- RECA was enacted in 1990 as partial restitution for qualifying radiation-exposed populations including underground uranium miners.
- Uranium mining/milling left abandoned-mine and environmental contamination problems on Navajo Nation lands; federal remediation and monitoring programs exist.

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete / requires primary-document indexing

- Historical accounts report PHS investigators withheld or limited hazard warnings to preserve access/cooperation with mining companies while the cohort matured. This is well described in secondary historical scholarship, but the book should not settle actor-specific intent until correspondence, memoranda and study protocols are page-indexed.
- Warning pamphlets were reportedly distributed by 1959, but actual reach, language accessibility, comprehension and mine-by-mine implementation need reconstruction.
- Ventilation improvements were technically available before epidemiological certainty; the timing, cost, enforcement authority and operator response varied and need mine/regulator-specific documentation.
- Navajo miners faced distinctive language and economic constraints. These are historically credible but should be developed with Navajo oral histories, tribal records and community scholarship rather than inferred from federal sources.

## Class 3 — disputed / insufficiently corroborated formulations

Do not state without much stronger evidence:

- “The government knowingly experimented on Navajo miners by deliberately exposing them to radon.” Observational study of an existing occupational exposure is not equivalent to assigned exposure.
- “All miners were intentionally kept ignorant.” Warning practices changed over time and must be reconstructed.
- “Uranium caused every cancer or illness reported by miners.” Radon progeny have strong lung-cancer evidence; other exposures and outcomes require separate causal treatment.
- “AEC officials possessed a single definitive lung-cancer answer in 1948.” Knowledge was distributed and evolved.

## Class 4 — synthesis / open conjecture

- **Research-inside-the-hazard thesis:** observational epidemiology can create an ethical conflict when researchers require continued access to a workforce that is still accumulating the suspected exposure. This is interpretation, not a claim that the PHS assigned exposure.
- **Uncertainty as institutional resource:** uncertainty can function asymmetrically—scientifically as a reason to gather data, operationally as a reason not to impose costly controls. Test this against actual memoranda rather than assuming motive.
- **Archive as second exposure:** long latency shifts the burden from physiology to records; missing employment/dose records can become a second mechanism of harm through compensation and causal-proof burdens. This requires claims-history evidence.

## Contradictions to preserve

1. Early hazard suspicion vs later causal certainty.
2. Federal demand for uranium vs private/state responsibility for mine operation.
3. Scientific value of the PHS cohort vs ethical criticism of disclosure.
4. Strong population-level radon/lung-cancer evidence vs difficulty assigning individual causation.
5. Navajo uranium employment as paid opportunity vs exposure produced within constrained economic choices.
6. Compensation as acknowledgment vs compensation criteria as proof filter.
