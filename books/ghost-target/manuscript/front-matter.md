# GHOST TARGET

## When the sensor is telling the truth about something that is not there

**Sven Hardy Benson**

A radar operator can truthfully report a target that no aircraft produced.

That sentence is the book.

It is not an argument that strange military observations are fake. It is an argument that the word *target* has quietly been allowed to mean *object*. Modern sensors do not hand us objects. They hand us measurements processed through hardware, software, geometry, assumptions, displays and human interpretation. Sometimes the thing being measured is an aircraft. Sometimes it is weather. Sometimes it is clutter. Sometimes it is another sensor. And sometimes somebody built the signal because they wanted the sensor to believe.

Cold War intelligence did this deliberately.

The important historical case is Project PALLADIUM, described publicly by former CIA technical officer Gene Poteat as a radar-deception effort in which false targets were injected into adversary radar in order to measure radar sensitivity and reaction. The extraordinary part is not that a government could make a false dot. The extraordinary part is that deception became measurement: create the target, listen to the adversary react, infer what his system can see.

That is an intelligence instrument made out of somebody else's perception.

This book follows the consequences into the modern sensor-fusion world and into UAP. It does not claim PALLADIUM explains the Nimitz encounters, Gimbal, GoFast, or any other named case. It does not claim a secret contemporary descendant exists unless public evidence supports one. It does not turn the generic existence of electronic warfare into a case-specific explanation.

It asks a harder question: what evidence is required to move from **measurement** to **track**, from track to **object**, and from object to **capability**?

## Evidence contract

Every controversial proposition is carried in one of four classes.

1. **Verified / documented.** Primary government records, official histories, contemporaneous technical documents, declassified material, or strong peer-reviewed measurement.
2. **Credible but incomplete.** Attributed technical testimony, serious reporting, memoir evidence, or reconstruction that has meaningful provenance but lacks complete independent confirmation.
3. **Disputed / unconfirmed.** Case claims, alleged programs, capabilities or interpretations that do not currently have enough public evidence to be promoted.
4. **Speculative synthesis.** My models and conjectures. These must be allowed to lose.

PALLADIUM itself requires care. The strongest detailed public narrative checked at project start is associated with Poteat's retrospective account. CIA's official OXCART material independently documents the surrounding reconnaissance, stealth and electronic-countermeasure problem. Those are not identical evidentiary objects. The manuscript will not silently convert a participant's retrospective description into a contemporaneous program file.

## Governing prohibition

**A real measurement does not guarantee a real object. A real object does not guarantee the inferred capability.**

The book's job is to keep those layers from impersonating one another.
