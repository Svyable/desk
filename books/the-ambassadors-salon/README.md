Pamela Harriman’s path to the American embassy in Paris ran through a world far from the career Foreign Service: British wartime politics, transatlantic society, American citizenship, Democratic Party organizing, fundraising, personal networks, and political access.

The book takes informal power seriously without romanticizing it. Its question is when social intelligence, hospitality, access, memory, money, and political relationships become instruments of public service—and when they remain merely influence.
