# The Ice Was the Vault — research ledger

## Research posture

This book is declassified/documentary-heavy on Camp Century's existence, engineering and Iceworm planning; peer-reviewed-heavy on the site's environmental afterlife; incomplete on office-by-office Danish knowledge and several engineering denominators. It must not use the existence of Iceworm as a rhetorical warrant for unrelated secret-program allegations.

## Four evidence classes

### 1 — Verified / declassified / documented

- Camp Century was constructed by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers in 1959 in northwest Greenland.
- It used cut-and-cover trench construction in near-surface firn and housed a substantial year-round installation.
- PM-2A provided portable nuclear power at the site; the reactor reaction chamber was later removed.
- The Army studied Project Iceworm, a proposed distributed ballistic-missile network beneath the Greenland Ice Sheet. Peer-reviewed synthesis describes the 1962 proposal as a subsurface railway across about 1.3 × 10^5 km² supporting 600 missiles; the project was rejected in 1963.
- Year-round Camp Century operations ceased in 1964; seasonal activity continued until abandonment in 1967.
- Camp Century's tunnel network deformed under ice/firn motion, undermining assumptions needed for large stable subsurface infrastructure.
- A 2016 GRL study reconstructed substantial abandoned physical, chemical, biological and radiological wastes and modeled future remobilization under changing surface mass balance.
- Camp Century ice-core and subglacial materials became important scientific archives independent of the military program.

### 2 — Credible but incomplete

- The degree to which particular Camp Century experiments were designed specifically for Iceworm rather than general cold-regions research.
- Exact knowledge distribution within Danish and U.S. institutions concerning Iceworm and nuclear deployment policy.
- Exact cost and maintenance denominators that made the scaled Iceworm network unattractive relative to competing basing systems.
- Updated site-specific timing for possible waste remobilization under post-RCP8.5 climate scenarios.

### 3 — Disputed / not independently established

- Claims that an operational nuclear missile force was secretly installed beneath Greenland.
- Claims that all Danish authorities knowingly approved Iceworm in its full proposed form.
- Claims that every public scientific purpose of Camp Century was merely fabricated cover.
- Claims assigning precise motives to officials without contemporaneous documentary support.

### 4 — Speculative synthesis / open conjecture

- Climate change can act as a 'physical declassifier' by making material residues of former secret infrastructure newly observable or politically active.
- Large clandestine physical programs should generally produce an industrial/institutional topology even when individual activities are compartmented; Camp Century/Iceworm is one historical comparator, not a universal law.
- Environmental media can function as temporary security infrastructure whose strategic value depends on assumptions about future physical stability.

## Contradictions and incentive audit

**Public research vs secret weapons relevance.** Do not force a binary. Cold-regions science and engineering were genuine; the resulting knowledge could also support strategic concepts. Dual-use overlap is itself the evidentiary problem.

**'Denmark knew' vs 'Denmark was deceived.'** Both formulations are too coarse without office/date/document resolution. Separate knowledge of Camp Century, U.S. nuclear activity in Greenland, and the specific Iceworm deployment concept.

**Spectacular reactor vs decisive deformation.** Public memory overweights PM-2A because a reactor under ice is vivid. The strategically decisive engineering issue for Iceworm was whether a vast transport/launch network could remain usable in deforming ice.

**2016 climate projection vs present climate state.** Do not freeze an RCP8.5-era projection into a current forecast. Preserve the study's conditional result and update with modern observations/models before publication.

**Environmental consequence vs moral allegory.** The wastes are measurable materials, not permission to turn every paragraph into a sermon about hubris.

## Chronology

- **1951:** U.S.–Denmark Defense of Greenland Agreement establishes the broader defense framework.
- **1959:** Camp Century construction begins.
- **1960–1963:** PM-2A era; radiological coolant waste later inventoried in scientific reconstruction.
- **1962:** Army proposes Project Iceworm to the Joint Chiefs; experimental subsurface railway work is part of the relevant engineering context.
- **1963:** Iceworm rejected.
- **1964:** Year-round Camp Century operations end.
- **1967:** Camp Century abandoned with minimal decommissioning.
- **1969:** Last reported USACE visit cited by later scientific literature.
- **2016:** Colgan et al. publish quantitative assessment of abandoned wastes and climate-remobilization pathway.

## Source spine

1. William Colgan et al., **“The abandoned ice sheet base at Camp Century, Greenland, in a warming climate,” Geophysical Research Letters 43 (2016), 8091–8096.** Open-access full text: https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/2016GL069688
   - Strong secondary/technical synthesis grounded in USACE records and modern climate modeling.
   - Documents Camp Century construction context, Iceworm scale as reconstructed from historical literature, abandonment, waste estimates, and conditional remobilization pathways.
   - Preserve its model assumptions; do not present its 2016 projections as current deterministic dates.

2. E. D. Weiss, **“Cold War under the ice: The Army's bid for a long-range nuclear role, 1959–1963,” Journal of Cold War Studies (2011).**
   - Priority archival history for Iceworm authorization, Army institutional motives, force concept and rejection.
   - Publication pass must page-index the article and trace its strongest claims back to cited primary documents where available.

3. G. Abele, **Production Analysis of Cut-and-Cover Trench Construction**, U.S. Army Cold Regions Research and Engineering Laboratory Technical Report 126 (1964).
   - Priority primary engineering source for construction method and productivity.

4. E. F. Clark and related USACE Camp Century reports (1960s).
   - Priority primary corpus for layout, operation, deformation, logistics, reactor/waste practices and closure assumptions.
   - Locate stable government/archive scans and page-index before final factual expansion.

5. 1951 **Defense of Greenland Agreement**.
   - Primary legal source. Needed for property/disposal/consultation language and to prevent loose claims about sovereignty or permission.

6. Modern Camp Century ice-core/subglacial-sediment literature.
   - Build a separate peer-reviewed ledger before Chapters 13–14 expansion; do not rely on press summaries for age/environment claims.

## Book-specific falsification tests

- If archival records show Iceworm never progressed beyond an unserious brainstorming document, rewrite the strategic half of the book.
- If engineering reports show deformation was manageable at projected network scale and was not a meaningful reason for rejection, remove the 'ice defeated the architecture' through-line.
- If modern site studies eliminate plausible future contaminant mobilization, Chapters 15–16 become a history of a superseded concern rather than an active environmental afterlife.
- If Danish archives demonstrate clear formal authorization of the full missile concept, remove ambiguity framing and state the documentary result.
