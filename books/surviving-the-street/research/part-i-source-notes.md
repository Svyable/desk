# Part I Source Notes — Survival

These notes support Chapters 2–4 of *Surviving The Street*. They record what each source is allowed to establish so later revisions do not inflate a vivid case into a universal claim.

## Chapter 2 — The Market Does Not Care How Smart You Are

### President's Working Group on Financial Markets, *Hedge Funds, Leverage, and the Lessons of Long-Term Capital Management* (1999)

https://www.cftc.gov/sites/default/files/tm/tmhedgefundreport.htm

Use for:
- LTCM's founding and the reputation of its principals.
- Approximate net returns reported for 1995–1997.
- The return of roughly $2.7 billion of capital to investors at the end of 1997, reducing the capital base to about $4.8 billion.
- More than 60,000 trades on the books.
- More than $125 billion in balance-sheet assets as of August 31, 1998.
- Balance-sheet leverage above 25-to-1 using the January 1998 capital figure.
- Large gross futures, swaps, options, securities, and other derivatives positions.
- The policy concern that leverage, large positions, and common market exposures could worsen forced liquidation.

Do not use the gross notional values as if every dollar represented identical net economic risk. The chapter explicitly distinguishes gross/notional exposure from net risk.

### Alan Greenspan, Federal Reserve testimony on the private-sector refinancing of LTCM (October 1, 1998)

https://www.federalreserve.gov/boarddocs/testimony/1998/19981001.htm

Use for:
- The Federal Reserve's explanation of why an uncontrolled LTCM liquidation was viewed as a potential threat to already unsettled financial markets.
- The distinction between the New York Fed facilitating discussions and private firms supplying the recapitalization capital.

Do not describe the transaction as a direct taxpayer bailout of LTCM.

### U.S. Government Accountability Office, *Long-Term Capital Management: Regulators Need to Focus Greater Attention on Systemic Risk* (GGD-00-3, October 29, 1999)

https://www.gao.gov/products/ggd-00-3

Use for:
- GAO's later finding that LTCM lost more than 90 percent of its capital in 1998.
- The regulatory concern around leverage, counterparties, and systemic risk.

The book uses LTCM as a financing-and-liquidity case, not evidence that quantitative or convergence investing is inherently defective.

## Chapter 3 — Drawdown Changes the Trader

### Terrance Odean, "Are Investors Reluctant to Realize Their Losses?", *The Journal of Finance* 53(5), 1998

https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/0022-1082.00072

Alternative author-hosted version:
https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers%20current%20versions/areinvestorsreluctant.pdf

Use for:
- The analysis of trading records from 10,000 accounts at a large discount brokerage house.
- Evidence that the sampled investors realized gains more readily than losses.
- Odean's tests against several alternative explanations, including rebalancing and trading costs.
- The finding that subsequent performance did not justify holding the losing positions in the sample.

Do not convert the result into a claim that every losing position should be sold or that every investor displays the disposition effect in every context.

### Narayanan Kandasamy et al., "Cortisol shifts financial risk preferences," *PNAS* 111(9), 2014

https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3948282/

Use for:
- A controlled experiment with 36 healthy volunteers in which sustained hydrocortisone treatment shifted measured financial risk preferences toward greater risk aversion.
- The authors' challenge to models that treat risk preference as a stable trait.

Limitations to preserve:
- Small experimental sample.
- Healthy volunteers, not a representative population of investors.
- The result does not establish a simple hormonal explanation for market crashes or individual portfolio errors.

### John M. Coates and Joe Herbert, "Endogenous steroids and financial risk taking on a London trading floor," *PNAS* 105(16), 2008

https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2329689/

Use for:
- Observational evidence from male London traders that cortisol varied with market volatility and variance of trading results.
- Motivation for later experimental work on whether physiological state can shift risk preference.

Do not use the study to imply that cortisol alone determines financial behavior.

## Chapter 4 — The Cost of Being Early

### Michael Burry, Vanderbilt University talk transcript, "Missteps to Mayhem" (April 5, 2011; transcript posted April 13, 2011)

https://news.vanderbilt.edu/2011/04/13/michael-burry-transcript/

This is a first-person retrospective account and should be identified as such.

Use for:
- Burry's description of studying mortgage securitization filings.
- His account that Scion agreed to its first subprime short trades with Deutsche Bank in May 2005 and executed the first trades in early June.
- His explanation of seeking standardized credit-default-swap contracts and using multiple dealer counterparties.
- His description of investor skepticism around the proposed dedicated vehicle.
- His account of 2006 disputes over dealer marks, angry investors seeking their money back, staffing cuts, legal pressure, consideration of liquidation, sales of other credit-short positions, and retention of mortgage positions he could continue to carry.

Do not treat every retrospective interpretation in the speech as independently verified causal history. Use the speech to establish what Burry said he saw, did, and experienced, and pair consequential systemic claims with commission or regulatory evidence where needed.

### Financial Crisis Inquiry Commission interview archive: Michael Burry

https://fcic.law.stanford.edu/interviews/view/14

Use to establish that the FCIC interviewed Burry as part of its crisis investigation and preserved that interview in the public archive.

### CFTC Swaps Report Data Dictionary — credit default swaps

https://www.cftc.gov/MarketReports/SwapsReports/DataDictionary/index.htm

Use for the basic mechanics of a credit default swap:
- protection seller provides a payment after a defined credit event;
- protection buyer makes periodic payments, described as the fee leg;
- maximum protection is tied to the swap's notional amount.

The chapter uses these mechanics to explain why a correct credit thesis can carry an explicit waiting cost.

## Editorial boundary for Part I

Part I should not imply that survival alone creates excess return. Risk reduction can also reduce return, and some apparently smooth strategies hide rare losses. The section's narrower claim is that any analytical, informational, or behavioral edge is unusable if its financing, sizing, liquidity, or human requirements cause the investor to abandon or liquidate it before the edge can express itself.
