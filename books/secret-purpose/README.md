# SECRET PURPOSE

## What ordinary wreckage can hide

**Author:** Sven Hardy Benson

A book about Project MOGUL, Roswell, and a neglected distinction in secrecy: governments often do not need to hide an object. They need to hide what the object is *for*.

## Reader promise

This is not another Roswell verdict book. It uses the collision between a documented classified nuclear-detection program and the later mythology surrounding debris near Roswell to ask a more general question: how does secrecy alter the meaning of otherwise ordinary evidence?

The manuscript will distinguish the existence and purpose of Project MOGUL, the documented NYU balloon work, the Air Force's later reconstruction of the Roswell debris, later witness claims, and extraterrestrial/crash-retrieval hypotheses. It will not promote a retrospective identification into contemporaneous certainty.

## Chapters

1. **The Wrong Secret** — The debris can be mundane while the reason for lying about it is not.
2. **Hear the Bomb** — Maurice Ewing, atmospheric sound channels, and the problem of detecting a Soviet test without entering Soviet territory.
3. **A Secret Inside a University Project** — How classified purpose can inhabit unclassified hardware and research.
4. **Six Hundred Feet of Ordinary Things** — Balloons, radar targets, telemetry, balsa, foil, neoprene, and why physical strangeness is observer-relative.
5. **The Ranch** — What can actually be reconstructed about the debris field and its discovery.
6. **Flying Disc** — The Army Air Field press release, its retraction, and the information environment of July 1947.
7. **What Marcel Saw** — Witness memory, elapsed decades, status, contradiction, and retrospective interpretation.
8. **Flight Four** — The difference between a plausible reconstruction and a proved provenance chain.
9. **Compartment** — What participants knew, what they did not, and why clearance is not universal knowledge.
10. **Cover Story** — When a false explanation protects a true but different secret.
11. **The First Soviet Bomb** — How nuclear-detection systems evolved and why MOGUL's original method lost the competition.
12. **The Secret Expires; the Story Doesn't** — Classification half-life versus narrative half-life.
13. **Bodies** — The later testimony problem and the Air Force's anthropomorphic-dummy explanation, treated separately from the 1947 debris claim.
14. **Evidence Contamination** — How books, documentaries, investigators and prior narratives can change later recollection.
15. **The Cover-Up Premium** — Why proof of concealment can irrationally raise confidence in the claim concealment supposedly denies.
16. **Purpose Is a Hidden Variable** — Cases beyond Roswell where public objects concealed classified missions.
17. **How to Test a Retrieval Claim** — Provenance, materials, logistics, documentary residue, independent witnesses and defeat conditions.
18. **The Wrong Secret** — What Roswell teaches if neither credulity nor ridicule is allowed to do the thinking.
