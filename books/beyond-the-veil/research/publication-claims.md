# Publication claim guardrails

Use these distinctions in jacket copy, catalog descriptions, interviews, and release notes.

## Supported by the manuscript

- Earth contains many systems that retain state, use feedback, adapt to perturbations, coordinate across components, and change later responses without requiring human-style sapience.
- Competence, consciousness, and sapience should not be treated as synonyms.
- Astrobiology already uses contextual and increasingly chemistry-agnostic approaches to life detection because unfamiliar life may not advertise itself through one Earth-specific molecule.
- A search for adaptive organization can be posed as a testable extension of that methodological caution.

## Hypothesis, not discovery

- Non-sapient intellect may be common on inhabited worlds even when technological civilization is rare.
- Some history-dependent adaptive signatures might eventually be distinguishable from ordinary biosignatures or abiotic dynamics.
- A useful observational category could exist between familiar life-detection and civilization-level technosignatures.

## Do not claim

- that extraterrestrial non-sapient intellect has been detected;
- that a planet, biosphere, slime mold, bacterium, immune system, or machine is conscious unless independent evidence supports that statement;
- that all self-organizing or history-dependent physical systems qualify as intellect;
- that the proposed category is already an accepted scientific taxonomy;
- that a null technosignature search is evidence that a world lacks adaptive life.
