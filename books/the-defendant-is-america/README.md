*26 Trials That Explain 250 Years of the United States*

One trial for every decade of the American experiment, from the Boston Massacre soldiers in 1770 to the first criminal trial of a former American president in 2024. The premise is deliberately restrictive: not the twenty-six most famous trials, and not a survey of Supreme Court doctrine, but the single courtroom test in each decade that most clearly exposed what the country was arguing about when the future was still undecided. The cases move through revolution, slavery, treason, Native sovereignty, Reconstruction, labor, segregation, lynching, immigration and war, civil rights, executive power, insanity, celebrity, terrorism, self-defense, and presidential accountability. The verdicts matter. Their afterlives often matter more.

This is a Desk working edition. The 2020s chapter is explicitly time-stamped because its appellate afterlife is still developing.
