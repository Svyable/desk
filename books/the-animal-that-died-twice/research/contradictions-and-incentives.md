# Contradictions and Incentives

## Live contradictions

### 1940 versus decades later
Carlson et al. obtain a most likely extinction near 1940; Brook et al. 2023 obtain a much later plausible tail. This is not resolved by choosing the more exciting paper. Reconstruct classification rules, likelihood functions, spatial assumptions and search-effort treatment.

### Good sightings versus no physical evidence
Tasmania records many reports, some historically judged high quality, while no conclusive specimen/photo has emerged. Both facts must remain present.

### Remote habitat versus modern sensor saturation
Western/southwestern Tasmania can be difficult terrain. At the same time, roads, phones, camera traps, ecological surveys and citizen-science systems increasingly sample wildlife. Survival claims must quantify both.

### Small enough to hide versus large enough to reproduce
Tiny populations are difficult to detect but vulnerable to demographic extinction. Late survival requires hidden generations, not merely one long-lived animal.

## Incentives

- **Government:** avoid wasting conservation resources; avoid appearing to ignore a surviving protected species.
- **Researchers:** novelty rewards both surprising survival and definitive closure.
- **Media:** ambiguous evidence produces repeatable stories; null surveys rarely do.
- **Search groups/tourism:** continued possibility can attract funding and attention.
- **Skeptics:** social reward for puncturing charismatic claims can encourage premature dismissal.
- **De-extinction ventures:** cultural salience of the thylacine increases the value of narratives around recoverability, genomics and return.

## Defeat conditions

The book's current synthesis must change materially if:

1. authenticated modern physical evidence is produced under secure chain of custody;
2. a comprehensive calibrated survey demonstrates near-certain detection of a viable population across plausible habitat;
3. re-analysis shows later high-quality records were systematically misclassified or dependent;
4. demographic modeling proves late persistence incompatible with any plausible hidden breeding population;
5. a new archival specimen shifts the hard-evidence boundary later than currently known.
