- IUCN Red List category definition for **Extinct (EX)** — a taxon is Extinct when there is no reasonable doubt the last individual has died; presumed extinction requires exhaustive surveys in known/expected habitat at appropriate times across the historic range over a timeframe appropriate to life cycle/life form: https://nrl.iucnredlist.org/ — use as the general decision standard, not as a thylacine-specific detection model.
- IUCN guidance on extinct categories / Possibly Extinct logic — emphasizes the conservation cost of declaring extinction too early (the “Romeo Error”) and the need to assess timing of confirmed records, subsequent reports, threat strength and survey adequacy. The precise current guidance/version should be cited in final publication metadata rather than relying on a historical PDF alone.

## Peer-reviewed core

- Carlson, Bond & Burgio, **Estimating the extinction date of the thylacine with mixed certainty data**, *Conservation Biology* 32, 477–483: https://doi.org/10.1111/cobi.13037 — combines physical evidence, expert-validated sightings and unconfirmed sightings; its Bayesian approach models valid and invalid sightings as separate processes. Reported most-likely extinction near 1940, with alternative models clustering in the late-1930s/early-1940s. Model output, not an observed death date.
- Brook, Sleightholme, Campbell & Buettel, **Deficiencies in estimating the extinction date of the thylacine with mixed certainty data**, *Conservation Biology* 32, 1195–1197: https://doi.org/10.1111/cobi.13186 — critique of Carlson et al.; preserve as methodological disagreement rather than treating either side as a final authority.
- Brook, Sleightholme, Campbell, Jarić & Buettel, **Resolving when (and where) the Thylacine went extinct**, *Science of the Total Environment* 877, 162878: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.scitotenv.2023.162878 — compiles 1,237 Tasmanian observational records from 1910 onward, scores uncertainty and models spatial/temporal decline. High-quality records alone imply a likely terminal window spanning roughly the 1940s–1970s; full uncertainty modelling permits a later tail into the late 1980s/early 2000s with small residual probability in remote southwest Tasmania. The late tail is inference from the sighting model, not physical confirmation.
- White, Mitchell & Austin, **Ancient mitochondrial genomes reveal the demographic history and phylogeography of the extinct, enigmatic thylacine**, *Journal of Biogeography* 45, 1–13: https://doi.org/10.1111/jbi.13101 — demographic/genomic context; important when asking what population sizes could plausibly persist, but not a direct post-1936 survival test.
- Thompson, Koshkina, Burgman, Butchart & Stone (2017), **Inferring extinctions II: A practical, iterative model based on records and surveys**, *Biological Conservation* 214, 328–335: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biocon.2017.07.029 — general extinction-inference framework integrating both positive records and unsuccessful surveys, including uncertainty in detection/identification. Important corrective to sighting-only chronologies: survey timing, extent, accessibility, method, observer skill and detectability are evidence, not background prose.

## Detection-model rules for this manuscript

- Do not equate **time since last record** with survey strength. A decade with little effective search can be weaker evidence than one season of high-detectability surveillance.
- Keep survival states separate: `at least one residual individual`, `non-reproducing remnant`, `minimally viable/reproducing population`, and `larger persistent population` imply different expected evidence production and therefore different detection probabilities.
- Simple multiplication of independent non-detections is allowed only when independence is defensible. Cameras sharing the same road-placement bias, seasons, habitat prior or equipment failure mode are correlated tests.
- For each sensor class, distinguish true-positive probability from false-positive probability. Human testimony, camera images, tracks and eDNA have different confusion matrices.
- Report effective effort rather than nominal effort: usable camera-days, downtime, habitat coverage, assay inhibition/recovery, environmental DNA persistence, trigger geometry and other operational denominators.
- Do not use illustrative abundance/confidence claims such as “20 animals excluded at 99%” unless the detection model that produces the figure is actually specified and calibrated.

## Working cautions

- No post-1936 sighting is treated as confirmed survival.
- The 2023 late tail is a model result, not physical evidence of thylacines in the 1980s.
- The 2017/2018 early date is likewise model-dependent and not a witnessed extinction event.
- Secondary accounts of the Naarding sighting should be replaced with original departmental report material during the publication pass.
- Popular claims about the last zoo animal's name/sex require museum-record provenance; the familiar 'Benjamin' story should not be repeated as settled fact.
