# Evidence Ledger

## Class 1 — verified / documented

- Thylacines existed in Tasmania into the twentieth century; extensive museum specimens, photographs and film survive.
- Government bounty schemes paid for thousands of killed animals in the late nineteenth/early twentieth centuries.
- A wild thylacine was documented shot at Mawbanna in 1930.
- The final known captive animal died in Hobart on 7 September 1936.
- No conclusive post-1936 physical evidence of survival has been accepted by Tasmanian authorities.
- Multiple organized searches occurred after 1936 and failed to obtain conclusive evidence.
- Carlson et al. published an early extinction estimate centered around 1940 under mixed-certainty models.
- Brook et al. published both a critique and a later 1,237-record analysis producing a longer plausible extinction tail.
- Historical/genomic work indicates low genetic diversity and longer-term demographic structure before final European-era collapse.

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete

- Some post-1936 eyewitness reports were made by experienced observers and deserve higher likelihood than casual reports.
- Hans Naarding's 1982 report is unusually strong testimony but lacks independent physical confirmation.
- A small remnant population may have survived beyond the final captive animal; the exact duration is unresolved.
- Southwest/western Tasmania is a plausible terminal refugium under some spatial reconstructions.

## Class 3 — disputed / unconfirmed

- Survival into the 1980s as a historical fact.
- Any surviving population in 2026.
- Modern mainland Australian thylacine survival.
- Unproven photographs, videos, tracks and scat attributed to thylacines.
- The popular identity details historically attached to the last captive animal when unsupported by primary records.

## Class 4 — speculative synthesis / open conjecture

- A joint demographic-detection model may narrow extinction timing more effectively than sighting-only estimators.
- Mainland modern sightings can serve as a partial empirical false-positive control for witness reports.
- Modern camera/eDNA coverage can be converted into a cumulative detection-opportunity denominator.
- Conservation search policy should optimize expected information and collateral biodiversity value rather than seek a binary dramatic rediscovery.
