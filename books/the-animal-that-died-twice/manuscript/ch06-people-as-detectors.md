# 6. People as Detectors

A human witness is a detector with excellent pattern recognition, terrible calibration records, moving thresholds, memory, pride and a face.

This is why arguments about sightings become personal too quickly. Calling a report unreliable sounds like calling the witness a liar. But a false positive does not require deceit. Tasmania contains dogs, fox-like silhouettes, quolls, shadows, wet roads, poor depth cues, and an animal people intensely want either to find or to mock.

The useful question is not whether witnesses are trustworthy in the moral sense. It is what their error distribution looks like.

Tasmania's own summary notes hundreds of post-1936 reports and a historical review in which a substantial fraction were considered 'good' sightings, while emphasizing that none became conclusive evidence. The department adds an especially sharp control: comparably persuasive reports have arisen on mainland Australia, where the species disappeared thousands of years earlier. That does not prove every Tasmanian witness wrong. It demonstrates that convincing false positives exist.

This is gold for inference. A region with near-zero prior probability of modern thylacines can estimate how often observers produce thylacine-like reports in the absence of animals. Mainland sightings are a natural false-positive control.

Yet controls must match. Tasmania's cultural saturation, habitat, road density and observer populations differ from Victoria or Queensland. A tourist's roadside glimpse should not receive the same weight as a wildlife officer's prolonged close observation. Nor should professional status make a report infallible.

The disciplined move is graded reliability. Evidence classes can be assigned before fitting extinction dates: physical specimen, photograph with provenance, expert observation, multiple independent witnesses, ordinary report, hearsay. Then analysts can test how conclusions change when those weights change.

The danger appears when classification is tuned after seeing the desired extinction date. If survivalists upgrade late reports because they are late, or skeptics downgrade them for the same reason, the analysis contains its conclusion in the labels.

Witness evidence is not embarrassing science. Astronomy relied on eyes. Natural history was built from collectors and observers. The embarrassment begins when we pretend that testimony is either equivalent to a carcass or equivalent to nothing.

A person is a noisy biological instrument. We know how to work with noisy instruments. We characterize them.
