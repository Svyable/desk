# 9. The Long Tail

In 2023, Barry Brook, Stephen Sleightholme, Cameron Campbell, Ivan Jarić and Jessie Buettel returned to the problem with 1,237 specimen and observation records from 1910 to 2019. Their *Science of the Total Environment* analysis modeled quality and spatial contraction and produced a materially longer extinction tail.

The University of Tasmania summarized the result carefully: extinction likely occurred within decades after the last capture, broadly from the 1940s to 1970s, while sensitivity analyses allowed later dates into the 1980s or even early 2000s with declining probability. A tiny probability of persistence remained in remote southwest Tasmania.

A headline can turn that into THYLACINE MAY STILL EXIST. The paper is more interesting than the headline.

Its significance is that adding geography and probabilistic reliability changes the shape of absence. A species under persecution should not disappear everywhere at once. A retreat into lower-access habitat is biologically plausible. If later high-quality reports cluster where a contracting population would be expected, they are not interchangeable with geographically random sightings.

But spatial coherence is also vulnerable to circularity. Searchers go where habitat looks good and where previous reports occurred. Future reports then cluster in those same places. Geography can contain animal signal, observer effort, or both.

The 2023 result therefore does not prove a hidden twentieth-century population. It proves that plausible modeling choices can keep meaningful posterior mass much later than 1940.

That is the collision this book cares about. Serious analysts can take a famous record set, reject present-day survival as unlikely, and still disagree by decades about the species' terminal history.

Instead of choosing the later date because it is thrilling or the earlier date because it is sober, we should ask what new data would make the models converge. Search-effort reconstruction. Witness calibration. Demographic constraints. Independent records. Geographic null models. Camera coverage.

When two models disagree, the useful output is not only two answers. It is a map of which assumptions buy the disagreement.
