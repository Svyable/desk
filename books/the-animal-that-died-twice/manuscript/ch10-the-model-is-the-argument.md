# 10. The Model Is the Argument

Extinction-date equations can look objective because they return years with decimal probabilities attached. The hardest judgment often happened before the calculation.

Which sightings are in the dataset? Which are physical? Which witnesses count as experts? Does a failed search reduce survival probability by one percent or fifty? Is detection constant through time? Does geography matter? Is a 1982 report independent of the publicity surrounding earlier searches? What minimum population can reproduce for another twenty years?

These are not nuisances around the model. They are the model.

A good analysis therefore needs an assumption ledger beside the result. Every major inference should be rerun across defensible alternative choices. The output should be a family of survival curves, not one authoritative extinction year detached from its priors.

The thylacine literature already points toward this. Carlson and colleagues separated evidence certainty. Brook and colleagues expanded the sighting corpus, disputed earlier treatment, incorporated spatial patterns and propagated uncertainty. The disagreement is productive because its components can be exposed.

The next step should be adversarial model exchange. Give survival-leaning analysts the skeptic model and skeptics the long-tail dataset. Freeze quality scores before fitting. Require each side to specify what observation would shift its prior most. Publish records rejected from the dataset and reasons for rejection.

This is ordinary open science applied where ordinary incentives are unusually distorted.

A particularly important missing variable is effort. A year with no reports during intensive camera coverage should count differently from a year with no reports when nobody searched a remote valley. Negative evidence gains meaning only when the probability of detection given survival is known.

The equation at the center is simple enough to say aloud: if thylacines were still there, how surprising is everything we failed to see?

The entire book is an attempt to answer that question without pretending we know more about the denominator than we do.
