# Back Matter

## Working chronology

- **1803 onward** — European settlement in Tasmania; conflict with thylacines grows with pastoral expansion.
- **1888–1909** — Tasmanian government bounty period; more than two thousand claims paid.
- **1930** — last known wild thylacine known to have been shot, at Mawbanna.
- **1933** — final known thylacine captured from the wild and later held in Hobart.
- **July 1936** — thylacine receives legal protection in Tasmania, extraordinarily late in its decline.
- **7 September 1936** — final known captive animal dies at Beaumaris Zoo.
- **1937** — official search activity follows immediate concern that the species may have vanished.
- **1940s–1960s** — repeated reports and periodic field searches, no conclusive physical evidence.
- **1980** — Smith and Pyrke conduct automatic-camera work; no thylacine photograph obtained.
- **1982** — wildlife officer Hans Naarding reports a close sighting in northwest Tasmania.
- **1982–83** — intensive confidential follow-up search fails to obtain confirmation.
- **1986** — thylacine formally treated as extinct under widely used international criteria.
- **2017/2018** — Carlson, Bond & Burgio publish mixed-certainty extinction dating, favoring ~1940 and no persistence beyond 1956 in their most optimistic model.
- **2018** — Brook and colleagues publish criticism of deficiencies in that treatment.
- **2023** — Brook et al. publish a 1,237-record spatial/uncertainty analysis yielding a substantially later plausible extinction tail.

## The rule carried forward

A last confirmed observation is a lower bound on persistence, not the extinction date. A non-detection becomes evidence only after the probability of detection under survival is quantified.
