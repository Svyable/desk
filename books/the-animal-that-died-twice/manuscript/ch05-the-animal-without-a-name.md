# 5. The Animal Without a Name

The last captive thylacine is often called Benjamin. The name is sticky, cinematic and probably wrong.

Tasmanian historical work and later museum investigation have dismantled parts of the familiar story. The last zoo animal's sex and popular name were repeated for decades with more confidence than the surviving records justified. In 2022, researchers announced that the remains of the final captive individual had been located in the Tasmanian Museum and Art Gallery collections after having effectively disappeared into the museum's own paperwork.

There is a lesson here more uncomfortable than folklore versus fact. Even the best-known individual of an iconic extinct species accumulated identity errors while its physical remains sat inside a scientific institution.

That should change how we treat weaker post-extinction records. Memory hardens. A label travels farther than its source. Retellings cite one another. By the time a claim becomes culturally famous, provenance can be more difficult to reconstruct than the animal.

This is not an argument that eyewitnesses are useless. It is an argument for chain of custody applied to information. Who first recorded the sighting? How soon after the event? Was the witness interviewed before seeing comparison images? Was the exact wording preserved? Did later summaries add diagnostic features? Does a photograph exist in its original format? Are location and time recoverable independently?

Scientists use provenance naturally for specimens because contamination is obvious. A bone without locality loses much of its value. A tissue sample without handling history is suspect. Testimony deserves the same hygiene, particularly when it becomes model input.

The unnamed final animal also exposes how extinction creates narrative suction. A species ending wants a protagonist. The lonely zoo animal becomes the 'last', acquires a name, and absorbs a whole species' death into one scene. But there may have been unseen animals alive that night. Or there may not have been. The image cannot answer.

That uncertainty is not a defect in history. It is the honest state of knowledge. The point of statistical extinction work is not to replace uncertainty with a cleaner story. It is to represent uncertainty without allowing every uncertainty to become permission for anything.
