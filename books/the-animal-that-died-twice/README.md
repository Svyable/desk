# The Animal That Died Twice

## Thylacine, False Sightings, and the Science of Proving Absence

**Sven Hardy Benson**

A Desk book about the peculiar scientific problem of extinction: a species does not become extinct on the date of its last photograph. It becomes extinct at an unknowable moment, and science must infer that moment from an increasingly contaminated mixture of specimens, searches, witnesses, cameras, habitat, incentives, and silence.

The thylacine is unusually valuable because the record is rich enough to be quantitative and strange enough to expose every weakness in our intuition. The last known captive animal died in Hobart on 7 September 1936. Tasmania still records hundreds of later reports and decades of organized searches. A 2017/2018 Bayesian analysis placed the most likely extinction around 1940 and judged persistence beyond 1956 implausible. A 2023 analysis of 1,237 records, with different treatment of witness quality and geography, put meaningful probability much later, with some model mass extending into the late twentieth century. Neither result resurrects the animal. Together they show that extinction is an inference problem, not a certificate.

## Manuscript

18 numbered chapters plus front matter and back matter/chronology.

## Evidence discipline

1. **Verified/documented:** specimens, bounty records, authenticated photographs/film, official searches, museum material, published statistical analyses.
2. **Credible but incomplete:** experienced-witness reports, plausible tracks, geographically coherent post-1936 reports without physical confirmation.
3. **Disputed/unconfirmed:** photographs without secure provenance, modern sighting claims, mainland survival claims, survival into the present.
4. **Speculative synthesis:** survival beyond the 1940s; a final southwest refugium; general rules for declaring species extinct from mixed-certainty evidence.

The book does not claim the thylacine survives.
