# Outsourcing Divinity — Research and Source Ledger

**Author:** Sven Hardy Benson  
**Draft status:** Working research ledger for the manuscript.  
**Method:** Prefer primary records, official institutions, original research, and first-party accounts for factual claims. Secondary reporting is used where no primary public record captures the event. Interpretive and theological claims remain identified as interpretation rather than empirical fact.

This ledger is not yet publication-formatted endnotes. It is the canonical research trail for editorial verification and can be converted to endnotes/bibliography during production.

## Chapters 1–2 — The Oracle / The Mediator

### Marilyn McCord Adams, *Housing the Powers: Medieval Debates about Dependence on God* (Oxford University Press, 2022)
- Source: https://academic.oup.com/book/38759
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780192862549.001.0001
- Use: Historical and philosophical precedent for talk of human cognitive powers being “outsourced,” including medieval Christian engagement with Islamic philosophical accounts.
- Caveat: This is a work of history of philosophy and philosophical theology, not evidence that modern AI recapitulates medieval doctrine.

### Marilyn McCord Adams and Cecilia Trifogli, “Outsourcing the Subject: Whose Thought Is It?”
- Source: https://academic.oup.com/book/38759/chapter-abstract/337553195
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780192862549.003.0004
- Use: Specific discussion of Averroist accounts in which intellectual thinking is attributed to a separate intellect, and Christian philosophical objections.

## Chapter 12 — Manufactured Providence

- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24626916/
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1248506
- Use: Google Flu Trends forecasting failure; illustrates dataset/product drift, overfitting, and the danger of treating large behavioral data as transparent ground truth.

### Danielle Ensign et al., “Runaway Feedback Loops in Predictive Policing,” FAT* / PMLR 81 (2018)
- Source: https://proceedings.mlr.press/v81/ensign18a.html

- Caveat: The paper models a class of feedback loops; it should not be cited as proof that every predictive-policing deployment behaves identically.

## Editorial evidence rules

1. **Primary before commentary.** Court opinions, official reports, original research, and first-party institutional documents should support factual claims wherever practical.
2. **Label the claim type.** Empirical finding, historical record, theology, philosophy, institutional policy, and speculation should not be blended into one voice of certainty.
3. **Do not convert analogy into evidence.** Challenger, religious office, constitutional checks, markets, and other comparisons are used to expose structural similarities; none proves that AI will follow the compared system’s history.
4. **Time-stamp current claims.** AI products, policy frameworks, company practices, and regulations change quickly. Publication edits should re-check current claims immediately before release.
5. **Keep counterevidence.** If a source complicates the manuscript’s thesis, preserve the complication rather than deleting it for rhetorical cleanliness.
6. **Do not imply consensus where none exists.** Religious traditions, technical researchers, policymakers, and philosophers disagree substantially about AI, mind, authority, consciousness, and governance.
