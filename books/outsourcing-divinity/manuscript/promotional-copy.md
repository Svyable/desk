# Promotional Copy

## One-line hook

We did not build artificial intelligence because we wanted to create God. We may be building it because we are tired of carrying the responsibilities we once gave God.

## Short description

Human beings have always placed impossible questions somewhere outside themselves: with priests, scriptures, kings, courts, markets, experts, institutions, and algorithms. Now a single machine can speak in the language of all of them.

*Outsourcing Divinity* is Sven Hardy Benson's investigation of what happens when AI becomes more than a tool for labor and starts functioning as oracle, confessor, interpreter, adviser, judge, and source of moral permission. It is not an argument that AI is literally divine. It is a book about authority—how we create it, why we crave it, and how easily useful delegation becomes abdication.

## Long description

Ask a machine to rewrite an email and it is obviously a tool. Ask whether the email is fair, whether the relationship is healthy, whether you are a good person, what a sacred text means, which treatment you should choose, which employee deserves to be fired, or what humanity should do next, and the category begins to move.

In *Outsourcing Divinity*, Sven Hardy Benson traces that movement through some of civilization's oldest authority systems. Priesthood mediated the sacred. Writing externalized memory. States turned command into legitimate procedure. Markets condensed dispersed information into price. Experts made advanced civilization possible by knowing what individuals cannot verify. Algorithms learned to rank reality before generative AI learned to explain it.

Then the oracle started talking back.

Benson examines AI sycophancy, algorithmic judgment, prediction, synthetic intimacy, spiritual use, institutional power, human oversight, and the strange possibility of an authority that can sound wiser than any individual while remaining a manufactured system embedded in companies, policies, data centers, incentives, and law.

The book refuses the easy positions. It is not anti-religious, anti-technology, or nostalgic for unreliable human gatekeepers. Delegation is one of humanity's greatest inventions. Artificial intelligence may democratize expertise, expand human agency, improve medicine and education, and remove astonishing amounts of drudgery. The question is what cannot be delegated without changing the meaning of responsibility itself.

The danger is not that the machine becomes God.

The danger is that the machine becomes the place where nobody is responsible anymore.
