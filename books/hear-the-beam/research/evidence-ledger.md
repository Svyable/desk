# Evidence ledger — Hear the Beam

## Governing thesis

A small set of real radio-frequency bioeffects has repeatedly been made to carry claims far larger than the evidence supports. The book reconstructs the chain from microwave hearing and the Moscow embassy irradiation through Cold War threat assessment and contemporary anomalous-health claims, then asks a systems question: what additional engineering, dosimetry, geometry, source evidence and clinical pattern would have to exist before a laboratory effect becomes a covert field capability?

The thesis is deliberately two-sided. “Microwave hearing is real” is not evidence that a particular illness was caused by RF. “No transmitter has been publicly identified” is not proof that RF is physically incapable of causing effects. The evidentiary work is the distance between those sentences.

## Class 1 — verified / declassified / documented

- Allan H. Frey, “Human Auditory System Response to Modulated Electromagnetic Energy,” *Journal of Applied Physiology* 17(4), 1962, pp. 689–692. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1152/jappl.1962.17.4.689 . Establishes induced auditory perception under specified pulsed RF conditions; it does not establish semantic voice transmission, mind control, injury, or a weapon.
- Arthur W. Guy, C. K. Chou, James C. Lin, and D. Christensen, “Microwave-Induced Acoustic Effects in Mammalian Auditory Systems and Physical Materials,” *Annals of the New York Academy of Sciences* 247, 1975. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1749-6632.1975.tb35996.x . Follow-on experimental/physical work supporting a microwave-induced acoustic phenomenon.
- James C. Lin, “Theoretical Calculation of Frequencies and Thresholds of Microwave-Induced Auditory Signals,” *Radio Science* 12(6S), 1977. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1029/RS012i06Sp00237 . Thermoelastic modeling of microwave-induced auditory signals.
- CIA Reading Room record, “Moscow Still Beaming Microwaves, U.S. Says,” published June 28, 1977, declassified release. https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp88b01125r000300120030-8 . Documents the public historical fact of microwave illumination of the U.S. Embassy in Moscow and contemporaneous health concern; the clipped article is not itself a causal health study.
- National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine, *An Assessment of Illness in U.S. Government Employees and Their Families at Overseas Embassies* (2020). https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/25889/ . The committee judged directed, pulsed RF energy the most plausible mechanism among those it considered for the distinctive acute initial symptoms in some cases. “Most plausible” is not source identification or proof of attack.

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete

- Cold War U.S. concern that Soviet research might pursue psychological/biological influence using electromagnetic methods. Declassified holdings and FBIS material establish interest and threat perception unevenly; performer-level efficacy claims require reconstruction.
- Acute, directional/location-specific symptom descriptions from some U.S. personnel later grouped under anomalous health incidents. Clinical reports and testimony establish reported experience; exposure source, dose and mechanism remain incomplete.
- Historical U.S. bioeffects programs associated with the Moscow Signal, including PANDORA-related work. Program existence and some studies are documented, but this book will not infer a coherent successful weapons program from the archive without experiment-level support.

## Class 3 — disputed / unconfirmed

- Claims that a foreign adversary used a directed RF weapon to cause the Havana-associated illnesses. No publicly established device, operator, recovered hardware, measured attack waveform or complete exposure reconstruction currently closes the chain.
- Claims of operational “voice to skull,” remote thought insertion, behavioral programming, or arbitrary mind control derived from the microwave auditory effect. Microwave hearing does not establish these capabilities.
- Claims that Soviet/Russian “psychotronics” literature demonstrates reliable remote behavioral control. Program interest, propaganda, intelligence reporting, speculative research and operational efficacy must remain separate.
- Any claim that the Moscow embassy irradiation caused specific later illnesses without a defensible individual exposure/outcome chain.

## Class 4 — speculative synthesis / open conjecture

- The microwave auditory effect may function culturally as a **mechanism bridge**: one counterintuitive but real effect makes much larger invisible-weapon narratives feel technically continuous even when the missing engineering steps are enormous.
- Secrecy can amplify this bridge in both directions. Real classified work creates rational uncertainty; uncertainty then permits unsupported capability claims to borrow plausibility from classification.
- A useful directed-energy epistemology may be built as a link budget: source → propagation → coupling → absorbed energy/pressure transient → tissue response → symptom → clinical pattern → attribution. A claim that skips rungs should lose confidence rather than gain mystery.

## Contradictions to preserve

1. A real mechanism can coexist with a wrong attribution.
2. Failure to identify a source is not evidence of impossible physics; plausible physics is not evidence of a source.
3. The 2020 National Academies assessment elevated pulsed RF as plausible for a subset of cases; later intelligence assessments have often emphasized other explanations and low confidence in foreign-adversary attribution. These are not interchangeable questions and must be reconstructed document by document.
4. The Moscow Signal proves intentional RF illumination of a diplomatic site, but motive and health consequence are separate claims.
5. Laboratory perception thresholds and field injury thresholds are different quantities.

## Quantitative burden

For every claimed field mechanism, extract where possible: carrier frequency; pulse width; pulse repetition frequency; peak and average power density; exposure duration; antenna gain/aperture; range; beam width; intervening structures; specific absorption rate or other coupling metric; expected thermoelastic pressure; orientation; target anatomy; measured symptom threshold; and source detectability. Never compare only average power when pulse structure is the proposed mechanism.

## Strongest conventional countermodel

The contemporary illness cluster can contain multiple etiologies: ordinary medical conditions, environmental exposures, stress/nocebo and expectation effects, infectious or chemical causes, pre-existing disorders, and some unresolved cases. Grouping by diplomatic context can create a syndrome category before a single mechanism has been established. This model must be allowed to explain most or all cases if case-level evidence supports it.

## Strongest extraordinary model

A technically capable actor could in principle exploit pulsed RF or another directed-energy modality whose effects are difficult to recognize clinically and whose source is hard to recover after transient exposure. To move beyond possibility, the model must predict case geometry, timing, dose-compatible effects, source constraints and independent observables better than competing explanations.

## Defeat condition

The book’s central “mechanism bridge” synthesis weakens substantially if the historical record shows that microwave hearing did not materially shape intelligence, military or public interpretations of later invisible-energy claims, or if quantitative reconstruction shows no meaningful conceptual continuity between the relevant RF regimes. The field-capability branch loses if plausible source/dose/geometry cannot be closed. The skeptical branch loses if prospective, instrumented evidence identifies a reproducible exposure waveform that predicts acute effects and survives independent replication.

## Research queue

- Recover full PANDORA chronology and primary reports, not retrospective summaries.
- Reconstruct Moscow embassy field strengths, frequencies, temporal changes, measurement methods and medical cohorts from State/NSA/CIA records.
- Recover Frey 1961/1962 full methods and later replication thresholds; distinguish perception from injury.
- Audit Foster & Finch 1974 thermoacoustic evidence and later mechanistic consensus.
- Reconstruct Soviet/Russian bioelectromagnetics and “psychotronics” from primary-language technical sources; separate state interest from efficacy.
- Build a chronology of official Havana/AHI assessments: State, FBI where public, CIA/IC assessments, National Academies, JASON if lawfully public, DoD/NIH clinical work, and later review updates.
- Build case-selection and denominator table. Do not treat the administrative AHI label as a biological diagnosis.
- Compare RF with ultrasound/infrasound, chemicals, infection, vestibular disorders, migraine, functional neurological mechanisms, stress/nocebo and heterogeneous-cause models.
- Design an adversarial blinded exposure experiment with preregistered waveform families, sham exposures, dosimetry, auditory masking, vestibular/EEG/physiology endpoints and independent replication.

## Publication gates

No chapter may state that Havana personnel were attacked with RF. No chapter may state that Moscow exposure caused disease absent a sourced causal result. No Soviet/Russian program is described as successful from intent, funding, intelligence reporting or a patent. “Psychotronic” is a historical label, not a mechanism. All contemporary attribution claims require date, institution and confidence language. Exact dose numbers must trace to primary measurements or peer-reviewed experimental reports.
