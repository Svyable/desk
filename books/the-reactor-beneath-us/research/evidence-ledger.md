# Evidence ledger — The Reactor Beneath Us

## Class 1 — verified / documented / directly measured

- Natural sustained nuclear fission occurred in multiple reactor zones in the Oklo/Okelobondo region of Gabon roughly two billion years ago. Meshik et al. 2004 provides isotope-based evidence for cycling operation. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.93.182302
- Electron antineutrinos from uranium/thorium decay inside Earth have been detected by KamLAND and Borexino. https://doi.org/10.1038/nature03980 and https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.101.012009
- Existing geoneutrino/reactor-antineutrino data constrain simple hypothetical georeactors to the terawatt scale; Borexino's published analysis yields location-dependent upper limits, including a several-terawatt central-source bound.
- Hollenbach/Herndon and later Lin/Sheu/Jiang published reactor-physics simulations showing that assumed actinide configurations can sustain long-lived fast-spectrum fission under specified conditions. This is class 1 evidence that the calculations exist and have those conditional outputs, not that Earth contains the modeled configuration.
- JUNO began physics data taking August 26, 2025. Its first 59-day oscillation analysis was published in *Nature* on June 10, 2026 and demonstrated high-precision detector performance. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10538-z

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete inference

- Estimates of how much measured geoneutrino flux originates in mantle rather than crust depend on geological models, especially local crust subtraction.
- Earth's uranium and thorium inventory, bulk-silicate composition and radiogenic heat fraction are constrained but not perfectly known.
- High-pressure/redox-dependent uranium partitioning during core formation remains relevant to whether enough actinide material could ever enter the core.
- Helium-isotope reservoirs and transport from deep mantle are measured and modeled, but source histories are not uniquely determined by a single ratio.

## Class 3 — disputed allegations / interpretations lacking independent confirmation

- A self-sustaining natural fission reactor currently exists at or near Earth's center.
- Such a reactor is the principal source of high 3He/4He ratios in oceanic basalts.
- Variations in deep reactor output drive geomagnetic reversals or major secular changes.

These claims have peer-reviewed proponents but are not established by the existence of the papers or by numerical reactor feasibility alone.

## Class 4 — speculative synthesis / open conjecture

- A geographically distributed detector network could localize a weak deep fission source even before strong event-by-event directionality exists.
- Hybrid or directional antineutrino detectors could eventually distinguish central, core-mantle-boundary and distributed geological sources with much less dependence on crust models.
- Prospectively combining antineutrino limits with helium source budgets could decisively remove some georeactor interpretations even if a tiny fission source remains allowed.

## Contradictions and disconfirming evidence

The strongest disconfirming evidence is not a single paper saying “no.” It is the combination of geochemical difficulty concentrating uranium in the core and direct antineutrino upper limits on fission power. Any version of the hypothesis requiring power above those limits is already in tension with observation.

The strongest counterargument to dismissal is Oklo plus reactor simulation: natural criticality is an observed category, and long-lived fast-spectrum operation is not neutronically impossible under every assumed composition. Therefore the productive dispute lies in initial planetary conditions and measured emissions, not rhetoric about whether nature is allowed to make reactors.

## Incentive / interpretation audit

There is little secrecy incentive here compared with intelligence-adjacent topics, but there are ordinary scientific incentives. An unconventional theory may accumulate citations from controversy. A null detector analysis is less newsworthy than a discovery. Geochemical models can absorb uncertain reservoirs. Reactor physicists can overvalue conditional criticality calculations. The book therefore privileges measurements that cross disciplinary boundaries: a nuclear hypothesis should be tested by nuclear messengers.
