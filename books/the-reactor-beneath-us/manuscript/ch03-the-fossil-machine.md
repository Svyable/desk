# The Fossil Machine

A fossil is evidence that an organism existed. Oklo is evidence that a process existed. The difference is useful.

The natural reactors are sometimes presented as geological curiosities, a good fact to pull out after dinner. Their deeper value is methodological. They preserve a process through several independent kinds of residue. Uranium tells us fuel was consumed. Fission products tell us how. Xenon provides timing information. Mineral hosts tell us something about retention and migration. Reactor physics checks whether the reconstruction is plausible.

This is what we should want from every claim about inaccessible machinery.

The word inaccessible is relative. Oklo's active reactors vanished roughly two billion years before anyone could measure them, yet their products remained available in rock. Earth's center is inaccessible in space rather than time. We cannot sample the core directly, but a central nuclear reactor, if it existed, would also have outputs that leave the source. Heat migrates. Helium can be transported, though tracing its exact source is difficult. Magnetic fields are observable but wildly non-unique as causal evidence. Antineutrinos are the cleanest because they are made in the relevant nuclear reactions and escape without waiting for mantle convection.

Oklo became scientifically powerful because it left an overdetermined story. A central georeactor hypothesis is weak wherever it relies on underdetermined observations—facts that many different mechanisms can explain.

Consider helium-3. Elevated helium-3 to helium-4 ratios in some mantle-derived materials are interesting. A georeactor model can produce helium isotopes. But the observation does not belong to that model. Primordial reservoirs and mantle dynamics provide competing explanations. If the same datum can be generated by several plausible histories, it does not discriminate strongly among them. The hypothesis needs a signature less promiscuous than “something unusual exists in the deep mantle.”

Or consider geomagnetic reversals. Earth’s magnetic field changes and reverses. A variable nuclear heat source could be invoked as a driver. But the geodynamo is already a complex fluid system capable of time-dependent behavior. Unless a georeactor model predicts a pattern the conventional dynamo does not, the reversal record is an invitation to storytelling.

The fossil-machine standard is harsher. What would be depleted? What would be produced? What spectral line or energy distribution follows from the reaction? What spatial pattern? What power level? What is the expected background? Could a detector distinguish the source from ordinary uranium and thorium decay?

This is why geoneutrinos matter so much. They convert a debate about inaccessible composition into a counting experiment. Not a perfect counting experiment. Reactor antineutrinos from power stations contaminate the same instruments. Crustal radioactivity contributes heavily to the measured geological signal. Present liquid-scintillator detectors have poor event-by-event directionality. Oscillation parameters matter. Detector response matters. Geological models are required to subtract local crust.

But the problem has become quantitative.

Oklo offers another lesson in humility: the residue survived because fission products and actinides were, in many cases, surprisingly retained in the geology. That has made the site useful as a natural analogue in discussions of nuclear waste isolation. Yet “surprisingly retained” does not mean nothing moved. Every use of Oklo as an analogue requires attention to mineralogy, water flow, element chemistry and time. Analogues are strongest when their differences are explicit.

The same should be true when Oklo is invoked as an analogue for a central reactor. The differences dominate: pressure, temperature, redox environment, composition, spectrum, moderator, fuel transport and geometry. Oklo is proof of category, not proof of instance.

Still, proof of category is not nothing. Before 1972, one could have treated a naturally occurring self-sustaining uranium chain reaction as an exotic thought experiment. After Oklo, nature had filed the paperwork in isotope ratios.

That should make us simultaneously more adventurous and less gullible.

More adventurous, because the planet can assemble machines without intention. Less gullible, because when it does, the machine leaves receipts.
