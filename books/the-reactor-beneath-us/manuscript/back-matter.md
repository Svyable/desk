# Back Matter

The evidentiary spine of this book is deliberately short enough to audit.

In 1972, anomalous uranium isotopic composition in ore from Gabon led to recognition of the Oklo natural fission reactors. In 2004, Meshik, Hohenberg and Pravdivtseva used xenon isotopes to infer cycling operation on roughly half-hour-on, multi-hour-off timescales. In 2001, Hollenbach and Herndon published fast-reactor simulations for a proposed deep-Earth georeactor; in 2003 Herndon published the associated helium-isotope argument. In 2005 KamLAND reported direct detection work on geologically produced antineutrinos. Borexino later accumulated a larger low-background geoneutrino sample and, by 2020, reported a comprehensive analysis capable of constraining hypothetical georeactor power. JUNO began data taking on August 26, 2025 and published its first high-precision oscillation result on June 10, 2026.

The chronology matters because the argument changes over time. A deep-Earth reactor proposal in 1996 or 2001 lived mainly in geochemistry, reactor calculation and indirect observables. After geoneutrino detection, it became partly a particle-detection problem. That shift is the reason for the book.
