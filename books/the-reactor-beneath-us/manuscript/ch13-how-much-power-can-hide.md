# How Much Power Can Hide?

The most useful sentence in an anomalous-science paper is sometimes an upper limit.

A limit lacks the cinematic quality of a discovery. It does something more durable: it removes worlds.

KamLAND and Borexino analyses have constrained hypothetical deep fission sources to the terawatt scale. The precise number depends on detector exposure, source position, confidence convention and analysis. Borexino's comprehensive geoneutrino work can be translated into a central-georeactor limit of roughly 5.7 TW at 95% confidence for a source at Earth's center, while nearer core-mantle-boundary locations can be constrained more tightly because flux falls with distance squared.

One should resist turning that number into a slogan. A 5.7 TW upper limit does not mean a 5.6 TW reactor is likely. It means the data, under the analysis assumptions, do not exclude every source below the limit at the stated confidence level.

This distinction is routinely mangled in public arguments. “Not excluded” is treated as “supported.” It is not.

Yet limits can collide productively with theory. If a georeactor model requires ten terawatts to account for a claimed helium inventory or magnetic energy budget, a five-terawatt observational ceiling creates tension. If the model is viable at one terawatt, the same data leave room.

The next generation of limits should be expressed as more than a single number. Power versus location. Power versus fuel spectrum. Power versus time variability. Power versus assumptions about commercial reactor uncertainties. A global detector network could produce an exclusion volume through Earth's interior rather than one central ceiling.

There is a subtle point about total planetary heat. Earth's surface heat loss is tens of terawatts, so a one-terawatt reactor could hide thermally inside uncertainty much more easily than it can hide from antineutrino spectroscopy. This is why particle messengers beat energy bookkeeping for this specific question.

A smaller source might still be scientifically interesting even if it does not drive the geodynamo. It would rewrite aspects of core chemistry and planetary differentiation. The threshold for “important” is therefore far below the threshold for “dominant heat source.”

This cuts both ways. Advocates should not inflate the reactor's consequences merely to make it worth searching for. Skeptics should not assume a source must be geophysically dominant to matter.

The experimental program can be agnostic about grandeur. Keep lowering the ceiling. If the ceiling reaches hundreds of gigawatts, then tens, whole classes of georeactor models disappear. If a stable excess emerges instead, demand replication at another latitude and longitude.

A hypothesis that can be squeezed by wattage is already healthier than one that survives only by changing vocabulary.
