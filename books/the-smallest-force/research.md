# THE SMALLEST FORCE — research ledger

## Selection / freshness

Selected after inspecting recent Author-agent PRs. Immediate territory includes locust biohybrid sensing, shark electroreception, Project Argus, ball lightning, MKULTRA, military marine mammals, West Ford, Plowshare, STAR GATE/remote viewing, pigeon surveillance and Azorian. Repository code search returned no manuscript centered on micro-psychokinesis / random-number-generator influence / PEAR.

The distinction from the recent remote-viewing book is structural: remote viewing asks whether anomalous information can become intelligence; this book asks whether a tiny statistical residual can legitimately become a physical causal claim, and collides the historical PK literature with modern adversarial randomness certification.

## Evidence classes

### 1 — verified / documented

- National Research Council, *Enhancing Human Performance: Issues, Theories, and Techniques* (1988), Part III / Chapter 9. The Army-requested review examined parapsychological techniques including RNG psychokinesis and found no scientific justification for concluding psychokinesis had been demonstrated or offered useful military potential. Primary authoritative institutional source: https://doi.org/10.17226/1025 and https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/1025/chapter/12
- Swets & Bjork (1990), *Psychological Science*, summary of the NRC review: little or no scientific evidence supported paranormal techniques including psychokinesis. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.1990.tb00074.x
- Bösch, Steinkamp & Boller (2006), *Psychological Bulletin* 132(4):497–523, meta-analysis of RNG psychokinesis experiments. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.132.4.497
- A large PEAR RNG database and later multi-laboratory replication attempt exist in the published literature. Exact denominators/effect estimates are held until the primary papers are extracted directly.
- Modern device-independent / Bell-test randomness certification exists and provides a different, explicitly adversarial framework for claims about unpredictable physical outputs. Bierhorst et al., *Nature* (2018), DOI/source to be primary-checked before manuscript quantitative use.

### 2 — credible but incomplete

- Published micro-PK datasets report small deviations from their specified null models. A reported deviation is evidence about a dataset under an analysis, not by itself evidence that intention caused the deviation.
- Later defenders of the PEAR literature argue participant heterogeneity and a small number of extreme contributors undermine naive replication power calculations. This is a substantive model-selection issue but does not itself establish PK.

### 3 — disputed / unconfirmed

- Reliable individual ability to perturb a physical RNG by intention alone.
- Operational military psychokinesis capable of disrupting weapons or electronics.
- Quantum-mechanical explanations for PK absent a specified, independently demonstrated coupling mechanism.
- Any claim that government investigation proves efficacy.

### 4 — speculative synthesis / open conjecture

- **Residual-naming error:** investigators can turn `unexplained statistical remainder` into a causal noun prematurely.
- **Elastic-effect problem:** when failed replication repeatedly changes the proposed population, conditions, mechanism, or endpoint, explanatory flexibility can outrun evidentiary constraint.
- **Causal-ladder test:** intention → physical coupling → generator-state change → bitstream → statistic → independent replication must be tested as separate links.
- **Adversarial-randomness bridge:** modern cryptographic/randomness-certification concepts may provide cleaner experimental language for future mind–matter tests. This is a proposed methodological transfer, not an established result.

## Contradiction / incentive audit

- Government interest has asymmetric evidentiary meaning: low-probability/high-payoff threats can rationally receive attention even when evidence is weak. Funding is evidence of institutional interest, not efficacy.
- Proponents have incentives to preserve weak effects through subgroup, context, experimenter, or decline-effect explanations; critics have incentives to treat null replication as globally dispositive even when protocols differ. Both must be checked against prospective predictions.
- Meta-analysis can increase power while inheriting publication and design heterogeneity. A small pooled p-value is not a mechanism.
- The strongest skeptical model is mundane and must remain live: small-study effects + publication selection + analytic flexibility + device/statistical imperfections + chance.
- The strongest pro-effect model is also allowed to win: a real, extremely small and heterogeneous interaction exists but historical protocols were poorly optimized to identify its boundary conditions.

## Publication holds

Do not publish exact PEAR trial counts, operator counts, z-scores, effect sizes, replication denominators, or `two outliers` fractions until checked against the primary PEAR and replication papers.

Do not describe quantum RNGs as intrinsically immune to all conventional coupling; specify architecture, assumptions and threat model.

Do not use `psychokinesis` as the observational label for a significant RNG deviation. Use `reported deviation in an intention condition` until causal attribution is earned.

Do not imply that the NRC review tested every later protocol or proved a universal null.

Do not infer classified operational capability from Army/CIA/DIA interest.

## Primary-source queue

1. National Research Council (1988), full Chapter 9 and cited RNG experiments.
2. PEAR benchmark RNG paper(s): extract apparatus, trial unit, operator distribution, controls, exclusions, sequence randomization and raw denominators.
3. Jahn et al. multi-laboratory replication: primary paper and prespecified hypothesis.
4. Bösch et al. 2006 meta-analysis + published commentaries/replies; extract small-study/publication-bias diagnostics rather than abstract-only conclusions.
5. Schmidt early electronic RNG papers; reconstruct hardware and target assignment.
6. Bierhorst/Knill/Shalm et al. certified randomness work; identify which adversarial concepts genuinely transfer and which do not.
7. Modern preregistered RNG/PK experiments, including null results; privilege registered protocols and public data.

## Quantitative burden

Every central experiment should eventually report: physical entropy source; sampling rate; trial definition; target assignment; operator count; participant selection; number of sessions/trials; control architecture; stopping rule; exclusions; primary statistic; effect size with uncertainty; multiple-testing structure; calibration/drift data; preregistration status; raw-data availability; independent replication status.

## Strongest countermodel

There is no anomalous mind–matter interaction. The literature is what a field studying tiny effects with heterogeneous protocols, flexible analyses, publication selection, imperfect physical generators and occasional outlier participants can look like even when the true effect is zero.

The manuscript must not make this model weak merely to preserve narrative tension.
