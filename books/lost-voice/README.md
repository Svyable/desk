*How the Trump Administration Dismantled the Public Broadcasting System*

In 2025 the second Trump administration turned a decades-old conservative objective into a coordinated campaign against the federal public-broadcasting architecture. President Donald Trump issued an executive order targeting federal support for NPR and PBS, challenged the independence of the Corporation for Public Broadcasting, and asked Congress to rescind CPB’s advance appropriations. Congress supplied the decisive constitutional power of the purse. CPB then wound down and dissolved. Months later, NPR and PBS won a major First Amendment ruling against the executive order — too late to restore the institution Congress had already defunded.

*Lost Voice* is deliberately narrower than a claim that NPR, PBS, or American public media disappeared. They did not. The book follows what actually changed: the federal corporation, advance-funding model, redistributive local-station grants, common infrastructure, and institutional firewall that had supported a decentralized public-media field since 1967. It gives the strongest case for ending the subsidy real weight, separates lawful congressional spending decisions from unlawful executive retaliation, and tracks the uneven first year of private, philanthropic, state, university, and member-funded adaptation.
