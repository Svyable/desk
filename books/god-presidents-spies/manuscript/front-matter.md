# God, Presidents & Spies

**Sven Hardy Benson**

---

The fantasy of secrecy is that somebody knows.

There is a room, a file, a general, a director, a president. Somewhere the fragments become the whole. The truth may be hidden from us, but it is not hidden from itself.

This is comforting.

It is also a poor description of how large secret systems work.

Classified information is partitioned on purpose. Access depends on more than rank. A person may need a clearance, a program indoctrination, a reason to know, and permission to enter a particular compartment. The architecture protects operations by making the organization less internally omniscient.

That creates a peculiar possibility.

Everybody can be telling the truth about what they saw.

Everybody can still be wrong about what happened.

This book is about that possibility.

It moves through presidents and spies because executive power depends on information that has already been collected, filtered, classified, summarized, and sometimes contested before it reaches the top. It moves through God because presidents also receive counsel from authorities that do not derive their legitimacy from intelligence tradecraft: faith, conscience, clergy, private relationships, moral conviction, and sometimes beliefs that cannot be tested by the methods of an intelligence service.

And it moves through the UFO problem because extraordinary secrecy claims expose every weakness in our model of hidden institutions. If a deeply compartmented program existed for decades, what would it look like from outside? If it did not exist, what ordinary features of secrecy could make it look as if it did?

Those are different questions.

We will keep them that way.
