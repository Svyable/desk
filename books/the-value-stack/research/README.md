# Research and source trail

The Value Stack is a synthetic work of trade nonfiction. Its central framework—substrate, capability, access, trust, meaning, institutional control, and the migration of scarcity—is original analysis. Historical examples should be checked against primary or high-quality secondary sources during revision.

## Core intellectual sources

- Ronald H. Coase, “The Nature of the Firm” (1937), for transaction costs and firm boundaries.
- Oliver E. Williamson, work on transaction-cost economics and governance.
- Michael E. Porter, *Competitive Advantage* (1985), for value-chain analysis and competitive positioning.
- David J. Teece, “Profiting from Technological Innovation” (1986), for complementary assets and value capture.
- Clayton M. Christensen, *The Innovator's Dilemma* (1997), for disruptive trajectories and incumbent resource allocation.
- Herbert A. Simon, work on bounded rationality and attention.
- Daniel Kahneman and Amos Tversky, work on judgment under uncertainty, prospect theory, and cognitive bias.
- Elinor Ostrom, work on institutions and governance of shared resources.
- Mark Granovetter, “Economic Action and Social Structure” (1985), for embeddedness of economic action.
- Erving Goffman, work on social presentation and identity; Pierre Bourdieu, work on distinction and forms of capital, as background for the meaning layer.

## Business and technology histories to verify in revision

- IBM's 1981 Personal Computer architecture, the role of Microsoft software, and the modular PC ecosystem: IBM Archives and contemporaneous corporate histories.
- Semiconductor specialization, foundry economics, and TSMC's development: TSMC annual reports and company history; semiconductor-industry technical histories.
- Cloud computing and infrastructure-as-a-service: Amazon annual reports and AWS historical materials.
- Apple's integrated hardware/software/services strategy: Apple annual reports, developer documentation, and public product history.
- Payment networks and trust infrastructure: Visa and Mastercard annual reports and regulatory filings.
- Platform economics, network effects, interoperability, and switching costs: peer-reviewed industrial-organization literature and competition-authority reports.

## Finance and risk

- Hyman Minsky's work on financial instability as historical background, without treating it as a complete model of every crisis.
- Bank for International Settlements publications for banking, liquidity, leverage, and financial-system structure.
- Financial Stability Board and central-bank materials for system-risk terminology.

## Geopolitics and supply chains

- U.S. Department of Commerce and Bureau of Industry and Security materials for semiconductor export-control facts.
- OECD and WTO publications for global value chains and trade dependencies.
- IEA materials for energy-system dependencies where energy examples are used.
- Company filings and government industrial-policy documents for claims about specific supply concentrations.

## Artificial intelligence

The AI chapter intentionally avoids time-sensitive numerical claims. Its claims about future value migration are analysis and forecast. Revision should use current benchmark, cost, adoption, and labor-market evidence from primary model-provider documentation, peer-reviewed research, official labor statistics, and company filings before publication.

## Falsification notes

The framework would be weakened if durable value capture systematically remained in layers that were neither scarce nor difficult to substitute, without regulation, coordination effects, information asymmetry, switching costs, or another identifiable mechanism. It would also be weakened if commoditization of a layer did not tend to shift bargaining power toward complements. Counterexamples should be preserved in revision rather than explained away.

The framework is descriptive, not a claim that captured value equals social contribution or moral worth. A major purpose of the book is to keep those concepts separate.
