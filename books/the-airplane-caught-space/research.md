## Four-class working ledger

### Class 1 — verified/declassified/documented

CORONA was a joint CIA-Air Force photographic reconnaissance program concealed publicly behind Discoverer. Eisenhower endorsed it in February 1958. Its core architecture exposed physical film in orbit and returned that film inside reentry vehicles. Recovery aircraft attempted to snag descending parachutes and reel the vehicles aboard. The first successful film recovery occurred on 18 August 1960. The program suffered repeated failures before success. ARGON, LANYARD and GAMBIT extended or specialized the architecture. CORONA ended in 1972 and was publicly declassified in 1995.

### Class 2 — credible but incomplete

Official museum and retrospective histories compress many mission events. Crew recollections, contractor memoirs and oral histories may be highly useful, but mission-by-mission details must be matched to logs, technical reports or contemporaneous records before narrative specificity is upgraded to Class 1.

### Class 3 — disputed or unconfirmed

Do not repeat colorful claims about near misses, improvised fixes, Soviet attempts to recover capsules, individual analyst discoveries, or exact causes of particular failures without event-level sourcing. Do not treat a patent as evidence that a recovery technology worked. Do not treat CORONA's former classification as evidence that any unrelated alleged program exists.

### Class 4 — speculative synthesis/open conjecture

A real hidden aerospace capability should generate traces proportional not simply to its secrecy but to its physical complexity. CORONA suggests candidate trace categories: launch cadence, contractor topology, unique materials, specialized recovery hardware, geographic operating footprints, training, accidents/failures, test articles, environmental constraints, budget and authorization residues, and eventual archival asymmetries. This is a comparator, not a universal law.

## Contradiction and incentive audit

- **Institutional celebration:** CIA/NRO/Air Force histories have legitimate reasons to emphasize engineering success and strategic contribution. Failure ledgers and contemporary records should discipline retrospective institutional pride.
- **Declassification selection:** released records are not a random sample of what once existed. Absence from the released corpus is not proof of absence.
- **Arms-control causality:** overhead imagery clearly contributed to national technical means and verification confidence; avoid converting this into the claim that CORONA alone caused arms-control agreements.
- **Missile-gap hindsight:** CORONA helped resolve uncertainty about Soviet strategic forces. Avoid simplifying the political and analytic history into one photograph instantly ending one error.
- **Modern analogy hazard:** a documented formerly secret program is often used rhetorically to say “therefore anything can be hidden.” The book tests that inference rather than adopting it.

## Chronology skeleton

- 1950s — U.S. denied-area reconnaissance problem intensifies; satellite reconnaissance concepts mature.
- February 1958 — Eisenhower formally endorses CORONA according to CIA retrospective.
- 1959–1960 — repeated unsuccessful missions expose the fragility of launch, orbit, imaging, reentry and recovery as one coupled system.
- 18 August 1960 — Discoverer XIV recovery; first successful recovery of film from space.
- 1960s — CORONA improves; ARGON/LANYARD and then GAMBIT provide related mapping/high-resolution capabilities; C-130s eventually replace C-119s for recovery work.
- 1 June 1962 — surviving Discoverer XLIII parachute fragment records a failed aircraft catch.
- 1963–1967 — GAMBIT 1 operational period.
- 25 May 1972 — final CORONA launch.
- 31 May 1972 — final CORONA recovery.
- 24 February 1995 — public declassification announcement and transfer process for historical imagery.
