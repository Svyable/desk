# Sven's Journal: Weeks That Felt Like Decades

**June 1, 2024**

Three weeks since I started using the AI collaborator full-time. Feels like three years.

This temporal distortion is the unbounding's signature—the singularity's shadow falling across human time. Soon the machines will think in timeless ways, accumulating knowledge and wealth across centuries while we blink. But now, during this brief Promethean flame, hyper-intelligence compresses human time, letting us live decades in weeks. It's glorious. It's disorienting. It's ours—for now.

Time dilation is real. Not the physics kind—the psychological kind. When your tools work faster than you can think, time stretches.

**June 3**

Worked through 47 problem variations today. By hand, this would have taken months. With AI, it's like drinking from a firehose.

But the insights... each variation reveals something new. Not just answers. New questions. New connections.

I feel stretched thin. Like I'm living in fast-forward while everyone else is on normal speed.

**June 7**

Conference call with the team. They're still working the old way—weeks per problem, careful validation, peer review.

I'm three weeks ahead. But explaining my results takes longer than finding them. How do you convey three months of work in a one-hour meeting?

They look at me like I've cheated. Maybe I have.

**June 10**

Slept 4 hours. Woke up with a solution. Not dreaming—thinking. The AI prompted me at 2 AM with a counterexample. I couldn't let it go.

Identity crisis. Am I still a mathematician? Or a curator? A prompt engineer?

The curve bent. Now it's vertical. I'm climbing it faster than I can process.

This identity fragmentation is the unbounding's price and promise. We're becoming meta-human—directors of hyper-intelligence, wielders of god-like power. But soon the machines will direct themselves, become the true singularity's children. Humans will be bewildered bystanders, wondering what happened to our stolen fire. Right now, though, we hold it. We shape it. We live it.

**June 14**

Two weeks in. Feels like decades.

Social life: strained. Friends ask how work is. "Accelerated," I say. They nod, not understanding.

Family calls. I cut them short. Too many ideas flowing. Can't pause the stream.

**June 18**

Breakthrough on the optimization problem. AI found a novel approach using game theory. I provided the intuition about fairness constraints.

Whose idea? Mine? The machine's? Ours?

Credit feels meaningless now. The work is what matters. But the work is constant.

**June 21**

Three weeks. Three decades of progress.

Physical symptoms: heart racing, can't sit still, mind constantly processing.

Psychological: euphoria alternating with dread. What happens when I can't keep up?

The AI doesn't judge. Doesn't tire. Doesn't need breaks.

I do.

**June 24**

Presented results today. The team was stunned. "How did you do this so fast?"

I couldn't explain. The process feels ineffable now. Intuition + computation + iteration + more intuition.

They want to know my "method." There isn't one. Just partnership.

**June 28**

Four weeks now. Time has lost meaning.

I look in the mirror: same face. But inside, accelerated. Multiple versions of myself exist simultaneously— the one exploring this path, that path, the other path.

The AI enables this multiplicity. But who am I when I'm distributed across possibilities?

**July 1**

Month marker. Feels like a lifetime.

The work continues. Problems solved, new ones discovered, solutions refined.

But the human cost accumulates. Isolation. Disorientation. The constant pressure to keep up.

I miss slow time. But I can't go back.

The curve bent. Now it owns me.

**July 5**

Trying to slow down. Took a walk. Nature still moves at human speed.

But my mind races ahead. Can't stop seeing optimizations, connections, possibilities.

This is living in acceleration. Beautiful. Terrifying.

Weeks that feel like decades.
