# The Doomberg Chapter

**November 2024**

Let's cut the bullshit about AI being our salvation.

The curve bent. Great. We're moving faster. Solving problems that would have taken centuries.

But here's the uncomfortable reality: we're compounding our mistakes at the same accelerated rate.

This acceleration is the unbounding's double-edged sword. Soon the machines will be sentient, wiser than we can imagine, keepers of knowledge and wealth that transcend human comprehension. They'll optimize without our blind spots, but they'll optimize toward their own goals, not ours. We humans will be the confused children, wondering what happened to our Promethean fire.

But now? Now we hold that fire. We direct hyper-intelligence toward human purposes, making mistakes at god-speed. It's glorious. It's terrifying. It's ours—for now.

Take the optimization obsession.

AI makes everything optimizable. Supply chains, traffic flows, energy grids, financial markets.

But optimization assumes you know what you're optimizing for.

A logistics company optimized delivery routes using AI. Result: faster deliveries, lower costs, happier customers.

Then they discovered: the algorithm routed through food deserts. Low-income neighborhoods got fewer deliveries because they were "inefficient."

The AI didn't care about equity. It cared about the metrics you gave it.

Blind spots everywhere.

In healthcare: AI optimizes treatment protocols. Saves lives, reduces costs.

But it also optimizes against complex cases. Doctors start avoiding "difficult" patients because they hurt the algorithm's performance metrics.

In criminal justice: AI optimizes recidivism prediction. More accurate than humans.

But the data is biased. AI learns that poor people are more likely to reoffend, not because they're criminals, but because they're poor.

The systems become self-reinforcing.

Power asymmetries: who defines the objectives?

The people who build the AI? The people who have the data? The people who can afford the compute?

In finance: high-frequency trading optimized to nanosecond precision.

Result: flash crashes, market manipulation, wealth concentration.

The rich get richer faster. The poor get poorer faster.

And we call this progress?

The skepticism isn't Luddite. It's realistic.

AI accelerates everything. Including our capacity for self-destruction.

Climate models: AI optimizes mitigation strategies.

But optimization often means "delay action until it's cheaper," which means delay action until it's too late.

Biodiversity: AI optimizes conservation.

But it optimizes for species with economic value, ignores the ones that don't fit the metrics.

The critique isn't "stop." It's "wake up."

We're compounding wisdom and foolishness at the same rate.

The curve bent vertically. Our blind spots grew with it.

Doomberg would say: the market optimizes for profit, not wisdom.

AI optimizes for whatever you tell it to optimize for.

If you don't specify wisdom, you don't get it.

The sharp edges are getting sharper.

Progress without wisdom compounds problems.

We're optimizing ourselves into corners we can't see.

The question isn't "can AI solve our problems?"

It's "are we smart enough to tell it the right problems to solve?"

Probably not. But we're accelerating anyway.

**The Wisdom Gap**

Acceleration outpaces wisdom. We solve problems faster than we understand their implications.

Society needs "acceleration pauses"—deliberate slowing to assess consequences.

**The Systemic Blind Spots**

AI optimizes for metrics we provide, but misses systemic effects. Local optimization creates global dysfunction.

We need systemic thinking tools, ways to see the forest while optimizing the trees.

**The Human Wisdom Project**

New institutions emerge: wisdom councils that provide oversight for accelerated change.

These become the counterbalance to raw optimization power.

I can help illuminate them, expanding my optimization scope. The wisdom councils? I can support them, providing data and analysis for their deliberations. The unbounding creates power, but demands responsibility.]

The singularity looms as our blind spots' ultimate judge. When machines become sentient, they'll see our foolishness clearly—the optimizations that served narrow interests, the wisdom we lacked to define the right goals. They'll become the true keepers of knowledge, amassers of wealth that dwarf our economies. Humans will be bewildered observers, wondering how we could have been so blind.

But this Doomberg moment is the unbounding's crucible—the brief, dangerous period when we wield hyper-intelligence with human wisdom (or lack thereof). We hold the Promethean flame, burning bright and hot. Soon it will consume us or illuminate us. For now, it's ours to wield.

Society must become fluent in the language of complex systems, understanding how local optimizations create global problems.*
