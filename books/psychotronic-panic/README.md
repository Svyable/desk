# PSYCHOTRONIC PANIC

**Subtitle:** How intelligence agencies learned to fear a science they could not verify

**Thesis:** Cold War intelligence did not need Soviet psychotronics to work in order for it to have strategic effects. Fragmentary Soviet publications, classified threat reporting, genuine uncertainty about adversary research, and the cost of being surprised could convert weak evidence into real American programs. This book reconstructs that feedback loop without assuming either that psi phenomena were real or that the Soviet threat was invented.

**Evidence posture:** declassified-intelligence / Soviet-publication / history-of-science heavy. U.S. intelligence reporting on Soviet parapsychology and later U.S. anomalous-cognition programs are documented. Soviet and post-Soviet researchers did conduct heterogeneous experiments under labels including bioinformation, bioenergetics, psychotronics and anomalous perturbation. Reliable operational telepathy, psychokinesis, mind control or a deployed Soviet psychotronic weapon remain unconfirmed. The book's `threat-without-capability` model is a speculative synthesis to be tested against the archival record.
