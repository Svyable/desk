# Evidence ledger — THE OCEAN IS WIRED

Working rule: a source earns only the claim it directly supports. Biological sensitivity is not operational range; response is not interpretation; military interest is not deployment; a patent or proposal is not efficacy.

## Class 1 — verified / documented

- R. W. Murray, “Electrical Sensitivity of the Ampullæ of Lorenzini,” *Nature* 187 (1960). https://doi.org/10.1038/187957a0 — electrophysiological evidence for electrical sensitivity. Do not generalize threshold across species or frequencies.
- A. J. Kalmijn, “Electro-perception in Sharks and Rays,” *Nature* 212 (1966). https://doi.org/10.1038/2121232b0 — behavioral/reflex sensitivity to weak alternating fields; the paper reports responses around 0.1 µV/cm in its experimental context.
- Nicholas Bellono, Duncan Leitch & David Julius, “Molecular basis of ancestral vertebrate electroreception,” *Nature* 543 (2017). https://doi.org/10.1038/nature21401 — CaV1.3/BK channel machinery in skate electrosensory cells.
- Bellono, Leitch & Julius, “Molecular tuning of electroreception in sharks and skates,” *Nature* 558 (2018). https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-018-0160-9 — physiological tuning differences; supports mechanism, not a universal behavioral threshold.
- H. R. Brown et al., “Evidence that geomagnetic variations can be detected by lorenzinian ampullae,” *Nature* 277 (1979). https://doi.org/10.1038/277648a0 — direct neurophysiological evidence reported for detection of telluric currents produced by geomagnetic variation. Requires full-text audit before quantitative use.
- NIWC Pacific, U.S. Navy Marine Mammal Program. https://www.niwcpacific.navy.mil/About/Departments/Intelligence-Surveillance-and-Reconnaissance/Marine-Mammal-Program/ — Navy states early work tested more than a dozen marine mammal species plus sharks, rays, sea turtles and marine birds. Evidence of research interest, not operational shark deployment.
- DARPA, Persistent Aquatic Living Sensors (PALS). https://www.darpa.mil/research/programs/persistent-aquatic-living-sensors — documented program intended to combine organisms’ environmental responses with man-made observation/interpretation for underwater-vehicle detection. Program page says complete. Proposal architecture is not proof of fielded capability.

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete

- Shark magnetic-stimulus behavioral experiments report discrimination and impairment effects while attempting to quantify electrical artifacts. Useful evidence that magnetic perception may not reduce cleanly to ampullary induction; mechanism and ecological transfer remain incomplete. Primary: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-017-11459-8
- Subsea-cable ecological literature: retain only studies with measured field geometry/exposure and species-specific outcomes. Separate detectable exposure from individual behavior and population consequence.
- Navigation literature: separate compass use, map use, route fidelity and cue integration. Do not convert any one magnetic manipulation into a complete navigation mechanism.

## Class 3 — disputed / unconfirmed

- Claims that sharks possess a precise global geomagnetic map.
- Claims that U.S. or other militaries fielded covert operational shark electroreception systems absent event-level documentation.
- Claims that subsea power cables cause population-scale migration failure without exposure-response and field evidence.
- Claims that unusual animal behavior around military/UAP events proves electromagnetic or exotic causation.

## Class 4 — speculative synthesis / open conjecture

- **Invisible-channel error:** humans systematically underrate information carried in physical variables we do not natively sense.
- **Borrowed umwelt:** engineering that uses an organism as a sensor inherits the organism’s cue weighting, motivation, adaptation and confounders.
- **Field ecology:** electrosensitive organisms may inhabit an electromagnetic information landscape that anthropogenic infrastructure modifies. This is broader than demonstrated cable effects and must remain labeled synthesis.
- **Translation tax:** converting a biological response into a low-false-positive intelligence product may be harder than achieving raw sensitivity.

## Contradictions / incentive audit

- Military program pages have institutional incentives to describe goals and successes; they do not supply a complete failure denominator.
- Environmental-impact studies may be optimized for regulatory questions rather than sensory mechanism; laboratory studies may exaggerate ecological relevance.
- Popular shark coverage rewards `sixth sense` language and collapses electroreception, magnetoreception and navigation.
- Defense/anomaly narratives reward the inverse error: because a sensory capability is real, rumored applications are treated as retrospectively validated.

## Publication holds

Do not publish a universal electroreception threshold. Do not state a cable-effect distance without a primary field measurement and geometry. Do not call magnetic response a map. Do not describe PALS as operationally deployed unless an authoritative source establishes deployment. Do not imply classified access. Do not use Navy testing of sharks as evidence of weaponization.

## Strongest countermodel

The apparently dramatic synthesis may dissolve into ordinary sensory ecology. Electroreception can be a well-understood near-field predation sense; magnetic responses can be cue-specific or multimodal; cable effects can be local and ecologically minor; PALS can be an exploratory defense program rather than evidence that biology beats hardware. The book survives only if it becomes more precise under this model rather than requiring mystery.

## Defeat conditions

The `field ecology` thesis must narrow if well-controlled field studies show anthropogenic subsea fields are generally below biologically relevant levels at realistic distances or produce no durable behavioral/ecological effects. The `translation tax` thesis weakens if PALS-like systems demonstrate low false-positive, high-specificity vehicle identification across uncontrolled environments. Claims about direct magnetoreception must narrow if artifact-controlled replication consistently supports induction through electroreception instead.
