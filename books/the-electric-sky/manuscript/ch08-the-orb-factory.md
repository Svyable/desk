# Chapter 8 — The Orb Factory

Round is the default shape of ignorance.

That sentence is unfair to actual spheres and useful for everything else.

A point source below the resolving power of an optical system acquires the system's point-spread function. Defocus turns point sources into circles or polygons determined partly by the aperture. Glare inflates bright targets. Atmospheric turbulence smears edges. Compression and sharpening manufacture boundaries. Infrared systems render small biological targets as blobs. A camera focused at infinity can turn nearby insects or dust into luminous soft-edged disks.

Then the witness says orb.

Orb is one of those words that quietly moves evidence between classes. "A light" is phenomenology. "A spherical craft" is ontology. "Orb" sits between them and benefits from both.

AARO's current public morphology data show why the category deserves suspicion. Round/orb/sphere is the leading morphology among reports with morphology data, followed by lights. That may reflect a real prevalence of round objects. It may also reflect what unresolved sources look like. The table is at https://www.aaro.mil/UAP-Cases/UAP-Reporting-Trends/ .

The only responsible move is to distinguish image-roundness from object-roundness.

Object-roundness requires evidence of a physical boundary. Resolved limb structure. Stable angular dimensions across focus changes. Parallax consistent with a finite object. Stereo imaging. Occlusion. Shadow. Radar cross-section compatible with a compact body. Surface detail. Polarization behavior. Something that survives a different optical system.

Without those, sphere is a hypothesis.

This matters because luminous atmospheric events naturally produce compact-looking sources at distance. So do aircraft landing lights. So do planets through turbulence. So do balloons carrying reflective or illuminated payloads. So do satellites near flare geometries. So do pixels.

There is a useful discipline here: every orb claim should have a resolution budget.

How many pixels span the target at native sensor resolution?

Not the enlarged screenshot. Not the stabilized crop. The native target.

If the answer is six pixels, there is no aerodynamic shape to analyze.

This is not skepticism. It is sampling theory.

The same discipline should be applied to color. A white balance pipeline can shift emissions. Saturated pixels lose spectral information. Smartphone computational photography combines frames and adjusts tone. A red orb is not automatically a red-emitting object.

The atmospheric science version of this problem has a better solution: spectrography.

A spectrum can tell you whether light is dominated by familiar atmospheric emissions, thermal continuum, combustion products, LEDs, reflected sunlight or something stranger. It does not solve range or mechanism by itself, but it converts color from adjective into data.

If recurring orb reports matter, the obvious response is to deploy low-cost slitless spectrographs alongside all-sky cameras in high-reporting areas. The technology is not exotic. Meteor networks already exploit spectral observations. Lightning and auroral research do the same.

The question is whether anomaly research wants less ambiguity badly enough to collect the data that would remove it.

Sometimes communities become attached to the orb precisely because it remains low-information.

Low-information objects are hospitable to high-information stories.

The camera gives six pixels. The narrative gives propulsion, intention and origin.

The ratio should run the other way.
