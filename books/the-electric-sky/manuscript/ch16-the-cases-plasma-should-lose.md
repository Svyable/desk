# Chapter 16 — The Cases Plasma Should Lose

A serious natural hypothesis needs a defeat condition.

Here are several.

A daylight event with resolved rigid structure observed from independent calibrated cameras at known range should be difficult to explain as a luminous atmospheric plasma.

A compact object leaving a reproducible aerodynamic wake or displacing cloud in a mechanically coherent way should shift weight toward a solid body.

A recovered material sample with chain of custody, composition and microstructure inconsistent with terrestrial manufacture would move the problem out of atmospheric physics entirely.

A repeated trajectory through quiet air, maintaining dimensions and velocity against measured wind while producing a stable radar cross-section, would strain drifting-aerosol and many plasma models.

A luminous body whose emitted spectrum is dominated by engineered narrowband sources synchronized to maneuvers would be poor evidence for spontaneous atmospheric chemistry.

A transmedium event with independently measured three-dimensional track crossing an air-water boundary, with corresponding hydrodynamic evidence, would not be explained by a projection artifact on one sensor.

These are not impossible standards.

They are what the words already imply.

The atmospheric hypothesis also loses when it becomes more elaborate than the technological one. If the explanation requires a rare plasma, rare propagation regime, rare sensor artifact and rare witness illusion all aligned in one event, Occam has stopped helping.

But Occam's razor is not a counting exercise. A balloon plus parallax may be a simpler causal model than unknown propulsion. A poorly understood electrical event may be simpler than a vehicle if the event occurs in a storm-coupled environment and shares the spectrum of atmospheric emissions.

Evidence decides simplicity after the mechanisms are specified.

The reason to write down defeat conditions is psychological.

Mystery communities reward hypothesis survival. Science rewards useful failure.

Project Condign's plasma emphasis should not become a doctrine. Hessdalen's unresolved lights should not become permanent anomalies. Ball lightning should not be stretched until it covers every luminous sphere. Sensor artifact should not become the skeptic's equivalent of magic.

Each explanation must be permitted to die.

That is how the residual gets cleaner.
