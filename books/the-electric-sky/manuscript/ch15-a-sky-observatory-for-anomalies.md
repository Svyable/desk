# Chapter 15 — A Sky Observatory for Anomalies

The solution to most UAP arguments is embarrassingly physical.

Build a better station.

Not a phone app. Not another reporting form. A station.

Two or more optical cameras separated by a known baseline. Calibrated visible and infrared channels. All-sky coverage. A low-resolution spectrograph always running. Passive RF monitoring. Lightning detection feeds. Local electric-field measurement. Magnetometer. Infrasound. Meteorological station. ADS-B reception. Satellite ephemerides. Precise time synchronization. Enough local storage to preserve raw pre-trigger data.

Then place a second station far enough away to create useful parallax.

The system should classify ordinary traffic first. Aircraft, helicopters, balloons, satellites, meteors, birds, insects, planets, stars, lanterns, drones, cloud reflections, lens flares. If it cannot reliably reject those, it has no business announcing anomalies.

The red team should be cruel.

Launch balloons at dusk.

Fly drones with unusual lighting.

Use infrared emitters.

Create laser scatter where legal and safe.

Drive vehicles along distant ridge roads.

Introduce camera insects.

Replay satellite flares.

Change exposure settings.

Generate RF interference.

The goal is to learn what false mysteries look like before the real archive begins.

This is an important cultural inversion. An anomaly project should spend most of its effort making anomalies disappear.

If ten thousand detections collapse into known categories and three survive, those three become valuable.

The station should also keep every rejected detection.

Otherwise there is no denominator and no way to measure classifier performance.

Hessdalen points toward this model because recurrence made fixed instrumentation possible. NASA's UAP study points toward it because the central deficit is data quality. Modern meteor networks prove that distributed low-cost sky monitoring can operate continuously. Lightning networks prove that transient events can be triangulated at continental scale.

Nothing in the architecture requires exotic technology.

That is its virtue.

The exotic hypothesis should be tested by boring hardware.
