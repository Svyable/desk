# 5 — Organizations Gain a New Species of Worker

Calling an AI a “worker” makes some people uncomfortable because the word carries moral and political meanings developed around human labor.

The discomfort is useful. We should keep it.

But organizations will still need a vocabulary for entities that accept objectives, use tools, coordinate with others, retain local history, produce artifacts, consume resources, and continue operating after the person who delegated the work has gone home.

Whatever word wins, the organizational category is new.

## Not employee, not software

Traditional software is predictable enough that we specify behavior in advance. Traditional employees are autonomous enough that we manage them through goals, norms, incentives, expertise, and accountability.

Agents occupy the uncomfortable middle.

They are software that must often be managed as if they were collaborators, and collaborators that can be copied, paused, inspected, permissioned, benchmarked, and rolled back like software.

This hybridity will produce strange institutions.

A single human may supervise dozens of persistent agents with different scopes. One agent may coordinate other agents. A temporary swarm may form around a project and dissolve at completion. Organizations may package their operating procedures into agent configurations that carry institutional competence into every new initiative.

The org chart becomes partly executable.

## Management becomes interface design

Managers traditionally spend enormous effort moving context between people: clarifying goals, resolving ambiguity, tracking commitments, noticing drift, transmitting decisions, and deciding when to intervene.

Persistent agents do not remove these functions. They expose them.

A vague organization will create vague agents. Contradictory incentives will become contradictory mandates. Unwritten exceptions will surface as failures. Missing ownership will become runaway loops or stalled work.

In this sense, agents are organizational mirrors.

To delegate effectively, a team must know what it wants well enough to express objectives and constraints. To grant autonomy, it must know which failures are tolerable. To evaluate results, it must possess evidence beyond managerial intuition.

Organizations with clear interfaces may gain disproportionately.

## Collaboration changes status

The screenshot also imagines agents collaborating with other agents and people. That matters because collaboration is where intelligence becomes social power.

A brilliant isolated system produces outputs. A networked system participates in coordination.

Once agents can request work from one another, negotiate dependencies, hand off artifacts, and preserve shared state, they begin to resemble an economy inside the organization. Compute, permissions, context, and human attention become resources allocated among machine participants.

This raises mundane questions that are more important than they sound. Who can create an agent? Who pays for its runtime? Which agent can authorize another? What identity signs a commit? Who owns a machine-generated decision? How does an agent know that another agent is trustworthy? What happens when two mandates conflict?

Institutions are largely answers to questions like these.

## Headcount loses meaning

For generations, organizational scale has been approximated by people. Ten employees. Ten thousand employees. A million civil servants.

Persistent agents break the measure.

A five-person company might operate the effective process volume of a hundred-person company. A large enterprise might have fewer humans and vastly more active organizational actors. A researcher might temporarily command more cognitive labor than an old laboratory could employ in a year.

The scarce quantity becomes less obvious.

Perhaps it is human judgment. Perhaps trusted authority. Perhaps capital, compute, proprietary context, distribution, legitimacy, or the ability to choose good objectives.

Probably it is different in every domain.

But once durable machine agency becomes cheap, “How many people work here?” stops telling you how much work can happen here.
