# Back Matter

## The artifact

This book began from a screenshot captured on August 30, 2026. The image showed a Reddit post in r/singularity titled “True if big,” embedding a social post that attributed several claims to reporting about a forthcoming AI model called “GPT-Astra.”

The visible claims were that the model was designed to work for days or weeks, remember corrections, collaborate with other agents and people, operate at a “superhuman, very fast” pace on software, and — in an attributed quotation — become the first model to invent new things “in a way that matters.”

This manuscript deliberately does **not** promote those statements to verified facts. Screenshots collapse provenance; product names, timelines, quotes, and specifications can change. The artifact is used as a timestamp on public expectation.

## Why preserve moments like this?

History is usually reconstructed from launches, papers, financial results, laws, and official statements. But technological expectations often change first in less formal places: group chats, feeds, demos, jokes, rumors, screenshots.

Those artifacts show what became thinkable before it became ordinary.

If durable autonomous AI becomes mundane, the phrase “works for days or weeks” may eventually sound as unremarkable as “always connected.” If it does not, this moment will remain useful for another reason: it will show what people expected, feared, and desired at the edge of the possible.

Either way, keep the screenshot.
