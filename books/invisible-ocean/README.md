# Invisible Ocean

## How Life Learned to See Fields We Barely Notice

Sven Hardy Benson

Thesis: human sensory bandwidth is not a map of physical reality. Sharks detect extremely weak electric fields, magnetotactic bacteria construct intracellular magnetic compasses, and other animals exploit magnetic information through mechanisms that remain under active study. The book follows experimentally demonstrated non-human sensing and asks what information channels biology can exploit that humans ordinarily do not perceive.

Evidence classes: (1) documented facts, (2) credible but incomplete evidence, (3) disputed or unconfirmed claims, and (4) speculative synthesis. The manuscript will not treat evidence for animal electroreception or magnetoreception as evidence for paranormal human perception.

Planned chapters: The Water Is Full of Things You Cannot Feel; A Shark Finds a Battery; The Ampullae; Bacteria Build Compasses; A Crystal Becomes an Organ; Birds and the Radical Pair; Competing Magnetoreception Mechanisms; Sensory Worlds; Human Instrument Bias; When Weak Fields Become Strong Claims; Geophysics and Biology; What Would Count as Human Magnetoreception?; Alien Senses; Reality Has More Channels Than Observers.

Status: early manuscript. Publication hold until primary-source reconstruction, quantitative field-strength tables, mechanism audit, and full-length chapter development are complete.
