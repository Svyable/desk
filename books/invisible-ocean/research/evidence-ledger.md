# Evidence ledger

## Class 1 — verified/documented

- Kalmijn, A.J. (1966), Nature, “Electro-perception in Sharks and Rays.” Reports behavioral sensitivity to very weak electric fields and identifies the ampullary system as the sensory pathway. https://www.nature.com/articles/2121232b0
- Adair, Astumian & Weaver (1998), Chaos, “Detection of weak electric fields by sharks, rays, and skates.” Quantitative biophysical treatment of ampullary electroreception. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12779761/
- Lefèvre & Bazylinski (2013), Microbiology and Molecular Biology Reviews, magnetotactic-bacteria ecology/evolution review. Magnetosomes contain magnetic iron minerals and chains align cells with field lines. https://journals.asm.org/doi/10.1128/mmbr.00021-13
- Uebe & Schüler (2016), Nature Reviews Microbiology, magnetosome biogenesis review. https://www.nature.com/articles/nrmicro.2016.99

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete

- Animal magnetic sensing beyond bacterial magnetotaxis is well supported in multiple taxa, while receptor identity and relative mechanistic contribution can remain unsettled. Later chapters must distinguish behavioral effect, magnetic dependence, receptor candidate, and molecular mechanism rather than use “magnetoreception” as one solved object.

## Class 3 — disputed/unconfirmed

- Claims that ordinary weak environmental electromagnetic variation produces specific complex human experiences require their own controlled evidence. Animal electroreception or magnetoreception is not supporting evidence for those claims.

## Class 4 — speculative synthesis

- Human sensory bandwidth may create a recurring epistemic error: observers classify a signal as implausible because the relevant variable is not directly represented in ordinary human sensation.
- Astrobiology should search not only for Earthlike sensory organs but for any environmental gradient capable of reliable transduction.

## Contradiction audit

- Do not confuse passive magnetic alignment in magnetotactic bacteria with a nervous-system percept.
- Do not assume one magnetic-sensing mechanism across taxa.
- Do not infer human sensitivity from non-human sensitivity.
- Separate detection threshold from ecological relevance.
- Separate field existence from biological transduction.

## Defeat conditions

The broad thesis weakens if supposedly unusual sensory channels collapse under controlled replication or if the proposed environmental information is too noisy to support biologically useful behavior. Individual mechanism claims must be discarded when receptor perturbation, genetic intervention, frequency dependence, or physical modeling contradicts them.
