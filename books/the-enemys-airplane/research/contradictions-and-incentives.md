# Contradictions and incentive audit

## Known tensions to preserve

- Official Air Force histories credit CONSTANT PEG as important to later combat effectiveness, but precise causal attribution across Topgun, Red Flag, aggressor units, weapons, doctrine and Desert Storm should not be simplified.
- Participant recollection can be strong on operational texture and weak on exact dates, program boundaries or acquisition provenance.
- Official declassification narratives may protect still-sensitive acquisition channels or compress institutional disputes; official status is not equivalent to completeness.
- The historical existence of secret MiG exploitation increases the plausibility of secret foreign-materiel programs in general but does not raise an origin claim from human adversary technology to non-human technology.
- Grusch's testimony and AARO's negative historical finding conflict at the level of public conclusion. Neither should be paraphrased as more than its evidentiary form permits.

## Incentives

### Government / military
- protect sources and methods;
- conceal capability gaps and adversary vulnerabilities;
- preserve alliance relationships;
- protect active compartments;
- defend institutional reputation after declassification;
- simplify histories for public communication.

### Whistleblowers / witnesses
- correct perceived wrongdoing or concealment;
- seek lawful oversight;
- preserve clearances and avoid classified disclosure;
- possible reputation, media or commercial incentives, varying by person and not assumed without evidence;
- normal memory distortion over long intervals.

### Contractors
- protect proprietary capability and classified contracts;
- seek future work;
- avoid liability or embarrassment;
- possible institutional prestige from association with advanced programs.

### Journalists / researchers
- incentive toward novelty and narrative coherence;
- source dependence;
- competitive pressure to publish before documentary confirmation;
- countervailing reputational cost for major errors.

### Intelligence services
- possible benefit from exaggerating, concealing or confusing assessments of technical capability;
- possibility of feeding authentic details around a false core claim;
- possibility of allowing folklore to mask conventional programs.

None of these incentives establishes deception. They define alternative hypotheses that should remain visible.
