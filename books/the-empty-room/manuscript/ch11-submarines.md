# Submarines

A submarine creates the opposite of the deprivation chamber in one respect: it is crowded with signals. Machinery, watches, voices, procedures, alarms. Yet it is isolated from the ordinary world, spatially confined, temporally repetitive, and deprived of natural environmental variation.

NASA's later reviews drew on submarine literature because long-duration spaceflight inherits this architecture. A crew can be highly selected, highly motivated, technically busy, and still experience monotony and confinement.

The military relevance of isolation therefore bifurcates. One branch asks how an adversary might exploit it. The other asks how to protect one's own people from it.

The second branch proved more durable.
