# Hallucinations Without Drugs

The deprivation chamber acquired its mythology through visions. Subjects described shapes, scenes, voices, and altered appearances after emerging. The reports are fascinating precisely because no exotic explanation is required to take them seriously.

Perception is inferential before anyone invokes mysticism. Under ordinary conditions, external signals constrain that inference brutally. A chair stays where the chair is. Remove structured input and the constraint weakens. Imagery, memory, expectation, spontaneous neural activity, and ambiguous bodily signals gain relative weight.

This makes deprivation research relevant to anomalous experience without making anomalous claims true. A sincere witness can encounter a vivid event generated partly or wholly within perception. Conversely, the existence of hallucination cannot be used as a universal solvent for reports that include independent measurements.

The chamber teaches calibration, not dismissal.
