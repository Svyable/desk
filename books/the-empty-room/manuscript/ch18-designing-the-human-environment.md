# Designing the Human Environment

The Cold War wanted to know whether an empty room could make a person easier to control. Mars planners need to know whether a full spacecraft can become, psychologically, too empty.

That is the book's collision.

NASA now treats isolation and confinement as one of the major hazards of human spaceflight. Modern analogs manipulate communication delay, workload, lighting, privacy, monotony, resource limits, and autonomy because the crew's environment is part of the vehicle.

The important engineering move is to stop treating human variability as noise outside the system boundary. A habitat has cubic meters, watts, kilograms, carbon dioxide, radiation shielding—and rhythms. It has social topology. It has opportunities for retreat and opportunities for contact. It has windows or simulations of windows. It has meals that can become landmarks. It has boredom budgets whether the designers calculate them or not.

The old deprivation chamber was built by subtracting the world. The future spacecraft has to decide, deliberately, which parts of the world to put back.
