# What Isolation Cannot Do

Sensory deprivation has accumulated claims larger than its evidence. It does not erase a personality on command. It does not prove that repeated messages can install arbitrary durable beliefs. It does not turn every internally generated image into hidden perception. It does not make all people respond alike.

Selection matters. Motivation matters. Duration matters. Expectation matters. Sleep matters. Social context matters. The laboratory literature itself contains inconsistent and sometimes contradictory results.

This is not a disappointing conclusion. A variable technology is exactly what one should expect when the instrument being manipulated is a living adaptive system.

The defeat condition for the book's central synthesis is straightforward: if careful studies show that isolation, confinement, monotony, and sensory restriction add no predictive value once sleep, workload, expectation, and social variables are controlled, then the “empty room” is mostly a misleading historical container. The components, not the room, deserve the theory.
