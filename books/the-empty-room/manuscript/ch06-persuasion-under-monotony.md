# Persuasion Under Monotony

Some early deprivation experiments reported increased susceptibility to persuasive material. That finding became catnip for anyone seeking a laboratory foundation for brainwashing.

But persuasion is not programming. Effect size, persistence, demand characteristics, subject selection, experimental framing, and replication matter. A temporary shift on an attitude measure does not establish durable involuntary conversion.

The useful question is narrower: does reduced stimulation, fatigue, dependency, or monotony change how people process claims? There are plausible routes. Attention can degrade. Motivation can narrow. Repetition can become unusually salient because little else competes with it. Social reference points disappear.

These mechanisms deserve study without the theatrical conclusion that a blank room creates obedient minds.
