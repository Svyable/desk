# Solitary Is Not a Laboratory

Prison isolation is often recruited into sensory-deprivation arguments because the resemblance is visually convenient: one person, one room, too little happening.

But solitary confinement carries coercion, uncertainty, social exclusion, institutional threat, loss of autonomy, often disrupted sleep, and frequently preexisting vulnerability. A laboratory chamber cannot ethically reproduce the package.

The comparison is therefore informative at the level of overlapping stressors and treacherous at the level of causal attribution.

This book will not use prison suffering as an oversized replication of a McGill experiment. It will use it as a warning about ecological validity: human environments acquire meaning from power.
