# The Third Quarter

Older isolation literature described a pattern sometimes called the third-quarter phenomenon: mood or behavior can worsen after the novelty of the beginning has died but before the end feels close enough to organize anticipation cleanly.

The exact universality of this pattern is debatable. The deeper idea is not. Human beings do not experience duration linearly. A mission's psychological geometry changes as its ending becomes imaginable.

That makes time an environmental variable.

A six-month mission is not merely six copies of a month. A prison sentence is not a long laboratory session. A Mars transit with no quick abort is not a longer HERA stay. Knowledge of exit changes the room while leaving every wall in the same place.
