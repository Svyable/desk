# Radar Screens and Vigilance

A radar operator can be surrounded by machinery and still inhabit a perceptually poor world. The screen repeats itself. The expected target is absent almost all the time. The job is to notice the exception.

The 1972 intelligence review explicitly linked early deprivation interest to lapses of attention during monotonous radar watching. That small historical detail changes the genre of the story. Sensory restriction was not merely a dark art. It was human-factors research wearing Cold War clothes.

Vigilance declines are not moral failures. They are properties of sustained attention under particular task conditions. Designing around them means admitting that the operator is part of the sensor system.

The same lesson now applies to security operations centers, drone feeds, autonomous-vehicle supervision, and any interface where a human is asked to watch automation be correct until suddenly it is not.
