# Secondary-history audit map — The Ten-Year Republic

This file is the second-pass reading map for the publication edit. The initial `source-ledger.csv` establishes the factual spine from primary documents and institutional archives. This map adds a serious secondary work for every chapter so the manuscript's causal argument can be tested against historical interpretation rather than only official chronology.

**Status convention**

- **Selected** — bibliographic work identified and publication metadata checked against a publisher, academic, library, or award source.
- **Audit target** — the exact question the publication edit must test against the work.
- A listed work is **not** treated as fully absorbed into the manuscript merely because it appears here. Final release review should record any chapter where the secondary reading materially changes the causal claim, runner-up, or wording.

## 1 — 1776–1785: Declaration of Independence

**Selected:** Pauline Maier, *American Scripture: Making the Declaration of Independence* (Knopf/Vintage, 1997/1998). Publisher record: https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/106260/american-scripture-by-pauline-maier/

**Audit target:** Test the chapter's claim that the Declaration's durable significance lies not only in separation but in the later political afterlife of its language. Distinguish the drafting/adoption history from nineteenth-century sanctification, and keep Yorktown as a real rival rather than making military victory disappear behind text.

## 2 — 1786–1795: Constitution

**Selected:** Pauline Maier, *Ratification: The People Debate the Constitution, 1787–1788* (Simon & Schuster, 2010). Publisher record: https://www.simonandschuster.com/books/Ratification/Pauline-Maier/9781451606362

**Audit target:** Keep the Constitution contingent through ratification rather than inevitable after Philadelphia. Check the manuscript's treatment of Anti-Federalist objections, state convention politics, and the Bill of Rights as the strongest rival event.

## 3 — 1796–1805: Louisiana Purchase

**Selected:** Peter J. Kastor, *The Nation's Crucible: The Louisiana Purchase and the Creation of America* (Yale University Press, 2004). Academic record: https://academic.oup.com/yale-scholarship-online/book/23081

**Audit target:** Test the claim that the purchase changed American scale by forcing incorporation of Native, African American, and Francophone populations as well as territory. Keep constitutional uncertainty and incorporation—not real-estate bargain rhetoric—at the center.

## 4 — 1806–1815: War of 1812

**Selected:** Alan Taylor, *The Civil War of 1812: American Citizens, British Subjects, Irish Rebels, & Indian Allies* (Knopf/Vintage, 2010/2011). Publisher record: https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/176724/the-civil-war-of-1812-by-alan-taylor/

**Audit target:** Preserve the war's porous loyalties and continental character. Test the chapter's postwar-nationalism argument against the costs borne by Native nations and against any overly clean U.S.-versus-Britain framing.

## 5 — 1816–1825: Missouri Compromise

**Selected:** Robert Pierce Forbes, *The Missouri Compromise and Its Aftermath: Slavery and the Meaning of America* (University of North Carolina Press, 2007; current UNC edition). Publisher record: https://uncpress.org/9798890870728/the-missouri-compromise-and-its-aftermath/

**Audit target:** Test the chapter's claim that Missouri converted slavery into explicit territorial and Senate arithmetic. Forbes's reinterpretation requires particular attention to antislavery sentiment, party formation, Monroe's role, and the danger of describing the compromise as merely a neat North/South bargain.

## 6 — 1826–1835: Indian Removal Act

**Selected:** Claudio Saunt, *Unworthy Republic: The Dispossession of Native Americans and the Road to Indian Territory* (W. W. Norton, 2020). Bibliographic record: https://books.google.com/books/about/Unworthy_Republic.html?id=SSCfDwAAQBAJ

**Audit target:** Test the manuscript against the proposition that removal was a contested political project rather than inevitable westward expansion. Deepen the links among federal administration, slaveholding interests, land speculation, Wall Street finance, and nation-specific Native resistance.

## 7 — 1836–1845: Annexation of Texas

**Selected:** Andrew J. Torget, *Seeds of Empire: Cotton, Slavery, and the Transformation of the Texas Borderlands, 1800–1850* (University of North Carolina Press, 2015). Publisher record: https://uncpress.org/9798890848093/seeds-of-empire/

**Audit target:** Test the chapter's expanded treatment of cotton, slavery, Mexican federalism, Tejano politics, Native power, and Anglo migration. Avoid either a simple liberty narrative or a single-cause slavery narrative; show how those forces interacted before annexation imported the border dispute into U.S. policy.

## 8 — 1846–1855: Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo

**Selected:** Amy S. Greenberg, *A Wicked War: Polk, Clay, Lincoln, and the 1846 U.S. Invasion of Mexico* (Knopf/Vintage, 2012/2013). Publisher record: https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/200246/a-wicked-war-by-amy-s-greenberg/

**Audit target:** Stress-test the causal chain from annexation and Polk's border policy to war and cession. Preserve Mexican political agency, American antiwar politics, treaty implementation, and the postwar reopening of the slavery question.

## 9 — 1856–1865: Civil War and emancipation

**Selected:** James Oakes, *Freedom National: The Destruction of Slavery in the United States, 1861–1865* (W. W. Norton, 2013). National Book Foundation record: https://www.nationalbook.org/books/freedom-national-the-destruction-of-slavery-in-the-united-states-1861-1865/

**Audit target:** Test the chapter's synthesis of Union policy, enslaved people's self-liberation, Black military service, executive action, congressional law, and the Thirteenth Amendment. Avoid a story in which emancipation is either entirely presidential or entirely spontaneous.

## 10 — 1866–1875: Fourteenth Amendment

**Selected:** Eric Foner, *The Second Founding: How the Civil War and Reconstruction Remade the Constitution* (W. W. Norton, 2019). Bibliographic record: https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Second_Founding.html?id=W_yKDwAAQBAJ

**Audit target:** Check the amendment as a Reconstruction settlement before treating its later doctrinal life. Separate what the ratification generation was trying to do from later incorporation, equal-protection, due-process, and Section Three jurisprudence.

## 11 — 1876–1885: Election settlement and end of Reconstruction

**Selected:** Eric Foner, *Reconstruction: America's Unfinished Revolution, 1863–1877* (Harper & Row, 1988; later Harper editions). Bibliographic record: https://books.google.com/books/about/Reconstruction.html?id=cwVkgrvctCcC

**Audit target:** Keep the manuscript's caution about the phrase “Compromise of 1877.” Test whether the narrative overstates a single bargain when federal retreat, white supremacist violence, economic crisis, judicial narrowing, and northern political exhaustion were already interacting.

## 12 — 1886–1895: Sherman Antitrust Act

**Selected:** Richard White, *Railroaded: The Transcontinentals and the Making of Modern America* (W. W. Norton, 2011). Author/project record: https://web.stanford.edu/group/spatialhistory/railroaded/book.html

**Audit target:** Use the railroad experience to test the chapter's central scale mismatch: national corporate power outrunning state regulation. Keep the act's weak early enforcement, labor applications, and the tension between efficiency and dominance visible.

## 13 — 1896–1905: Spanish-American War

**Selected:** Daniel Immerwahr, *How to Hide an Empire: A History of the Greater United States* (Farrar, Straus and Giroux, 2019). Publisher record: https://us.macmillan.com/books/9780374715120/howtohideanempire/

**Audit target:** Test the shift from continental expansion toward overseas possession and the constitutional problem of territories not automatically headed toward statehood. Keep Puerto Rico, Guam, Cuba, and the Philippines distinct rather than collapsing them into one imperial status.

## 14 — 1906–1915: Federal Reserve Act

**Selected:** Roger Lowenstein, *America's Bank: The Epic Struggle to Create the Federal Reserve* (Penguin, 2015/2016). Publisher record: https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/313479/americas-bank-by-roger-lowenstein/

**Audit target:** Test the manuscript's claim that the Fed was a federalist compromise produced by recurrent financial panic and mistrust of both Wall Street and centralized government. Keep the distance between the 1913 institution and the later modern Fed explicit.

## 15 — 1916–1925: Nineteenth Amendment

**Selected:** Ellen Carol DuBois, *Suffrage: Women's Long Battle for the Vote* (Simon & Schuster, 2020). Publisher record: https://www.simonandschuster.com/books/Suffrage/Ellen-Carol-DuBois/9781501165184

**Audit target:** Check the full movement coalition and its fractures over race, federalism, tactics, and party strategy. Keep the distinction between removal of sex as a voting barrier and actual ballot access for Black, Native, Asian American, and other women.

## 16 — 1926–1935: Great Depression / banking collapse

**Selected:** David M. Kennedy, *Freedom from Fear: The American People in Depression and War, 1929–1945* (Oxford University Press, 1999). Bibliographic record: https://books.google.com/books/about/Freedom_from_Fear.html?id=fehZMhL5T_EC

**Audit target:** Keep the 1933 bank holiday as the chapter's concrete hinge without turning it into a single-cause explanation of the Depression. Test the manuscript's account of New Deal experimentation, institutional change, and limits against a broad synthesis.

## 17 — 1936–1945: Pearl Harbor

**Selected:** Roberta Wohlstetter, *Pearl Harbor: Warning and Decision* (Stanford University Press, 1962). Bibliographic record: https://books.google.com/books/about/Pearl_Harbor.html?id=pO4JxYdXP04C

**Companion synthesis:** David M. Kennedy, *Freedom from Fear*.

**Audit target:** Test the chapter's treatment of intelligence warning, strategic surprise, and hindsight. Preserve the strongest rival—the atomic bombings—without implying that Pearl Harbor alone created every element of the wartime state.

## 18 — 1946–1955: Brown v. Board of Education

**Selected:** Richard Kluger, *Simple Justice: The History of Brown v. Board of Education and Black America's Struggle for Equality* (Knopf, 1975/1976; Vintage reissue). Publisher record: https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/93824/simple-justice-by-richard-kluger/

**Audit target:** Keep the long NAACP legal strategy, plaintiffs, local communities, and enforcement problem visible so the Supreme Court does not become the sole hero of desegregation.

## 19 — 1956–1965: Civil Rights Act of 1964

**Selected:** Clay Risen, *The Bill of the Century: The Epic Battle for the Civil Rights Act* (Bloomsbury Press, 2014). Bibliographic record: https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Bill_of_the_Century.html?id=VBzgAgAAQBAJ

**Audit target:** Test the legislative-coalition story: movement pressure, Kennedy administration development, Johnson's use of presidential power, House strategy, Dirksen and Republican votes, Senate filibuster, and cloture. Avoid reducing passage either to one president or to inevitability after Birmingham.

## 20 — 1966–1975: Watergate

**Selected:** Garrett M. Graff, *Watergate: A New History* (Avid Reader Press/Simon & Schuster, 2022). Author/publisher record: https://www.garrettgraff.com/books/watergate/

**Audit target:** Test the chapter's claim that the defining change was institutional accountability and the later trust deficit rather than simply Nixon's resignation. Keep the Pentagon Papers, campaign finance, Senate investigation, special prosecutor, tapes, Court ruling, and congressional impeachment process connected.

## 21 — 1976–1985: Volcker anti-inflation turn

**Selected:** William L. Silber, *Volcker: The Triumph of Persistence* (Bloomsbury Press, 2012). Author record: https://williamlsilber.com/books/volcker

**Audit target:** Stress-test the claim that monetary stabilization created part of the environment in which the Reagan political order consolidated. Preserve distributional costs—unemployment, manufacturing distress, farm and developing-country debt—and do not write the result as painless technocratic success.

## 22 — 1986–1995: Fall of the Berlin Wall / end of the Cold War

**Selected:** M. E. Sarotte, *Not One Inch: America, Russia, and the Making of Post-Cold War Stalemate* (Yale University Press, 2021/2022). Publisher record: https://yalebooks.yale.edu/book/9780300268034/not-one-inch/

**Audit target:** Keep the Soviet and Eastern European actors central and resist a presidential-credit story in which the United States simply “wins” the Cold War by policy choice. Trace the post-1989 settlement forward into NATO enlargement and later tension while preserving the early web as a serious rival.

## 23 — 1996–2005: September 11 attacks

**Selected:** Lawrence Wright, *The Looming Tower: Al-Qaeda and the Road to 9/11* (Knopf/Vintage, 2006). Publisher record: https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/194238/the-looming-tower-pulitzer-prize-winner-by-lawrence-wright/9780307266088/

**Audit target:** Test the pre-attack intelligence and organizational history against the 9/11 Commission rather than relying only on the attack day's chronology. Separate al-Qaeda's development, interagency failure, the attacks themselves, and later policy choices that were responses rather than inevitable consequences.

## 24 — 2006–2015: Financial crisis of 2008

**Selected:** Adam Tooze, *Crashed: How a Decade of Financial Crises Changed the World* (Viking/Penguin, 2018). Publisher record: https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/301357/crashed-by-adam-tooze/

**Competing mechanism:** Gary B. Gorton, *Slapped by the Invisible Hand: The Panic of 2007* (Oxford University Press, 2010). Bibliographic record: https://books.google.com/books/about/Slapped_by_the_Invisible_Hand.html?id=lUDM4pd7hlwC

**Audit target:** Explicitly compare competing explanations: mortgage origination and securitization, shadow-banking runs, leverage, ratings, regulatory failure, global dollar funding, and policy response. Do not let Lehman become a monocausal explanation merely because it provides the cleanest date.

## 25 — 2016–2025: COVID-19 pandemic

**Selected economic history:** Adam Tooze, *Shutdown: How Covid Shook the World's Economy* (Allen Lane/Penguin, 2021). Publisher record: https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/669575/shutdown-by-adam-tooze/

**Selected institutional narrative:** Michael Lewis, *The Premonition: A Pandemic Story* (W. W. Norton, 2021). Bibliographic record: https://books.google.com/books?id=0IHBzgEACAAJ

**Audit target:** Because this is the least historically settled chapter, do not rely on instant-history books alone. Pair them with revised CDC, BLS, Census, Federal Reserve, educational, mortality, vaccination, and Long-COVID datasets during final fact checking. Re-run the runner-up test against January 6, *Dobbs*, the 2016 political realignment, and generative AI immediately before first Shelf release.

## Cross-chapter publication checks

The secondary pass should also test recurring claims that cross individual chapter boundaries:

1. **Scale mismatch:** whether institutions repeatedly emerge because older governance structures become too small for new geographic, economic, technological, or military scale.
2. **Crisis as state-building:** whether the manuscript overgeneralizes from Constitution, Civil War, Depression, World War II, 2008, and COVID into a claim that every crisis produces durable capacity.
3. **Rights versus enforcement:** whether the Declaration, Reconstruction amendments, *Brown*, Civil Rights Act, and Voting Rights Act are distinguished clearly enough between legal principle and administrative enforcement.
4. **Expansion and coercion:** whether territorial growth is consistently narrated from Native, enslaved, Mexican, territorial, and imperial-subject perspectives rather than as a federal map exercise.
5. **Hindsight control:** whether chapters retain the uncertainty contemporary actors faced and avoid making later outcomes read as foreordained.
6. **Runner-up integrity:** whether each losing event is argued strongly enough that the selected winner actually has to earn its slot.

The release review should be updated when this audit changes any chapter's selection, causal framing, or high-risk factual claim.
