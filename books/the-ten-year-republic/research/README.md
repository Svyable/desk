# Research notes — The Ten-Year Republic

This folder is the factual and editorial trail for *The Ten-Year Republic*.

## Research rules

1. Each chapter is an argument for one defining event in one ten-year window, not a claim of objective historical measurement.
2. Concrete dates, vote counts, territorial figures, casualty figures, legal holdings, economic statistics, and attributed statements must be traceable to a source in `source-ledger.csv` or to a chapter-specific source added later.
3. Prefer primary documents and institutional archives for what happened: National Archives, Library of Congress, congressional historical offices, Supreme Court records, Federal Reserve History, Department of State Office of the Historian, National Park Service, CDC, the 9/11 Commission, and the Financial Crisis Inquiry Commission.
4. Use serious secondary histories to interpret causation and to test the manuscript against competing explanations. Do not turn a source's interpretation into settled fact merely because it is convenient to the chapter thesis.
5. Never invent dialogue, private thoughts, room details, weather, clothing, facial expressions, or other scene material not supported by the record.
6. Distinguish what people knew at the time from what later outcomes make obvious.
7. Preserve counter-evidence. Every chapter brief contains a runner-up event and a falsification question. If later research shows the runner-up had greater downstream reach, change the chapter choice.
8. Treat Indigenous nations, enslaved people, freedpeople, immigrants, workers, civilians, and other affected populations as historical actors rather than scenery around presidents and institutions.
9. Use exact quotations sparingly and only when checked against a source. Paraphrase by default.
10. The manuscript may make a strong judgment, but the research trail must show where that judgment could be wrong.

## Selection test

The working selection test asks four questions:

- **Institutional reach:** Did the event change the rules, powers, borders, or machinery under which later events occurred?
- **Breadth:** How many domains of American life did it affect—politics, rights, economy, society, territory, security, technology, or global position?
- **Duration:** Did its effects persist beyond the immediate crisis or administration?
- **Path dependence:** Did it make later outcomes materially easier, harder, or newly possible?

There is no numerical score. The categories are prompts for judgment, not pseudo-precision.

## Novelty check

A September 2026 web check found existing books and articles organized around American turning points and the 250th anniversary, including general “moments that made America” treatments. The manuscript therefore does **not** claim that no one has written a turning-points history or an America-at-250 book. Its specific organizing device is narrower: twenty-five consecutive ten-year windows beginning with 1776 rather than conventional calendar decades, with one defended selection and one explicit rival for each window.
