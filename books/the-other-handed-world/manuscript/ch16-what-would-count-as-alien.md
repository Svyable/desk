# What Would Count as Alien

A returned sample contains amino acids.

That sentence used to sound extraordinary. Meteorites made it ordinary. Amino acids can form abiotically and arrive from space. Finding glycine on another world does not settle biology.

Suppose instead the sample contains a strong enantiomeric excess. Better, but still not decisive. Murchison and other meteorites show that prebiotic chemistry can become chiral without life. The magnitude, compound distribution, isotopic context and geological setting matter.

Suppose many classes of complex molecules are nearly homochiral and coordinated in the opposite direction from terrestrial biology.

Now the argument changes.

The point is not that D-amino-acid proteins would be aliens by definition. Humans can synthesize D-proteins. A spacecraft can contaminate a sample with engineered molecules. Chain of custody remains essential. The point is that a coherent mirror biochemical network would be difficult to explain by casual terrestrial contamination because ordinary life does not build one.

This gives chirality an unusual evidentiary role. It can be both a contamination discriminator and a biosignature candidate.

But only if the instrument actually measures it.

Many biological assays are intentionally biased. They exploit enzymes because enzymes are sensitive and selective. That selectivity becomes a liability when the target may be mirrored. A negative result can mean no target, or it can mean the molecular lock did not fit.

The same problem appears in UAP and anomalous-materials research in a different form: an instrument is never a neutral window. It encodes assumptions about what kind of thing is worth detecting. Mirror biology provides a cleaner laboratory demonstration because the incompatibility can be built and tested without metaphysics.

A robust alien-life claim would therefore need converging evidence. Chiral distributions measured by non-biological analytical chemistry. Repeating polymers or structures. Isotopic patterns inconsistent with simple abiotic synthesis. Compartmentalization. Energy use. Replication if safely observable. Environmental context showing the material belongs where it was found.

The more extraordinary the claim, the more boring the chain of custody should become.

Mirror life also teaches the opposite lesson: familiar chemistry can hide radical biological difference. An alien microbe may not need an exotic element or impossible energy source to be genuinely foreign. Reverse enough molecular interfaces and terrestrial biology can lose compatibility while carbon chemistry remains recognizable.

That is a useful antidote to science fiction's obsession with exotic substrates. The strangest alien we can currently describe with experimental support is made of the same kinds of atoms, using mirror versions of molecules we already know.

Alienness can be geometric.
