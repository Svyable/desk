# Back Matter

## Evidence note

This manuscript distinguishes four classes throughout the research apparatus: documented experimental facts; credible but incomplete scientific inference; disputed or model-sensitive claims; and speculative synthesis. A self-replicating mirror bacterium has not been reported in the public scientific literature. Risk claims about such an organism are therefore prospective and mechanistic, not observations of an existing organism.

## Chronology

- **1848** — Louis Pasteur's tartrate work establishes a foundational connection between molecular asymmetry and optical activity.
- **1969** — The Murchison carbonaceous meteorite falls in Australia and becomes a major source for extraterrestrial organic chemistry.
- **1997** — Cronin and Pizzarello report L-enantiomeric excesses in several non-protein meteoritic amino acids.
- **2016** — Wang et al. report a synthetic mirror molecular system capable of mirror-image genetic replication and transcription.
- **2017** — A larger D-amino-acid polymerase supports mirror-image PCR on L-DNA.
- **2021** — Fan, Deng and Zhu report high-fidelity mirror polymerase work enabling kilobase-scale L-DNA and bioorthogonal information storage.
- **2024** — Automated flow synthesis expands access to D-protein targets for mirror-image ligand discovery.
- **December 2024** — Adamala et al. publish `Confronting risks of mirror life` in *Science* with an accompanying technical report on mirror bacteria.
- **March 9, 2026** — The UN Secretary-General's Scientific Advisory Board publishes a brief calling for proactive multilateral governance and clear red lines around self-replicating mirror life.
- **August 31, 2026** — A biosafety-policy analysis in *Applied Biosafety* argues that mirror life is a useful case for adaptive risk governance before capability exists.

## What would defeat or narrow the thesis

The book's risk thesis would need substantial narrowing if future work showed that robust mirror organisms cannot access environmentally common resources; that broad classes of natural predators, immune mechanisms or degradative pathways remain effective despite chiral inversion; that reliable containment and treatment can be demonstrated without creating comparable new hazards; or that the engineering barriers make autonomous mirror life physically implausible on relevant timescales.

The book's historical thesis—that biological handedness is contingent in direction even if homochirality is functionally important—would need revision if mirror molecular systems consistently failed for a fundamental stereochemical reason rather than for engineering difficulty. Existing mirror polymerase and D-protein work already argues against a simple version of that claim.
