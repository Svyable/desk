# Contradictions, Incentives, and Failure Modes

## Builder incentives

Researchers developing mirror peptides, proteins and nucleic acids have real biomedical and technical incentives to emphasize useful orthogonality. The fact that several prominent builders later coauthored the 2024 warning strengthens the relevance of their concern but does not make their forward risk models automatically correct.

## Catastrophe incentives

Biosecurity arguments gain attention from catastrophic framing. Claims such as `immune systems would be useless` or `mirror life would destroy the ecosystem` should be unpacked into specific mechanisms and conditional assumptions. The manuscript avoids treating worst-case language as an observed outcome.

## Technology-timeline incentives

Synthetic biology has a history of both unexpectedly rapid advances and overoptimistic forecasts. Claims that mirror bacteria are `ten years away` are planning estimates, not deadlines.

## Origin-of-life overreach

Meteoritic L-enantiomeric excess is evidence that asymmetric organic chemistry predates terrestrial biology. It does not establish that meteorites caused life's handedness, much less panspermia.

## Astrobiology overreach

Opposite chirality would be an interesting biosignature, not proof of extraterrestrial life by itself. Abiotic chemistry can create enantiomeric excess, and contamination must be excluded.

## Governance incentive

Institutions gain legitimacy by appearing anticipatory after recent pandemic and biotechnology debates. The UN's 2026 engagement is evidence of policy attention, not independent experimental validation of the hazard model.

## Known unresolved tension

Orthogonality can increase safety when it creates dependence on synthetic nutrients or prevents gene exchange, while the same orthogonality may reduce predation and molecular degradation. Any manuscript claim that mirror biology is inherently contained or inherently uncontrollable is too broad.
