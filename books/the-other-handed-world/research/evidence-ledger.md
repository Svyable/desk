# Evidence Ledger

## Class 1 — Verified / documented

- All known terrestrial life uses a strongly conserved homochiral convention: proteins overwhelmingly use L-amino acids; DNA/RNA backbones use D-sugars.
- Extraterrestrial amino acids in carbonaceous meteorites can exhibit measured enantiomeric excesses.
- Chemically synthesized D-proteins can fold and function as mirror counterparts of natural proteins.
- Published mirror polymerases have catalyzed replication/transcription of mirror nucleic acids; high-fidelity systems have assembled kilobase-scale L-DNA.
- Mirror biomolecules can resist degradation by many natural enzymes and have legitimate therapeutic/information-storage applications.
- Adamala et al. published a 2024 Science warning and detailed technical assessment of mirror-bacteria feasibility and risk.
- The UN Scientific Advisory Board published a mirror-life governance brief on March 9, 2026.
- No self-replicating mirror bacterium is documented in the public scientific literature reviewed for this draft.

## Class 2 — Credible but incomplete

- A sufficiently robust mirror bacterium could evade important components of innate/adaptive immune recognition, natural proteases/nucleases, bacteriophages and protist predation.
- Environmental enemy release could allow unusual persistence or spread under some metabolic designs.
- Technical barriers to mirror translation and whole-cell integration may erode substantially with advances in chemical protein synthesis and synthetic biology.
- Meteoritic enantiomeric excess may have contributed to, but does not uniquely explain, terrestrial homochirality.

## Class 3 — Disputed / unconfirmed

- Mirror bacteria would be universally invisible to immune systems.
- Existing antibiotics would all be useless.
- A released mirror organism would inevitably become globally invasive.
- Mirror life would necessarily cause human extinction or ecosystem collapse.
- Current research groups secretly possess autonomous mirror organisms.

## Class 4 — Speculative synthesis / open conjecture

- Biological chirality may be best understood as an ancient frozen standard whose direction was contingent but whose later consistency became mandatory.
- A naturally evolved extraterrestrial biosphere could use the opposite chiral convention while remaining carbon-based and otherwise chemically familiar.
- Mirror systems below autonomous replication could serve as valuable astrobiology detector controls without crossing the highest-risk threshold.
- Mirror-life governance may become a model for scientific red lines established before a capability exists rather than after an incident.
