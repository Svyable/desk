# The Body Is an Antenna

**Subtitle:** The Strange Physics of Life in an Electromagnetic World

**Author:** Sven Hardy Benson

**Status:** Drafting

## Thesis

Biology is not electromagnetically inert. Living systems contain charge separation, ion currents, radical pairs, electrically excitable membranes and proteins whose chemistry can respond to weak fields. That documented fact sits uncomfortably close to a century of exaggerated claims about invisible influence. This book asks a harder question than whether “EMF affects biology”: **what information, if any, can a living system actually extract from a weak electromagnetic field, through what receptor, at what signal-to-noise ratio, and with what behavioral consequence?**

The collision is unusually productive. Animal magnetoreception and radical-pair chemistry establish that evolution can exploit field-sensitive physics. DARPA’s completed RadioBio program explicitly asked whether purposeful electromagnetic signaling occurs between cells. A 2026 Nature Biotechnology paper demonstrates radio-wave manipulation of spin chemistry in flavoproteins. None of those facts establishes endogenous radio communication, remote mind control, a human magnetic sense, or operational psychotronic capability. The book is about the distance between those propositions.
