# North Is Not Up

## Working subtitle

**Magnetic Animals, Quantum Chemistry, and the Sense Humans May Still Have**

## Thesis

Magnetoreception is a useful corrective to the human habit of treating our conscious sensorium as a map of what biology can detect. Animals demonstrably extract directional information from Earth's weak magnetic field; a migratory bird cryptochrome is magnetically sensitive in vitro; and a 2019 human EEG experiment reported direction-selective responses to Earth-strength field rotations without demonstrating conscious perception or navigation. The collision is more interesting than either "quantum birds" or "humans have a sixth sense": biology may contain channels whose transduction, neural processing, conscious availability, and behavioral usefulness are four different questions.

## Evidence posture

1. **Verified/documented:** animal magnetoreception is experimentally established across multiple taxa; radical-pair chemistry is a real physical mechanism; European-robin CRY4 showed magnetic sensitivity in vitro; the 2019 eNeuro experiment reported repeatable alpha-band responses under specified geomagnetic rotations.
2. **Credible but incomplete:** CRY4 is a leading candidate for the avian compass but the complete in-vivo receptor/transduction chain remains unresolved; the human EEG result is provocative but does not by itself establish a human magnetic sense in the behavioral meaning of the word.
3. **Disputed/unconfirmed:** robust conscious human magnetic orientation, geomagnetic effects on mood or cognition, and broad claims that magnetic sensitivity explains anomalous human experiences.
4. **Synthesis/open conjecture:** humans may retain biologically processed environmental channels that rarely or never enter conscious awareness; the history of magnetoreception may reveal a general category error in consciousness research—equating absence from report with absence from nervous-system processing.
