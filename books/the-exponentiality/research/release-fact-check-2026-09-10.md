# Release fact-check — 2026-09-10

This note records the final current-source refresh for the Desk revision being prepared for Shelf release. It focuses on the manuscript’s time-sensitive institutional and scientific examples rather than treating model-release cadence as the book’s thesis.

## Human oversight / institutional speed

Chapter 12’s distinction between nominal human presence and effective human oversight remains supported by current authoritative sources.

- The consolidated EU Artificial Intelligence Act text dated July 27, 2026 requires covered high-risk AI systems to permit **effective** human oversight and makes oversight measures commensurate with risk, autonomy, and context. Article 14 describes powers including understanding limitations, avoiding automation bias, interpreting outputs, disregarding or overriding outputs, and interrupting systems where appropriate: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:02024R1689-20260727
- NIST’s current AI Risk Management Framework materials continue to emphasize clearly defined human roles and responsibilities and context-dependent human-AI configurations. NIST also states that AI RMF 1.0 is being revised, so the book should treat it as current guidance rather than a permanent final framework: https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework and https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/appendices/app-c-ai-risk-management-and-human-ai-interaction/

### Boundary preserved

These sources support the conceptual claim that oversight must include real powers and capacity. They do not establish that a specific human-review architecture is empirically optimal, and they do not justify a universal requirement for case-by-case manual approval.

## Scientific discovery / physical bottlenecks

The book’s argument that improved computational reasoning can shift scarcity toward physical experiment throughput remains supported by current Berkeley Lab work.

- Berkeley Lab describes A-Lab as an example of AI plus automated laboratory systems used to accelerate materials discovery: https://www.lbl.gov/research/ai/
- Berkeley Lab’s August 3, 2026 report on a new solid-state reaction model says synthesis can remain a weeks-to-years trial-and-error bottleneck even when computational tools can identify promising materials, and describes modeling work intended to accelerate the path from prediction to successful synthesis: https://newscenter.lbl.gov/2026/08/03/new-ai-modeling-approach-accelerates-the-development-of-advanced-materials/
- The Ceder group describes A-Lab as a closed-loop autonomous system combining robotic synthesis and characterization, machine-learned interpretation, and AI-enabled decision-making: https://ceder.berkeley.edu/research-areas/autonomous-experimentation-for-accelerated-materials-discovery/

### Boundary preserved

A-Lab is evidence that laboratories can integrate AI, robotics, and closed-loop experimentation. It is not evidence that scientific discovery as a whole has become autonomous, nor that physical validation ceases to be a bottleneck.

## Release judgment

No checked authoritative source requires a material correction to the manuscript’s framing of institutional oversight or scientific bottleneck migration. The release should continue to present *The Exponentiality* as a framework for changing bottlenecks rather than a forecast that every capability improves exponentially or that institutions should operate at machine speed.
