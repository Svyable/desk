# A Retrieval Claim That Could Win

What evidence would be enough?

The question is usually answered theatrically: a craft on the White House lawn, an alien body, a presidential confession. Those would work. They are not necessary.

A retrieval claim could win incrementally.

Start with a direct witness whose identity, assignment, access, and chronology can be independently verified. Add contemporaneous records placing the witness at the claimed facility and connecting the facility to a technical program. Add a physical sample with documented custody from the program. Analyze split portions at independent laboratories blinded to provenance. Obtain measurements that exclude known terrestrial industrial processes under predeclared criteria. Recover a technical report from the same program whose measurements match the modern sample. Find another witness from a different organizational path who supplies details not previously public. Establish that those details predict a record discovered later.

At some point the posterior moves.

Notice that no single step requires trust in a government press office or a celebrity whistleblower. The evidentiary channels are partially independent.

The same design protects against false positives. A sincere witness can be wrong without making the sample anomalous. A strange sample can be industrial without making the witness dishonest. A real secret program can exist without possessing non-human hardware.

The hypothesis wins only when the pieces close together.

This is how extraordinary claims should be treated if we actually want one to have a chance.
