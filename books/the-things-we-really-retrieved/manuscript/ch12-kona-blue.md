# KONA BLUE

KONA BLUE is one of the strangest controls in the entire subject because it was, according to the public record, a proposed secret program for something its later mythology treated as already accomplished.

AARO’s public records describe KONA BLUE as a proposed Department of Homeland Security Prospective Special Access Program. Supporters, emerging from the AAWSAP/AATIP milieu, wanted a compartment that could resume anomalous research and potentially receive and reverse-engineer recovered off-world technology they hoped might be transferred to it. DHS did not approve the program. AARO states that KONA BLUE received neither material nor funding. [AARO’s UAP records page](https://www.aaro.mil/UAP-Records/) summarizes that history and links the released documentation.

The distinction between a plan and a possession is the whole chapter.

Organizations write plans for capabilities they do not have. Militaries draft requirements for weapons not yet built. Scientists propose laboratories for samples not yet collected. Intelligence agencies create collection plans for targets they may never access.

A document describing procedures for “non-human biologics” would therefore be extraordinary evidence of bureaucratic interest in that concept. It would not, by itself, be evidence that non-human biologics existed in custody.

KONA BLUE demonstrates how an authentic paper trail can become a false material trail if tense is lost.

The program is also a useful lesson in circularity. People who believe hidden material exists may design a program to receive it. Later, the existence of the proposed receiving program can be cited as evidence that hidden material existed. The conclusion has been smuggled into the premise.

This feedback loop is not unique to UAPs. Intelligence history is full of threat assessments generating collection programs whose existence later gets mistaken for proof of the assessed threat. Research programs do the same with speculative hypotheses.

The cure is brutally simple: ask what entered the door.

No amount of proposal language substitutes for a custody record.
