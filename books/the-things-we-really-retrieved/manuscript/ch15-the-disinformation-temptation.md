# The Disinformation Temptation

Once secrecy enters the room, disinformation soon follows as an explanation for everything inconvenient.

Perhaps the government seeded UFO stories to hide stealth aircraft. Perhaps intelligence officers encouraged absurd claims to discredit real witnesses. Perhaps foreign services injected stories to probe American collection systems. Perhaps none of those things happened in the specific case being discussed.

All are historically plausible classes of behavior. Plausibility is cheap.

Intelligence services use deception. Cover stories are documented in programs such as AZORIAN. Military deception has an enormous literature. But the existence of deception as a tradecraft does not license the researcher to invent a particular deception operation when evidence is missing.

This rule is essential because disinformation is a universal solvent. Any contradictory witness can be an agent. Any denial can be cover. Any absurdity can be deliberate contamination. A theory that uses deception this way becomes impossible to falsify.

So require a mechanism and a trace. Who had the objective? What audience was targeted? What channel carried the story? What behavior was the deception meant to induce? Did contemporaneous records indicate the campaign? Did the story actually protect the sensitive capability?

AZORIAN’s Hughes mining cover passes those tests. It explains observable behavior and is documented by the institution that used it.

Most alleged UFO disinformation campaigns do not yet meet that bar.

The disciplined position is not that intelligence services never manipulate anomaly narratives. It is that every such attribution remains a claim until anchored to evidence.
