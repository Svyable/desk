# The Witness Who Is Not Read In

David Grusch’s July 26, 2023 congressional testimony changed the political status of crash-retrieval allegations without changing their public evidentiary status in the same degree.

Before the House Oversight subcommittee, Grusch described information he said had been provided to him during official work and alleged a longstanding crash-retrieval and reverse-engineering program. The hearing and his prepared statement are public records. [The committee page](https://oversight.house.gov/hearing/unidentified-anomalous-phenomena-implications-on-national-security-public-safety-and-government-transparency/) establishes what was said and under what institutional setting.

Testimony under oath matters. It is not physical proof.

The distinction is especially important because much of the retrieval claim was secondhand in public. Grusch described speaking with people he regarded as credible and with direct knowledge. The identities, documents, locations, and corroborating material necessary to independently evaluate the strongest allegations were not placed into the public record at the hearing.

This does not make the testimony worthless. It gives it a specific evidence class: credible but incomplete testimony containing disputed allegations that require independent confirmation.

The foreign-materiel comparison helps because it shows how indirect access behaves. A person tasked to investigate compartmented programs may encounter references, witnesses, security barriers, and authentic program names without being read into the underlying activity. That can be exactly the position from which important wrongdoing is discovered. It can also be exactly the position from which unrelated compartments are accidentally connected.

The job of evidence is to distinguish those possibilities.

AARO’s 2024 historical report argues that interviewees repeatedly misidentified authentic sensitive programs as UAP exploitation efforts and states that none of the interviewees providing named reverse-engineering program claims had firsthand access to those alleged programs. AARO further says it found no empirical evidence that the government or contractors possessed or reverse-engineered extraterrestrial technology. [The report](https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/AARO_Historical_Record_Report_Vol_1_2024.pdf) is itself an official claim supported by an investigative process whose classified portions cannot be independently audited by the public.

So we have competing institutional assertions under asymmetric access.

The wrong response is to choose the institution one already trusts.

The right response is to identify claims that can migrate out of testimony. A specific material with custody. A program document whose provenance can be authenticated. A budget action. A facility modification. A named direct witness willing to provide testable details. A technical result. An inspector-general finding whose language can be released without exposing unrelated classified programs.

The witness gets the investigation started. The artifact has to finish it.
