# The Things We Really Retrieved

## Foreign Materiel Exploitation, Black Programs, and the Test for a Hidden UAP Program

Sven Hardy Benson

There is a peculiar weakness in almost every argument about crashed UFOs. The believer starts with the alleged crash. The skeptic starts with the implausibility of aliens. Both skip the warehouse.

Warehouses are boring. So are customs arrangements, cargo manifests, test stands, machinists, metallurgy reports, flight clearances, safety investigations, contractor invoices, range schedules, broken bolts, incompatible lubricants, security rosters, and the engineer who has to discover which Soviet hydraulic fluid will not eat an American seal. Yet those are exactly the things a real exploitation program produces.

We know because governments have really done this.

The CIA really built a ship behind a Howard Hughes mining cover to raise a Soviet ballistic-missile submarine from more than three miles of Pacific water. The Air Force really acquired Soviet MiGs, pulled them apart, restored them, flew them in Nevada, and trained American pilots against them. The United States really organized technical-intelligence institutions around foreign hardware. It really chased fallen spacecraft debris abroad. It really compartmented programs so thoroughly that outsiders could observe a sliver and misunderstand the whole.

None of this proves that the United States ever recovered a non-human craft. It gives the question teeth.

If a hidden UAP exploitation program exists, it is not exempt from organizational physics. Matter has to move. People have to touch it. Facilities have to support it. Hazards have to be managed. Knowledge has to cross compartment boundaries eventually or the exploitation fails. Contractors have to be selected. Instruments have to be calibrated. Samples have to acquire provenance. A program can hide its purpose; it cannot make every consequence of having a purpose disappear.

This book is an attempt to inventory those consequences.

The evidence labels used throughout are deliberately severe. Class 1 is verified, declassified, or otherwise documented fact. Class 2 is credible but incomplete reporting or testimony whose important portions cannot yet be independently checked. Class 3 is disputed allegation without independent confirmation. Class 4 is synthesis: the argument I am building from the other three, which may be useful and may be wrong.

The point is not to make mystery respectable. The point is to make it testable.
