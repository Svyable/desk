# SRI origin and first CIA contract audit

This note freezes the earliest documented transition from Puthoff's private curiosity to government-sponsored parapsychology at SRI. The later mythology is large enough that the small first checks matter.

## Sources and source classes

Two source families are especially useful and should not be collapsed.

1. **Puthoff's own retrospective account:** Harold E. Puthoff, “CIA-Initiated Remote Viewing Program at Stanford Research Institute,” *Journal of Scientific Exploration* 10, no. 1 (1996). This is first-person participant testimony written more than two decades after the events. It is useful for sequence and motivation but should be labeled retrospective.
2. **Declassified CIA internal history:** the CIA paper commonly released under the title *Parapsychology and Personalogy* / internal program history, preserved as CIA-RDP88G01116R000600690003-8. This is an institutional retrospective built from agency records. It is stronger for the contracting sequence and dates, while still remaining a later reconstruction rather than a raw 1972 contracting file.

## The prehistory

Puthoff's 1996 account says that in early 1972 he was still doing laser research at SRI while circulating a proposal on quantum biology. Ingo Swann reportedly saw that proposal while visiting Cleve Backster's laboratory in New York and wrote to Puthoff. Puthoff invited Swann to SRI for a week in June 1972.

The biographical importance is that the remote-viewing program did not begin because CIA recruited an established parapsychology team. Puthoff was already inside SRI as a physicist and only then began testing Swann.

## The magnetometer episode and CIA contact

The internal CIA history says Swann participated in an SRI experiment involving a superconducting magnetometer. Puthoff regarded the apparent instrument disturbance as unusual enough to report to a CIA acquaintance, Kenneth Green in the Office of Scientific Intelligence, in a letter dated 27 June 1972.

The event should be preserved as a reported anomaly, not as proof of psychokinesis. The important historical fact is what it triggered administratively: an intelligence officer became interested enough to arrange a controlled demonstration.

## The first money was tiny

The internal history records an initial CIA Office of Technical Service contract/work order of **$874** for an August 1972 demonstration at SRI.

That number is useful precisely because it is not impressive.

It looks less like a secret program already convinced of psychic spying than a cheap technical question: can these people show us anything worth investigating?

The agency's internal reconstruction says the demonstration was regarded positively enough that the OTS director authorized a second work order of **$2,500** for additional investigation.

Only after those preliminary steps did the funding become recognizably programmatic.

## The $50,000 expansion

By October 1972 the same internal history says OTS funded approximately **$50,000** of expanded parapsychology work at SRI under Contract 8473, dated 1 October 1972.

The sequence is therefore:

- private/SRI curiosity and Swann testing;
- Puthoff contact with CIA/OSI after the magnetometer anomaly;
- $874 demonstration;
- $2,500 follow-on;
- $50,000 expanded effort.

That progression is a much better institutional narrative than saying merely that “the CIA funded remote viewing.” It shows an organization buying information in stages.

## Why intelligence sponsorship was rational even under low prior probability

The CIA history places the work against contemporary reports of Soviet and Eastern Bloc parapsychology/psychotronics research. This does not mean Soviet psychic capability had been validated. It means the intelligence problem had two layers:

1. is the phenomenon real enough to matter?;
2. regardless of the answer to (1), what are adversaries spending effort on and why?

An intelligence service can rationally investigate a claim that turns out to be false if the cost of a small test is lower than the cost of strategic surprise.

Government interest therefore changes the prior probability of institutional concern, not the truth value of ESP.

## Puthoff's own later characterization

In a 1995 interview, Puthoff described the work as having “started as a lark” and recalled that the first substantial CIA support in late 1972 was roughly $50,000. That memory is broadly consistent with the declassified internal contracting chronology, although the CIA history preserves the smaller $874 and $2,500 steps that disappear in the simplified recollection.

This is a useful example of how participant memory compresses bureaucracy. Nobody later tells the legend with the $874 check.

## What this establishes

**Verified/documented to a strong archival standard:** CIA/SRI contact in 1972; CIA-sponsored demonstrations; staged funding that expanded from a sub-$1,000 test to a $50,000 program; Puthoff's central role in originating the SRI work.

**Credible but participant-dependent:** Puthoff's detailed recollection of how Swann encountered his quantum-biology proposal and the interpersonal texture of the first invitation.

**Not established by the contracting record:** that the magnetometer disturbance was psychokinetic; that remote viewing was already validated before CIA funding; that the agency possessed a secret theory of psychic functioning; that subsequent long-term funding constituted proof of efficacy.

## Narrative consequence

The origin chapter should be built around escalation rather than revelation.

A physicist at a contract-research institute encounters an extraordinary claimant. A strange instrument episode attracts an intelligence contact. The government spends less than a thousand dollars to see a demonstration. Then a few thousand more. Then fifty thousand.

The program becomes serious one administrative step at a time.

That is how institutions often approach edge claims: not with belief, but with optionality.
