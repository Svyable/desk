# AAWSAP / BAASS contract audit

This note anchors the later Puthoff/Bigelow chapters in released DIA and Pentagon records rather than participant recollection alone.

## Contract identity

Primary DIA records identify the contract as **HHM402-08-C-0072**, awarded in September 2008 to Bigelow Aerospace Advanced Space Studies (BAASS), LLC. The public contracting record and later Pentagon memorandum describe BAASS as the sole bidder.

Contract modification / option-year record: https://www.dia.mil/FOIA/FOIA-Electronic-Reading-Room/FileId/237652/

Aerospace contract status briefing: https://www.dia.mil/FOIA/FOIA-Electronic-Reading-Room/FileId/237642/

## What the government said the first year produced

A released DIA status briefing says BAASS performance had been **excellent** and that the contractor was in full compliance. It records:

- extensive monthly status reports;
- twelve project-management plans;
- **26 detailed research reports by 30 June 2009**, described as twice the minimum requirement;
- overwhelmingly positive internal reviews of the technical reports;
- a recommendation that DIA proceed with the first option year, subject to funding.

The same briefing lists subjects including inertial electrostatic confinement fusion, pulse-power weaponry, spacetime modifications, biosensors, metamaterials and invisibility, wormholes, gravity-wave communication, superconducting gravity experiments, antigravity studies, field effects on biological tissues, matter-antimatter energy, vacuum energy, metallic glasses, programmable matter, spintronics, lasers, quantum-entanglement communications, advanced nuclear propulsion, and other future-aerospace topics.

The important evidentiary point is that the positive performance evaluation is real. A skeptical history should not erase it.

The equally important point is what was being praised: contractual performance and the quality/value of technical studies. That is not equivalent to experimental confirmation of the most speculative physics discussed in the studies.

## What the Pentagon said the program was for

An October 2009 DIA memorandum states that the goal of the contract was to identify technologies and physics concepts that might support revolutionary aerospace vehicle research and development, then use those studies to guide more focused searches into foreign aerospace R&D. It characterizes the first-year reports as academic/basic scientific research and says later work could move toward scientific and technical intelligence and possible classified experimental verification.

This is a much more precise statement than either 'a UFO program' or 'nothing but theoretical papers.'

The public record supports a future-aerospace intelligence contract whose scope included unusually speculative subjects. Other released and reported material connects BAASS to UAP/anomaly investigations. The manuscript should keep those layers separate unless a document directly connects a specific technical study to a specific observed vehicle or recovered artifact.

## Harry Reid's SAP request

Senator Harry Reid wrote Deputy Secretary of Defense William Lynn on 24 June 2009 requesting restricted Special Access Program protection for what his office called the Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program. The later Pentagon package explicitly says the DIA-managed contract's official name was the **Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Application Program (AAWSAP)** and notes that Reid's office used the AATIP label.

That naming distinction matters. Later public accounts frequently use AAWSAP and AATIP as if the terms were perfectly interchangeable across all periods and activities. The source-specific names should be preserved.

Reid argued that the work had identified highly sensitive unconventional aerospace findings and warranted tighter protection. The request therefore belongs in the evidence ledger as documented evidence that a senior senator regarded the work as important and sensitive.

It is not the last document in the chain.

## The request was rejected

The 17 November 2009 memorandum from Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence James R. Clapper Jr. recommended **against** establishing the requested Special Access Program at that time.

The package states that DIA's program leadership saw no justification for SAP protection based on the FY2009 deliverables or anticipated FY2010 work. Its talking points say the current level of scientific capability did not appear to pose the kind of grave national-security damage that would justify the requested compartmentation, while noting that individual topics might warrant ordinary Secret classification depending on subject matter.

This is unusually valuable evidence because it cuts against a common self-sealing story. There really was an attempt to place the program behind stronger access controls. A higher-level review really did decline to do so.

That does not prove that no sensitive work existed elsewhere. It does mean the surviving record does not support describing this specific request as approved or the public BAASS work as hidden inside the requested SAP.

## Funding and option-year posture

The Pentagon package says Senators Reid and Inouye co-sponsored a $10 million FY2009 earmark and that a further $12 million had been allocated for FY2010 support. The September 2009 contract modification exercised the first option year subject to availability of funds and revised the period of performance.

The modification identifies continuing deliverables including project-management plans, research reports, and a comprehensive integrated threat assessment.

This helps explain the familiar $22 million figure without treating it as a single cash payment for one narrow UFO investigation.

## What this establishes about Puthoff

Public participant accounts place Puthoff in the technical network that helped recruit and produce advanced-aerospace studies, and his own vacuum/metric-engineering paper sits in the DIRD lineage. The DIA records establish the contractor, contract, topics, deliverables, reviews, funding posture, and SAP history. They do not, by themselves, establish every claimed subcontract relationship or every participant's exact role.

For the manuscript:

- **verified/documented:** contract, BAASS, deliverables, positive performance assessment, subject areas, option-year intent, Reid SAP request, SAP rejection;
- **credible but requires document-by-document confirmation:** Puthoff's precise role in selecting every DIRD author, exact EarthTech subcontract scope, and which later UAP cases were worked under which task line;
- **not established by these documents:** possession of recovered non-human hardware, successful warp-drive engineering, or proof that any UAP required the speculative physics surveyed in the technical reports.

## Narrative consequence

The best chapter is not 'the Pentagon secretly proved impossible physics' and not 'the entire program was a joke.'

The stranger and better documented story is that a real defense-intelligence contract paid a private organization to survey a spectrum running from conventional advanced technology to extreme theoretical possibilities; DIA thought the contractor performed well enough to continue; senior political sponsors sought stronger secrecy; and the Pentagon declined the requested special-access status because the work in hand did not justify it.

That tension is more interesting than either mythology.
