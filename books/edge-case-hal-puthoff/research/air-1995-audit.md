# AIR 1995 audit: what the final review actually decided

This note freezes the 1995 American Institutes for Research review before the manuscript expands Chapter 9. The goal is to prevent later prose from collapsing three separate questions into one: whether laboratory scores departed from chance, whether those departures established an anomalous information channel, and whether remote viewing worked as an intelligence collection tool.

Primary record: Michael D. Mumford, Andrew M. Rose, and David A. Goslin, *An Evaluation of Remote Viewing: Research and Applications*, American Institutes for Research, 29 September 1995, CIA document CIA-RDP96-00791R000200180006-4. Preservation copy: https://irp.fas.org/program/collect/air1995.pdf . CIA-source facsimile identifier: https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp96-00791r000200180006-4

## What AIR was asked to do

The review was commissioned before a possible transfer of the program to a new intelligence-community sponsor. AIR divided the task into two major pieces: a review of the research database and a review of operational intelligence utility. The foreign-assessment component of the larger program was outside AIR's scope.

That separation is essential. A finding about statistical anomaly in a laboratory task is not automatically a finding about intelligence usefulness. Conversely, poor operational usefulness does not by itself prove that every laboratory deviation was artifactual.

## Why Utts and Hyman were both there

AIR deliberately recruited experts from opposite sides of the parapsychology dispute. Jessica Utts, a statistician, had prior familiarity with the database and argued that the statistical evidence for anomalous cognition was compelling. Ray Hyman, a psychologist and long-standing critic of parapsychology, accepted that unusual statistical patterns existed in parts of the database but argued that methodological weaknesses, questions about independence, possible procedural artifacts, and insufficient genuinely independent replication blocked the inference that psychic functioning had been established.

AIR also included its own senior scientists to provide a methodological perspective without prior commitment to the parapsychology literature.

This was not a report in which one government reviewer 'debunked' the work and everyone agreed. Nor was it a report in which the CIA certified psychic functioning. It preserved a real expert split.

## Points of convergence

The most useful part of the report is where disagreement narrows.

Both expert reviewers treated some of the laboratory results as statistically nontrivial. The disagreement was over what that fact licensed. Utts gave much greater evidentiary weight to the accumulated effect and to later SRI/SAIC protocol improvements. Hyman emphasized that statistical departure from a null does not uniquely identify a paranormal cause and that a research tradition can preserve hidden dependencies, design choices, judging effects, selective analysis, or laboratory-specific artifacts across nominal replications.

For the manuscript, the safe formulation is:

- **documented:** reviewers agreed that some analyzed datasets contained departures from chance expectations;
- **documented disagreement:** Utts regarded the evidence as establishing psychic functioning to a very high degree of confidence, while Hyman did not;
- **not established by the review:** a known physical mechanism for remote information transfer;
- **not established by the review:** that laboratory scoring translated into dependable intelligence collection.

## Operational utility is the harder ending

AIR did not evaluate operations by retelling the celebrated anecdotes that dominate remote-viewing lore. It examined tasking and interviewed intelligence users. The final institutional problem was not whether a viewer occasionally produced a striking correspondence. It was whether the product was sufficiently accurate, specific, timely, and reliable to support intelligence decisions.

The review found that the information was inconsistent and typically required interpretation alongside other intelligence. Users could not know prospectively when a session would be correct. This is a devastating distinction for an operational capability. An intelligence method can occasionally be right and still be unusable if the consumer cannot identify the right occasions before acting.

A good way to state the operational defeat condition is:

> A collection method is not operational merely because some outputs can be matched to truth after the fact. It has to improve decisions prospectively at an acceptable false-positive and false-negative rate.

That is the standard the program did not clear to AIR's satisfaction.

## What the review does not prove

The 1995 review does not prove that every SRI or SAIC session can be explained by cueing. It does not prove that every participant fabricated results. It does not retroactively erase all statistical anomalies. It also does not prove that the anomalous statistics reflected extrasensory perception.

Program termination is evidence about institutional judgment and utility, not a physics experiment on the existence of psi.

Likewise, later claims that termination was merely a cover for a deeper classified continuation require independent evidence. The mere fact that intelligence programs can be compartmented cannot carry that claim.

## Failure-heavy reconstruction queue

The next expansion should deliberately bias source collection toward failure rather than folklore. For each available operational task or experimental series, capture:

1. date and sponsor;
2. viewer and protocol, where releasable;
3. target-selection method;
4. raw response availability;
5. judging method;
6. prospective success criterion;
7. positive correspondences;
8. incorrect statements and misses;
9. whether analysts received multiple-choice or post-hoc target sets;
10. whether the result had independent operational consequence;
11. contemporaneous user evaluation;
12. later retelling and whether the retelling omits misses.

A famous hit without its denominator is biography, not validation.

## Narrative consequence for Puthoff

Puthoff left SRI before the final 1995 review, so the report should not be written as a personal verdict on him. It is better evidence than that. It is the long-duration stress test of the institutional project he helped start.

The strongest biographical conclusion is that his early work succeeded at making a forbidden question administratively and scientifically investigable. It did not produce, over the full government life of the program, a capability that the final reviewing institution judged reliable enough for intelligence operations.

That is a historically consequential mixed result, and the manuscript should protect the mixture.
