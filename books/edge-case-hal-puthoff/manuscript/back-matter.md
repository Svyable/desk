# Back Matter

## Author’s note on evidence

This biography is written from public, lawfully available material. It does not claim access to classified records, private NIDS files, proprietary EarthTech client data, unreleased sensor data, or non-public material samples. Where a participant describes such material, the manuscript treats the description as testimony unless independent public evidence establishes more.

The most important distinction in the book is between **existence of investigation** and **validation of the investigated claim**. CIA and DIA interest are historical facts. They do not prove remote viewing, exotic propulsion, or non-human technology. Peer-reviewed publication is meaningful but does not eliminate the need for replication. A patent records a legal filing/grant and is not evidence by itself that a device performs as claimed. A corporate filing establishes corporate facts, not scientific efficacy.

## Evidence classes

**1. Verified / declassified / documented facts.** Public contracts, government releases, SEC filings, peer-reviewed papers, contemporaneous program reports, dated institutional records, and other records with checkable provenance.

**2. Credible but incomplete reporting, testimony, or inference.** Claims from identifiable participants, serious journalism, or multiple records that materially raise probability but do not independently close the question.

**3. Disputed allegations or claims without independent confirmation.** Extraordinary performance claims, hidden capabilities, non-human origin claims, or causal interpretations for which strong alternative explanations remain or decisive evidence is unavailable.

**4. Speculative synthesis / open conjecture.** The author’s attempt to connect documented patterns into hypotheses about institutions, research taste, networks, or future tests. These are explicitly defeasible.

## Defeat conditions

This book’s skeptical boundaries should change if the evidence changes.

The conclusion that remote viewing was not a dependable intelligence capability would require revision if a preregistered, independently replicated protocol produced prospective target information with stable sensitivity, specificity, and operational value across laboratories and viewers.

The conclusion that practical vacuum-energy extraction is unproven would require revision if an independently replicated device delivered sustained net work with a complete energy balance, calibrated instrumentation, blinded controls, and no conventional source capable of accounting for output.

The conclusion that controllable inertial modification is unproven would require revision if multiple independent laboratories measured a reproducible change in inertial response under controlled conditions while excluding electromagnetic, mechanical, thermal, acoustic, buoyant, and analysis artifacts.

The conclusion that public UAP evidence does not establish non-human technology would require revision if a physically recovered artifact had secure provenance, independent laboratory analysis, reproducible properties outside known manufacture, and evidence tying it to an observed vehicle or event; or if calibrated multisensor data established performance incompatible with known natural and technological causes and the result survived independent analysis.

Conversely, Puthoff’s favored or entertained hypotheses should lose probability when repeated high-quality tests fail, when proposed mechanisms are mathematically inconsistent, when anomalies disappear under improved controls, or when source dependence explains supposedly independent testimony.

## Research appendix — questions still worth forcing into the open

A complete archival edition should reconstruct the early SRI contract chain year by year, with sponsor, amount, target protocol, viewer, judge, method, outcome, and later critique. Celebrated sessions should be paired with failed sessions from the same period rather than narrated alone.

The 1995 AIR dataset should be rebuilt in modern reproducibility form: identify every experiment included by Utts and Hyman, protocol lineage, independence assumptions, effect sizes, analyst choices, and which raw data remain public.

Puthoff’s post-SRI zero-point work should receive a technical literature map separating stochastic electrodynamics results that reproduce established quantum predictions from those that fail, along with the strongest contemporary critiques beyond the 2009 inertia paper.

EarthTech’s public testing history should be cataloged claim by claim: device, claimed mechanism, sponsor where public, measurement method, outcome, whether negative results were published, and whether independent replication followed.

NIDS should be reconstructed from contemporaneous documents rather than later Skinwalker mythology: board composition, research protocols, instrument deployments, case selection, publication output, negative results, and which records remain inaccessible.

AAWSAP/BAASS requires a contract-first reconstruction using DIA releases: solicitation, statement of work, deliverables, funding, modifications, DIRD authorship, EarthTech subcontract documentation, program reviews, Reid’s SAP request, DIA response, and termination. “AATIP” should be used only where a source supports that label for the specific activity.

Modern UAP cases should be kept outside the biography unless they illuminate Puthoff’s documented role. Where used, the evidence should be sensor-specific and source-dependent claims should be traced to their common ancestor.

## Closing note

The public record supports a stranger and more useful biography than either hagiography or debunking. Hal Puthoff repeatedly helped serious institutions formulate tests around questions they could investigate more comfortably than they could endorse. The right legacy is not a verdict on every mystery. It is a demand that the next edge institution become better at letting its favorite possibility lose.
