## Thesis

For more than half a century Harold “Hal” Puthoff repeatedly occupied a peculiar institutional role: the technically credentialed physicist willing to investigate claims that mainstream science considered premature, contaminated, or absurd, while military and intelligence sponsors sometimes considered them too consequential to ignore. His career does not prove remote viewing, vacuum-energy engineering, exotic propulsion, or non-human technology. It reveals something more documentable and in some ways stranger: how American institutions create temporary shelters in which extraordinary claims can be investigated without the institution itself having to believe them.

The manuscript distinguishes sponsorship from validation, testimony from proof, peer review from replication, classification from importance, and a provocative theoretical possibility from an engineered capability.
