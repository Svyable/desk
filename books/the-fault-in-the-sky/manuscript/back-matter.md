# Back Matter

## Evidence posture

This manuscript distinguishes four evidence classes throughout.

**Verified / documented.** These are claims grounded in public primary project records, official records, or peer-reviewed experimental observations: Project Hessdalen existed and conducted the 1984 campaign; its report records visual observations and radar detections; stressed igneous rocks have produced measurable electrical currents in laboratory experiments; granite faulting has produced photographed luminescence under laboratory conditions; Cen, Yuan and Xue recorded the optical spectrum of a natural ball-lightning event after cloud-to-ground lightning; Heraud and Lira analyzed security-camera flashes recorded during the 15 August 2007 Pisco earthquake.

**Credible but incomplete.** These include the positive-hole explanation for some field-scale earthquake lights, the reported association between earthquake-light cases and rift/steep-fault settings, and the proposition that a residual class of Hessdalen events remains after ordinary-source controls. The evidence is substantial enough to investigate and incomplete enough that mechanism should not be narrated as settled.

**Disputed or unconfirmed.** These include reliable earthquake prediction from electromagnetic or luminous precursors, the claim that all significant Hessdalen radar returns represent the visible phenomenon, plasma or geological explanations for Hessdalen as established facts, and claims of vehicle or non-human origin.

**Speculative synthesis.** The book’s broad synthesis is that geophysical and atmospheric luminous phenomena belong in the differential diagnosis of anomalous aerial events, and that prospective multisensor observatories at natural laboratories could improve both geophysics and UAP classification. The manuscript does not claim that earthquake lights, ball lightning and Hessdalen share one mechanism.

## Research note

Several historical cases in the manuscript, especially Matsushiro, require a deeper primary-source pass before release. The current draft preserves that limitation rather than converting later summaries into primary evidence. Likewise, the Hessdalen archive needs event-level reconstruction from original logs and instrument records before aggregate claims can carry more weight.

No classified or non-public material was used. A public project record establishes what investigators reported, not automatically what caused the reported event. A peer-reviewed mechanism paper establishes the experiment or argument it describes, not field efficacy beyond the data.

## Acknowledgment of uncertainty

A good unexplained event can end unresolved without becoming evidence for the most interesting available theory. The point of the research program proposed here is to make that unresolved category smaller, more physical and more expensive to occupy.
