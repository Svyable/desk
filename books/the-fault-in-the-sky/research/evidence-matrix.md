# Evidentiary matrix

The manuscript uses four evidence classes throughout. The classes describe support for a claim, not the prestige of the person making it.

| Claim | Class | Current posture | What would move it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unusual light emissions have been recorded during some earthquakes | 1 — verified/documented | Strongest at Pisco 2007, where a fixed security camera recorded flashes that Heraud & Lira time-correlated with local seismic acceleration | Independent raw-video/timing reconstruction and infrastructure maps would strengthen; demonstrated transformer/grid sources would weaken specific events |
| Granite fracture can produce visible luminescence in laboratory conditions | 1 | Demonstrated in published experiments | Independent replication across lithologies and calibrated spectral/electrical measurements |
| Stressed igneous rocks can generate and conduct electrical currents in laboratory conditions | 1 | Demonstrated in Freund/Takeuchi/Lau experiments | Independent replication and better scaling laws |
| Positive-hole charge carriers explain natural earthquake lights | 2 — credible but incomplete | Plausible mechanism with laboratory basis; field scaling and uniqueness remain incomplete | Field measurements linking stress, surface potential, atmospheric ionization and optical emission at the same event |
| Earthquake lights preferentially occur in rift settings | 2 | Report-catalogue pattern published in SRL; susceptible to case-selection and historical-report quality | Prospective denominator-based catalogue with geology coded before light reports are assessed |
| Earthquake lights provide a reliable earthquake precursor | 3 — disputed/unconfirmed | Not established; USGS states no convincing evidence for reliable electromagnetic precursors | Prospective, preregistered predictions with false-alarm accounting and independent replication |
| The Matsushiro photographs document genuine earthquake-generated light | 2/3 | Historically important but timing, exposure and alternative-source questions remain | Original negatives, camera metadata, observer logs, independent contemporaneous records |
| A distinct luminous phenomenon recurred in Hessdalen in the early 1980s | 1/2 | Numerous reports plus project photography/radar claims establish a serious observation problem; event heterogeneity is high | Open raw datasets with synchronized optical/radar/weather/traffic/astronomy data |
| Every 1984 Hessdalen radar return represents the luminous phenomenon | 3 | Not established | Raw radar data, calibration, clutter characterization, independent optical coincidence and track reconstruction |
| Hessdalen lights are plasma | 3 | Suggested by some spectra/EM interpretations; no consensus mechanism | Repeated calibrated spectra, electron-density diagnostics, thermal/chemical measurements |
| Hessdalen lights are geologically powered | 3 | Plausible research program, not demonstrated | Temporal coupling between subsurface electrical/geochemical variation and luminous events, with ordinary-source controls |
| Hessdalen lights are craft or non-human technology | 3 | No independent evidence establishes this | Recovered/traceable material, resolved structure and dynamics incompatible with known natural/technological sources, reproducible multisensor data |
| Ball lightning exists as a natural mobile luminous atmospheric phenomenon | 1 | Strong, including 2014 spectrographic observation following cloud-to-ground lightning | Already well supported; broader mechanism taxonomy remains open |
| Some UAP reports may be geophysical or atmospheric luminous phenomena | 4 — speculative synthesis | Reasonable differential-diagnosis claim; event-specific attribution required | Prospective UAP datasets with geophysical instrumentation and identified correlated cases |
| One mechanism explains earthquake lights, ball lightning and Hessdalen | 4 | Explicitly not claimed | Would require common quantitative signatures and replicated mechanism across settings |

## Defeat conditions

The book’s broadest useful thesis is not `the lights are geology`. It is that geophysical luminous mechanisms deserve a place in the differential diagnosis of anomalous aerial events. That thesis weakens materially if prospective instrumented programs fail to find any residual natural luminous class after ordinary sources and lightning are removed.

The stronger synthesis — that stressed rock can sometimes couple into atmospheric light at meaningful scale — should be treated as defeated if independent laboratory work cannot reproduce relevant charge transport or if field campaigns measure stresses without the predicted electrical/atmospheric signatures.

No outcome in this matrix can establish non-human intelligence by elimination alone.
