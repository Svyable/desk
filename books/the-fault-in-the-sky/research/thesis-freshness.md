## Selected thesis

A serious anomalous-aerial taxonomy is incomplete unless it includes geophysical and atmospheric luminous phenomena. Earthquake lights, stress-induced rock currents, ball lightning and Hessdalen do not yet form one demonstrated mechanism. They do show, with very different evidence strengths, that the ground and lower atmosphere can produce luminous events that are rare, mobile, structured-looking, sometimes instrument-detectable and easy to overtranslate into `craft`.

The book therefore asks a stricter question than `what are UFOs?`: what observations survive after ordinary misidentification, infrastructure failure, camera artifacts and radar clutter are removed, and what measurements could distinguish a geophysical luminous event from a physical vehicle?

## Why this is distinct

The empirical core is solid-Earth geophysics, rock electrification, dielectric breakdown, atmospheric plasma, optical spectroscopy, field instrumentation, radar phenomenology, fault geology and anomaly classification. It is not primarily about consciousness, intelligence programs, classified aerospace, remote sensing from space, or biological sensing.

## Defeat condition

The central synthesis should be abandoned or sharply narrowed if (a) the strongest modern earthquake-light cases collapse under timing/infrastructure review; (b) stress-induced rock charge cannot scale to fields capable of surface air ionization under plausible crustal conditions; and (c) Hessdalen's high-quality events can be accounted for by ordinary sources without leaving a residual class requiring additional physics. The thesis does not require all three domains to share one mechanism.
