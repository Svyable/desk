# Source log

Public, lawfully available sources checked for this drafting pass. A link here records provenance; it does not promote every claim in the source to established fact.

## Earthquake lights and rock electrification

1. J. A. Heraud and J. A. Lira, “Co-seismic luminescence in Lima, 150 km from the epicenter of the Pisco, Peru earthquake of 15 August 2007,” *Natural Hazards and Earth System Sciences* 11 (2011): 1025–1036. https://doi.org/10.5194/nhess-11-1025-2011 — peer-reviewed analysis of PUCP security-camera light events time-correlated with local seismic acceleration during the Mw 8.0 Pisco earthquake. Strong for existence of recorded co-seismic flashes; weaker for mechanism.
2. Robert Thériault, France St-Laurent, Friedemann T. Freund, and John S. Derr, “Prevalence of Earthquake Lights Associated with Rift Environments,” *Seismological Research Letters* 85 (2014): 159–178. https://doi.org/10.1785/0220130059 — catalogue-based geological pattern argument. Useful but selection and historical-report quality must remain explicit.
3. France St-Laurent, John S. Derr, and Friedemann T. Freund, “Earthquake lights and the stress-activation of positive hole charge carriers in rocks,” *Physics and Chemistry of the Earth* 31 (2006): 305–312. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pce.2006.02.003 — proposed positive-hole/corona mechanism.
4. Friedemann T. Freund, Akihiro Takeuchi, and Bobby W. S. Lau, “Electric currents streaming out of stressed igneous rocks,” *Physics and Chemistry of the Earth* 31 (2006): 389–396. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pce.2006.02.027 — laboratory current measurements and extrapolative seismo-electromagnetic interpretation.
5. Friedemann T. Freund, “Pre-earthquake signals — Part I: Deviatoric stresses turn rocks into a source of electric currents,” *NHESS* 7 (2007): 535–541. https://doi.org/10.5194/nhess-7-535-2007 — rock-battery hypothesis and laboratory basis.
6. Akihiro Takeuchi, Bobby W. S. Lau, and Friedemann T. Freund, “Current and surface potential induced by stress-activated positive holes in igneous rocks,” *Physics and Chemistry of the Earth* 31 (2006): 240–247. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pce.2006.02.036 — laboratory stress/current work.
7. Yoshiyuki Enomoto and H. Hashimoto, “Photographic evidence of luminescence during faulting in granite,” *Earth, Planets and Space* 62 (2010): 785–794. https://doi.org/10.5047/eps.2010.02.004 — laboratory fracture luminescence recorded during granite faulting; authors favor a piezoelectric contribution. Important alternative to a single positive-hole mechanism.
8. U.S. Geological Survey, Geomagnetism Program, “Are earthquakes associated with variations in the geomagnetic field?” https://www.usgs.gov/programs/geomagnetism/science — official skeptical boundary: post-earthquake electromagnetic variations occur, but USGS states there is no convincing evidence for reliable electromagnetic precursors.
9. U.S. Geological Survey Publications Warehouse, bibliographic record for St-Laurent, Derr & Freund 2006. https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70028206 — authoritative source metadata, not an institutional endorsement of the mechanism.

## Ball lightning as control case

10. Jianyong Cen, Ping Yuan, and Simin Xue, “Observation of the Optical and Spectral Characteristics of Ball Lightning,” *Physical Review Letters* 112, 035001 (2014). https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.112.035001 — spectrographic observation of a luminous ball following cloud-to-ground lightning; persistent soil-element spectral lines. Strong evidence that at least one class of rare, mobile luminous object can be atmospheric and chemically coupled to the ground.

## Hessdalen

11. Erling P. Strand, *Project Hessdalen 1984 — Final Technical Report*. https://old.hessdalen.org/reports/hpreport84.shtml — primary project report covering visual observations, photographs, radar, magnetometer, spectrum analyzer, laser, Geiger counter and infrared viewer. Establishes what the project reported, not that every detection represented a novel phenomenon.
12. Project Hessdalen, current papers index. https://www.hessdalen.org/papers — primary project archive and bibliography; useful for provenance and later work.
13. Project Hessdalen, reports archive. https://old.hessdalen.org/reports/ — archive includes the 1984 report, EMBLA material, radar reports, and Matteo Leone’s 2003 rebuttal. Important because the project’s own archive preserves criticism rather than only favorable interpretations.
14. G. N. Vargemezis, J. Zlotnicki, B. G. Hauge, A. L. Kjøniksen, and E. P. Strand, “Contribution of VLF electromagnetic survey to the investigation of Hessdalen lights (Norway),” *Journal of Applied Geophysics* 226 (2024): 105398. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jappgeo.2024.105398 — modern geophysical characterization of the valley; useful for testing geological coupling without assuming causation.
15. Etienne Caron and Pouya Faridi, “To Investigate or Not to Investigate? Researchers’ Views on Unexplored Atmospheric Light Phenomena,” *Frontiers in Earth Science* 4 (2016): 17. https://doi.org/10.3389/feart.2016.00017 — meta-scientific discussion of research barriers and Hessdalen as a case study. Use cautiously for sociology rather than physical proof.
16. Matteo Leone, “A rebuttal of the EMBLA 2002 report on the optical survey in Hessdalen” (2003), indexed in the Project Hessdalen reports archive: https://old.hessdalen.org/reports/ — skeptical analysis emphasizing possible car-headlight and interpretation problems. The manuscript treats this as substantive counterevidence.

## Historical and classification context

17. The 2014 Thériault et al. catalogue is the main working entry point for the Matsushiro, Saguenay/Quebec, Tagish Lake and other historical cases. Historical reports are never treated as equivalent to modern multisensor records merely because they appear in a peer-reviewed catalogue.
18. Japan Meteorological Agency earthquake database and Matsushiro swarm materials, accessible through JMA public data; an English overview of the swarm source-region figure is preserved at https://www.hp1039.jishin.go.jp/eqchreng/f6-41.htm . These establish the seismic swarm context, not the authenticity of particular light photographs.

## Claim-handling rules

- A project report proves that a project measured or reported something; it does not prove the project’s interpretation.
- A patent or speculative mechanism is never used as evidence that the mechanism works in nature.
- A radar echo without independent range, Doppler, track continuity and clutter analysis is not promoted to a solid object.
- A video taken during an earthquake must be checked against electrical infrastructure failure before being labeled an earthquake light.
- “Unidentified” means the available record does not support an identification. It does not mean vehicle, intelligence, plasma, or geophysical origin.
