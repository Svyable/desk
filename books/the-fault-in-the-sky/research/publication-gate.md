# Publication / expansion gate

This automated pass is a working manuscript. It is not a release candidate unless every objective and editorial gate below is actually satisfied.

## Required evidence work before release

1. Reconstruct the Pisco 2007 case frame by frame from the original PUCP video, accelerogram and camera geometry; map known electrical infrastructure visible from the camera and test whether flash timing favors local grid failure, distant infrastructure or non-infrastructure emission.
2. Recover and page-index the primary Yasui/Matsushiro light publications and contemporaneous observation records. Separate Kuribayashi photographs, Yasui interpretations, magnetic/radio observations and later retellings.
3. Build a quantitative scaling ledger for the positive-hole hypothesis: laboratory stress, current density, carrier propagation distance, plausible crustal source area, surface potential, atmospheric breakdown threshold and optical energy. Record every extrapolation.
4. Reproduce Enomoto and Hashimoto’s granite-fracture conditions and compare their duration/spectrum with the long-duration field reports the mechanism is sometimes asked to explain.
5. Expand the ball-lightning control using the Cen/Yuan/Xue raw geometry, spectrum, lifetime and soil-element interpretation, plus serious competing ball-lightning models.
6. Download and preserve the 1984 Hessdalen report appendices, including individual visual and radar event tables. Build synchronized event-level records rather than quoting aggregate counts.
7. Reconstruct radar specifications, beam geometry, clutter environment, range/Doppler limits and weather/traffic/astronomy controls for the strongest Hessdalen radar events.
8. Page-index EMBLA optical/radar results and Matteo Leone’s rebuttal side by side. Any event surviving ordinary-source analysis should be identified by record number, not by generic “Hessdalen lights” language.
9. Audit published Hessdalen spectral claims for calibration, exposure, source identification and reproducibility. Do not state temperature, power or plasma parameters unless the underlying measurement supports them.
10. Incorporate the 2024 VLF survey quantitatively and determine what, if anything, it changes about causal claims rather than merely characterizing local geology.
11. Rebuild the Thériault et al. 65-event catalogue into a denominator-aware table: event date, magnitude, tectonic setting, report quality, timing relative to rupture, observer density, electrical infrastructure, weather, and source grade.
12. Add modern earthquake videos as negative controls, especially events where transformer/power-line arcing is visible. The book needs a strong false-positive library.
13. Audit the seismo-electromagnetic precursor literature beyond luminous events, including nulls and prospective failures. Keep earthquake prediction separate from earthquake-light physics.
14. Design a prospective field network with optical all-sky cameras, calibrated spectrographs, broadband RF/VLF/ULF, electric-field mills, magnetometers, infrasound, weather, lightning location, traffic/ADS-B, satellite ephemerides and at least one radar channel with preserved raw data.
15. Define preregistered event classes and kill tests before deployment so the array cannot relabel ordinary events after the fact.
16. Expand each numbered chapter to at least 3,000 substantive words, and the chapter-only manuscript to Desk’s 65,000-word one-shot threshold, using deeper evidence rather than repetition.
17. Run `python3 scripts/check-book-length.py the-fault-in-the-sky`, the cover/metadata checker, global Desk validation if the book is cataloged, and a whole-book anti-slop/editorial read.

## Release boundary

No shared catalog, Reader, Shelf or publication-state surface should be changed until the length, research, rights and whole-book editorial gates pass. A draft PR is the correct completion artifact for this pass.
