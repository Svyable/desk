## Reader promise

This book asks a narrow question with uncomfortable consequences: how much of the anomalous sky can be generated by the ground beneath it? It follows earthquake lights, stressed-rock electricity, ball lightning, and the Hessdalen observations without pretending that one mechanism explains them all. The aim is not to debunk UAP by relabeling them plasma, nor to promote luminous anomalies into exotic craft. It is to build a better category system for phenomena that are real enough to instrument and still poorly enough understood to invite mythology.

## Distinct contribution

The book joins two literatures that are usually kept apart: seismo-electromagnetic and atmospheric-luminous research on one side, and instrumented anomalous-aerial observations on the other. Its central claim is methodological. A recurring light can be physically real, externally generated, instrument-detectable, and still fail to establish a vehicle. Geology and atmosphere therefore belong inside any serious UAP differential diagnosis.

## Strongest competing explanation

The strongest countercase is that most apparent earthquake lights and many Hessdalen events are heterogeneous ordinary phenomena: electrical-grid failures, car headlights, stars and planets, aircraft, camera artifacts, radar clutter, mirage, lightning, or selective reporting. The manuscript treats that explanation as a live competitor and asks where it succeeds, where it is incomplete, and what evidence would be required to establish a distinct geophysical luminous mechanism.
