# Source Log

## Core human evidence

- Wang, Connie X., et al. “Transduction of the Geomagnetic Field as Evidenced from alpha-Band Activity in the Human Brain.” *eNeuro* 6(2), 2019. https://www.eneuro.org/content/6/2/ENEURO.0483-18.2019 — controlled Earth-strength field rotations; alpha-ERD; polarity/orientation dependence; open data and analysis code.
- PubMed record for Wang et al.: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31028046/
- Kirschvink, J. L., A. Kobayashi-Kirschvink, and B. J. Woodford. “Magnetite biomineralization in the human brain.” *PNAS* 89(16), 1992. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1502184/ — magnetite detected in human brain tissue.
- Foley, Lauren E., Robert J. Gegear, and Steven M. Reppert. “Human cryptochrome exhibits light-dependent magnetosensitivity.” *Nature Communications* 2, 356 (2011). https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms1364 — human CRY2 rescues magnetosensitivity in cryptochrome-deficient flies; does not establish a human magnetic sense.
- Maher, Barbara A., et al. “Magnetite pollution nanoparticles in the human brain.” *PNAS* 113(39), 2016. DOI 10.1073/pnas.1605941113 — identifies abundant combustion-derived magnetite nanoparticles and separates them from a population attributed to endogenous formation.

## Mechanism / comparative biology

- Xu, Jingjing, et al. “Magnetic sensitivity of cryptochrome 4 from a migratory songbird.” *Nature* 594, 535–540 (2021). https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-03618-9 — ErCRY4 is magnetically sensitive in vitro; site-specific mutations map radical-pair chemistry.
- Warrant, Eric J. “Unravelling the enigma of bird magnetoreception.” *Nature* 594, 497–498 (2021). https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-021-01596-6 — context for CRY4 evidence.
- Kerpal, Christian, et al. “Chemical compass behaviour at microtesla magnetic fields strengthens the radical pair hypothesis of avian magnetoreception.” *Nature Communications* 10, 3707 (2019). https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-11655-2 — chemical compass behavior at geomagnetic-scale fields.
- Kirschvink, J. L., A. Kobayashi-Kirschvink, J. C. Diaz-Ricci, and S. J. Kirschvink. “Magnetite in human tissues: A mechanism for the biological effects of weak ELF magnetic fields.” *Bioelectromagnetics* 13(S1), 1992. https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/bem.2250130710 — mechanism proposal, not evidence that human magnetite is a sensory receptor.

## Research cautions

- Behavioral human magnetoreception claims, especially the Robin Baker homing literature, require source-by-source reconstruction and replication auditing before being promoted beyond Class 2/3.
- “Magnetoreception exists” does not imply electromagnetic hypersensitivity, navigation ability, clinical effects, or conscious perception.
- A material component is not a receptor. A receptor is not a pathway. A neural response is not a behavior. A behavior is not an ecological function.
