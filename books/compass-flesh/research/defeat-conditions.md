# Defeat Conditions

The book's strongest synthesis — that humans may retain a genuine but largely unconscious geomagnetic transduction pathway — should be weakened or abandoned if:

1. multiple independent laboratories using faithful, preregistered versions of the Wang et al. paradigm fail to reproduce field-specific neural effects;
2. a nonmagnetic artifact is shown to generate the reported alpha response;
3. the effect disappears under hardware designs that eliminate coil-specific cues;
4. response geometry fails to track field variables in a physically coherent way;
5. proposed ferromagnetic receptor material proves wholly exogenous, anatomically random, and uncoupled to neural tissue;
6. proposed cryptochrome pathways fail mechanism-specific tests at geomagnetic field strengths;
7. responder classifications are not stable within people over time;
8. no neural, behavioral, or physiological consequence survives once preregistration and independent replication become routine.

A failure of conscious navigation alone would not defeat neural transduction. A failure of one receptor hypothesis would not defeat all magnetic biology.
