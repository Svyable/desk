# Chronology

- 1970s–1980s — Human homing and magnetic-orientation experiments attract attention and controversy; replication is inconsistent.
- 1992 — Kirschvink, Kobayashi-Kirschvink, and Woodford report magnetite biomineralization in human brain tissue.
- 1992 — Mechanistic arguments are developed for weak-field interactions with biogenic magnetite.
- 2011 — Foley, Gegear, and Reppert report that human CRY2 can rescue light-dependent magnetosensitivity in cryptochrome-deficient fruit flies.
- 2016 — Maher and colleagues report abundant pollution-derived magnetite nanoparticles in human brain samples, complicating simple sensory interpretations of brain magnetite.
- March 18, 2019 — Wang et al. publish controlled EEG evidence for orientation- and polarity-dependent responses to Earth-strength magnetic-field rotations.
- August 16, 2019 — Kerpal et al. report chemical compass behavior at microtesla fields relevant to avian magnetoreception.
- June 23, 2021 — Xu et al. publish in-vitro magnetic sensitivity of European-robin CRY4 and mutation-based radical-pair mechanistic evidence.
- 2026 — Human receptor localization, broad independent replication, behavioral use, and ecological function remain open questions.
