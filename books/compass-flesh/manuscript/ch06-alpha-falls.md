# Alpha Falls

Alpha rhythms are not a magnetic meter.

That sentence needs to sit close to any discussion of the 2019 human experiment because the result is easy to oversell. EEG alpha power changes with attention, visual state, sensory processing, expectation, arousal, and other conditions. A decrease after a magnetic-field rotation is interesting only if the magnetic manipulation is isolated convincingly from nonmagnetic differences.

The Wang et al. design tried to do that. Participants sat in darkness. The coil system could generate field rotations or active sham conditions. The authors analyzed responses time-locked to field changes and reported strong alpha-band desynchronization for particular rotations. The effect was not uniform across every subject. A subset showed larger responses. The geometry mattered: one horizontal rotation under a downward static vertical component, ecologically ordinary for the Northern Hemisphere, produced a response pattern that disappeared or changed under manipulations the authors considered biologically unnatural.

The strangest feature was directionality. Certain counterclockwise rotations produced strong responses while the corresponding clockwise rotations did not. That asymmetry is not the simple output one would draw on a whiteboard for a generic magnetic detector.

It could be biology. Sensory systems are full of adaptation, tuning, asymmetry, and circuit history.

It could also be where an artifact hides.

The experiment's credibility therefore depends less on the attractiveness of alpha desynchronization than on whether electrical induction, coil heating, vibration, acoustic cues, timing artifacts, analysis flexibility, and other field-correlated signals were excluded. The authors used polarity and static-field dependence to argue against electrode induction, because induction depends on change rather than the absolute polarity of a static field. They reported active sham controls and an electrically quiet chamber.

Those are meaningful controls. They are not a replication.

A mature version of this field needs adversarial replication: independent coils, independent shielding, preregistered analysis, automated blinding, larger samples, laboratories in both magnetic hemispheres, deliberate reversal of local inclination, and hardware designed by people hoping the effect disappears. It should test whether responders remain responders over time and whether response magnitude follows field strength or rotation parameters in a coherent way.

If the effect survives that, alpha is not the discovery. Alpha is the smoke.

The discovery would be that a human nervous system contains information about the geomagnetic field that the person may not know they possess.
