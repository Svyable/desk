# Publication Source Ledger Addendum — Chapters 13–16

Verified 2026-09-03. This addendum covers the four chapters added after the original 12-chapter release review. It should be read with `source-ledger.md`; the intent is to close the factual gate for the expanded manuscript without pretending that every metaphor is a technical term.

## Chapter 13 — The Heat Debt

- **Claim ID:** C13-01
- **Claim supported:** Dense computing infrastructure creates a thermal-management obligation; liquid-cooling systems can move heat from IT equipment into recirculating water loops rather than relying only on room air.
- **Source:** Cooling Water Efficiency Opportunities for Federal Data Centers
- **Organization:** U.S. Department of Energy, Federal Energy Management Program
- **Source type:** Federal technical guidance
- **URL:** https://www.energy.gov/cmei/femp/cooling-water-efficiency-opportunities-federal-data-centers
- **Primary/secondary:** Primary / authoritative guidance
- **Confidence:** High
- **Boundary:** The manuscript should not imply that liquid cooling is required for every data center or that one cooling architecture dominates every density and climate regime.
- **Last verified:** 2026-09-03

- **Claim ID:** C13-02
- **Claim supported:** Future high-power processors and racks create heat-flux and cooling challenges significant enough to motivate dedicated federal R&D in advanced data-center thermal management.
- **Source:** DOE Announces $40 Million for More Efficient Cooling for Data Centers
- **Organization:** U.S. Department of Energy
- **Source type:** Federal program announcement describing funded technical objectives
- **URL:** https://www.energy.gov/articles/doe-announces-40-million-more-efficient-cooling-data-centers
- **Primary/secondary:** Primary / first-party program source
- **Confidence:** High for the existence and direction of the R&D challenge
- **Boundary:** Project goals are not proof that every proposed cooling technology will succeed commercially.
- **Last verified:** 2026-09-03

## Chapter 14 — The Night Shift of Civilization

- **Claim ID:** C14-01
- **Claim supported:** Maintenance in modern manufacturing still relies substantially on human problem-solving and works best when sensing, analytics, and decision-support tools augment rather than simply replace human knowledge.
- **Source:** Where Do We Start? Guidance for Technology Implementation in Maintenance Management for Manufacturing
- **Organization:** National Institute of Standards and Technology
- **Source type:** Peer-reviewed conference paper / NIST publication
- **Date:** 2019
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1115/1.4044105
- **Primary/secondary:** Primary research / authoritative technical publication
- **Confidence:** High
- **Boundary:** This supports the chapter's human-systems argument; it does not establish that all maintenance work must remain manual.
- **Last verified:** 2026-09-03

- **Claim ID:** C14-02
- **Claim supported:** Condition-based and predictive maintenance use equipment data to detect degradation before failure and still operate inside an organizational O&M process.
- **Source:** Energy Management Information System Capabilities
- **Organization:** U.S. Department of Energy, Federal Energy Management Program
- **Source type:** Federal technical guidance
- **URL:** https://www.energy.gov/cmei/femp/energy-management-information-system-capabilities
- **Primary/secondary:** Primary / authoritative guidance
- **Confidence:** High
- **Boundary:** Predictive maintenance can reduce some failure modes and unnecessary work; it does not eliminate calibration, inspection, repair, spare-parts logistics, or human judgment.
- **Last verified:** 2026-09-03

## Chapter 15 — The Planetary Cleanroom

- **Claim ID:** C15-01
- **Claim supported:** Earth's climate emerges from coupled interactions among atmosphere, land, oceans, cryosphere, energy, water, carbon, and other cycles rather than from a single controllable setpoint.
- **Source:** Earth Systems Processes, Fifth National Climate Assessment Chapter 3
- **Organization:** U.S. Global Change Research Program / NASA GISS publication record
- **Date:** 2023
- **URL:** https://www.giss.nasa.gov/pubs/abs/le09900v.html
- **Primary/secondary:** Authoritative scientific assessment / primary federal publication record
- **Confidence:** High
- **Boundary:** The manuscript's cleanroom analogy is explicitly metaphorical; Earth is not an engineered facility.
- **Last verified:** 2026-09-03

- **Claim ID:** C15-02
- **Claim supported:** Solar geoengineering research raises technical, ecological, social, ethical, legal, and international-governance questions and should be treated as a governed research problem rather than a simple control-system analogy.
- **Source:** Reflecting Sunlight: Recommendations for Solar Geoengineering Research and Research Governance
- **Organization:** National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine
- **Date:** 2021
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.17226/25762
- **Primary/secondary:** Consensus study / authoritative synthesis
- **Confidence:** High
- **Boundary:** The chapter must continue distinguishing emissions reduction, adaptation, carbon removal, weather modification, and sunlight-reflection strategies; they differ materially in mechanism, maturity, reversibility, and governance.
- **Last verified:** 2026-09-03

## Chapter 16 — The Last Free Input

- **Claim ID:** C16-01
- **Claim supported:** Building efficiency and passive design can increase the time a building remains habitable during loss of heating/cooling service, supporting the chapter's claim that broader tolerance can substitute for some active control.
- **Source:** Resilience — Better Buildings & Better Plants Initiative
- **Organization:** U.S. Department of Energy
- **Source type:** Federal resilience guidance
- **URL:** https://betterbuildingssolutioncenter.energy.gov/resilience/about
- **Primary/secondary:** Primary / authoritative guidance
- **Confidence:** High for the qualitative resilience claim
- **Boundary:** Passive survivability depends strongly on climate, building type, envelope, occupancy, outage duration, and event severity; it is not a guarantee of safety.
- **Last verified:** 2026-09-03

- **Claim ID:** C16-02
- **Claim supported:** The expanded manuscript's counter-thesis—that better system design can sometimes reduce environmental-control dependence—is consistent with engineering practice that seeks lower loads, fault tolerance, and more resilient operating envelopes rather than only tighter control.
- **Sources:** DOE resilience guidance; NIST reliability and fault-detection work for building mechanical systems
- **Organizations:** U.S. Department of Energy; National Institute of Standards and Technology
- **URLs:** https://betterbuildingssolutioncenter.energy.gov/resilience/about ; https://www.nist.gov/programs-projects/reliability-mechanical-systems-buildings
- **Primary/secondary:** Cross-source synthesis
- **Confidence:** Medium-high
- **Boundary:** This is a synthesis and design principle, not a claim that robustness always beats environmental control economically or technically.
- **Last verified:** 2026-09-03

## Expansion-gate conclusion

No new chapter requires a hard numerical forecast to carry its argument. The strongest factual claims in Chapters 13–16 are qualitative and now have authoritative support with explicit boundaries. The new concepts — **heat debt**, **maintenance paradox**, **planetary cleanroom**, and **last free input** — remain authorial framing devices, not claims of established technical nomenclature.

The factual gate for Chapters 13–16 is therefore **passed for editorial progression**, subject to final copyedit preserving the qualifications above.
