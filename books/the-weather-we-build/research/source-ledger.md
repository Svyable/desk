# Publication Source Ledger — The Weather We Build

Verified 2026-09-03. This ledger attaches publication-grade evidence to the manuscript's strongest factual lanes while preserving the book's explicit distinction between technical terms and the framing device **manufactured weather**. The prose is intentionally qualitative; these sources support the factual spine rather than convert the manuscript into a claim of one unified engineering discipline.

## Intellectual-predecessor / novelty ledger

The book does **not** claim that the phrase “manufactured weather,” controlled environments, artificial climates, microclimates, air conditioning, cleanrooms, cold chains, dry rooms, or environmental control are newly discovered categories.

- **N-01 — Gail Cooper, _“Manufactured Weather”: A History of Air Conditioning in the United States, 1902–1955_ (doctoral work cataloged by Smithsonian Libraries; later related book _Air-Conditioning America: Engineers and the Controlled Environment, 1900–1960_).** This is a direct lexical predecessor for “manufactured weather,” focused on air-conditioning history. Smithsonian record: https://www.si.edu/object/manufactured-weather-history-air-conditioning-united-states-1902-1955-gail-ann-cooper:siris_sil_465208
- **N-02 — Simon Marvin and Jonathan Rutherford, “Controlled environments: An urban research agenda on microclimatic enclosure,” _Urban Studies_ 55(6), 2018.** This explicitly treats controlled microclimatic enclosures as a cross-cutting urban research object. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1177/0042098018758909
- **N-03 — T. Takakura, _Climate Under Cover: Digital Dynamic Simulation in Plant Bio-Engineering_.** A longstanding technical treatment of artificial greenhouse climates, reinforcing that artificial-climate engineering is an established field rather than a novel discovery.

**Novelty conclusion:** the defensible contribution of *The Weather We Build* is the narrative/economic synthesis that follows a common enabling layer across precision manufacturing, cold chains, battery dryness, vibration-sensitive metrology, buildings, human heat, modular conditioning, industrial geography, and spaceflight. The manuscript already says the component disciplines are mature and that exact technical terminology outranks the metaphor. Publication should preserve that modest novelty claim and should not represent the words “manufactured weather” themselves as original coinage.
