# Research ledger — GRAVITY WENT NOWHERE

## Class 1 — documented

- E. Podkletnov and R. Nieminen published a 1992 paper in *Physica C* reporting an apparent reduction in measured weight above a levitating YBCO superconducting disk. Publication establishes the claim, not the phenomenon.
- NASA Marshall researchers David Noever and Ronald Koczor, with Rick Roberson, reported high-precision gravimeter tests using large bulk YBCO superconductors and rotating magnetic fields. Their NASA Technical Reports Server record states that no effect of the rotating magnetic field, thermal environment, or disk rotation was observed within their measurement precision. NTRS document 19990019627: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19990019627
- A 2002 NASA/ASEE faculty-fellowship report by Larry Smalley discusses electromagnetic-gravitational coupling and explicitly frames Podkletnov’s result as anomalous and not successfully repeated at that time. NTRS document 20020068834: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20020068834
- NASA’s Breakthrough Propulsion Physics project publicly examined highly speculative propulsion questions. Program existence and study are not evidence that any particular breakthrough effect was demonstrated.

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete

- Exact apparatus-to-apparatus comparability between Podkletnov’s reported configuration and NASA replication hardware needs reconstruction: YBCO composition, disk geometry, levitation state, field strength/frequency, rotation, vibration isolation, barometric correction, gravimeter geometry and claimed signal location.
- The institutional route by which the claim reached Marshall, and the stopping decision after null/ambiguous results, requires primary correspondence or oral-history support.
- Later replication attempts need a denominator: not just famous positive or negative attempts, but all identifiable serious tests and their sensitivity.

## Class 3 — disputed / unconfirmed

- No public evidence presently establishes a reproducible percent-scale gravitational shielding effect from rotating superconductors.
- Claims that NASA, aerospace contractors, defense programs, or foreign states secretly achieved operational gravity control are not established by the fact that NASA investigated Podkletnov-like claims.
- Patents, proposals and program names are evidence of filing or interest, not device efficacy.

## Class 4 — speculative synthesis

### Investigation laundering

An institution’s decision to investigate an extraordinary claim can later be recycled as evidence that the institution validated it. The semantic mutation is: **NASA tested X → NASA worked on X → NASA had X → NASA hid X.** Each arrow needs independent evidence.

### Replication distance

A failed replication only bears strongly on the original claim to the extent that the relevant experimental state was reproduced and the test had enough sensitivity to detect the claimed effect. `Replication distance` should therefore be made quantitative rather than rhetorical.

### Negative-result half-life

A null experiment can disappear from popular retellings faster than the fact of institutional interest. This is testable by tracing citation chains and comparing how often later accounts cite the investigation versus its result.

## Contradictions / incentives

- Extraordinary-propulsion advocates have an incentive to treat institutional curiosity as validation.
- Institutions have an incentive to describe exploratory work conservatively after programs fail, which can itself feed retrospective suspicion.
- Null replication can be weak evidence if apparatus mismatch is large. It cannot simply be promoted to universal disproof.
- Conversely, endless apparatus-specific escape clauses can make a claim immunized against falsification. The manuscript must identify in advance what would count as a fair replication.

## Publication holds

Do not state without primary support that NASA “spent millions” specifically replicating Podkletnov; that the original effect was 2% in every configuration; that the unpublished 1996 Podkletnov paper was withdrawn because of suppression; that Boeing or another contractor reproduced the effect; or that classified programs possess working antigravity.

## Defeat condition

The book’s stronger epistemic thesis is weakened if a well-documented chain shows that NASA or another independent laboratory reproduced the claimed weight anomaly under controlled conditions at the reported magnitude and then suppressed or failed to publish the result. The physical thesis is defeated in the other direction if high-fidelity preregistered replications spanning the claimed operating envelope repeatedly return null results at sensitivity far below the claimed effect.
