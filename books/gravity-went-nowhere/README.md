# GRAVITY WENT NOWHERE

## How NASA Chased a Superconductor That Was Supposed to Make Things Lighter

A Sven Hardy Benson book.

### Thesis

In the 1990s, a disputed superconducting “gravity shielding” result crossed the boundary conspiracy culture often claims mainstream science never crosses: NASA researchers took it seriously enough to build hardware and try to measure it. Their high-precision experiment did not reproduce the claimed effect. That failure is more interesting than either ridicule or belief. It gives us a rare public control case for exotic-propulsion claims: what happens after an extraordinary effect is specific enough to put on a bench, and what evidentiary obligations remain when replication fails?

This book follows the Podkletnov claim, NASA Marshall replication work, superconductivity, gravimetry, the Breakthrough Propulsion Physics era, later electromagnetic-gravity proposals, and the mythology that grew in the gap between “NASA investigated it” and “NASA verified it.” Its governing question is simple: **when an institution investigates an extraordinary claim, what exactly has been established?**

### Evidence classes

1. **Verified / documented.** Published Podkletnov/Nieminen claim; NASA/NASA-affiliated technical reports documenting replication attempts and propulsion interest; established superconductivity and gravimetry; public Breakthrough Propulsion Physics program history.
2. **Credible but incomplete.** Engineering details, exact sensitivity budgets, unpublished/null replication history, institutional decision-making, and the reasons particular follow-up paths ended.
3. **Disputed / unconfirmed.** Large gravity-shielding effects, impulse-gravity devices, operational propellantless propulsion derived from the claimed phenomenon, hidden successful replication.
4. **Speculative synthesis.** `investigation laundering`, `replication distance`, and the proposition that failed public replication can increase mythology when the investigation itself is later cited as evidence of validation.
