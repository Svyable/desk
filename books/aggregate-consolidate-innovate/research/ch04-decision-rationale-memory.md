# Chapter 4 research note — decision rationale as external memory

## Why this belongs in the chapter

Chapter 4 argues that organizations often preserve the output of a decision while losing the conditions and reasoning that produced it. That claim can be grounded more precisely than a generic appeal to institutional memory: NASA's current systems-engineering guidance treats documented decision rationale, assumptions, uncertainties, alternatives, and sensitivities as work products of decision analysis, while older NASA/JPL knowledge-capture work explicitly studied the capture of design rationale.

The useful distinction for the chapter is between an archive that can reproduce **what was decided** and one that can reconstruct **why that decision was reasonable at the time**. The second form is more useful when conditions change because it exposes which assumptions deserve to be retested.

## Primary sources checked

### NASA Systems Engineering Handbook — Decision Analysis

NASA's current Decision Analysis guidance says decisions are made under incomplete knowledge and stresses documenting assumptions and limitations. For significant decisions, it says decision-analysis information should be documented and describes a decision report that can include the mission/system context, intended outcome, criteria, alternatives, methods, assumptions, uncertainties, sensitivities, evaluation results, recommendation, final decision with rationale, and lessons learned.

Source: NASA, “6.8 Decision Analysis,” Systems Engineering Handbook guidance.
https://www.nasa.gov/reference/6-8-decision-analysis/

This is unusually close to the chapter's proposed small decision record: what was decided, what was believed, alternatives considered, evidence that mattered, and conditions that should trigger reconsideration. NASA's version is appropriately heavier because it is written for consequential engineering decisions; the underlying information structure is the relevant evidence, not the bureaucracy around it.

### JPL design-knowledge capture

A 2002 Jet Propulsion Laboratory conference paper archived by NASA's Technical Reports Server is explicitly indexed under “knowledge capture” and “decision rationale.” Its existence is useful corroboration that preserving design reasoning is a recognized knowledge-management problem rather than a productivity-book invention.

Source: Lynne P. Cooper and Ann Majchrzak, “Lessons learned in design knowledge capture (DKC),” JPL IT Forum, November 4, 2002, NASA NTRS document 20060044116.
https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20060044116

## Strongest counterargument / boundary

More documentation is not automatically more memory. Formal decision records can become expensive, defensive, performative, or so detailed that nobody retrieves them. NASA's own guidance says many decisions do not require extensive analysis and warns that formal decision analysis can consume substantial time and resources. That is important counterevidence to any implication that every ordinary choice deserves a dossier.

Documentation can also preserve the wrong thing with impressive durability. A rationale written after consensus may rationalize rather than recover the actual reasoning; assumptions can be recorded without being monitored; and a record can become another stale artifact that later readers mistake for current policy.

The chapter should therefore defend **proportionate rationale capture**, not documentation maximalism. The value comes from preserving the few contextual facts that let a future reader interpret or reopen a consequential decision.

## Editorial use

A later manuscript pass could strengthen the decision-record passage by replacing the purely hypothetical list with a brief NASA-grounded observation: serious engineering practice records not just the selected alternative but the criteria, assumptions, uncertainty, and rationale around it. Then return immediately to the chapter's lighter proposal for ordinary organizations.

The sharper claim is: **a decision record is useful when it preserves the conditions under which the decision should stop being trusted.** That connects external memory directly to adaptation rather than treating documentation as an end in itself.
