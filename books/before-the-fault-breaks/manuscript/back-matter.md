# Back Matter

## A short chronology of the precursor problem

**373 BCE — Greece.** Ancient reports later cited in earthquake-animal literature describe rats, snakes, weasels and other animals leaving an area before a destructive earthquake. These reports are historical testimony, not instrumented evidence.

**Early twentieth century onward.** Modern seismology develops instruments capable of distinguishing earthquake phases, allowing apparently prophetic animal reactions seconds before strong shaking to be reinterpreted in part as sensitivity to earlier P-wave arrivals.

**1960s–1970s.** Earthquake prediction becomes a major scientific and government research aspiration in several countries. Candidate precursors include seismicity, strain, groundwater, geoelectric changes, radon and animal behavior.

**February 4, 1975 — Haicheng, China.** A magnitude 7-class earthquake follows a warning and large-scale evacuation episode. Later U.S. scientific review treats the pronounced foreshock sequence as the strongest part of the successful case while other alleged precursors are harder to evaluate.

**July 28, 1976 — Tangshan, China.** A magnitude 7.6 earthquake devastates Tangshan without a comparable successful warning. The event becomes the essential countercase to Haicheng.

**Late 1970s–1980s — Parkfield, California.** Regular-looking recurrence of moderate earthquakes on a section of the San Andreas Fault motivates a major U.S. prediction experiment and dense monitoring network.

**1992 — Parkfield alert.** A magnitude 4.7 event triggers a short-term warning procedure for a possible larger Parkfield earthquake. The larger event does not follow in the warning window.

**1993–1994 — Parkfield reassessment.** Published critiques and reviews document that the celebrated high-confidence forecast window has failed and that its statistical assumptions were too simple.

**September 28, 2004 — Parkfield earthquake.** The anticipated M6.0 event finally occurs and is exceptionally well instrumented, but without revealing a universal short-term countdown signal.

**2004–2010 — DEMETER.** France operates the DEMETER microsatellite, designed in part to investigate electromagnetic emissions and ionospheric phenomena associated with earthquake regions. The mission produces a large literature of candidate effects and methodological controversy.

**2006 — Stressed-rock electrical mechanisms.** St-Laurent, Derr and Freund publish work linking earthquake-light reports to stress-activated positive-hole charge carriers in rock. The mechanism remains a debated scaling hypothesis rather than an established warning method.

**April 6, 2009 — L’Aquila, Italy.** A magnitude 6.3 earthquake kills more than 300 people after months of seismic unrest and intense public debate about risk and alleged radon-based prediction.

**2012–2014 — L’Aquila legal aftermath.** Six scientists and one official are convicted at first instance in a manslaughter case centered on risk assessment and communication. In 2014 an appeals court acquits the six scientists and reduces the government official’s conviction.

**March 11, 2011 — Tohoku-Oki.** The magnitude 9.0 Japanese earthquake and tsunami generate strong, measurable coseismic ionospheric perturbations. These established post-rupture effects become important context—and a source of possible confusion—for disputed claims about pre-seismic ionospheric anomalies.

**2014 — Earthquake lights review.** Thériault, St-Laurent, Freund and Derr publish a systematic treatment of better-documented earthquake-light cases, reporting a strong association in their sample with rift environments and steep faults.

**2015 — Radon/thoron proposal.** Oh and Kim publish a cave-based radon/thoron case study proposing that the short half-life of thoron can help discriminate local earthquake-linked changes from slower environmental effects.

**February 2, 2018 — CSES-01.** China launches the first China Seismo-Electromagnetic Satellite, with instruments for electric and magnetic fields, ionospheric plasma, GNSS and energetic particles. Italy participates through the Limadou collaboration.

**2020s — Multichannel and machine-learning era.** Researchers increasingly apply machine learning to radon, ionospheric, electromagnetic and other precursor data sets. Methodological concerns shift toward leakage, nuisance correction, class imbalance, heterogeneous anomaly definitions and prospective validation.

**June 14, 2025 — CSES-02 / Zhangheng 1B.** China and Italy launch the second CSES spacecraft. The mission improves coverage and extends a research program that, by ASI’s account, had already produced more than two hundred CSES-01 scientific papers.

**2026 — AI/radon systematic review.** A systematic review of AI-based radon earthquake-precursor work finds a growing but methodologically heterogeneous field, with substantial variation in sensors, anomaly definitions, environmental correction, coupling rules and validation.

## The evidence rule

The book uses four evidence classes throughout:

1. **Documented fact:** the observation, event, program, measurement or published result is directly supported by public records or primary/authoritative sources.
2. **Credible but incomplete evidence:** a pattern or mechanism has serious support but lacks sufficient replication, specificity, prospective validation or causal closure.
3. **Disputed claim:** an allegation or interpretation exists but lacks independent confirmation, depends strongly on contested analysis, or has substantial counterevidence.
4. **Open conjecture:** an explicit synthesis offered for testing rather than as established fact.

These classes attach to propositions, not subjects. “Earthquake lights have been reported and studied” can be Class 1 while “a specific light was caused by pre-seismic charge migration” remains Class 2 or 3 and “earthquake lights can predict earthquakes” remains unsupported.

## What would change the conclusion

The manuscript’s central conjecture would weaken substantially if long-duration prospective multichannel networks, using frozen algorithms and strong environmental controls, repeatedly failed to show any incremental pre-rupture information beyond conventional seismicity, deformation and hazard models.

It would strengthen substantially if independent teams produced calibrated prospective forecasts across held-out regions or future periods, with manageable false-alarm rates, physically constrained mechanisms and cross-platform replication.

The book does not require deterministic earthquake prediction to be possible. Its narrower claim is that the existence or nonexistence of useful preparatory-state information remains experimentally tractable if the field stops grading itself retrospectively.

## A note on uncertainty

No non-public, classified, restricted, or proprietary information was used. No unpublished government access is implied. The book relies on public scientific literature, official mission material, public historical records, and clearly attributed reporting.

The unresolved material is left unresolved on purpose.
