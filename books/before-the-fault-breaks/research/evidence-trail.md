# Research and evidence trail

## Book thesis

*Before the Fault Breaks* asks a narrower question than “can earthquakes be predicted?”: whether some fault systems enter measurable preparatory states that alter short-term probabilities across seismic, hydrological, chemical, electromagnetic, atmospheric, ionospheric, or biological channels—and whether any such signal survives prospective testing with real false-alarm accounting.

The manuscript does **not** claim that deterministic earthquake prediction currently works. It treats the U.S. Geological Survey’s negative position on major-earthquake prediction and electromagnetic precursors as strong counterevidence. The open conjecture is that heterogeneous, mechanism-specific precursors may exist without supplying a universal countdown.

## Evidence classes

### Class 1 — verified / documented / directly established

- The February 4, 1975 Haicheng earthquake was preceded by a warning/evacuation episode studied by U.S. scientists; later assessments emphasize the pronounced foreshock sequence as the strongest part of the prediction case.
- The July 28, 1976 Tangshan earthquake occurred without a comparable successful warning and caused catastrophic loss of life.
- The Parkfield prediction experiment publicly forecast a moderate San Andreas earthquake within a window that expired before the event; the M6.0 Parkfield earthquake finally occurred September 28, 2004.
- Earthquakes can produce **coseismic** ionospheric perturbations through acoustic/gravity-wave coupling; this is distinct from disputed pre-seismic ionospheric anomalies.
- Radon is environmentally variable and influenced by meteorology, hydrology, site geometry, and ventilation; earthquake-focused anomaly interpretation therefore requires nuisance correction.
- Animals can respond to P waves seconds before humans perceive stronger shaking; this does not imply days-ahead prediction.
- DEMETER was a French low-Earth-orbit mission explicitly designed in part to study electromagnetic emissions associated with earthquake regions.
- CSES-01 launched in 2018. CSES-02 / Zhangheng 1B launched successfully on June 14, 2025. The China-Italy program measures electric/magnetic fields, ionospheric plasma and related variables and explicitly studies possible seismic correlations.
- Six scientists and one official were convicted in the 2012 first-instance L’Aquila manslaughter case concerning risk assessment/communication; six scientists were acquitted on appeal in 2014 while the official’s conviction was reduced. The case was not simply a prosecution for “failing to predict an earthquake.”

### Class 2 — credible but incomplete evidence

- Some radon and radon/thoron studies report anomalies temporally associated with earthquakes and propose physically motivated transport interpretations, but prospective performance and cross-site generality remain unresolved.
- Some well-documented luminous phenomena have been reported before or around earthquakes; a 2014 Seismological Research Letters study found an apparent concentration in rift environments and around steep faults. Mechanism and predictive utility remain uncertain.
- Laboratory and theoretical work shows several ways mechanical stress/fracture in rock can generate electrical or magnetic effects (piezoelectric, electrokinetic, piezomagnetic, fracture electrification, proposed positive-hole mechanisms). Scaling to reliable pre-seismic surface/ionospheric signals is unresolved.
- DEMETER, CSES, Swarm and GNSS data sets contain published candidate ionospheric/electromagnetic anomalies around earthquakes. Results are method-sensitive and vulnerable to geomagnetic, atmospheric, orbital and multiple-comparison confounders.
- Unusual animal behavior is reported before some earthquakes, and biology provides known sensory mechanisms that humans lack, but no reproducible general animal prediction system has been demonstrated.

### Class 3 — disputed / unconfirmed allegations or interpretations

- Claims that radon alone supplies reliable short-term earthquake prediction.
- Claims that animal behavior provides a general days-ahead “earthquake sense.”
- Claims that individual TEC, magnetic, infrared, or ULF anomalies near famous earthquakes establish a tectonic precursor without prospective controls.
- Claims that earthquake lights, as a broad category, have one established crustal mechanism.
- Claims that the existence of CSES or DEMETER validates earthquake prediction rather than demonstrating institutional scientific interest.
- Claims that Giampaolo Giuliani’s pre-L’Aquila radon method constituted a validated predictive system.

### Class 4 — speculative synthesis / open conjecture

- Some faults may enter physically distinct preparatory regimes measurable across several channels even if deterministic time/magnitude prediction is impossible.
- Multichannel fusion may add incremental probability information only when channels are selected by mechanism before outcomes are inspected.
- Biological behavior could occasionally function as an indirect transducer of weak ordinary physical signals (microseismic, chemical, electromagnetic, acoustic), rather than a distinct “earthquake sense.”
- The fundamental ceiling on earthquake prediction may partly arise because final rupture extent is not fully determined far in advance; a system can be unstable before the exact event exists as a fixed future quantity.

## Contradictions and disinformation / incentive hazards

1. **Haicheng vs Tangshan:** a memorable success can obscure the base rate of unheralded earthquakes and false warnings.
2. **Case-study selection:** famous earthquakes receive more retrospective analysis and therefore accumulate more candidate precursors.
3. **Flexible windows:** changing spatial radius, lead time, magnitude threshold, anomaly definition or filtering after seeing the catalog can manufacture apparent skill.
4. **Space weather:** geomagnetic and ionospheric variability can imitate purported seismo-electromagnetic anomalies.
5. **Infrastructure:** power systems, railways, pipelines, transmitters and other cultural electromagnetic noise can imitate local crustal signals.
6. **Folklore selection:** unusual animal behavior is remembered before disasters and forgotten during the vastly more common non-disaster periods.
7. **Program-existence fallacy:** government or space-agency study is evidence of interest, not efficacy.
8. **Publication incentive:** positive anomalies are easier to publish than long null streams; rare-event fields therefore require full prospective alert logs.
9. **Machine-learning leakage:** time leakage, event-cluster leakage, station/geography memorization, balanced test sets and post hoc target definitions can create false predictive performance.
10. **Political pressure:** public agencies bear costs for both false alarms and false reassurance; communication incentives can distort scientific uncertainty in either direction.

## Primary and authoritative source trail

### Earthquake prediction baseline and animal behavior

- U.S. Geological Survey, “Can you predict earthquakes?” / Earthquake Hazards FAQs. USGS states that neither USGS nor other scientists have predicted a major earthquake in the strict time-place-magnitude sense and notes no convincing evidence for electromagnetic precursors. https://www.usgs.gov/programs/earthquake-hazards/science/faqs
- USGS, “Can animals predict earthquakes?” Notes abundant anecdotes, P-wave sensitivity as an explanation for seconds-ahead behavior, and the lack of consistent/reliable pre-event behavior. https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/can-animals-predict-earthquakes
- USGS, Lott, Hart & Verosub (1981), *Baseline studies of the feasibility and reliability of using animal behavior as a component in the prediction of earthquakes*, Open-File Report 81-378. https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/ofr81378

### Haicheng / Tangshan / Parkfield

- B. Raleigh (1977), USGS record, “Earthquake research in China.” Discusses the Haicheng warning and U.S. scientific delegation. https://www.usgs.gov/publications/earthquake-research-china
- USGS Parkfield history / repeating-earthquakes page, including Haicheng/Tangshan context and the Parkfield experiment. https://earthquake.usgs.gov/learn/parkfield/eq_predict.php
- Roeloffs & Langbein (1994), “The earthquake prediction experiment at Parkfield, California,” *Reviews of Geophysics*. USGS record: https://www.usgs.gov/publications/earthquake-prediction-experiment-parkfield-california
- James C. Savage (1993), “The Parkfield prediction fallacy,” *Bulletin of the Seismological Society of America*. USGS record and DOI: https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70207074
- USGS, “The Parkfield, California, Earthquake Experiment,” documenting the September 28, 2004 M6.0 event captured by the network. https://earthquake.usgs.gov/learn/parkfield/

### Radon

- Oh & Kim (2015), “A radon-thoron isotope pair as a reliable earthquake precursor,” *Scientific Reports* 5, 13084. This is treated as a positive case study, not as proof of general predictive efficacy. https://www.nature.com/articles/srep13084
- 2026 systematic review, “Artificial Intelligence for Radon Anomalies as Earthquake Precursors: A Systematic Review of Methods and Performance,” *Geosciences* 16(5), 166. Useful for methodological heterogeneity, nuisance variables and validation issues. https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3263/16/5/166

### Earthquake lights / stressed rock

- Thériault, St-Laurent, Freund & Derr (2014), “Prevalence of Earthquake Lights Associated with Rift Environments,” *Seismological Research Letters* 85, 149–178. DOI landing page: https://doi.org/10.1785/0220130059
- NASA publication index entry for the same 2014 earthquake-lights paper: https://airbornescience.nasa.gov/content/Prevalence_of_Earthquake_Lights_Associated_with_Rift_Environments
- St-Laurent, Derr & Freund (2006), “Earthquake lights and the stress-activation of positive hole charge carriers in rocks,” *Physics and Chemistry of the Earth*. USGS record: https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70028206
- Jánský et al. (2018), “Earthquake Lights: Mechanism of Electrical Coupling of Earth’s Crust to the Lower Atmosphere,” *Journal of Geophysical Research: Atmospheres*. https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2018JD028489

### Ionosphere / satellites

- ASI, June 14, 2025, announcement of successful CSES-02 launch and Italian participation. https://www.asi.it/2025/06/la-scienza-italiana-nello-spazio-con-cses-02-al-via-una-nuova-missione-di-osservazione-della-terra/

## Research needs for the next deepening pass

- Primary French mission documents and peer-reviewed statistical syntheses for DEMETER rather than relying heavily on individual event analyses.
- More direct primary literature on earthquake nucleation and whether final rupture magnitude is inferable from early rupture behavior.
- Prospective rather than retrospective studies of animal activity near faults.
- Ground magnetometer array studies with explicit local-vs-global source separation.
- High-quality null studies for radon, TEC, thermal infrared, and ULF precursor claims.
- Operational false-alarm / decision-theory literature from earthquake forecasting and civil protection.
- Japanese earthquake-prediction program history and the transition toward probabilistic hazard and early warning.
- Detailed CSES-01 statistical results with independent replication and explicit handling of geomagnetic/local-time confounders.
