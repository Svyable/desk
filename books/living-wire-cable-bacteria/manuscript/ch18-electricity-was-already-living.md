# Electricity Was Already Living

Biology has always been electrical. Cells depend on ion gradients, membrane potentials and electron transfer. Photosynthesis and respiration are built around moving charge.

Cable bacteria change something more specific. They extend electron transport across centimeter distances and make that transport part of a multicellular metabolism. Oxidation can happen in one chemical layer, reduction in another, while a living filament connects the two.

The discovery arrived in a useful sequence. First came an unexplained geochemical coupling. Then competing mechanisms. Then long filaments. Then severing experiments. Then direct current measurements. Then conductive-fiber anatomy and nickel-bearing protein chemistry. At each stage the phenomenon became better constrained.

Important questions remain. The microscopic charge-transfer mechanism is not complete. The evolutionary history is incomplete. Ecological importance varies among environments. Interactions with neighboring microbes are still being resolved. Proposed technological uses remain research questions rather than established applications.

The secure result is enough. In ordinary sediment, a bacterial filament can occupy chemically different zones and conduct the electrons that allow reactions in those zones to remain coupled.

The mud was not just chemically layered. It was electrically connected.
