# What Counts as an Individual

Cable bacteria make biological scale measurable.

Sulfide oxidation occurs in deeper cells. Oxygen reduction occurs nearer the surface. The complete respiratory circuit spans the filament, so no single cell contains the whole process.

Biology already offers many collective forms: fungal mycelia, biofilms, colonial animals, syncytia and cellular chains. Cable bacteria add an unusually clean physical variable because electrical continuity can be interrupted and conductance can be measured. Their distributed physiology is not only a metaphor for cooperation.

One cell remains a real compartment with its own membrane and molecular machinery. Yet an important energetic function depends on remote cells occupying a different chemical layer. Break continuity and a function disappears that the local cells cannot reproduce by themselves.

This does not settle what biologists should call an individual. Different questions pick different useful scales: cell, filament, population or sediment community. The cable simply makes it harder to assume in advance that the cell is always the complete physiological unit.

The book's speculative step is modest. Some living bodies may be best understood partly through the distance across which their components keep one another metabolically possible. In cable bacteria, that distance is electrical and directly testable.
