# Flocks Around a Wire

A wire through an anoxic environment is valuable even to organisms that did not build it.

Microscopy around active cable bacteria revealed dense flocks of other bacteria associated with the filaments. Metagenomic work published in 2023 suggested that many of these partners were aerobes or organisms with metabolisms that could plausibly benefit from access to an electron pathway ultimately connected to oxygen. The proposed possibility is startling: some neighbors may transfer electrons to cable bacteria and use the filament as a conduit to an electron acceptor they cannot reach directly.

The evidence does not justify saying that cable bacteria run a universal microbial power grid. Association is not the same as demonstrated electron transfer for every partner, and the identities and mechanisms remain incomplete. But the observations enlarge the ecological question. A conductive filament may be infrastructure not only for its own cells but potentially for a local consortium.

That possibility changes what 'environment' means. For a microbe in anoxic sediment, oxygen may be physically distant. If an electrically connected organism passes nearby, the effective redox landscape could change without any oxygen molecule arriving.

Ecologists are accustomed to food webs, metabolite exchange and chemical cross-feeding. Cable bacteria introduce an unusually literal form of energetic adjacency. Two cells can be chemically separated yet electrically coupled through a third organism's body.

Whether that arrangement is common, occasional or highly specialized remains a research question. The important discipline is to keep the observation and the interpretation apart. Flocks are observed. Their likely metabolisms can be inferred from genomes. Direct electron donation by particular partners requires stronger demonstration.

Even with that restraint, the image is difficult to forget: bacteria gathering around a living conductor in mud where oxygen itself cannot go.
