# Working Notes

## Distinct contribution

Most popular treatments stop at “bacteria act like electrical cables.” This book treats that as the beginning. The argument connects four levels that are often separated: sediment-scale geochemical inference, causal interruption experiments, conductive anatomy/material chemistry, and the biological-scale problem created when respiration is distributed across thousands of cells.

## Strongest competing explanation

Cable bacteria are fascinating but conceptually less radical than the book suggests: all respiration is electron transfer, multicellularity already distributes function, and cable bacteria are simply one specialized sulfur oxidizer with an unusually long conductor. The manuscript must earn its stronger interpretation by showing exactly which processes become impossible to describe correctly at the single-cell scale and by avoiding rhetorical inflation of ordinary bioelectrochemistry.

## Contradictions and tensions to preserve

- The 2010 conductor hypotheses were broader than the cable-bacterium mechanism identified later.
- Direct high conductivity in prepared fibers does not mean identical effective conductivity under every natural condition.
- Nickel dependence narrows material mechanism without completing the microscopic theory of charge transport.
- Strong local biogeochemical effects do not establish global dominance.
- Dense bacterial flocks near cables support interaction hypotheses but do not prove direct electron exchange for every partner.
- Technology studies show interesting material behavior, not a mature engineering platform.

## Narrative rule

Begin with measurements and interventions, not futurist analogy. Let the reader discover the organism in approximately the order the field did.
