# Chronology

- 2010 — Nielsen et al. report centimeter-scale electrical coupling between oxygen consumption near the sediment surface and sulfide oxidation deeper below; conductor remains unidentified.
- 2012 — Pfeffer et al. identify long filamentous Desulfobulbaceae as the conductors and show that severing filaments disrupts long-distance electron transport.
- 2014 — Biogeobattery mapping work sharpens source/sink geometry and summarizes filter, cutting and matrix-replacement evidence.
- Mid-2010s — Cable bacteria are documented across marine and freshwater settings; taxonomy, distribution and geochemical consequences expand.
- 2019 — Direct measurements show nanoampere currents through intact centimeter-scale filaments; a highly conductive parallel fiber network is identified in the cell envelope.
- 2020 — Freshwater experiments show cable-bacterium activity can elevate sulfate availability and substantially stimulate sulfate reduction without changing sulfate-reducer abundance.
- 2021 — High-resolution spectroscopy and imaging identify a conductive protein core associated with sulfur-ligated nickel; conductivity falls when nickel is altered or removed.
- 2023 — Dense communities associated with cable filaments are characterized; metagenomic evidence supports hypotheses that some neighboring microbes may use cable bacteria as part of an electron-transfer route, while direct partner-specific mechanisms remain incomplete.
- 2024 — Isolated cable-bacterium structural material is studied as an electrochemically active electrode-like material, extending the materials-science branch of the field.

## Expansion need

This chronology must be deepened with taxonomic milestones, named field sites, discovery of *Electrothrix* and *Electronema*, direct dates for the fiber-junction/cartwheel studies, first freshwater reports, and the sequence of quantitative biogeochemical studies.
