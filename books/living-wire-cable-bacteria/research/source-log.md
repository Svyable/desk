# Source Log

## Primary research spine

- Nielsen et al. (2010), “Electric currents couple spatially separated biogeochemical processes in marine sediment,” *Nature* 463: 1071–1074. DOI: 10.1038/nature08790. Establishes centimeter-scale coupling from oxygen/sulfide dynamics, pH profiles and mass balance; conductor not yet identified.
- Pfeffer et al. (2012), “Filamentous bacteria transport electrons over centimetre distances,” *Nature* 491: 218–221. DOI: 10.1038/nature11586. Identifies cable bacteria and uses abundance, physical interruption and matrix-replacement evidence to assign long-distance electron transport to filaments.
- Risgaard-Petersen et al. (2014), “Mapping electron sources and sinks in a marine biogeobattery,” *JGR Biogeosciences*. DOI: 10.1002/2014JG002673. Useful synthesis of cutting, pore-size and glass-microsphere tests and spatial current budgets.
- Meysman et al. (2019), “A highly conductive fibre network enables centimetre-scale electron transport in multicellular cable bacteria,” *Nature Communications*. DOI: 10.1038/s41467-019-12115-7. Direct current measurements and conductive-fiber anatomy.
- Sandfeld et al. (2020), “Electrogenic sulfide oxidation mediated by cable bacteria stimulates sulfate reduction in freshwater sediments,” *ISME Journal* 14: 1233–1246. DOI: 10.1038/s41396-020-0607-5. Quantifies freshwater sulfur-cycle feedback.
- Boschker et al. (2021), “Efficient long-range conduction in cable bacteria through nickel protein wires,” *Nature Communications* 12. DOI: 10.1038/s41467-021-24312-4. Links conductivity to sulfur-ligated nickel in protein cores.
- Cable-bacteria microbial-association study, *Nature Communications* (2023), DOI: 10.1038/s41467-023-37272-8. Documents diverse bacterial flocks and proposes electron-transfer relationships; mechanism remains incomplete.
- Digel et al. (2024), “Cable Bacteria Skeletons as Catalytically Active Electrodes,” *Angewandte Chemie International Edition*. DOI: 10.1002/anie.202312647. Technology-relevant material study; not evidence of scalable devices.

## Claim boundaries

The manuscript does not treat “bacterial wire,” “battery,” “grid,” or “infrastructure” as literal equivalence. It does not claim neural signaling, intelligence, global climate control, or immediate engineering utility. It distinguishes direct electronic conduction from ionic transport and keeps ecosystem-scale extrapolation separate from laboratory effects.
