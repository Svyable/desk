# Evidence Matrix

1. Verified/documented: sediment oxygen use and deep sulfide oxidation can be electrically coupled across centimeter distances in studied sediments; cable-bacterium filaments track and are functionally required for that coupling; intact filaments conduct current; conductive fibers are located in the cell envelope; nickel-bearing protein chemistry is linked to conductivity.

2. Credible but incomplete: exact microscopic charge-transfer mechanism; quantitative reliability supplied by parallel fibers; prevalence of direct electron transfer from associated microbial partners; global magnitude of cable-bacterium effects on element cycling.

3. Disputed or not established: universal sediment conduction by cable bacteria; neuron-like cognition; broad climate-control claims; immediate scalable electronics applications.

4. Speculative synthesis: cable bacteria as a case where biological individuality is usefully analyzed at the scale of an electrical circuit spanning many cells.

## Revision conditions

The central argument would require major revision if direct filament conductance were shown to be artifactual or primarily ionic, if intact cable bacteria were not the dominant conductive path in the best-characterized electrogenic sulfur-oxidation systems, or if the nickel-bearing fibers proved incidental rather than functionally tied to long-range electron transport. Ecological extrapolations must be revised independently as larger system budgets become available.
