# The Creature in the System

A machine accepts a command without first deciding whether the command interests it. That banal property explains much of engineering. Animals lack it.

The intelligence services that experimented with animals were not merely being eccentric. They were buying capabilities that technology could not yet package at useful size, weight, power, stealth, or cost. A pigeon could navigate home while carrying almost nothing. A dolphin could search turbid water with a sensory system built by evolution rather than procurement. A sea lion could dive, discriminate, maneuver, and return. Each animal arrived as an integrated system whose manufacturing history was measured in millions of years.

The bargain contained a hidden term. The platform had motives.

That makes animal intelligence programs unusually revealing. They expose a problem now returning in autonomous machines: how much control must a system surrender before it can exploit competence it did not itself design? The answer is not that animals were primitive drones. The answer is almost the opposite. They were useful precisely because they were not drones.
