# What Evolution Already Solved

Engineers sometimes approach biology as a catalog of clever mechanisms. Echolocation. Olfaction. Homing. Adhesion. Schooling. That catalog is real but incomplete. Evolution's deeper achievement is integration under severe constraints.

A dolphin does not carry separate procurement programs for propulsion, obstacle avoidance, acoustic sensing, pressure tolerance, energy storage, repair, and navigation. The systems grew together. Their interfaces were tested by survival rather than by a design review.

This is why copying one biological trick can disappoint. The copied feature depended on a body, behavior, and environment that the engineered system lacks. Intelligence programs that used whole animals bypassed that decomposition problem, but inherited agency and unpredictability instead.

There is no free route. Build the machine and pay the integration cost. Recruit the organism and pay the autonomy cost. Biomimicry becomes interesting when it stops pretending those costs can be wished away.
