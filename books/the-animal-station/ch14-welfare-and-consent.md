# Welfare and Consent

Animals cannot consent to military or intelligence service in the human sense. That fact does not end the analysis; it begins an ethical obligation that technical histories often treat as scenery.

Modern Navy research operates under animal-use and veterinary requirements, and official programs emphasize health and longevity. Those structures matter because capability does not erase dependency. A trained animal's usefulness is inseparable from human control over food, housing, transport, medical care, and exposure to operational risk.

Historical programs should be judged with attention to their period without laundering harms through nostalgia. The amusing names make that easy to do. A cat wired for collection or a bird carrying equipment can become a charming anecdote once the animal disappears behind the gadget.

A serious history keeps the creature visible. The ethical question is not whether humans may ever work with animals. It is what obligations arise when another species becomes part of a security system designed around human purposes.
