# Beyond Biomimicry

The obvious lesson from spy animals is to copy nature better. The less obvious lesson is to copy the relationship between sensing and action.

Animals do not passively wait for complete information. They move to change what can be sensed. They allocate attention. They revisit uncertain cues. They exploit memory and context. They sometimes choose not to act. These properties are increasingly relevant to autonomous sensors, but they are not captured by copying an eye or sonar pulse.

The animal is an active observer. Intelligence collection becomes more powerful when the observer can reposition itself, test hypotheses, and seek discriminating evidence. It also becomes harder to audit because observation and intervention blur together.

The old programs therefore point toward a modern design problem: how to build systems that are curious enough to collect useful evidence without becoming so autonomous that operators can no longer explain what they did or why.
