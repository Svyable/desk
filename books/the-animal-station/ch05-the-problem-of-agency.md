# The Problem of Agency

Engineers often use the word platform to flatten differences. Aircraft, trucks, satellites, ships, dogs, dolphins: all can carry payloads. The noun is convenient and dangerous.

An animal is not simply a platform with stochastic behavior. It perceives the mission environment according to its own sensory priorities. It learns associations the trainer may not intend. It can generalize, refuse, improvise, anticipate reward, become stressed, and change with age. These properties are not noise surrounding the capability. They are part of the capability.

The more sophisticated the task, the more the handler depends on judgment inside the animal. A dolphin searching for a mine-like object cannot be micromanaged through every meter of water. A dog following odor must resolve a chemical world its handler cannot directly inspect. The system works by delegating perception.

That delegation is why animal programs belong in the history of autonomy. They forced institutions to operate competent agents whose internal representations were only partly legible decades before software engineers began using similar language.
