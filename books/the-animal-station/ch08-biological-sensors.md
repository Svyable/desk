# Biological Sensors

The phrase biological sensor invites a category error. A sensor in an engineering diagram converts a physical quantity into a signal. An animal does considerably more. It filters, attends, predicts, compares, moves to improve its own observation, and changes behavior according to context.

Dogs demonstrate this on land through olfaction. Dolphins demonstrate it underwater through biosonar. The useful output is rarely a raw measurement. It is a trained decision: investigate this location, indicate this odor, mark this object.

That compression is both gift and liability. The handler receives an answer without direct access to every intermediate variable. A machine can log voltage traces; an animal's evidence is partly private. Training and controlled testing become the calibration procedure.

Intelligence organizations are therefore forced into an epistemology familiar to experimental psychology. What exactly does the subject know? Which cue produced the response? Can an unintended handler cue explain performance? Does success transfer to unfamiliar environments? A remarkable sense is not automatically a reliable instrument.
