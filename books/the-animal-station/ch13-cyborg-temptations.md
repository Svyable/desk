# Cyborg Temptations

The cyborg proposal is seductive because it appears to split the difference. Let biology provide locomotion, sensing, healing, and energy management; add electronics for communication or steering. The result seems cheaper than building the whole organism from scratch.

The difficulty is that organisms are not modular in the way procurement diagrams imply. Neural control is entangled with motivation, sensation, stress, and homeostasis. An intervention that produces movement in a demonstration may not produce robust navigation in weather, crowds, predators, or unfamiliar terrain.

Public research into insect-scale interfaces and biohybrid systems proves that such manipulation can be scientifically real without proving an operational intelligence capability. This distinction matters. A laboratory demonstration is evidence of a mechanism. It is not evidence of a deployed fleet.

The history of animal espionage gives us a useful prior: whenever a proposal seems to obtain an extraordinary platform for free, look for the behavioral invoice.
