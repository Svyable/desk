# Dolphins at the Harbor Mouth

The U.S. Navy's marine-mammal program is not rumor. Official Navy and Defense Department material describes bottlenose dolphins and California sea lions trained to detect, locate, mark, and recover objects and threats in harbors, coastal areas, and open water. The program began exploring species in the late 1950s and 1960s before concentrating on those two.

The dolphin's attraction is obvious once the ocean is treated as an information problem. Visibility collapses. Sound behaves strangely. Currents move objects. Human divers tire. Electronics face packaging, power, corrosion, and false-alarm constraints. A dolphin enters with mobility and biosonar already integrated.

Official descriptions include mine detection and protection against underwater threats. They do not justify every tale attached to military dolphins, especially stories of exotic offensive missions. The disciplined claim is impressive enough: a modern navy still finds evolved perception operationally useful.

That fact should make technologists uncomfortable in a productive way. It means some sensing problems are not waiting for invention. They are waiting for us to admit that another lineage solved a version first.
