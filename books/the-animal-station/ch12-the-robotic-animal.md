# The Robotic Animal

CIA's public museum and historical material includes robotic animal forms, including a catfish-like underwater vehicle. Once engineers imitate an animal rather than recruit one, the design problem changes.

The robot keeps some advantages of appearance and morphology while surrendering biological autonomy. It can be commanded, instrumented, logged, repaired, and replicated. But it must now pay for propulsion, sensing, control, power, and robustness that the living animal carried as a package.

Biomimicry can therefore be read as a negotiation. Which biological features are worth copying? Shape? Locomotion? sensory geometry? camouflage? collective behavior? Copy too literally and engineering inherits constraints without the organism's integrated compensations. Copy too abstractly and the advantage disappears.

The animal programs sit at one end of this spectrum and animal-shaped machines at another. Between them lies the recurring dream of the cyborg: keep evolution's hardware, replace just enough control.
