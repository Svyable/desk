# Acoustic Kitty

Acoustic Kitty survives in public memory because the phrase sounds invented. The underlying lesson does not require embellishment. Intelligence engineers explored whether an ordinary animal could carry concealed listening equipment into spaces where a conspicuous human or device could not.

Accounts of the project's details vary, and some of the most repeated anecdotes are poorly sourced. That is exactly why the case belongs here. The verified core should be kept separate from the folklore accreted around it. The interesting engineering question survives either way: can an animal whose ordinary behavior provides camouflage be made to produce repeatable collection geometry?

A microphone is not useful merely because it is hidden. It must be near the right conversation, oriented well enough, powered long enough, recoverable or transmissive, and attached to a carrier whose behavior does not ruin the collection. Every requirement tightens the leash on the very autonomy that made the animal inconspicuous.

The cat is therefore less a punch line than a systems diagram with fur drawn over the interfaces.
