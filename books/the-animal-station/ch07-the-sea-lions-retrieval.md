# The Sea Lion's Retrieval

California sea lions occupy a different niche in the Navy program. Their value includes finding and attaching recovery hardware to objects at depth. A human team can then retrieve what the animal has located or marked.

This is not glamorous espionage. It is better: a concrete task with measurable success and failure. The animal must travel through a three-dimensional environment, discriminate a target, perform a trained action, and return. The useful intelligence is embodied in a sequence of perception and movement rather than in a report.

Such work complicates the standard story of automation replacing biology. In many systems the real competition is not animal versus machine. It is animal-machine team versus either component alone. Humans supply mission intent, logistics, veterinary care, interpretation, and recovery infrastructure. The animal supplies capabilities difficult to duplicate under the same environmental constraints.

The result is not mystical. It is a hybrid system whose interfaces happen to include trust, training, appetite, and attention.
