# Training as Interface Design

Training is usually described as something done to the animal. Operationally it is also the construction of an interface between species.

The handler needs a vocabulary of cues the animal can distinguish and responses the human can interpret. Reward schedules shape persistence. Repetition creates expectations. The environment contributes cues whether the designer wants them or not. A sloppy protocol can train the wrong behavior while producing apparently excellent results.

This is why animal work and autonomous-system work rhyme without being identical. In both cases the operator specifies goals through an interface rather than by controlling every internal step. In both, benchmark success can hide shortcut learning. In both, distribution shift matters: the field is not the training pool.

The older programs therefore deserve attention beyond their gadget value. They are records of humans learning to command systems whose competence exceeded their transparency. The best handlers did not pretend the animal was deterministic. They designed procedures around the fact that it was alive.
