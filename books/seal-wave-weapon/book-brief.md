# SEAL: Weaponizing the Ocean

## Thesis

Project SEAL is not interesting because the Allies secretly possessed a working “tsunami bomb.” The public record does not establish that. It is interesting because serious wartime institutions tested the boundary between an observed physical effect and an operational weapon, then left behind exactly the kind of archive from which later culture tends to delete the failed engineering and preserve the terrifying noun.

The book follows that deletion. Underwater explosions can make waves. Thomas Leech and wartime collaborators investigated whether carefully arranged explosive charges could make waves useful in amphibious warfare. Small-scale trials occurred; a full-scale destructive tsunami weapon was not demonstrated. Later technical work on explosion-generated water waves supplies a second evidence stream: scaling is the whole problem. A spectacular local effect is not the same proposition as an efficient long-range weapon.

That collision—classified weapons research plus nonlinear geophysics—makes SEAL a book about how secret programs become legends when mechanism, scale, military utility, and archival provenance are collapsed into one sentence.

## Evidence classes

1. **Verified / documented:** Project SEAL existed; Thomas D. J. Leech led relevant New Zealand work; the New Zealand DSIR published *Project SEAL: The Generation of Waves by Means of Explosives* in 1950; archival holdings exist in New Zealand; wartime and postwar official interest is documented in released records.
2. **Credible but incomplete:** press reporting based on released New Zealand files describes U.S./British interest, Karl Compton’s involvement, Whangaparāoa trials, and postwar continuation. These claims require reconstruction against the underlying files before promotion to book-level fact at full specificity.
3. **Disputed / unconfirmed:** popular claims that SEAL had already produced a practical city-destroying weapon, that a specific Japanese city was an approved target, or that later natural tsunamis were covert weapons events.
4. **Speculative synthesis:** secrecy plus scale-insensitive retelling systematically converts experimental possibility into retrospective capability. This is the book’s interpretation, not an archival fact.

## Defeat condition

If the primary record shows that the often-repeated scale claims were merely speculative calculations with no validated scaling law, the book must say so. If primary records instead demonstrate a validated operational design, the manuscript must change accordingly. Neither outcome is protected in advance.
