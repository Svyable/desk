# Source notes and evidence ledger

## Primary / authoritative spine

- Archives New Zealand, research portal and Collections search: https://www.archives.govt.nz/ and https://collections.archives.govt.nz/ . Use to resolve the released Project SEAL files at item level; archive notes that many records require reading-room access and that publication of archival reproductions can require Crown-copyright permission.
- Thomas D. J. Leech / New Zealand Department of Scientific and Industrial Research, *Project SEAL: The Generation of Waves by Means of Explosives* (Wellington, 1950). WorldCat record: https://search.worldcat.org/title/31071831 . Treat the bibliographic record as proof of publication/existence, not as evidence for technical claims until the report itself is acquired.

## Secondary bridge to underlying files

- *New Zealand Herald*, “Tsunami bomb NZ's devastating war secret” (1999): https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/tsunami-bomb-nzs-devastating-war-secret/AEHW23IAWH3JRLUTZR4PRAPS64/ . Reports from released MFAT papers that U.S. and British officials remained interested after the war, that Karl Compton visited New Zealand, and quotes a July 1946 Washington-to-Wellington letter. All specifics remain queued for file-level verification.
- *Independent*, 26 September 1999, “Experimental bomb to create huge tidal wave was tested in 1944”: https://www.independent.co.uk/news/experimental-bomb-to-create-huge-tidal-wave-was-tested-in-1944-1122610.html . Corroborating contemporary report based on the New Zealand release; not independent primary proof.

## Claims requiring provenance reconstruction

- “3,700 tests/explosions”: locate experimental log/report denominator. Does this mean detonations, measurements, charge units, or trial runs?
- “2,000,000 kg over 8 km”: recover exact calculation, geometry, water depth, assumed target bathymetry, and confidence language. Never write it as demonstrated weapon performance unless the source warrants that.
- “as powerful/important as the atomic bomb”: identify speaker, date, exact wording, and whether this was advocacy, assessment, or later paraphrase.
- Japan as target: do not state without operational planning record.
- Bikini connection: distinguish proposed data-sharing/scientific comparison from any claim that SEAL became a nuclear tsunami weapon.

## Contradictions / incentives

Military sponsors had incentives to preserve potentially useful research under wartime uncertainty. Engineers had incentives to continue technically interesting work. Later newspapers had incentives to foreground “tsunami bomb,” an accurate shorthand for the aspiration but a poor description of demonstrated capability. Post-2004 conspiracy ecosystems have an incentive to use SEAL’s documented existence as collateral for unrelated claims. None of these incentives independently decides the evidence.

## Publication holds

No numerical performance claim enters final prose without the underlying report or archival file. No quotation migrates from a newspaper paraphrase if the original document can be obtained. No natural tsunami is described as weapon-caused absent event-specific evidence. Patents, if later used, establish filing and claimed design only.
