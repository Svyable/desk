# SEAL — chapter architecture

1. **Nouméa, 1944** — Begin with the physical event: explosives underwater, a wave, and the dangerous distance between seeing an effect and possessing a weapon.
2. **A Wave Is Not a Tsunami** — Definitions, dispersion, depth, wavelength, bathymetry, shoaling, breaking.
3. **Thomas Leech** — Reconstruct the engineer before the legend.
4. **Halsey’s Problem** — Amphibious warfare and why inundation looked useful in the Pacific.
5. **Whangaparāoa** — Trial geography, instrumentation, charge placement, what was actually measured.
6. **Three Thousand Seven Hundred Explosions** — Audit the famous number against logs and denominators.
7. **Scaling Up** — Why laboratory and harbor effects refuse simple multiplication.
8. **Two Million Kilograms** — Trace the provenance of the canonical explosive estimate and test what it meant.
9. **Bikini** — Compton, nuclear testing, and the documented versus imagined connection.
10. **The Weapon That Missed Its War** — Operational timing, Japanese surrender, and postwar continuation.
11. **Breaking on the Shelf** — Later hydrodynamic work and the energy lost before a wave reaches a coast.
12. **How to Hide a Failure** — Classification, compartmentalization, and why partial archives preserve possibility better than performance.
13. **Tsunami Bomb** — The phrase enters public culture and starts doing work the engineering did not.
14. **After 2004** — How a real natural catastrophe became attached to weapon folklore; provenance without laundering conspiracy claims.
15. **Geophysical Weapons** — Compare documented environmental-modification research without treating adjacency as equivalence.
16. **The Denominator Problem** — How many strange military experiments fail, and why survivors dominate memory.
17. **Capability Is a Chain** — Effect → controllability → scaling → delivery → repeatability → military advantage.
18. **Keep the Failed Machine** — What SEAL teaches us about evaluating modern classified-technology claims.

Each chapter must preserve the four evidence classes in notes and distinguish experiment, calculation, demonstrated scaling, and operational capability.
