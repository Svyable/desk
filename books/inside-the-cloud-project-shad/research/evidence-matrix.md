# Evidence matrix

## Class 1 — verified / documented

Project 112 and SHAD existed; Deseret Test Center planned and conducted chemical/biological vulnerability testing; SHAD was the shipboard component; active agents, simulants, tracers and decontaminants were used across the program; approximately 5,900–6,000 servicemembers are reported as participants; DoD began declassifying and sharing records with VA around 2000; participant notification began in 2002; SHAD II studied 5,868 participants and 6,753 comparison veterans and found no clear overall long-term health difference in available data.

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete / model-dependent

Individual exposure intensity; test-specific dose; protective status for every participant; completeness of reconstructed rosters; health effects in small exposure subgroups; interpretation of participant memories decades later; exact relationship between classification decisions and delayed medical recognition.

## Class 3 — disputed / not established

A broad SHAD-caused chronic disease syndrome; intentional injury of servicemembers as the program objective; exposure of every SHAD participant to live warfare agents; causal attribution of an individual veteran’s illness from program membership alone; claims that negative epidemiology proves no participant could have been harmed.

## Class 4 — speculative synthesis

Secrecy can operate as a post-exposure multiplier by delaying clinical context, cohort construction, participant notification and independent scrutiny even after physical exposure has ended. SHAD may therefore be read as an information-hazard case as much as a chemical/biological-defense case.

## Defeat conditions

The book’s secrecy thesis weakens if records show participants were consistently informed in clinically useful detail soon after tests, if declassification delays had no material effect on roster construction or health inquiry, or if the later reconstruction was substantially complete much earlier than current public chronology suggests. The health chapter must change if stronger well-controlled evidence establishes a reproducible exposure-specific long-term effect.
