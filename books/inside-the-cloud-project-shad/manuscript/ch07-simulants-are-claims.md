# Simulants Are Claims

A simulant is an argument embodied in material. It says: for the property we care about, this substance can stand in for something more dangerous. The claim may concern particle size, aerosol persistence, detectability or dispersal. It does not mean the substitute is biologically inert in every circumstance.

Historical biological-defense work repeatedly depended on organisms regarded at the time as suitable surrogates. The right retrospective question is not whether later knowledge makes earlier scientists look foolish. It is what safety evidence existed then, what endpoints were considered, what populations were assumed, and whether the surrogate’s convenience outran its characterization.

For SHAD, this matters twice. First, some veterans were associated with simulant rather than active-agent tests. Second, later epidemiology has to resist grouping chemically and biologically unlike exposures into a single moral category. “Test participant” is a historical status, not a dose.

The book will therefore keep two ledgers: what each substance was meant to simulate, and what was known about the substance itself. If those ledgers diverge, that divergence is evidence. If they do not, suspicion alone cannot manufacture it.
