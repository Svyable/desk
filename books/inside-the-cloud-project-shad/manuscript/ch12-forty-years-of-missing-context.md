# Forty Years of Missing Context

A secret exposure ages differently from an ordinary one. The chemistry may leave the body; the uncertainty does not.

By the 1990s, some SHAD veterans were reporting health concerns to VA. The program had been designed for a different era, under a different threat model, and the people asking questions were now older men with the ordinary accumulation of disease that comes with age. That is an epidemiologist’s nightmare and a conspiracy theory’s ideal habitat.

Long delay damages inference in several directions. Individual dose may never have been measured. Records disappear or remain compartmented. Recall becomes less precise. Comparison populations accumulate other military exposures. Diseases with long latency become more relevant just as causal attribution becomes harder.

This is why the later absence of a clear overall SHAD health signal should be treated seriously without being treated as omniscience. Negative epidemiology can reduce the plausibility of a large common effect. It cannot reconstruct every missing dose or prove that no individual was harmed.

The lost decades are therefore not merely empty time between test and disclosure. They changed the kind of answer that would still be scientifically possible.
