# SHAD II

The second National Academies study added years of follow-up and additional health-record sources to the earlier work. Its study population included 5,868 SHAD participants and 6,753 comparison veterans.

The broad result was not evidence of a large overall difference in long-term health outcomes between the groups. The committee reported no statistically significant overall mortality difference and no clear overall morbidity difference in the available data. An earlier heart-disease concern did not persist as a general finding in the later analysis.

That result belongs near the center of this book. It is substantial counterevidence to claims of a broad, easily detectable SHAD-caused syndrome. It does not answer every individual causal question, because historical exposure reconstruction and observational health studies have limits.

This distinction keeps two arguments from being improperly fused. The ethics and administration of classified testing and delayed disclosure can be evaluated from documents, policy and participant experience. Long-term medical effects require epidemiological and clinical evidence. A conclusion in one domain does not automatically settle the other.

The documentary story remains consequential even when the health study refuses to provide a dramatic ending.
