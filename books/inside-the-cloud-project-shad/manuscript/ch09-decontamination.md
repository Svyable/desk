# Decontamination

Decontamination is where an elegant defensive concept meets paint seams, ducts, fabric, bilges, skin, tools and time. A warship cannot simply be thrown away after exposure. The military problem was how to restore function without spreading contamination while doing it.

SHAD used decontaminants as well as agents, simulants and tracers. That complicates retrospective exposure. A sailor’s relevant contact may not be limited to the challenge material; cleaning chemistry and the work of recovery belong in the ledger too.

The eventual chapter will follow one or more documented decontamination sequences closely: what was applied, where samples were taken, what counted as clean, what protective equipment was used, and whether the process generated secondary exposure opportunities. Until those records are page-indexed, the manuscript will not infer dose from job title.

There is a larger point hiding in the hoses. Institutions often document the hazard they are testing more carefully than the ordinary labor required to clean up after the test. If SHAD’s records show that pattern, it will matter. If they do not, the book should say so. The archive gets the last word.
