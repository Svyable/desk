# Finding the Rosters

Before anyone could ask whether SHAD veterans were sicker, somebody had to decide who counted as a SHAD veteran.

That sounds trivial until the program is forty years old. The test center is closed. Records live in different services and locations. A ship’s crew list does not necessarily identify who performed a particular test task. A test document may name a unit without naming every man.

DoD’s reconstruction effort produced rosters that allowed VA notification and later epidemiology. The National Academies’ SHAD II population included 5,868 participants. The number is an achievement of archival work, not a natural fact that had been waiting in a drawer.

Cohort construction matters because every later rate inherits it. Miss exposed people and the signal can dilute or disappear elsewhere. Include people whose exposure opportunity was weak and the exposed group becomes heterogeneous. Choose a poor comparison population and military service itself becomes a confounder.

The full chapter will treat roster-building as scientific instrumentation. A cohort is a measuring device made out of names. Its calibration begins in the archive.
