# Architecture

Working progression for a full 65,000–80,000-word trade nonfiction book. Chapter titles are provisional. Each chapter must earn a distinct evidentiary job rather than restating the signal/effect/intent distinction.

1. **The Beam** — Establish the positive control: a real hidden stimulus, uncertain purpose, contested consequence. Introduce signal/effect/intent without solving it.
2. **Five Microwatts** — Reconstruct the measurement history. Frequency, power density, spatial distribution, duty cycle, instrumentation, changing estimates, and why a wrong classified number is still wrong.
3. **PANDORA** — Why ARPA and other agencies investigated biological effects; institutional chronology, contracts, hypotheses, and what “studied” does and does not mean.
4. **The Monkey Worked** — Reconstruct primate experiments, especially the exposure-estimate correction and behavioral endpoints. Ask what changed when the simulated signal became closer to the measured one.
5. **Blood Under Cover** — Medical sampling, cover stories, secrecy rationales, consent, counterintelligence, and the long epistemic cost of concealing why data were collected.
6. **What Was Moscow Doing?** — Competing operational hypotheses: passive-device activation/interrogation, collection, jamming, harassment, biological effect. Separate documentary support from later inference.
7. **The Thing** — The Great Seal passive resonator as an existence proof that externally illuminated espionage hardware was real, while refusing to identify it automatically as the Moscow Signal’s purpose.
8. **A Building Full of Receivers** — RF engineering of structures, electronics, unintended demodulation, shielding, apertures, and why “directed energy” names a delivery mode rather than an effect.
9. **The People in the Building** — Reconstruct exposure cohorts and the Lilienfeld Foreign Service health study: denominator, comparator posts, outcomes, follow-up, exposure classification, and statistical power.
10. **The Null Is Not Nothing** — What negative epidemiology can constrain; later methodological criticism; why uncertainty cannot become a universal rescue mechanism.
11. **Standards Are Not Biology** — U.S. versus Soviet exposure standards, thermal/nonthermal debates, regulatory precaution, and the invalid move from different limits to demonstrated injury.
12. **Secret-Exposure Debt** — Compare Moscow with other historical classified or delayed-disclosure exposures. Test whether secrecy predictably damages later causal reconstruction or whether ordinary archival decay explains as much.
13. **Causal Overhang** — How real classified events accumulate explanations after disclosure. Compare with cases where institutional investigation later became folklore of validation.
14. **The Havana Shortcut** — Carefully compare Moscow with later anomalous health incidents. No event-level causal claim without matching exposure physics and clinical evidence.
15. **The Weapon That Has to Close** — Quantitative burden for an operational directed-energy biological claim: source, propagation, target field, coupling, endpoint, dose-response, repeatability, concealment, and operational advantage.
16. **What Intelligence Knows When It Does Not Know** — Decision-making under uncertainty: why rational agencies investigate hypotheses they do not believe, and how outsiders misread that portfolio as a secret consensus.
17. **A Better PANDORA** — Design a modern preregistered, blinded, parameter-closed experiment and epidemiological protocol that would distinguish exposure, expectation, device artifact, thermal effect, nonthermal effect, and chance.
18. **Leave the Triangle Open** — Return to signal/effect/intent. State what the archive establishes, what remains genuinely unresolved, and what evidence would force revision.

## Progression guardrails

The book must not become a Havana Syndrome book with Moscow as prologue. Moscow remains the governing object. Later cases appear only where they stress-test a method developed from the archive.

The book must not turn “the signal was real” into “the weapon was real.” The title is deliberately dangerous; every chapter has to police the distinction.

The Great Seal bug is a comparator, not an identification claim. Patent existence is never efficacy evidence. Classification is never efficacy evidence. Institutional attention is never efficacy evidence.

## Strongest competing explanation

The simplest durable account may be that the Soviets used microwave energy for an intelligence/electronic purpose unrelated to harming personnel; U.S. officials prudently investigated biological possibilities because they did not know the purpose; the feared behavioral effects failed to emerge robustly at corrected exposure levels; later health data did not establish a clear broad adverse effect; and secrecy plus incomplete exposure reconstruction magnified the episode’s explanatory afterlife. The manuscript must be able to lose to this account.

## Defeat condition

The book’s broader `secret-exposure debt` hypothesis is weakened if matched nonclassified hazardous-exposure programs from the same period show equally poor person-level exposure reconstruction, disclosure lag, follow-up, and causal uncertainty. `Causal overhang` is weakened if blinded readers cannot distinguish later explanatory inflation in secret cases from comparable public technical controversies.
