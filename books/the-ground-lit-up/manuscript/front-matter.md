# THE GROUND LIT UP

*What earthquake lights teach us about signals before catastrophe*

Sven Hardy Benson

## A note about evidence

This book begins with an awkward object: a light in the sky reported near an earthquake. The object is awkward because almost every part of that sentence can fail independently. A witness can misidentify a transformer flash. A camera can saturate. A real luminous event can coincide with shaking without preceding it. A physical precursor can exist without being useful for prediction. A proposed mechanism can reproduce one part of the phenomenon while failing its energy budget.

So four bins stay separate.

**Documented.** Instrumented earthquakes, dated photographs or video with recoverable context, geological setting, laboratory measurements of stressed rock, published field measurements, and quantitative models whose assumptions can be inspected.

**Credible but incomplete.** Historical or modern reports with good temporal and geographic context but incomplete instrumental corroboration; repeatable laboratory effects whose transfer to crustal scale is unresolved; correlations that survive some controls but have not become prospective predictors.

**Disputed.** Reports whose timing, location, provenance, alternative electrical sources, weather, camera artifacts, or chain of custody cannot be adequately reconstructed; claims that earthquake lights constitute a validated forecasting tool; broad claims that unrelated luminous anomalies share one geophysical mechanism.

**Speculative.** The synthesis developed here: that earthquake lights may be useful less as a single solved phenomenon than as a test case for how science should handle rare precursor claims. A signal can be physically real, causally related, and still nearly useless for prediction if its base rate, specificity, timing, detection probability, or false-positive ecology is wrong.

The book will not turn an eyewitness report into a voltmeter. It will not turn a paper proposing stress-activated charge carriers into proof that every reported light came from stressed rock. It will not treat an association with rift environments as a forecasting system. It will not import UFO, plasma, animal-behavior, ionospheric, or electromagnetic claims into the earthquake-light category merely because the vocabulary overlaps.

The governing distinction is simple and severe: **a precursor is not a prediction until the denominator exists.**
