# THE GROUND LIT UP — evidence ledger

Research state: opening-pass source map. Public/lawful sources only. Claims remain at the evidence class stated here until primary extraction supports promotion.

## Selected collision

Earthquake lights + stress-activated rock electricity + quantitative atmospheric-discharge modeling + the statistical problem of earthquake precursors.

Book-level question: **Can a rare anomaly be physically real and causally related to earthquakes while still failing as a prediction signal?**

This is distinct from the recent ball-lightning manuscript. That project asks whether an appearance-based luminous category splits into physical families after measurement. This project asks what happens when a claimed luminous phenomenon is promoted into a *precursor*, where base rates, lead time, specificity and prospective testing become part of the physics claim.

## Class 1 — verified / documented

### USGS publication record: St-Laurent, Derr & Freund (2006)

- F. St-Laurent, J. S. Derr, F. T. Freund, “Earthquake lights and the stress-activation of positive hole charge carriers in rocks,” *Physics and Chemistry of the Earth* 31 (2006), 305–312.
- USGS record: https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70028206
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pce.2006.02.003
- What it establishes: a published mechanism proposal linking stress-activated charge carriers in rock to surface dielectric breakdown/corona and optical/RF emission.
- What it does **not** establish: that the mechanism caused any particular historical light report; that all EQL reports share one mechanism; operational earthquake prediction.

### Thériault et al. (2014)

- Robert Thériault, France St-Laurent, Friedemann T. Freund, John S. Derr, “Prevalence of Earthquake Lights Associated with Rift Environments,” *Seismological Research Letters* 85(1), 159–178.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1785/0220130059
- Corpus: 65 selected/documented cases described in the paper and contemporary summaries.
- Reported association: strong concentration near rift environments/subvertical faults.
- Hold: recover full methods, inclusion criteria, case table and denominator before manuscript uses percentages or converts the association into a population claim.

### Jánský & Pasko (2018)

- J. Jánský and V. P. Pasko, “Earthquake Lights: Mechanism of Electrical Coupling of Earth's Crust to the Lower Atmosphere,” *Journal of Geophysical Research: Atmospheres* (2018).
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1029/2018JD028489
- Open article: https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2018JD028489
- Quantitative boundary: their modeled deep, large-scale dipole example (poles at 5 and 15 km) requires ~1.1×10^19 J for 1 s, exceeding the ~10^16 J seismic-wave energy reference they use for a M7.5 event; moving the upper source to tens of meters depth can reduce required electrical energy to ~10^12 J.
- Interpretation: source geometry and depth are decisive. This is a constraint on a mechanism, not validation of a historical report.

### USGS public position

- USGS earthquake-light public material should be recovered directly before quotation.
- Current authoritative summaries describe scientific disagreement: some geophysicists doubt individual reports establish EQL; others regard at least some as plausible.
- Publication hold: no claim that USGS “recognizes” EQL as a validated precursor.

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete

- Well-contextualized historical reports tied to known earthquakes but lacking calibrated optical instrumentation.
- Modern video where time/place can be established but grid failures, lightning, weather, camera artifacts or exact relation to rupture remain incompletely controlled.
- Laboratory stress/electrical effects that are reproducible but whose crust-to-atmosphere scaling remains uncertain.
- Geological association from selected case corpora before an adequate prospective denominator exists.

## Class 3 — disputed / unconfirmed

- Earthquake lights as a validated earthquake forecasting method.
- Any claim that a luminous report days/weeks before a quake is causal merely because it preceded the event.
- Claims that animal behavior, ionospheric anomalies, ULF signals, radon changes and earthquake lights are manifestations of one established coupling mechanism.
- Social-media “earthquake light” videos without recoverable provenance and exclusion of grid/weather alternatives.
- UFO attribution for earthquake-associated lights absent independent evidence.

## Class 4 — speculative synthesis / open conjecture

### Precursor inflation

A real measured effect can be promoted through increasingly strong claims without new evidence: laboratory electrical effect → field anomaly → earthquake-associated anomaly → precursor → predictor → operational warning system. Each arrow requires its own denominator and test.

**Defeat condition:** if a single evidence stream closes those arrows prospectively with predefined thresholds and independent replication, the framework becomes unnecessary and should be cut.

### Retrospective abundance

Large numbers of channels and time windows inspected after a rare event make apparently prophetic anomalies easy to discover.

**Defeat condition:** preregistered prospective monitoring that preserves the effect at useful sensitivity/specificity sharply weakens this explanation.

### The denominator rule

A precursor claim requires, at minimum: number of target earthquakes, non-earthquake observation time, spatial alarm area, lead-time window, detection probability, false-positive rate, instrumentation duty cycle, and predefined event classification. Without these, “seen before earthquakes” cannot become a forecast-performance claim.

## Contradictions and incentive audit

- **Witness incentive:** salience increases after disaster; memory and reporting are not independent of knowing the earthquake occurred.
- **Media incentive:** visually spectacular precursor stories outperform null results and transformer failures.
- **Research incentive:** a mechanism associated with earthquake prediction can attract attention beyond what a bounded atmospheric-electricity result receives.
- **Skeptical incentive:** dismissing the category wholesale can erase physically interesting rare events merely because forecasting claims are weak.
- **Infrastructure confound:** modern power systems generate luminous failures during shaking; historical corpora and modern video therefore have different false-positive ecologies.
- **Selection problem:** a curated catalogue of best cases is useful for mechanism generation but cannot by itself supply incidence or predictive value.

## Strongest conventional countermodel

There is no practically useful pre-seismic optical signal. The historical category combines ordinary electrical/weather phenomena, co-seismic infrastructure failures, camera artifacts, rare unrelated atmospheric lights and retrospective selection. Stress-induced rock electricity may exist in laboratory or shallow/local settings without generating a repeatable field-scale precursor.

The manuscript must allow this model to win.

## Prospective experiment the book should earn

Deploy continuously operating, calibrated optical stations across multiple active fault systems with co-located electric-field, magnetic, RF and meteorological instruments. Integrate lightning and electrical-grid event logs. Predefine luminous-event classifiers, spatial radius, lead windows and target earthquake magnitude before observation. Maintain matched control regions and publish continuous exposure time, not just events. Blind optical classification to future seismic outcomes where practical. Report false alarms as prominently as apparent hits.

The key result is not “did we see a light?” It is the likelihood ratio supplied by a defined optical/electromagnetic event for a defined future earthquake under continuous observation.

## Publication holds

- Do not say earthquake lights are proven.
- Do not say earthquake lights predict earthquakes.
- Do not use “65 cases” as an incidence denominator.
- Do not imply a rift association proves the positive-hole mechanism.
- Do not use the 2018 model as evidence that the required source currents actually occur.
- Do not convert eyewitness timing into calibrated lead time.
- Do not merge co-seismic and pre-seismic lights in a predictive analysis.
- Do not use patents, proposals or model feasibility as evidence of operational efficacy.
