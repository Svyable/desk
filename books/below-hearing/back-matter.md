# Research Apparatus

## Four evidence classes

**Class 1 — verified / documented.** Infrasound is a real long-range atmospheric-wave phenomenon; nuclear explosions, volcanoes, meteors, storms, rockets and other energetic events can generate it. The CTBT verification architecture includes a global infrasound network. Declassified U.S. intelligence records document official interest in Soviet acoustic bioeffects research.

**Class 2 — credible but incomplete.** Historical technical-intelligence uses beyond publicly specified treaty monitoring; exact Cold War array performance and collection priorities; some participant accounts and partially released program details.

**Class 3 — disputed / unconfirmed.** Claims of operational long-range infrasonic mind-control weapons, magic-frequency incapacitation independent of dose, or attribution of particular unexplained illnesses/events to covert infrasound without measured exposure.

**Class 4 — synthesis / conjecture.** The proposal that the history of infrasound offers a general model for anomalous research: compare the institutional maturation of a real weak-signal capability with adjacent claims that never acquired calibrated public infrastructure.

## Contradiction and incentive audit

Intelligence agencies have incentives to investigate weak possibilities because surprise is costly. That makes funding a poor proxy for efficacy. Adversaries have incentives to exaggerate capabilities, conceal failures and seed uncertainty. Contractors and researchers can benefit from threat salience. Popular writers benefit from collapsing 'studied' into 'proved.' Skeptics can make the opposite error by treating the presence of folklore as evidence that no underlying physical effect exists.

## Defeat conditions

The weapon-history synthesis should be weakened if primary records demonstrate reproducible, operationally useful infrasonic behavioral effects at remote-relevant exposures, or strengthened if controlled dose-response literature and program records repeatedly fail to bridge the source-power/range/effect gap. The detection-history account should be revised wherever declassified program records contradict the public chronology.

## Remaining research

Retrieve primary U.S. nuclear-detection infrasound histories; early microbarograph network records; AFTAC and national-laboratory material where public; Soviet primary acoustic literature behind the 1972 assessment; modern controlled human low-frequency exposure studies; atmospheric propagation and array-processing papers; bolide and volcanic case studies; and quantitative source-power calculations for alleged weapon scenarios.
