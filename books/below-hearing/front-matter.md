# Below Hearing

There are events the atmosphere reports before any witness does. Not with a voice. With pressure.

A nuclear explosion pushes the air. A bolide does. A volcano does. A rocket climbing through layers of wind does. The signal can sink below twenty hertz, beneath ordinary hearing, and keep going for hundreds or thousands of kilometres. Human silence is not atmospheric silence.

This book began with a peculiar collision. The intelligence record contains serious work on infrasound as a means of detecting remote energetic events, and also Cold War assessments of Soviet interest in low-frequency sound as a possible means of altering behaviour. One line matured into treaty verification. The other accumulated anecdotes, weak experiments, frightening frequency numbers and eventually a mythology of invisible acoustic weapons.

They deserve to be put on the same table without being confused.

The governing rule is simple: detection physics is not bioeffects physics; bioeffects are not weapon efficacy; government interest is not demonstrated capability. The interesting history lies in how often those categories were allowed to touch.
