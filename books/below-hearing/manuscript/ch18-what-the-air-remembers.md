# What the Air Remembers

The atmosphere does not remember for long. That is exactly why listening matters.

An event happens. Pressure leaves it. Winds bend the evidence around the planet. Arrays catch fragments before they dissolve into weather. Analysts reconstruct a source that may be thousands of kilometres away. The record becomes durable only because somebody was already listening.

This is the respectable strangeness of infrasound. No paranormal mechanism is required. The world is simply more communicative than our senses suggest.

The Cold War found two dreams in that fact. One was to hear the adversary's explosions. The other was to imagine that low-frequency sound might reach inside the adversary's body and behavior. The first accumulated instruments, calibration, networks and treaty institutions. The second accumulated scattered experiments, uncertain reports and stories that outran their dosimetry.

That divergence is useful. It shows what mature evidence looks like. Not certainty. Infrastructure.

A claim grows up when it acquires calibrated instruments, known noise, replication, adversarial tests and explicit failure conditions. Until then, government interest is history, not proof.

Below hearing, the planet is loud. The difficult work is deciding what it said.
