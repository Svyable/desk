# The Atmosphere as Waveguide

The air above us is not acoustically uniform. Temperature changes with altitude. Winds change with altitude. Effective sound speed therefore bends trajectories, creating ducts in which low-frequency energy can travel surprising distances and shadow zones where a source may be difficult to hear.

This makes atmospheric listening both powerful and treacherous. A signal recorded far away may have taken a path no person standing at the source would intuit. Seasonal winds can change detectability. Stratospheric circulation can favor one direction. The atmosphere is not empty space between source and microphone. It is part of the instrument.

Long-range infrasound therefore became entangled with atmospheric science. To locate a secret explosion better, one must know the winds. To understand the winds better, known explosions, volcanoes and launches can become calibration sources. Intelligence collection and geophysics exchange tools because they are solving opposite sides of the same propagation problem.

A secret event cannot choose the weather. That is a strategic limitation and an epistemic gift.
