# Rockets Without Seeing Them

Rockets are loud in more ways than spectators hear. Launches and atmospheric flight can generate low-frequency acoustic energy detectable at distance. That makes infrasound potentially useful as one strand of technical intelligence around missile and space activity.

The attraction is obvious. A passive sensor does not illuminate the target. It can operate continuously. It listens to a consequence the vehicle must produce rather than a cooperative transmission. But the atmosphere again mediates everything: winds, source geometry, trajectory and background conditions alter what arrives.

The deeper point is about unavoidable signatures. Technology can hide appearance, encrypt communications and reduce radar cross-section, but movement through a physical medium produces consequences. Intelligence engineering is often the art of finding the consequence an adversary forgot to classify.
