# The Vela Problem in Air

Every remote-monitoring system eventually encounters the same humiliation: an instrument can be correct while the story built around it is wrong.

The nuclear-detection problem is full of ambiguous signatures because energetic natural events imitate pieces of the target phenomenon. Infrasound adds another channel, but it does not abolish ambiguity. Its proper role is often corroborative: timing, bearing, location, yield-related information, or evidence that an atmospheric disturbance accompanied another signal.

The discipline is to ask what proposition each instrument actually supports. A coherent infrasonic arrival may support the existence of a remote pressure source. It does not by itself identify the explosive chemistry, political actor or intent. Those are downstream inferences.

Intelligence organizations live downstream. Their institutional temptation is to turn a technically bounded measurement into the answer a policymaker wants. Good verification systems resist that temptation structurally by preserving channels, uncertainties and alternative explanations.
