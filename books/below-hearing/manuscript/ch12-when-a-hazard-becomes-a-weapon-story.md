# When a Hazard Becomes a Weapon Story

Noise can harm people. Intense acoustic exposure can be dangerous. Low-frequency noise can be annoying and, at sufficient levels and circumstances, produce physiological or perceptual effects. None of those propositions requires a secret weapon.

The mistake is to move from hazard to controllable remote capability without paying the engineering bill. A weapon needs a source, power, coupling, useful range, targeting, repeatability and an effect that survives variation among targets. If the claim involves covert operation, it also needs some account of how the source remains concealed while producing the required field.

Cold War files often preserve the exploratory stage: someone wondered, collected papers, funded a test, wrote an assessment. Later mythology edits out the uncertainty and leaves only the institutional letterhead.

The correction is not ridicule. It is reconstruction.
