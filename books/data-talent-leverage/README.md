# Data Talent & Leverage

*How the New Economy Compounds Power*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Authors** | Sven Hardy Benson |
| **Status** | Drafting |
| **Chapters** | 1 of 20 drafted |

A strange thing happened to the old production function.

Factories still matter. Capital still matters. Labor still matters. But in the parts of the economy where advantage compounds fastest, three other assets increasingly determine what a firm can see, decide, and multiply: data that competitors cannot easily reproduce, people whose judgment changes the quality of the system around them, and leverage that lets a small number of decisions travel through software, AI, capital, distribution, networks, and institutions.

*Data Talent & Leverage* is about the interaction among those assets.

The working thesis is that none of the three is sufficient alone. Data without judgment becomes a warehouse. Talent without leverage becomes expensive craftsmanship. Leverage without good information or good judgment scales mistakes. The extraordinary organizations are those that connect the three into a feedback loop: use produces data, data improves decisions, better decisions improve the product or system, the system reaches more users, and the expanded reach produces another round of information and opportunity.

That loop can create enormous value. It can also concentrate power, create dependence on a handful of people or platforms, turn surveillance into a business model, and make an apparently efficient organization dangerously brittle.

This book treats the loop as an empirical claim to be tested rather than a slogan. It follows AI laboratories, software companies, scientific organizations, financial firms, defense contractors, platforms, professional services, and individual operators. The question throughout is simple enough to ask and difficult enough to matter: **what exactly is compounding here, who controls it, and what would cause the advantage to stop?**
