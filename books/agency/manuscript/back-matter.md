# Afterword: Remain the Principal

The vocabulary of artificial intelligence changes quickly.

Assistant becomes copilot. Copilot becomes agent. Agent becomes a system of agents. Models become tools inside orchestration layers whose users may never know which model handled which part of the work.

The names will keep moving.

The principal problem will not.

Somebody wants something to become true.

Somebody or something acts on that intention.

The path contains uncertainty.

Authority has to be bounded.

Evidence has to survive the action.

Consequences have to belong to someone.

And the person or institution at the center needs enough retained capacity to change course when the world refuses the plan.

That is agency.

The economic opportunity is enormous because the cost of carrying intention through execution is falling. Tasks that were too small to delegate, too irregular to automate, too expensive to coordinate, or too ambitious for one person are becoming addressable.

The risk is equally simple.

We may confuse more execution with more agency.

The two can move together.

They can also move in opposite directions.

A person can have a thousand machine actions running and be living inside objectives chosen elsewhere.

A company can automate every process and become less able to change strategy.

A manager can receive perfect dashboards and understand the organization less.

A citizen can receive faster decisions from institutions and have less ability to contest them.

A professional can produce more and slowly lose the judgment required to know what deserves production.

The distinction is not human versus machine.

It is principal versus passenger.

A principal can use extraordinary amounts of machine intelligence.

A principal can delegate deeply.

A principal can allow agents to act without constant human approval.

A principal can rely on systems no individual could reproduce manually.

The retained property is authorship over consequential direction.

What are we trying to make true?

What are we unwilling to trade away?

What authority has been granted?

What evidence tells us the system is working?

Where does the system stop?

What can still be reversed?

What must remain portable?

Who owns the outcome?

Those questions are not a framework to memorize.

They are what remains when the model names disappear.

The next economy will be full of people who know how to make agents do things.

That skill will become common.

The rarer skill will be knowing which things deserve to be caused, at what scale, under whose authority, with what evidence, and at whose expense.

Intelligence is acquiring hands.

Keep the right to decide where they reach.

— Sven Hardy Benson
