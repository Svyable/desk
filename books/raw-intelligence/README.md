# RAW INTELLIGENCE

## The psychic program that produced a statistical anomaly and failed as a spy system

Sven Hardy Benson

## Thesis

For roughly two decades, U.S. intelligence organizations kept returning to remote viewing because two propositions that are usually collapsed into one were never actually the same proposition: **a laboratory protocol can produce a statistical anomaly while an operational intelligence system remains useless.**

RAW INTELLIGENCE reconstructs that separation. It follows the work from SRI through GRILL FLAME and STARGATE, the operational sessions, the methodological arguments, the 1995 AIR review, and the later public mythology. The book is not a brief for psi and not a debunking exercise. Its subject is harder: what should a state do when an effect may be statistically non-random but cannot be made sufficiently reliable, specific, independently replicated, or decision-useful?

The central object is not the psychic. It is the **translation layer** between experiment and intelligence.

## Governing prohibition

A statistically significant anomaly is not a sensor. A sensor is not an intelligence product. An intelligence product is not a decision advantage.

## Evidence classes

1. **Verified / declassified / documented:** the programs existed; operational sessions were conducted; government requestors used them; formal protocols and session reports survive; the 1995 review occurred; CIA declined to restore the program.
2. **Credible but incomplete:** claims that some sessions produced striking correspondences; participant and evaluator accounts of operational successes; laboratory aggregate effects whose interpretation remains contested.
3. **Disputed / unconfirmed:** reliable clairvoyance, telepathy, precognition, nonlocal information transfer, and specific celebrated operational hits when independent scoring/provenance is incomplete.
4. **Speculative synthesis:** `translation loss`, `specificity tax`, `retrospective elasticity`, `decision threshold`, and the possibility that a weak anomaly could be scientifically real yet strategically unusable.

## Book progression

1. **Raw Intelligence** — a session report that warns the reader not to treat its contents as evaluated intelligence.
2. **The Money Was Real** — why funding proves institutional interest, not paranormal efficacy.
3. **SRI** — how laboratory remote viewing entered the intelligence ecosystem.
4. **The Soviet Mirror** — foreign-threat reporting, psychotronics, and the danger of reciprocal belief.
5. **Protocol** — targets, cueing, judging, feedback, leakage, randomization.
6. **The Hit** — why vivid correspondences dominate memory.
7. **The Miss** — what archives preserve when the viewer gets almost nothing right.
8. **Tehran** — remote viewing used against an actual hostage crisis.
9. **Raw** — the operational distinction between impressions and evaluated intelligence.
10. **Specificity Tax** — why vague-but-correlated description can survive a lab score and fail a mission.
11. **The Judge** — the hidden importance of target pools, rank ordering and analytical degrees of freedom.
12. **Replication** — what independent laboratories owe an extraordinary effect.
13. **Utts and Hyman** — the 1995 disagreement that should not be flattened into a verdict.
14. **Useful** — the separate question of intelligence utility.
15. **Decision Augmentation** — models that move the anomaly from perception to choice without solving ontology.
16. **The Archive Effect** — why declassification makes discarded programs look endorsed.
17. **Build the Test Again** — a modern preregistered, adversarial, multi-lab protocol.
18. **If It Works, Make It Work** — what evidence would justify calling anomalous cognition a capability.
