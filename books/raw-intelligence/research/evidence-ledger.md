# RAW INTELLIGENCE — evidence ledger

Working firewall between archive fact, reported experience, disputed interpretation and book-level synthesis.

## Class 1 — verified / declassified / documented

### The programs existed
CIA publicly acknowledges investigation of remote viewing, transfer of the work, and a mid-1990s review before declining to restore the program.

Source: https://www.cia.gov/stories/story/ask-molly-did-cia-really-study-psychic-powers/

Do not inflate into: CIA proved psychic ability.

### Operational requests existed
Declassified GRILL FLAME reports state that sessions were conducted in response to government requests. Tehran-hostage-related sessions are explicit examples.

Sources:
- https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp96-00788r000800760001-1
- https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp96-00788r000800580001-1

Do not inflate into: the material was accurate or operationally decisive.

### Reports labeled impressions raw intelligence
Multiple reports say viewer impressions were raw intelligence data, not yet analyzed/evaluated/collated, with interpretation left to the requestor.

Sources:
- https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp96-00788r000700070001-8
- https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp96-00788r000600030001-3

Book significance: contemporary program documentation distinguishes collection-like output from evaluated intelligence.

### Explicit misses survive
A 1978 training report says target images showed “absolutely no target correlation.” A 1982 report says post-session analysis indicated “very little correlation.”

Sources:
- https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp96-00788r000900390001-1
- https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp96-00788r001400250001-0

Book significance: failure was present in the record; hit-only storytelling is incomplete.

### The 1995 evaluation contained disagreement
Surviving AIR-evaluation material records a skeptical objection that claims of anomalous cognition were premature absent adequate independent replication. CIA's later public account summarizes the institutional outcome as apparent departures from randomness coupled with unreliability/inconsistency and lack of intelligence usefulness.

Sources:
- https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/CIA-RDP96-00789R003200290001-5.pdf
- https://www.cia.gov/stories/story/ask-molly-did-cia-really-study-psychic-powers/

Publication hold: recover complete AIR report and quote exact evaluator conclusions from a stable primary copy before the full chapter.

### Decision Augmentation Theory was proposed
May, Utts and Spottiswoode presented DAT in 1995 as a model for anomalous mental phenomena.

Source: https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp96-00791r000200280002-5

Do not inflate into: proof of anomalous information or a nonlocal mechanism.

### Ganzfeld replication dispute is published
Milton & Wiseman (1999) reported that 30 studies from seven laboratories failed to reproduce the earlier main effect. Storm & Ertel (2001) argued that a larger database supported a positive conclusion.

Sources:
- https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.125.4.387
- https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.127.3.424

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete

### Celebrated operational hits
Participant and later historical accounts describe sessions said to contain strikingly accurate details. No case enters prose as an operational success until the following are recovered where possible: contemporaneous tasking; viewer information state; full transcript/drawings; target definition; complete target pool; contemporaneous evaluation; misses/false specifics; feedback timing; independent corroboration; operational consequence.

Rule: no `psychic found X` sentence on memoir alone.

### Laboratory above-chance effects
Some evaluators and meta-analysts report aggregate departures from chance. Interpretation remains sensitive to protocol quality, independence, publication selection, analytical choices and replication definition.

Rule: report effect estimate, uncertainty, study set and major counteranalysis together whenever practical.

## Class 3 — disputed / unconfirmed

- Humans reliably obtain target information without known sensory channels.
- Remote viewing accesses distant locations directly.
- Remote viewing accesses future information.
- Telepathy, clairvoyance or precognition are established mechanisms behind program results.
- Specific famous sessions could not arise through ordinary information, flexible matching, leakage, coincidence or retrospective selection.
- U.S. intelligence secretly retained a validated operational psychic capability after STARGATE.
- Soviet/Russian psychotronic work produced deployable anomalous-cognition or mind-control capability.

These require evidence beyond program existence, classification markings, patents, participant confidence or threat reporting.

## Class 4 — speculative synthesis

### Translation loss
A weak real laboratory anomaly may decay when translated into free-response operational information because the operational task adds ambiguity, specificity requirements, uncertain base rates, analyst interpretation and asymmetric error costs.

Falsifier: blinded operational tasks show stable, calibrated decision advantage comparable to laboratory effect stability.

### Specificity tax
The more a task requires exact location, identity, timing, quantity or discriminating detail, the less useful generic semantic overlap becomes.

Falsifier: accuracy remains stable as preregistered target-specificity requirements increase.

### Retrospective elasticity
After target revelation, humans can expand the semantic matching function—synonyms, rotations, scale changes, selective descriptors—making weak material appear more specific.

Falsifier: prospective blind judging with fixed similarity rules produces equivalent or stronger performance.

### Decision threshold
Intelligence usefulness should be measured as improvement in a real decision under explicit loss functions, not aggregate correspondence alone. This is an evaluation proposal, not an ontological claim.

## Contradictions to preserve

1. Government persistence vs government termination.
2. Reported statistical anomaly vs operational unreliability.
3. Vivid hits vs documented misses.
4. Participant expertise vs evaluator independence.
5. Unknown mechanism vs disputed effect.

## Incentive / disinformation audit

- Cold War threat inflation could encourage U.S. investigation of Soviet paranormal claims.
- Soviet publications could reflect research, ideological fashion, strategic signaling, internal competition or Western misinterpretation.
- Contractors had incentives to continue funded research.
- Program managers had incentives to surface successes.
- Skeptical evaluators may impose demanding standards; standards must be explicit.
- Sympathetic evaluators may treat aggregate significance as stronger evidence of mechanism than warranted.
- Media privilege spectacular cases over denominator data.
- Sincere participant memories can change after decades of retelling.

## Strongest countermodel

No anomalous information channel is required. Methodological leakage, flexible judging, target-set artifacts, optional analysis, publication/selection effects, ordinary inference, coincidence and retrospective storytelling can generate small laboratory effects and memorable operational hits. Institutional persistence follows from low cost, Cold War uncertainty and occasional striking anecdotes.

This model wins if modern adversarial preregistered multi-lab work converges on null results and archival reconstruction shows celebrated cases lose specificity when scored prospectively against complete alternatives.

## Strongest pro-anomaly model

A small information-bearing anomaly exists under some controlled conditions but has low signal-to-noise, unstable individual expression and poorly understood boundary conditions. It can therefore produce statistically detectable aggregate effects while failing intelligence requirements.

This model gains ground if independent preregistered laboratories repeatedly recover a predeclared effect under leakage-resistant conditions, especially if trial-level confidence or physiological markers predict accuracy before target revelation.

## Modern decisive protocol

1. Independent laboratories including skeptical and psi-sympathetic teams.
2. Public preregistration and immutable analysis code.
3. Cryptographically auditable target generation after participant isolation.
4. Large target universe minimizing semantic overlap.
5. Automated capture of every session; no discarded trials.
6. Independent blind judges plus predeclared machine similarity metrics.
7. Negative controls and sham sessions.
8. Explicit leakage/red-team audit.
9. Fixed stopping rule.
10. Cross-lab replication threshold, not pooled significance alone.
11. Second-stage operational test requiring discrimination among live alternatives.
12. Utility metric: decision improvement over conventional-information baseline, with false-positive cost.
