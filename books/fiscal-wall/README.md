*The Great Refinancing*

The United States does not face a single $10 trillion bill arriving on one morning. It faces something more consequential: a rolling calendar in which trillions of dollars of existing Treasury securities mature, short bills reset repeatedly, new deficits require fresh financing, and every auction reprices part of the government's balance sheet. This version of *Fiscal Wall* follows the market machinery—Treasury debt management, auctions, dealers, repo, foreign demand, the Federal Reserve, and the budget consequences of carrying a much larger debt stock at rates that are no longer near zero.

The argument is deliberately two-sided. A refinancing wall is not the same thing as insolvency. The United States borrows in its own currency, has the deepest sovereign debt market in the world, can change its maturity mix, and still attracts enormous pools of global capital. The danger is not a cinematic default. It is a loss of fiscal room, market elasticity, and political choice as more of yesterday's debt is repriced into today's interest-rate regime while deficits remain historically large.
