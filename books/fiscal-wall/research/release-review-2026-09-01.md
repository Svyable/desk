# Release review — 2026-09-01

## Scope

This review rechecks the principal time-sensitive fiscal, monetary-policy, Treasury-market, foreign-demand, and market-structure claims in *Fiscal Wall: The Great Refinancing* before preparing a Shelf release.

The book's central claim remains deliberately narrower than a crisis headline: the United States faces a large rolling refinancing and repricing cycle, not a single $10 trillion payment date or an imminent mechanical funding failure.

## Treasury financing and the gross-versus-net distinction

Treasury's August 3, 2026 financing estimate continues to report $739 billion of privately held net marketable borrowing for July–September 2026 and $628 billion for October–December 2026. These figures are net financing needs, not gross auction rollover.

Source:
https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0584

Treasury's August 5, 2026 quarterly refunding statement continues to provide the manuscript's clearest concrete example of the distinction: $125 billion of 3-, 10-, and 30-year securities were offered to refund about $96.3 billion of privately held maturities and raise about $28.7 billion of new cash. Treasury also continued to say that bills would absorb seasonal or unexpected financing variation and that nominal coupon and FRN auction sizes were expected to remain in place for at least the next several quarters.

Source:
https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0590

No manuscript correction is required. The draft consistently treats gross maturities, repeated bill turnover, and net new borrowing as different quantities.

## Federal budget path

CBO's February 2026 baseline remains the current long-horizon anchor used by the manuscript. It projects a fiscal-year 2026 deficit of about $1.9 trillion, debt held by the public equal to 101 percent of GDP, and net interest outlays of about $1.0 trillion. By 2036 it projects debt held by the public at 120 percent of GDP and net interest outlays of about $2.1 trillion, or 4.6 percent of GDP. CBO explicitly emphasizes uncertainty around the baseline.

Sources:
https://www.cbo.gov/publication/62105
https://www.cbo.gov/publication/62184

No manuscript correction is required. The book treats these values as projections rather than observed outcomes.

## Federal Reserve operating framework

The Federal Reserve's July 29, 2026 implementation note remains current as of this review. The FOMC maintained a 3.5–3.75 percent federal-funds target range, directed standing overnight repo operations at 3.75 percent, authorized purchases of Treasury bills and if needed other Treasuries with remaining maturities of three years or less to maintain ample reserves, and directed the Desk to roll over all Treasury principal payments.

Source:
https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260729a1.htm

No manuscript correction is required. The book's distinction between reserve-management purchases and quantitative easing remains important and accurate.

## Treasury-market leverage and intermediation

The Federal Reserve's June 22, 2026 FEDS Note continues to report that large hedge funds' gross U.S. Treasury exposures reached about $4.0 trillion as of September 2025, with about $3.0 trillion of repo cash borrowing and an estimated cash-futures basis trade of about $830 billion. The note repeatedly cautions that decomposition by strategy is difficult and data have limitations.

Source:
https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/decomposing-hedge-funds-u-s-treasury-exposures-20260622.html

No manuscript correction is required. The draft uses these estimates as evidence about market structure and leverage, not as a claim that the entire gross exposure is directional speculation or imminent loss.

The SEC's Treasury Clearing Implementation page continues to list December 31, 2026 as the compliance date for eligible cash-market transactions and June 30, 2027 for eligible repo transactions. The Commission continues to work through implementation questions and exemption requests.

## Foreign demand

Treasury's August 17 release for June 2026 continues to report a net TIC inflow of $133.5 billion and net foreign purchases of long-term U.S. securities of $207.1 billion. The next TIC release, for July 2026 data, is scheduled for September 16, 2026.

Source:

## Debt-stock freshness

The manuscript and research brief use dated Treasury debt-stock observations rather than presenting them as timeless constants. Those values will continue to change daily. The release should preserve the observation date anywhere a debt-stock number is described as current, and future editions should refresh Debt to the Penny before replacing the Shelf snapshot.

Source:
https://fiscaldata.treasury.gov/datasets/debt-to-the-penny/debt-to-the-penny

## Release decision

The release review found no time-sensitive claim in the core evidence set that requires a manuscript correction before publication.

The strongest counter-case also remains intact: large maturities are ordinary in a continuously refinancing sovereign market; bills create repeated gross turnover while serving deep cash and collateral demand; lower rates or stronger nominal growth could ease the fiscal path; and the United States retains exceptional currency, legal, market-depth, and safe-asset advantages.

The release candidate can therefore proceed from the committed Desk snapshot. Future editions should refresh Debt to the Penny, quarterly borrowing estimates and refunding documents, the FOMC implementation note, CBO projections, SEC clearing implementation, TIC data, Social Security Trustees dates, and Treasury-market leverage evidence before replacing the Shelf edition.
