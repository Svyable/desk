# Manuscript architecture

1. **The Gargle Sample** — A biological reading survives without the sailor's identity; the book's governing object appears.
2. **A Ship Is Easier Than a Body** — Why vulnerability testing naturally privileges compartments, airflow and equipment over longitudinal human exposure.
3. **Project 112** — Deseret Test Center, the 1962–74 program, classification, scope, and the distinction between planned, cancelled and conducted tests.
4. **SHAD** — The shipboard problem: attack geometry, aerosol penetration, ventilation, masks, washdown and operational continuity.
5. **What Was Released** — Agent-by-agent and simulant-by-simulant reconstruction. No generic use of the word `chemical` or `biological` where the specific material is knowable.
6. **The Harmless Simulant Problem** — Historical safety assumptions, changing toxicology, dose, route and why `simulant` describes purpose rather than universal biological harmlessness.
7. **Where Were You Standing?** — Exposure reconstruction as geometry: station, deck, compartment, wind, time, mask state and sampler distance.
8. **The Human Sampler** — Nasal swabs, gargles, biological monitoring and the evidentiary consequences of unlinked measurements.
9. **Classified Medicine** — What secrecy does to informed awareness, symptom reporting, later medical history and the ability of participants to explain exposure to clinicians.
10. **The Closed Test Center** — Deseret Test Center disappears; records scatter; institutional memory becomes archival archaeology.
11. **5,842 Names** — DOD's reconstruction, GAO's audit, the 21 land tests with no identified service members, self-identifying veterans and the denominator problem.
12. **The Archive Has a Detection Limit** — Missingness as a measurable property. What kinds of causal claims each missing field destroys.
13. **The First Health Study** — The 2007 IOM design, participant/control construction, mortality, morbidity, response bias, exposure groups and the heart-disease signal.
14. **No Clear Evidence** — Why absence of a clear disease pattern matters; why it does not equal proof of zero individual effect.
15. **Seven More Years** — The 2016 National Academies follow-up, administrative health data, the disappearance of the earlier heart-disease signal, and what stronger negative evidence should update.
16. **Epidemiological Debt** — Formalize the concept across occupational, military, industrial and classified exposure systems. Debt = exposure uncertainty × latency × record decay × cohort-identification loss, not a rhetorical score.
17. **Build the Future Archive** — Minimum prospective data architecture for hazardous classified testing: identity escrow, person-location linkage, exposure metrology, biological banking, declassification triggers, independent custody and medical-access exceptions.
18. **The Name Beside the Number** — Return to the gargle sample. The ethical and scientific requirement is not omniscience. It is preserving enough of the join that a future investigator can still ask whether the number belonged to this body.

## Quantitative burdens before full draft

Every test discussed at length must carry, where recoverable: test date; ship/site; material; live agent versus simulant; release method at non-operational descriptive resolution; concentration metric; environmental conditions; sampler geometry; protective equipment; decontamination; participant roster denominator; biological samples; acute events; and later cohort inclusion.

Every epidemiological claim must carry: cohort definition; exposure grouping; control selection; follow-up interval; ascertainment source; response rate where applicable; effect estimate; uncertainty; multiple-comparison burden; and whether the result replicated in the later follow-up.

## Strongest countermodel

The archive may be messy mostly because it is old, multi-service military history—not because secrecy uniquely degraded health knowledge. SHAD also may have produced little or no measurable long-term population harm, making some missing exposure detail scientifically frustrating but not outcome-changing. The book must compare SHAD's information loss against contemporary nonclassified hazardous-test and occupational cohorts before assigning a secrecy-specific penalty.

## Defeat-capable comparative test

Build a blinded sample of classified and nonclassified U.S. military/industrial exposure programs from the same era, matched on size, hazard, duration and organizational complexity. Score later reconstructability of participant identity, individual exposure, acute effects and longitudinal outcomes without revealing classification status to scorers. If classification status does not predict reconstructability after matching, the secrecy component of `epidemiological debt` loses.
