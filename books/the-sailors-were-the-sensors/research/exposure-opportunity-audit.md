# Exposure-opportunity audit

## Bounded question

What did the later SHAD epidemiology actually know about exposure, and what did it have to substitute for dose?

This matters because the book's `broken join` argument gets weaker if it implies that later investigators had only ship rosters and agent names. The 2016 National Academies study reconstructed substantially more than that. Its limitation is more interesting: it could count opportunities for exposure without turning those opportunities into individual absorbed dose.

## Authoritative source check

### National Academies 2016: trial-level reconstruction

The 2016 National Academies report says SHAD comprised 21 tests involving at least 19 vessels and seven Marine Corps units, with at least 16 test substances over seven years. Tests contained multiple trials; ships, substances, and conditions could vary by trial, and crews could change between tests.

For epidemiological analysis, the committee represented each participant's exposure using the number of trials and potential exposure opportunities for a test substance. A release line could count as an additional opportunity. The report gives a concrete Autumn Gold / Eager Belle II example: a sailor present for all nine Autumn Gold trials and all three relevant Eager Belle II trials would be assigned 12 BG exposure opportunities; presence for only five Autumn Gold trials would yield eight.

Source: National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine, *Assessing Health Outcomes Among Veterans of Project SHAD (Shipboard Hazard and Defense)* (2016), chapter 3, "Data and Methods for the SHAD II Study," especially the exposure-assessment discussion around pp. 65 onward. https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/21846/chapter/6

Appendix D reports the distribution used in one analysis: among participants in the relevant chemical-substance analysis, 1,812 were in the lower exposure-opportunity group (1–20 opportunities) and 724 were in the upper quartile (21 or more). The report explicitly defines these opportunities as releases of test substances on or over the ship or unit.

Source: National Academies 2016, Appendix D. https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/21846/chapter/13

### What the tests were designed to measure

The same report says SHAD tests were designed to gather operational information about penetration of test substances into ships or their behavior in marine environments, rather than to evaluate health effects in people. It notes exceptions in which human-exposure indicators were collected to evaluate protective equipment, including gargle samples or clothing dosimeters in Autumn Gold, DTC 69-10, and Copper Head.

For Autumn Gold specifically, the report describes nine trials conducted in three phases under different ship material-readiness conditions. Four regular Navy ships participated, and two A4B aircraft disseminated BG along a release line in each trial.

Source: National Academies 2016, chapter 2, "Background." https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/21846/chapter/4

## What this changes in the book

Do **not** write that later investigators had no exposure reconstruction. They did. They reconstructed test participation at trial/release-opportunity level and used those counts analytically.

Do **not** translate `exposure opportunity` into `dose`. A release over or near a ship does not by itself establish an individual's breathing-zone concentration, route, duration, protective-equipment performance, internal uptake, or absorbed dose. The epidemiological variable is therefore both more informative than a roster and less physiological than its everyday label can sound.

A better narrative hinge is:

> Forty years later, the epidemiologists could sometimes count how many chances a sailor had to be exposed. They still could not turn those chances into the dose that entered that sailor's body.

That sentence should migrate only after the manuscript checks its surrounding terminology against the report; `chance` is explanatory prose, not the committee's technical term.

## Strongest counterargument / disconfirming evidence

The missing-dose problem does not make the epidemiology meaningless. If exposure-opportunity count is positively correlated with true dose, large common effects can still emerge despite noisy classification, and the 2016 study deliberately examined higher-versus-lower opportunity groups and substance-specific hypotheses. The absence of broadly worse outcomes therefore deserves evidentiary weight.

Nor is the record uniformly broken. Trial identity, ship participation, release events, substances, and some personal-exposure indicators survived well enough to support more detailed reconstruction than the book's opening metaphor might suggest.

The counterargument cuts both ways: opportunity counts can dilute a real association when actual individual dose varies sharply within the same nominal release, but that limitation cannot be invoked after the fact to preserve every possible causal claim. The manuscript should distinguish effects the available study had reasonable power to detect from smaller, rarer, latency-sensitive, or test-specific effects it could not exclude well.

## Publication holds

Before manuscript prose turns this audit into a quantitative claim, extract from the 2016 report:

- exact participant denominator for each exposure-opportunity analysis;
- how missing trial attendance was handled;
- whether opportunity counts were weighted by release geometry, sampler data, ship location, readiness condition, or protective equipment;
- the specific outcomes tested against higher/lower opportunity groups;
- effect estimates and confidence intervals, not significance labels alone;
- multiple-comparison handling and the committee's own interpretation of subgroup results;
- whether the Autumn Gold gargle/swab measurements can be associated with ship, trial, readiness condition, or only an anonymous sample set.

## Why this is useful

The stronger version of `epidemiological debt` is not that secrecy erased exposure. It is that a program built to measure ship vulnerability preserved one kind of denominator unusually well—tests, trials, releases, penetration—while leaving later investigators with a much weaker person-level map from release to dose to biological response. That asymmetry is concrete enough to test against nonclassified military programs, and specific enough to lose if matched controls preserved the same kinds of records equally badly.
