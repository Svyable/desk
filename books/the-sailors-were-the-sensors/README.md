# THE SAILORS WERE THE SENSORS

## How a Secret Weapons Test Became an Epidemiology Problem Forty Years Later

Sven Hardy Benson

**Status:** Drafting

## Thesis

Project 112/SHIPBOARD HAZARD AND DEFENSE (SHAD) was built to answer an engineering question: could a warship remain operational after chemical or biological attack? Decades later the surviving archive was forced to answer a different question it had not been designed to answer: exactly who inhaled what, at what dose, and with what long-term consequence? The collision matters far beyond SHAD. A secret test can generate excellent weapons-effect data while producing terrible human-exposure data, and classification can turn that mismatch into an epidemiological debt that comes due after the instruments, rosters, samples, and memories have dispersed.

This is not a book arguing that every SHAD participant was harmed. The best follow-up studies did not find a clear overall pattern of specific long-term disease attributable to participation. Nor is that negative result proof that no participant was harmed. The more interesting object is the information architecture itself: the Navy could instrument a ship densely enough to study penetration while leaving future investigators unable to reconstruct every person's exposure.

## Evidence classes

1. **Verified / documented.** Project 112 operated from 1962–1974; SHAD was its shipboard component; DOD later identified thousands of potentially exposed service members; some tests used simulants and some Project 112 tests involved active chemical or biological agents; participant identification was incomplete; GAO found important record and notification gaps; National Academies follow-up found no clear overall specific long-term health effect associated with SHAD participation within the available data.
2. **Credible but incomplete.** Test-level individual exposure, exact dose, protective-equipment use, decontamination, acute symptoms, and the interpretation of particular later illnesses where records are incomplete.
3. **Disputed / not established.** Claims that SHAD caused a broad hidden epidemic; claims that every participant was knowingly used as a human test subject; claims that a negative cohort study proves zero individual injury; claims that missing records establish deliberate destruction to conceal health effects.
4. **Speculative synthesis.** `epidemiological debt`: when a secret operational experiment records the machine more faithfully than the humans around it, uncertainty is not eliminated—it is transferred into the future, where it becomes harder and more expensive to resolve.

## Defeat condition

The central thesis weakens if the primary test archive shows that participant identity, location, agent concentration, protective equipment, biological sampling, acute outcomes, and individual dose were in fact systematically linked at the person level across SHAD, or if comparable nonclassified military test programs routinely produced equally poor retrospective exposure reconstruction. The argument is comparative, not moral by assertion.
