# The Arithmetic of Dose

Every exotic weapon story eventually reaches the part where somebody has to carry the battery.

This is not a joke about engineering conservatism. It is where possibility becomes architecture.

Microwave hearing can be produced at relatively modest absorbed energy per pulse because the auditory system is sensitive to the resulting thermoelastic pressure transient. Harmful tissue effects generally require a different exposure regime. A hypothetical device intended to cause the broad acute and persistent symptoms reported in some AHI cases therefore cannot be evaluated by citing the existence of the Frey effect alone.

The device needs a dose story.

Dose begins at the transmitter but does not end there. The antenna converts electrical energy into a field with a spatial pattern. The beam propagates through air. It encounters walls, windows, furniture, bodies, and multipath reflections. Some energy reaches the target. Some is reflected. Some is absorbed superficially. Some penetrates farther depending on frequency. The head's geometry creates hotspots and nulls. Pulse duration and repetition determine peak and average deposition.

Then biology begins.

A sensory threshold can be far below an injury threshold. A thermal injury depends on temperature, duration, blood flow, tissue type, and heat diffusion. A mechanical effect depends on stress amplitude and rise time. A proposed nonthermal interaction needs a reproducible mechanism stronger than noise at the relevant exposure.

The phrase “pulsed RF” is therefore radically underdetermined.

A nanosecond pulse at one frequency is not equivalent to a microsecond pulse at another. A gigahertz carrier absorbed near the surface does not deposit energy like a lower-frequency field with deeper penetration. A narrow beam at close range is not a distant source through reinforced concrete. A single startling click is not a repeated exposure lasting minutes.

This parameter freedom is dangerous because it can make the hypothesis impossible to falsify in conversation.

If one waveform fails, choose another.

If heating is too large, shorten the pulse.

If peak power becomes implausible, move the source closer.

If close access becomes operationally implausible, increase antenna gain.

If the antenna becomes too large, invoke classified miniaturization.

Each move may be individually possible. Together they can form a machine that exists only because every constraint is allowed to slide.

The antidote is a complete link budget and biological endpoint model.

A link budget is just accounting. How much power leaves the source? How much gain does the antenna provide? How much is lost with range and obstruction? What field reaches the body? How much is absorbed? What pressure transient or temperature rise follows? What biological structure responds?

The numbers do not need to be exact initially. Orders of magnitude are enough to kill many fantasies.

This book will not provide a construction recipe for harming people. The point of dose analysis is epistemic, not operational. A claimed mechanism should live in a physically coherent range supported by published exposure studies and safety literature.

Microwave-hearing experiments give one anchor. Foster and Finch measured thermoacoustic transients consistent with auditory perception. Human RF-coil studies found thresholds that varied with exposure geometry and pulse energy. Animal work recorded cochlear-nucleus responses and showed dependence on absorbed energy per pulse. These studies constrain the perception problem.

The injury problem is less neatly anchored to the Havana phenotype.

High-power microwave systems can damage tissue and electronics. RF heating is not controversial at sufficient dose. But a covert system that creates dizziness, cognitive symptoms, ear pressure, headache, and prolonged dysfunction without obvious burns or a consistent MRI lesion occupies a narrower space. That does not make it impossible. It makes dose-response evidence the central missing piece.

The 2022 expert panel's plausibility judgment implies that its members believed some parameter space remained physically credible for the core acute cases. The 2025 assessment's split on foreign capability implies that some intelligence components now assign greater probability to engineered systems occupying that space.

Neither public document gives the parameters.

That is precisely why public certainty is unwarranted.

There is also a detection tradeoff. Higher peak power and tighter beams may improve effect at range but create stronger emissions for RF monitoring to detect. Lower power improves concealment but reduces delivered dose or demands closer access. Lower frequencies may penetrate some materials better but require larger antennas for equivalent directivity. Higher frequencies permit smaller apertures for narrow beams but change tissue penetration and building transmission.

Engineering is full of these triangles.

A secret program can optimize them.

It cannot abolish them.

The arithmetic of dose is therefore one of the most valuable places for declassification. A government need not reveal sensitive intelligence sources to publish bounded feasibility studies. If a laboratory can demonstrate AHI-relevant effects under controlled exposures, the ranges can be described with enough abstraction to let outside scientists test the biology. If no such effects are reproducible, that information should lower confidence.

The 2025 assessment says U.S.-government-affiliated laboratories were continuing work on RF bioeffects and that limited recent studies had produced mixed preliminary results. It also says the IC chose to rely on published findings in that assessment.

That is a healthy restraint.

Preliminary data should not carry geopolitical attribution.

But once the work is mature, silence becomes expensive too.

Without parameters, the public argument will keep substituting adjectives for dose.
