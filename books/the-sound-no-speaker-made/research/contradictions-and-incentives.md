# Contradictions, Incentives, and Failure Modes

## Mechanism versus attribution

The strongest recurring contradiction is only apparent. The 2022 IC expert panel found pulsed RF physically plausible for a subset, while 2023 and 2025 attribution assessments judged foreign responsibility very unlikely overall. These products answer different questions. Preserve that separation.

## 2019 versus 2024 imaging

The Penn 2019 exploratory neuroimaging study reported group differences. NIH's larger 2024 reproducibility-focused study did not replicate a consistent structural or functional signature. Do not accuse either team of misconduct. Treat this as normal evidentiary updating and emphasize study design, controls, multiple comparisons, cohort heterogeneity, and timing.

## Patient legitimacy

Institutional fear of invalidating symptomatic personnel can create pressure to preserve an external-cause narrative. The inverse pressure also exists: agencies criticized for protection failures may prefer conventional explanations. Care and causal attribution should remain separate.

## Adversary threat inflation

Foreign research into directed energy can be real without producing an operational AHI-like weapon. Intelligence organizations, contractors, and legislators all have incentives to investigate low-probability threats. Program existence is evidence of interest, not efficacy.

## Classification asymmetry

Public analysts cannot inspect the reporting that moved two IC components in 2025. Do not use the classification gap as evidence for either a weapon or its absence. High classification protects sources and methods, not truth status.

## Survivor and referral bias

Early clinical cohorts were assembled from people whose symptoms were serious enough and similar enough to receive specialized evaluation. Later cohorts used broader definitions. Do not compare percentages across studies without accounting for inclusion criteria.

## Recorded sound versus causal stimulus

A phone microphone records airborne acoustic pressure, not absorbed RF energy. JASON's cricket finding is strong evidence about the recordings and weak evidence about every possible unrecorded stimulus. Do not say either "the cricket caused Havana Syndrome" or "the cricket is irrelevant."

## Moscow analogy

The Soviet microwave illumination of the U.S. Embassy in Moscow is documented. Its operational purpose remains uncertain in the public record, with electronic surveillance/counter-surveillance explanations more directly supported than a deliberate health-attack mission. Do not retrofit Havana intent onto Moscow.
