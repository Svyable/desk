# GREEN EYES — research and evidence ledger

## Selection / freshness

Selected after inspection of recent Author-agent PRs. Immediate territory includes remote viewing/operational utility, animal intelligence platforms, CORONA recovery logistics, COLDFEET site exploitation, GENETRIX, AZORIAN, foreign-materiel exploitation, Argus, strategic sensor networks and West Ford. This book is deliberately not another animal-platform book: its center is the conversion of **living environmental response into remotely readable measurement**, and the epistemic problems created when the sensor is an ecosystem participant.

## Evidence classes

### 1. Verified / documented

- DARPA Advanced Plant Technologies (APT) publicly sought plants as persistent ground-based sensor technologies for CBRNE threats, extending innate stimulus-response mechanisms and adding remotely monitored reporting mechanisms. The program page states initial work was contained and later field trials, if successful, would fall under USDA APHIS protocols. https://www.darpa.mil/research/programs/advanced-plant-technologies
- DARPA Insect Allies publicly pursued insect vectors and plant viruses to deliver protective traits to mature plants, with work described as contained. https://www.darpa.mil/research/programs/insect-allies
- DARPA Tellus publicly explored microbe-based sense-and-respond devices for environmental monitoring. https://www.darpa.mil/research/programs/tellus
- DARPA Convergence publicly describes work on non-pathogenic microbial consortia as environmental biosensors. https://www.darpa.mil/research/programs/convergence

### 2. Credible but incomplete

Program-level claims about anticipated robustness, persistence, remote readability and operational relevance where public pages describe goals rather than validated field performance. These must never be rewritten as achieved capability without primary performance records.

### 3. Disputed allegations

Any claim that APT or adjacent biological programs produced undisclosed operational landscape-surveillance deployments; claims that ordinary vegetation is presently part of a covert sensing network; claims of extraordinary plant perception beyond supported mechanisms. No such claim is accepted by this manuscript absent independent evidence.

### 4. Speculative synthesis / open conjecture

A mature biological sensor network could alter the economics of persistence, signature, maintenance and camouflage. It could also create new counterintelligence problems: adversarial stressors, ecological spoofing, reporter suppression, seasonal confounds and uncertainty about whether an observed phenotype is target-caused. These are forward inferences, not descriptions of existing systems.

## Contradictions / incentive audit

- Program descriptions are advocacy documents as well as records of program existence and goals. They establish intent, not efficacy.
- Defense programs have incentives to emphasize potential capability; critics may have incentives to collapse dual-use biological research into weapon narratives. Both distortions must be resisted.
- Biological sensing literature often demonstrates component-level effects under controlled conditions. Operational language must not outrun field validation.
- Remote sensing adds a second instrument chain after the biological one. A strong biological reporter can still be a weak remote sensor.

## Publication holds

Do not claim field deployment, operational CBRNE detection performance, classified users, detection ranges, false-positive rates, satellite readability, or adversary use unless supported by direct records.

Do not infer weaponization from Insect Allies' use of insect vectors. Treat its publicly stated crop-protection goal and containment posture as documented facts; discuss dual-use criticism only with attributable sources.

## Defeat condition

The book's strongest thesis would weaken if mature biological sensing systems demonstrate that ecological variability, specificity, reporter stability, remote readability and calibration can be engineered so thoroughly that living sensors behave operationally like conventional calibrated instruments across environments. That would not make the epistemic chain disappear, but it would reduce the special burden biology introduces.
