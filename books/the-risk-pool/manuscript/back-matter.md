## A note on evidence

*The Risk Pool* is a work of narrative nonfiction about insurance as an economic institution. It is not an insurance policy, legal opinion, investment recommendation, actuarial filing, or guide to buying coverage. Insurance law, rate regulation, policy language, market conditions, and public programs differ across jurisdictions and change over time.

The manuscript distinguishes among three kinds of claims wherever the distinction matters: what an institution currently reports, what a model or scenario estimates, and what the book infers from those facts. Catastrophe-loss estimates are not forecasts of the next year. Exposure is not the same thing as probable loss. A residual market's policy count is not by itself proof that a private market has failed. A rising premium can reflect changing hazard, exposure, rebuilding costs, litigation, reinsurance, capital, market structure, regulation, or several of those at once.

That complexity is part of the argument.

Insurance is useful because it turns uncertain futures into contracts that can be financed. The contract cannot abolish the uncertainty. It can only decide where the financial consequences go.

The research trail in this Desk project records the principal sources used for concrete institutional and quantitative claims, along with chapter-level counterevidence and falsification tests. Primary public sources are preferred for program rules and official statistics; industry sources are used for market data and are identified as such rather than treated as neutral regulators.

Research was refreshed through September 2026.
