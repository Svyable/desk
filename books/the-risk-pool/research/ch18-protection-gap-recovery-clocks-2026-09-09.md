# Chapter 18 evidence note — protection gaps and recovery clocks

Checked 2026-09-09 for the Chapter 18 revision.

## Current primary / authoritative evidence

### Swiss Re Institute — 2025 realized losses

Swiss Re Institute's final 2025 catastrophe accounting reports about USD 220 billion of global economic losses from natural catastrophes and USD 107 billion of insured natural-catastrophe losses, a 49% insured share. This is a realized-loss measure for one year, not the same quantity as Swiss Re's modeled protection-need gap.

Source: https://www.swissre.com/institute/research/sigma-research/sigma-2026-01-natcat-2025-wildfire-storm-risk/global-natcat-losses-2025.html

### Swiss Re Institute — modeled protection need

Swiss Re's 2026 protection-gap work estimates a USD 424 billion natural-catastrophe protection gap for 2025 and a resilience index of about 27%. It says the absolute gap grew as exposed asset values rose even though insurance protection broadly kept pace with rising risk. That distinction is important counterevidence against the easy claim that a larger dollar protection gap necessarily means insurance markets are covering a shrinking share of risk.

The same work reports a median projected benefit-cost ratio of 1.86 across adaptation project proposals. This is a modeled/project-appraisal result, not a realized universal return. Manuscript wording should therefore use it only to support the narrower proposition that risk reduction can complement insurance and may sometimes reduce expected loss more efficiently than adding risk transfer alone.

Source: https://www.swissre.com/institute/research/topics-and-risk-dialogues/climate-and-natural-catastrophe-risk/growing-exposure/Natcat-protection-gap.html

### Regional heterogeneity

Swiss Re's 2025 Asia accounting reports roughly USD 65 billion of economic natural-catastrophe losses and USD 5.2 billion insured, or about 8% covered. The global insured share therefore should not be presented as a representative household or country experience.

Source: https://www.swissre.com/institute/research/topics-and-risk-dialogues/climate-and-natural-catastrophe-risk/natural-catastrophes-shaping-risk-landscapes/asia-natural-catastrophes-2025.html

## Claim boundary

The chapter should not equate:

- annual realized uninsured loss with modeled protection need;
- a growing absolute gap with declining insurance penetration;
- insurance coverage with resilience;
- fast parametric liquidity with full indemnification;
- modeled adaptation benefit-cost estimates with guaranteed realized returns.

## Strongest counterargument

A protection gap is not automatically a market failure and closing it does not mean insuring every exposed dollar. Households, firms, and sovereigns can rationally retain losses within their capacity; mitigation can reduce the underlying loss; public finance can be appropriate for systemic layers; and insurance coverage can rise while the absolute modeled gap also rises because the amount at risk is growing.

This counterargument improves the book's thesis. The economically important gap is not simply `economic loss - insured loss`. It is the portion of shock that exceeds the relevant absorber's capacity on the clock recovery requires. That framing links Chapter 18's household/sovereign discussion to Chapter 19's state-backstop argument without pretending private insurance is the only form of protection.
