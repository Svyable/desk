# Chapter 8 research note — California recovery is early, real, and not yet proven

**Checked:** September 6, 2026

Chapter 8, “When the Carrier Leaves,” argues that insurance availability can deteriorate before formal exit and can later recover when pricing, legal, reinsurance, and capital conditions change. California now supplies a useful live countercase to a one-way-collapse narrative, but the evidence is still early enough that the manuscript should preserve the distinction between **commitments to write**, **slower residual-market growth**, and **realized voluntary-market recovery**.

## Primary evidence

### California Department of Insurance — May 1, 2026

The Department reported that the California FAIR Plan added about **16,000 residential policies in Q1 2026**, approximately **2.4% growth from the prior quarter**. That was a substantial slowdown from 2024 through September 2025, when quarterly growth had ranged from roughly 35,000 to 50,000 residential policies. The Department described the slowdown as an early sign of stabilization, while explicitly saying additional July and September data would be needed to assess longer-run progress.

Source: California Department of Insurance, *Momentum continues to build under Insurance Commissioner Ricardo Lara’s Sustainable Insurance Strategy*, May 1, 2026. https://www.insurance.ca.gov/0400-news/0102-alerts/2026/Momentum-continues-to-build-under-Insura.cfm

### California FAIR Plan — data through June 2026

The FAIR Plan’s own quarterly data prevent the stabilization story from becoming too neat. As of June 2026 it still reported **696,562 dwelling and commercial policies in force**, up **8% from September 2025**, and **$768 billion of exposure**, up **11% from September 2025**. Slower growth is therefore not the same thing as depopulation, and a large stock of residual-market exposure remains.

Source: California FAIR Plan, *Key Statistics & Data*, latest data through June 2026. https://www.cfpnet.com/key-statistics-data/

### California Department of Insurance — July 23, 2026

By July, the Department said **11 homeowners insurance groups and two major commercial insurers** had committed to grow in California under the Sustainable Insurance Strategy. The announcement included filings from major carriers to expand in wildfire-distressed areas, adding to earlier commitments by Mercury, CSAA, Farmers, USAA, AAA SoCal, Travelers, Horace Mann, Pacific Specialty, and California Casualty.

Source: California Department of Insurance, *Insurance surge expanding options for Californians in wildfire distressed areas*, July 23, 2026. https://www.insurance.ca.gov/0400-news/0102-alerts/2026/Insurance-surge-expanding-options-for-Ca.cfm

### California Department of Insurance — December 20, 2025

One of the earliest approved filings under the new strategy gives a concrete scale to the commitment mechanism. Mercury committed to increasing its California homeowners policy count by more than **38,000 policies over the longer term**, beginning with more than **6,000 new policies over two years**, especially in wildfire-distressed areas and through FAIR Plan depopulation. CSAA also committed to continued writing and longer-term FAIR Plan depopulation support.

Source: California Department of Insurance, *Mercury Insurance and CSAA Expand Homeowners Coverage Under Commissioner Lara’s Sustainable Insurance Strategy*, December 20, 2025. https://www.insurance.ca.gov/0400-news/0102-alerts/2025/Mercury-Insurance-and-CSAA-Expand-Homeow.cfm

## Strongest counterargument

These data do not yet establish that California has solved its homeowners-insurance availability problem or that the Sustainable Insurance Strategy caused the improvement. The Department is both regulator and sponsor of the reform and has an institutional interest in describing early signs positively. Carrier filings are commitments, not completed policy counts. The FAIR Plan was still growing through June 2026, and its exposure remained historically large. Reinsurance conditions, catastrophe experience, legal changes, rate approvals, construction costs, insurer portfolio decisions, and broader capital-market conditions can all influence availability at the same time.

The strongest bounded conclusion is therefore temporal rather than triumphalist: **market retreat can slow before it reverses, and policy reform should be judged by realized voluntary-market capacity and durable FAIR Plan depopulation rather than announcements alone.**

## Why this matters for Chapter 8

The chapter already says insurance crises can reverse, using Florida as its clearest example. California now adds a more immediate and intellectually useful tension. In the same market, the evidence can support two true statements at once:

- voluntary-market participation appears to be improving at the margin; and
- the residual pool remains enormous and was still growing as of the latest FAIR Plan quarter.

That is exactly the chapter’s argument about availability as a moving quantity rather than a binary state. A later prose pass can use California to replace some generic recovery language with a more specific sequence: first fewer exits, then carrier commitments, then slower FAIR Plan growth, and only later—if the data support it—actual depopulation.

## Falsification / update trigger

This note should be revised if later 2026 or 2027 data show one of two clearer outcomes: sustained FAIR Plan depopulation accompanied by realized voluntary-market growth, which would strengthen the recovery case; or renewed acceleration in residual-market growth despite carrier commitments, which would weaken claims that the strategy has materially restored capacity.
