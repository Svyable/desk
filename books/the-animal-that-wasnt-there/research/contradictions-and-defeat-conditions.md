# Contradictions, incentives, and defeat conditions

## Contradictions to preserve

- eDNA is more sensitive than many conventional surveys, yet that sensitivity makes contamination consequential.
- Molecular detection can precede physical capture, yet DNA can be transported independently of a living local organism.
- Repeated non-detection can support extinction inference, yet detection probability may remain poorly known for a rare taxon.
- Rediscoveries happen, yet famous rediscoveries do not raise the prior probability of every cryptid equally.
- Public attention can fund conservation, yet the same attention creates incentives to overstate ambiguous positives.

## Incentives

Researchers gain attention from rediscovery claims. Agencies face asymmetric costs: acting too late can permit invasion or extinction; acting too strongly on weak evidence can waste resources. Cryptozoological communities may have identity or tourism incentives that make null results socially difficult to accept. Skeptics can also become overcommitted to extinction or nonexistence and discount genuinely new evidence.

## Defeat conditions for the book's strongest synthesis

The proposed forensic model would be weakened if well-controlled eDNA positives routinely failed to improve subsequent physical detection compared with conventional search design, or if transport/contamination proved so dominant that spatial inference was generally unusable. Conversely, the claim that controversial-animal searches can become scientifically tractable would be strengthened by preregistered cases where independent eDNA detection reliably predicts later specimen, image, or capture confirmation.

For individual survival hypotheses, defeat conditions must be claim-specific. A bounded-lake breeding-population claim should lose probability rapidly after repeated, high-coverage, multi-season molecular and conventional surveys with validated sensitivity. A wide-ranging terrestrial species requires a different detection model and should not inherit the same threshold.
