# Chronology

- 1938 — Living coelacanth specimen recognized from South Africa, converting a paleontological lineage into a living zoological one.
- 1936 — Last known captive thylacine dies in Hobart; later survival claims continue without accepted physical confirmation.
- 1944 — Last commonly agreed U.S. ivory-billed woodpecker sighting, according to current U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service material.
- 2004 — Kubodera and Mori photograph a living giant squid in its natural habitat; paper follows in 2005.
- 2005 — Fitzpatrick et al. publish Arkansas ivory-bill survival claim in *Science*; dispute follows.
- 2008 — Ficetola et al. demonstrate freshwater species detection from water eDNA in a foundational modern study.
- 2009–2010 — Asian-carp eDNA in Chicago waterways becomes a high-stakes management case separating DNA detection from physical capture.
- 2013 — Mahon et al. publish validation work comparing Asian-carp eDNA detection with fish abundance after piscicide treatment.
- 2016 — Goldberg et al. publish critical-considerations synthesis emphasizing contamination, validation, transport and uncertainty.
- 2017 — White et al. publish thylacine mitochondrial population-history work from historical and subfossil material.
- 2018–2019 — Loch Ness eDNA survey becomes a major public demonstration of metabarcoding applied to a famous cryptozoological claim.
- 2021 — U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service proposes ivory-bill delisting due to extinction.
- 2022 — Service reopens comments and extends the ivory-bill decision.
- 2023 — Service delists 21 other species for extinction but explicitly continues reviewing the ivory-bill.
