10. Cerco, C. F. et al. (2018). A fate and transport model for Asian carp environmental DNA in the Chicago area waterways system. *Journal of Great Lakes Research* 44, 813–823. 11. “Making sense of the noise: The effect of hydrology on silver carp eDNA detection in the Chicago area waterway system” (2017). Finds a positive relationship between Lake-directed flow-reversal volume and silver-carp eDNA detection probability; useful evidence that sampling location and source location are connected by hydrology rather than identity. PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28675881/

Explicitly states that eDNA alone could not determine whether a live fish, dead material, or transported water was the source and describes intensified conventional monitoring after detections. https://icrcc.fws.gov/carp/news/asian-carp-regional-coordinating-committee-intensify-monitoring-chicago-area-waterway-system

### Claim boundary for the Chicago case

- A validated positive establishes target DNA in the processed sample, not automatically a living fish at the bottle location.
- The December 2009 recovery of one bighead carp upstream of the electric barrier means the early Chicago eDNA alarm cannot fairly be dismissed as a pure molecular false alarm.
- Experimental feasibility of birds, carcasses, slime, vessels, gear, sewage/storm inputs, or hydrologic transport does **not** prove that any particular historical positive came from those routes.
- A single fish upstream of a barrier does not establish a reproducing population, and repeated DNA detections do not by themselves locate an invasion front without a transport/detection model.
- Management action can rationally occur at a lower evidentiary threshold than a scientific claim of established population presence; keep those thresholds explicit.
