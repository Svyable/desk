# The Animal That Wasn't There

## Cryptids, eDNA, and the Mathematics of Looking

**Sven Hardy Benson**

### Thesis

The serious question in cryptozoology is not whether surprising animals can exist. They plainly can. The serious question is what repeated failure to detect an alleged animal means when detection itself is imperfect. Giant squid, coelacanths and saola prove that large animals can remain outside formal science for a long time. The ivory-billed woodpecker, decades of thylacine reports, and modern eDNA false-positive work prove something equally important: ambiguity can also survive for a long time when observers, instruments and institutions repeatedly sample the same uncertainty without measuring detectability.

This book treats cryptozoology as a problem in field ecology, signal detection, chain of custody and Bayesian updating rather than as a referendum on whether witnesses are foolish or monsters are real.

### Evidence classes

1. **Verified/documented:** specimens, genomes, reproducible photographs/video with provenance, museum material, validated environmental DNA, official search records, peer-reviewed detection studies.
2. **Credible but incomplete:** high-quality sightings, ambiguous imagery, acoustic signatures, low-copy molecular detections, local ecological knowledge lacking a specimen or independently reproducible record.
3. **Disputed/unconfirmed:** contested rediscoveries, alleged surviving extinct species, cryptid photographs or samples without secure provenance, claims that cannot exclude known species or contamination.
4. **Speculative synthesis:** the proposition that cryptid debates become scientifically tractable when every claim is translated into expected encounter rate, detection probability, false-positive rate and evidentiary decay over time.
