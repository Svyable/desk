# How to Search for an Extraordinary Animal

Start with the animal, not the legend.

Describe a body plan. Estimate diet. Choose habitat. Decide whether the claim requires one individual, a seasonal migrant or a breeding population. Identify close relatives. Write down what physical traces the animal should leave.

Then design a search that is allowed to disappoint you.

For an aquatic claim, map hydrology before collecting water. Sample independent basins and depths. Include field blanks. Preserve filters immediately. Use at least two diagnostic loci when possible. Validate primers against sympatric relatives. Keep positive-control material physically separated. Split strong positives to an independent laboratory.

For a terrestrial claim, use environmental bottlenecks: waterholes, latrines, dens, trails, snow, caves. Pair molecular sampling with cameras and acoustics. A DNA signal without an image is useful; a matching image without DNA is useful; both together are much harder to dismiss.

Pre-register thresholds.

One amplification in one well from one sample should not become a species announcement. Require repeat extraction or independent amplification. Require sequence confirmation. Require spatial or temporal replication for a population claim. Define what contamination in controls does to interpretation.

Publish negatives.

This is harder culturally than technically. Failed searches are often treated as non-events, which means the literature accumulates exciting positives and forgets the denominator. For controversial species the denominator is the story.

Preserve samples.

A future assay may answer a question today's primers cannot. Archive filters, extracts and metadata under conditions that make reanalysis possible. Record weather, flow, temperature, location, volume filtered, inhibition tests and instrument batches.

Invite adversaries before discovery.

A skeptic who helps design the controls is more valuable than one recruited after a positive appears. The point is not consensus. It is to prevent the experiment from having an obvious escape route.

Finally, decide in advance what would count as failure.

If the animal is claimed to occupy a bounded lake year-round, a multi-season, multi-depth, multi-method survey with validated detection sensitivity should update belief sharply if it returns nothing. If the animal is a wide-ranging terrestrial carnivore, the same volume of water samples may barely move the needle.

Extraordinary claims do not need magical standards. They need ordinary standards applied without mercy.
