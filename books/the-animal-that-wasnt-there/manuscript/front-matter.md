# Front Matter

There is an old mistake in natural history: thinking that an animal is either present or absent and that the hard part is merely looking carefully enough.

The modern version of that mistake has a sequencer attached to it.

Environmental DNA is one of the most powerful tools ever handed to field biology. An animal swims through water, brushes against a bank, sheds mucus, scales, skin, feces, gametes, cells. The animal leaves. The molecules do not leave immediately. A filter, a polymerase, a primer set and a reference sequence can sometimes recover the trace after the body has vanished from sight.

That is the miracle. It is also the problem.

DNA moves. DNA persists. DNA contaminates. DNA is carried in guts, on boots, through sewage, in ballast, on birds, through currents and laboratory aerosols. A sequence can be real while the story told about the organism is wrong. A negative can be real while the animal is still there.

This book is about that gap.

It is also about cryptozoology, although I use the word with some reluctance. Most cryptozoological claims are not secretly good science waiting to be respected. Many are anecdotes with weak provenance, images too poor to discriminate, folklore inflated into zoology, or claims that survive because no outcome is allowed to count against them. But hidden animals are not inherently ridiculous. Coelacanths were real before 1938. Giant squid were real before we photographed living ones. Species are rediscovered. Range maps fail. Taxonomies change. The scientific problem is not whether an extraordinary animal may exist. It is what evidence would force us to admit it, and what evidence should make us stop looking.

Environmental DNA makes that question operational. It turns the monster hunt into a chain-of-custody problem.
