# The Mathematics of Not Finding Something

Suppose a survey has a fifty percent chance of detecting a species on one independent visit when the species is present. One miss is weak evidence. Ten misses are different.

That intuition underlies occupancy modeling: separate the probability that a species occupies a site from the probability that we detect it conditional on occupancy.

The distinction rescued ecology from a common error. Before explicit detection models, absence data often mixed two states: species absent and species present but missed.

Environmental DNA does not abolish this distinction. It gives us a new detection process to model.

Replicate water samples can estimate imperfect detection, although independence assumptions need care. Replicate PCR wells are not equivalent to replicate field sites. Three amplifications from the same extract tell us something about molecular stochasticity, not whether sampling covered the habitat.

The hierarchy matters.

Field sites contain samples. Samples contain extracts. Extracts contain PCR replicates. Sequence reads sit below all of them. A hundred reads from one contaminated tube are not a hundred ecological observations.

The same hierarchy should govern extraordinary claims. Evidence volume is not evidence independence.

Ten witnesses standing together may have seen one event. Ten websites may trace to one witness. Ten eDNA positives may trace to one transported DNA source. Repetition strengthens a case only when the repeated observations have failure modes that are not perfectly shared.

This is where Bayesian language becomes useful if it is kept modest. A claim begins with some prior plausibility informed by biology, geography and history. Evidence updates that plausibility according to how expected the evidence would be if the claim were true versus false.

A diagnostic sequence recovered independently from multiple locations is much more expected under a surviving population than under no surviving population—unless contamination or transport makes the same pattern likely.

The arithmetic forces us to describe alternatives.

That is the virtue. Probability is less useful here as a machine for producing a final number than as a discipline for asking what would generate the observations under competing stories.

Extinction is ultimately a probabilistic conclusion about an unobserved world. So is persistence.
