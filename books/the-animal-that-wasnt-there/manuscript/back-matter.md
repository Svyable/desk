# Back Matter

This book uses four evidentiary classes throughout the research apparatus.

**Class 1 — verified / documented.** Peer-reviewed detections, physical specimens, official agency records, validated assays, accepted historical observations and directly documented methodological facts.

**Class 2 — credible but incomplete.** Serious observations or analyses with meaningful evidentiary value but missing replication, resolution, provenance or ecological closure.

**Class 3 — disputed allegation.** Survival claims, sightings, images, sounds or interpretations that remain contested and lack independent confirmation adequate to establish the animal claimed.

**Class 4 — speculative synthesis.** Proposed search designs, cross-domain inferences and open conjectures. These are arguments about what should be tested, not claims that the tests have succeeded.

The central rule is deliberately conservative: a molecular trace is evidence of a molecular trace first. Every additional ecological statement requires another inference.
