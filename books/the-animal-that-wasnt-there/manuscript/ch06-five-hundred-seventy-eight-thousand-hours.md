# 6. Five Hundred Seventy-Eight Thousand Hours

A 2023 *Ibis* analysis by Troy and colleagues estimated that more than 578,000 hours were spent searching for the ivory-billed woodpecker across the southeastern United States from 2004 through 2021. The authors argued that this enormous effort failed to produce indisputable evidence and supported declaring the species extinct.

The number is almost too good. It feels like a verdict.

But hours are not detections.

To interpret 578,000 hours, we need to know how effort overlaps habitat, how observers were distributed, how often an ivory-bill should be available to detection, what fraction of encounters would produce diagnostic evidence, whether the species changed behavior around humans, whether search sites were chosen using previous ambiguous reports, and how effort accumulated over seasons.

This does not rescue the bird. It rescues inference.

A badly distributed million hours can be weaker than a well-designed hundred thousand. Yet an enormous, geographically broad, multi-method search that repeatedly fails to produce a diagnostic photograph or specimen still pushes the probability downward. It is irrational to pretend otherwise.

The key phrase is `diagnostic evidence`.

Search campaigns can generate observations indefinitely if the threshold for an observation remains below the information content needed to distinguish target from confounders. Every ambiguous double-knock, fleeting black-and-white bird or distant kent-like call can restart attention. The process resembles a slot machine because ambiguous positives arrive intermittently while definitive evidence remains absent.

This is not fraud. It is an ecology of reinforcement.

When a rare target is emotionally valuable, institutionally salient and similar to common confounders, false positives can sustain a search long after strong positives should have appeared under the target-present hypothesis.

The scientific response is not to mock the searchers. It is to pre-register what would count, what would not, and how non-detection will update the model.

Otherwise the search can never lose.
