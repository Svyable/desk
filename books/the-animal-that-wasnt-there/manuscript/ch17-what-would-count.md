# 17. What Would Count

People ask skeptics what evidence would change their minds as if this were a personality test.

It should be a protocol.

For a claimed surviving species known from historical specimens, the cleanest evidence would be fresh biological material with secure provenance and independent genomic confirmation, ideally paired with imagery or a recoverable individual. For an entirely unknown large mammal, a type-quality specimen remains powerful, but modern documentation can reduce the need to kill the animal: multiple independent high-resolution records, environmental context, genetic material, morphology and repeat observation can converge.

One spectacular data stream is weaker than several ordinary ones that agree.

This is sensor fusion applied to biology.

A thermal camera sees a body shape. A conventional camera sees markings. Stereo gives scale. Audio gives call structure. eDNA supplies sequence. Tracks connect motion through space. A scat sample provides diet and genome. Each method has different failure modes. Convergence matters because the confounder that fools one channel may not fool the others.

The inverse is also true. Ten cameras using the same compression pipeline are not ten independent witnesses. Ten PCR replicates from one contaminated extraction are not ten animals.

Independence is an evidentiary resource.

Before the next famous search launches, someone should write the acceptance criteria on paper and publish them.

Not because nature owes us compliance.

Because we owe ourselves a way to recognize when nature has answered.
