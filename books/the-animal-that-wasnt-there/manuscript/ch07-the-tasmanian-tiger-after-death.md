# 7. The Tasmanian Tiger After Death

The last known thylacine died at Hobart Zoo in 1936. The sightings did not.

Tasmania has spent the decades since producing one of the world's most durable extinction disputes: roadside glimpses, tracks, photographs, videos, stories from hunters and bushwalkers, occasional official files, and periodic headlines announcing that perhaps this time the tiger has returned.

The thylacine is almost designed to survive as a claim. It was real within living memory. It lived in rugged country. It was persecuted. It looks enough like a dog under poor viewing conditions to generate confusions and enough unlike a dog to make a good sighting unforgettable.

There is also a strange modern reversal. We can know the dead animal with exquisite molecular detail. Researchers have sequenced a thylacine genome from museum material. In 2023 scientists reported recovery and analysis of historical RNA from a roughly 130-year-old museum specimen. We can interrogate transcriptional residue from the dead while failing to obtain a single authenticated fresh hair from the allegedly living.

That contrast is brutal.

It does not prove no thylacine survived beyond 1936. Small remnant populations can persist unseen. But persistence is not a timeless binary. A viable population has demographic requirements. Animals must reproduce. They must die. Bodies must go somewhere. They interact with prey, roads, fences, dogs, traps, cameras, water and human infrastructure.

A single hidden survivor is biologically different from a population surviving ninety years.

This is why cryptid claims should be forced to specify population models. `The thylacine survives` implies some minimum number of breeding animals through time. That number implies expected traces. The lower the number, the harder long-term demographic persistence becomes. The higher the number, the harder universal detection failure becomes.

Somewhere between those constraints, possibility gets squeezed.

The thylacine teaches a useful cruelty: a claim can remain emotionally vivid after its ecological parameter space becomes very small.
