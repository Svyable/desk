The Most Important Company of Every American Decade—and the Stories That Built a Nation.

*Republic of Companies* tells the economic history of the United States through one deliberately arguable choice for every decade from the 1770s through the 2020s. The winner is not necessarily the decade’s biggest company, richest company, most admirable company, or best investment. It is the company whose methods, infrastructure, products, labor system, financing, distribution, or political relationship most changed the operating system of American life.

The book begins with a company that was not American at all: the British East India Company, whose privileged tea became an object of colonial revolt. It then moves through the first national bank, Slater’s mechanized mill, DuPont, Astor’s fur empire, Lowell, the B&O, the telegraph, Singer, Union Pacific, Standard Oil, AT&T, Sears, U.S. Steel, Ford, General Motors, Procter & Gamble, Boeing, McDonald’s, IBM, Intel, Microsoft, Walmart, Google, Amazon, and NVIDIA. Together they form a second history of the republic: not a parade of founders, but a history of the organizational machines that taught Americans how to work, buy, travel, communicate, borrow, compute, and imagine scale.

The argument is not that corporations built America by themselves. Every company in this book sits inside public law, public infrastructure, labor, migration, war, land, science, finance, and political choices. Several of the companies became powerful through arrangements that imposed severe costs on workers, competitors, Indigenous nations, communities, or the public. Importance is therefore not praise. The companies worth understanding are often precisely the ones whose achievements and harms became difficult to separate.

The 2020s selection is intentionally provisional. As of September 2026, NVIDIA best fits the book’s operating-system criterion because accelerated computing has become a scarce industrial input beneath rival AI futures. The chapter is written to be revisited before a Shelf release if the unfinished decade changes the evidence.
