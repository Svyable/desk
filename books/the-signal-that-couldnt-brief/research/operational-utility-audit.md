# Operational utility audit — The Signal That Couldn't Brief

## Why this note exists

The opening currently risks making the 1995 AIR review sound cleaner than the surviving government record. The strongest version of the book does not need a cleaner verdict. It needs the messier institutional sequence: customers tried remote viewing because difficult intelligence problems made a free additional source worth trying; evaluators then had to separate descriptive correspondence from information specific enough to change an operation.

## Primary records checked — 17 September 2026

### STAR GATE operational user interview, 26 July 1995

CIA Reading Room document CIA-RDP96-00791R000200300002-2:
https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/CIA-RDP96-00791R000200300002-2.pdf

One operational customer described tasking aimed at a person, location, vessel, or similar target. The interview is valuable because it records the customer's decision context rather than a laboratory score. The user said STAR GATE was attractive as an alternate source partly because there was no cost to the user and, given the difficulty of the work, trying a weak additional source was not unreasonable. This is direct evidence for the book's proposed 'cheap hedge' mechanism, but not evidence that the source worked.

Editorial use: Chapter 2 should build the institutional motive from this documented customer logic rather than generic Cold War credulity. The absurdity is useful: a program can survive for rational reasons even when the phenomenon underneath it is extraordinary. Zero marginal cost to a customer changes the adoption threshold.

### Proposed management strategy after the AIR review

CIA Reading Room document CIA-RDP96-00791R000100150004-8:
https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/CIA-RDP96-00791R000100150004-8.pdf

The post-review CIA management paper recommends terminating both remote-viewing operations and related R&D in the Intelligence Community. In summarizing AIR, it says operational information was too vague and ambiguous to yield information of sufficient quality and accuracy for actionable intelligence. It separately says the laboratory studies did not establish the source or origin of the reported phenomenon and that whether remote viewing itself had been demonstrated remained unclear.

This is a stronger source for the book's governing distinction than a retrospective paraphrase because the document itself keeps operational utility and scientific interpretation separate.

### Earlier CIA management option before AIR was complete

CIA Reading Room document CIA-RDP96-00791R000100160001-0:
https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/CIA-RDP96-00791R000100160001-0.pdf

Before the blue-ribbon review was completed, an internal CIA memorandum records an alternative recommendation: take National Research Council advice and conclude without further review that STAR GATE was unlikely to be of value to CIA. That matters because the eventual termination decision did not occur in an institutional vacuum. There was already internal pressure toward a utility judgment before AIR supplied its final technical framing.

Publication rule: do not narrate AIR as a pristine experiment that mechanically caused termination. Reconstruct the congressional transfer requirement, CIA's pre-existing management choices, AIR's review, user interviews, and the later management recommendation as separate steps.

## Strongest counterargument to the book's thesis

The phrase 'a real weak signal could still be useless' is intellectually attractive enough to become a refuge. It can preserve the paranormal interpretation after operational failure by moving the claimed effect below whatever threshold intelligence requires. The book must not let that happen.

There are at least three competing models:

1. **Weak real signal:** an anomalous information channel exists, but effect size, specificity, or reliability is too low for operational use.
2. **Method artifact plus ordinary matching:** laboratory scoring creates an apparent anomaly through leakage, target structure, judging flexibility, analytical choices, or dependence; operational work exposes the absence of a transferable signal.
3. **Human-information system:** viewers produce broad associative material; analysts and customers sometimes extract useful correspondences through ordinary inference, cueing, selection, or hindsight, without either paranormal information or a simple laboratory artifact explaining every case.

The manuscript should not privilege model 1 merely because it creates the most elegant bridge between Utts and operational failure. The operational archive cannot distinguish these models by itself.

## Quantitative burden for Chapter 16

'Actionable' needs a denominator. Reconstruct, where records permit:

- number of operational tasks, sessions per task, and customers;
- whether evaluations were prospective or retrospective;
- specificity of requested information before the session;
- number of independently checkable propositions in raw output;
- true/false/unknown rates rather than selected correspondences;
- analyst editing or interpretation between viewer and customer;
- baseline probability of each claimed feature;
- whether the information changed collection, targeting, search, resource allocation, or another decision;
- the cost of acting on false detail versus ignoring true detail;
- whether conventional intelligence later supplied the feedback used to score the session.

A p-value is not the comparison. The comparison is expected decision value with and without the report.

## Narrative opportunity

The July 1995 customer interview gives the book a better recurring object than a generic 'briefing room': a difficult target, an alternate source that costs the customer nothing, and a report whose useful-looking fragments still have to compete with ambiguity. Return to that customer after the laboratory chapters. By then the reader should understand why 'better than chance' can be scientifically interesting while still failing to answer the customer's original question.

## Publication holds

- Recover locator-level text from the full AIR report before quoting Utts, Hyman, or the operational conclusion directly.
- Build a task-level table from surviving operational evaluations before giving a global hit/miss rate.
- Do not equate the roughly 45-box transferred collection with a complete archive of every CIA/DIA/SRI/SAIC record.
- Do not write that AIR alone caused termination; the pre-review management record shows CIA was already considering a no-transfer/no-value path.
- Do not use CIA's 2021 retrospective summary to collapse AIR's nuanced laboratory conclusion into 'enough accurate experiences existed to defy randomness' without checking the underlying report.
