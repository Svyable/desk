# Evidence ledger — The Signal That Couldn't Brief

## Class 1 — verified / declassified / documented

- The U.S. government funded remote-viewing/anomalous-cognition research and operational tasking across CIA, DoD/DIA and contractor settings over multiple decades. CIA's current public account summarizes the institutional history and links the declassified collection: https://www.cia.gov/stories/story/ask-molly-did-cia-really-study-psychic-powers/
- The 1995 CIA-commissioned American Institutes for Research evaluation is the governing capstone source. Its operational evaluation says laboratory conditions did not map cleanly to intelligence collection; end users found some broad descriptive accuracy but insufficient concrete specifics; reports were inconsistent and interpretation-heavy; and the reviewed information had not been used to guide intelligence operations. Primary CIA copy/search target: CIA STARGATE collection; public transcription cross-check: https://irp.fas.org/program/collect/stargate.htm
- A declassified STAR GATE operational-tasking summary states that more than 200 tasks were received from operational military organizations between 1986 and early FY1995 and describes a later numerical end-user evaluation process. CIA document: https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/CIA-RDP96-00791R000200300002-2.pdf
- The AIR review preserved a substantive expert disagreement. Jessica Utts argued that laboratory evidence established anomalous functioning; Ray Hyman argued that such a conclusion was premature and emphasized independent replication and methodological alternatives. Their disagreement must be quoted only from recovered primary text.
- CIA's retrospective public account says the agency did not restore the program after the AIR evaluation.

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete

- Some laboratory remote-viewing datasets have repeatedly produced above-chance statistical results under published protocols. A 2023 systematic review/meta-analysis reports a positive pooled effect across selected remote-viewing studies. This is evidence about that literature, not proof of extrasensory causation: https://doi.org/10.31275/20232931
- A later registered-report meta-analysis of ganzfeld anomalous-perception studies reports a small pooled effect across a larger related literature. Its peer-review history includes reservations and it is not a direct replication of STAR GATE: https://doi.org/10.12688/f1000research.51746.4
- Specific historical operational cases may contain impressive correspondences, but case-level evidentiary weight depends on contemporaneous tasking, timestamped raw output, target definition, feedback history, analyst editing, and the complete miss rate.

## Class 3 — disputed allegations / unconfirmed interpretation

- That remote viewing demonstrates a psychic, nonlocal, precognitive, or otherwise extrasensory information channel. Statistical anomaly does not identify mechanism.
- Claims that individual remote viewers repeatedly delivered decisive intelligence successes unless supported by complete contemporaneous operational files and downstream-use records.
- Claims that termination proves the phenomenon was false, or conversely that classification/termination proves it worked too well. Neither inference follows from program disposition.
- Claims about Soviet/Russian operational capability require their own source chain; U.S. threat perceptions are evidence of what U.S. officials believed or feared, not proof of foreign efficacy.

## Class 4 — speculative synthesis / open conjecture

- A weak anomalous-information effect could in principle be real while remaining below the threshold for intelligence utility.
- The STAR GATE archive may be a general case study in the gap between statistical significance, predictive performance, calibration, and decision value.
- Classification plus ridicule may produce a distorted evidence ecology in which poor public information increases confidence at both extremes.

## Contradictions and incentive audit

Program researchers had incentives to preserve funding and interpret anomalies as meaningful. Skeptical evaluators had reputational commitments but also supplied essential methodological criticism. Intelligence customers had incentives to demand specificity and reliability rather than scientific novelty. Agencies could fund a low-cost hedge because adversary capability was uncertain. Later memoirs reward memorable hits more than ordinary misses. Declassification is selective in survival and context even when lawful release is broad. None of these incentives determines the truth of the phenomenon; each changes how evidence should be weighted.

## Strongest conventional countermodel

The apparent laboratory anomaly can be produced by some combination of methodological leakage, target-set structure, judging flexibility, selective reporting, non-independent replication, analytical degrees of freedom, and ordinary statistical fluctuation. Operational anecdotes are then amplified by retrospective selection and subjective matching. The book must allow this model to win.

## Defeat condition

The central 'signal versus utility' thesis weakens if a citation-level reconstruction shows that the supposedly anomalous laboratory effects disappear under high-quality independent preregistered replication. In that case the operational failure requires no weak-signal explanation; the more economical story is artifact plus folklore. Conversely, evidence for a robust laboratory anomaly still does not establish psychic mechanism until conventional information channels and methodological alternatives are excluded.
