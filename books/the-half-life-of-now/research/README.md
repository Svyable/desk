# Research trail

*The Half-Life of Now* is narrative nonfiction built around the original synthesis of **freshness debt**: the liability created when the representations used to make decisions remain formally valid but no longer correspond closely enough to current conditions.

The book deliberately avoids inline academic citation in the main prose. This directory records the evidence architecture behind the manuscript so later revisions can inspect the age of the book's own claims.

## Research method

Sources are chosen to establish the real mechanisms behind the book's cross-domain argument: information decay and half-life, cultural and institutional lag, model and concept drift, latency in markets, revision and retention in law, currentness in public statistics, clinical guideline maintenance, data protection and rectification, climate observation, and the governance of rapidly changing technological systems.

For every future revision, the editor should ask four questions of an important source: What did it establish? Where is it used? What newer evidence could supersede it? What would falsify the chapter's interpretation?

## Counterevidence standard

The thesis does **not** claim that newer information is automatically better, that all institutions should update faster, or that old evidence loses truth merely because time passes. Many facts are durable; many institutions benefit from slow clocks; rapid updating can amplify noise, instability, surveillance, and error. The argument is narrower: consequential systems should know enough about the age and conditions of their knowledge to determine whether it remains current enough for the decision at hand.
