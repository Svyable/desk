# FALSE FOSSIL

## The Martian rock that taught us how not to discover life

Sven Hardy Benson

---

This is a book about a claim that became more useful after it became less convincing.

In 1996 a team led by David McKay reported several features in Martian meteorite ALH84001 that, taken together, they argued were consistent with possible ancient biological activity. The wording mattered: *possible*. The public image was less cautious. A microscopic form in a rock from Mars became, for a moment, the closest thing civilization had to a photograph of extraterrestrial life.

The ensuing argument did not merely knock down a fossil. It built a discipline's immune system.

This manuscript uses four evidence classes throughout:

1. **Documented / verified.** Measurements, sample provenance, published experiments, official records and findings reproducible enough to be treated as facts about the rock or the history of its study.
2. **Credible but incomplete.** Interpretations with serious empirical support that remain underdetermined or depend on incomplete reconstruction.
3. **Disputed / unconfirmed.** Claims for biological origin, diagnostic morphology or mechanisms that have credible opposition or lack independent confirmation.
4. **Speculative synthesis.** The book's own proposals about biosignature epistemology, counterfeit libraries and how future life-detection experiments should be designed.

The governing prohibition is simple: **indigenous is not biological; organic is not biological; resemblance is not ancestry; several ambiguous clues do not become one unambiguous clue merely by being placed in the same paragraph.**

That prohibition also runs in reverse. Finding an abiotic route for one feature does not prove every molecule in the sample formed that way. A mechanism's availability is not instance identification. The standard has to cut both directions or it is not a standard.
