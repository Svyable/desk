# RED SIGNAL

## How an enemy's impossible research becomes your own secret program

**Sven Hardy Benson**

This is a book about an intelligence problem disguised as a paranormal one.

It does not assume remote viewing works. It does not assume it does not. It asks a prior question: what happens inside a security state when the adversary may be spending money on a capability that sounds impossible, the cost of checking is modest, and the cost of being wrong is imagined as strategic surprise?

The archival record is unusually good at proving interest and unusually bad at proving the extraordinary mechanism. That asymmetry is the subject.

## Evidence discipline

The manuscript keeps four classes separate.

1. **Verified / declassified / documented.** Programs, session reports, translated foreign literature, contracts, official evaluations, dates, institutional transfers and conclusions present in public records.
2. **Credible but incomplete reporting or testimony.** Participant recollections and historical reconstructions that fit surviving records but lack a complete contemporaneous documentary chain.
3. **Disputed allegations.** Claims of operationally useful paranormal acquisition, psychotronic weapons, reliable telepathy or other capabilities not independently established by the public evidence used here.
4. **Speculative synthesis / open conjecture.** The book's model of *threat mirroring*: an adversary's reported interest can become an input to one's own research program even when neither side possesses the capability feared.

A classified stamp proves classification. A budget proves expenditure. A session report proves a session occurred. A translated Soviet article proves that somebody translated it. None of those facts, alone, proves psi.

The reverse error is prohibited too. Institutional embarrassment, weak operational utility, methodological criticism or eventual cancellation do not logically prove that every statistical anomaly in the experimental literature is zero. Mechanism, effect size and usefulness are different questions.

The book will not turn testimony into proof or missing files into capabilities. Where a foreign program is known principally through U.S. intelligence reporting, that mediation is part of the evidence rather than scenery.
