# RED SIGNAL — evidence ledger

## Thesis under test

Cold War psi research can be read as a case of **threat mirroring**: reports that an adversary was investigating anomalous cognition increased the rational incentive to investigate it oneself, while one's own secret investigation could later become evidence, rumor or stimulus for the adversary. This feedback can sustain programs longer than the underlying phenomenon alone would predict. The model does **not** require psi to be false; it predicts a funding dynamic under uncertainty either way.

## Class 1 — verified / declassified / documented

- CIA publicly acknowledges U.S. government research into remote viewing and the later independent 1995 evaluation; CIA says the program was not restored because the phenomenon was judged too unreliable, inconsistent and sporadic for intelligence use. https://www.cia.gov/stories/story/ask-molly-did-cia-really-study-psychic-powers/
- CIA FOIA hosts the STARGATE collection and operational/training session reports. Example: 26 August 1980 session D-8 concerning the Iran hostage situation explicitly calls the impressions `raw intelligence data` and places interpretation/use on the requester. https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp96-00788r001000090001-2
- A 1978 CIA-hosted JPRS translation volume documents Soviet public discussion using terms including parapsychology, bioinformation and psychotronics. It is evidence that U.S. institutions collected and translated such material; individual Soviet claims inside it require their own validation. https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/CIA-RDP96-00787R000500430001-1.pdf
- The CIA FOIA archive contains U.S. analytical material discussing Soviet/Eastern European interest in parapsychology and bioeffects. The existence of such reporting demonstrates collection and concern, not the truth of every reported foreign result. https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/
- Milton & Wiseman (1999) meta-analyzed 30 post-Honorton Ganzfeld studies and reported failure to replicate the main above-chance effect. PMID 10414223; DOI 10.1037/0033-2909.125.4.387. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10414223/
- The Ganzfeld literature remained contested afterward; Storm & Ertel (2001) and later exchanges argued positive aggregate results. This is evidence of a durable methodological dispute, not settled mechanism. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11393304/

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete

- Participant histories and declassified program narratives can reconstruct how perceived Soviet interest affected U.S. sponsor attention, but each claimed causal arrow—foreign report → briefing → contract → operational requirement—must be sourced rather than inferred from chronology.
- Some U.S. evaluators accepted that laboratory data contained statistical departures they did not reduce to chance while rejecting intelligence utility. That distinction should be reconstructed from the original 1995 AIR report before publication rather than compressed into later CIA summary language.

## Class 3 — disputed allegations

- Reliable remote viewing as an intelligence collection channel.
- Soviet operational psychotronic weapons or reliable paranormal espionage capabilities.
- Claims that long program duration or secrecy itself proves successful anomalous cognition.
- Claims that program cancellation proves all laboratory effects were artifacts.

## Class 4 — speculative synthesis / open conjecture

### Threat mirroring
A state can rationally fund investigation of a low-prior capability when the expected cost of adversary surprise is high. If the adversary applies the same reasoning, reciprocal concern can create a research ecology partly driven by observations of each other's *interest* rather than demonstrated capability.

### The interest/capability gap
`They studied X` and `they possessed X` are different propositions. Intelligence archives are biased toward the first because collection systems preserve leads, rumors, translations and requirements even when capabilities fail.

### Archive amplification
Declassification can make failed or ambiguous secret research look retrospectively more evidential than it was operationally because the surviving archive contains the machinery of attention: taskings, reports, classifications and program names. Bureaucratic density is not effect size.

### Mirror test
For any alleged foreign anomalous capability, ask what evidence would remain if every document describing *interest in the capability* were removed. What remains should be measurements, independently validated performance, hardware, reproducible protocols or outcomes. If almost nothing remains, the case may be primarily an intelligence-interest story.

## Contradictions to preserve

- Government sponsorship is compatible with both a real weak phenomenon and strategic hedging against rumor.
- A statistical anomaly can be real yet operationally useless.
- An operational anecdote can be impressive yet scientifically weak because target selection, feedback, scoring and failed attempts are incompletely specified.
- Foreign-source translation is simultaneously valuable primary evidence of what U.S. analysts consumed and secondary evidence for what actually happened abroad.

## Publication holds

- Do not describe STARGATE as one continuous CIA program; institutional names, sponsors and transfers changed.
- Do not use `psychotronics` as if it denoted one coherent demonstrated Soviet technology.
- Recover and quote the 1995 AIR evaluation directly before final manuscript claims about its statistical and operational conclusions.
- Separate contemporaneous Soviet/Russian primary publications from U.S. intelligence summaries of them.
- Do not infer classified success from program longevity, destroyed/missing records, redactions or secrecy.
- Treat patents as evidence of filing only, never efficacy.

## Defeat condition

The threat-mirroring thesis weakens substantially if contemporaneous records show that program continuation was primarily driven by independently replicated, prospectively scored operational performance rather than adversary concern, sponsor uncertainty or ambiguous experimental evidence. It also weakens if foreign programs can be documented as possessing robust capabilities independently of U.S. threat reporting.
