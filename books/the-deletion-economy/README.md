# The Deletion Economy

*Why the Next Great Industries Will Be Built by Taking Things Away*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Author** | Sven Hardy Benson |
| **Status** | Drafting |
| **Chapters** | 19 of 19 drafted |

The modern economy knows how to celebrate beginnings. This book argues that endings—wells plugged, platforms removed, reactors dismantled, mines reclaimed, lead pipes excavated, persistent chemicals managed, carbon stored, dead satellites removed, invasive species eradicated, electronics recovered, data sanitized, and legacy systems retired—are not peripheral chores. They are an economy of their own.

Every durable asset creates present usefulness and a future obligation. When that obligation is deferred or orphaned, the result is a **negative asset**: something whose market value can approach zero while its cost to monitor, remove, neutralize, dismantle, or contain remains real.

The deeper shift is from an economy that asks mainly, “What can we add?” to one that also asks, “What must be ended, who owns the ending, and was the ending funded when the beginning was profitable?”
