# Research Notes — The Deletion Economy

This ledger supports the manuscript with primary and high-authority sources. It is not intended to turn the book into a literature review. Its purpose is to keep factual claims traceable, time-bounded, and falsifiable while leaving the manuscript readable.

## Research approach

Preference order: regulators and official program pages; government oversight and audit bodies; standards organizations; original peer-reviewed research; then first-party technical material. Market-size claims are deliberately avoided where a defensible apples-to-apples estimate does not exist.

Sources were checked during drafting in 2026. Program totals, inventories, regulatory requirements, and cost estimates can change. Readers and future editors should treat dates as part of the claim.

## Falsification ledger

| Claim or framing | What would falsify or materially weaken it | Evidence status |
|---|---|---|
| Orphaned wells can become negative assets whose plugging cost exceeds residual productive value. | Broad evidence showing end-of-life well costs are normally trivial or fully covered by surviving operators. | Supported by DOI programs, GAO oversight, and published cost research; costs vary widely. |
| Offshore decommissioning represents a material long-dated liability. | Current comprehensive evidence showing obligations are both timely and fully secured across the relevant federal offshore portfolio. | GAO-24-106229 documented overdue infrastructure and a large assurance gap as of its reporting period; time-bounded. |
| Nuclear retirement requires dedicated financial planning over long horizons. | Evidence that decommissioning is routinely inexpensive and can be funded from residual asset value at shutdown. | NRC requires financial assurance and publishes decommissioning guidance; site outcomes vary. |
| PFAS separation can create a residual stream rather than destroy the molecules. | Treatment evidence showing common separation processes universally mineralize PFAS with no residual management problem. | Supported; EPA distinguishes treatment, destruction/disposal pathways, and uncertainties. |
| Carbon removal is complementary to deep emissions reduction rather than a blanket substitute. | A future scientific consensus showing durable CDR can safely and economically replace deep mitigation across emissions sources. | Supported by IPCC framing; technologies and costs continue to evolve. |
| Orbital debris is an accumulated shared-environment problem. | Sustained evidence that debris populations and collision risk decline without improved mitigation or remediation. | ESA and NASA support mitigation; active-removal economics remain uncertain. |
| E-waste generation substantially exceeds formally documented collection/recycling. | New global measurement showing nearly all generated e-waste enters documented formal systems. | ITU's 2024 monitor reports a large gap using 2022 data; update when a new global monitor supersedes it. |
| Real data deletion is a systems and lineage problem, not merely a user-interface action. | Architectures and standards making deletion reliably complete across replicas, media, backups, and derived systems with negligible coordination. | NIST sanitization and data-protection enforcement support the distinction; model-derived state remains an evolving area. |
| Legacy-system retirement is a distinct engineering problem. | Evidence that modern migration tools make consequential legacy retirement routine, low-risk, and inexpensive across institutions. | GAO documents continued federal legacy-system cost and risk; private-sector generalization should remain cautious. |

## Open questions

The manuscript intentionally does not attach a single dollar figure to the deletion economy. A credible market size would require avoiding double counting across waste management, remediation, decommissioning, cybersecurity, reverse logistics, carbon removal, demolition, insurance, and public infrastructure. Future research could build a stock-and-flow model instead.

Other open questions include the economics of active orbital debris removal; durable business models for long-tail monitoring; the effect of producer responsibility on product design; how software and AI systems should prove deletion of derived state; and whether capital markets will develop a standardized measure of retirement readiness.
