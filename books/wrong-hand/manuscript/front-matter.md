# WRONG HAND

## Life chose a side. The universe did not.

**Sven Hardy Benson**

This book begins with an asymmetry small enough to draw on paper and large enough to divide the living world from a hypothetical second biology.

Known terrestrial proteins overwhelmingly use L-amino acids. DNA and RNA use D-sugars. Chemistry can make both hands. Meteorites can even arrive with an excess of one hand without having been alive. Meanwhile synthetic biology has advanced far enough that serious researchers are asking humanity not to build a fully self-replicating organism whose molecular handedness is reversed.

Those facts do not add up to a secret second biosphere. They add up to a better question: **when does molecular asymmetry become evidence of life, and when is it only chemistry with a bias?**

The book keeps four ledgers.

1. **Verified / documented.** Measurements, peer-reviewed experiments, mission capabilities, established molecular biology, and public technical assessments.
2. **Credible but incomplete.** Results whose interpretation remains open, proposed mechanisms with partial empirical support, and observations that need stronger provenance or replication.
3. **Disputed / unconfirmed.** Claims of shadow biospheres, alien biology, decisive chiral biosignatures, or capabilities not independently established.
4. **Speculative synthesis.** The book's own proposed tests and concepts. These are allowed to be interesting only if they are allowed to lose.

A patent is evidence that somebody filed a patent. A model is evidence that a model produced an output. A molecule is evidence that the molecule is present. None of these facts automatically supplies the ontology people want from it.

The governing prohibition is simple:

> **Handedness is a clue. It is not a birth certificate.**

The stranger corollary runs the other direction. If we ever encounter a coherent biology built from the opposite molecular hand, the difference may be more informative than a thousand familiar-looking organic molecules. Alienness might not look like a monster. It might look like glucose that turns the wrong way.
