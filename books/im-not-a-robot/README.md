# I’m Not a Robot

**And Other Stories I Tell Myself**

| **Author** | Sven Hardy Benson |

Every few days, somewhere on the internet, a machine asks a person to certify a fact the person has never seriously doubted: *I’m not a robot.* The box is funny until you remember what came before it. Humans once proved their humanity by reading words machines could not read, identifying objects machines could not see, and performing tiny acts of perception that helped make the machines better at reading and seeing. The test kept moving because the machines kept learning from the people taking it.

This book begins inside that small historical joke and follows it into a larger one. We like to imagine a clean border between human beings and machines: machines run scripts; people choose. Machines predict; people understand. Machines remix; people create. Machines autocomplete; people mean what they say. But ordinary human life has always depended on repetition, habit, borrowed language, memory reconstruction, social imitation, defaults, tools, institutions, and stories assembled after decisions have already begun.

The argument is not that people are machines. It is that **“not a robot” is a surprisingly bad definition of a human being**. The more capable machines become, the more fragile any identity built from whatever they cannot yet do becomes. A better distinction is harder and more demanding: a human life contains scripts, but a person can notice a script, refuse it, revise it, explain it badly, inherit another one, regret the revision, and still remain responsible for what happens next.

*I’m Not a Robot* is about the stories that make that responsibility bearable: that I chose this, that I know why, that I remember, that my taste is mine, that I am consistent, that I am paying attention, that my work came from me, that I am in control. Most are not simply false. They are useful compressions. Trouble begins when we mistake the compression for the whole person.
