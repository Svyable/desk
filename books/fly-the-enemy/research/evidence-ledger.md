# FLY THE ENEMY — Evidence ledger

Working rule: **a retrieved object is the beginning of an evidence chain, not the end of one.**

## Evidence classes

### 1 — Verified / declassified / documented

- The U.S. Air Force operated Soviet-designed MiG-17, MiG-21 and MiG-23 aircraft in the classified Constant Peg program through the 4477th Test and Evaluation Squadron. Official National Museum of the U.S. Air Force history says the program ran from 1977–1988, flew more than 15,000 sorties and trained almost 6,000 U.S. aircrew.
  - https://www.nationalmuseum.af.mil/Visit/Museum-Exhibits/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/1682967/constant-peg-secret-migs-in-the-desert/
- The same official history states that late-1960s HAVE DOUGHNUT / HAVE DRILL testing supplied technical exploitation of MiG-21/MiG-17 aircraft and that Constant Peg later used actual MiGs for operational training.
  - same source above.
- National Security Archive's Area 51 documentary collection reports that Israel acquired an Iraqi MiG-21 in 1966 after a defection arranged in advance by Mossad; the aircraft was loaned to the U.S. Air Force from 23 January to 8 April 1968 and exploited as YF-110 under HAVE DOUGHNUT at Area 51. Treat the underlying released documents as the publication target for exact dates/details, not the briefing-book prose alone.
  - https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/briefing-book/intelligence/2013-10-29/area-51-file-secret-aircraft-soviet-migs
- An actual MiG-23 flown by the 4477th was declassified and transferred to the National Museum of the U.S. Air Force in February 2017.
  - https://www.nationalmuseum.af.mil/Visit/Museum-Exhibits/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/1162493/mikoyan-gurevich-mig-23ms-flogger-e/
- Air Combat Command/Nellis publicly documents Constant Peg's mission and participants.
  - https://www.acc.af.mil/News/Article/988343/capt-carlisle-unveiled-mig-23-dedicated-to-acc-commander/

### 2 — Credible but incomplete reporting / testimony

- Participant memoirs, reunion histories and interviews can add acquisition, maintenance, flight-test and compartmentation detail, but must be attributed and checked against released program records before being promoted to Class 1.
- Claims that Constant Peg materially caused later U.S. air-combat dominance are plausible and frequently made by participants/institutional histories, but causal attribution is multivariate (training reforms, weapons, sensors, doctrine, adversary quality, etc.). Do not turn post hoc performance into a single-program proof.

### 3 — Disputed allegations

- Public allegations that the U.S. government or contractors possess recovered UAP craft, non-human technology, biological material, or long-running reverse-engineering programs are not established by the foreign-materiel precedents in this book. Attribute each claim to the person/document making it and track corroboration separately.
- A person's clearance, title, military service, intelligence employment or access to a complaint process is not proof of the underlying extraordinary claim.
- Patents associated with alleged exotic propulsion/materials programs are evidence of filing and text, not demonstrated efficacy.

### 4 — Speculative synthesis / open conjecture

- **Exploitation wake:** valuable physical objects perturb institutions through facilities, personnel, measurement, maintenance, procurement, safety, training, doctrine and derivative engineering. This is a proposed comparative framework, not a law.
- **Maintenance truth:** operational hardware creates practical knowledge that may be harder to suppress indefinitely than abstract program claims. Test comparatively; do not assume leaks are inevitable.
- **Black hangar fallacy:** classification neither makes every hidden program plausible nor makes large hidden programs impossible.
- **Artifact test:** evaluate provenance → custody → inventory → measurement → exploitation attempts → failures → derivative knowledge → institutional wake → externally observable consequences.

## Contradictions / incentives to track

1. **Institutional commemoration bias.** Official Air Force histories may simplify or celebrate programs. Use released technical/program records for precise claims.
2. **Participant prestige.** Retrospective accounts can exaggerate uniqueness, danger or downstream impact.
3. **Security residue.** Acquisition pathways, liaison relationships and technical details may remain redacted after a program's broad existence is public.
4. **UAP analogy pressure.** Historical retrieval programs are rhetorically attractive to both proponents and skeptics. Do not let analogy substitute for event-specific evidence.
5. **Survivorship bias.** We know Constant Peg because it was eventually declassified. Unknown successful programs are absent from the comparison set by definition.
6. **Technology-distance problem.** A Soviet fighter and a hypothetical non-human artifact are not equally exploitable. The framework must scale expected wake to technical distance rather than demand MiG-like outputs from every object.

## Publication holds

- Do not state exact HAVE DOUGHNUT sortie counts, test findings, acquisition chain details or performance measurements until checked against the released reports themselves.
- Do not imply all Constant Peg MiGs came through the same acquisition channel.
- Do not state that absence of a visible exploitation wake disproves a retrieval claim. Use it as Bayesian/diagnostic pressure conditioned on expected scale and observability.
- Do not name any contemporary contractor as holding anomalous material without primary documentary evidence specific to that claim.
- Do not present congressional testimony, whistleblower complaints or secondhand briefings as physical corroboration.

## Defeat conditions

The book's strongest proposed framework weakens if historical foreign-materiel cases show that technically consequential exploitation programs can run for decades while producing essentially no discoverable logistics, technical derivative, personnel footprint, documentation, artifact, accident history, institutional descendant or eventual archival trace even after comparable declassification windows.

Conversely, the framework strengthens if multiple independent exploitation cases show recurring, measurable wakes whose structure scales with object count, technical distance, operational use and program duration.
