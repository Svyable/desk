# FLY THE ENEMY

## What real secret retrieval programs leave behind

**Sven Hardy Benson**

---

There is a sentence people use when they want secrecy to do evidentiary work.

*Of course there is no paper trail. It was classified.*

Sometimes that sentence is true. It is also incomplete.

The United States really did acquire Soviet fighter aircraft it was not supposed to have. It really did hide them in Nevada. It really did test them, take them apart, keep them flying, teach American pilots how to fight them, and conceal the enterprise from most of the country. Some of the aircraft arrived through intelligence relationships that could not safely be advertised. Some programs stayed dark for years.

And yet the secrecy did not make the machines weightless.

They needed runways. Fuel. Mechanics. Spare parts. Technical intelligence. Test cards. Pilots. Accident investigations. Hangars. Training ranges. Money. People who knew which switch not to touch. Eventually they left histories, declassified program names, surviving aircraft, institutional descendants and thousands of trained aircrew.

That is the subject of this book.

Not whether governments retrieve foreign technology. They do.

Not whether they exploit it in secret. They do that too.

The harder question is what a *real* exploitation program looks like when the object is difficult, scarce, dangerous, politically sensitive and technologically valuable—and what that historical control does, and does not, permit us to infer about modern allegations of recovered anomalous or non-human technology.

This book uses four evidence classes throughout:

1. **Verified / declassified / documented.** Official records, released program histories, surviving artifacts, contemporaneous technical material and other independently checkable evidence.
2. **Credible but incomplete reporting or testimony.** Named participants, officials, investigators or journalists whose accounts may be informed but do not independently establish every asserted fact.
3. **Disputed allegations.** Claims lacking sufficient independent confirmation, including allegations of hidden crash-retrieval or reverse-engineering programs.
4. **Speculative synthesis.** Explicit conjecture used to generate tests, not to smuggle conclusions into the record.

A patent proves that somebody filed a patent. A clearance proves access to some protected information, not the truth of every story told by the cleared person. A program name proves only what the underlying record supports. Testimony is testimony. Classification can explain missing evidence; it cannot become evidence of the thing allegedly classified.

The governing rule is simple:

**A retrieved object is the beginning of an evidence chain, not the end of one.**
