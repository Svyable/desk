# Contradiction and Incentive Audit

## `Nuclear pacemakers failed because radiation was too dangerous`

Current public evidence does not support that as the principal explanation. Published follow-up reported strong generator survival and no obvious toxicity signal in the observed cohorts. That is not proof of zero risk; it shifts the burden to dose reconstruction, cohort size and documented adverse events.

## `They were a forgotten superior technology killed by regulation`

Also too simple. Lithium batteries improved rapidly, pacemaker electronics became more capable, and inappropriate pacing mode was a leading reason for replacing nuclear units in the major decade-long series. Special nuclear-material regulation was a real burden, but the competitor improved simultaneously.

## `Several curies inside a patient means a large patient dose`

False equivalence. Activity, source containment, radiation type, geometry and dose are different quantities. Model-specific dosimetry is required.

## `The capsule survived cremation tests, so retrieval did not matter`

Wrong system boundary. Physical containment is a fallback layer. Regulations still required explantation/return because a surviving source in a furnace, ash stream, grave or waste chain remains controlled material.

## `Lost source means exposed public`

Not established. NRC enforcement proves loss of control/accountability. Exposure or contamination requires separate evidence.

## `Thirty-four years proves lifetime safety`

No. It proves extraordinary individual generator longevity and informs feasibility. Population safety needs cohort/dose evidence.

## Incentives to track

- **AEC / nuclear agencies:** incentive to demonstrate beneficial civilian isotope applications; also institutional responsibility for source safety.
- **NUMEC / manufacturers:** contract and product incentives to demonstrate performance; liability and regulatory incentives to document containment.
- **Cardiologists:** incentive to reduce repeat surgeries and adopt devices that improve patient care; professional enthusiasm may favor novel systems.
- **Lithium/device manufacturers:** incentive to frame chemical battery progress as eliminating need for nuclear systems.
- **Regulators:** incentive to avoid orphan sources, sometimes independent of clinical utility.
- **Later storytellers:** incentive to exaggerate either the `atomic miracle` or `plutonium in people` angle because the ordinary systems trade is less dramatic.
