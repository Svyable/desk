# Research ledger — The Animal Could See North

## Working rule

Do not use the word *quantum* as incense. Every quantum claim must terminate in a chemical observable, a behavioral result, or an explicit theoretical prediction. A radical pair is not mystical because electron spin appears in its kinetics. Conversely, do not erase the genuinely odd scale problem: Earth's field is roughly tens of microtesla, yet controlled animal behavior and protein photochemistry indicate biological access to directional information at that scale.

## Contradiction and incentive audit

### The right-eye lesson
An elegant result can become a story too quickly. The 2002 claim that robins could not orient magnetically with only the left eye fed a memorable lateralization narrative. Hein et al. reported that they could not independently confirm it under double-blind protocols. This belongs in the book not as scandal but as method: magnetoreception experiments are unusually vulnerable to small environmental differences, enclosure artifacts, uncontrolled electromagnetic noise, seasonal state, and analyst expectation.

### The RF lesson
Oscillating radio-frequency fields have been treated as a near-signature of radical-pair physics because weak RF perturbations can, in some models, affect spin dynamics. But localization experiments and replications do not form a perfectly clean ladder. A 2020 garden-warbler study applying oscillating fields locally to the eyes found no compass disruption and explicitly rejected the most ambitious version of the retinal-RF argument while leaving radical-pair mechanisms more broadly open. The manuscript must preserve that distinction.

### The MagR lesson
A protein complex described as a biological compass needle is irresistible copy. The IscA1/MagR proposal drew serious physical and biochemical criticism. Treat filing, modeling, magnetic material detection, and functional receptor evidence as different evidentiary objects. Never write “scientists discovered the bird's compass needle.”

### The quantum-branding incentive
“Quantum biology” attracts attention, grants, popular coverage, and metaphysical freeloaders. That creates incentives in opposite directions: overstatement by enthusiasts and reflexive dismissal by people who associate the word with pseudoscience. The correct defense is quantitative. Spin chemistry is quantum mechanics whether or not anybody likes the branding. The question is whether the proposed reaction survives biological noise, orientation, lifetime, signaling, and intact-animal perturbation.

## Chronology

- 19th century: geomagnetic cues enter serious navigation speculation as animal migration becomes experimentally tractable.
- Mid-20th century: controlled orientation experiments increasingly establish magnetic compass behavior in birds.
- 1970s–1990s: light dependence, inclination rather than polarity behavior, and radical-pair ideas sharpen the avian compass problem.
- 2000s: retinal/brain localization, RF perturbation, cryptochrome candidates, and magnetite/trigeminal hypotheses compete.
- 2009: Zapka et al. show Cluster N requirement while trigeminal section leaves tested magnetic compass orientation intact.
- 2011: double-blind work fails to confirm complete right-eye lateralization.
- 2018: European-robin CRY4 localization and seasonal-expression evidence strengthens candidacy.
- 2020: Cry4 interaction/isoform work deepens molecular picture; garden-warbler eye-localized RF result complicates strong RF accounts.
- 2021: Xu et al. report magnetic sensitivity of purified European-robin CRY4 and comparative sensitivity against chicken/pigeon proteins.
- 2023: Drosophila experiments add functional evidence that radical-pair magnetosensitivity can be genetically manipulated and that ErCRY4 can support magnetic sensitivity in a heterologous system.
- 2024: quantum-Zeno work broadens plausible radical-pair parameter space for tightly bound cryptochrome radicals.
