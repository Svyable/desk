# Gasbuggy radionuclide boundary audit

## Why this note exists

Chapter 1 is strongest when the well itself becomes the interface between an extraordinary state capability and ordinary infrastructure. That argument depends on getting the contamination claim exactly right. The tempting shorthand — *the gas was too radioactive to sell* — currently outruns the source trail. The narrower claim is both better supported and more interesting: the shot produced radionuclides in the cavity gas, tritium became the long-lived migration indicator, and gas directly affected by the detonation was flared during production testing rather than simply becoming pipeline product.

## Authoritative source checked

DOE Office of Legacy Management, *Gasbuggy, New Mexico, Site* long-term surveillance material, surfaced through OSTI as LMS/GSB/S05374:

https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1023213

The DOE document says post-test gas sampling detected tritium, krypton-85, iodine-131, and argon-37, citing Smith and Momyer (1968). It reports estimated generated inventories of 36,000 curies of tritium and 350 curies of krypton-85, and says cavity-gas tritium concentrations were approximately two orders of magnitude above krypton-85. Most importantly for Chapter 1, DOE states that natural gas directly affected by the detonation — including tritiated methane — was flared during post-detonation production tests.

Those statements support the chapter's present distinction between a contained underground detonation and a later product-quality/interface problem. They do **not**, by themselves, establish the eventual commercial rejection threshold, customer dose, applicable pipeline specification, treatment feasibility, or the proposition that all stimulated gas was categorically unsalable.

## What the numbers do and do not mean

The 36,000-curie tritium and 350-curie krypton-85 figures are estimated generated inventories, not pipeline concentrations. They should not be placed beside a regulatory concentration limit as though the units were interchangeable. The useful next reconstruction is time-resolved concentration in produced gas, with volume and chemical form, followed by the contemporaneous dilution/treatment/dose assumptions.

Likewise, "no surface radiation release from the detonation" and "radionuclides in produced gas" are not contradictory claims. They describe different pathways at different times: prompt containment versus later recovery of gas that contacted the shot-affected zone. That distinction is narratively valuable because it shows the interface problem emerging only when the experiment tries to become a product.

## Strongest counterargument

Radioactivity alone may not explain why nuclear gas stimulation failed commercially. A technology can produce contaminated test gas and still be technically treatable or dilutable; the decisive veto might instead have been economics, production performance, regulation, public acceptance, changing conventional stimulation, or some combination. Until contemporaneous AEC/industry commercialization records and concentration/dose calculations are recovered, the manuscript should not let contamination become a monocausal ending.

The counterargument strengthens the book's governing idea. The point is not that an exotic technology inevitably betrays itself through one spectacular signature. The point is that a useful system inherits every interface it must cross. At Gasbuggy those interfaces include geology, gas quality, radiological accounting, processing, regulation, economics, and customer acceptance. Failure can occur at any one of them or in their combination.

## Manuscript implications

Keep Chapter 1's current formulation that radionuclides were detected and directly affected gas was flared during post-detonation production testing. Do not upgrade it to "too radioactive to sell" without the missing commercial chain.

When this material is expanded into the manuscript, the strongest narrative sequence is likely:

1. the underground shot appears contained and the fracture experiment has physically worked;
2. production begins, forcing material back across the boundary;
3. sampling identifies radionuclides, with tritium dominating the long-lived cavity-gas signal;
4. directly affected gas is flared during testing;
5. only then does the chapter ask what concentration, treatment, dose, regulation, economics, and market acceptance would have been required for ordinary sale.

That sequence lets the evidence create the argument rather than announcing "interface leakage" before the reader has watched an interface become consequential.
