# Back Matter

*Keep Cooking* is a work of narrative nonfiction about a technical tendency that is easy to describe and difficult to price: once useful machine cognition becomes cheaper, people and institutions tend to buy more of it.

The phrase “token maxing” is used throughout the book as shorthand for that tendency. It includes long context windows, repeated sampling, verifier loops, reasoning models, tool use, agent swarms, persistent memory, retrieval, simulation, and any workflow in which more machine inference is spent because the expected value of another pass remains positive. The phrase is cultural as much as technical. It describes the habit of asking a system to keep working because additional cognition has become cheap enough to feel nearly free.

The manuscript does not assume that more inference always improves an answer. Research on test-time compute shows that the value of additional inference depends on task difficulty, strategy, verifier quality, and allocation. Research on workplace AI likewise shows heterogeneous effects: strong gains in some settings, weaker or negative effects in others. Those differences are central to the argument rather than inconvenient exceptions.

The book also distinguishes technical capability from economic capture. A technology can create large consumer surplus while producing weak returns for some investors. A company can automate tasks without eliminating a job. A country can adopt AI without owning frontier compute. A worker can become more productive while losing bargaining power. A falling price per token can coexist with rising total electricity demand if usage expands faster than efficiency improves.

Concrete empirical claims should be read with their date and source in mind. The AI industry changes quickly. Prices, benchmark performance, hardware efficiency, model architecture, adoption, regulation, and energy demand can move materially between editions. The research directory records the principal sources used to ground the first complete Desk draft and notes what evidence would weaken its main claims.

The scenario chapter is deliberately not a forecast. “Broad abundance,” the “cognitive landlord economy,” and the “token arms race” are equilibrium sketches intended to clarify which institutional variables change distributional outcomes. Real economies can contain all three at once.

The central claim of the book is therefore conditional rather than prophetic. If machine cognition continues becoming cheaper and useful across a widening set of tasks, then scarcity is likely to migrate toward complementary resources: trusted context, judgment, physical action, energy, distribution, accountability, attention, credibility, institutional capacity, and the right to decide. Who wins depends less on possessing intelligence in the abstract than on controlling, accessing, or improving those complements.

This edition is meant to be argued with. The research trail is kept beside the prose so later revisions can replace confident stories with better evidence when the world refuses to cooperate.
