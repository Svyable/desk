# Chapter Briefs and Falsification Notes

## Ch 1 — The Meter Starts Running

**Question:** What changes when useful cognition becomes metered, purchasable, and cheap enough to spend repeatedly?

**What would make this false?** If inference cost stops falling or users show little willingness to buy additional machine work once baseline quality is adequate, the cultural and economic premise weakens.

**Primary sources:** S01, S02.

## Ch 2 — Thinking Longer

**Question:** When does extra test-time compute improve results, and when is it merely more activity?

**What would make this false?** Broad evidence that additional sampling, search, verification, or reasoning almost never improves real tasks after controlling for model quality and workflow design.

**Primary sources:** S01.

## Ch 3 — The Price Collapse

**Question:** Can rapidly falling cost per unit of machine cognition increase total consumption rather than reduce total spending?

**What would make this false?** If demand saturates quickly enough that efficiency gains consistently reduce aggregate inference and electricity use.

**Primary sources:** S02, S09, S12.

## Ch 4 — Context Is Capital

**Question:** Does persistent context become more valuable as generic model capability becomes abundant?

**What would make this false?** If models perform nearly as well without proprietary history, context becomes highly portable by default, or users show low willingness to trade privacy for continuity.

**Primary sources:** S10; firm evidence to be expanded in later revisions.

## Ch 5 — The Agent Factory

**Question:** What happens when a single request can cheaply summon multiple specialized machine workers?

**What would make this false?** If multi-agent workflows remain too unreliable, expensive, slow, or coordination-heavy to outperform simpler single-model systems in production.

**Primary sources:** S01; production evidence to be expanded.

## Ch 6 — The Compressed Apprentice

**Question:** Can AI transfer patterns of expert performance to less-experienced workers quickly enough to compress experience curves?

**What would make this false?** If novice gains disappear across settings, fail to persist without assistance, or are offset by slower development of underlying skill.

**Primary sources:** S03.

## Ch 7 — The Centaur and the Cyborg

**Question:** Which division of labor between human and AI performs best across a jagged capability frontier?

**What would make this false?** If one stable human-AI interaction style dominates across tasks rather than performance depending on task structure and capability boundaries.

**Primary sources:** S04, S05.

## Ch 8 — The Infinite Colleague

**Question:** What changes when a worker can request unlimited drafts, explanations, critiques, and alternatives?

**What would make this false?** If review and integration costs rise so quickly that additional machine output produces little net increase in personal throughput.

**Primary sources:** S03, S05, S06.

## Ch 9 — The Verification Tax

**Question:** Does cheap generation make verification, provenance, and stewardship relatively more expensive?

**What would make this false?** If model reliability and automated verification improve fast enough that human review burdens shrink at least as quickly as output expands.

**Primary sources:** S04, S05, S06.

## Ch 10 — The New Status Game

**Question:** When competent output becomes easy to generate, do taste, trusted identity, track record, and responsibility become stronger status signals?

**What would make this false?** If audiences and employers continue treating polished output itself as a durable signal of scarce ability despite ubiquitous generation.

**Primary sources:** S06; labor-market evidence to be expanded.

## Ch 11 — The Company That Thinks Too Much

**Question:** Can cheap analysis create organizational overthinking rather than better decisions?

**What would make this false?** If firms consistently pair more machine analysis with faster decisions and better outcomes without measurable coordination or review overhead.

**Primary sources:** S04, S05.

## Ch 12 — Software Eats Headcount

**Question:** How does machine labor change the relationship between output, revenue, and human headcount?

**What would make this false?** If AI adoption mainly increases complementarity and hiring, leaving revenue-per-employee and task composition broadly unchanged.

**Primary sources:** S03, S07, S08.

## Ch 13 — Small Firms, Big Context

**Question:** Can small firms rent enough administrative and analytical competence to compete at scales previously reserved for larger organizations?

**What would make this false?** If integration, trust, compliance, and data costs remain too fixed and expensive for SMEs to capture meaningful gains.

**Primary sources:** S10; SME adoption evidence to be expanded.

## Ch 14 — The Moat Moves Downstack

**Question:** As model capability commoditizes, does economic power migrate toward chips, cloud, distribution, identity, proprietary context, and workflow control?

**What would make this false?** If durable margins remain primarily at the model layer or interoperability prevents lower-stack bottlenecks from accumulating rents.

**Primary sources:** S02, S10; market-structure evidence to be expanded.

## Ch 15 — The Capital Furnace

**Question:** Can socially useful AI infrastructure still destroy investor capital through overbuilding, rapid depreciation, and competition?

**What would make this false?** If utilization, pricing, and end-demand growth remain high enough for new capacity to earn attractive returns even through aggressive buildout.

**Primary sources:** S02, S09.

## Ch 16 — The Grid Behind the Mind

**Question:** Which physical constraints become binding when machine cognition scales: electricity, transmission, generation, cooling, transformers, land, or construction?

**What would make this false?** If efficiency, geographic flexibility, and grid expansion keep data-centre demand easy to absorb without material local bottlenecks or cost shifts.

**Primary sources:** S09, S12, S13.

## Ch 17 — Nations at the Context Window

**Question:** Does cheap model access narrow global capability gaps, or do compute, connectivity, context, and skills keep benefits concentrated?

**What would make this false?** Rapid convergence in business and government AI adoption across income groups despite infrastructure and skill differences.

**Primary sources:** S08, S10, S14.

## Ch 18 — Labor Without Ladders

**Question:** If routine junior cognitive work automates, how do workers acquire the experience required for senior judgment?

**What would make this false?** Stable or rising entry-level hiring, preserved learning outcomes, and new apprenticeship structures that replace routine work without reducing skill formation.

**Primary sources:** S03, S07, S08.

## Ch 19 — Cheap Expertise, Expensive Judgment

**Question:** Does abundant first-pass expertise increase the relative value of accountability and judgment in medicine, law, engineering, finance, and other high-stakes domains?

**What would make this false?** If end-to-end autonomous systems become reliable enough that human responsibility contributes little incremental value in high-stakes work.

**Primary sources:** S04, S06, S07.

## Ch 20 — The Abundance Trap

**Question:** When generation becomes abundant, do attention, authentication, provenance, and filtering become more expensive?

**What would make this false?** If synthetic volume rises without increasing fraud, discovery costs, verification burdens, or the value of trusted channels.

**Primary sources:** S06; fraud and attention evidence to be expanded.

## Ch 21 — Who Wins, Who Loses

**Question:** Which people, companies, asset owners, professions, and countries capture gains if the price of cognition falls?

**What would make this false?** A broad distribution of gains that is largely insensitive to ownership of energy, compute, distribution, context, skills, or decision rights.

**Primary sources:** S03, S07, S08, S09, S10.

## Ch 22 — The Household Balance Sheet

**Question:** How much value appears as saved time, avoided professional fees, better household decisions, and improved access rather than higher wages?

**What would make this false?** If household adoption remains low, saved time is mostly consumed by verification, or gains accrue mainly to already-advantaged users.

**Primary sources:** S10; household causal evidence to be expanded.

## Ch 23 — Markets Full of Minds

**Question:** What happens to financial and commercial markets when research, pricing, negotiation, screening, and strategic response can be automated at scale?

**What would make this false?** If machine participation changes workflow speed without materially changing information advantages, market structure, strategic behavior, or the value of proprietary data.

**Primary sources:** S02; market microstructure evidence to be expanded.

## Ch 24 — Three Futures for Cheap Thought

**Question:** Which equilibrium dominates: broad abundance, cognitive landlordism, or a token arms race?

**What would make this false?** The scenarios are analytical rather than predictive. They fail if the proposed hinges — portability, competition, infrastructure concentration, strategic complementarity, and institutional adaptation — do not explain meaningful differences in outcomes.

**Primary sources:** S07, S09, S10, S12, S13.

## Ch 25 — When to Stop Cooking

**Question:** What practical stopping rules let individuals, firms, investors, and governments capture the gains from additional cognition without confusing activity for value?

**What would make this false?** If maximum available inference is nearly always economically optimal, human escalation adds little safety, portability has little effect on competition, apprenticeship remains intact automatically, and physical costs are negligible.

**Primary sources:** S01, S04, S05, S06, S09, S10.

## Revision priority

The strongest empirical chapters in the first complete draft are those grounded in randomized or field evidence on workplace productivity and in institutional datasets on energy, labor exposure, and global infrastructure. The most speculative chapters are those on long-run firm structure, household surplus, status, financial markets, and equilibrium outcomes. Those chapters should receive disproportionate attention in the next research pass rather than being treated as established because the narrative is plausible.
