# The Treaty Line

By 1963, the atmosphere and near-Earth space were becoming politically unavailable as nuclear test media.

The Limited Test Ban Treaty prohibited nuclear explosions in the atmosphere, outer space and underwater. Underground testing remained permitted under conditions intended to prevent radioactive debris from crossing national borders. The treaty was not written as an environmental ethics document for the magnetosphere. It was driven by fallout, arms competition, verification and politics. Yet one consequence was to close the experimental pathway that had produced ARGUS and Starfish Prime.

This matters scientifically. Some experiments become impossible not because the instruments vanish but because the intervention itself becomes unacceptable. No modern researcher can reproduce Starfish at full scale merely to improve the data set. The historical measurements therefore acquire a peculiar value: they are records of an experiment that should not be repeated.

That creates epistemic asymmetry. Models of high-altitude nuclear effects are tested against sparse, old, heterogeneous events conducted with 1950s and 1960s instrumentation. Modern simulations can be sophisticated while still leaning on a small empirical foundation.

It also creates room for exaggeration. Where the data are old and classified details remain unavailable, certainty can be borrowed from the apparent authority of secret programs. The correct response is not to assume the classified answer is dramatic. It is to map what the public record can support and mark the missing variables.

The treaty line therefore separates two eras. Before it, nuclear powers could treat upper atmosphere and space as experimental volumes for live detonations. After it, high-altitude nuclear effects became increasingly a matter of archived data, underground component tests, simulation, radiation sources, theoretical work and survivability engineering.

The sky stopped being a nuclear test range by law before anyone had finished understanding what the tests had done to it.
