# The Satellites That Died

A list of satellites allegedly killed by Starfish Prime circulates widely. Lists are dangerous here because the causal confidence is not uniform.

Several spacecraft experienced severe degradation or failure in the months after the test, including Ariel 1, TRAAC, Transit 4B and Telstar 1. Contemporary and later technical literature ties substantial solar-cell and electronics damage to the enhanced radiation environment. But a responsible account has to distinguish three things: a spacecraft exposed to the artificial belt, a spacecraft measurably degraded by that exposure, and a spacecraft whose final loss can be assigned primarily to Starfish.

Those are not interchangeable claims.

Solar-cell damage is the cleanest bridge because radiation dose and photovoltaic degradation can be compared quantitatively. Hess used reconstructed fluxes to calculate exposures and found reasonable agreement with observed damage. Other electronics failed through more device-specific pathways.

The historical denominator also matters. Early satellites failed often. Components were immature. Redundancy was limited. Natural radiation was already hazardous. A post-Starfish failure therefore cannot be attributed merely because the calendar lines up.

Yet skepticism can overshoot too. The artificial belt was intense enough and persistent enough to create a new environmental stressor. Modern papers still use Starfish data because the event remains one of the few empirical examples of a massive artificial injection into the inner magnetosphere.

The correct conclusion is narrower than the folklore and stronger than dismissal: Starfish Prime materially damaged multiple satellites and helped reveal how vulnerable early spacecraft were to energetic electrons. The exact causal fraction differs by spacecraft.

That is also the form the evidence should take in any modern survivability discussion. “A HANE destroys satellites” is not a model. Orbit, spectrum, shielding, duration, component technology and belt evolution are the model.
