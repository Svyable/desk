# Source log — The Second Belt

## Primary / official / near-primary

1. U.S. Naval History and Heritage Command, “Norton Sound.” Official ship history records the ship’s 1958 participation in Project ARGUS, the three rocket launches south of the Falkland Islands, approximate 300-mile detonation altitude, and Explorer IV/rocket monitoring. https://www.history.navy.mil/research/histories/ship-histories/danfs/n/norton-sound.html
2. NASA NTRS, D. S. Beall, *The Artificial Electron Belt, October 1963 to October 1966*, NASA-CR-90542 (1967). Long-duration measurements of Starfish artificial electrons. https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19680002358
3. Treaty Banning Nuclear Weapon Tests in the Atmosphere, in Outer Space and Under Water (1963). Use U.S. Department of State / UN treaty text for legal claims. https://history.state.gov/

## Contemporary technical literature

4. W. N. Hess, “The artificial radiation belt made on July 9, 1962,” *Journal of Geophysical Research* 68 (1963), 667–683. Combines Injun, Telstar, TRAAC and Ariel observations; reports broad consistency; estimates spacecraft flux/dose and compares with solar-cell damage. https://doi.org/10.1029/JZ068i003p00667

## Later technical reconstruction

5. G. S. Cunningham et al., “Neoclassical Diffusion of Radiation-Belt Electrons Across Very Low L-Shells,” *JGR Space Physics* (2018). Uses historical Starfish measurements to explain long persistence at low L. https://doi.org/10.1002/2017JA024931
6. M. A. Belyaev, D. J. Larson and B. I. Cohen, “Particle-In-Cell Simulations of Starfish Prime,” *JGR Space Physics* (2025). Modern simulation of the transient Starfish plasma/electromagnetic response. https://doi.org/10.1029/2024JA033681
7. L. Olifer and I. R. Mann, “Self-Limiting of Artificial Electron Radiation Belts: Evidence From the Starfish Prime High-Altitude Nuclear Explosion,” *JGR Space Physics* 131 (2026). Digitizes Injun/STARAD historical data and argues that below about 2 MeV the post-Starfish population may have approached a Kennel-Petschek-like flux cap. https://doi.org/10.1029/2026JA035724
8. L. J. Lanzerotti, “Space weather research: Earth’s radiation belts,” *Space Weather* (2017). Historical discussion of Telstar radiation measurements and the role of Starfish exposure. https://doi.org/10.1002/2017SW001654
