# Unencrypted

*Operation Ivy Bells and the Dangerous Belief That Distance Is Security*

Sven Hardy Benson

## Reader's note

This is a book about a security assumption that looked like geography.

The historical core is Operation Ivy Bells: the publicly documented Cold War effort in which American submarines collected Soviet naval communications from undersea cables. The important fact is not that somebody once did something clever under the Sea of Okhotsk. Cleverness ages badly. The important fact is that an institution can mistake the difficulty of reaching a channel for the impossibility of reading it.

That distinction is still alive.

This manuscript keeps four evidence classes separate. **Documented** means supported by declassified records, official histories, court records, or other primary material. **Credible but incomplete** means reporting or participant testimony that is plausible and attributable but not independently complete. **Disputed** means a claim that remains materially contested or rests on weak provenance. **Conjecture** means mine: synthesis, analogy, or a question asked beyond what the record proves.

No testimony becomes proof because it is vivid. No code name becomes evidence merely because it sounds classified. No modern capability is inferred from a Cold War precedent without a separate evidentiary bridge. This is history and epistemology, not a manual for communications interception.
