# Military Inference

## How Cheap Machine Reasoning Changes the Character of War

**Author:** Sven Hardy Benson  
**Status:** Drafting  
**Planned chapters:** 24

*Military Inference* is a book about warfare when machine reasoning becomes a consumable military resource. It is not a catalog of autonomous weapons and it does not provide operational instructions for violence. Its subject is the larger system upstream of force: sensing, interpretation, uncertainty, deception, coordination, logistics, command, verification, authorization, and the institutions responsible for deciding when machines should and should not be trusted.

The central thesis is that industrial warfare rewarded mass production, network warfare rewarded sensing and communication, and inference warfare increasingly rewards the ability to turn incomplete and adversarial information into justified decisions without allowing speed, automation, or apparent confidence to outrun evidence and legitimate human authority.

## Working doctrine

The book keeps a deliberate distinction between analysis and action. It examines military decision systems, organizational consequences, failure modes, strategic incentives, governance, and defensive resilience at a conceptual level. It avoids tactical targeting guidance, weapon-construction instructions, or procedures that would meaningfully facilitate violence.
