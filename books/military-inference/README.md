# Military Inference

## How Cheap Machine Reasoning Changes the Character of War

| | |
|---|---|
| **Authors** | Sven Hardy Benson |
| **Status** | Drafting |
| **Chapters** | 0 of 25 drafted |

*Military Inference* is a book about what happens when militaries can spend machine reasoning the way industrial armies once spent shells, fuel, steel, radio bandwidth, and staff hours.

Its central thesis is that the next major change in warfare is not simply autonomous weapons. It is the construction of forces that can continuously form, test, revise, and act on models of an adversarial world faster than opponents can corrupt them—while retaining the institutional capacity to recognize confident error and slow down before uncertainty becomes catastrophe.

The book treats future war as a contest over **epistemic systems**: sensors, models, analysts, agents, communications, provenance, simulations, uncertainty estimates, authorization boundaries, staffs, doctrine, and the feedback mechanisms by which a force discovers that its picture of reality is wrong. The relevant sequence is not a weapon catalog but a loop:

**observation → inference → decision → coordination → action → feedback → revised inference**

The adversary participates in that loop. Deception, camouflage, manipulated telemetry, false correlations, corrupted provenance, information overload, missing data, and ambiguity all become forms of **counter-inference**. The result is a central paradox: the faster a military can infer and act, the more dangerous a confidently wrong inference can become.

This is strategic and organizational nonfiction. It discusses command and control, intelligence, deception, logistics, software, human-machine teaming, strategic stability, testing, governance, and historical cases at a conceptual level. It does not provide tactical targeting procedures, weapon-construction instructions, autonomous-weapon deployment recipes, or actionable methods for attacking real military systems.
