# Evidence ledger — The Ghost in the Radar

Updated: 2026-09-16

## Selected collision

**Electronic warfare + ELINT + stealth engineering + anomalous sensor epistemology.** PALLADIUM is valuable not because it is a UFO explanation but because it provides a historically grounded example of a deliberately manufactured detection being used as an intelligence instrument.

## Class 1 — verified / declassified / documented

- CIA publicly documents OXCART/A-12 as a secret high-altitude, Mach-3 reconnaissance program and describes the radar/air-defense problem that shaped it.
  - CIA A-12 OXCART history/search entry: https://www.cia.gov/stories/story/a-12-oxcart/
- CIA documents GRAB as an operational 1960–1962 ELINT satellite that collected Soviet air-defense radar information. This is useful adjacent evidence for the period's intense U.S. effort to characterize Soviet radar, not evidence of PALLADIUM itself.
  - https://www.cia.gov/stories/story/grab-first-signals-intelligence-satellite/
- CIA Reading Room preserves large bodies of period radar, Cuba, SAM, and UFO material. Individual documents establish their own contents only.
  - Example, 1966 Air Force request concerning a CIA UFO report: https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/0000015430
  - Example, 1966 CIA imagery report explicitly distinguishing dummy and active SAM sites in Cuba: https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp79-00849a000100030005-5

## Class 2 — credible but incomplete reporting / testimony

- Gene Poteat, a CIA technical officer associated with ELINT and OXCART-era work, publicly described PALLADIUM as a radar-deception system that generated controlled false targets and used adversary reaction to characterize radar capability. This is the current narrative spine but must be decomposed into item-level claims and checked against contemporaneous/declassified records.
- Poteat's retrospective account describes CIA, NSA, and military roles and a Cuba operation involving electronic false targets and calibrated physical radar reflectors. Treat operational particulars as retrospective testimony until primary documentation is recovered.

## Class 3 — disputed / not independently confirmed

- Exact claims that a Cuban pilot visually saw the nonexistent electronic target.
- Exact dates, platforms, target profiles, and response sequence for famous PALLADIUM Cuba anecdotes unless recovered in primary operational records.
- Claims that PALLADIUM, or a direct descendant, caused any named modern UAP incident.
- Claims that radar deception establishes holographic or physical apparition technology.
- Claims that PALLADIUM was designed as a UFO cover operation.

## Class 4 — synthesis / open conjecture

- **Dependency thesis:** a single engineered sensor input can generate multiple downstream records and reactions that look like independent corroboration unless their causal genealogy is reconstructed.
- **Adversarial epistemology thesis:** sensor reliability must be evaluated differently when another intelligent actor has both motive and capability to manipulate the measurement environment.
- **Secrecy thesis:** classification can hide the causal ancestor of a public event while leaving downstream observations intact, producing a genuine but misleading evidentiary ecology.
- **UAP comparator:** modern cases should be scored for raw-data availability, sensor independence, geometry, processing provenance, and plausible adversarial access before sensor multiplicity is treated as physical corroboration.

## Contradictions / pressure points

1. PALLADIUM's best-known public operational account is retrospective. The book must not describe every anecdotal detail as though a contemporaneous operations file has been located.
2. Radar deception is a broad, documented engineering class; PALLADIUM's exact implementation and limits are narrower and require primary reconstruction.
3. A false radar target does not imply a false visual observation. If a pilot reported a visual target, possibilities include expectation effects, unrelated physical objects, inaccurate retelling, or a more complex operation. Do not silently choose among them.
4. Multi-sensor evidence can be genuinely independent. The dependency thesis must not become a universal solvent.
5. Modern digital RF memory and networked EW can be discussed from authoritative technical sources, but capability descriptions do not establish operational use in a particular UAP event.

## Incentive audit

- **CIA/participants:** institutional pride and technical-war-story incentives may favor elegant retrospective narratives.
- **UFO advocates:** incentive to use a verified black program to legitimate broader hidden-capability claims.
- **Skeptics:** incentive to use a verified deception capability as an explanation without case-specific evidence.
- **Military/intelligence institutions:** incentives to conceal EW capabilities, collection methods, vulnerabilities, and adversary knowledge can all distort the public record in different directions.

## Primary-source acquisition queue

1. Recover Gene Poteat's original PALLADIUM text in its authoritative publication context and page-index every operational claim.
2. Search CIA Reading Room for PALLADIUM spelling/codeword variants and associated OXCART/ELINT histories.
3. Recover declassified OXCART radar-cross-section and Soviet-radar evaluation material sufficient to establish the engineering question independently of retrospective narrative.
4. Identify NSA histories or declassified records supporting/limiting the claimed communications-monitoring feedback loop.
5. Reconstruct Cuba chronology against destroyer/submarine availability and contemporaneous air-defense records where public.
6. Build a technical primer from radar/EW textbooks and peer-reviewed literature: range deception, velocity deception, repeater jamming, false-target generation, DRFM, angle deception, track-file formation.
7. Build a modern case matrix only after the historical mechanism is stable. Never infer PALLADIUM lineage from phenomenological resemblance alone.

## Defeat condition

The book's strongest version fails if PALLADIUM's public history collapses under primary-source audit or if the claimed false-target mechanism did not produce operationally credible adversary tracks/reactions. The modern comparator also weakens wherever independent calibrated sensors establish object geometry in ways an injected common-mode signal cannot plausibly explain.

The opposite result matters too: even perfect proof of PALLADIUM does **not** prove deception in a single modern UAP case. Attribution remains an additional evidentiary burden.
