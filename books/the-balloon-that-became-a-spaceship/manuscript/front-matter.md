# The Balloon That Became a Spaceship

## Project MOGUL, Roswell, and How a Real Secret Grows a Better Secret

Sven Hardy Benson

### A note on what this book is allowed to know

There is an easy way to write about Roswell and it has ruined the subject twice.

One version begins with aliens and makes every missing page a hangar door. The other begins with embarrassment and makes every witness an idiot. Both methods save time. Neither is investigation.

This book uses four shelves. A document whose provenance can be checked goes on the first. Attributed testimony with missing corroboration goes on the second. Claims that remain disputed go on the third. My own synthesis goes on the fourth. Things can move shelves. They do not move because I like them.

That matters here because Roswell contains a fact conspiracy culture sometimes gets right and then spends too extravagantly: governments keep secrets. Project MOGUL was real. Its purpose mattered. The public did not receive a useful explanation of it in July 1947. None of that proves a spaceship.

The reverse error is just as cheap. Calling something a balloon does not end the inquiry. Which balloon? Launched when? Built how? Carrying what? Where did it travel? Which debris is contemporary and which description arrived decades later? What records survive? What records were destroyed under routine schedules? Which witnesses spoke before they knew what Roswell was supposed to mean?

The book will keep asking those irritating little questions until one story earns the right to be larger than another.

A patent proves that somebody filed a patent. A classification marking proves that somebody classified a record. A witness proves that a person said something. A missing file proves, at most, that the file is missing. An `unidentified` case proves that identification failed. These are small rules. Most extraordinary histories become less extraordinary when small rules are applied without mercy.

Roswell may survive that. MOGUL may not. Good. A thesis that cannot lose is décor.
