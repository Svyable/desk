# Roswell records-disposition audit

Checked 2026-09-16. This is a bounded research note for Chapters 13–16, not manuscript prose.

## The correction

The draft is right to treat missing records as an evidentiary problem, but later prose should resist collapsing `records were destroyed` into `Roswell records were destroyed` and then into `Roswell evidence was destroyed`.

GAO's 28 July 1995 report is the useful control because its assignment was not to decide what crashed. It searched for government records and asked how records concerning an event like Roswell should have been handled. GAO reported that it could not locate some Roswell Army Air Field administrative records for the relevant period and that the Air Force records-management center could not explain the disposition. That is a real archival gap. It does not identify the contents of the missing files, establish that they contained crash-recovery evidence, or by itself establish deliberate destruction to conceal Roswell.

Primary source: U.S. General Accounting Office, *Government Records: Results of a Search for Records Concerning the 1947 Crash Near Roswell, New Mexico*, NSIAD-95-187, 28 July 1995: https://www.gao.gov/products/nsiad-95-187

Full report: https://www.gao.gov/assets/nsiad-95-187.pdf

## What survived matters too

The same GAO search found contemporary material rather than a uniformly empty archive. Its report reproduces the July 1947 unit history material and the FBI teletype of 8 July. This matters methodologically: an incomplete record series is not the same thing as a documentary blackout.

That distinction should govern Chapter 14. For each missing series, the book should ask:

- what office created it;
- what dates and record type are actually missing;
- whether the series was scheduled as temporary or permanent;
- when and under what authority disposition occurred, if known;
- what parallel records survived in other offices;
- and what proposition the absent series could realistically have tested.

Do not infer the missing file's contents from the allegation it would be convenient for the file to settle.

## Archival topology is a stronger scene than `the files vanished`

NARA's current Roswell/UAP guidance makes the topology visible. Project BLUE BOOK is not a 1947 Roswell case file: NARA says it has been unable to locate BLUE BOOK documentation discussing the 1947 incident. NARA separately identifies the later *Roswell Report Source Files, 1987–1996* and other Air Force record series. A search can therefore be both competent and incomplete if it is aimed at the wrong record-producing institution or series.

NARA, Project BLUE BOOK / Roswell research guide: https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos

NARA, current textual and microfilm UAP holdings guide: https://www.archives.gov/research/topics/uaps/textual-and-microfilm

The eventual chapter can make this concrete without turning into an archive tutorial: records follow offices and functions, not later folklore labels. A classified balloon program, a base public-information office, an FBI field office, a records center, and a later UFO investigation can all leave different paper trails about adjacent pieces of the same episode.

## Strongest counterargument

The skeptical misuse of this distinction would be to say that ordinary records disposition explains every absence. It does not. GAO's inability to determine what happened to relevant Roswell Army Air Field administrative records is itself a legitimate unresolved fact, and an anomalous or unauthorized disposition finding would materially strengthen a concealment argument.

The book should therefore keep two possibilities alive until the record-series history is reconstructed: mundane loss/disposition and purposeful removal. The evidence needed to distinguish them is not the emotional force of the Roswell story but the schedule, accession, transfer, destruction authorization, neighboring series, and surviving copies.

## Defeat test for the chapter

The `missing records` argument should change materially if primary records show that a relevant series was destroyed outside the governing schedule, after a Roswell-specific retrieval or classification order, or in a pattern that selectively removed records bearing on the event while comparable records survived. Conversely, routine scheduled disposition with surviving parallel records would narrow what the absence can support.

## Editorial consequence

This gives Chapters 13–16 a cleaner progression: Congress asks for records; GAO searches by record-producing institution; some contemporary records survive; some expected administrative records do not; provenance of the absence becomes a question in its own right. The mystery is then neither inflated nor dismissed. It is measured.
