## Four evidence classes

### 1 — Verified / declassified / documented

- K-129, a Soviet Golf II-class ballistic-missile submarine, sank in March 1968 with all hands.
- Soviet search efforts failed to recover it; U.S. intelligence located the wreck in deep North Pacific water.
- CIA, with Defense support, led a covert recovery project later publicly known as AZORIAN.
- Hughes and his corporate ecosystem supplied a cover story involving deep-sea manganese-nodule mining.
- Hughes Glomar Explorer was purpose-built with a derrick, long pipe string, capture vehicle, moon pool and associated heavy-lift systems.
- The 1974 recovery obtained part, not all, of the targeted wreck section; a substantial portion fell back to the seabed.
- Six Soviet submariners' remains were recovered and buried at sea; a film was later presented to Boris Yeltsin.
- Public exposure in 1975 helped prevent the planned follow-on recovery.
- The episode generated the enduring "Glomar response" convention.

### 2 — Credible but incomplete

- Exact intelligence value of specific recovered components.
- Fine-grained mechanics of capture-system failure and the causal allocation among claw design, wreck integrity, load path and operational conditions.
- Exact Soviet suspicion/knowledge timeline during the 1974 operation.
- Participant recollections decades later, useful but not substitutes for contemporaneous records.

### 3 — Disputed / not independently confirmed in this run

- Specific claims that particular codebooks, cryptographic systems, nuclear warheads or other named strategic artifacts were successfully recovered.
- Strong claims that Soviet intelligence knew the complete mission before or during the lift.
- Popular claims that compress all recovery failure into a single broken-claw explanation without technical documentation.
- Any claim that AZORIAN establishes a precedent for recovery of non-human technology.

### 4 — Speculative synthesis / open conjecture

- Industrial-footprint analysis can be used as a discriminating test for modern alleged retrieval programs.
- The most probative evidence for a long-lived physical retrieval program may be procurement/logistics/failure/facility traces rather than testimony about ultimate object origin.
- Cover stories are strongest when composed of independently verifiable truths surrounding one hidden objective.

## Contradictions / wording traps to preserve

- CIA public pages give slightly varying distances from Hawaii and describe the wreck/recovered section with differing dimensions. Do not harmonize until underlying documents are page-indexed.
- CIA summaries differ on whether the submarine section broke apart roughly one-third or halfway through ascent. Preserve discrepancy pending technical record.
- Missile designation varies across public CIA materials (SS-N-4 versus SS-N-5). Resolve against authoritative Soviet-system and contemporaneous intelligence records before publication.
- Institutional CIA retrospectives have an incentive to present AZORIAN as an engineering triumph and intelligence coup. Use them for documented institutional claims and artifacts, but independently audit success/value claims.
- Journalists in 1975 had incentives toward sensational disclosure; officials had incentives toward minimization/nonconfirmation; contractors had incentives toward technical pride and contractual discretion; Soviet/Russian retrospectives have their own national-security and reputational incentives.

## Chronology skeleton

- **March 1968:** K-129 lost in North Pacific.
- **1968 onward:** U.S. localization and feasibility work.
- **1970:** CIA public history says technical study converges on large mechanical capture/lift concept.
- **1972:** Sun 800 heavy crane built for Glomar construction work, per CIA's 2024 account.
- **1974:** Glomar sails; arrives over recovery site July 4; partial recovery succeeds, substantial section lost during ascent.
- **1974:** Hughes-office burglary creates documentary exposure pathway.
- **February 7, 1975:** *Los Angeles Times* publishes report connecting Hughes/CIA/Glomar.
- **March 18, 1975:** Jack Anderson broadcast widely exposes operation.
- **1975:** follow-on recovery abandoned as cover collapses and Soviet attention hardens.
- **1992:** Gates provides Yeltsin burial-at-sea film.

## Retrieval-claim comparison protocol

A modern alleged retrieval program should not be credited merely because AZORIAN proves secrecy is possible. Test alleged programs against predicted traces:

1. detection/localization chain;
2. recovery geometry and mass;
3. transport and hazard controls;
4. specialized facilities;
5. contractor competencies and compartment boundaries;
6. procurement and budget traces;
7. test and exploitation instrumentation;
8. failure/accident/redesign history;
9. cover mechanism capable of absorbing visible activity;
10. legal authority, custody and ownership questions;
11. documentary leakage modes;
12. independently verifiable outputs.

Absence of a trace is not automatically disproof; the claim must first predict that the trace should be observable. Conversely, secrecy cannot be invoked as a universal explanation for every missing predicted consequence.
