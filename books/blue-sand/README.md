## Thesis

France's Saharan nuclear program is most revealing not as a story in which officials secretly knew every future health consequence, nor as a story in which an empty desert made testing harmless, but as a split-archive problem created by colonial geography and classification: French institutions measured fallout, containment failures, personnel dose and site conditions in extraordinary technical detail while Algerian workers and communities often inherited the land, the residual hazards and the burden of reconstructing exposure without equal access to the records. The strongest history comes from putting French military and parliamentary documents, IAEA measurements, compensation records and Algerian Arabic testimony on the same evidentiary map, separating what is documented from what remains plausible, disputed or unknown.
