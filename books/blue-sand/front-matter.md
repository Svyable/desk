# Blue Sand

*France's Nuclear Desert and the Records Algeria Still Wants*

Sven Hardy Benson

This book investigates France's nuclear tests at Reggane and In Ekker from 1960 to 1966 using lawfully public records. It does not claim access to classified material. It does not presume that every illness reported near the former sites was caused by radiation, that every document gap proves concealment, or that official dose estimates are complete merely because they are official.

The evidentiary rule is simple: a nuclear test, a containment failure, a measurement or an archival record can be established from documents; an individual's later disease requires a different chain of evidence. The manuscript keeps those levels separate.

French-language official records and Algerian Arabic scholarship are treated as parallel evidence streams rather than one being a commentary on the other. Where Algerian sources use legal or political conclusions such as "crime" or "barbarity," the book preserves attribution and independently tests the underlying factual proposition. Where French sources use terms such as "negligible," "contained" or "within standards," the book asks what quantity and comparison the term refers to.

The subject is not radioactive mystery. It is distributed knowledge: who measured what, who was told, who kept the files, what survived decolonization, and what can still be known now.
