# Chronology

- **1945** — France creates the Commissariat à l'énergie atomique (CEA).
- **1957** — French military planning advances toward a full-scale nuclear test range near Reggane in the Sahara.
- **November 1959** — UN political debate records African and Asian opposition to planned French tests in the Sahara.
- **February 13, 1960** — **Gerboise Bleue**, first French nuclear test, atmospheric at Reggane.
- **April 1, 1960** — **Gerboise Blanche**, atmospheric test at Reggane.
- **December 27, 1960** — **Gerboise Rouge**, atmospheric test at Reggane.
- **April 25, 1961** — **Gerboise Verte**, final atmospheric test at Reggane.
- **November 7, 1961** — **Agate**, first underground French nuclear test at In Ekker.
- **March 1962** — Evian settlement creates the framework for Algerian independence and temporary continued French use of specified military facilities.
- **May 1, 1962** — **Béryl** underground shot suffers major containment failure at In Ekker; radioactive material escapes from the tunnel.
- **July 5, 1962** — Algeria becomes independent.
- **March 30, 1963** — **Améthyste**, later identified in French official sources among shots with incomplete containment.
- **August 1963** — Partial Test Ban Treaty opens for signature, prohibiting tests in the atmosphere, outer space and underwater for parties while permitting underground tests under treaty conditions.
- **October 20, 1963** — **Rubis**, later identified among imperfectly contained Saharan underground tests.
- **May 30, 1965** — **Jade**, later identified among imperfectly contained Saharan underground tests.
- **February 1966** — final French nuclear test in the Algerian Sahara.
- **July 2, 1966** — France begins atmospheric nuclear testing in French Polynesia with Aldébaran.
- **1966–1967** — French authorities conduct post-program radiological assessments of Saharan sites later referenced in official responses.
- **1995** — France publicly releases a detailed list of its nuclear tests as debate intensifies around renewed Pacific testing.
- **1996** — France conducts its final nuclear test in the Pacific and announces the end of testing.
- **1999** — Algeria requests IAEA assessment of former French test sites at Reggane and In Ekker.
- **2005** — IAEA publishes *Radiological Conditions at the Former French Nuclear Test Sites in Algeria: Preliminary Assessment and Recommendations*.
- **2010** — France enacts the law recognizing and compensating victims of French nuclear tests, commonly called the Morin law.
- **2013** — French Senate review concludes that the compensation law has not achieved its objectives; reforms follow. A French military Gerboise Bleue fallout map is declassified in the wider veterans/litigation context and becomes publicly reported the following year.
- **2014** — French press widely reports the declassified Gerboise Bleue fallout chronology showing very broad geographic detection.
- **2020** — French parliamentary questions press the government for information on waste left in Algeria; the Ministry of Armed Forces cites dismantlement, site transfer, the IAEA mission and historical radiological records.
- **2021–2025** — Algerian scholarship, veterans' advocacy and diplomatic demands continue to focus on archives, waste maps, remediation and compensation.
- **2026** — French parliamentary debate again addresses nuclear-test victim recognition and the Sahara remains explicitly identified as an unresolved knowledge and compensation problem.
