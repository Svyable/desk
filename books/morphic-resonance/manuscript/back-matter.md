# Back Matter

*Morphic Resonance* is a work of narrative nonfiction about a disputed scientific hypothesis and the broader problem of how history becomes causally active in living, social, institutional, and computational systems.

The book's use of the phrase **resonance infrastructure** is original terminology for a descriptive synthesis: the distributed structures that lower or raise the cost of a pattern recurring. It is not proposed as a new physical field, force, or biological mechanism.

Rupert Sheldrake's hypothesis of morphic resonance is presented as his hypothesis, not as established science. His own writings are used to state the theory on its own terms. Independent scientific literature is used for adjacent mechanisms, methodological standards, and counter-explanations. Where the manuscript proposes experiments, those are proposals for discriminating tests rather than reports of completed studies.

The research trail for this edition lives with the manuscript in `research/README.md`, `research/chapter-briefs.md`, and `research/source-ledger.csv`. The source ledger records the claim for which each source is being used and, where necessary, a limitation designed to prevent the source from being stretched beyond what it establishes.

The drafting principle is simple: an unexplained recurrence is not evidence of a new mechanism merely because the ordinary mechanism is difficult to see. The reverse is also true. An unfamiliar mechanism cannot be ruled out merely because the existing catalogue of explanations is extensive. A genuine anomaly should become more persuasive as controls improve.

The manuscript therefore keeps two questions separate until the end.

How much of the world's apparent memory can be traced through known carriers of history?

And what evidence would justify saying that something remains after those carriers fail?

The first question is already large enough to change how we see bodies, cultures, cities, institutions, markets, and machine learning.

The second belongs to experiment.
