# Chapter Briefs

These briefs are the claim-and-falsification map for the manuscript. They are not a substitute for chapter-level fact checking.

## Chapter 1 — The Pattern That Came Back

**Job:** Establish the motivating question: where does a pattern go after it has happened once?

**Primary distinction:** recurrence is evidence that the present is history-dependent, not evidence by itself for a nonlocal mechanism.

**Evidence lanes:** Sheldrake's own hypothesis; ordinary examples of state, inheritance, feedback, path dependence, and trained systems.

**What would make this ordinary?** A trace in bodies, environments, artifacts, institutions, data, or shared constraints that measurably changes the next system's starting conditions.

## Chapter 2 — A Memory Without a Place

**Job:** Separate explicit stored representation from distributed preservation of consequences.

**Evidence lanes:** extended-mind debate as conceptual background; immune memory and other mechanistic examples of history-dependent response.

**Falsification risk:** do not call every stateful system “memory” without explaining that the book is using an operational analogy.

## Chapter 3 — Sheldrake's Heresy

**Job:** State the hypothesis accurately before assessing it and separate social controversy from experimental evidence.

**Evidence lanes:** Sheldrake primary sources; original editions and contemporaneous reviews before final publication.

**What would force an update?** Reliable historical evidence that the manuscript has misstated Sheldrake's prediction, chronology, or scope.

## Chapter 4 — What Would Count as Evidence?

**Job:** Establish the book's methodological contract: preregistration, predeclared outcomes, blinding, strong nulls, dose manipulation, and independent replication.

**Evidence lanes:** National Academies reproducibility/replicability report; current open-science methodology.

**Falsification risk:** avoid implying one experimental template works unchanged across biology, psychology, chemistry, and physics.

## Chapter 5 — The Similarity Problem

**Job:** Show that a causal theory based on similarity requires a similarity measure that predicts before outcomes.

**Evidence lanes:** experimental design; statistical multiple-comparison logic; machine-learning similarity as an engineered comparison.

**What would make this weak?** If “similarity” remains purely rhetorical and no chapter can identify measurable dimensions relevant to a claimed effect.

## Chapter 6 — Genes Are Not the Whole Archive

**Job:** Replace a genes-versus-field false choice with modern developmental and inheritance mechanisms.

**Evidence lanes:** gene regulation; developmental systems; epigenetic mechanisms; mammalian reprogramming; transgenerational-inheritance limits.

**What would force an update?** Stronger human transgenerational evidence that survives genetic, prenatal, social, and environmental confounding.

## Chapter 7 — The Inheritance of Environment

**Job:** Demonstrate ecological inheritance: descendants can encounter environments modified by predecessors.

**Evidence lanes:** niche construction; ecosystem engineering; ecological inheritance.

**Falsification risk:** do not treat all organism-driven environmental change as evolutionarily important niche construction without checking definitions.

## Chapter 8 — The Body Rehearses Itself

**Job:** Describe embodied learning as altered readiness rather than inaccessible magic.

**Evidence lanes:** neural plasticity; memory consolidation/reconsolidation; skill learning; immune and physiological adaptation where specifically relevant.

**What would make this ordinary?** Prior exposure that changes later performance through measurable local state even when conscious source memory is absent.

## Chapter 9 — Culture Is a Ratchet

**Job:** Show how socially transmitted knowledge can accumulate across generations and lower the cost of later competence.

**Evidence lanes:** cumulative cultural evolution; Tomasello; Caldwell and Millen; Mesoudi and Thornton.

**Falsification risk:** “ratchet” cannot imply inevitable improvement, irreversibility, or human uniqueness without source-specific qualification.

## Chapter 10 — The Intelligence in the Trail

**Job:** Introduce stigmergy as a mechanism by which earlier action leaves environmental traces that coordinate later action.

**Evidence lanes:** Grassé's original termite work; later social-insect/swarm literature.

**What would make this ordinary?** Removing or changing the trace changes the later coordination pattern.

## Chapter 11 — The Room Remembers

**Job:** Show how built environments, tools, defaults, and interfaces encode prior decisions as present friction.

**Evidence lanes:** choice architecture/default research; human factors; design and affordance literature where concrete claims require it.

**Falsification risk:** avoid treating architecture as deterministic; people can resist and repurpose environments.

## Chapter 12 — The City Remembers

**Job:** Make path dependence visible through roads, parcels, utilities, land use, reputation, and accumulated complementary investments.

**Evidence lanes:** urban historical examples to be chosen during expansion; Pierson/Arthur for path-dependence mechanisms.

**What would make this ordinary?** Altering a specific inherited urban constraint changes later behavior or investment in the predicted direction.

## Chapter 13 — Institutions Have Ghosts

**Job:** Distinguish archives from operational memory in procedures, roles, budgets, code, and professional constituencies.

**Evidence lanes:** historical institutionalism; increasing returns; organizational routines and policy feedback.

**Falsification risk:** do not explain every institutional persistence by origins; persistence may acquire new causes and beneficiaries.

## Chapter 14 — Markets as Memory

**Job:** Show how prices, inventories, capital stock, contracts, leverage, and narratives carry prior decisions into present conditions.

**Evidence lanes:** increasing returns; network effects; capital-cycle and market-microstructure literature during expansion.

**Falsification risk:** similar price charts are not evidence of the same mechanism. Specify the feedback channel before using historical analogy.

## Chapter 15 — Algorithms of Return

**Job:** Demonstrate an engineered system in which past similar cases can change treatment of present cases through data and ranking.

**Evidence lanes:** recommender popularity bias; longitudinal feedback-loop studies; selection effects.

**What would force an update?** Field evidence showing particular deployed systems increase rather than reduce diversity should narrow any broad language about homogenization.

## Chapter 16 — The Model Trained on Yesterday

**Job:** Separate generative novelty from causal independence and statistical learning from exact memorization.

**Evidence lanes:** Carlini et al.; Nasr et al.; Lee et al.; model/data documentation for any named current systems.

**Falsification risk:** never describe an LLM as merely a lookup table or imply a clean source genealogy for ordinary generation.

## Chapter 17 — Resonance Infrastructure

**Job:** Name the book's central descriptive concept: accumulated structure that changes the cost of recurrence.

**Evidence lanes:** cross-domain synthesis only after each domain has an independent mechanism.

**What would make the concept false or useless?** If it merely renames “history matters” and cannot identify a carrier, state variable, feedback, switching cost, or manipulation.

## Chapter 18 — The Activation Energy of a Pattern

**Job:** Explain why a superior new pattern can lose because transition costs, compatibility, learning, uncertainty, and missing complements favor the incumbent.

**Evidence lanes:** Arthur on increasing returns and lock-in; defaults; switching-cost and innovation-adoption literature during expansion.

**Falsification risk:** activation energy is explicitly an analogy, not a quantitative cross-domain law.

## Chapter 19 — When the Known Channels Fail

**Job:** Make skepticism vulnerable by specifying what a persuasive anomaly would look like.

**Evidence lanes:** Sheldrake's stated predictions; reproducibility/replicability methodology; domain experts for any actual protocol.

**What would force an update?** A preregistered, blinded, adequately powered, multi-site effect that tracks prior repetition and predeclared similarity while surviving conventional transmission controls.

**What would reduce confidence in morphic resonance?** Repeated nulls at predicted effect sizes or systematic disappearance under stronger controls.

## Chapter 20 — How to Make Something New

**Job:** Convert the descriptive argument into a restrained practical conclusion: new patterns require managing inherited gradients, not pretending influence can be escaped.

**Evidence lanes:** cumulative culture; exploration/exploitation; recommendation diversity; innovation infrastructure.

**Falsification risk:** keep practical prescriptions conditional and mechanism-specific. Do not turn the final chapter into a universal creativity framework.
