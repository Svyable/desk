# Source notes — Magnetecture

This file records the evidentiary role of the main sources used in the complete Desk draft. It is not a bibliography of every background concept in electromagnetism. Mature claims about magnetic fields, gradients, Lorentz force, magnetic circuits, hysteresis, saturation, induction, eddy currents, permanent magnets, magnetic bearings, and MRI architecture are treated as textbook engineering background unless a specific record or contemporary claim is made.

Fast-moving subjects are anchored to dated peer-reviewed reviews or primary papers so later revisions can tell what changed.

## Magnetic soft matter

### Bao et al. — Real-time in situ magnetization reprogramming for soft robotics

Nature 645, 375–384 (2025). Published 4 August 2025. DOI: 10.1038/s41586-025-09459-0.

Use: Chapters 1 and 5. This is the primary evidence for the manuscript's statement that researchers demonstrated real-time in-situ magnetization reprogramming by redistributing magnetic units in soft robotic structures.

Boundary: the paper demonstrates a method and examples; it does not establish a general-purpose shape-shifting robot platform, clinical readiness, or arbitrary programmable matter.

https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09459-0

### Data-driven design of shape-programmable magnetic soft materials

Nature Communications (2025). DOI: 10.1038/s41467-025-58091-z.

Use: Chapter 4. Supports the claim that data-driven inverse-design approaches are being applied to connect target shapes with realizable magnetic soft-material configurations.

Boundary: supports a design framework, not a claim that all magneto-mechanical inverse problems are solved or that fabricated systems perfectly match simulated targets.

https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-58091-z

## Altermagnetism

### Song et al. — Altermagnets as a new class of functional materials

Nature Reviews Materials 10, 473–485 (2025). DOI: 10.1038/s41578-025-00779-1.

Use: Chapters 1 and 7. Main recent review for the compensated collinear order, spin-split electronic structure, experimental evidence, and proposed functional directions in altermagnets.

Boundary: a review of an emerging field. Proposed device applications should remain prospective.

https://www.nature.com/articles/s41578-025-00779-1

### Jungwirth et al. — Symmetry, microscopy and spectroscopy signatures of altermagnetism

Nature 649, 837–847 (2026). Published 21 January 2026. DOI: 10.1038/s41586-025-09883-2.

Use: Chapter 7. Supports the need to identify altermagnetism through symmetry and multiple experimental signatures rather than a single transport curve.

Boundary: should not be read as settling every material classification or scientific controversy.

https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09883-2

### Altermagnetic spintronics

Nature Physics (2026). DOI: 10.1038/s41567-026-03337-w.

Use: Chapter 7. Current review/context for spintronic implications and the field's relationship to earlier antiferromagnetic spintronics.

Boundary: potential high-frequency, low-stray-field, and transport advantages remain material- and device-dependent.

https://www.nature.com/articles/s41567-026-03337-w

## Skyrmions and spin textures

### Hassan et al. — Dipolar skyrmions and antiskyrmions of arbitrary topological charge at room temperature

Nature Physics 20, 615–622 (2024). DOI: 10.1038/s41567-023-02358-z.

Use: Chapter 8. Primary evidence for room-temperature observation of dipolar skyrmions and antiskyrmions with multiple topological charges in Co/Ni multilayers.

Boundary: room-temperature observation is not commercial memory readiness. The manuscript explicitly preserves nucleation, motion, readout, defects, and integration as open engineering burdens.

https://www.nature.com/articles/s41567-023-02358-z

### Bernstein — Spin-torque driven skyrmion resonance

Nature Reviews Physics 7, 469 (2025). DOI: 10.1038/s42254-025-00868-8.

Use: Chapter 8. Current context for current-driven skyrmion dynamics and measuring spin-torque effects in textured magnets.

Boundary: should not be used to claim universal low-current operation across all skyrmion materials or devices.

https://www.nature.com/articles/s42254-025-00868-8

## Spin-based computing and magnonics

### Kurebayashi et al. — Metrics for spin-based computing

Nature Reviews Physics 8, 208–225 (2026). Published 27 February 2026. DOI: 10.1038/s42254-025-00918-1.

Use: Chapters 6, 9, and 10. Important corrective source: spintronic computing must be evaluated with hardware- and task-specific system metrics rather than isolated switching figures.

Boundary: the review maps potential architectures; it does not establish that spin computing is generally superior to CMOS.

https://www.nature.com/articles/s42254-025-00918-1

### Pirro et al. — Advances in coherent magnonics

Nature Reviews Materials 6, 1114–1135 (2021). DOI: 10.1038/s41578-021-00332-w.

Use: Chapter 9. Background on spin waves and magnons as information carriers, interference, nonlinear dynamics, and device concepts.

Boundary: coherent propagation and functional demonstrations do not erase damping or transduction overhead.

https://www.nature.com/articles/s41578-021-00332-w

### Marrows et al. — Neuromorphic computing with spintronics

npj Spintronics 2, 12 (2024). DOI: 10.1038/s44306-024-00019-2.

Use: Chapters 6 and 10. Review of spintronic neurons, synapses, and magnetic reservoir-computing approaches.

Boundary: notes substantial post-processing and system integration requirements; do not present neuromorphic spintronics as a mature general AI accelerator.

https://www.nature.com/articles/s44306-024-00019-2

### Yaremkevich et al. — On-chip phonon-magnon reservoir for neuromorphic computing

Nature Communications 14, 8296 (2023). DOI: 10.1038/s41467-023-43891-y.

Use: Chapter 10. Primary example of a hybrid physical reservoir in a nanodevice using propagating phonon and magnon modes.

Boundary: the experimental laser-based write/read apparatus should remain inside any system-level interpretation.

https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-43891-y

### Lee et al. — Task-adaptive physical reservoir computing

Nature Materials 23, 79–87 (2024). Published online 13 November 2023. DOI: 10.1038/s41563-023-01698-8.

Use: Chapter 10. Supports the claim that magnetic phase space can be deliberately reconfigured to change reservoir properties for different tasks.

Boundary: demonstrations span specific chiral magnets and operating regimes; do not generalize performance to all magnetic reservoirs.

https://www.nature.com/articles/s41563-023-01698-8

### Jeon et al. — Time multiplexed single node reservoir computing enabled by transient photon magnon coupling

npj Spintronics 3, 26 (2025). Published 30 June 2025. DOI: 10.1038/s44306-025-00092-1.

Use: Chapter 10. Current example of time-multiplexed reservoir computing using transient photon-magnon dynamics.

Boundary: benchmark accuracy is architecture- and dataset-specific and should not be compared casually with unrelated systems.

https://www.nature.com/articles/s44306-025-00092-1

## Magnetoelectric materials

### Spaldin — The chemistry of magnetoelectric multiferroics

Nature Reviews Chemistry 10, 415–426 (2026). Published 30 April 2026. DOI: 10.1038/s41570-026-00827-6.

Use: Chapter 11. Main review for why combining ferroelectricity and magnetic order is chemically difficult, and for the materials families enabling magnetoelectric coupling.

Boundary: the review supports material mechanisms and open questions, not a blanket claim of practical low-voltage magnetic switching.

https://www.nature.com/articles/s41570-026-00827-6

### Zhang et al. — Magnetoelectric effect in van der Waals magnets

npj Quantum Materials 10, 6 (2025). DOI: 10.1038/s41535-025-00725-y.

Use: Chapter 11. Current review of magnetoelectric effects in two-dimensional van der Waals magnetic systems and heterostructures.

Boundary: device implications are emerging and should remain prospective.

https://www.nature.com/articles/s41535-025-00725-y

## Quantum magnetometry

### Aslam et al. — Quantum sensors for biomedical applications

Nature Reviews Physics 5, 157–169 (2023). DOI: 10.1038/s42254-023-00558-3.

Use: Chapter 12. Review of NV-center and optically pumped magnetometer platforms, biomedical cases, and commercialization challenges.

Boundary: sensitivity, spatial resolution, stand-off, apparatus, and clinical integration must be compared together.

https://www.nature.com/articles/s42254-023-00558-3

### Rovny, Kolkowitz and de Leon — Multi-qubit nanoscale sensing with entanglement as a resource

Nature 647, 876–882 (2025). Published 26 November 2025. DOI: 10.1038/s41586-025-09760-y.

Use: Chapter 12. Supports the statement that multi-qubit control and entanglement can enable new nanoscale sensing modalities and sensitivity gains for particular estimation problems.

Boundary: do not convert this into a generic claim that entanglement makes every sensor better after overhead and noise are counted.

https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09760-y

### Li et al. — Multimodal scanning-probe quantum sensing of quantum materials

Nature Materials 25, 1507–1518 (2026). Published 6 July 2026. DOI: 10.1038/s41563-026-02648-w.

Use: Chapter 12. Current review for spin-defect scanning probes and multimodal sensing of magnetic/electronic state in quantum materials.

Boundary: high-value research microscopy is not the same market as low-cost embedded field sensing.

https://www.nature.com/articles/s41563-026-02648-w

## Magnetocaloric cooling

### Zhang et al. — Refrigeration down to 0.16 K using a frustrated magnet Gd2B2MoO9

Nature Communications 17, 1554 (2026). Published 7 January 2026. DOI: 10.1038/s41467-025-68278-z.

Use: Chapter 13. Primary current example of ultra-low-temperature magnetic refrigeration using a frustrated Gd-dominated magnet and a custom quasi-adiabatic demagnetization apparatus.

Boundary: this is cryogenic refrigeration, not evidence for household magnetocaloric cooling. The custom apparatus and field changes stay inside the claim.

https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-68278-z

## High-temperature superconducting magnets

### Hahn et al. — 45.5-tesla direct-current magnetic field generated with a high-temperature superconducting magnet

Nature 570, 496–499 (2019). DOI: 10.1038/s41586-019-1293-1.

Use: Chapter 14. Primary evidence for the 45.5 T DC record system using a 14.4 T REBCO insert inside a 31.1 T resistive background magnet, and for the role of compact high-current-density no-insulation winding.

Boundary: the result is a hybrid system, not a standalone all-superconducting 45.5 T magnet. The manuscript avoids collapsing those details into the headline number.

https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-019-1293-1

### Kim et al. — Field uniformity enhancement in a prototype high-temperature superconducting dipole magnet

Scientific Reports 16, 3589 (2026). Published 7 January 2026. DOI: 10.1038/s41598-025-33641-z.

Use: Chapter 14. Current example of the field-quality engineering required as HTS magnets move beyond record field strength toward accelerator-relevant dipole performance.

Boundary: a prototype is not a production accelerator magnet.

https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-33641-z

## Permanent magnets and material abundance

### Makino et al. — Artificially produced rare-earth free cosmic magnet

Scientific Reports 5, 16627 (2015). DOI: 10.1038/srep16627.

Use: Chapter 15. Primary example of laboratory production of ordered L1_0 FeNi/tetrataenite-like hard magnetic material and the kinetic challenge posed by natural meteorite formation.

Boundary: the authors' mass-production optimism is not treated as evidence that tetrataenite has displaced NdFeB commercially. The manuscript uses it as a materials-processing example.

https://www.nature.com/articles/srep16627

Supply-chain concentration claims in a release edition should be refreshed against current USGS, IEA, EU, and producer data because percentages and policy conditions change quickly. This Desk draft deliberately avoids hard current market-share percentages in the narrative.

## Magnetic medicine

### Pacchioni — Magnetic microrobots approach the clinic

Nature Reviews Materials 10, 883 (2025). Published 21 November 2025. DOI: 10.1038/s41578-025-00867-2.

Use: Chapter 16. Research highlight pointing to the Science 2025 `Clinically ready magnetic microrobots for targeted therapies` platform integrating navigation, therapeutic delivery, and imaging under clinically motivated conditions.

Boundary: “approach the clinic” is the source's title, not proof of approved broad clinical use. The manuscript keeps clinical translation conditional.

https://www.nature.com/articles/s41578-025-00867-2

### Shakeri-Zadeh and Bulte — Imaging-guided precision hyperthermia with magnetic nanoparticles

Nature Reviews Bioengineering 3, 245–260 (2025). Published online 7 November 2024. DOI: 10.1038/s44222-024-00257-3.

Use: Chapter 16. Review of magnetic nanoparticle imaging and hyperthermia with emphasis on locating particles and controlling treatment rather than treating heating alone as sufficient.

Boundary: specific clinical status varies by technology and jurisdiction; no broad cancer-treatment claim should be inferred.

https://www.nature.com/articles/s44222-024-00257-3

## Source-handling rules for the next edit

- Refresh altermagnetism, medical microrobotics, HTS magnets, and rare-earth supply data if more than six months passes before release.
- Prefer a primary paper when a specific experiment is described; use reviews for field boundaries and competing interpretations.
- Keep record metrics attached to apparatus, temperature, geometry, volume, and time scale.
- Do not write “room temperature” as a synonym for commercially practical.
- Do not write “topologically protected” as a synonym for indestructible.
- Do not write “high-temperature superconductor” as a synonym for room-temperature superconductor.
- Do not convert a material's intrinsic property into an end-to-end device claim without transduction and peripheral costs.
- Keep medical statements at technology/engineering level and avoid treatment recommendations.
- Maintain the novelty distinction: `magnetecture` is the author's synthesis; the science is prior work.
