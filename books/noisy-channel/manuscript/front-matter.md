# NOISY CHANNEL

*Why a statistically strange signal can still be useless intelligence*

Sven Hardy Benson

This book deals with a subject that punishes certainty in both directions.

The United States government funded remote-viewing research and operational tasking. That is documented. Declassified records contain training sessions, operational requests, transcripts, evaluations, contracts, memoranda, and arguments over whether anything useful was happening. The existence of those records proves that institutions investigated the subject. It does not prove that remote viewing works.

The evidence in this book is kept in four classes.

**Verified / documented.** Declassified records, official histories, contemporaneous program documents, and peer-reviewed or otherwise inspectable research establish that a program, experiment, protocol, decision, or statement existed.

**Credible but incomplete.** Testimony, retrospective accounts, and research results with meaningful controls but unresolved methodological or replication questions. These may change probabilities. They do not become proof by repetition.

**Disputed.** Claims whose evidentiary chain is incomplete, whose interpretation depends heavily on retrospective selection, or which lack independent confirmation. Famous stories belong here unless their fame is matched by records.

**Speculative synthesis.** Hypotheses about what could explain the residual evidence if ordinary leakage, judging artifacts, selection effects, fraud, memory, and methodological weaknesses are insufficient. Speculation is permitted only when it wears its own name.

A classified stamp is evidence about information control, not truth. A government contract is evidence that somebody paid to investigate a proposition, not that the proposition survived investigation. A low p-value is evidence about a statistical model, not automatically about a mechanism. An intelligence customer saying that a description was interesting is not the same as demonstrating that it improved a decision.

The book therefore keeps asking two questions that are often collapsed into one: **Is there information here?** And, separately, **can anyone use it?**
