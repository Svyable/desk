# NOISY CHANNEL

*Why a statistically strange signal can still be useless intelligence*

The United States spent decades asking whether people could acquire information about distant or hidden targets without ordinary sensory access. The most interesting surviving result is not a clean yes or no. In 1995, after reviewing the program that became known as STARGATE, evaluators could simultaneously argue that laboratory results contained a statistically anomalous effect and that the output had not demonstrated useful intelligence value.

NOISY CHANNEL is about that gap. It asks what happens when an effect may be real enough to survive some statistical tests but too weak, unstable, underspecified, or interpretation-dependent to support decisions. Remote viewing is the central case, not a proof of psi and not a punchline. The book follows the entire information chain: target selection, blinding, cueing, response production, judging, feedback, operational interpretation, tasking, and consequence.

The governing distinction is simple but expensive: **evidence that a channel carries information is not yet evidence that the channel carries usable intelligence.**
