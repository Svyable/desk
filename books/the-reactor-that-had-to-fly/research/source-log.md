# Source log

Checked 2026-09-14 unless otherwise noted. This is a working provenance ledger, not a substitute for claim-level citations in a later publication pass.

## Primary / declassified / official

### Lawrence Radiation Laboratory, *Tory II-A: A Nuclear Ramjet Test Reactor* (UCRL-5484)

OSTI record: https://www.osti.gov/biblio/4262720

**Use:** primary design basis for Tory II-A; compact high-temperature/high-power-density core; approximately half of reactor volume required for coolant flow; severe material, pressure, thermal, radiation and structural requirements; explicit statement that II-A was an experimental reactor designed to attack core mechanical/chemical engineering feasibility rather than every flight requirement.

**Boundary:** a design report establishes the reactor and intended test burden, not an operational SLAM mission or successful flight.

### Lawrence Radiation Laboratory, *Engineering Design of the Tory II-C Nuclear Ramjet Reactor* (UCRL-7679)

UNT/declassified record: https://digital.library.unt.edu/ark:/67531/metadc1032461/

**Use:** principal technical record for Tory II-C; prepared November 1964 after cancellation; describes the reactor as the culmination of Pluto development from 1957–1964 and documents core, supports, controls and assembly.

**Boundary:** exact power figures must be tied to design point or individual tests; later institutional histories round differently.

### Lawrence Radiation Laboratory, Pluto fuel-element fabrication report (UCRL-12391)

OSTI record: https://www.osti.gov/biblio/4583594

**Use:** extrusion/manufacturing development for tubular beryllia-based fuel elements; near-theoretical density, dimensional tolerances, chemical purity, phase distribution, defects, strength; industrial production by Coors Porcelain for Tory II-C.

**Boundary:** the production quantity and reject/yield denominator require full report extraction before publication.

### U.S. Atomic Energy Commission, annual / reactor inventory records

OSTI search entry for Pluto/Tory reactor inventory: https://www.osti.gov/biblio/4026898

**Use:** independent official chronology; Tory II-C successfully tested at full design power in May 1964; project cancellation July 1, 1964; reactor disposition after cancellation.

### Nevada National Security Site, Project Pluto / Area 26 history

NNSS history portal: https://nnss.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/DOENV_715_Rev1.pdf

**Use:** official site history for Site 401/Area 26 infrastructure, Jackass Flats, roads/buildings/utilities, compressed-air storage using oil-well casing, automated rail transport, Tory II-A chronology and program termination.

**Boundary:** institutional history is authoritative for site chronology but not a substitute for original test reports on exact performance.

### Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory institutional histories

LLNL historical publication: https://str.llnl.gov/content/pages/past-issues-pdfs/1998.09.pdf

LLNL anniversary history: https://annual.llnl.gov/sites/annual/files/2022-01/five-decades-of-accomplishments.pdf

**Use:** institutional summaries of Pluto’s origin, Tory II-A and Tory II-C, ceramics/materials challenge, test-plant operation, approximately 500-MW-class Tory II-C performance, successful test sequence and cancellation after lack of military commitment.

**Boundary:** retrospective laboratory histories have an understandable incentive to foreground technical accomplishment. Cancellation causation is cross-checked against contemporaneous reporting.

### Air Force Magazine, “Pluto: A New Strategic System or Just Another Test Program?” July 1964

Air & Space Forces archive: https://www.airandspaceforces.com/article/0764pluto/

**Use:** unusually valuable contemporaneous evidence that Pluto’s strategic future and next development phase were contested before the familiar post-test cancellation story solidified; discusses Department of Defense reluctance, the flight-test/weaponization decision and cost range.

**Boundary:** magazine reporting is not a cancellation directive. It establishes contemporaneous debate and attributed positions, not a single official cause.

## Secondary / historical reporting

### Gregg Herken, “The Flying Crowbar,” *Air & Space*, 1990

Smithsonian/Air & Space historical article; archived copies circulate widely.

**Use:** participant recollection, program culture, later public rediscovery of Pluto, source genealogy for many vivid claims repeated online.

**Boundary:** recollections decades after the event are not silently promoted into design requirements. Spectacular claims require independent primary confirmation.

### Nevada Test Site history, *Battlefield of the Cold War*

OSTI: https://www.osti.gov/biblio/1087656

**Use:** broader context for Jackass Flats, Rover/Pluto and the Nevada nuclear-testing ecosystem.

## Modern comparison: 9M730 Burevestnik

### Reuters, October 26, 2025 — Russian test announcement

https://www.reuters.com/world/china/russia-tested-new-nuclear-powered-cruise-missile-top-general-says-2025-10-26/

**Use:** records Russian official claims that an October 21, 2025 Burevestnik test covered about 14,000 km in roughly 15 hours and operated on nuclear power throughout; Putin said crucial testing was complete and infrastructure/deployment work should proceed.

**Boundary:** the distance, duration and continuous nuclear-operation details are Russian state claims reported by Reuters, not independently released telemetry.

### Reuters explainer, October 26, 2025

https://www.reuters.com/business/aerospace-defense/what-is-russias-burevestnik-missile-2025-10-26/

**Use:** public program chronology since 2018; low-flying/nuclear-powered description; expert skepticism about strategic marginal value and radiological implications; history of test failures and reported linkage to the 2019 fatal incident.

**Boundary:** public details of reactor architecture are sparse. Do not map Tory geometry, fuel or exhaust behavior onto Burevestnik without evidence.
