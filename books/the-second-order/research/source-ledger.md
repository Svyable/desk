# Source ledger — The Second Order

Sources are recorded at the chapter where they materially support factual claims. Interpretive synthesis remains the author's unless otherwise noted.

## Chapter 1 — The Forecast Enters the World

### Robert E. Lucas Jr. — econometric policy evaluation

- **Source:** Robert E. Lucas Jr., “Econometric Policy Evaluation: A Critique,” *Carnegie-Rochester Conference Series on Public Policy*, vol. 1 (1976), pp. 19–46.
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/S0167-2231(76)80003-6
- **Publisher record:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167223176800036
- **Supports:** the historical claim that Lucas challenged policy evaluation based on behavioral relationships estimated under a prior policy regime because expectations and decisions can change when policy rules change.
- **Caution:** the chapter uses the critique as a general lesson about policy-conditioned behavior, not as evidence that every empirical parameter is unstable or that reduced-form forecasting is useless.

### Donald T. Campbell — consequential social indicators

- **Source:** Donald T. Campbell, *Assessing the Impact of Planned Social Change*, Occasional Paper Series #8, Evaluation Center, Western Michigan University, December 1976.
- **Authoritative record:** https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED303512
- **Supports:** Campbell's work on evaluation research and the political-methodological problems that arise when quantitative evidence is embedded in programs and social decision-making; this work is the historical source associated with the later shorthand “Campbell's law.”
- **Caution:** the manuscript does not present a compressed modern wording as a verbatim 1976 quotation. It paraphrases the underlying mechanism.

## Chapter-level falsification note

The opening argument should be weakened if consequential deployment routinely leaves the relevant behavior and data-generating process unchanged in domains where actors can observe incentives and respond. Evidence that well-designed targets remain informative under sustained pressure is not an exception to hide; it is evidence about design conditions the book needs to explain. Likewise, feedback that improves the actual objective counts against any simplistic claim that adaptation is inherently corrupting.
