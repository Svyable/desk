# Research brief — The Second Order

## Working question

What changes when a model, metric, ranking, forecast, or decision rule becomes part of the system it is trying to describe?

The book follows a family of related mechanisms without treating them as interchangeable: proxy gaming, substitution around a rule, treatment effects caused by prediction, selection created by model-based decisions, equilibrium adaptation, strategic response, performativity, and data feedback loops. The recurring distinction is between first-order predictive accuracy and the consequences of deploying the prediction.

## Research discipline

Concrete historical, empirical, legal, technical, and institutional claims should be traceable to primary or authoritative sources where practical. Each chapter should record the strongest evidence against its preferred interpretation rather than using sources only as support.

The manuscript should distinguish three things that are often collapsed:

- a measure becoming less informative because actors optimize the proxy;
- a behavioral relationship changing because incentives or policy rules changed;
- an outcome changing because the prediction triggered treatment or selection.

Those mechanisms can coexist, but they imply different remedies.

## Chapter 1 — The Forecast Enters the World

The opening chapter establishes the core move from observation to intervention. Its historical spine uses 1970s monetary-policy debates, Lucas's critique of policy evaluation, and Campbell's work on consequential social indicators. Goodhart is treated through Bank of England archival evidence about his direct involvement with monetary targets rather than through an unsourced version of the famous aphorism.

### Falsification / counterevidence note

The chapter's thesis would be too strong if it implied that every consequential metric decays, every policy change destabilizes behavioral relationships, or every feedback loop is harmful. Many measurements remain useful under pressure; some targets work because improving the measured quantity genuinely advances the objective; some actors cannot observe or respond to the rule; and intervention-induced changes can be the intended mechanism. Weather and astronomical forecasts are useful contrasts because action on the forecast ordinarily does not alter the underlying physical event.

The durable claim is narrower: when information changes incentives, treatment, selection, or expectations, post-deployment data cannot automatically be interpreted as though the model remained a passive observer.

## Planned evidence domains

Later chapters should build from primary or high-authority material in economics and policy evaluation, education and accountability, clinical prediction and causal inference, policing and administrative data, recommender systems, online experimentation, financial-market microstructure, AI benchmarks/evaluations, adversarial systems, and institutional design.
