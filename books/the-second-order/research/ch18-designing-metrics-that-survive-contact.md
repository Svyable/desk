# Chapter 18 research — Designing Metrics That Survive Contact

## Core evidence

### Donald T. Campbell — consequential indicators and corruption pressure

- **Source:** Donald T. Campbell, *Assessing the Impact of Planned Social Change*, Occasional Paper Series #8, Evaluation Center, Western Michigan University, 1976; later reprinted in *Journal of MultiDisciplinary Evaluation* 7(15), 2011.
- **Record:** https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED303512
- **Supports:** the chapter's core design premise that quantitative social indicators can become vulnerable to corruption pressures and distort the processes they are intended to monitor when consequences are attached to them.
- **Caution:** the chapter paraphrases Campbell's mechanism rather than presenting a modern compressed formulation as a verbatim quotation.

### Bengt Holmstrom and Paul Milgrom — multitask incentives

- **Source:** Bengt Holmstrom and Paul Milgrom, “Multitask Principal-Agent Analyses: Incentive Contracts, Asset Ownership, and Job Design,” *Journal of Law, Economics, & Organization* 7 (special issue), 1991, pp. 24–52.
- **Publisher record:** https://academic.oup.com/jleo/article-abstract/7/special_issue/24/2194011
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1093/jleo/7.special_issue.24
- **Supports:** the chapter's argument that strong incentives on measurable tasks can distort effort away from important tasks that are difficult to measure, helping explain why muted incentives or broader job design can sometimes outperform simple high-powered performance pay.
- **Caution:** the formal principal-agent model is not a universal empirical law. The chapter uses it as a mechanism for understanding multidimensional work rather than proof that strong incentives are generally harmful.

### UK Home Office — targets in policing

- **Source:** Home Office, *The Use of Targets in Policing — Review 2015*, led by Chief Superintendent Irene Curtis, published 8 December 2015.
- **Record:** https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-use-of-targets-in-policing
- **Supports:** the chapter's concrete evidence that performance-management systems can create target-chasing, gaming, manipulation risks, bureaucracy, and distorted priorities while still serving legitimate management purposes. The review drew on all 43 police forces in England and Wales and an online survey receiving more than 6,000 responses from officers and staff.
- **Caution:** policing is one institutional setting; the chapter does not generalize every specific finding mechanically to schools, hospitals, firms, or machine-learning benchmarks.

## Falsification / counterevidence note

The chapter should weaken if high-powered metrics routinely improve all important dimensions of multidimensional work without substitution, gaming, classification changes, threshold effects, or deterioration in unmeasured outcomes. It should also weaken if independent measurement channels provide little additional information once a primary metric becomes consequential.

Counterevidence runs in the other direction as well. Clear metrics can reveal neglect, improve coordination, increase accountability, and focus effort on genuinely important objectives. The durable claim is narrower: metric design should anticipate behavioral response, protect important unmeasured work, retain evidence channels that fail differently, and weaken or revise incentives when the proxy's relationship to the objective deteriorates under pressure.
