Why Civilization Pays for Things Not to Happen.

The working thesis is that modern economies systematically undervalue redundancy, slack, reversibility and alternative pathways because their costs are visible every quarter while their benefits appear mainly as disasters that did not occur. The book follows the economics of reliability through engineering, supply chains, finance, grids, hospitals, cloud systems, cities, strategic reserves, ecology and autonomous AI.

The argument is deliberately two-sided. Redundancy can become bureaucracy, stockpiles can rot, safety rules can outlive their hazards, and excessive buffers can make useful systems unaffordable. The question is not whether more resilience is always better. It is how to identify the options worth paying to keep alive.
