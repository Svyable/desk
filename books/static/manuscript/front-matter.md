# STATIC

## The invisible ecology animals can feel and humans forgot to look for

Sven Hardy Benson

---

Electricity has a branding problem.

To a human being it is usually infrastructure, injury, weather, or metaphor. A socket. A lightning bolt. A shock. Chemistry with a cable attached.

To other animals it can be landscape.

This book is about the part of the world that was always physical and only recently became perceptual to us: weak electric fields used as biological information. Sharks and rays detect fields in water. Platypuses hunt with eyes, ears and nostrils closed while electroreception helps locate prey. Bumblebees can discriminate floral electric fields, and their mechanosensory hairs provide a plausible transduction route in air.

None of this proves that humans possess a comparable electric sense. None of it validates vague claims that any weak electromagnetic exposure must have a meaningful biological effect. In fact the opposite rule governs this book:

**A field is not a signal until an organism has a transduction path, and a biological effect is not a sense until it carries usable information.**

That rule cuts both ways. It blocks electromagnetic mysticism. It also blocks the lazy assumption that a physical variable cannot matter biologically merely because humans do not consciously perceive it.

The interesting territory lies between those errors.

## Evidence classes

Every controversial claim is carried in one of four bins.

1. **Verified / documented.** Replicated or well-supported biological capabilities, measured fields, anatomy, physiology, and peer-reviewed experiments.
2. **Credible but incomplete.** Results with plausible mechanisms or good experiments that still lack broad replication, ecological validation, or a complete transduction chain.
3. **Disputed / unconfirmed.** Human electric-sense claims, broad weak-field health claims, extraordinary bioeffect claims, and any inference whose mechanism or replication remains inadequate.
4. **Speculative synthesis.** The book's proposed framework for treating electric ecology as an information problem: field → coupling → receptor → neural representation → behavior.

The categories are not permanent verdicts. They are prices. Better evidence can move a claim. Rhetoric cannot.
