# Evidence ledger — STATIC

## Governing thesis

Biology has repeatedly turned weak environmental electric fields into information. The epistemically useful question is therefore not `can a field affect biology?` but `what physical coupling, receptor, neural representation and behavioral advantage connect this field to this organism?` This chain lets extraordinary electromagnetic claims be tested without either ridicule or mechanism-free acceptance.

## 1 — Verified / documented

### Bumblebees discriminate floral electric fields
Clarke, Whitney, Sutton & Robert reported that *Bombus terrestris* detects floral electric fields, can discriminate field patterns, and uses electric information alongside other floral cues.

- Science (2013): https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1230883
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23429701/

### Bumblebee mechanosensory hairs respond to weak electric fields
Sutton et al. measured mechanical and neural responses and concluded that mechanosensory hairs provide an electromechanical transduction route for ecologically relevant electric fields.

- PNAS (2016): https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1601624113

### Electroreception is established across multiple aquatic lineages and in monotreme mammals
The comparative literature includes sharks/rays, electric fishes, amphibians, platypus, echidnas and the Guiana dolphin. Platypus electroreception is integrated with mechanoreception during underwater prey detection.

- England & Robert, Biological Reviews (2022), ecology of electricity/electroreception: https://doi.org/10.1111/brv.12804

## 2 — Credible but incomplete

### Aerial electroreception may be more widespread than the best-established bee work
Mechanosensory hairs are widespread among arthropods, making Coulomb-force coupling a plausible broader sensory route. Species-by-species ecological function still requires demonstration; anatomical availability is not behavioral proof.

### Electric ecology may supply rapidly changing information unavailable from static visual/chemical cues
Floral potentials can change after pollinator visitation. The information value is experimentally supported in bumblebees, but the ecological importance relative to odor, color, humidity and mechanosensation varies by context and should not be universalized.

## 3 — Disputed / unconfirmed

### Humans possess a useful weak-electric-field sense
Do not infer this from animal electroreception. Human skin/hair responses to static electricity are obvious at sufficiently high fields, but that is not evidence for a dedicated, ecologically useful electroreceptive sensory system at weak ambient levels.

### Weak environmental electromagnetic exposure is biologically meaningful merely because a field is measurable
A measurable field is an exposure fact. Biological coupling, dose, frequency dependence, receptor or target, reproducible effect and functional consequence are separate evidentiary jobs.

### Electromagnetic sensitivity reports establish an external field mechanism
Symptoms and experiences are real data about symptoms and experiences; attribution to a particular electromagnetic cause requires blinded exposure-response evidence and appropriate controls. Do not convert phenomenology into mechanism.

## 4 — Speculative synthesis / open conjecture

### The transduction ladder
For any proposed field-mediated biological phenomenon, require:

`field → physical coupling → receptor/target → transduction → neural/cellular representation → reproducible effect → ecological/functional information`

Claims can be strong at one rung and weak at the next.

### Information-dose split
Two exposures with similar energy can differ in temporal/spatial structure; conversely, a structured field is not biologically informative unless the organism has machinery capable of resolving that structure. This is a research framework, not a claim that low-energy structure bypasses ordinary biophysics.

### Anthropocentric null
Humans systematically underweight environmental variables outside conscious perception. This is a hypothesis about scientific attention, not evidence that any particular hidden sense exists.

## Contradictions / traps

- `Animals sense electricity` does not imply `humans sense electricity`.
- `A receptor moves in a field` does not imply the organism uses that movement behaviorally.
- `A biological effect is statistically detectable` does not imply the effect is adaptive, perceptual, harmful, or consciously accessible.
- `Mechanism unknown` is not evidence for an exotic mechanism.
- `Mechanism plausible` is not evidence that a disputed effect occurred.
- Electric and magnetic sensing must not be casually merged. Different coupling physics can produce superficially similar language.

## Publication holds

- Do not describe human electroreception as established.
- Do not use animal electroreception to validate Havana-syndrome, psychotronic, directed-energy, paranormal, or generalized EM-sensitivity claims.
- Do not imply that a patent demonstrates efficacy.
- Do not call weak fields harmless or harmful without exposure-specific evidence.
- Do not promote laboratory bee results into a universal insect sense without species-specific support.

## Defeat condition

The book's broader synthesis weakens if apparently electric-mediated behaviors fail under artifact-resistant replication, if proposed receptors do not transduce ecologically realistic fields, or if removing electric information produces no measurable loss once correlated cues are controlled. The framework must allow `the field exists but the organism does not use it` as a normal outcome.
