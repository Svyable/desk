# Back Matter

## A note on a story still happening

*To The Stars* was researched and drafted against a September 3, 2026 factual baseline, six days after President Donald J. Trump signed Executive Order 14423 at NASA's Johnson Space Center.

That timing shapes the book.

The United States Space Academy is not presented here as an operating university. As of the baseline date, the executive order had established a Presidential Commission and directed it to recommend the Academy's structure within 120 days. Major questions remained unresolved: governance, legal authority, accreditation, curriculum, degrees, admissions, service obligations, funding, permanent location, faculty model, first-class timing and the exact relationship with the U.S. Air Force Academy, Space Force training institutions, NASA workforce programs, universities and commercial partners.

Where the manuscript discusses possible designs, those passages are arguments, comparisons or scenarios—not reports of decisions that had already been made.

The distinction matters because current-affairs books can age badly in a peculiar way. A proposal becomes familiar, and later readers unconsciously import the finished institution backward into the moment when it was still uncertain. This book tries to preserve the uncertainty the people making the decision actually faced.

Future revisions should therefore begin with the primary record. The commission's report, any presidential implementation decisions, congressional authorization and appropriations, accreditation actions, site selection, admissions material and actual first-class records should supersede this draft wherever they establish facts that were unknown on September 3, 2026.

## Source method

The research trail favors primary sources whenever the claim is about what an institution did, said or formally required. The core record includes Executive Order 14423 and its Federal Register publication; White House and NASA materials; statutes and historical material surrounding NASA and the federal service academies; official U.S. Space Force and STARCOM records; U.S. Air Force Academy histories and current program material; and congressional or elected-official statements when documenting political argument.

High-quality contemporary reporting is used for reaction, context and developments not fully captured in agency releases. Interpretive claims are kept separate from established facts in the research notes.

The durable research files for this edition live alongside the manuscript in `research/`. They include the book bible, the baseline research spine, and a chapter evidence map identifying the source families and falsification conditions behind each section.

## Terminology

Uniformed members of the United States Space Force are **Guardians**. The book does not use *soldier* as a generic term for them.

The official 2026 project is the **United States Space Academy**. “Space Force Academy” and “West Point for Space” appear only as shorthand, analogy or quoted framing. The executive order describes a proposed NASA-led federal academy broader than a single military commissioning institution.

## Revision trigger

The first major factual revision should occur after the Presidential Commission's 120-day report or any earlier release of substantive recommendations. A later edition should change the narrative where reality has chosen among the alternatives this draft leaves open. It should not simply append an update while leaving superseded uncertainty intact.

The story should be allowed to become more specific as the institution does.
