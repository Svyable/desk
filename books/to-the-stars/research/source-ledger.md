# Source Ledger and Falsification Notes

**Book:** *To the Stars: The Story of the United States Space Academy*  
**Author:** Sven Hardy Benson  
**Research cutoff:** September 3, 2026

This ledger is the checkable evidence trail for the manuscript. It records the sources behind concrete claims, the chapters where they are used, and the principal counter-claim each chapter must survive. It is not a substitute for rereading a source before a later edition; the Academy is a fast-moving policy project and some facts will change.

## Chapter-by-chapter evidence and falsification

### Prologue — The Order Signed in Houston

**Primary evidence:** S01, S03, S04, S05.  
**Claims allowed:** date, location, commission creation, NASA leadership, broad workforce purpose, Artemis II ceremony context if verified in contemporaneous sources.  
**What would make the framing false?** If later records show the Academy announcement was legally or institutionally distinct from the August 28 event in a way current coverage missed, revise the scene. Do not invent the room, gestures, applause, private motives, or dialogue.

### Chapter 1 — An Academy Before a Campus

**Primary evidence:** S01, S02, S05.  
**Claims allowed:** the Commission exists; the Academy remains proposed; report due in 120 days; location, governance, accreditation, curriculum, admissions, service obligations, legislation, and implementation remain design questions.  
**What would make the argument false?** A later statute or presidential action that settles these questions. Update the chapter rather than preserving “unknown” as historical theater.

### Chapter 2 — The Long Line

**Primary evidence:** S07, S08, S09, S10.  
**Claims allowed:** dates and institutional reasons behind West Point and Annapolis; technical-professional origins; service academies as culture-forming institutions.  
**What would make the analogy false?** If the Space Academy's mission becomes predominantly civilian or consortium-based, the service-academy lineage may be only partial. Preserve that possibility.

### Chapter 3 — When the Air Force Needed Its Own School

**Primary evidence:** S11, S12, S13.  
**Claims allowed:** 1947 service independence; 1954 authorization; site search; temporary Lowry campus; first class; move and first graduation.  
**What would make the analogy false?** The Space Academy may not become a separate military-service academy at all. NASA-led governance is a major structural difference.

### Chapter 4 — Space Cadets Before the Space Force

**Primary evidence:** S13, S14, S15, S16.  
**Claims allowed:** long-standing astronautics education at USAFA; Guardians commissioned from USAFA beginning in 2020; existing statutory pathway for USAFA graduates to enter Space Force.  
**What would make the need claim false?** If USAFA and existing commissioning sources can scale and specialize enough to meet future demand, a separate Academy may be redundant.

### Chapter 5 — The Astronaut Problem

**Primary evidence:** S17, S18, S19.  
**Claims allowed:** Mercury Seven selection; military-test-pilot origins; expansion toward scientists and other technical professions; contemporary highly selective astronaut pipeline.  
**What would make the lesson false?** If the Academy is designed mainly for operators, acquisition officers, or civil-service engineers rather than astronaut development, astronaut history should remain illustrative rather than dominant.

### Chapter 6 — The Service Born in Orbit

**Primary evidence:** S20, S21, S22.  
**Claims allowed:** 2019 creation; inherited mission; Guardian terminology; stated institutional values.  
**What would make the cultural argument false?** If Space Force professional identity stabilizes successfully through existing institutions, the Academy may reinforce rather than create that identity.

### Chapter 7 — Guardians Need a Profession

**Primary evidence:** S23, S24, S25, S26.  
**Claims allowed:** STARCOM mission; OTC scale and curriculum; cross-career-field education; component-staff course; doctrine ecosystem.  
**What would make the chapter's tension false?** If the Academy is explicitly designed to leave professional military formation to STARCOM, overlap may be smaller than the chapter anticipates.

### Chapter 8 — The Missing People

**Primary evidence:** S01, S03, S27.  
**Claims allowed:** GAO workforce figures and findings; administration's broader talent rationale.  
**What would make the Academy-as-workforce-response argument false?** Evidence that shortages are driven mainly by billets, retention, compensation, clearance delays, or flawed requirement-setting rather than lack of undergraduate entrants.

### Chapter 9 — What the Order Actually Says

**Primary evidence:** S01, S02, S03.  
**Claims allowed:** Commission composition and all nine required recommendation areas.  
**What would make the interpretation false?** Later legal guidance or amendments. Quote sparingly and prefer precise paraphrase.

### Chapter 10 — Why NASA Is in the Chair

**Primary evidence:** S01, S04, S17-S19, S30-S32.  
**Claims allowed:** NASA Administrator chairs the Commission; NASA-led academy is an option explicitly to be evaluated; NASA history spans human spaceflight, science, engineering, and commercial partnership.  
**What would make the institutional-integration argument false?** If NASA governance proves incompatible with military commissioning or if the final recommendation places the Academy elsewhere.

### Chapter 11 — Who Gets In

**Primary evidence:** S01, S16-S19.  
**Claims allowed:** Commission must consider citizenship, security clearances, government employment or military status; historical NASA selection criteria; existing service-academy obligation framework.  
**What would make proposed admissions reasoning false?** The Commission may choose a radically different pathway, including post-undergraduate admission, federal employees only, or a distributed scholarship model.

### Chapter 12 — What They Must Learn

**Primary evidence:** S01, S23-S26, S28-S32, S33.  
**Claims allowed:** Commission must recommend academic and leadership curriculum; existing Guardian training crosses space operations, cyber, intelligence, acquisitions; modern space work spans law and commercial integration.  
**What would make the proposed curriculum obsolete?** Changes in technology, classification, automation, mission design, or final governance. Write this chapter as design principles, not a fake course catalog.

### Chapter 13 — Warfighters in a Peaceful Domain

**Primary evidence:** S26, S33-S35.  
**Claims allowed:** treaty constraints; military reliance on space; existence of counterspace threats; need for escalation and legal judgment.  
**What would make a statement false?** Any claim that “space is peaceful by law” or “the treaty bans military space activity” is too broad and must be rejected.

### Chapter 14 — The Commercial Classroom

**Primary evidence:** S28-S32.  
**Claims allowed:** Space Force strategy toward hybrid commercial/government architectures; NASA COTS and CRS as public-private precedents; commercial transportation policy.  
**What would make the argument false?** If core Academy roles are narrow enough that industry fluency is peripheral, or if security requirements limit meaningful commercial integration.

### Chapter 15 — The Campus Question

**Primary evidence:** S01, S05, S06, S11, S12.  
**Claims allowed:** location is unresolved and must be addressed by the Commission; historical USAFA site competition; temporary-campus precedent.  
**What would make any location passage false?** A final selection. Political bids must never be upgraded into facts before that point.

### Chapter 16 — The Price of a Free Education

**Primary evidence:** S01, S06, S15, S16.  
**Claims allowed:** Commission must recommend service obligations; existing USAFA baseline is five years active duty; Space Academy service may include Armed Forces or civilian federal service.  
**What would make the analogy false?** A service model based on scholarships, reserve service, civilian years, repayment, or mixed obligations rather than a traditional academy contract.

### Chapter 17 — The Case Against Another Academy

**Primary evidence:** S05, S06, S14-S16, S23-S27.  
**Claims allowed:** existing USAFA, commissioning, STARCOM, and workforce systems; publicly reported skepticism; small size of the Space Force.  
**What would make skepticism weaker?** Evidence that the Academy creates a genuinely new cross-agency pipeline unavailable through existing institutions, at acceptable cost and without cannibalizing them.

### Chapter 18 — The China Clock

**Primary evidence:** S29, S34-S36.  
**Claims allowed:** official U.S. assessments of growing Chinese and Russian space and counterspace capabilities; commercial and military space expansion; need for resilience and expertise.  
**What would make the causal claim false?** The Academy is not a short-cycle weapon system and should not be sold as one. Its relevance depends on durable human-capital needs, not a single threat forecast.

### Chapter 19 — A Culture Not Yet Written

**Primary evidence:** S10, S12, S22.  
**Claims allowed:** service academies deliberately cultivate values, honor systems, rituals, and professional identity; Space Force already articulates Guardian values.  
**What would make the culture argument false?** If the Academy becomes a civilian institute or consortium with no residential cadet culture, tradition will work differently.

### Chapter 20 — The First Class

**Primary evidence:** S01, S02, S11, S12, S27.  
**Claims allowed:** legal and administrative steps still outstanding; historical ability to begin an academy at a temporary location; workforce urgency.  
**What would make the scenario false?** Almost every operational detail remains contingent. Keep future verbs conditional until official action occurs.

### Epilogue — To the Stars

**Primary evidence:** S01, S27-S36.  
**Claims allowed:** the proposal sits at the intersection of exploration, security, science, government, and commerce.  
**What would make the conclusion false?** The institution's eventual value must be measured by outcomes, not founding rhetoric. A later edition should examine graduates, retention, mission performance, scientific contribution, public-service careers, and institutional adaptability.

## Claims deliberately excluded from the first draft

The following may be tempting but are not sufficiently settled as of the research cutoff:

- A specific permanent campus location.
- A firm opening year or first graduating class year.
- A fixed class size.
- A fixed tuition/scholarship mechanism beyond the broad federal-academy concept reported publicly.
- A specific admissions nomination system.
- A fixed active-duty term for Space Academy graduates.
- A promise that every graduate will enter the Space Force.
- A claim that the Academy will replace USAFA, ROTC, OTS, STARCOM, NASA internships, or university partnerships.
- A claim that the Academy will be legally equivalent to West Point, Annapolis, or USAFA.
- A claim that a Space Academy graduate is automatically an astronaut candidate.
- A prediction that the Academy will definitely be enacted by Congress.
