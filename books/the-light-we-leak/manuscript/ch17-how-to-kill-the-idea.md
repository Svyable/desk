# How to Kill the Idea

A theory that cannot die is not being studied.

The broad fact of ultraweak photon emission is secure enough that no single failed signaling experiment will erase it. That is useful because it lets us be ruthless with the stronger claim.

What would count against biological optical communication?

First, photon budgets that fail by orders of magnitude after realistic geometry and receptor sensitivity are included. If the best measured source cannot deliver enough photons to change the proposed receiver, speculation about information content is premature.

Second, a failure of optical isolation. If distant biological effects persist when the optical path is blocked but disappear when gas exchange, vibration or electrical coupling is changed, the mechanism is elsewhere.

Third, artificial replay failure. If a receiver responds to a nearby stressed culture but not to a calibrated copy of that culture's measured photon stream, photons are unlikely to be sufficient.

Fourth, receptor failure. If candidate photoreceptors can be removed without affecting the alleged interaction, that mechanism loses support. The hypothesis can move to another receptor, but each move should cost probability rather than reset the argument.

Fifth, intensity mismatch. If reported biological responses require laboratory illumination millions of times stronger than endogenous emission, those studies cannot be used as evidence that spontaneous photons perform the same function.

Sixth, spectral mismatch. A source emitting primarily where the proposed receiver barely absorbs has a problem. So does a receptor located behind tissue that strongly attenuates the relevant wavelengths.

Seventh, replication asymmetry. If positive results cluster in a few laboratories using flexible analyses while independent groups repeatedly fail under tighter controls, the field should update toward artifact or context dependence.

This list is not a ritual. It is a commitment to losing.

The opposite results would strengthen the case. Measured endogenous flux sufficient for known photochemistry. Receiver effects gated by an optical shutter. Replay at matched intensity. Genetic dependence on a plausible receptor. Predictable wavelength and distance curves. Independent replication.

A particularly strong result would link all of those in one system.

The field also needs defeat conditions for its more extravagant branches. Claims of coherent organism-wide optical fields should specify measurable coherence lengths and times, not only unusual decay curves. DNA-laser models should identify gain and resonant behavior. Brain-photon theories should state what computation fails when optical transport is disrupted. Human interpersonal signaling claims should survive environmental photon backgrounds and blinded conditions.

Without such conditions, the theory expands to fit every null result. The photons were too weak that day. The organism was stressed. The detector disturbed coherence. Consciousness changed the field. The barrier blocked an unknown wavelength. This style of rescue can protect any idea forever.

A serious anomalous science does the opposite. It advertises the knife.

There is no shame if the communication hypothesis dies. The remaining science is still rich: excited-state chemistry in living tissue, redox imaging, plant defense dynamics, skin photobiology, neural oxidative emission, detector physics and the history of a century-long scientific controversy.

The subject does not need a hidden language in order to matter.

That freedom is exactly what makes it possible to test the hidden language honestly.
