# The Light We Leak

*Biophotons, Oxidative Fire, and the Question of Whether Cells Talk in Light*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Authors** | Sven Hardy Benson |
| **Status** | Drafting |
| **Chapters** | 18 of 18 drafted |

**Reader promise.** Living tissue emits measurable photons without luciferase, fluorescence lamps, or mystical assistance. By the end of this book, the reader should understand exactly what that statement means chemically, why it took a century to become technically uncontroversial, and why it still does not answer the more interesting question: whether evolution ever turned this unavoidable optical exhaust into a usable signal.

**Distinct contribution.** The book joins a history of failed and successful detection, modern redox chemistry, plant and animal imaging, neural measurements, physical signal-to-noise constraints, and the persistent cell-communication literature. It treats “biophoton” neither as a synonym for nonsense nor as evidence of a hidden optical nervous system.

**Strongest competing explanation.** Ultraweak photon emission is a chemically inevitable by-product of oxidative metabolism whose correlations with physiology make it a useful readout but not a communication channel. On this account, most signaling claims are artifacts, uncontrolled chemistry, or interpretations that outrun photon budgets and receptor evidence.
