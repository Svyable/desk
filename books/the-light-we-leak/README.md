## Reader promise

Living tissue emits light. That sentence is no longer the strange part. Sensitive detectors can measure ultraweak photon emission from plants, microbes, isolated organelles, animal tissue, and human skin, and much of the emission can be traced to ordinary oxidative chemistry. The difficult question is what follows. A century ago Alexander Gurwitsch claimed that dividing cells emitted ultraviolet radiation capable of stimulating mitosis in nearby cells. The claim became famous, then embarrassing, then half-forgotten. Modern biophotonics has independently recovered the emission side of the story while leaving the signaling side unsettled.

This book follows that split. It asks how a real physical phenomenon became entangled with a much stronger biological claim, why the older literature collapsed under replication problems, what modern instruments have actually established, and what experiment would be capable of showing that living systems do more than merely leak photons as metabolic exhaust.

## Distinct contribution

The book does not treat “biophotons” as proof of a hidden biological language, nor does it treat the failure of the original mitogenetic-radiation program as proof that ultraweak photon emission is imaginary. It separates emission, reception, information, and function as four different burdens of evidence. It then uses the history of Gurwitsch’s onion-root work, modern oxidative-chemiluminescence physics, cell and mitochondrial experiments, neural UPE studies, instrumentation limits, and the recurring temptation to infer communication from correlation to build a falsifiable account of what is known and what remains open.

## Strongest competing explanation

The strongest parsimonious explanation is that biologically emitted photons are predominantly by-products of oxidative metabolism and stress, useful as biomarkers because they report chemistry but biologically inert at natural intensities. On this account, apparent distant-cell effects arise from uncontrolled chemical, volatile, thermal, vibration, electrical, optical-background, or statistical artifacts. The manuscript keeps this explanation active throughout rather than treating it as a ceremonial skeptic’s paragraph.

## Evidence classes

1. **Verified / documented:** ultraweak photon emission is measurable from living systems; known oxidative reactions generate electronically excited species capable of photon emission; human and animal emission can covary with metabolism and oxidative stress; Gurwitsch and contemporaries actually conducted and published a large mitogenetic-radiation research program.
2. **Credible but incomplete:** some modern experiments report non-chemical effects between optically coupled but chemically separated biological preparations; neural and mitochondrial UPE can track physiological state; spectral and temporal structure may contain more information than bulk photon counts reveal.
3. **Disputed / not independently established:** natural UPE has been shown to be a reliable causal communication channel between cells; Gurwitsch’s original mitogenetic effect has been reproduced under modern preregistered conditions; living systems possess dedicated receptors for endogenous UPE; coherent “biophoton fields” organize development or consciousness.
4. **Speculative synthesis / open conjecture:** metabolic photon leakage may occasionally be biologically readable without constituting a purpose-built language, especially in geometrically constrained, low-noise, high-sensitivity microenvironments. If so, biology could exploit a physical by-product after the fact rather than having evolved a separate light-emitting system.
