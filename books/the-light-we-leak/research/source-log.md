# Source Log

Checked for this draft on 2026-09-14. Links are public DOI, PubMed, PMC, journal, or publisher locations where available. A paper’s inclusion records what it establishes or claims; it does not imply endorsement of its interpretation.

## Historical mitogenetic-radiation record

- **Gurwitsch, A. (1933), “Mitogenetic Radiation of Nerve,” Nature 131, 912–913.** https://doi.org/10.1038/131912a0  
  Historical primary-source reply by Gurwitsch to A. V. Hill’s criticism. Establishes that the controversy over nerve-associated mitogenetic radiation was live in a major scientific journal and that Gurwitsch explicitly defended his laboratory’s work. Does not establish that the claimed nerve radiation existed.

- **Nature (1937), “Mitogenetic Rays?” 140, 1007–1008.** https://doi.org/10.1038/1401007c0  
  Contemporary report on Hollaender and Claus’s National Research Council investigation. Notes extensive precautions and negative biological and physical detection results, and remarks that roughly six hundred papers had already appeared. Important counterevidence to retrospective claims that the original phenomenon was simply accepted and later forgotten.

- **Glasser, O. & Barth, H. (1938), “Studies on the Problem of Mitogenetic Radiation,” Radiology 30, 62–67.** https://doi.org/10.1148/30.1.62  
  Particularly useful because the paper juxtaposes a laboratory reporting nonconfirmation with continued work by an investigator who had obtained positive results in collaboration with Gurwitsch. Demonstrates genuine contemporary disagreement and the central problem of reliable detection.

- **Gurwitsch, A. & Gurwitsch, L. (1939), “Ultra-Violet Chemi-Luminescence,” Nature 143, 1022–1023.** https://doi.org/10.1038/1431022b0  
  Primary historical source connecting the claimed mitogenetic phenomenon to ultraviolet chemiluminescence and chemical reactions. Supports the book’s argument that the field was already moving toward an ordinary excited-state chemical source rather than a sui generis life force.

- **Hollaender, A. (1939), “The Present Status of Mitogenetic Radiation,” Radiology 32, 404–410.** https://doi.org/10.1148/32.4.404  
  Contemporary critical review. Useful for the exact epistemic question: whether the phenomenon could be demonstrated reliably by appropriately trained independent investigators. Also documents yeast and bacteria as common biological detectors and the susceptibility of those systems to age, nutrient, temperature, and other variables.

- **Gurwitsch, A. A. (1988), “A historical review of the problem of mitogenetic radiation,” Experientia 44, 545–550.** https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01953301 ; PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3294029/  
  Later historical review from within the Gurwitsch intellectual lineage. Valuable but not neutral. Use for chronology and the field’s own account, not as independent confirmation of disputed experimental claims.

- **Naumova, E. V. et al. (2018), “Historical review of early researches on mitogenetic radiation: from discovery to cancer diagnostics,” Journal of Biomedical Photonics & Engineering.** https://doi.org/10.18287/JBPE18.04.040201  
  Detailed modern historical review of 1923–1948 work. Useful for mapping experiments and proposed diagnostic applications. Treat interpretive rehabilitation claims separately from primary documentation.

- **Volodyaev, I. & Beloussov, L. V. (2015), “Revisiting the mitogenetic effect of ultra-weak photon emission,” Frontiers in Physiology 6:241.** https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2015.00241 ; PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26441668/ ; full text: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2015.00241/full  
  Modern argument for reopening parts of the historical question. Reports the scale of early literature and discusses possible information transfer. Valuable as a pro-reconsideration source; not independent proof that the historical mitogenetic effect was real.

## Modern UPE mechanism and measurement

- **Cifra, M. & Pospíšil, P. (2014), “Ultra-weak photon emission from biological samples: definition, mechanisms, properties, detection and applications,” Journal of Photochemistry and Photobiology B 139.** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jphotobiol.2014.02.009 ; PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24726298/  
  Core modern review. Defines UPE as biological chemiluminescence associated with electronically excited species formed during oxidative metabolism/stress; reviews spectral ranges, intensities, detection, and applications. Primary backbone for the established-mechanism sections.

- **Rastogi, A. & Pospíšil, P. (2011), “Spontaneous ultraweak photon emission imaging of oxidative metabolic processes in human skin,” Journal of Biomedical Optics 16(9).** https://doi.org/10.1117/1.3616135 ; PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21950919/  
  Experimental support linking skin UPE to molecular oxygen, ROS, and antioxidant systems.

- **Tsuchida, K. et al. (2020), “Ultraviolet A irradiation induces ultraweak photon emission with characteristic spectral patterns from biomolecules present in human skin,” Scientific Reports.** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-020-78884-0 ; PubMed/PMC: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33303911/  
  Useful for molecular/spectral contributions and the oxidative-stress mechanism in skin. Supports plural-source rather than single-emitter models.

- **Prasad, A. & Pospíšil, P. (2013), “Towards the two-dimensional imaging of spontaneous ultra-weak photon emission from microbial, plant and animal cells,” Scientific Reports 3:1211.** https://doi.org/10.1038/srep01211  
  Cross-system imaging evidence and instrumentation reference.

## Human and neural emission

- **Kobayashi, M., Kikuchi, D. & Okamura, H. (2009), “Imaging of ultraweak spontaneous photon emission from human body displaying diurnal rhythm,” PLOS ONE 4:e6256.** https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0006256 ; PubMed/PMC: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19606225/  
  Direct human-body UPE imaging with reported daily variation. Establishes an instrumentally measurable physiological pattern; does not establish communication or visible auras.

- **Kobayashi, M. et al. (1999), “In vivo imaging of spontaneous ultraweak photon emission from a rat’s brain correlated with cerebral energy metabolism and oxidative stress,” Neuroscience Research.** https://doi.org/10.1016/S0168-0102(99)00040-1 ; PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10498336/  
  Animal-brain UPE imaging tied to metabolism, cortical activity, blood flow, and hyperoxia. Supports biomarker interpretation.

- **Raskopf, W. & Redmond, B. (2026), “Ultraweak Photon Emission as a Noninvasive Biomarker of Neural Metabolism: A Narrative Review of Emerging Evidence,” Neurology 106(11 Suppl 1).** https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000216482  
  Very recent review/meeting abstract framing UPE as an emerging neural metabolic biomarker. Useful for current research direction; evidence is review-level and the published item carries an explicit abstract disclaimer.

- **Erboz, A. et al. (2024), “Electromagnetic radiation and biophoton emission in neuronal communication and neurodegenerative diseases,” Progress in Biophysics and Molecular Biology 195:87–99.** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pbiomolbio.2024.12.004 ; PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39732343/  
  Recent review of stronger neural-communication hypotheses. Use as a map of claims and mechanisms, not as proof of endogenous communication.

## Non-chemical / candidate optical interactions

- **Kučera, O. & Cifra, M. (2013), “Cell-to-cell signaling through light: just a ghost of chance?” Cell Communication and Signaling 11:87.** https://doi.org/10.1186/1478-811X-11-87 ; PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24219796/  
  Essential quantitative counterweight. Reviews candidate cell-to-cell optical communication and argues that low emission intensity plus ambient-light signal-to-noise are important obstacles under natural conditions. Strong source for the book’s insistence on photon budgets.

- **Mould, D. et al. (2023), “Non-chemical signalling between mitochondria,” Frontiers in Physiology / PMC.** PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37811497/ ; full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10560087/  
  Reports respiration changes between chemically/physically separated mitochondrial populations and light dependence; authors interpret results as supporting non-chemical signaling with a possible light basis while explicitly acknowledging that mechanism remains unresolved. Core Class 2 source.

- **Kam, J. H. H., Kalampouka, I. & Cantat-Moltrecht, T. (2026), “Light talks: The role of biophoton in cellular communication,” International Review of Neurobiology 189:71–104.** https://doi.org/10.1016/bs.irn.2026.01.014 ; PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42613147/  
  Current review of communication claims. Useful for state-of-field mapping. Review evidence must be followed to primary experiments before any strong manuscript claim is upgraded.

## Coherence and stronger theoretical interpretations

- **Popp, F. A. et al. (1984), “Biophoton emission. New evidence for coherence and DNA as source,” Cell Biophysics 6:33–52.** https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02788579 ; PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6204761/  
  Primary source for Popp-school coherence/DNA claims. Establishes what was claimed, not that modern quantum-optical standards have confirmed those claims.

- **Popp, F. A. et al. (1988), “Physical aspects of biophotons,” Experientia 44:576–585.** https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01953305 ; PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3294033/  
  Primary source for coherent-field interpretation based on delayed luminescence and theoretical modeling. Treat coherence inference as disputed pending modern matched-control quantum-optical characterization.

- **Babcock, N. S. (2024), “Open quantum systems theory of ultraweak ultraviolet photon emissions: Revisiting Gurwitsch’s onion experiment as a prototype for quantum biology,” Computational and Structural Biotechnology Journal 26:78–91.** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.csbj.2024.11.030 ; PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39717158/  
  Modern theoretical treatment. Useful for generating quantitative experiments and showing current interest; theory is not empirical replication of Gurwitsch’s effect.

## Source-use rule

Patents, alternative-medicine summaries, “biofield” claims, aura photography, and unsourced quantum-consciousness material are not used as evidence for efficacy or mechanism. If later included to document cultural diffusion, they should be explicitly labeled as claims or commercial/historical artifacts. The manuscript makes no claim of non-public access, classified information, or proprietary experimental data.
