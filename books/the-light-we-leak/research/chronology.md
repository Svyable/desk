# Chronology

- **1923–1928:** Alexander and Lydia Gurwitsch publish a sequence of studies on “mitogenetic radiation,” using biological induction assays and optical barriers.
- **1934:** Eugene Lorenz reports a sensitive photoelectric search that fails to detect the claimed radiation in tested biological materials.
- **1960s–1980s:** Improved photomultiplier technology supports broader investigation of ultraweak biological luminescence; biochemical excited-state mechanisms develop in parallel.
- **1984:** Popp and collaborators publish influential claims of coherence and DNA involvement in biophoton emission.
- **2001:** Kataoka and colleagues report activity-dependent ultraweak photon emission from neural tissue associated with oxidation.
- **2005–2009:** Plant defense/wounding and human-body imaging studies demonstrate spatial and physiological structure in UPE.
- **2009:** Kobayashi, Kikuchi and Okamura image diurnal variation in spontaneous human-body photon emission with a cryogenic CCD.
- **2013:** Kučera and Cifra publish a quantitative critique of cell-to-cell optical signaling feasibility and ambient-noise constraints.
- **2015:** Volodyaev and Beloussov revisit the mitogenetic-effect literature and argue for renewed controlled study.
- **2018:** Wound-induced plant UPE is reported to be suppressed under anoxic stress, strengthening the oxidative-generation interpretation.
- **2019–2020:** Human-skin studies connect UV-induced UPE, oxidative damage, antioxidant effects and spectral signatures.
- **2024:** A modern review summarizes UPE mechanisms and applications; separate DNA-interface work renews strong coherence/source interpretations that remain unproven in vivo.
- **2026:** The research landscape still supports a sharp distinction between emission, physiological correlation and demonstrated endogenous communication.
