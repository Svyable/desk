# Thesis Freshness Record

## Selected thesis

**The Light We Leak: Biophotons, Mitogenetic Radiation, and the Signal Hidden in Metabolism** asks why a historically discredited claim about mitosis-inducing radiation partially reappeared as a much better established physical phenomenon—ultraweak photon emission—without thereby validating the original biological function. Its empirical core is oxidative chemiluminescence, low-count optical instrumentation, historical Soviet/Russian developmental biophysics, cell/mitochondrial non-contact experiments, candidate photoreception, and the distinction between biomarker information and biological signaling.

## Why this thesis was selected

Two neglected evidence streams collide unusually cleanly:

- A historically controversial Soviet-origin research program claimed weak ultraviolet radiation from living tissue that could alter cell division, then suffered major replication and detection failures.
- Modern mainstream photobiology independently establishes that living systems really do emit ultraweak photons generated substantially by oxidative chemistry and can measure those photons with modern detectors.

The surprise is not “Gurwitsch was right.” The book exists in the gap between *physical emission* and *biological function*. That gap is empirically testable and epistemologically rich.
